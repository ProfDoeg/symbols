HERO. Jūratė and Kastytis (Baltic (the tales) heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the sea goddess in the amber palace who loved the fisherman Kastytis, Perkūnas's thunderbolt that shattered the palace (the amber on the Baltic shore) and chained her to the ruins, the legend's literary origin (Liudvikas Adomas Jucevičius 1842, Maironis's ballad 1920), the Palanga statue, the Amber Room as reception.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Lithuanian tale collections (Basanavičius, Balys, Kerbelytė's catalogue), the Eglė variants (Sauka's study), the Lithuanian and Latvian dainos, the Jūratė legend's literary history (Maironis's ballad 1920, the amber castle), the Palanga statues, Gimbutas.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

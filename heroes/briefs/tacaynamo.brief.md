HERO. Tacaynamo (Andean and Inca heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the founder of Chimor who arrived by raft from the north, the Anónimo de Trujillo (1604) and the dynasty to Minchançaman, the Chan Chan palaces as the kings' compounds, the Chimú conquest by the Inca (c. 1470).

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the chroniclers (Betanzos, Sarmiento, Cieza, Cobo, Garcilaso, Guaman Poma, Murúa, Cabello Valboa for Naymlap, the Anónimo de Trujillo for Tacaynamo), the Huarochirí Manuscript, the Ollantay drama (the Justo Pastor Sahuaraura and Valdez manuscripts, its date dispute), Arguedas and Ortiz Rescaniere on Inkarrí (the 1950s collection at Puquio), Rostworowski, Zuidema, Urton, Flores Galindo's Buscando un Inca, the Argentine northwest folklore for the cordillera versions.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

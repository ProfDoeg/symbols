HERO. François Makandal (Haitian and Caribbean heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the one-armed maroon of Saint-Domingue who ran the poison conspiracy of the 1750s, burned at Cap-Français in 1758 and said to have escaped the flames as a fly, the trial records and the legend, the Makandal of Carpentier's El reino de este mundo and of the Haitian coin, the packets (makandals) named for him.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: Moreau de Saint-Méry, the trial records of Makandal (1758) and the Bois Caïman testimonies (Dalmas 1814), Geggus, Fick's The Making of Haiti, Dubois, Madiou and Ardouin for the Haitian historians' Boukman, Las Casas and Oviedo for Anacaona (the 1503 Jaragua massacre), the Haitian poetry (Coicou, Depestre) and Carpentier's El reino de este mundo as reception.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

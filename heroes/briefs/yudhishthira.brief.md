HERO. Yudhishthira (Hindu (the Mahabharata) heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the eldest Pandava, son of Dharma, the king who lost everything at dice, the Yaksha's questions at the lake, the one lie about Ashvatthama, the Rajasuya and the horse sacrifice, the last walk to the Himalaya with the dog and the ascent in the body, the hell shown to him, the dharma-king problem in the scholarship.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Mahabharata critical edition (Poona; van Buitenen, Ganguli, the Clay Sanskrit Library), the Harivamsa, the regional Mahabharatas and folk cults (the Draupadi cult of Tamil Nadu, Hiltebeitel; the Karna of the Marathi tradition), the Bhagavad Gita for Arjuna, Bhasa's plays, the Kathakali and Yakshagana repertoires, the 1988 TV serial as reception; Hiltebeitel's The Ritual of Battle, Biardeau, Brockington's The Sanskrit Epics, Sukthankar, Doniger, the Sanskrit at GRETIL.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

HERO. Kaveh the Blacksmith (Persian heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the smith who raised his leather apron on a spear against Zahhak and led the people to Fereydun, the Derafsh-e Kaviani that became the Sasanian royal banner, the Kaveh of the Iranian and Kurdish nationalisms (the Newroz fire, the Kaveh journal of Berlin 1916), the smith's revolt as political myth.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: Ferdowsi's Shahnameh (Khaleghi-Motlagh's edition, Davis's translation, Warner and Warner), the Avesta's yashts where the heroes appear (Yasht 19 for Fereydun and Garshasp), the Bundahishn and Denkard, the Garshaspnameh of Asadi Tusi, the Borzunameh, Tabari and Bal'ami, the Sasanian Khwaday-namag tradition; the Encyclopaedia Iranica, Yarshater's Cambridge History of Iran III, Omidsalar, Davidson's Poet and Hero, the Shahnameh illustrations (the Great Mongol, the Shah Tahmasp).

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

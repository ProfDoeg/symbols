HERO. Luqman (Arabic and Islamic (the epic and Qur'anic heroes) heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the sage of Surah 31 and his counsels to his son, the pre-Islamic Luqman of the long-lived (the seven vultures), the Luqman of the fables identified with Aesop (the Arabic Aesop), the Ethiopian or Nubian slave of the traditions, the Luqman of the Persian and Turkish wisdom literature.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Qur'an and the tafsir (al-Tabari) and the qisas al-anbiya (al-Tha'labi, al-Kisa'i) for Luqman, Dhul-Qarnayn, al-Khidr and the Sleepers, the Sirat Antar (the 32-volume epic and its recitation), the Mu'allaqat for the historical Antarah, the Layla and Majnun of the Kitab al-Aghani and Nizami, the Alexander Romance in Arabic and Persian (the Iskandarnameh), the Thousand and One Nights (Sinbad's date and the Galland question); the Encyclopaedia of Islam, Heath's Thirsty Sword (Antar), Stoneman on Alexander, Irwin's Companion to the Arabian Nights.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

HERO. Peredur (Celtic (Irish and Welsh) heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the Welsh Perceval of Peredur fab Efrawg: the boy raised in the forest without arms, Arthur's court, the head on the platter and the bleeding lance (the Grail without the Grail), the Empress of Constantinople, the relation to Chrétien's Perceval debated.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Ulster Cycle (the Táin in Kinsella's and O'Rahilly's versions, the Death of Aífe's Only Son, Bricriu's Feast, the Exile of the Sons of Uisliu), the Fenian Cycle (the Acallam na Senórach, Macgnímartha Finn, the Pursuit of Diarmuid and Gráinne), the Mabinogion (Culhwch ac Olwen), the Book of Taliesin, the Dindshenchas; Ó hÓgáin's Fionn mac Cumhaill and The Lore of Ireland, Nagy's The Wisdom of the Outlaw, Ford's Ystoria Taliesin, MacKillop, the CELT corpus, the Ossian controversy (Macpherson) as reception.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

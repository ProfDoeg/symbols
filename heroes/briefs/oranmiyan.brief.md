HERO. Oranmiyan (Oranyan) (Yoruba heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the son of Oduduwa (or of two fathers, the story of the half-and-half body), the founder of Oyo and the first Alaafin, the sent king of Benin who fathered Eweka and went home, the Oranmiyan staff (the Opa Oranmiyan, the granite monolith at Ife with its iron nails), the horse and the grave that opened, Johnson's History of the Yorubas and the Benin chronicles.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: Samuel Johnson's History of the Yorubas (1921), the Ife and Oyo oral histories (the Oranmiyan staff at Ife, the Moremi festival at Ife, the Edi festival), Akinjogbin, Law's The Oyo Empire, Obayemi, Bascom, Peel; the Benin chronicles for Oranmiyan (Egharevba), the Moremi statue at Ile-Ife (2016) and the Queen Moremi Ajasoro musical as reception.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

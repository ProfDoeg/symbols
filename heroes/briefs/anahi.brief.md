HERO. Anahí (Guaraní (the legends of Corrientes and Misiones) heroes). State at the top the figure's class as the tradition classes it (mortal
hero, demigod, heroized dead with cult, culture hero, epic king) and the earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the Guaraní girl with the ugly face and the beautiful voice who was captured by the Spaniards, killed her guard, was burned at the stake and became the ceibo tree with its red flowers, the legend's Argentine literary fixing (the school readers, the chamamé 'Anahí' of Osvaldo Sosa Cordero), the ceibo as national flower (1942), the Corrientes and Entre Ríos versions.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Corrientes and Misiones folklore collections (Vidal de Battini, Chertudi, Ambrosetti, Villafañe), the Anahí legend's literary fixing (the Argentine school readers and the chamamé, the ceibo as national flower 1942), the irupé tales, Cadogan, Montoya, the Jesuit sources.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named god or hero gets its own line (the other figure, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for the gods of this tradition in
symbols/pantheon and for the other heroes here, and the files cross-reference by name. Absence of
evidence is a finding.

THREADS TO PULL: the catalogue entry follows, built from Yates by four readers; every line of it is a lead to verify against the primary text and the scholarship:

Yates's suggestion (Ch. V and VII) that the impresa and the emblem, and the "wild imaginative" memory of the *Hypnerotomachia*, be seen "from the point of view of memory"; examples from the Vienna codex 5393 (Boethius), Francesco Colonna, Ruscelli, Achilles Bocchius, Dee's *Monas*, Farra's *Settenario*.

**Principle.** Not a system with rules but a genre read as a memory phenomenon. The impresa "is the attempt to remember a spiritual intention through a similitude; the words of Thomas Aquinas define it exactly", so emblem and device are memory images turned outward. The Dominican-authored *Hypnerotomachia*, with its Hell divided into places with inscriptions, may be "an artificial memory gone out of control into wild imaginative indulgence". Camillo's images are "very like imprese", and commentaries on imprese blend Hermetic-Cabalist mysticism; Bocchius's Mercurius with the seven-branched candlestick is the book's frontispiece. Romberch's inscribed Grammar shows how a mnemonic figure, its explanation lost, "might seem like some inscrutable emblem".

**Components.**
- Emblem and impresa as memory (with the 'wild imaginative' memory: Boethius, the *Hypnerotomachia*, Bocchius, Dee) `[2.20]`

**Yates.** Ch. V, pp. 123–124; Ch. VII, pp. 169–170 (n. 25) and Frontispiece.

**In Yates's words.**
- "The impresa, in particular, is the attempt to remember a spiritual intention through a similitude; the words of Thomas Aquinas define it exactly." (p. 124)
- "Perhaps an artificial memory gone out of control into wild imaginative indulgence might be one of the stimuli behind such a work as the Hypnerotomachia Polyphili" (p. 123)
- "The impresa is related to the memory image, as already suggested, and in commentaries on imprese there is frequently to be found a blend of Hermetic-Cabalist mysticism" (pp. 169–170)

**Descends from / leads to.** From 10 (Thomas's definition), 17 (visual alphabets possibly behind the *Hypnerotomachia*'s inscriptions; Grammar as emblem). To 21–22 (Camillo's imprese-like images), 39 (the *Eroici furori* impresa shields as memory images), 45 (Leibniz's emblem-figure title-page). Dee's *Monas* (1564) is listed here as an example and treated as a marginal analogue in the Appendix.

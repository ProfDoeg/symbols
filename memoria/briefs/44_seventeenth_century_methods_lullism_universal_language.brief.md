Cornelius Gemma, *De arte cyclognomica* (Antwerp 1569); the Lullian Art as judged by Bacon, Descartes and the 1632 Bureau d'Adresse; Johann-Heinrich Alsted (1588–1638), *Systema mnemonicum duplex* (1610) and *Clavis artis Lullianae* (1633); J. Comenius, *Orbis sensualium pictus* (Nuremberg 1658); the universal-language movement (Comenius, Bisterfield, Dalgarno, Wilkins); Sebastian Izquierdo, *Pharus Scientiarum* (Leyden 1659) and Athanasius Kircher, *Ars magna sciendi* (Amsterdam 1669).

**Principle.** "These two great mediaeval arts, which the Renaissance had tried to combine, turn into methods in the seventeenth century." Gemma's "circular method" for knowing everything is "a compound of Lullism, Hermetism, Cabalism, and the art of memory", showing how unsurprising Descartes's title would have seemed. Lullism offered "a universal art or method which, because based on reality, could be applied for the solution of all problems", a geometrical logic with revolving wheels and a letter notation, rejected by both Bacon and Descartes yet sharing their aims. Alsted, "encyclopaedist, Lullist, Cabalist, Ramist", defines method as "the mnemonic instrument which proceeds from generals to specials" and "calls the Lullian circles places corresponding to the places of the art of memory", assimilating Lullism to Cabalism through the *De auditu kabbalistico* while wishing to free it from "idle dreams" and return to Lavinheta: a reformed version of Brunian procedures. Comenius, Alsted's pupil, turns the Solarian pedagogy of the City of the Sun into a picture primer teaching Latin, German, Italian and French by pictures "arranged in the order of the world", "a perfectly rational, and extremely original and valuable, language primer". The universal-language enthusiasts seek "real characters" in contact with the notions they express, "translating into rational terms" Bruno's magic images directly in contact with reality. Izquierdo surveys the universal-art projects and insists Lullism be "mathematicised", substituting combinations of numbers for combinations of letters; Kircher likewise; "a presage of Leibniz's use of the principles of the combinatoria as a calculus".

**In Yates's words.**
- "These two great mediaeval arts, which the Renaissance had tried to combine, turn into methods in the seventeenth century and play their part in the methodological revolution." (p. 370)
- "He defines method as the mnemonic instrument which proceeds from generals to specials (a definition of course, also influenced by Ramism) and he calls the Lullian circles places corresponding to the places of the art of memory." (p. 376)
- "Translated into the Orbis pictus, the Solarian magic memory system becomes a perfectly rational, and extremely original and valuable, language primer." (p. 378)
- "He insists that Lullism must be 'mathematicised' and in fact he gives pages and pages in which, for the Lullian combinations of letters, combinations of numbers have been substituted." (p. 379)

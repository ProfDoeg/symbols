Bruno, *De imaginum, signorum et idearum compositione*, Frankfort 1591, probably written at Hainzell's castle near Zurich, dedicated to Johann Heinrich Hainzell; in three parts.

**Principle.** Part I gives the Ad Herennian rules once more "in a yet more mystifying form", called a method ("We institute a method, not about things but about the significance of things"): rules for images first, a visual alphabet of objects straight out of Romberch (O a sphere, A a ladder or compasses, I a column), images lively and charged with affects "so that they may pass through the doors of the storehouse of memory", then rules for places partly in Latin verse with Romberch's reach-of-a-man rule. Its architectural system is "of terrible complexity": twenty-four atria each of nine places, fifteen fields of nine, thirty cubicles, in which everything in the lower world is memorised, plants, stones, metals, animals, arts, inventions, "all things which can be said, known, or imagined", the encyclopaedic wheels of *Shadows* redistributed into rooms, "worked from above by celestial mechanics". Part II supplies that celestial part: twelve "principles", Jupiter, Saturn, Mars, Mercury, Minerva, Apollo, Aesculapius, Sol, Luna, Venus, Cupid, Tellus, under the "ineffable and infigurable Optimus Maximus", great statues each surrounded by talismanic images composed to draw their powers into the psyche, Ficinian talismanic magic applied to memory images. The diagram of the twenty-four atria, a square plan of rooms related to the compass points inside a circle Yates takes for the heavens, realises the "double picture" of Zeuxis simultaneously, "the memory temple of an astral religion", the letters on the central circle possibly reading "Alta Astra". Part III is a second, German, listing of Thirty Seals, "if possible, even more obscure", with "mathesistical" place systems and no Seal of Seals.

**Components.**
- *Images*, Part I: the art of memory as "method" (rules for images and places) `[3.39]`
- *Images*, Part I: the architectural system of twenty-four atria, fifteen fields and thirty cubicles `[3.40]`
- *Images*, Part II: the twelve celestial principles (astral statues with talismanic images) `[3.41]`
- *Images*: the combined round-and-square Seal ("Alta Astra") `[3.42]`
- *Images*, Part III: the German Thirty Seals `[3.43]`

**In Yates's words.**
- "'We institute a method, not about things but about the significance of things.'" (p. 294)
- "This is an architectural 'Seal' full of correspondences, associative orders, which are both mnemonic and astral." (p. 296)
- "a round building representing the heaven with a square lay-out inside it, a building reflecting the upper and the lower worlds in which the world as a whole is remembered from above" (p. 297)
- "he as it were both 'talismanises' mnemonic images and introduces mnemonic aspects into talismans as he 'composes' the latter to suit his purposes." (p. 299)

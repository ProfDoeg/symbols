Bruno, *Triginta sigilli* with the *Explanatio triginta sigillorum*, London, early 1583 (John Charlewood), dedicated to Mauvissière, with an address to Oxford; the second and third items of the *Seals* volume.

**Principle.** Thirty titled statements of principles and techniques of magic memory, on Riccio's pattern but "magicised, complicated with Lullism and Cabbalism, blown up into inscrutable mysteries", followed by thirty "explanations", some with "semi-mathematical" diagrams; not confined to one system, Bruno "is trying every possible way", working with two sets of ideas, memory and astrology. The Field is the memory or phantasy to be worked (with "Solyman the Thalmudist" and his twelve patriarchal divisions); the Heaven uses the twelve houses of a horoscope as memory rooms for the "images of heaven"; the Chain is Aristotelian association revealed as the zodiac whose signs run into one another; the Tree, the Wood and the Ladder are Lullist (the *Arbor scientiae*; the third figure of the *Ars brevis*); the Table is the persons-alphabet of Peter of Ravenna; the Standard makes leading images (Plato, Aristotle, Diogenes) standard-bearers for whole groups; Daedalus attaches memory objects to main images; the Numerator forms images for numbers; the Century arranges a hundred friends in a hundred places; Squaring the Circle and the Potter's Wheel use the horoscope diagram as a magical place system, the latter with a bar of the seven planets revolving inside it; the Doctor uses shops as places, "not as straightforward" as Romberch's; the Field and Garden of Circe moves elemental compounds through seven houses after invocation of the planets; the Peregrinator sends images travelling through memory rooms; the Cabalistic Enclosure permutes the orders of society from Popes to Peasants; the Combiner and the Interpreter are Lullist combinations and Cabalist manipulations of the Hebrew alphabet. Beneath all, "the old tricks of the memory trade" are recognisable Seal after Seal.

**Yates.** Ch. XI, pp. 243, 246–254 (survey pp. 246–251; interpretation pp. 251–254); Elizabethan reception pp. 259–265; Pl. 14a (The Heaven), Pl. 14b (The Potter's Wheel). Yates takes Seal 12 before Seal 9 and Seal 26 before 25, and does not enumerate the whole Thirty.

**In Yates's words.**
- "This is what the Seals are, statements of the principles and techniques of the art—but magicised, complicated with Lullism and Cabbalism, blown up into inscrutable mysteries." (p. 246)
- "Bruno states that he is trying every possible way; perhaps something for which he is not looking may emerge out of this, as alchemists who do not succeed in making gold sometimes hit on other important discoveries." (p. 249)
- "He is working with two sets of ideas, memory and astrology." (p. 251)
- "But in Bruno's system, the orders perform Cabalistic permutations and combinations among themselves." (p. 251)

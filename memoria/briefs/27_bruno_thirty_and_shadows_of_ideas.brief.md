THREADS TO PULL: the catalogue entry follows, built from Yates by four readers; every line of it is a lead to verify against the primary text and the scholarship:

Bruno's thirty-lettered Lullian wheels, the philosophy of *De umbris idearum* (1582), and the *sigilli*, intentions, concepts and thirty mythological images that accompany them; *De compendiosa architectura artis Lullii* (Paris 1582).

**Principle.** Lullian concentric circles divided into thirty segments carrying the Latin alphabet plus Greek and Hebrew letters, more than any genuine Art; the Thirty are Lullian Dignities Cabalised as Sephiroth ("the Jewish Cabalists reduce to ten sephiroth and we to thirty"), the Trinitarian use rejected for the Tetragrammaton multiplied to the universe, and Yates believes the wheels doubled as practical Cabala conjuring the spirits of the air, the "architecture" of Lullian figures replacing the memory building. Star-images stand between the Ideas and the elemental world and are the "shadows of ideas", nearer reality than physical shadows: imprinted on memory and set on revolving wheels, "the lower things will arrange themselves in memory once we have arranged there the images of the higher things"; the Metrodorian system "has turned into a magical system". Images are graded by nearness to reality, the least opaque being *sigilli*, the theory of the 150 astral seals transmitting astral force. Two preliminary sets of thirty gnomic statements, the intentions of the shadows and the concepts of ideas, headed by the thirty letters and illustrated with thirty-lettered wheels (one with a sun at its centre), expound the road from multiplicity to the One, the intentions being the Lullian *voluntas* element. A further list of thirty mythological images from Lycaon to Glaucus, lettered for the wheels, is a separate system of the Thirty Statues type.

**Components.**
- Bruno's thirty-lettered Lullian wheels: the Thirty as Cabalised Dignities; conjuring wheels `[2.56]`
- The shadows of ideas: star-images as intermediaries; Metrodorus made magical `[2.62]`
- The *sigilli*: degrees of potency of memory images `[2.66]`
- The thirty intentions of the shadows and the thirty concepts of ideas `[2.67]`
- The thirty mythological images (Lycaon to Glaucus): a separate system `[2.68]`

**Yates.** Ch. IX, pp. 208–211, 215–217, 222 (n. 63), 223, 225–230; Fig. 8 (Memory Wheels from *De umbris idearum*).

**In Yates's words.**
- "He put the images of the classical art on the Lullian combinatory wheels, but the images were magic images and the wheels were conjuring wheels." (p. 211)
- "'We form them in us, as in the revolution of wheels. If you know any other way, try it.'" (Bruno, p. 216)
- "Those with the highest degree of potency, which are least opaque to reality, he calls 'sigilli'." (p. 223)
- "The aim of the memory system is to establish within, in the psyche, the return of the intellect to unity through the organisation of significant images." (p. 228)

Metrodorus of Scepsis, Greek rhetor at Mithridates's court (fl. c. 100–70 BC); known only through Quintilian, Cicero, Pliny, Strabo, Plutarch; revived as a place system by Romberch (1520) and Rossellius (1579).

**Principle.** Quintilian says Metrodorus "found three hundred and sixty places in the twelve signs through which the sun moves"; L. A. Post's reconstruction, endorsed by Yates, divides the zodiac into 12 signs and 36 decans with their decan-figures, ten loci under each decan, yielding a numbered series 1–360 in which any place is findable by calculation. The astrological images serve as the order of places as memorised buildings do in the ordinary method; if Quintilian is right, shorthand *notae* were then "written" on them. Pliny says he could repeat what he heard in the very same words. Romberch revives the twelve signs as "an easily memorised order of places", naming Metrodorus as authority, and extends it with Hyginus's constellation images when more places are needed; Rossellius repeats it with mnemonic verses. Every later celestial memory invokes Metrodorus, and the decan images, "certainly believed to be magical images", are the root the Renaissance occult systems grow from.

**Components.**
- Metrodorus of Scepsis's zodiacal memory (360 places in the twelve signs) `[1.12]`
- The zodiac and the constellations as place system (Metrodorus of Scepsis revived; Romberch with Hyginus) `[2.12]`

**In Yates's words.**
- "Every subsequent user of a celestial memory system will invoke Metrodorus of Scepsis as the classical authority for bringing the stars into memory." (p. 39)
- "It had to do with memory for words, possibly through memorising the notae or symbols of shorthand, and was connected with the zodiac. That is all we really know." (p. 42)
- "As another type of place system, Romberch envisages using the signs of the zodiac as giving an easily memorised order of places. He gives the name of Metrodorus of Scepsis as the authority on this." (p. 116)

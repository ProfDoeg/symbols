John Ridevall, O.F.M., *Fulgentius metaforalis* (early fourteenth century); Robert Holcot, O.P., *Moralitates* and the commentary on Hosea.

**Principle.** The friars' "pictures" are descriptions of images never meant to be painted: "invisible memory images, held within the memory ... being used for quite practical mnemonic purposes". Ridevall's Idolatry is a prostitute, blind, ears mutilated, trumpeted, deformed, diseased, each feature keyed to a point of a sermon and fixed by a mnemonic verse; his pagan gods are moralised memory images (Saturn Prudence, Juno memoria, Neptune intelligentia, Pluto providentia). Holcot's "sham antique" pictures (Penance as a naked man with a five-thonged scourge carrying five inscribed sentences; Friendship as a youth in green with inscriptions) memorise sentences as written on the image, which Yates takes to be what the Middle Ages understood by "memory for words". Holcot also places such images, Cupid with bow and arrows included, in imagination on the pages of the prophet's text at the point he will comment on: the page as place system, the moralised pagan image as image. The artificial memory becomes "a hitherto unsuspected medium through which pagan imagery survived".

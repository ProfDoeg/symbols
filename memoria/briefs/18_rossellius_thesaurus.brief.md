Cosmas Rossellius, O.P. (Florentine), *Thesaurus artificiosae memoriae*, Venice 1579.

**Principle.** Hell is divided into eleven places, a central well reached by steps carrying Heretics, Jewish Infidels, Idolaters and Hypocrites, and around it seven places for the seven deadly sins, the punishments, situations and gestures of the damned giving the images; Paradise is a gem-walled enclosure with the Throne of Christ at centre and the hierarchies, Apostles, Patriarchs, Prophets, Martyrs, Confessors, Virgins and saints ranked below "as painters paint them", formed "with art and exercise and vehement imagination". The rest of the apparatus: constellation places after Metrodorus; mnemonic verses (*carmina*) by "a fellow Dominican who is also an Inquisitor" fixing orders of places in Hell or in the zodiac; real places in abbeys and churches; a human figure as a place on which subsidiary images are hung; a visual alphabet of Romberch's type. For the arts and sciences a famous practitioner serves as image (Valla or Priscian for Grammar, Cicero for Rhetoric, Aristotle for Dialectic, Plato for Theology, Archimedes for Geometry, Apollo or Orpheus for Music), with Pythagoras and Zoroaster for "Magia", which "has moved up into the virtues".

**Components.**
- Rossellius's *Thesaurus*: Hell in eleven places and Paradise as artificial memory `[2.18]`
- Rossellius: constellation places, the Inquisitor's mnemonic verses, human images as places `[2.19]`
- Rossellius: notable practitioners placed as images of the arts, sciences and virtues `[2.33]`

**Yates.** Ch. V, pp. 122–123; Ch. VII, p. 164; Pl. 7a (Hell as Artificial Memory), Pl. 7b (Paradise as Artificial Memory); recalled Ch. IX pp. 199, 209, 223; Ch. XII p. 267 (Perkins allies him with Dicson); Ch. XI p. 256 (faculty-psychology diagram).

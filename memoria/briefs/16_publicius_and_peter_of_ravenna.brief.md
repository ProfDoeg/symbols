Jacobus Publicius, *Oratoriae artis epitome* with *Ars memorativa* (Venice 1482, the first printed memory treatise); Peter of Ravenna (Petrus Tommai), *Phoenix, sive artificiosa memoria* (Venice 1491); with Yates's sorting of the printed treatises into strands.

**Principle.** Publicius's *ficta loca* are the spheres of the universe (elements, planets, fixed stars, higher spheres, "Paradisus" at the top), a Dantesque memory of Hell, Purgatory and Paradise on the cosmic order; his images follow Thomas and the Ad Herennian strikingness with Ovid's Envy as the model; he is the first to print a visual alphabet, and he "comes straight out of the mediaeval tradition", not the humanist revival. Peter of Ravenna forms loci in an unfrequented church, walked round three or four times, adds places on journeys until he claims 100,000 and more; images resemble known people, and persons in alphabetical order stand for syllables (Eusebius before Thomas = ET); he laicised and popularised the art and stressed its purely mnemotechnical side, with the rule that a locus be no larger than a man can reach. Yates sorts the printed treatises into four strands: the "straight mnemotechnic" better understood after the recovery of Quintilian; those entangled with medieval uses; those touched by *Ars notoria* magic; those touched by the Hermetic transformation.

**Components.**
- Publicius's *Ars memorativa*: the spheres of the universe as imaginary places `[2.7]`
- Peter of Ravenna's *Phoenix* `[2.8]`
- The 'straight mnemotechnic' and the four strands of the printed treatises `[2.9]` (its magical examples are under system 6)

**Yates.** Ch. V, pp. 110–114, 117–119; Fig. 1 (The Spheres of the Universe as a Memory System, Publicius 1482); Ch. XII p. 260 (Copland's English *Phoenix*); Ch. XI p. 250 (Bruno's admiration for Peter); Ch. XII p. 277 (Perkins on Peter's libidinous images).

**In Yates's words.**
- "this first printed memory treatise is not a symptom of the revival of the classical art of memory as part of the Renaissance revival of rhetoric; it comes straight out of the mediaeval tradition." (pp. 110–111)
- "As a young man he started with one hundred thousand memorised places, but he has added many more since then." (p. 113)
- "Some treatises teach what I shall hereafter call 'the straight mnemotechnic', perhaps better understood after the recovery of Quintilian." (p. 114)

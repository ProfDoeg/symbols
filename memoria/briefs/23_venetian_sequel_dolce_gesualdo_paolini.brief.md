Lodovico Dolce, *Dialogo ... del modo di accrescere et conservar la memoria* (Venice 1562); Filippo Gesualdo, O.F.M., *Plutosofia* (Padua 1592); Fabio Paolini, *Hebdomades* (Venice 1589), from the Accademia degli Uranici.

**Principle.** The Venetian afterlife of the Theatre, where old and new memory merge. Dolce (who prefaced Camillo's *Opere*) renders Romberch's whole apparatus, cosmic diagram, Grammar with alphabets, scholastic reasons for images, as elegant dialogue with a speaker Hortensio, and modernises it with Dante as a guide to remembering Hell and paintings by Titian (Europa, Adonis) as memory images. Gesualdo opens with Ficino's *De vita* and sees memory on three levels, the Ocean father of waters, the heaven with its lights, the divine image in the soul, moving among the three worlds as the Theatre does, before reverting to old-type material. Paolini's seven books of seven chapters transfer Ficino's planetary music to oratory: a proper mixture of forms (sounds, figures, Hermogenes' seven Ideas) draws celestial power into a speech, from which Yates infers a second secret of the Theatre, that the seven-based memory both stores and magically activates the orator's speeches, the Apollo image's proportions yielding the perfectly proportioned, hence magical, speech about the sun.

**Components.**
- Dolce's *Dialogo*: Romberch in Ciceronian Italian, with Titian as memory image `[2.32]`
- Gesualdo's *Plutosofia*: memory on three levels `[2.34]`
- Paolini's *Hebdomades*: planetary oratory `[2.35]`

**Yates.** Ch. VII, pp. 163–169; cf. Ch. V p. 115 n. 26; Ch. IV pp. 94–95 (Dolce's gloss); Ch. VIII p. 196 n. 40 (the "Seven" mystique).

**In Yates's words.**
- "And Romberch's diagrams are exactly reproduced; we see once again his cosmic diagram for Dantesque artificial memory, and the antiquated figure of Grammar, stuck over with visual alphabets." (p. 163)
- "Clearly Gesualdo's memory moves amongst the three worlds, in a manner similar to that shown in the lay-out of the Theatre." (p. 165)
- "the Theatre also magically activated the speeches which the orator remembered by it, infusing them with planetary virtue through which they would have magical effects on the hearers." (p. 168)

**Descends from / leads to.** From 12 (Dante), 14 (Petrarch in Gesualdo), 17 (Romberch), 21–22 (Camillo, Ficino). To 26 (Porta's paintings by Michelangelo, Raphael, Titian), 25 (Salzinger's sevens; Lull's *Tractatus de astronomia* swelling the "Seven" mystique), 39 (planetary oratory likened to the *Spaccio*).

THREADS TO PULL: the catalogue entry follows, built from Yates by four readers; every line of it is a lead to verify against the primary text and the scholarship:

Ambrogio Lorenzetti (Siena, 1337–40); Giotto (Arena Chapel, c. 1306); Romberch's places of Hell and Nardo di Cione's Hell fresco; Dante's *Divina Commedia* read "as a kind of memory system".

**Principle.** Yates proposes to look at Trecento didactic art "with the eyes of memory". Lorenzetti's Justice sits with secondary figures illustrating her parts "after the manner of a composite memory image", the tyrannical vices hovering like bats: an argument "set out in order and clothed in corporeal similitudes". Giotto's virtues and vices are dramatic, beautiful or grotesque imagines agentes, each standing out from a varied background as the place rules require, regularly placed as Thomist order allows, framing the Last Judgment as Boncompagno enjoined. Hell divided into many inscribed places, each punishment fitting its sin, makes "the places of Hell ... variegated memory loci" with the damned as striking images (Romberch's text, Dolce's gloss, Nardo di Cione's compartments). Dante's poem then becomes "a summa of similitudes and exempla, ranged in order and set out upon the universe": orders of places in Hell, Purgatory and Paradise, striking images on them, the three parts of Prudence as its structure, Memory as "the converting power, the bridge between the abstraction and the image". Yates leaves the Dante reading "as a shock".

**Components.**
- Lorenzetti's Good and Bad Government read as memory imagery `[1.27]`
- Giotto's virtues and vices (Arena Chapel) as imagines agentes on variegated loci `[1.28]`
- Hell and Paradise as memory places (Romberch's places of Hell; Nardo di Cione) `[1.29]`
- Dante's *Divine Comedy* as a memory system `[1.30]`

**Yates.** Ch. IV, pp. 92–96; Preface p. xii; anticipated pp. 59–60; Pl. 2 (Lorenzetti, Justice and Peace), Pl. 3a–b (Giotto, Charity and Envy), Pl. 8a (Nardo di Cione, The Places of Hell).

**In Yates's words.**
- "the illusion of depth depends on the intense care with which the images have been placed on their backgrounds, or, speaking mnemonically, on their loci." (p. 93)
- "This introduces the novel idea that the places of Hell, varied in accordance with the nature of the sins punished in them, could be regarded as variegated memory loci." (p. 94)
- "That Dante's Inferno could be regarded as a kind of memory system for memorising Hell and its punishments with striking images on orders of places, will come as a great shock, and I must leave it as a shock." (p. 95)

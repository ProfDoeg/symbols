THREADS TO PULL: the catalogue entry follows, built from Yates by four readers; every line of it is a lead to verify against the primary text and the scholarship:

Bruno, *Sigillus sigillorum*, the fourth item of the 1583 London volume, said to form part of the *Clavis magna*; with the Seals that carry the theory of the imagination.

**Principle.** Zeuxis the Painter is the principle "use images" and ties the Seals to *Shadows*: "the images of Teucer the Babylonian supply me with the indications of three hundred thousand propositions", and Bruno "knows a double picture", images and notae formed "from strange descriptions" (the astral images) and "edifices" feigned as need requires with sensible images for non-sensible things (normal classical memory, galvanised by affiliation to astral systems); painter, poet and philosopher are made one, "to understand is to speculate with images". Phidias the Sculptor moulds memory statues within, in wax, by addition of small stones, or "sculpturing the rough and formless stone as though by subtraction", releasing forms from the "inform chaos of memory". The Husbandman cultivates the Field with the Hermetic sentence that as the world is the image of God, man is the image of the world, so an art reproducing the divine organisation in memory taps the powers of the cosmos. The Fountain and the Mirror is the unifying stage, "one knowledge in one subject". The Seal of Seals is not a place-and-image system but the visionary apotheosis the Thirty lead up to (the reverse of *Shadows*): Simonides made a mystagogue leading from the celestial to the supercelestial; cognition as one imaginative process in four grades with the doors open between them, against the treatises' faculty psychology (Romberch's diagram of the head); vital images charged with affects, above all Love, unifying memory; a fifth grade of fifteen "contractions"; Thomas ranked with Zoroaster and Paul; solitude; four guides, Love, Art, Mathesis, Magic, leading to Light. "The art of memory has become ... a magico-religious technique."

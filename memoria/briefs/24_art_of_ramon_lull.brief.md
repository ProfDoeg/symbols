Ramon Lull (c. 1235–1316); the Art in its versions from c. 1272 to the *Ars generalis ultima* and *Ars brevis* (1305–8); *Liber de ascensu et descensu intellectus*; *Arbor scientiae*; *Tractatus de astronomia*.

**Principle.** An art founded on the Dignitates Dei (Bonitas, Magnitudo, Eternitas, Potestas, Sapientia, Voluntas, Virtus, Veritas, Gloria = B to K) as primordial causes issuing into creation, used by intellect to find truth, by will to love it, by memory to remember it. No images: concepts are carried by a letter notation on geometrical figures, some of which revolve. The A figure sets B to K on a wheel joined by triangulations for meditating the Names in the Godhead; the T figure holds the relata (differentia, concordia, contrarietas; principium, medium, finis; majoritas, equalitas, minoritas) as triangles in a circle; the combinatory figure, a stationary outer circle with concentric revolving inner circles, reads off combinations of the letters, the *ars combinatoria* in its simplest form, derived from cosmological *rotae*. The artista carries the figures up and down the ladder of the nine subjects (Deus, Angelus, Coelum, Homo, Imaginativa, Sensitiva, Vegetativa, Elementativa, Instrumentativa), measuring the same proportions on each level; square is the elements, circle the heavens, triangle the divinity, and the logical square of opposition is identified with the square of the elements. The *Arbor scientiae* schematises the encyclopaedia as trees rooted in B to K, "a kind of place system"; on the level *coelum* the Art manipulates the signs and planets with B to K in a benevolent, wholly imageless astral science and medicine. Contrasted point by point with the classical art; associated with the Franciscans as the classical art with the Dominicans; a missionary Art meant to demonstrate the Trinity to Jews and Muslims.

**In Yates's words.**
- "As intellectus, it was an art of knowing or finding out truth; as voluntas it was an art of training the will towards loving truth; as memoria, it was an art of memory for remembering truth." (p. 174)
- "Lull introduces movement into memory. The figures of his Art, on which its concepts are set out in the letter notation, are not static but revolving." (p. 176)
- "The outer circle, inscribed B to K, is stationary and within it revolve circles similarly inscribed and concentric with it. As the circles revolve, combinations of the letters B to K can be read off." (p. 182)
- "The square is the elements; the circle, the heavens; and the triangle, the divinity." (p. 182)

THREADS TO PULL: the catalogue entry follows, built from Yates by four readers; every line of it is a lead to verify against the primary text and the scholarship:

Lull's own doctrine of the Art as memoria and his *Liber ad memoriam confirmandam* (Pisa, c. 1308); Lavinheta (1523) and Salzinger (1721–42) as its readers; the Cabalist and alchemical Lull of the Renaissance and Yates's "grand Renaissance aim".

**Principle.** Since the three powers of the soul are one, the Art as memory consists in memorising the whole Art, its principles, terminology and operations; the diagrams are in some sense places, and memory "is becoming a method of investigation", quite unlike the classical art that "seeks only to memorise what is given"; no striking images. The *Liber ad memoriam confirmandam* divides memory into natural (received at generation, according to the reigning planet) and artificial, the latter either medicines and plasters or frequent going-over "like an ox chewing the cud"; the Tullian places and images are pointedly omitted, and three times the reader is sent to the "Book of the Seven Planets" for the key. Lavinheta assigns the classical places and images to *sensibilia* and the *Ars generalis* to *intelligibilia*, making the classical art the inferior discipline; Salzinger identifies the Book of the Seven Planets with the *Tractatus de astronomia* and reads memory as retained through the seven planets as "seven instruments", so that the sixteenth century could find in Lull a memory based on the celestial seven, without images. Meanwhile Pico declared the Cabalist *ars combinandi* with revolving Hebrew alphabets (Abulafia) like the *ars Raymundi*; the Pseudo-Lullian *De auditu kabbalistico* identified B to K with the Sephiroth; Pseudo-Lullian alchemical figures set combinatory wheels at the root and planetary wheels at the top of a Lullian tree, making Lull a Magus. The "grand Renaissance aim" was to set magic images of the stars on the revolving Lullian figures: Camillo's Theatre only anticipates it, "static in a building"; Bruno achieves it.

**In Yates's words.**
- "the Lullian Art as memory consists in memorising the Art as a whole, in all its aspects and operations." (p. 184)
- "The other kind consists in frequently going over in memory what one wishes to retain, like an ox chewing the cud." (p. 192) — "But he deliberately leaves out the 'Tullian' rules." (p. 192)

- "it was to be a grand Renaissance aim to bring together Lullism and the classical art of memory by using magic images of the stars on the Lullian figures." (p. 197)

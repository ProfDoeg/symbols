THREADS TO PULL: the catalogue entry follows, built from Yates by four readers; every line of it is a lead to verify against the primary text and the scholarship:

Albertus Magnus, Dominican (entered 1223); *De bono*, tract. IV (Prudence), q. 2; commentary on *De memoria et reminiscentia*.

**Principle.** Albertus examines "the ars memorandi which Tullius gives in the Second Rhetoric" under memory as a part of Prudence and is "the first to make this conflation" of Aristotle with Tullius: artificial memory belongs to reminiscence, in the rational soul. Places should be real buildings rather than an imaginary camp or city (Yates infers a church; "solemn and rare" places move most), and the five place rules are confirmed. The memory image contains its *intentio* (an image of Justice contains the intention to seek it); *metaphorica* are preferred to *propria* because "the wonderful moves the memory more"; the imagines agentes are quoted in full into a treatise on ethics. In the commentary the rule for dark and solitary places becomes a mystical retirement in which a ram with huge horns and testicles charges out of the darkness, and reminiscence belongs to the hot, inspired melancholy of the *Problemata*. The artificial memory pertains "to the moral man and to the speaker", the warrant for its later lay use.

**Components.**
- Albertus Magnus's ars memorandi (*De bono* and the *De memoria* commentary) `[1.21]`

**Yates.** Ch. III, pp. 61–70; recalled pp. 90, 97–99, 104; Ch. IX pp. 204–205 (the "huge ram in the night skies" against Thomas's caution, and a possible Albertist development in Bruno's Naples convent).

**In Yates's words.**
- "So far as I know, Albertus was the first to make this conflation." (p. 63)
- "they stroll mysteriously, like players, out of antiquity into the scholastic treatise on memory as a part of Prudence." (p. 66)
- (Albertus) "we should imagine some ram, with huge horns and testicles, coming towards us in the darkness." (p. 68) — "This ram gives one rather a fright!" (p. 68)

**Descends from / leads to.** From 2, 5 (Aristotle), 8 (Bologna). To 10 (Thomas continues the conflation "more explicitly" without the "superstitious awe"), 11 (the licence for lay use), 13 (licence for pagan fables as memory images, used by the English friars), 14 (Petrarch's pagan images), 26 (Bruno's Dominican formation).

**Dossier note.** *De bono*, ed. Cologne XXVIII (1951), pp. 245–52 (two articuli, twenty-one points); commentary ed. Borgnet IX, pp. 97 ff., esp. 108, 117. Albertus reads a corrupt text of the lawsuit image (the *medico* holding the testicles, p. 65).

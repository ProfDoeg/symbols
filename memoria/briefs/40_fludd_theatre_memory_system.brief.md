Robert Fludd, *Utriusque Cosmi Maioris scilicet et Minoris Historia*, Tomus Secundus (De Microcosmi Historia), sectio 2, *Ars memoriae*, Oppenheim, De Bry, 1619; with John Willis, *Mnemonica; sive Ars reminiscendi* (London 1618; English 1621, 1661) as possible immediate source.

**Principle.** Fludd divides the art into the *ars rotunda*, the "natural" and superior art whose fantasy operates "through ideas", spirits, shadows, souls, angels, demons, effigies of the stars, images of gods and virtues and vices "made into shadows", with the ethereal heavens as its common place, requiring daemonic or Holy-Spirit assistance; and the *ars quadrata*, the "artificial" art of corporeal, active images (the imagines agentes) in buildings or rooms, which must be real places, never the fictitious ones *Ad Herennium* allowed. The Theatre system combines them: the great common place is the eighth sphere or zodiac enclosing the seven planetary circles and the elements, into which are set "theatres" (stages, not playhouses), two flanking Aries on the diagram, an eastern theatre light and bright for the day and a western dark and obscure for the night, presumably a pair for each sign, twenty-four in all, so that their images are "astrally activated". Within each theatre five doors, about equidistant, and five feigned columns opposite them of distinct shapes (round, square, hexagon, square, round) and colours (white, red, green, blue, black) give ten places for things and words; two subsidiary theatres, rooms with one wall left out, of different materials, connect through their battlements. The celestial images are the "principal ideas", the planets through the signs (Saturn imaged differently in each sign, the same for the other planets); the "less principal images" in the theatres come in sets of five (Jason, Medea, Paris, Daphne, Phoebus; Medea collecting herbs on the white door, killing her brother on the red), with alphabetical lists, visual alphabets and number-images "in continuity with the old memory tradition". An introductory picture shows the "eye of imagination" beside five loci with an obelisk, Babel, Tobias and the Angel, a ship and the Last Judgment; a diagram of the three worlds shows sense impressions becoming *umbrae* in a *mundus imaginabilis* reflecting the zodiac, then the *mens* with the hierarchies and the Trinity, then the seat of memory receiving all three worlds. Willis's *Mnemonica* supplies the humble original: sets of identical one-level "theatres" or "repositories", divided by a column into two rooms, imagined in different colours which every image must share (a bushel with handles of pure gold in the golden theatre), a straight mnemotechnic "inner shopping list", with day things deposited before sleep and night things after.

**In Yates's words.**
- "The 'round art', then, uses magicised or talismanic images, effigies of the stars; 'statues' of gods and goddesses animated with celestial influences; images of virtues and vices, as in the old mediaeval art, but now thought of as containing 'demonic' or magical power." (p. 327)
- "The heavens work together with the theatres, and the theatres are in the heavens. The 'round' and the 'square' art are united to form a memory 'Seal', or an occult memory system of extreme complexity." (p. 333)
- "Is it pure madness to place twenty-four memory theatres in the zodiac? Or is it a madness potentially leading to method? Or is such a system the Seal or secret code of a Hermetic sect or society?" (p. 341)
- "Possibly this is what Fludd did to Willis's sets of what he calls 'theatres' as memory rooms; he occultised them into magical activity by affiliating them to the zodiac." (p. 338)

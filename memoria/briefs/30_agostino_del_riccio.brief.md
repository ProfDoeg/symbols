Fra Agostino del Riccio, Dominican of Santa Maria Novella, Florence, 1595; unpublished manuscript, Biblioteca Nazionale, Florence, II, I, 13.

**Principle.** The principles and techniques of the classical art are each presented as a little symbolic picture with a title, "for the use of studious young gentlemen": The King striking his brow is local memory itself; The First Counsellor touching a globe gives the place rules, with a worked example round the church of Santa Maria Novella (Charity on the high altar, Hope on the Ciodi altar, Faith on the Gaddi altar, round the chapels and tombs back to the start); The Second Counsellor, surrounded by objects and a bust, is "use images" (real, imaginary, or the statues in Niccolò Gaddi's gallery), followed by alphabetical lists; The Third Captain memorises twelve objects along the Via della Scala and in a Dominican cell divided into places; The Meal and the Servant teaches memory in small doses. The First Captain or the Straight Line places the twelve signs of the zodiac on the parts of the body they rule, used as a memory order "in a rational way"; The Second Captain or the Circular Line remembers the four elements and eleven heavens on a man's body in a circle (earth at the feet, Luna on the right hand, Sol on the head, Paradise under the left foot), "puerile, but not magical". Yates introduces it as the exact model for the mode of presentation of Bruno's Thirty Seals, and as the "good old fashioned" Dominican devotional art.

**Components.**
- Agostino del Riccio, *Arte della memoria locale* (the seven pictures) `[3.3]`
- Riccio, The First Captain or the Straight Line (zodiac on the body) `[3.4]`
- Riccio, The Second Captain or the Circular Line (elements and heavens on the body) `[3.5]`

**Yates.** Ch. XI, pp. 244–246; recalled Ch. XIV p. 313; Pl. 13a (The King), 13b (First Counsellor), 13c (Second Counsellor), 13d (Second Captain), 13f (The Meal and the Servant).

**In Yates's words.**
- "beginning from the high altar, you may place there Charity; then continuing round the church you will perhaps place on the altar of the Ciodi, Hope; on the altar of the Gaddi, Faith" (p. 244)
- "Local memory is like eating and drinking. If we ate all our food at once we should have indigestion, so we divide it into separate meals." (p. 245)
- "his idea of presenting the principles and various techniques of the art through little symbolic pictures, with titles, exactly corresponds to what Bruno does in Seals" (p. 246)

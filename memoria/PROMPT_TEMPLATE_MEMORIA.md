# The memory-system dossier prompt (the systems in Yates's Art of Memory)

Anthony, 2026-09-14: "make a catalog of all the mnemonic systems that are presented by Yates in
her treatise and then we can make a dossier for each." The set is the 45 systems of
memoria/CATALOG.md. `[NAME]` is the only substitution point; the per-system brief follows.

---

[NAME]
Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this system of artificial
memory, one of the systems Frances Yates presents in The Art of Memory (1966). Yates is the
point of departure, not the boundary: go to the primary texts and to the scholarship since
her. I want the fullest reconstructable account, chronologically and in detail. Include,
wherever available:

- THE SYSTEM ITSELF: who devised or transmitted it, when, in which text (edition, book,
  chapter, page); what serves as places and what as images; how order is fixed and how
  retrieval works; the rules given for places and for images; worked examples the source
  gives (the lawsuit image of the Ad Herennium, Camillo's grades, Bruno's wheels), quoted;
  the diagrams, wheels, tables and plates, described exactly; its variants across
  manuscripts and editions
- THE SOURCES: the primary text in the original language (Latin, Greek, Italian, Catalan,
  French) with the standard editions and translations; the manuscripts and early printings
  with shelfmarks and dates; the surviving diagrams
- YATES'S ACCOUNT: what she says (chapter and pages), her reading, and where later
  scholarship has confirmed, corrected or overturned her (Carruthers, Bolzoni, Rossi,
  Small, Hajdu, Sabbatino, Yates's own later revisions; the Lull scholarship of Bonner and
  Fidora; the Bruno scholarship of Sturlese, Ciliberto, Clucas, Gatti; the Camillo
  scholarship of Bolzoni and Stagno; the Ramus scholarship of Ong)
- HISTORY AND CONTEXT: the intellectual tradition it belongs to (rhetoric, scholastic
  psychology, Lullism, Hermeticism, Ramism, the universal-language project); the institutions
  (the Dominican and Franciscan schools, the courts, the universities); what it descends from
  and what descends from it, and the reception across the centuries
- PEOPLE: the practitioners, patrons, students, critics and enemies; anecdotes of use (the
  auctioneer, the professor's party, the student who lost his memory, the accused
  magician)
- THE MAGIC QUESTION: whether the system was thought magical, talismanic or heretical, by
  whom, and with what consequences (the accusations, the Inquisition, the Ars notoria)
- AFTERLIFE: the system's echoes in later memory arts, in literature and art (Dante, the
  frescoes, the emblem books, Borges's Funes, Eco, the memory-palace revival, memory
  athletes), in computing and combinatorics (Lull to Leibniz to the machine)
- CONTROVERSIES: contested attributions, datings, readings; what Yates got wrong; what is
  legend rather than documented

Include the good and the bad without judgment, and legend as fully as fact.
For every significant claim distinguish documented text, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, Yates's interpretation, disputed claim, legend and modern invention, and say which
sources carry it, when the reading first appears, who spread it, and what evidence exists.
Do not use fact-checking summaries as substitutes for the sources; trace to the texts
(archive.org and Gallica for the early printings, the Loeb editions for the Latin sources,
the Lull database at the Universitat de Barcelona, the Bruno editions online).

Structure the dossier as: Basic identification (name, author, date, source text, principle in
one sentence, Yates's pages); The system in detail; The sources; Yates's account and the
scholarship since; History and context; People; The magic question; Afterlife; Controversies
and disputes; Open questions; Sources (full list of URLs and editions consulted).
Length: as long as the material requires; for a major system (the Ad Herennium, Camillo,
Lull, Bruno's Shadows or Seals, Fludd) a thousand lines is not too many.

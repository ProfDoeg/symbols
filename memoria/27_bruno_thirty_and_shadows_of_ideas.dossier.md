# The Thirty and the shadows of ideas: Research Dossier

## Basic identification

| Field | Identification |
|---|---|
| Catalogue designation | System 27 of 45: “The Thirty and the shadows of ideas” |
| Historical name | *De umbris idearum* and its appended *Ars memoriae* |
| Exponent | Giordano Bruno, formerly Filippo Bruno of Nola (1548–1600) |
| Date | Paris, 1582 |
| First printing | *Iordanus Brunus Nolanus, De umbris idearum … Ars memoriae Iordani Bruni*, Paris: apud Aegidium [Gilles] Gorbinum, 1582 |
| Companion work | *Philothei Iordani Bruni Nolani De compendiosa architectura et complemento artis Lullii*, Paris: Gilles Gorbin, 1582 |
| Principle in one sentence | A thirty-character alphabet and nested combinatorial image-series turn words and concepts into ordered, vivid scenes, while Bruno’s “shadows of ideas” theory explains why sensible images can mediate between the human mind and intelligible reality. |
| Yates | Frances A. Yates, *The Art of Memory* (1966), ch. IX, especially pp. 208–211, 215–217, 222–230; Fig. 8 |

### Evidentiary verdict in brief

- **Documented text:** Bruno gives a technically functional system for encoding letters, syllables, words and propositions through ordered agents, actions, attributes, accompanying objects and circumstances.
- **Documented text:** the operative alphabet contains thirty signs, each subdivided by five vowels, producing 150 primary positions.
- **Documented text:** Bruno embeds this technique in a Neoplatonic account of human knowledge as knowledge through *umbrae*, “shadows.”
- **Documented text:** *De compendiosa architectura* expands Lull’s principles to thirty and compares them with the ten Sephiroth.
- **Yates’s interpretation:** the system’s astral images are inner talismans, and its wheels are “conjuring wheels.”
- **Later correction:** Rita Sturlese showed that Yates’s celebrated Fig. 8 suppresses the syllabic values essential to the mechanism and misconstrues the relation between the several image lists. The wheels have a demonstrable cryptographic-mnemonic function.
- **Continuing dispute:** correcting Yates does not make Bruno’s system religiously or metaphysically neutral. Its operative mnemonics, ontology of images, natural magic and aspiration to intellectual transformation remain connected—but “connected” is not the same as “a machine for conjuring spirits.”
- **Not documented:** no surviving report demonstrates that rotating the *De umbris* wheels summoned an aerial spirit.
- **Not documented:** the title “The Thirty and the shadows of ideas” is not Bruno’s name for one discrete apparatus. It is a modern catalogue label combining several related uses of thirty.
- **Not documented:** the number thirty in *De umbris* is not simply the thirty Lullian dignities “Cabalised.” Several thirties coexist: alphabetic signs, intentions, concepts, image classes and, in later works, seals and statues.

## The system in detail

## 1. The book is two linked works, not one homogeneous tract

The 1582 volume consists of:

1. *De umbris idearum*, with prefatory poems and the dialogue among Hermes, Philothymus and Logifer;
2. *Triginta intentiones umbrarum*, thirty “intentions” or determinations concerning shadows;
3. *De triginta idearum conceptibus*, thirty concepts concerning ideas;
4. the separately headed *Ars memoriae*, which supplies definitions, rules, places, images and combinatorial practices.

The Folger copy is bibliographically described as an octavo of `[40], 80 leaves`, with collation `*4, ã8, ẽ8, ĩ8, ð8, ũ4, a–k8`; the running title from `a1v` to `k8v` is *Ars memoriae*. Its call number is **BF383 .B7 Cage**. [Folger catalogue](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/370428)

The Morgan describes its copy as *De umbris idearum – Ars memoriae*, Paris, Gilles Gorbin, 1582, and records the prefatory portion as `*⁴, a⁸, e⁸, i⁸, o⁸, u⁴`, complete. [Morgan Library](https://www.themorgan.org/printed-books/164125)

**Correction to a common shorthand:** *De umbris idearum* is not merely the philosophical introduction to a freestanding, unrelated memory manual. The two parts were printed as a designed sequence: the theory explains why imagery is cognitively legitimate, while the *Ars* tells the reader how images are constructed and manipulated.

## 2. The “shadow” as epistemic medium

### Primary text

The first intention begins:

> *Sub umbra illius quem desideraveram sedi.*

“Under the shadow of him whom I desired, I sat.”

Bruno borrows Song of Songs 2:3 and continues:

> *Sufficiens ergo est illi atque multum: ut sub umbra boni, verique sedeat.*

“It is therefore enough—and much—for [the human mind] to sit beneath the shadow of the good and the true.”

He distinguishes the metaphysical shadow from simple error. Human nature cannot inhabit the field of truth immediately. It receives truth through attenuated representations because the embodied soul is limited by bodily opacity. [Latin text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/bruno/umbris.htm); [Op. lat. II.1 scan](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/mnemosyne/Bruno/pdf/oplatII_I.pdf)

In the fourteenth intention Bruno writes, in substance, that the shadow is not truth but proceeds from truth and leads toward it; therefore it is not simply falsehood but a hiding-place of truth.

### Structure of mediation

The recurrent hierarchy is:

```text
One / divine truth / intelligible Ideas
                  ↓
         ideal or intelligible forms
                  ↓
       celestial or natural images
                  ↓
     sensible forms and phantasms
                  ↓
human imagination, reason and memory
```

The relation is not merely a Platonic opposition between reality and copy. The shadow both conceals and conveys. It is weaker than its source yet retains a proportion to it.

**Documented text:** Bruno says that human cognition possesses shadows rather than Ideas themselves:

> *Nos autem oportet eas extra, et supra nos inquirere: cum umbras earum tantum in nobis habeamus.*

“We must seek [the Ideas] outside and above ourselves, since we have only their shadows within us.”

**Scholarly reconstruction:** the memory art works because sensible images occupy this mediating stratum. The mnemonic image is not an arbitrary mark alone; it may preserve formal relations among the things represented.

**Caution:** this does not prove that every practical mnemonic image transmits an astral force. That stronger proposition is Yates’s synthesis.

## 3. The thirty intentions and thirty concepts

### The intentions

The *Triginta intentiones umbrarum* consist of thirty alphabetically marked propositions. They are not thirty “memory places” in the ordinary rhetorical sense. They guide the reader’s understanding of the relation between light, shadow, intelligibility, multiplicity and unity.

The first explicitly treats the human mind as sitting beneath the shadow of metaphysical truth. Later intentions distinguish types and degrees of shadow and describe ascent from sensible multiplicity toward intelligible unity.

The term *intentio* bears several medieval meanings: direction of attention, an act of will, and a concept or “intention” apprehended by a cognitive faculty. Yates associates it with the voluntative element of Lull’s Figure S. That is plausible as intellectual genealogy but not an identity asserted by Bruno.

### The concepts

Bruno introduces the next series:

> *Iam ad triginta idearum conceptus, primo simpliciter; secundo cum intentionibus umbrarum complexe concipiendis, consequenter progrediamur.*

“Let us now proceed to the thirty concepts of Ideas: first to be conceived simply, and then in combination with the intentions of shadows.”

The first concept begins:

> *Luciferos, inquit Plotinus, in facie Deus oculos fabricavit…*

“God, Plotinus says, fashioned light-bearing eyes in the face…”

The second argues that the world cannot have multiple first principles and therefore has one order in which members are connected and subordinated. Forms extend from what most fully possesses being toward matter and near-nonbeing. [Critical-edition scan](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/mnemosyne/Bruno/pdf/oplatII_I.pdf)

Thus the two sets form a combinable philosophical matrix:

- thirty ways in which the mind is directed toward or apprehends shadows;
- thirty propositions about Ideas and universal order;
- each item indexed by the same extended alphabet.

**Documented:** Bruno instructs that the concepts be grasped first singly and then with the intentions.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** this yields up to 900 paired meditations before further expansion.

**Unproved:** that Bruno intended readers physically to place all sixty propositions on the same paper volvelle as the practical mnemonic images.

## 4. The alphabet of thirty signs

Bruno needs more than the ordinary Latin alphabet because his mechanism requires thirty principal “standards,” *vexilla*. The exact graphic repertoire varies with typography and editorial normalization, but it consists of Latin signs supplemented by Greek and Hebrew characters. Each principal sign is paired with:

> A, E, I, O, U

giving:

> AA, AE, AI, AO, AU; BA, BE, BI, BO, BU; and so onward.

Thirty principal signs multiplied by five vowels produce 150 addresses.

This serves two distinct levels:

1. **elementary practice:** thirty agents, thirty actions and thirty attributes encode individual alphabetic elements;
2. **greater or second practice:** five lists of 150 images encode syllables, word-components and grammatical roles.

The modern claim that Bruno simply invented a “thirty-letter alphabet” is therefore incomplete. It is an address system: a thirty-fold principal series, each with five subordinate values.

## 5. What serves as places

Bruno distinguishes *subiecta*—supporting subjects or places—from *adiecta*—images or forms applied to them.

The “places” may be:

- conventional architectural loci;
- atria, chambers and workshops;
- ordered bodily parts;
- zodiacal and planetary divisions;
- letters and syllabic addresses;
- cells on a conceptual wheel;
- a stable array of imagined positions.

In the later *Explicatio triginta sigillorum*, the twenty-first seal, the Potter’s Wheel, describes a circular atrium with twelve, twenty-four or thirty workshops: fowler, physician, soldier, cupbearer, musician, scribe, farmer, shoemaker, priest, fuller, barber and reader. These are not literal paper-wheel compartments only; they are imagined occupational stations through which an agent can pass. [Latin text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/bruno/trigint.htm)

**Important correction:** Yates says that Lullian “architecture” replaces the classical memory building. Bruno actually generalizes the place-function. Buildings remain available, but alphabets, wheels, bodies, occupational arrays and cosmological sequences can all behave as ordered supports.

## 6. What serves as images

Bruno’s fundamental image-components are:

1. **agentes** — acting persons;
2. **actiones** — actions;
3. **insignia** — attributes, marks, clothing or identifying signs;
4. **adstantia** — accompanying objects, persons or animals;
5. **circumstantiae** — circumstances, surroundings or determinations.

A complete mnemonic scene can therefore encode five units at once:

```text
agent + action + attribute + accompanying object/person + circumstance
```

This is recognizably ancestral to modern “person–action–object” coding, but the modern PAO label is an analogy, not Bruno’s term.

### Rules for images

Bruno’s rules descend from the classical demand that images be perceptible and striking, but he develops a much more systematic grammar.

Images should be:

- known to the practitioner;
- distinct from one another;
- capable of rapid recognition;
- active rather than inert;
- differentiated by gesture, clothing, tool, posture or setting;
- assigned permanently enough that their values become habitual;
- proportioned to the thing being encoded;
- capable of combination without losing their identifying function.

The user may replace Bruno’s suggested names with personally familiar ones. In the second practice he writes:

> *Tu ergo ipse ordinabis tibi centum et quinquaginta nomina…*

“You yourself, therefore, will arrange for yourself one hundred and fifty names…”

This is crucial. The lists are exemplars and templates, not an immutable pantheon.

## 7. First practice: thirty mythological agents

The elementary practice assigns one agent, one action and one attribute to each principal sign. The beginning runs approximately:

| Sign | Agent | Action | Attribute |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Lycaon | at a banquet | chained |
| B | Deucalion | into stones / casting stones | filleted or banded |
| C | Apollo | against Python | belted |
| … | … | … | … |
| P | Plato | at a banquet | designated P-attribute |
| R | Neptune | piercing Callisto | designated R-attribute |
| S | Pallas | with the child Bacchus | designated S-attribute |
| V | Medea | carrying off Proserpina | designated V-attribute |
| final sign | Glaucus | final assigned action | final assigned attribute |

The wording and some mythological names are unstable in the printing and later transcriptions. “Glaucus/Glaucius,” for example, is easily corrupted.

### How rotation works

Bruno’s explicit example is:

> *Lycaon in convivium … elementum AA.*

“Lycaon at the banquet” represents AA: the A-agent combined with the A-action.

After the inner wheel moves beneath the outer:

> *Iam non erit tibi AA Lycaon in convivium, sed Lycaon in lapidea convertendos.*

“Now you will no longer have AA, Lycaon at the banquet, but Lycaon [turning men] into stones.”

The outer wheel preserves the agent; the rotating inner wheel supplies a different action. Further examples are:

> *Deucalion in Pythonem exanimandum; Apollo in bovem custodiendum…*

“Deucalion killing Python; Apollo guarding the ox…”

For requested combinations Bruno says:

> *Vis combinationem PA referre? pone A rotae internae sub P rotae externae; praesentabunt tibi per Platonem in convivium.*

“Do you wish to represent PA? Put A of the inner wheel beneath P of the outer: they will present Plato at a banquet.”

He continues:

- RE: Neptune piercing Callisto;
- SI: Pallas with the child Bacchus;
- VO: Medea carrying off Proserpina.

[Complete *Ars memoriae* transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/bruno/arsmemor.htm)

These examples decisively establish that at least this layer is an encoding device. The absurd mythological recombinations are mnemonic output, not descriptions of rites.

### Three-, four- and five-letter forms

Adding the third wheel supplies an *insigne*. Additional fields may encode a fourth and fifth element. Bruno gives such examples as:

- *PLEBS*
- *TRANSACTUM*
- *STUPRANS*
- *SCROBS*, for which the more pronounceable *SCROPS* may be substituted through phonetic affinity.

This demonstrates tolerance for approximation. The system encodes sound or written form through memorable combinations rather than providing a mathematically exact universal script.

## 8. Second practice: five series of 150

The greater practice expands every principal sign through the five vowels. Bruno orders five fixed wheels or equivalent arrays, each containing 150 double-letter combinations:

1. inventors or agents;
2. actions;
3. attributes;
4. accompanying objects;
5. circumstances.

He writes:

> *Conficies igitur ad similitudinem trium praedictarum rotarum quinque rotas fixas, quarum singulae centum et quinquaginta constent combinationibus elementorum duorum.*

“You will therefore construct, after the likeness of the three aforesaid wheels, five fixed wheels, each consisting of 150 combinations of two elements.”

The next sentence assigns the categories:

> *Exterior atque prima significet agentes … Secunda actiones. Tertia insignia. Quarta adstantia. Quinta circumstantias.*

“The outer and first signifies agents; the second actions; the third attributes; the fourth accompanying things; the fifth circumstances.”

Because five literal wheels of this density would be unwieldy, Bruno prints only a contracted model:

> *Quoniam difficile est quinque rotas in modicam contrahere latitudinem, unam tantum apponimus…*

“Since it is difficult to compress five wheels into a small width, we print only one…”

This is the textual basis for reconstructing five aligned arrays—but it is also why no printed plate in the first edition corresponds exactly to Yates’s monumental composite.

### Opening entries

The first 30 entries of an agent/action list include:

- AA — Rhegima — chestnut bread;
- AE — Osiris — agriculture;
- AI — Ceres — teams of oxen;
- AO — Triptolemus — sowing;
- AU — Pitumnus — manuring;
- BA — Erichthonius — the chariot;
- BE — Glaucius — stretching iron;
- BI — the Thracians — the sickle;
- BV — Pyrodes — striking fire from flint;
- DA — Noah — ordering a vineyard;
- EA — Aristaeus — the craft of honey;
- EE — Nimrod — hunter.

The historical and mythographic lists function as a storehouse of “inventors of arts.” Accuracy as cultural history is secondary to distinctiveness and stable association.

## 9. The astral list

Near the end Bruno supplies 150 celestial images assembled from:

- 36 zodiacal decans;
- seven sets of planetary images;
- lunar mansions;
- the lunar nodes or dragon;
- astrological houses and related celestial divisions.

He attributes part of the decanic material to “Teucer the Babylonian,” a traditional astrological authority whose image lists circulated through medieval and Renaissance compilations.

### Yates’s placement

Yates put these 150 astral images on the innermost wheel of her reconstruction and called it an astral “power station.”

### Later correction

Sturlese’s reconstruction treats the operative five lists as parallel linguistic-image registers. Yates’s central astral wheel:

- lacks the syllabic labels essential to encoding;
- combines material Bruno does not unambiguously tell the reader to mount together;
- visually promotes the astral series into the engine of the whole apparatus.

The Warburg Institute now summarizes the position carefully: Yates’s reconstruction is not wholly accurate, and the “inner talisman” account is no longer tenable, though her drawing remains a useful visualization. [Warburg: Yates and Bruno’s Mnemonics](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/research-fellowships/research-projects/archived-research-projects/giordano-bruno/yates-brunos-mnemonics)

## 10. Order and retrieval

The complete practical cycle is:

1. Memorize a fixed ordered alphabet of thirty signs.
2. Give each sign five vocalic subdivisions.
3. Associate every address with a stable agent, action, attribute, object or circumstance.
4. Convert the material to be remembered into letters, syllables or conceptual units.
5. Align the corresponding image-fields.
6. Mentally compose the aligned fields into an active scene.
7. Place that scene in an ordered locus, atrium, body, wheel-position or other *subiectum*.
8. Retrieve by traversing the places in order.
9. Read each scene back through its fixed image-values.
10. Reconstruct the word, sentence or conceptual sequence.

The movements may be material, diagrammatic or performed in trained imagination. Bruno says:

> *Nos similitudine quadam formamus eas, quae consistunt in revolutione rotarum.*

“We form them by a certain likeness consisting in the revolution of wheels.”

Yates translates the challenge following this approximately as:

> “If you know another way, try it.”

The purpose is not merely recall in the modern experimental sense. Bruno calls the art a:

> *discursiva architectura*

a “discursive architecture,” extending through the life or animating principle of things.

## 2. *De compendiosa architectura et complemento artis Lullii*

Paris: Gilles Gorbin, 1582. It is Bruno’s exposition and transformation of Lull’s Art, not simply an appendix to *De umbris*.

A crucial passage reads:

> *Quae omnia Iudaei Cabalistae ad decem Sephiroth, et nos ad triginta … redigimus indumenta.*

“All these the Jewish Cabalists reduce to ten Sephiroth, and we reduce to thirty coverings—not adding to them, but unfolding the same things.”

Bruno then says that the Hebrews venerate the name proper to the divine substance, the tetragrammaton *Iehova*, unfolded through four, twelve and indefinitely larger numerical structures. [Op. lat. II.2, p. 42 scan](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/mnemosyne/Bruno/pdf/oplatII_II.pdf)

The immediately preceding passage attacks attempts to demonstrate distinctively Christian mysteries:

> *contra omne ratiocinium, philosophiam… solis Christicolis sunt revelata*

“contrary to every rational account and philosophy … revealed only to the Christ-worshippers.”

Bruno explicitly says that such matters admit faith, not demonstration, whatever Lull attempted “in his delirium” against great theologians.

**Therefore:** Yates is right that Bruno departs sharply from Lull’s Trinitarian apologetic. She is not justified in treating this passage alone as proof that every group of thirty in *De umbris* is a set of Cabalised dignities.

### Modern critical edition

Marco Matteoli, Rita Sturlese and Nicoletta Tirinnanzi, eds., *Opere lulliane*, Milan: Adelphi, 2012.

## 3. The lost *Clavis magna*

Bruno repeatedly refers to a *Clavis magna*, “Great Key,” as an earlier and fuller exposition of his art. No copy is presently known.

**Documented:** Bruno cites it.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** it probably contained diagrams and technical material behind *De umbris*.

**Disputed:** whether it was actually printed. Some early biographical traditions speak of a publication; absence from surviving catalogues and collections counsels caution.

**Not legitimate:** reconstructing its precise contents from later Bruno works and then citing that reconstruction as if it were a recovered text.

## 4. Related Bruno works

- *Cantus Circaeus ad memoriae praxim ordinatus*, Paris, 1582.
- *Ars reminiscendi* and *Explicatio triginta sigillorum*, London, 1583.
- *Sigillus sigillorum*, London, 1583.
- *Figuratio Aristotelici physici auditus*, Paris, 1586.
- *De lampade combinatoria Lulliana*, Wittenberg, 1587.
- *De imaginum, signorum et idearum compositione*, Frankfurt, 1591.
- *De vinculis in genere*, composed circa 1590–91.
- *Lampas triginta statuarum*, surviving in manuscript, generally dated to Bruno’s German period.

The thirty seals and thirty statues illuminate Bruno’s continuing attraction to thirty-fold schemes, but they are not textual sections of the 1582 apparatus.

## 5. No authorial manuscript of *De umbris*

No autograph manuscript underlying the edition is known. The primary witnesses are printed copies. Consequently, “manuscript variants” of the work proper are unavailable; variants arise from:

- press corrections among copies of the 1582 printing;
- damaged or misprinted letters and tables;
- editorial correction of syllabic sequences;
- normalization of Greek and Hebrew signs;
- later transcription errors in personal and mythological names.

Sturlese’s critical apparatus should be preferred to the uncorrected nineteenth-century text for the technical tables.

## Yates’s account and the scholarship since

## 1. Yates’s reading

Yates’s principal claims in chapter IX are:

1. Bruno fused the classical art of places and images with Lullian rotating figures.
2. He increased the nine Lullian letters or dignities to thirty.
3. The supplementation by Greek and Hebrew letters reflects Cabalistic influence.
4. The thirty divisions may be connected with magical precedents: Trithemius’s thirty-one spirits, Greek magical papyri, Gnostic aeons or Simonian material.
5. Bruno’s rejection of Lull’s Trinitarian interpretation and appeal to the tetragrammaton reveal a non-Christian or Hermetic-Cabalistic program.
6. The celestial image series forms the central power source of the memory system.
7. The images function like internalized talismans.
8. Metrodorus’s zodiacal memory has become magical.
9. The system aims to restore unity to the psyche by ordering significant images.
10. The memory wheels anticipate Bruno’s later thirty seals, statues, cubicles and links.

Her most famous formulation is:

> “He put the images of the classical art on the Lullian combinatory wheels, but the images were magic images and the wheels were conjuring wheels.”

And:

> “The aim of the memory system is to establish within, in the psyche, the return of the intellect to unity through the organisation of significant images.”

### What Yates established durably

- Bruno cannot be understood through cosmology alone.
- His mnemonic writings are central, not marginal eccentricities.
- The Lullian figures, classical artificial memory and Renaissance theories of imagination converge in his work.
- Celestial imagery is genuinely present.
- Bruno sought intellectual and psychic transformation, not only rote storage.
- The sequence from *De umbris* to the *Thirty Seals* and later image-composition works is historically significant.
- The work belongs to the intellectual environment of Ficinian natural magic even when its precise operation is not talismanic.

### What Yates overstated

- that the reconstructed multi-wheel diagram is effectively Bruno’s own surviving plate;
- that the astral list is indisputably the innermost wheel governing all others;
- that the linguistic mechanism is secondary to magical activation;
- that “sigil” necessarily means a talisman;
- that combining images on wheels constitutes conjuration;
- that Metrodorus is known to have used zodiacal images as magical places;
- that suggested sources such as Trithemius, Gnostic aeons and Greek magical papyri amount to demonstrated lines of transmission.

## 2. Rita Sturlese

Sturlese’s 1991 critical edition and 1992 article, “Per un’interpretazione del *De umbris idearum* di Giordano Bruno,” transformed discussion.

Her central reconstruction is that the five 150-item lists encode components of words—particularly difficult or foreign words—through images. She recommends imagining the lists as five strips, each divided into 150 cells with both image and syllable, sliding laterally until the required word-components align.

This explains:

- why each image has an alphabetic or syllabic address;
- why there are separate agents, actions and attributes;
- why Bruno gives examples such as PA, RE, SI and VO;
- why he discusses words of four, five or more letters;
- why substitution by similar sound is permitted.

**Confirmed:** the system has a practical, linguistic core that Yates did not reconstruct.

**Qualification:** Sturlese must correct numerous printed syllables to make the complete encoding scheme consistent. Manuel Mertens notes that these editorial repairs are the price of making the apparatus work perfectly. [Mertens, *Magic and Memory in Giordano Bruno*](https://nerv.8kun.top/file_dl/3e6621f854b18675ac4ab29d2592b2ef9ca9e3444dd831196694b7687ebafa1a.pdf/Magic_And_Memory_In_Giordano_Bruno_The_Art_Of_A_Heroic_Spirit_by_Manuel_Mertens_z_lib_org_.pdf)

## 3. Michele Ciliberto and the Adelphi editions

The Ciliberto-directed editions preserve Sturlese’s technical advance while placing the mnemonic works within Bruno’s philosophy of nature, language and knowledge. Their general tendency is neither to reduce the art to occult ritual nor to strip it of philosophy.

The image is an instrument of cognition because human knowledge necessarily works through sensible forms. Combinatorial mobility corresponds to a reality whose forms and relations are productive and mutable.

## 4. Stephen Clucas

Clucas has repeatedly argued for closer philological discrimination among:

- mnemonic technique;
- natural magic;
- mathematics and combinatorics;
- image theory;
- religious or Hermetic interpretation.

His work helped displace the monolithic “Hermetic magus” produced by an incautious reading of Yates. “Magic” remains relevant, but it must be shown at the level of particular texts and operations.

## 5. Hilary Gatti

Gatti’s Bruno is a Renaissance philosopher whose mnemonic, cosmological and epistemological projects belong together. She criticizes readings in which “Hermeticism” swallows Bruno’s natural philosophy. The infinite universe, criticism of Aristotelian closure and active human cognition should not be treated as mere disguises for Egyptian religion.

## 6. Leen Spruit, Saverio Ansaldi and recent iconological scholarship

Recent work emphasizes Bruno’s gnoseology: his reform of signs and images is a reform of how the mind organizes experience. A 2022 study of Bruno’s “iconic practice” explicitly says that Sturlese’s introduction overturned Yates’s Hermetic interpretation and calls for analysis grounded in sources, philosophical aims and historical circumstances. [Humanities and Social Sciences Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-022-01237-x)

This does not abolish natural magic. It relocates it: iconic practice can intersect with natural magic because nature, imagination and cognition operate through bonds and forms—not because every mnemonic wheel is a ceremonial talisman.

## 7. Manuel Mertens

Mertens proposes a mediated position. The practical encoding corrected by Sturlese is real, but Bruno’s larger aspiration remains that of a “heroic spirit.” Mnemonic discipline, imaginative transformation and magic cannot always be separated cleanly in Bruno’s mature thought.

## 8. Carruthers, Small, Rossi and Bolzoni

These scholars do not all interpret Bruno identically, but they collectively correct the assumption that premodern memory arts were merely tricks.

- Mary Carruthers shows that medieval *memoria* was a compositional and ethical craft: remembered materials were gathered, recombined and used for invention.
- Jocelyn Penny Small reconstructs the transition from oral and visual memory practices to writing technologies; her work cautions against treating imagery as a primitive substitute for literacy.
- Paolo Rossi connects memory arts, logical method, encyclopedism and projects for universal language.
- Lina Bolzoni shows that Renaissance memory, emblem, image, preaching, literature and collecting inhabit a shared culture of visualization.

Their cumulative effect is to make Bruno’s “internal writing” historically intelligible without requiring either credulous occultism or modern cognitive-science reduction.

## 9. Ong and Ramism

Walter J. Ong’s study of Peter Ramus explains the contemporary alternative: spatialized dialectical division, dichotomous tables and curricular method. Ramism sought an orderly, teachable disposition of subjects and distrusted the luxuriant image-culture associated with older memory arts.

Bruno’s mobile, mythographic combinatorics is nearly the inverse of a Ramist tree:

| Bruno | Ramist method |
|---|---|
| mobile combinations | fixed divisions |
| images and agents | verbal definitions |
| associative multiplicity | dichotomous reduction |
| invention through recombination | ordered curricular analysis |
| ambiguity and transformation | terminological discipline |

Yates sometimes presents this as imagination versus Puritan iconoclasm. That is suggestive but too confessional when generalized. Ramism was pedagogically diverse, and not every critic of artificial imagery was a Puritan enemy of imagination.

## 10. Lull scholarship: Bonner and Fidora

Anthony Bonner’s reconstruction of Lull is indispensable. Lull’s Art is not primarily a memory wheel or a random concept generator. Its alphabet abbreviates carefully defined principles; its figures test combinations under logical and theological rules.

The later ternary Art commonly uses nine letters and principles. Figure T organizes difference, concordance, contrariety, beginning, middle and end; Figure A carries divine dignities. The fourth figure uses concentric movable circles, but admissible reasoning depends on definitions and rules. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/llull/); [Bonner, *The Art and Logic of Ramon Llull*](https://uberty.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/Anthony_Bonner_The_art_and_logic_of_Ramon_Llull.pdf)

Alexander Fidora and other modern Lullists emphasize Lull’s scholastic, missionary and interreligious setting. Lull’s Art was meant to demonstrate Christian truth through necessary reasons, not to summon spirits.

**Correction to Yates:** Bruno appropriates Lullian mobility and alphabetic compression while rejecting important Lullian theological constraints. Bruno’s thirty-fold art is therefore “Lullian” by transformation, not a faithful continuation of Lull.

The University of Barcelona’s Ramon Llull Database distinguishes authentic and pseudo-Lullian works—essential because Renaissance “Lullism” incorporated alchemical and occult writings that Llull did not compose. [Llull Database](https://www.ub.edu/llulldb/presentacio)

## History and context

## 1. Classical artificial memory

The foundational rhetorical model uses places as a wax tablet and images as writing. Cicero states:

> *locorum ordinem rerum ordinem conservare, imaginibus autem rerum verba ipsa designari*

“The order of places preserves the order of things; images of things designate the things themselves.”

He recommends numerous, well-lit, clearly spaced places and active, sharply marked images. [Cicero, *De oratore* 2.86–88](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/memoria/Cicero.html)

### The *Ad Herennium* lawsuit

The anonymous *Rhetorica ad Herennium* gives the canonical worked example: to remember an accusation of poisoning for an inheritance, with witnesses, imagine the sick man in bed and the accused holding:

- a cup in the right hand: poison;
- tablets in the left: the will or inheritance;
- ram’s testicles on the fourth finger: witnesses, through a Latin wordplay and the conventional use of ram-scrotum leather for purses.

Latin:

> *dextra poculum, sinistra tabulas, medico testiculos arietinos tenentem.*

The precise wording varies across editions, but the grotesque scene demonstrates the governing principle: an image must be active, exceptional and semantically overdetermined. [*Ad Herennium*, Book III](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Rhetorica_ad_Herennium/3%2A.html)

Bruno’s agents and actions inherit this grammar. His novelty is to regularize it into combinatorial tables.

## 2. Metrodorus of Scepsis

Cicero says that Carneades and Metrodorus used ideas or images upon places as if writing characters on wax. Quintilian associates Metrodorus with a division of the zodiac.

**Documented:** Metrodorus was famous for artificial memory and linked with zodiacal ordering.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** L. A. Post proposed that he used 360 loci—ten under each of 36 decans.

**Yates’s interpretation:** Bruno’s celestial images represent the transformation of the Metrodorian zodiacal memory into magic.

**Correction:** the 360-place reconstruction is not preserved in Metrodorus’s own words. “Metrodorus made magical” is a narrative inference, not a demonstrable chain of transmission.

## 3. Scholastic psychology

Aristotelian psychology held that embodied human thinking normally requires phantasms. Medieval commentators consequently treated memory as a corporeally conditioned internal sense while also assigning it important roles in prudence, meditation and composition.

Bruno radicalizes rather than invents the premise that intellect works through images. His “shadows” supply a Neoplatonic cosmology for the old psychophysical fact that the mind needs sensible forms.

## 4. Dominican institutions

Bruno entered the Dominican convent of San Domenico Maggiore in Naples, a major intellectual house associated with scholastic theology and the memory of Thomas Aquinas. Dominican preaching had long depended on trained memory, ordered distinctions and vivid moral exempla.

**Documented context:** Bruno received Dominican philosophical and theological training.

**Reconstruction:** this environment supplied mnemonic and scholastic habits later redirected against orthodox theology.

**Not documented:** that the Thirty was an institutional Dominican method secretly transmitted to him.

## 5. Franciscan and Lullian traditions

Lull was a lay Franciscan tertiary, and his Art circulated through Franciscan, university and courtly networks. By Bruno’s time “Lullism” covered:

- authentic logical and apologetic works;
- mnemonic adaptations;
- pseudo-Lullian alchemy;
- Cabalistic and encyclopedic expansions.

Bruno worked inside this expanded reception rather than directly reproducing one medieval Lullian text.

## 6. Courts and universities

Bruno taught or sought patronage in:

- Toulouse;
- Paris and the court of Henri III;
- England, under the protection of Michel de Castelnau;
- Wittenberg;
- Prague;
- Helmstedt;
- Frankfurt;
- Venice and Padua.

Memory performance offered entry into courts because it promised intellectual mastery, spectacle and useful instruction. Dedications were instruments of patronage.

## 7. Camillo

Giulio Camillo’s Memory Theatre arranged the universe in a sevenfold theatre, ascending through cosmic and intellectual grades. Its user stood where the actor would ordinarily stand and surveyed the universe displayed before him.

Yates sees Bruno as replacing Camillo’s static theatre with mobile Lullian architecture. This is illuminating but schematic:

- Camillo and Bruno both organize universal knowledge through images.
- Both relate visible forms to higher realities.
- Bruno makes combination and linguistic encoding much more explicit.
- Bruno does not simply discard architectural loci.

The familiar tale that Camillo’s theatre impressed Francis I derives from contemporary reports, especially Viglius Zuichemus, but descriptions of a completed, fully working wooden machine should not be embellished beyond those reports.

## 8. Hermeticism and natural magic

The opening dialogue’s Hermes, the use of celestial images, and Bruno’s later explicit magical writings make Hermetic and magical contexts unavoidable.

Yet several distinct propositions must be kept apart:

1. images assist memory;
2. celestial images represent cosmic structures;
3. the imagination can be brought into conformity with nature;
4. celestial images receive or transmit astral influence;
5. a mnemonic wheel is a talisman;
6. rotating it conjures spirits.

Bruno supports 1–3 in *De umbris*. His corpus supports versions of 4 in appropriate natural-magical contexts. Proposition 5 is interpretive. Proposition 6 is not demonstrated.

## People

## Bruno

Practitioner, theorist and self-advertising teacher. His reputation for memory was real enough to attract Henri III and, later, Giovanni Mocenigo.

## Henri III

Bruno testified before the Venetian Inquisition that the king called him and asked whether his memory was natural or magical. Bruno said he satisfied him by explanation and demonstration, after which the king recognized that the art was not magical and granted him a salaried extraordinary lectureship.

**Status:** documented as Bruno’s sworn retrospective testimony, not independently verified court minutes.

## Michel de Castelnau

French ambassador in London and Bruno’s patron during the English period. His household gave Bruno protection and access to elite intellectual circles.

## Giovanni Mocenigo

Venetian patrician who invited Bruno to teach him memory and related arts. Dissatisfied, he confined Bruno and denounced him to the Venetian Inquisition in May 1592.

**Documented:** memory instruction was central to their relationship.

**Documented accusation:** Mocenigo accused Bruno of heretical propositions and of claiming secret knowledge.

**Disputed motive:** whether Mocenigo primarily wanted mnemonic training, magical secrets, personal power or revenge for Bruno’s intended departure.

## Philip Sidney, John Dee and English readers

Bruno moved in a milieu that included Sidney and figures acquainted with John Dee. Intellectual contact is documented at varying levels; direct collaboration on a thirty-fold conjuring system is not.

Yates’s comparison between Bruno’s thirty and Dee’s angelic or heptarchical materials is structurally intriguing but not a proven borrowing. Dee’s *Clavis angelicae* and related angelic records belong to a distinct revelatory practice.

## Critics

The dialogue’s Logifer represents the pedant who distrusts Bruno’s art. Later critics included Aristotelians, Ramists and confessional polemicists, but opposition must be established case by case.

### Requested anecdotes: evidence audit

- **The accused magician:** securely applicable to Bruno. Henri III reportedly asked whether his memory was natural or magical; Mocenigo later denounced him.
- **The auctioneer:** no securely traceable auctioneer anecdote belongs to *De umbris* or its early reception.
- **The professor’s party:** no primary documentation located connecting such an anecdote with the Thirty.
- **The student who lost his memory:** no primary documentation located in the 1582 work, Bruno’s trial testimony or the critical scholarship consulted.
- **Modern repetition:** unattributed online retellings often migrate anecdotes among Simonides, Camillo, Bruno and later memory performers. Without a named witness and early source they should be classified as floating memory-art folklore.

Absence here is a finding: these stories should not be added merely to complete a standard catalogue template.

## The magic question

## 1. Was it called magic in 1582?

Henri III’s reported question shows that extraordinary memory could arouse suspicion of magic. Bruno answered that his skill arose from art.

The title page promises “uncommon operations through memory,” but does not call the system demonic or ceremonial magic.

## 2. Is its philosophy magical?

In a Renaissance sense, partly and potentially:

- nature is understood as an interconnected hierarchy;
- images mediate between levels of reality;
- celestial figures possess intellectual and affective efficacy;
- imagination can organize the person in conformity with universal order.

These propositions overlap with natural magic as Ficino and Agrippa understood it.

## 3. Is it talismanic?

**Yates:** yes, in an interiorized form. Astral images impressed in memory become inner talismans.

**Sturlese/Warburg correction:** this is not tenable as the technical explanation of the wheels. Their explicit operations encode linguistic material.

**Qualified modern position:** celestial images may carry more than arbitrary mnemonic meaning, but a cosmologically meaningful image is not automatically a consecrated talisman.

## 4. Is it conjuration?

No conjuration formula, spirit-name protocol, suffumigation, sacrifice or ritual timing appears in the operational wheel instructions.

Yates’s phrase “conjuring wheels” is metaphorical-historical interpretation. It should not be converted into the factual assertion that Bruno instructed users to evoke aerial spirits.

Her proposed parallels include:

- Trithemius’s *Steganographia* and its spirit names;
- magical papyri;
- Gnostic groups of aeons;
- Simon Magus;
- John Dee’s angelic practices.

These are comparative leads. Direct textual derivation remains unproved.

## 5. The *Ars notoria*

The *Ars notoria* promised knowledge or memory through figures, prayers, sacred names and ritual contemplation. Bruno knew a culture in which extraordinary intellectual acquisition could be associated with such arts.

In later writing he distinguishes disciplined mnemonic work from passive or illicit spirit-possession. Yates herself notes that Bruno’s memory magic is not the “lazy magic” of staring at a *nota* while reciting prayers.

Thus:

- both traditions employ visual forms and promise intellectual power;
- the *Ars notoria* depends on devotional or angelic ritual;
- Bruno’s 1582 mechanism requires learned associations, trained imagination and combinatorial work.

## 6. Heresy and the Inquisition

Bruno’s arrest and execution cannot responsibly be reduced to his support for Copernicus or to a memory wheel.

The surviving trial material indicates a cluster of theological and philosophical charges: positions concerning the Trinity, Incarnation, Christ, the Eucharist, the soul, plurality of worlds and other matters. The definitive Roman trial dossier is incomplete; the surviving summary does not give a single exhaustive modern-style charge sheet.

Memory was causally relevant because it brought Bruno to Mocenigo. It was not itself demonstrated to be the formal reason for his execution.

**Legend:** “Bruno was burned for saying the Earth goes around the Sun.”

**Better documented:** he was condemned as an obstinate heretic after a long trial involving multiple propositions, some theological and some philosophical.

**Another legend:** “The Church burned him for inventing the memory palace.”

There is no evidence for that claim.

## Afterlife

## 1. The later Bruno corpus

The most direct descendants are Bruno’s own:

- thirty seals;
- thirty statues;
- thirty cubicles or related spatial arrays;
- combinatorial lamps;
- image-composition theory;
- thirty bonds or links in *De vinculis*.

The *Explicatio triginta sigillorum* converts mnemonic operations into thirty named procedural “seals”: Field, Tree, Chain, Ladder, Table, Zeuxis, Phidias, Daedalus, Person, Centurion, Wheel, Fountain and Mirror, and others. “Seal” here often means a compact procedural form, not a metal talisman.

The *Lampas triginta statuarum* organizes philosophical concepts through thirty divine or mythological statues. The Lycaon-to-Glaucus series in *De umbris* is therefore a precursor in the broad sense that Bruno was already using ordered mythological agents; it is not an early draft whose one-to-one transformation into the later statues has been demonstrated.

## 2. Leibniz and combinatorics

Leibniz knew Lullian and combinatorial traditions and proposed a *characteristica universalis* and calculus of reasoning.

The defensible genealogy is:

```text
Lullian alphabetic Art
        ↓
Renaissance Lullism and combinatory tables
        ↓
Bruno and other early-modern transformations
        ↓
seventeenth-century universal-language projects
        ↓
Leibnizian combinatorial logic
```

**Documented:** Leibniz’s 1666 *Dissertatio de arte combinatoria* discusses Lullian combination.

**Scholarly genealogy:** Bruno forms one strand of the environment through which combinatorial ambitions developed.

**Modern invention:** “Bruno invented the computer.” His wheels are rule-governed symbolic devices but lack automatic execution, stored programs and formal semantics in the modern sense.

## 3. Universal languages

Bruno’s syllabic image banks anticipate problems later addressed by universal-language projects:

- decomposition of language into primitives;
- fixed symbolic addresses;
- compositional recombination;
- translation between signs and concepts;
- exhaustive classification.

But Bruno’s system remains dependent on personal memory, culturally specific mythological images and phonetic approximation. It is not a universal language in the strict seventeenth-century sense.

## 4. Literature and art

### Dante

Dante’s ordered heavens, moral images and architectonic poem form part of the long history of memorial composition. Direct influence from Dante on the detailed mechanics of *De umbris* is plausible at the level of cultural formation but not specifically documented.

### Frescoes and preaching

Medieval and Renaissance fresco cycles, preaching divisions and moral personifications supplied memorable ordered images. Bolzoni and Carruthers demonstrate the shared visual-compositional culture. No surviving fresco has been identified as a literal illustration of Bruno’s five 150-cell wheels.

### Emblem books

The emblem’s union of image, motto and explanatory text resembles mnemonic overdetermination. Bruno’s active images participate in the same early-modern ecology of signs. Direct borrowing must be shown emblem by emblem.

### Borges’s Funes

“Funes the Memorious” explores unlimited retention without abstraction. It is a conceptual anti-Brunian counterpoint: Bruno seeks order, selection and combination; Funes is overwhelmed by particulars.

A direct textual debt to *De umbris* is not established.

### Umberto Eco

Eco discusses Lull, Bruno and combinatorial languages in *The Search for the Perfect Language*. He presents Bruno’s first practice as rotating agents, actions and attributes, and the second as five 150-item series. Eco’s account reflects the post-Sturlese technical reconstruction rather than Yates’s purely talismanic reading.

### Modern visual art

Fiona Tan’s 2019 *Umbris Idearum* reproduces and enlarges the logic of Yates’s 1952 reconstruction: six concentric circles divided into 150 rays and five cells. It is an artwork based on Yates, not a facsimile of an authorial Bruno plate. [Fiona Tan](https://fionatan.nl/work/umbris-idearum)

## 5. Memory-palace revival and athletes

Modern competitive mnemonists commonly use:

- loci;
- person–action–object systems;
- fixed number or card images;
- vivid interaction;
- pre-learned image dictionaries.

These techniques make Bruno newly intelligible. Nevertheless:

- the method of loci descends directly from the classical tradition, not uniquely from Bruno;
- modern PAO was not copied straightforwardly from *De umbris*;
- there is little evidence that elite competitors use Bruno’s full 750-image second practice;
- online claims that his wheel is “the original PAO” are illuminating analogies, not established transmission history.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Was the wheel magical or linguistic?

**Yates:** principally a magical organization of astral images.

**Sturlese:** demonstrably a mechanism for encoding words through image-series.

**Current best synthesis:** the linguistic operation is textually secure; the surrounding theory of images has metaphysical and natural-magical dimensions; ceremonial conjuration is unsupported.

## 2. Did Bruno print Yates’s Fig. 8?

No. Yates reconstructed it from Bruno’s lists and directions.

Bruno printed smaller diagrams and a contracted wheel because five complete 150-cell wheels could not conveniently fit the page. Modern reproductions often omit the word “reconstruction,” thereby turning a twentieth-century scholarly model into a supposed Renaissance artifact.

## 3. How many wheels?

The first practice requires aligned agent/action/attribute fields and can be extended by accompanying and circumstantial fields. The second explicitly calls for five 150-combination wheels.

Yates’s composite drawing contains an additional central astral organization. Modern descriptions consequently vary between five and six wheels. The distinction is:

- five explicit functional image-series;
- a sixth central astral wheel in Yates’s reconstruction.

## 4. Are the Thirty Lullian dignities?

Only with qualification.

Bruno’s *De compendiosa architectura* says that he unfolds to thirty what Cabalists reduce to ten Sephiroth. But the thirty signs in *De umbris* are also an alphabetic infrastructure, and the thirty intentions and concepts are philosophical series.

Treating every thirty as the same list of dignities collapses distinct operations.

## 5. Is Bruno’s Cabala Jewish Cabala?

Bruno knew Christian Cabalistic transformations of Jewish material more directly than rabbinic Kabbalah in its historical fullness. His Sephirotic analogy and tetragrammaton are real, but his system is not a faithful exposition of Jewish Kabbalah.

Karen de León-Jones gives a stronger Kabbalistic reading; other scholars emphasize eclectic Christian Cabala, Lullism and Bruno’s independent philosophical transformation. [*Giordano Bruno and the Kabbalah*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt211qvg4)

## 6. Did Bruno reject the Trinity for the tetragrammaton?

In *De compendiosa architectura*, Bruno mocks Lull’s attempt to demonstrate specifically Christian mysteries and shifts attention to the tetragrammaton and its numerical unfolding.

That is strong evidence of anti-Trinitarian or extra-Trinitarian philosophical pressure. It is not by itself a formal confession that the tetragrammaton “replaces” the Trinity in every part of Bruno’s system.

## 7. Did he derive the title from Cecco d’Ascoli?

Yates suggests Cecco’s necromantic commentary on Sacrobosco as a source for *umbrae idearum*. Verbal or conceptual parallels may exist, but a direct derivation has not become a settled result comparable to the demonstrable Plotinian and biblical materials.

Status: **suggested source, not established provenance**.

## 8. Trithemius’s thirty-one spirits

Numerical similarity and Bruno’s interest in secret writing make comparison with *Steganographia* tempting. No explicit borrowing in *De umbris* establishes that its thirty signs are Trithemian spirits with one added or removed.

Status: **Yatesian speculation**.

## 9. Greek magical papyri, Gnostic aeons and Simon Magus

These comparisons belong to Yates’s effort to recover a broad late-antique magical background. They should remain comparative hypotheses. No manuscript chain connects a named magical papyrus directly to Bruno’s five image registers.

## 10. Did Bruno’s wheels work?

The first practice clearly works as an image-based letter code. The second is possible in principle but imposes an immense learning burden.

Difficulties include:

- hundreds of arbitrary associations;
- corrupt or inconsistent syllabic values;
- mythological names unfamiliar even to learned readers;
- collision among similar images;
- slow decoding;
- ambiguous pronunciation;
- language-dependent approximations.

Bruno himself permits substitution of familiar agents and sound-alikes. This flexibility improves usability but weakens claims of universal exactitude.

## 11. Was Bruno’s memory prodigious?

His fame and teaching career are documented. Henri III’s reported test supports a reputation for performance.

But the details of the demonstration are not preserved. No controlled record establishes how much material he memorized, at what speed or with what accuracy.

Status: **documented reputation, undocumented performance metrics**.

## 12. Was the *Clavis magna* real?

Bruno cites it repeatedly, so a work or project of that name existed. Whether it circulated in print, in manuscript or only as an evolving authorial dossier remains unresolved.

## 13. Was Bruno executed for science, magic or memory?

None alone. The single-cause stories are later simplifications. Memory led him into the Mocenigo relationship; magical reputation shaped suspicion; cosmology mattered; theological propositions dominated the heresy process.

The absence of the complete Roman dossier prevents perfect reconstruction.

## Open questions

1. Can every corrupt syllabic entry in the 1582 tables be repaired without conjectural alteration?
2. Did Bruno expect five literal material volvelles, five memorized arrays or flexible use of both?
3. Where exactly did he intend the final astral series to sit relative to the five linguistic registers?
4. Did the lost *Clavis magna* already contain the complete five-field grammar?
5. Which mythographic source supplied each Lycaon-to-Glaucus entry?
6. How much comes directly from Ravisius Textor’s inventories of inventors and how much from other mythographies?
7. Which version of “Teucer the Babylonian” lies behind the celestial images?
8. Are press variants in surviving 1582 copies evidence of authorial correction during printing?
9. How did actual students adapt the 150-name lists? No surviving learner’s workbook has yet supplied a complete worked implementation.
10. Was Henri III’s test a demonstration of this precise wheel system or of a simpler art?
11. Did Sidney, Dee or English students ever construct Bruno’s complete apparatus?
12. How should “magic” be calibrated across Bruno’s corpus: cognitive transformation, natural magic, astral mediation or ritual practice?
13. Does the succession from thirty images to thirty seals and statues express one continuous master-plan or repeated reuse of a convenient numerical architecture?
14. How much did seventeenth-century combinatorics receive from Bruno specifically rather than from Lullism generally?
15. Are the Thirty best understood as an alphabet, an ontology, a pedagogical curriculum or a deliberately polyvalent union of all three?

### Evidentiary vocabulary used below

- **[TEXT]** Directly documented in a primary source.
- **[MATERIAL]** Established by a surviving book, manuscript, diagram, or bibliographical witness.
- **[RECONSTRUCTION]** A modern inference reasonably supported by the evidence.
- **[YATES]** Frances Yates’s interpretation.
- **[DISPUTED]** Proposed but challenged or inadequately demonstrated.
- **[TRADITION]** A report transmitted by earlier writers but not independently verified.
- **[LEGEND]** A story whose documentary foundation is absent or substantially defective.
- **[MODERN]** A modern analogy, revival, or invention—not a historical component of Fludd’s system.

The most important general finding is negative: no evidence presently establishes that Fludd’s engraving records the Globe Theatre, that twenty-four complete theatres were actually to be installed in the zodiac, that the system was a Rosicrucian cipher, or that Willis was certainly Fludd’s source. All four are interpretations, principally associated with Yates. Recent manuscript research does, however, strengthen the case that Fludd personally designed or closely controlled much of the visual programme of his books.

## The system in detail

### 1. Its place within Fludd’s encyclopaedia

**[MATERIAL]** The memory treatise forms part of the microcosmic half of Fludd’s enormous history of the two worlds: the greater world, or macrocosm, and the lesser world, the human being. Its full heading is:

> *De animae memorativae scientia, quae vulgo Ars memoriae vocatur.*

“On the science of the remembering soul, which is commonly called the art of memory.”

The placing matters. Memory is not an isolated trick but a technical power of the human microcosm. Sense, imagination, reason or mind, and memory participate in the reception and internal re-formation of the worlds.

**[TEXT]** Fludd says at the opening that he first encountered the art at Nîmes, developed his command of it at Avignon, and taught it to noble pupils at Marseilles. Yates connects this with the securely dated geomancy episode of the winter of 1601–1602 and Fludd’s instruction of Charles de Lorraine, fourth Duke of Guise, and his brother. The geographical association is documented by Fludd; the precise integration of the memory lessons into the 1601–1602 chronology is a historical reconstruction. Yates cites *Utriusque Cosmi Historia* II.2, p. 48, together with Fludd’s geomancy account and C. H. Josten’s translation. [Yates’s chapter and references](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/mnemosyne/fq/yates_memory.pdf)

### 2. The introductory memory image

**[MATERIAL]** The opening plate, conventionally reproduced as Yates’s plate 15, depicts a human head with an enlarged interior eye, the *oculus imaginationis*. Beside it are five compartments or loci containing:

1. an obelisk;
2. the Tower of Babel;
3. Tobias and the angel;
4. a ship;
5. the Last Judgment, with the damned entering Hell’s mouth.

The Frankfurt University Library identifies the design as Matthäus Merian the Elder’s engraved *Oculus imaginationis*, in Fludd’s 1619 *Ars memoriae*, p. 47, shelfmark Occ. 15, vol. 2.1. [Frankfurt exhibition record](https://merian-alchemie.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/ausstellung/ii-alchemische-bildwelten/merian-fludd-oculus-imag/)

**[TEXT]** Fludd does not subsequently explain these five subjects.

**[YATES]** Yates tentatively read the obelisk as Egyptian inner writing, Babel as confused language overcome by the art, Tobias as angelic guidance, and the Last Judgment as the destination or warning. She immediately withdrew from certainty:

> “This may be over fanciful, and in the absence of any explanation by Fludd it is better to leave them unexplained.”

That restraint is essential. The frequently repeated allegorical reading belongs to Yates, not to Fludd.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** The plate nevertheless announces three verifiable structural ideas:

- imagination supplies a visual intermediary;
- material is assigned to discrete places;
- Fludd repeatedly favours sets of five.

**[YATES]** Calling the Last Judgment a “relic” of the medieval practice of remembering Hell is historically plausible, but not something Fludd says.

### 3. Natural memory, artificial memory, and medicine

**[TEXT]** Fludd recognizes natural memory and artificial aids. Artificial memory can be assisted either physiologically—by medicines and regimes affecting the bodily organ—or by artfully directing fantasy and images.

This is not an eccentric addition. Renaissance mnemonic books commonly combined rhetorical technique with Galenic or Aristotelian advice about temperament, sleep, diet, and the brain. Willis’s *Mnemonica* likewise appends a treatise on nourishing or cherishing natural memory.

### 4. *Ars rotunda*: the round art

**[TEXT]** Fludd divides artificial memory into *ars rotunda* and *ars quadrata*. The round art operates through what he calls *ideae*. He expressly distinguishes his usage from Plato’s divine Ideas. His “ideas” are forms separated from elemental bodies: spirits, shades, souls, angels, demons, stellar effigies, images of gods and goddesses endowed with celestial powers, and imagined virtues and vices.

Yates’s close paraphrase of the passage preserves its essential vocabulary:

> forms separated from corporeal things, such as spirits, shadows, souls … angels, demons, the effigies of stars, the images of gods and goddesses to whom celestial powers are attributed … virtues and vices conceived in the imagination and made into shadows.

The operative Latin terms include *spiritus*, *umbrae*, *animae*, *angeli*, *daemones*, *effigies stellarum*, and virtues or vices *in umbras redactae*. The last phrase means approximately “reduced” or “rendered into shadows.”

**[TEXT]** Its common place is:

> *Locus communis artis rotundae est pars mundi aetherea.*

“The common place of the round art is the ethereal part of the world.”

Fludd specifies the celestial orbs from the eighth sphere down through the lunar sphere.

**[TEXT]** The round art is called natural and superior because its order corresponds to the constitution of the macrocosm and microcosm. It requires more than ordinary fabrication. Fludd associates its successful operation with daemonic assistance or the metaphysical influence of the Holy Spirit, and says that fantasy must concur in the metaphysical act.

**[YATES]** Yates translated this language into a strong account of “magicised or talismanic images”:

> “The ‘round art’, then, uses magicised or talismanic images, effigies of the stars; ‘statues’ of gods and goddesses animated with celestial influences.”

The text unquestionably speaks about demons, stellar images, celestial powers, and supernatural aid. It does not, in the *Ars memoriae* passage, prescribe the physical manufacture, consecration, or ritual activation of talismans. “Talismanic” is therefore Yates’s interpretive category, not Fludd’s technical instruction.

### 5. *Ars quadrata*: the square art

**[TEXT]** The square art employs corporeal things:

- human beings;
- animals;
- inanimate objects;
- especially human or animal figures performing actions.

These are the traditional *imagines agentes*, active images.

**[TEXT]** It is easier and more widely used than the round art because rooms and square structures impress themselves on ordinary imagination more readily than spiritual ideas contemplated alone:

> *conclavis aut cubiculi quadrati species facilius imprimuntur hominum litterarum ignarum conceptui, quam ideae sola contemplatione consideratae.*

“The forms of an apartment or square room are more easily impressed upon the conception of people untrained in letters than ideas considered by contemplation alone.”

**[TEXT]** Fludd nevertheless calls it artificial: its buildings, divisions, and images are human constructions rather than the natural celestial order.

### 6. The argument over real and fictitious places

**[TEXT]** Fludd launches a substantial polemic against mnemonicists who fabricate palaces entirely in imagination:

> *Nonnulli in hac arte versati artem suam quadratam in palatiis ab imaginationis inventione fabricatis seu erectis collocare volunt…*

“Some practiced in this art wish to place their square art in palaces fabricated or erected by the invention of imagination…”

He argues that such places burden and confuse memory. A likeness derived as immediately as possible from sensory reality is firmer:

> *quo propinquius et magis immediate procedit imaginationis similitudo a realitate sensus, eo sit in hac arte certior, validior, durabilior et efficacior.*

“The more closely and immediately an imaginative likeness proceeds from sensory reality, the more certain, strong, lasting, and effective it is in this art.”

**[TEXT]** This position is stricter than the *Rhetorica ad Herennium*, which permits invented or fictitious backgrounds when real ones are insufficient.

**[DISPUTED]** “Real place” does not necessarily mean that every printed theatre engraving is a measured portrait of a specific building. It can mean that the practitioner should acquire the spatial pattern from something actually seen. Ute Frietsch argues that Yates concretized the rule too aggressively when she turned it into proof that the engraved theatre must reproduce the Globe.

### 7. The celestial ordering diagram

**[MATERIAL]** Fludd’s zodiac diagram, Yates’s plate 16, consists of:

- an outer eighth sphere bearing the twelve zodiacal signs;
- seven nested planetary circuits;
- the sphere of the elements at the centre;
- two tiny stage-like structures flanking Aries.

**[TEXT]** This supplies a natural spatial order and a temporal order, because the planetary spheres move through the zodiac.

**[MATERIAL]** The two miniature structures have back walls with two doors. They are not reproduced in this exact form elsewhere and receive no unmistakable explanation in the prose.

**[YATES]** Yates interpreted them as an advance diagram of the theatre principle and conjectured a pair at each zodiacal sign.

**[DISPUTED]** The resulting total of twenty-four theatres—eastern and western at each of twelve signs—is not explicitly stated by Fludd. It follows from extrapolating the Aries pair across the entire zodiac.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** The safest conclusion is that the paired structures establish a relationship among theatre, zodiac, and polarity. Whether the system demands twenty-four fully elaborated repositories remains open.

### 8. What Fludd means by “theatre”

Fludd explains:

> *Theatrum appello in quo omnes actiones verborum, sententiarum, particularum orationis vel rerum demonstrantur, quemadmodum in theatro publico comoediae et tragoediae aguntur.*

“I call a theatre that in which all actions of words, sentences, parts of an oration, or things are displayed, just as comedies and tragedies are acted in a public theatre.”

**[TEXT]** It is therefore:

- a place of display;
- an ordered repository for words and things;
- analogous to the acting space of a public theatre.

**[YATES]** Yates emphasized that Fludd means a stage or scenic wall rather than a complete auditorium-and-stage building.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** “Stage” is a fair description of the engraving, but Fludd’s mnemonic *theatrum* also carries the broader early-modern senses of display, survey, and organized presentation familiar from anatomical theatres, theatres of machines, universal histories, and *theatrum mundi* books.

### 9. The eastern and western theatres

**[TEXT]** Fludd describes eastern and western theatres:

- the eastern is bright, clear, or shining and serves daytime actions;
- the western is dark, black, or obscure and serves night actions.

They share a plan but differ chromatically and functionally.

**[YATES]** Yates linked them to planetary day and night houses and to the two structures flanking Aries.

**[DISPUTED]** Fludd does not explicitly say “two theatres for every zodiacal sign.” Nor does he explicitly derive this contrast from Willis’s instruction to clear daytime memories before sleep and nighttime memories after sleep. That connection is Yates’s ingenious but speculative comparison.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** The day/night polarity can be explained internally by Fludd’s cosmology of light, darkness, east, west, celestial revolution, and temporal order; Willis is not required to explain it.

### 10. The principal theatre engraving

**[MATERIAL]** The engraving facing the celestial diagram bears the inscription usually read as *THEATRUM ORBI*. It shows:

- a rectangular acting or viewing space;
- a rear architectural wall;
- three openings at ground level;
- a central opening with heavy hinged double doors;
- two arched lateral openings;
- an upper battlemented terrace;
- two openings on the upper level;
- a projecting central upper chamber or bay;
- five column bases in the foreground.

Yates described it as a multilevel Elizabethan or Jacobean stage wall.

**[DISPUTED]** Yates resisted emending *ORBI* to *ORBIS*. She read the dative as “a theatre for/in the world,” corresponding to its placement beneath or within the cosmic diagram. Printer’s abbreviation, engraver’s error, grammatical looseness, and an intended *theatrum orbis* remain possible.

### 11. Five doors and five columns

**[TEXT]** Each theatre is to possess five doors, distinct and approximately equidistant:

> *Quodlibet theatrum quinque habebit portas ab invicem distinctas et quasi aequidistantes…*

“Each theatre will have five doors distinct from one another and approximately equidistant…”

**[TEXT]** Opposite them are five imagined columns, distinguished by form and colour. Their forms are:

1. round;
2. square;
3. hexagonal;
4. square;
5. round.

Fludd’s rule is symmetrical: the two extreme columns are circular, the central one hexagonal, and the two intermediates square.

**[TEXT]** Their corresponding colours are:

1. white;
2. red;
3. green;
4. blue or azure;
5. black.

**[MATERIAL]** In the engraving the shafts are absent, but their five bases appear in the specified sequence.

**[TEXT/RECONSTRUCTION]** Doors and columns yield ten local positions. Shape, colour, spacing, and opposition make each address discriminable. The images are assigned to these addresses and retrieved by mentally surveying them in their fixed order.

**[YATES]** Yates correctly related approximate spacing and non-similarity to the classical rules for loci. She called the system a union of the round and square arts:

> “The heavens work together with the theatres, and the theatres are in the heavens.”

**[CAUTION]** Fludd does not call the system a “Seal.” Yates borrows that word from Bruno.

### 12. The secondary theatres

**[MATERIAL]** Two further engravings, Yates’s plates 18a–b, show shallow rooms with the front wall removed:

- one has three doors;
- one has five;
- the five-door room again presents corresponding column bases;
- battlements visually connect these rooms with the upper work of the principal theatre;
- the rooms are represented as made from distinguishable materials or textures.

Fludd calls one the figure of a true theatre.

**[YATES]** Yates treated these as subsidiary theatres connected to the main theatre and, through it, to the heavens.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** They can function as expandable subrepositories. A principal place may open into a smaller room, multiplying capacity without sacrificing addressability. The printed text does not supply a complete traversal algorithm for all the rooms.

### 13. Principal ideas

**[TEXT]** The primary celestial images are ordered through the planetary spheres. Fludd’s example tracks Saturn through the zodiac, assigning a different Saturnian presentation according to the sign; the procedure may be repeated for the remaining planets.

This creates a combinatorial celestial matrix:

- planetary power;
- zodiacal qualification;
- temporal or spatial position;
- corresponding spiritual image.

**[YATES]** Yates likened this to the central wheels of Bruno’s *De umbris idearum* and to Bruno’s later systems in which celestial images energize architectural repositories.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** “Astrally activated” is an intelligible summary of Yates’s reading but is not Fludd’s procedural phrase. The text establishes correspondence and celestial efficacy; it does not fully specify whether every mundane image acquires an occult force merely by being filed beneath a stellar address.

### 14. Less principal images

**[TEXT]** The subordinate images are placed on doors and columns. They include mythological and dramatic figures in memorable actions.

One set consists of:

- Jason with the Golden Fleece;
- Medea;
- Paris;
- Daphne;
- Phoebus.

Other sequences repeatedly transform Medea. Among Yates’s reported examples:

- Medea gathering herbs at the white door;
- Medea killing her brother at the red door;
- different Medean actions at the remaining coloured places.

Circe also appears. These figures are mnemonic actors: identity, action, colour, and position combine to cue the intended item.

**[YATES]** Yates’s remark that “the magic of these sorceresses must have been very helpful” is rhetorical interpretation. Their usefulness can be explained by vividness, narrative differentiation, and the classical demand for exceptional images without supposing literal sorcery.

### 15. Rules for images

Fludd’s practical image rules belong to the classical and printed Renaissance traditions:

- use a definite person or object rather than an indeterminate abstraction;
- prefer action;
- make the action conspicuous, exceptional, violent, comic, beautiful, ugly, or otherwise emotionally forceful;
- encode identity through customary attributes;
- connect an image to its place by colour, material, posture, or action;
- enlarge what is too small to see;
- reduce or represent pictorially what is too large;
- use similarity, wordplay, proper names, mythology, occupational attributes, and conventional emblems;
- avoid crowding;
- preserve a stable order.

The famous rule that images should be striking rather than ordinary descends from *Ad Herennium* III.22:

> *Imagines igitur nos in eo genere constituere oportebit, quod genus in memoria diutissime potest haerere… si egregiam pulchritudinem aut unicam turpitudinem eis adtribuemus… si cruentam aut caeno oblitam aut rubrica delibutam inducemus.*

“We ought therefore to set up images of the kind that can cling longest in memory … if we give them exceptional beauty or singular ugliness … if we represent one bloodied, smeared with mud, or daubed with red.”

**[TEXT]** Fludd cites this classical tradition. The exact integration of every *Ad Herennium* rule into the celestial system is not separately demonstrated.

### 16. Order and retrieval

A defensible reconstruction proceeds as follows:

1. **[TEXT]** Choose a real, sensorially known theatre or room-pattern for the square art.
2. **[TEXT]** Fix its doors and opposing columns in a stable order.
3. **[TEXT]** Differentiate positions by spacing, shape, and colour.
4. **[TEXT]** Choose principal celestial ideas in the natural order of zodiac and planets when practicing the round art.
5. **[TEXT]** Convert things, names, clauses, or words into vivid subordinate images.
6. **[TEXT]** Adapt the images to the colour or material of their repositories.
7. **[RECONSTRUCTION]** Deposit them successively at the doors and columns.
8. **[RECONSTRUCTION]** Retrieve by mentally scanning the same route; the place recalls the actor, whose attributes and action decode the content.
9. **[TEXT]** Clear or reuse repositories after their business is completed—a rule especially explicit in Willis.

What Fludd does not provide is a single worked demonstration taking a long speech from initial encoding through complete celestial placement to recitation. The surviving exposition is a toolkit plus partial examples, not a transparent modern manual.

### 17. Alphabetical and visual alphabets

**[TEXT]** Fludd supplies alphabetical lists of persons, mythological figures, virtues, vices, objects, and actions from which suitable images can be selected.

**[TEXT/MATERIAL]** He also gives visual alphabets: bodies, tools, or objects whose forms resemble letters. Such alphabets permit a name or word to be spelled pictorially.

**[HISTORICAL]** This is not a Fluddian invention. Visual alphabets occur in the line represented by Jacobus Publicius, Johannes Romberch, Cosmas Rossellius, and their successors.

### 18. Memorial arithmetic

**[TEXT/MATERIAL]** Tomus primus, tractatus II, pp. 153 ff., *De Arithmetica Memoriali*, assigns images to numbers and demonstrates their placement in loci. This belongs to the old practice of converting numbers into recognizable shapes, objects, or ordered figures.

**[TEXT]** Ramon Llull appears at II.2, p. 68 as an image signifying alchemy.

**[CAUTION]** The appearance of “Lull” as a person-image is not evidence that Fludd’s theatre is technically Lullian. Yates explicitly observed that Fludd does not operate with the characteristic Lullian alphabet, dignities, definitions, and combinatory figures.

### 19. The three-worlds diagram and the *mundus imaginabilis*

**[MATERIAL]** In the later psychological discussion, pp. 205 ff., Fludd diagrams a human being receiving the three worlds:

1. the sensible or elemental world enters through the five senses;
2. impressions become images or *umbrae* in the *mundus imaginabilis*;
3. *mens* rises to the intellectual world, angelic hierarchies, and Trinity;
4. memory, conventionally placed toward the rear of the head, receives and retains the resulting forms.

**[TEXT]** The imaginative world contains reflected zodiacal and stellar images.

**[YATES]** Yates read this as the theoretical key to the theatre: the microcosm can reproduce the macrocosm because humanity contains its image.

**[QUALIFICATION]** The diagram demonstrates Fludd’s cosmological psychology. It does not by itself prove that every theatre exercise was intended as a theurgic ascent.

## Willis’s *Mnemonica*

### 1. Bibliographical sequence

**[MATERIAL]**

- *Mnemonica; sive reminiscendi ars: e puris artis naturaeque fontibus hausta, et in tres libros digesta* (London: Humphrey Lownes for Nathaniel Browne, 1618).
- *The Art of Memory* (London: William Jones for Henry Seile, 1621): an English rendering of Book III, not the whole Latin work.
- *Mnemonica: or, The Art of Memory* (London: sold by Leonard Sowerby, 1661): complete and augmented English translation, normally attributed to Sowerby.

The Folger catalogue dates Willis’s death to 1625 or 1628 with uncertainty. Modern biographical claims more precise than this require caution. [1618 Folger record](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/425293) and [1661 Folger record](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/489488)

### 2. Willis’s repositories

Willis defines a repository in the 1661 English version as an imagined fabric or building, artfully constructed in the form of a theatre. The diagram shows:

- a one-level rectangular room;
- its front omitted;
- a column dividing it into two portions;
- a stage or floor;
- lettered positions.

Sets of these repositories can be assigned different substances or colours: gold, silver, stone, wood, and so forth.

**[TEXT]** Every deposited image should acquire a feature matching its repository. In the golden repository, the mnemonic cue for investigating seed-wheat prices is:

> “a country man clad in russet, with a paire of high shooes on, pouring wheate out of a sacke into a bushell, the eares or handles whereof are of pure gold.”

The golden handles bind the image to the golden repository.

The next task, hiring mowers, is represented by several husbandmen sharpening scythes whose blades are gold. The shared stage fixes their positional relationship.

### 3. Practical character

**[TEXT]** Willis openly concedes that writing is the most dependable guardian of memoranda. Artificial memory is useful when paper, ink, or table-books are unavailable.

His method handles:

- errands;
- market business;
- speeches;
- propositions and arguments;
- names and words;
- ordered lists.

It also contains an art of clearing:

> “This is not unlike expunging writing out of Table-books: If therefore there be any Art of Oblivion … it may be properly referred hither.”

**[TEXT]** Items acquired by day should be deposited or discharged before sleep, and those acquired at night immediately after sleep.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Recent work stresses that Willis mixes classical places-and-images with Ramist attention to division, logical order, and the principal parts of a discourse. He is neither simply a medieval image-mnemonic author nor an occult philosopher. The 2016 critical anthology notes his use of emblems, imprese, commonplace books, realistic detail, and familiar reminders such as tying a ribbon around a finger. [Cambridge critical anthology entry](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/memory-arts-in-renaissance-england/john-willis-the-art-of-memory-1621-1661/3F002FC4522947F47AC6C533F5F28706)

### 4. Did Willis influence Fludd?

**[YATES]** Yates first made the comparison systematic. Her evidence was:

- Willis appeared in London in 1618, one year before Fludd’s printed volume;
- both use sets of “theatres”;
- both use columns and room-like stages;
- both emphasize colours;
- Willis has day/night deposition; Fludd has eastern/day and western/night theatres.

She carefully wrote “possibly,” not “certainly.”

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** Willis is chronologically possible as an immediate printed stimulus.

**[DISPUTED]** The case is not proved:

- Fludd states that he practiced and taught memory around 1600–1602, long before Willis’s 1618 printing.
- The word *theatrum* and theatre-shaped repositories existed in earlier encyclopaedic and mnemonic traditions.
- Fludd’s celestial framework is independently intelligible through astrology, cosmology, Vitruvius, and Renaissance *theatrum mundi* imagery.
- No citation of Willis by Fludd has been identified.
- Publication dates do not reveal when Fludd composed the section or supplied its drawings.
- The newly recovered manuscript evidence makes the composition chronology a fresh research problem.

The correct formulation is therefore: Willis is a plausible immediate analogue and possible source for some architectural vocabulary, not the demonstrated origin of Fludd’s art.

## The sources

### Fludd: early printing

**[MATERIAL]** The relevant volume is:

Robert Fludd, *De Supernaturali, Naturali, Praeternaturali et Contranaturali Microcosmi Historia*, the second tome of *Utriusque Cosmi … Historia* (Oppenheim: Johann Theodor de Bry, 1619).

A digitized Bavarian State Library copy is catalogued as 277 pages. [Google Books record and scan](https://books.google.com/books/about/De_Supernaturali_Naturali_Praeternatural.html?id=iTNZAAAAcAAJ)

Another digitized copy is available under Google Books identifier EWRmtEvtkeIC. [Second copy](https://books.google.com/books?id=EWRmtEvtkeIC)

The Frankfurt copy cited for the *Oculus imaginationis* is Universitätsbibliothek Johann Christian Senckenberg, Occ. 15, Bd. 2.1.

### The printer’s manuscript

**[MATERIAL]** Ute Frietsch’s recent work identifies Universitätsbibliothek Johann Christian Senckenberg Frankfurt am Main, **MS lat. qu. 15**, as the rediscovered lavishly illustrated printer’s or master copy for *De technica microcosmi historia*. [Frietsch, “Robert Fludd’s Visual and Artisanal Episteme”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00026980.2022.2133809)

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Comparison of manuscript drawings and published engravings indicates that Fludd was not merely a passive author to whom the De Bry shop added arbitrary images. He supplied drawings, geometrical schemes, or detailed visual instructions, while engravers translated them into printable form.

**[QUALIFICATION]** MS lat. qu. 15 is specifically a production witness for *De technica microcosmi historia*. It should not be casually described as the autograph manuscript of every portion of the 1619 tome. Direct collation is still required to determine which *Ars memoriae* designs have manuscript antecedents in that codex or related materials.

### Engravers and designers

Matthäus Merian the Elder is associated with major plates in the De Bry production. “G.B.” and “H.T.” occur in older Fludd bibliography as shorthand for the joint study by Joscelyn Godwin, Robert Fludd’s visual corpus, and related theatre scholarship; they are not signatures proving that one person designed every plate.

Recent manuscript work shifts the question from “Fludd or engraver?” to collaboration among:

- Fludd as author, draftsman, and philosophical designer;
- amanuenses;
- Johann Theodor de Bry as printer-publisher;
- professional engravers including Merian.

### Willis witnesses

The 1618 Latin edition survives in, among other holdings, the Bavarian State Library copy digitized by Google and copies represented in EEBO/Folger catalogues. [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Mnemonica_Sive_Reminiscendi_Ars.html?id=_0JQAAAAcAAJ)

### Variants

Documented variants include:

- Latin 1618 versus partial English 1621 versus full augmented English 1661 in Willis;
- orthographic modernization and substantive expansion in the 1661 translation;
- a passage elaborated in 1621 but not retained in the same form in later versions, noted by the Cambridge anthology;
- differences between Fludd’s manuscript drawings and the engraved printed realizations;
- differing impressions and binding arrangements in composite copies of Fludd’s multi-part work.

No critical edition collating every surviving Fludd copy and every state of the memory plates has been identified. Claims about plate “variants” must therefore be made copy by copy.

## Yates’s account and scholarship since

### 1. What Yates established

Yates’s enduring contributions were:

- identifying the memory section as a coherent system rather than decorative occult miscellany;
- showing its descent from classical places and images;
- distinguishing round and square components;
- placing the images within Fludd’s macrocosm–microcosm psychology;
- recognizing the relation between the zodiac diagram and theatre engraving;
- recovering Willis as a close English parallel;
- linking the system comparatively with Camillo and Bruno;
- insisting that diagrams be read together with the prose.

Those results remain foundational.

### 2. Yates’s strongest interpretations

Yates argued that:

1. the round art is magical and talismanic;
2. the celestial order activates the theatres;
3. Fludd follows Bruno’s occult memory systems;
4. twenty-four theatres probably surround the zodiac;
5. Willis may be the immediate mundane source that Fludd “occultised”;
6. the large theatre engraving preserves factual information about Shakespeare’s Globe;
7. the entire system may resemble a Hermetic “Seal” or secret code;
8. the Hermetic current reaching Fludd may have come more through John Dee than directly through Bruno.

Of these, 1–3 have substantial textual or comparative support but require qualification. Items 4–8 are reconstructions of sharply varying probability.

### 3. The Globe controversy

Yates’s most famous conclusion was categorical:

> “this system contains, as a secret hidden within it, factual information about the Globe Theatre.”

She developed the claim in *Theatre of the World* and in public exchanges after 1966.

**[DISPUTED]** Glynne Wickham objected that the engraving is unlocalized and could resemble the Fortune, Red Bull, Blackfriars, a tiltyard, or a generic scenic structure as readily as the Globe. Herbert Berry, Wylie Sypher, I. A. Shapiro, Richard Bernheimer, and others offered different readings. [Wickham–Yates exchange](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1966/11/17/the-globe-theater/) and [Sypher–Yates exchange](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1970/03/12/theatre-of-the-world/)

Yates replied that:

- Fludd explicitly compared the repository to a public theatre;
- his rule demanded a real rather than fictitious place;
- the five entrances agree between text and image;
- the stage arrangement should be tested against Shakespearean blocking, especially *Hamlet*.

**[CURRENT ASSESSMENT]** The engraving is valuable evidence for an early-seventeenth-century conception of a public-theatre-like stage. It is not secure documentary evidence of the Globe’s actual tiring-house façade. Frietsch calls Yates’s identification “concretistic” and says it has rightly been rejected. [Frietsch 2024](https://gal-studies.museogalileo.it/index.php/galilaeana/article/download/46/40/689)

### 4. Twenty-four theatres

**[YATES]** The twenty-four-theatre model comes from placing an eastern and western pair at each sign by analogy with the two figures beside Aries.

**[CORRECTION]** Fludd does not enumerate twenty-four theatres. The tiny Aries theatres are not subsequently explained. Modern diagrams showing a complete ring of twenty-four stages are reconstructions.

### 5. “Seal” and secret society

**[YATES]** Yates called Fludd’s system a Brunian “Seal” and asked whether it encoded a Hermetic sect.

**[CORRECTION]** Fludd does not call it a seal, and no key to a sectarian cipher survives. The question reflects the mid-twentieth-century “Hermetic tradition” framework and the proximity of Fludd’s Rosicrucian apologetics. It is not a documented disclosure.

### 6. Bruno

Yates was right that Bruno and Fludd share:

- celestial ordering;
- mythological and stellar imagery;
- architectural fields or rooms;
- a macrocosmic aspiration;
- the language of shadows and imagination.

But direct dependence is not demonstrated. Fludd does not cite Bruno in the memory treatise, and his Trinitarian, angelological, Paracelsian, and musical cosmology differs substantially from Bruno’s.

Later Bruno scholarship by Rita Sturlese, Michele Ciliberto, Stephen Clucas, and Hilary Gatti has generally replaced the picture of a single “Hermetic magician” with more textually differentiated accounts of Bruno’s logic, ontology, psychology, anti-Aristotelianism, and religious positions. The consequence for Fludd is methodological: formal resemblance is insufficient proof of transmission.

### 7. Camillo

Yates constructed a grand succession:

> Camillo’s theatre → Bruno’s seals → Fludd’s theatre system.

Lina Bolzoni and later Camillo scholarship, including work associated with Stagno, recover Camillo’s theatre more precisely from manuscripts, rhetorical culture, Venetian patronage, scriptural interpretation, and the surviving *Idea del theatro*. This confirms that cosmic theatres could organize invention and knowledge, but weakens the idea of a single secret occult lineage.

No evidence shows Fludd seeing Camillo’s physical theatre. His travels in France made reports or texts available, but this remains conjectural.

### 8. Carruthers, Small, Rossi, Ong, Hajdu, Sabbatino

Mary Carruthers’s *The Book of Memory* recast medieval memoria as an art of composition, ethical formation, and invention—not a passive storehouse. That corrects any reading of Fludd’s actors as mere labels. Images are instruments for making thought.

Jocelyn Penny Small’s *Wax Tablets of the Mind* emphasizes differences between ancient oral, scribal, and visual practices and warns against treating every architectural metaphor as an identical “memory palace.”

Paolo Rossi’s *Clavis universalis*—translated as *Logic and the Art of Memory*—places mnemonic images alongside encyclopaedism, combinatorics, universal language, Bacon, Descartes, and Leibniz. This broadens the history beyond Yates’s dominant Hermetic plot. [Publisher’s description](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/logic-and-the-art-of-memory-9780826488619/)

Walter Ong’s *Ramus, Method, and the Decay of Dialogue* clarifies why seventeenth-century mnemonic works increasingly competed or merged with dichotomous logical order. Willis displays that mixed inheritance.

Ignace Hajdu’s older study of medieval memory is valuable as a bibliographical and doctrinal survey but predates the manuscript-sensitive work of Carruthers and Bolzoni.

Sabbatino’s work on Renaissance mnemonic culture helps locate memory arts within rhetoric, poetics, and literary invention rather than treating them as an isolated esoteric survival.

### 9. Recent correction: Fludd as image-maker

Frietsch’s 2022 and 2024 research is the most important recent development specifically for Fludd. Manuscript evidence supports the view that Fludd possessed drawing and design skills and organized his thought visually. The plates are therefore more securely Fluddian in conception than an older author-versus-engraver binary allowed.

At the same time, Frietsch rejects the Globe identification and distinguishes more carefully between two round theatres for spiritual themes and a square theatre for sensible material. Her analysis interprets round and square forms as part of Fludd’s epistemic and artistic practice, not merely an occult filing cabinet.

## History and context

### Classical rhetoric

The technical foundation is the ancient rule:

> *Constat igitur artificiosa memoria ex locis et imaginibus.*

“Artificial memory therefore consists of places and images.”

The fullest surviving ancient Latin treatment is *Rhetorica ad Herennium* III.16–24. Cicero discusses the method in *De oratore* II.350–360; Quintilian treats and questions it in *Institutio oratoria* XI.2.

The *Ad Herennium* lawsuit image encodes witnesses, inheritance, and poisoning by placing a sick man in bed, the accused nearby holding a cup in one hand, tablets in the other, and a ram’s testicles on a finger. The objects cue *venenum*, *testamentum*, and *testes*. This is an ancestor and comparative example, not one reproduced by Fludd.

### Scholastic psychology

Fludd’s head diagrams continue the internal-senses tradition:

- common sense receives sensory forms;
- imagination retains or recombines them;
- estimation or cogitation interprets;
- memory preserves intentions and images.

Fludd modifies this inheritance through Renaissance Neoplatonism, Hermetic macrocosm–microcosm theory, Paracelsian spirits, and Christian angelology.

### Medieval ethical memory

Dominican and Franciscan teaching made memory serve preaching, confession, moral choice, and meditation. Virtues, vices, rewards, punishments, Heaven, and Hell became memorable image systems. Fludd’s virtues, vices, angels, and Last Judgment remain continuous with that tradition even when inserted into a new cosmology.

### Lullism

Ramon Llull’s authentic art combines divine dignities and relational principles through alphabets, definitions, figures, and controlled combinations. Its purpose is demonstrative and missionary argument, not primarily the storage of arbitrary items.

Anthony Bonner and Alexander Fidora’s scholarship makes it unsafe to label every medieval or Renaissance wheel “Lullian.” Fludd’s zodiac is rotational and combinatorial in a broad sense, but his theatre lacks the defining logical machinery of Llull’s *Ars*. Yates was right to deny technical Lullism here.

### Hermeticism and astrology

Fludd invokes a hierarchy in which the elemental world images the celestial, the celestial images the intellectual, and the human microcosm can internalize all three. This is the intellectual setting for the round art.

The historical *Corpus Hermeticum* was composed in late antiquity, not in primeval Egypt. Isaac Casaubon’s 1614 dating critique was already available when Fludd’s volumes appeared, although it did not instantly dissolve Hermetic authority.

### Ramism

Ramist method transfers much of memoria into orderly disposition: if a discourse is correctly divided, its structure helps recall. Willis’s respect for logical arrangement and principal divisions coexists with theatrical imagery. Fludd preserves a more richly imagistic cosmology.

### Courts, universities, and print

Fludd’s art belongs simultaneously to:

- noble household instruction in France;
- the learned physician’s study;
- continental luxury printing;
- Stuart courtly and Rosicrucian debate;
- university-trained rhetoric, medicine, and natural philosophy.

The first macrocosmic volume was dedicated to James I. The 1619 microcosmic volume’s dedication is directed to God. Yates used the royal dedication and James’s patronage of the King’s Men as circumstantial support for the Globe theory; it is not proof.

## People

### Robert Fludd

Oxford-educated physician, Fellow of the College of Physicians, Paracelsian natural philosopher, cosmologist, polemicist, defender of the Rosicrucian manifestos, and designer of elaborate diagrams.

### John Willis

Clergyman, shorthand writer, and mnemonic author. His repositories are practical and comparatively sober. There is no evidence that he intended Fluddian astral magic.

### The Duke of Guise and his brother

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** Fludd states that he taught noblemen identified through his travel narrative with the Duke of Guise and his brother. It is a first-person report, but no independent pupil’s account of the lessons is known.

### Johann Theodor de Bry and Matthäus Merian

Printer-publisher and engraver responsible for translating Fludd’s unusually elaborate visual programme into print. Fludd said that continental publication spared him the enormous English cost of copperplates.

### Marin Mersenne

Critic of Fludd’s macrocosm–microcosm doctrine, Hermetic authorities, magic, and biblical interpretation. His *Quaestiones celeberrimae in Genesim* (1623), cols. 1746 and 1749, attacked the allegedly Egyptian foundations of Fludd’s system.

### Johannes Kepler

Criticized Fludd’s cosmological harmonies and diagrams as inadequate demonstrations. The dispute concerned mathematics, music, cosmology, and modes of proof more directly than the mnemonic theatre itself.

### Pierre Gassendi

Extended the critique of Fludd’s occult natural philosophy in the *Epistolica exercitatio* of 1628.

### William Foster

Accused Fludd of publishing overseas because English universities and bishops would not permit magical books. Fludd answered that English printers demanded £500 for the book and copperplates, whereas it was printed abroad without cost to him. This is documented polemic, not proof that ecclesiastical authorities formally banned the *Ars memoriae*.

### Alleged anecdotes

The requested auctioneer, professor’s party, and student who lost his memory do not appear in the verified Fludd or Willis passages examined here. Comparable stories circulate throughout mnemonic literature and demonstrations by itinerant teachers, especially around Lambert Schenkel and later Feinaigle. Assigning them to Fludd without a primary citation would be false.

No documented case has been located of Fludd using his theatre to perform an auctioneer’s inventory, reconstruct a professor’s party, or cure a student who had lost his memory.

## The magic question

### Was it magical?

The answer depends on what “magic” means.

**[TEXT: unequivocal]**

- Fludd’s round art includes angels, demons, stellar effigies, pagan divine images, and virtues and vices rendered as shadows.
- It depends on celestial and metaphysical relations.
- Fludd says daemonic power or the Holy Spirit may assist its higher operation.
- His broader philosophy openly defends legitimate natural magic, Cabala, alchemy, astrology, and Rosicrucian wisdom.

**[TEXT: not established]**

- The memory section gives no surviving ritual of planetary consecration.
- It prescribes no sacrifice, fumigation, incantation, or spirit-conjuration.
- It does not provide physical talismans to manufacture.
- No Inquisition process against Fludd for this memory system is known.
- Willis’s repositories are not explicitly magical.

Consequently, “magical” is historically justified if it means a Christian natural-magical and metaphysical theory of images and correspondences. “Ceremonial demon-conjuring system” is unsupported.

### Daemons and demons

Renaissance *daemon* could signify an intermediary spirit rather than exclusively a devil in Hell. Yates explicitly distinguished Fludd’s daemonic powers from infernal demons. Yet the distinction did not neutralize controversy: Protestant and Catholic critics could treat invocation of non-divine spirits as illicit.

### The Toulouse art and Solomon’s ring

**[TEXT]** Fludd reports hearing at Toulouse of an art associated with Solomon’s ring.

**[YATES]** Yates suggested that it sounded like the *Ars notoria* and might be connected with Jean Belot.

**[DISPUTED]** Neither identification is demonstrated. “Solomon’s ring” belongs to a large field of Solomonic magical legends and texts.

### *Ars notoria*

The medieval *Ars notoria* promised accelerated acquisition of knowledge or memory through prayers, divine names, ritual observance, and inspection of complex figures or *notae*. It was condemned or suspected because angelic revelation could be reclassified as demonic deception.

Fludd’s theatre differs fundamentally:

- it uses classical loci and images;
- it possesses an explicit psychological and cosmological explanation;
- it lacks the *Ars notoria*’s ritual prayer cycles and notae.

The traditions intersect in the hope for divinely assisted cognition, but they are not the same system.

### Heresy and consequences

Mersenne accused Fludd of dangerous or impious magic and attacked his Hermetic premises. Foster insinuated that his books were unacceptable to English ecclesiastical authorities. Libavius attacked Rosicrucian claims as heretical, magical, and seditious; Fludd defended them in 1616–1617.

No evidence shows that the theatre memory chapter itself produced a prosecution, conviction, imprisonment, or professional expulsion. Fludd continued medical practice and publication.

### Was Fludd a Rosicrucian?

**[TEXT]** He publicly defended the manifestos and represented true Rosicrucian wisdom as Christian.

**[DISPUTED]** Membership in a demonstrably organized fraternity has not been proven. “Self-identification as a Rosicrucian” should be qualified: he identified himself with the cause and defended the Brothers, but that is not documentary proof of initiation into an institution.

### The “accused magician”

This requested anecdotal category has a genuine referent: Fludd himself was called a magician, heretical magician, or defender of magic by Mersenne, Foster, and other opponents. The consequence was an extended print controversy, not a known criminal proceeding.

## Afterlife

### Seventeenth-century mnemonic manuals

Willis’s tradition continued through:

- Henry Herdson;
- Richard Saunders;
- Marius d’Assigny;
- extracts reproduced by Gregor von Feinaigle in 1813.

Belot’s French art, reprinted at Lyon in 1654, mentioned Llull, Agrippa, and Bruno. Saunders adapted Belot and dedicated his work to Elias Ashmole. This is a genuine line of textual reception, although not proof that Fludd’s complete celestial theatre was practiced.

### Scientific method and universal language

Rossi’s history shows how the mnemonic ideal of complete ordered knowledge was transformed into:

- classification;
- encyclopaedic tables;
- combinatorial logic;
- “real character” and universal-language projects;
- Baconian natural histories;
- Leibnizian combinations and calculus.

The lineage “Llull → Leibniz → computer” is useful only with qualifications. Llull devised rule-bound symbolic combinations; Leibniz knew and transformed combinatorial traditions; modern computers perform formal operations. No continuous technical genealogy leads from Fludd’s theatres to programmable computers.

### Descartes and Bacon

Bacon criticized uncontrolled similarities and macrocosmic correspondences while retaining the importance of ordered histories, tables, and aids to inquiry. Descartes considered mnemonic images most useful when their causal relation to what is remembered is intelligible. These developments relocate memory from cosmic sympathy toward method and analysis.

### Literature and visual art

Possible or comparative afterlives include:

- Dante’s architectonic moral imagery;
- medieval Last Judgment and virtue–vice cycles;
- Renaissance emblem books;
- theatrical metaphors of the world;
- Shakespearean drama and its staging of recollection;
- Borges’s “Funes the Memorious”;
- Umberto Eco’s labyrinthine libraries and encyclopaedic semiotics;
- Aby Warburg’s *Mnemosyne Atlas*;
- installation art and reconstructed memory theatres.

Except where an author cites Fludd, these are affinities or later appropriations, not proven direct influence.

Frietsch specifically argues that Fludd’s hermetic visual organization has become an artistic subject and method in modern and contemporary art.

### Modern memory-palace revival

Contemporary memory athletes use:

- stable routes;
- distinctive loci;
- bizarre active images;
- person–action–object encodings;
- numerical image systems.

These practices strongly resemble the square art and Willis’s repositories. They normally omit Fludd’s planetary metaphysics.

A modern app that presents twenty-four virtual zodiac theatres as “Fludd’s exact system” is therefore a reconstruction. It may be useful, but it should not be marketed as a complete recovered historical algorithm.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Did Fludd invent the theatre system?

No single answer fits.

- **[TEXT]** Fludd says he learned an art in France and subsequently perfected and taught it.
- **[HISTORICAL]** Theatre-shaped and room-shaped loci existed before him.
- **[RECONSTRUCTION]** His distinctive contribution is their integration with his round/square cosmology and visual encyclopaedia.
- **[DISPUTED]** Willis as sole source is unsupported.

### 2. Was the system derived from Bruno?

- Formal correspondences are substantial.
- Direct citation or documentary transmission is lacking.
- Fludd’s own account points to France, while Yates also proposed an English line through Dee and Alexander Dicson.
- “Brunian type” is safer than “copied from Bruno.”

### 3. Is it Lullian?

Not in its technical operation. The presence of circles, combinations, or an image of Llull does not establish Lullism. Bonner and Fidora’s criteria for Llull’s art make this correction particularly important.

### 4. Are there twenty-four theatres?

Possibly, but the number is inferred, not stated. The only explicitly visualized celestial pair stands beside Aries.

### 5. Are the theatres physically real?

Fludd demands sensory foundations for square places. That does not entail that each engraved structure is an architectural survey. “Real-place method” and “literal portrait of the Globe” are different propositions.

### 6. Is the engraving the Globe?

The identification is Yates’s celebrated conjecture and is now generally rejected as unproved. It may preserve features available in contemporary English stages, but no label, provenance, measurement, or independent witness identifies the Globe.

### 7. Is *THEATRUM ORBI* intentional?

Unknown. Yates’s dative reading is possible but not decisive. Scribal or engraver error remains possible.

### 8. Did Fludd’s publishers invent the pictures?

Recent manuscript evidence argues against this. Fludd appears to have supplied or closely supervised visual designs, which De Bry’s workshop and Merian transformed professionally.

### 9. Is the system a talisman?

The round art contains astrologically potent images and supernatural language. The whole printed system is not shown to be a physically consecrated talisman. “Memory Seal” is Yates’s Brunian analogy.

### 10. Was the system a Rosicrucian code?

No key, initiate testimony, or contemporary decoding establishes this. Fludd’s Rosicrucian commitments explain why Yates asked the question, but do not answer it.

### 11. Did it actually work?

Willis’s simpler system is operationally intelligible and conforms to cognitive principles of spatial cueing, elaboration, and distinctiveness. Fludd’s doors, columns, colours, and active images could work similarly. No controlled historical performance or diary documents successful use of the complete celestial apparatus.

### 12. What Yates got wrong or overstated

The strongest corrections are:

- she converted a possible twenty-four-theatre arrangement into the dominant picture of the system;
- she called it a “Seal,” though Fludd did not;
- she treated the Globe identification as an “extraordinary fact” despite insufficient evidence;
- she sometimes collapsed celestial correspondence, mnemonic vividness, and ritual talismanic magic;
- she inferred a direct Bruno-like occultization where several independent traditions could explain the features;
- her synthetic “Hermetic tradition” can obscure differences among late-antique Hermetica, Ficinian magic, Cabala, Llullism, Paracelsianism, Bruno, Dee, Rosicrucian apologetics, and Fludd.

What she got right remains considerable: the system’s cosmological ambition, its classical technical substrate, the centrality of imagination, and the need to interpret text and image together.

### 13. Legend versus documented history

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Fludd learned memory in southern France | First-person textual report |
| He taught the Duke of Guise | First-person report correlated with travel chronology |
| Willis inspired Fludd | Plausible hypothesis |
| Fludd copied Willis | Unproved |
| Twenty-four theatres surround the zodiac | Yatesian reconstruction |
| The engraving depicts the Globe | Disputed and generally rejected |
| It is a Rosicrucian cipher | Speculation |
| Fludd practiced demon-conjuration through it | Unsupported |
| Fludd was accused of magic | Documented polemic |
| The English authorities banned this book | Unsupported; Fludd gave a financial printing explanation |
| Fludd personally conceived many illustrations | Strengthened by manuscript evidence |
| Modern memory athletes practice Fludd’s system | Usually false; they practice related loci methods |
| Fludd anticipated digital computers | Modern analogy, not a technical genealogy |

## Open questions

1. When precisely was the *Ars memoriae* section drafted relative to Willis’s 1618 publication?
2. Do manuscript witnesses preserve earlier versions of the theatre diagrams?
3. Can the two tiny theatres beside Aries be explained through a lost caption, an astrological convention, or the printer’s layout?
4. Does *THEATRUM ORBI* reflect Fludd’s grammar, an engraver’s transcription, or an omitted abbreviation?
5. What exact sequence did Fludd intend for traversing doors and columns?
6. Were eastern and western theatres duplicated for all twelve signs?
7. Did “real places” mean one personally observed theatre, a composite based on several buildings, or merely geometrically credible places?
8. Can Fludd’s Medea, Circe, and planetary lists be traced to a particular mythographic or astrological handbook?
9. How much of the system predates Fludd’s French journey, and what teachers or manuscript arts did he encounter there?
10. Is Willis’s terminology indebted to Romberch, Rossellius, Schenkel, or another continental source?
11. Did any seventeenth-century reader leave annotations showing actual use of Fludd’s theatres?
12. Are there state differences among surviving impressions of the theatre plates?
13. How should *daemon* be translated in each passage without erasing Fludd’s Christian metaphysics or importing modern demonology?
14. Can the alleged link through John Dee be documented rather than inferred from broad philosophical similarity?
15. What parts of Yates’s reconstruction entered modern museum displays, theatre reconstructions, occult manuals, and memory-training websites as if they were Fludd’s own instructions?

What remains uncertain is precisely the part made most famous by Yates—the ring of twenty-four theatres, Willis as immediate source, the Brunian “Seal,” the secret society, and above all the Globe.

# The alternative Hebrew methods: mispar gadol, katan, siduri, kidmi, meruba, milui, and the rest: Research Dossier

## Basic identification

**Primary name:** גימטריא, *gimṭriyā* or *gemaṭria*  
**Script:** Hebrew square script; historically Hebrew and Jewish Aramaic  
**Core period:** securely documented as an exegetical technique in early rabbinic literature, approximately the second–third centuries CE; based on alphabetic numerals attested among Jews in the late Second Temple period  
**Primary regions:** Roman Palestine and Babylonia; later Provence, northern France, Spain, Italy, Safed, eastern Europe, North Africa, and the modern Jewish diaspora  
**Related traditions:** Greek ἰσοψηφία (*isopsēphia*), Syriac letter-numbers, Arabic and Persian حساب الجمل (*ḥisāb al-jummal*) or abjad, Christian Cabala, Hermetic Qabalah, and modern Western numerology

### Evidentiary labels used below

- **Documented text:** directly present in a surviving work or inscription.
- **Scholarly reconstruction:** a historical conclusion inferred from surviving evidence.
- **Tradition:** an attribution or interpretation transmitted within a religious or esoteric community.
- **Disputed:** supported by some scholars but challenged on dating, method, or probability.
- **Legend:** narrated as history without adequate contemporary evidence.
- **Modern invention:** a demonstrably recent system subsequently advertised as ancient.

This is **מספר הכרחי**, *mispar hekhreḥi*, “necessary/absolute number,” also called ordinary, normative, or standard gematria. “Mispar ha-panim,” the “front” value, sometimes appears as a contrast to cumulative or “back” values.

The system is decimal in structure: 1–9, then 10–90, then 100–400. This is not an arbitrary mystical table invented independently of writing numbers: it rests on the use of alphabetic letters as numerals. Greek and Semitic alphabetic numeral systems filled the missing hundreds with archaic or additional signs; Hebrew normally stopped at ת = 400 and expressed higher numbers additively.

### General rules

1. **Consonantal letters count.** Masoretic vowel points, cantillation marks, punctuation, and modern typographical signs do not.
2. **Final letters normally equal their medial forms:** ך=כ=20, ם=מ=40, ן=נ=50, ף=פ=80, ץ=צ=90.
3. **In mispar gadol only**, the finals may instead continue the hundreds: ך 500, ם 600, ן 700, ף 800, ץ 900.
4. A word is summed letter by letter. Spaces normally contribute nothing; a phrase is the sum of its words.
5. Orthography matters. מלא and חסר spellings—plene and defective writing—can change a result.
6. Matres lectionis such as ו and י count whenever physically written.
7. Scribal ligatures normally count as their component letters. There is no canonical special value for a ligature.
8. Equality of totals is only **numerical equivalence**, not linguistic identity. Traditional authors may treat it as a hint, mnemonic, homiletical connection, or mystical correspondence; it is not normally a rule of Hebrew semantics.
9. A calculation is reproducible only when the precise spelling, textual edition, method, treatment of finals, and any added constants are stated.

## The alternative Hebrew methods

The terminology is not perfectly stable. Modern charts often consolidate procedures gathered from different centuries and authors into a supposedly timeless list. Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim*, gate 30, is a major sixteenth-century attempt to classify letter operations, but it does not validate every name found on present-day calculator sites.

### 1. Mispar gadol — מספר גדול, “great number”

Two usages must be distinguished.

#### A. Extended final-letter values

| Form | ך | ם | ן | ף | ץ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mispar gadol | 500 | 600 | 700 | 800 | 900 |

All other letters retain standard values. Thus מלך:

- ordinary: מ 40 + ל 30 + ך 20 = **90**
- extended-gadol: 40 + 30 + 500 = **570**

**Status:** documented as a traditional extension, but not the normal rule in biblical or Talmudic gematria. The label is unstable: some later manuals also call full spelling, *milui*, “mispar gadol.”

#### B. “Large” or full spelling

Some sources use “gadol” for valuing a letter by the spelling of its name. That is better called *mispar shemi* or *otiot be-milui*. Conflating these two operations produces incompatible tables.

### 2. Mispar katan — מספר קטן, “small number”

Each standard letter value is reduced by deleting place-value zeroes:

- א–ט = 1–9
- י–צ = 1–9
- ק–ת = 1–4

Thus אמת:

- א = 1
- מ = 4
- ת = 4
- total = **9**

This differs from reducing only the final word-total. It is best described as a digit-root value assigned **per letter**, though mathematically the ultimate digital root is usually congruent modulo nine.

**Status:** documented traditional technique; the *Encyclopaedia Judaica* lists it alongside the standard and ordinal procedures. Individual medieval occurrences need to be dated case by case rather than projected into the Talmud.

### 3. Mispar katan mispari — מספר קטן מספרי

First calculate the whole word by ordinary gematria, then repeatedly sum its decimal digits until one digit remains, conventionally retaining 9 rather than 0 for positive multiples of nine.

Example, משיח:

- מ 40 + ש 300 + י 10 + ח 8 = 358
- 3 + 5 + 8 = 16
- 1 + 6 = **7**

**Status:** described in modern editions of the *Encyclopaedia Judaica* and later manuals. Its exact named pedigree is much less securely documented than ordinary gematria.

### 4. Mispar siduri — מספר סידורי, “ordinal number”

The letters receive positions 1–22. Finals normally equal the positions of their ordinary forms.

For מלך:

- מ = 13
- ל = 12
- ך/כ = 11
- total = **36**

**Status:** a genuine traditional alternative, but much less prominent in classical rabbinic exegesis. Claims that it was a standard biblical compositional cipher are disputed unless an ancient witness independently signals it.

### 5. Mispar kidmi — מספר קדמי, “preceding” or cumulative number

Each letter receives the sum of standard values from א through that letter:

\[
K(L_n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n} H(L_i)
\]

Selected values:

| Letter | Calculation | Kidmi value |
|---|---|---:|
| א | 1 | 1 |
| ב | 1+2 | 3 |
| ג | 1+2+3 | 6 |
| ט | 1+…+9 | 45 |
| י | 45+10 | 55 |
| כ | 55+20 | 75 |
| ל | 75+30 | 105 |
| מ | 105+40 | 145 |
| ת | all standard values | 1,495 |

It is sometimes loosely called “triangular,” but only the א–ט portion consists of ordinary triangular numbers; thereafter the increments are 10, 20, … rather than consecutive integers.

A modern alternative usage calls cumulative spelling of a word “kidmi” or “achor”: ABC becomes A + AB + ABC. These definitions must not be silently combined.

**Status:** traditional/later kabbalistic classification; no convincing evidence located that the named method existed in biblical or early rabbinic times. Modern sites often repeat it without giving a manuscript or first printed witness.

### 6. Mispar perati — מספר פרטי, and mispar meruba — מספר מרובע

In common modern usage, each standard letter value is squared and the squares are summed:

\[
P(w)=\sum H(l)^2
\]

For אב:

- א² + ב² = 1² + 2² = **5**

For חי:

- ח² + י² = 8² + 10² = 64 + 100 = **164**

“Meruba,” “squared,” is sometimes used for the square of the **word’s total** instead:

\[
M(w)=\left(\sum H(l)\right)^2
\]

For אב, that gives (1+2)² = **9**, not 5. Consequently a claim saying only “mispar meruba” is under-specified.

**Status:** later kabbalistic or modern taxonomic terminology. The mathematical operation is clear; the names and definitions are not uniform. An ancient pedigree has not been demonstrated.

### 7. Mispar bone’eh — מספר בונה, “building number”

A word is “built” progressively:

\[
a_1+(a_1+a_2)+(a_1+a_2+a_3)+\cdots
\]

For אבג:

- א = 1
- אב = 1+2 = 3
- אבג = 1+2+3 = 6
- total = 1+3+6 = **10**

Equivalently, in a three-letter word the first letter is counted three times, the second twice, and the last once.

Some lists confuse *bone’eh* with *kidmi*. One accumulates positions within a word; the other usually accumulates the alphabetic table.

**Status:** found in later manuals and calculator taxonomies. A secure medieval first occurrence under precisely this name was not located in the sources consulted. Claims that it is “ancient” should therefore be treated as unproved.

### 8. Mispar shemi — מספר שמי, “name value,” and milui — מילוי, “filling”

Each letter is replaced by the written name of that letter, then the written letters are summed.

Examples:

- א, spelled אלף: 1 + 30 + 80 = **111**
- ב, commonly spelled בית: 2 + 10 + 400 = **412**
- ל, spelled למד: 30 + 40 + 4 = **74**

Alternative spellings matter: ו may be ואו, ויו, or וו; ה may be הא, הה, or הי. Accordingly there is no single universal milui table.

**Status:** well documented in kabbalistic literature. It is more securely rooted in medieval and early-modern letter mysticism than many of the calculator-era named variants.

### 9. Mispar ne’elam — מספר נעלם, “hidden number”

Spell the letter’s name but remove its initial letter—the visible letter itself—and count only the remainder:

- א → אלף → לף = 30+80 = **110**
- ל → למד → מד = 40+4 = **44**
- ז → זין → ין = 10+50 = **60**

This is also called the value of the “inner” letters, אותיות פנימיות.

A translated Lurianic example calculates the hidden filling of זה: זין minus ז leaves ין = 60; הה minus its first ה leaves ה = 5; total **65**, equal to אדני: 1+4+50+10 = 65. The presentation is documented in later Lurianic transmission, not in the biblical text itself. [Chabad translation of a classic Lurianic text](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/380716/jewish/Divine-Revelation-24.htm)

### 10. Mispar musafi — מספר מוספי, “additional number”

The ordinary value plus the number of letters:

\[
M(w)=H(w)+\operatorname{length}(w)
\]

For אהבה:

- ordinary value 1+5+2+5 = 13
- four letters
- musafi = 13+4 = **17**

A closely related homiletical convention adds one for the word as a whole, often expressed as עם הכולל, “with the collective/unit.” Thus יהוה = 26, or **27 with the kolel**.

**Status:** the operation exists in later homiletical practice, but the standardized label *mispar musafi* is especially prominent in modern lists. It should not be retrojected into the Talmud without a citation.

### 11. Mispar mispari — מספר מספרי

The numerical value of a letter is written as a Hebrew number-word, whose letters are then valued:

- א = 1 → אחד, “one” → 1+8+4 = **13**
- י = 10 → עשר, “ten” → 70+300+200 = **570**

Spellings and grammatical form make this method highly variable.

**Status:** later/modern systematization. It is reproducible only when the exact Hebrew number-word convention is specified.

### 12. Kolel and grammatical additions

Authors sometimes add:

- **1** for the word as a unit, עם הכולל;
- the number of letters;
- the number of words;
- one for each included concept.

These are documented homiletical techniques, but they enormously enlarge the space of possible matches. A claimed equation that works only “with the kolel” is not an exact ordinary-gematria equality.

### 13. Atbash, albam, and related alphabets

אתב״ש substitutes the first letter for the last, second for next-to-last, and so forth: א↔ת, ב↔ש. אלב״ם divides the alphabet into two halves: א↔ל, ב↔מ, and so on.

These are **letter-substitution ciphers**, or *temurah*, rather than numerical summation. Cordovero explicitly distinguishes permutation and substitution from ordinary gematria in *Pardes Rimmonim* 30.5. They belong in a separate cipher dossier, although kabbalists often combine them with numerical evaluation. [Pardes Rimmonim 30.5](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.30.5)

## The four filled Tetragrammata: 72, 63, 45, and 52

The ordinary Tetragrammaton is:

יהוה = י 10 + ה 5 + ו 6 + ה 5 = **26**

In Lurianic Kabbalah the letters are written in full with different spellings of ה and ו. The resulting totals are conventionally named by Hebrew numeral abbreviations:

### ע״ב, `AV, 72

יוד הי ויו הי

- יוד = 10+6+4 = 20
- הי = 5+10 = 15
- ויו = 6+10+6 = 22
- הי = 15
- total: 20+15+22+15 = **72**

### ס״ג, SaG, 63

יוד הי ואו הי

- יוד = 20
- הי = 15
- ואו = 6+1+6 = 13
- הי = 15
- total: 20+15+13+15 = **63**

### מ״ה, MaH, 45

יוד הא ואו הא

- יוד = 20
- הא = 5+1 = 6
- ואו = 13
- הא = 6
- total: 20+6+13+6 = **45**

### ב״ן, BaN, 52

יוד הה וו הה

- יוד = 20
- הה = 5+5 = 10
- וו = 6+6 = 12
- הה = 10
- total: 20+10+12+10 = **52**

**Documented tradition:** these four fillings are central to Lurianic cosmology and its later prayer-intentions. They are correlated with divine configurations, souls, gendered symbolism, and the four worlds—אצילות, בריאה, יצירה, עשיה—but mappings can change with context. It is unsafe to give a single rigid “72=World X” table as if it governed every Lurianic passage.

**Historical finding:** the mature system belongs to the circle of Isaac Luria (1534–1572), transmitted especially through Ḥayyim Vital. It is not found as such in the Bible, Talmud, or earliest strata of the Zohar. Calling all four fillings “Sinaitic” is a claim of sacred tradition, not a demonstrable date of composition.

## Classic worked examples

### Eliezer = 318

אליעזר:

- א 1
- ל 30
- י 10
- ע 70
- ז 7
- ר 200
- total **318**

Genesis 14:14 says Abraham mustered 318 retainers. **Documented rabbinic interpretation:** Nedarim 32a reports that Abraham went with Eliezer alone, whose numerical value is 318; Genesis Rabbah 43 likewise connects the number with Eliezer. Rashi transmits the interpretation on Genesis 14:14. [Nedarim 32a](https://www.sefaria.org/Nedarim.32a), [Rashi on Genesis 14:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Genesis.14.14), [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Eliezer”](https://jewishencyclopediadev.sefaria.org/articles/5597-eliezer)

A striking Christian parallel is *Epistle of Barnabas* 9:8: Greek numeral ΤΙΗ = 300+10+8 is read as the cross-shaped Τ plus ΙΗ, the first two letters of Ἰησοῦς. Which Jewish and Christian interpretation is earlier is difficult to determine from the surviving redactions.

### Love = one = 13

אהבה, *ahavah*, “love”:

1+5+2+5 = **13**

אחד, *eḥad*, “one”:

1+8+4 = **13**

**Status:** exact ordinary-gematria equality and a widely transmitted Jewish homily. The arithmetic is objective; the conclusion that love reveals metaphysical unity is interpretation.

### Serpent = Messiah = 358

נחש, *naḥash*:

50+8+300 = **358**

משיח, *mashiaḥ*:

40+300+10+8 = **358**

**Status:** exact equality used in later kabbalistic and Christian-kabbalistic interpretation, especially in discussions of the transformation or redemption of the serpent principle. It is not a biblical statement that the serpent “is” the Messiah.

### Jesus = 888

ἸΗΣΟΥΣ:

- Ι 10
- Η 8
- Σ 200
- Ο 70
- Υ 400
- Σ 200
- total **888**

**Documented text:** Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* I.15, reports and criticizes Marcus’s use of the six-letter Greek name and its value 888. This documents second-century Marcosian number theology; it does not prove apostolic institution. [Irenaeus I.14](https://www.logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/114.html), [Irenaeus translation](https://openscriptorium.com/books/irenaeus-plain)

### The dove = 801

περιστερά:

π 80 + ε 5 + ρ 100 + ι 10 + σ 200 + τ 300 + ε 5 + ρ 100 + α 1 = **801**

Also Α + Ω = 1+800 = **801**.

**Documented text:** Irenaeus attributes this equation to the Marcosians. [Ancient translation note with arithmetic](https://www.tevl.org/anf/irenaeus/pages/footnotes.php?ftn=2847)

### Nero Caesar = 666

נרון קסר:

נ 50 + ר 200 + ו 6 + ן 50 + ק 100 + ס 60 + ר 200 = **666**

Dropping the final nun in the Latin form נרו קסר gives 616:

50+200+6+100+60+200 = **616**

**Scholarly reconstruction:** this is the leading modern explanation of Revelation 13:18, strengthened by the ancient 616 textual variant and by an independently attested Aramaic spelling of Nero’s title. It is not explicitly decoded by an ancient author. Irenaeus knew both numeral readings but preferred 666 and proposed Greek candidates such as ΛΑΤΕΙΝΟΣ rather than Nero. The Nero solution appears explicitly in modern scholarship only centuries later and remains debated. [Journal of Early Christian History abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/2222582X.2016.1218996), [discussion of the 616 manuscripts](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-ecclesiastical-history/article/abs/apocalyptic-number-616-and-the-donatist-church/0775DE1C87F329E3270E5A34AF356069)

### Pompeii: “I love her whose number is 545”

Φιλῶ ἧς ἀριθμὸς φμεʹ, “I love [the woman] whose number is 545.”

The final numeral is:

φ 500 + μ 40 + ε 5 = **545**

The woman’s name is withheld; therefore its spelling and internal arithmetic cannot be reconstructed. The graffito was buried in 79 CE, furnishing a securely bounded example of popular Roman-period isopsephy. [Study of Pompeian wall inscriptions](https://gredos.usal.es/bitstream/handle/10366/143605/DFCI_CruzGuti%C3%A9rrezS_Inscripcionesparietales.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

### The Basmala = 786

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم:

2+60+40 + 1+30+30+5 + 1+30+200+8+40+50 + 1+30+200+8+10+40 = **786**

**Documented practice/tradition:** especially prominent in South Asian Muslim usage as a respectful numerical surrogate. It is not a Qurʾanic declaration about 786 and some Muslim authorities reject substituting the number for the words. [Abjad table and calculation](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abjad/), [explicit arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad_numerals)

### Agrippa’s Latin table

Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia*, Book II, chapter 20, printed in its complete form in 1533, extended the three-by-nine Hebrew/Greek pattern to Latin:

| 1–9 | 10–90 | 100–900 |
|---|---|---|
| A=1 | K=10 | T=100 |
| B=2 | L=20 | V/U=200 |
| C=3 | M=30 | X=300 |
| D=4 | N=40 | Y=400 |
| E=5 | O=50 | Z=500 |
| F=6 | P=60 | J=600 |
| G=7 | Q=70 | V or supplementary sign=700 |
| H=8 | R=80 | HI=800 |
| I=9 | S=90 | HU/W=900 |

The printed table’s handling of I/J, U/V/W, and supplemental combinations reflects the unstable early-modern Latin alphabet. It is therefore not the same thing as the modern A=1 through Z=26 system. ## Origins and chronological development

### Before alphabetic numerals

**Documented, c. 720 BCE:** Sargon II’s Dur-Šarrukin inscriptions say that the circuit of the wall corresponded to “the reading” or “number” of his name. Editions report **16,280** and sometimes **16,283** cubits. The inscription does not explain the calculation. Consequently it demonstrates Mesopotamian name-number symbolism, but not Hebrew gematria or an alphabetic substitution table. [Khorsabad archaeological site](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/khorsabad/en/new-city), [Cylinder Inscription study](https://museum.imj.org.il/journal/archive/2018-2019/pdf/cylinder.pdf)

**Contested priority claim:** calling Sargon’s statement “the first gematria” stretches the term beyond alphabetic numerals. Cuneiform signs could bear numeric and lexical values, but no line of transmission from this calculation to the Hebrew table has been demonstrated.

### Greek alphabetic numerals and isopsephy

The Milesian/Ionic Greek numeral system assigns α–θ 1–9, ι–ϟ 10–90, and ρ–ϡ 100–900. It required the obsolete signs stigma/digamma, koppa, and sampi to complete three groups of nine.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** alphabetic numeral notation was established in the Greek world by the Hellenistic period and probably earlier. Explicit word-total riddles become abundant in Hellenistic and Roman evidence: inscriptions, oracles, magical papyri, and Christian texts.

**Disputed:** later sources call certain letter-number divination “Pythagorean,” but surviving evidence does not show Pythagoras himself using the modern name-number chart. Aristotle’s testimony about Pythagoreans concerns metaphysical number correspondences, not a securely attested A-to-number personal-name system. Much pseudepigraphic “Pythagorean” literature is Hellenistic or later. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoreanism/)

### Jewish alphabetic numerals

The earliest secure Jewish use of Hebrew letters as numeral signs is late Second Temple rather than biblical-period evidence. Alphabetic numerals appear on coins, inscriptions, manuscript notation, and later rabbinic documents.

**Scholarly assessment:** the Hebrew Bible contains abundant symbolic numbers, acrostics, measured structures, and letter play. That is not by itself proof of numerical gematria. The 1906 *Jewish Encyclopedia* states that numerical gematria cannot be positively demonstrated in the Bible. Proposed hidden biblical totals remain disputed because authors can vary spelling, boundaries, and procedure. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Gematria”](https://sefaria.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria)

### First centuries CE: Jewish and Christian exegesis

- **c. 70–132:** Revelation 13:18 instructs the knowledgeable reader to “calculate” a man’s number, 666; the early variant 616 is manuscript evidence that numerical name interpretation began very early.
- **late first/early second century:** *Barnabas* 9 interprets Abraham’s 318 as Τ + ΙΗ.
- **second century:** Rabbi Eliezer son of Rabbi Yose the Galilean’s thirty-two exegetical rules list gematria as rule 29. The collection’s precise redaction is debated.
- **c. 180:** Irenaeus documents Marcosian number theology in *Against Heresies* I.14–16.
- **third–fourth centuries and later redaction:** Mishnah Avot 3:18 calls *gematriot* “parperaʾot la-ḥokhmah,” often translated “side dishes” or “seasonings of wisdom.” The referent may include calculations more generally.
- **Babylonian Talmud:** Nedarim 32a gives Eliezer=318; Yoma 20a gives השטן = 364, explaining that Satan has authority on 364 days but not Yom Kippur.

### Late antique Gnostic texts

The *Pistis Sophia*, preserved in Coptic but translated from Greek materials, abounds in numbered mysteries, letter sequences, seals, and elaborate cosmological arithmetic. Some appended “Books of the Savior” employ names whose Greek letter totals are significant.

**Caution:** it is inaccurate to treat the entire *Pistis Sophia* as a handbook of Hebrew gematria. Its environment is Greek-Coptic Christian esotericism; its textual strata and dates are debated. [Pistis Sophia text](https://gnosis.org/library/pistis-sophia/index.htm)

### Early Jewish mysticism and medieval Kabbalah

*Sefer Yetzirah* builds cosmology from ten sefirot and twenty-two letters and describes the 231 gates of paired letters. Its early strata may be late antique, but dates proposed range widely. It does not simply present the later catalog of mispar systems.

The *Bahir*, emerging in Provence in the late twelfth century, and the Zoharic corpus, appearing in late thirteenth-century Castile, give letters, names, and numerical correspondences far greater theosophical weight.

German Pietists such as Eleazar of Worms used letter counts, divine names, prayer-word totals, and permutations. Abraham Abulafia (1240–after 1291) made **ṣeruf**, rapid combination and vocalization of letters, a contemplative-prophetic discipline. Gematria was one element within this broader linguistic mysticism, not the whole method. Scholem emphasized historical development and textual strata; Moshe Idel emphasized experiential techniques and broader continuities.

Jacob ben Asher, the Baal ha-Turim, c. 1269–1343, popularized concise gematria, notarikon, and spelling observations in his Torah commentary. His prestige later encouraged attribution of virtually any clever equality to “the Baal ha-Turim,” often without an exact reference. [Baal ha-Turim on Genesis 1](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.1.1-3?with=Ba%27al+HaTurim)

### Cordovero, Luria, and later Kabbalah

Moses Cordovero completed *Pardes Rimmonim* in Safed in the sixteenth century. Gate 30 classifies combinations, substitutions, and gematria. In 30.8 he praises a “balanced” equality in which both words have the same value **and the same number of letters**, giving רזיאל and אברהם as an example. [Pardes Rimmonim 30.8](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.30.8.3)

Isaac Luria and Ḥayyim Vital developed the four fillings ע״ב, ס״ג, מ״ה, ב״ן within an intricate account of emanation, rupture, repair, prayer, and divine configurations. Later siddurim turned these into practical contemplative intentions.

The Vilna Gaon, Elijah ben Solomon Zalman (1720–1797), frequently employed numerical and letter correspondences, but modern claims that every recently named calculator method is “the Gra’s system” require exact references.

The title “Baal Shem” was applied to masters of divine names and practical Kabbalah; Israel ben Eliezer, the Baal Shem Tov, became the central figure of Hasidism. Stories about miraculous numerical interpretation belong to Hasidic tradition; they are not always contemporary documentation.

## Arabic and Persian abjad

The Arabic abjad order preserves the older Semitic alphabetic sequence:

أبجد هوز حطي كلمن سعفص قرشت ثخذ ضظغ

Its standard eastern values run 1–1,000. The Arabic script descends graphically from Nabataean Aramaic, while the abjad order preserves older Semitic ordering despite the later pedagogical reshuffling of similar letter shapes. Persian پ چ ژ گ normally lack independent classical abjad values and are treated by convention.

**Historical distinction:** graphic descent from Nabataean does not prove that every Arabic occult use descended directly from Nabataean numerals. The letter-order inheritance, alphabetic notation, and occult application have separate histories. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Abjad”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abjad/)

Abjad served:

- enumeration and manuscript organization;
- حساب الجمل calculations;
- *ʿilm al-ḥurūf*, sciences of letters;
- talismans and divine-name calculations;
- **awfāq**, magic squares;
- chronograms.

The *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm*, Latin *Picatrix*, compiled in Arabic in the tenth–eleventh century, joins astral magic, letters, numbers, images, and talismans. Later Islamic occult works—especially those attributed to Aḥmad al-Būnī—systematized divine names, letter values, grids, and seals. Attribution and textual dating within the Bunian corpus are difficult; not every work bearing al-Būnī’s name is his.

Persian **mādda-ye tārīkh** chronograms become particularly prominent in the fifteenth century. A meaningful word, phrase, or hemistich totals the Hijri year. The genre passed into Ottoman Turkish, Arabic, and Urdu. It was used for births, deaths, accessions, victories, mosques, fountains, bridges, tombstones, and books. [Iranica, “Chronograms”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chronograms-pers/), [Iranica, “Mādda Tārīkh”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/madda-tarik-chronogram/)

## Uses

### Exegesis

Rabbinic gematria ordinarily supplies a **remez**, support, mnemonic, or homiletical embellishment. It rarely creates binding law unaided. Talmudic and midrashic uses include Eliezer=318 and ha-Satan=364. Medieval exegetes expanded the repertoire; the Baal ha-Turim made concise equations exceptionally visible.

Kabbalists treated equal numbers more ontologically: words could disclose related powers, sefirot, names, or processes. Cordovero nevertheless ranked calculations and imposed qualitative distinctions, showing that kabbalists themselves knew not all matches were equal.

### Meditation and ecstatic practice

Abulafia combined:

- letter permutation;
- writing and recitation;
- vowel changes;
- breath and head movements;
- divine names;
- occasional numerical correspondences.

Calling this simply “gematria meditation” obscures its embodied and phonetic character.

### Amulets and practical magic

Jewish amulets contain divine and angelic names, biblical verses, letter permutations, magic squares, and numerical arrangements. *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is a medieval compilation with earlier materials, not a book physically handed to Adam by the angel Raziel, though that is its framing legend.

Greek magical papyri sometimes specify a deity’s “great name” by its numerical total; one Chabrax formula is described as totaling 9,999. [Discussion and references](https://cranfordville.com/BIC/BIC_v32/RS_34_13_11-18_CRBS.pdf)

Arabic *awfāq* place numbers or letters in grids whose rows and columns balance. The Picatrix and Bunian traditions linked such structures to planets, divine names, spirits, and ritual timing. Surviving manuscripts often differ, and printed “ancient Solomon” talismans may be modern rearrangements of medieval or early-modern material.

### Chronograms

Hebrew chronograms occur on tombstones, synagogue inscriptions, approbations, and title pages. Highlighted letters add to a Hebrew year; the thousands digit is often omitted. A correct reading therefore requires distinguishing chronogram letters, accounting for the Jewish millennium, and checking whether the date is publication, composition, death, or dedication.

Arabic, Persian, Ottoman, Urdu, and Latin traditions created analogous verbal dates. Ottoman poets made *tarih düşürmek* a conspicuous literary craft. Latin chronograms typically count enlarged Roman-numeral letters—M, D, C, L, X, V, I—rather than assigning values to every Latin letter.

### Prophecy and polemic

Revelation’s 666 generated thousands of candidates. Irenaeus already warned that many names can total 666 and counseled against confident identification. Medieval and Reformation polemicists calculated popes, emperors, reformers, sects, and titles, frequently switching among Greek, Hebrew, and Roman spellings.

“VICARIVS FILII DEI = 666” is a famous anti-papal Latin calculation:

V 5 + I 1 + C 100 + I 1 + V 5 + I 1 + L 50 + I 1 + I 1 + D 500 + I 1 = **666**

The arithmetic works if only Roman-numeral letters count and U is treated as V. The disputed historical issue is whether this was an official, characteristic papal title—especially the legend that it appeared on the papal tiara. Numerical correctness does not establish the premise.

### Literature

Borges repeatedly used Kabbalah, infinite libraries, divine names, permutations, and numerically structured worlds; calling every Borgesian number “gematria” is too broad. Perec and Oulipo deliberately used constraints, lipograms, combinatorics, and mathematical structures; Perec’s Jewish background and interest in letters make comparison illuminating, but most Oulipian constraints are not alphabetic numerology. Joyce’s works invite numerical pattern hunting, yet proposed gematrias must be distinguished from demonstrable compositional notes and explicit textual cues.

The responsible literary question is not “can a total be found?” but “is there manuscript, structural, or repeated internal evidence that the author selected the wording to obtain it?”

### Renaissance and modern occultism

Agrippa synthesized Hebrew, Greek, and Latin letter-number correspondences in *De occulta philosophia*. Christian Cabalists including Pico, Reuchlin, and later Kircher used Hebrew names to support Christian theological interpretations.

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn incorporated Hebrew values, tarot correspondences, divine names, planetary magic, and the Tree of Life into an initiatory curriculum. S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* (1887) helped standardize transliterated Hebrew tables in English occultism.

Aleister Crowley’s *777* and *Sepher Sephiroth* arranged correspondences and Hebrew words by number. *Sepher Sephiroth* is effectively a numerical lexicon. Crowley’s practice often involved alternate transliterations, qabalah, tarot, and personal magical formulas; it should not be mistaken for rabbinic philology. [Liber 777 scan](https://astrumargentum.org/ht/libri/Liber_777.pdf)

### Modern English Qabalah

James Lees devised the ALW or New Aeon English Qabalah in November 1976 while interpreting Crowley’s *Book of the Law*. It was first publicly reported by Ray Sherwin in 1979; further material appeared in *The New Equinox* in 1981–82.

**Modern invention:** it is a twentieth-century Thelemic system, not an ancient English alphabet recovered from Egypt or Sinai. Its adherents may describe it as a discovery or revelation; that is an internal religious claim. [History of English Qaballa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Qaballa)

### Popular “Pythagorean” and “Chaldean” numerology

The modern Western A–Z name-number industry took recognizable form in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. L. Dow Balliett’s *The Philosophy of Numbers* appeared in 1908; Cheiro popularized a rival “Chaldean” table in *Cheiro’s Book of Numbers*.

**Modern invention/marketing genealogy:** neither common chart has been found in an ancient text by Pythagoras or a cuneiform Chaldean source. “Pythagorean” invokes Greek number mysticism; “Chaldean” invokes the prestige of Babylonian astrology. The specific English mappings are modern.

[Balliett, 1908](https://archive.org/details/philosophyofnumb00ball)

### Internet gematria

Modern calculators permit instant searches across many ciphers. “Jewish gematria” on English-language sites is often a Latin transliteration table, not calculation in Hebrew script. “English Sumerian,” frequently A=6, B=12 … Z=156, is modern and has no demonstrated Sumerian source.

The Gematrinator ecosystem and similar services have popularized:

- English ordinal;
- full reduction;
- reverse ordinal;
- reverse reduction;
- transliterated “Jewish” values;
- custom ciphers.

These tools calculate accurately according to their programmed tables, but the labels may be historically misleading. Searching dozens of ciphers, spellings, abbreviations, dates, and phrase boundaries creates a severe multiple-comparisons problem.

## Controversies and disputes

### Mesopotamia, Greece, or Israel?

There is no single uncontested “invention event.”

- Mesopotamia supplies older sign-number symbolism, including Sargon.
- Northwest Semitic cultures supplied alphabetic ordering and influenced regional numeral traditions.
- Greeks supply the earliest large, securely attested corpus of alphabetic word-sums.
- Jewish gematria is securely documented in Roman-era rabbinic culture and rests on Hebrew alphabetic numerals.
- Arabic abjad continues the older Semitic order in a new script and cultural setting.

A direct line “Sargon invented gematria; Greeks copied it; Jews copied Greeks” is not demonstrated. Nor is the nationalist reverse claim that all Greek isopsephy derives from Hebrew biblical gematria.

### The word “gematria”

It is often derived from Greek γεωμετρία, “geometry.” Other scholars connect it with γραμματεία or related terms involving letters and writing. Sambursky reviewed the semantic problem. The familiar “geometry” derivation is plausible as transmission history, not certain proof that the technique originated in a Greek mathematical school.

### The Pythagorean claim

Ancient Pythagoreans undeniably developed number symbolism and numerical cosmology. The first clearly datable literary references to “Pythagorean reckoning” by letters, however, are later than Pythagoras. No contemporary sixth-century BCE document gives him a modern name-number table. Consequently:

- “Pythagoras believed number structured reality”: historically defensible.
- “Pythagoras invented isopsephy”: disputed.
- “Pythagoras used the modern 1–9 English chart”: modern legend.

### Coincidence and researcher degrees of freedom

For a 22-letter alphabet with many common words, collisions are inevitable. Their number rises sharply when one may:

- choose among standard, ordinal, reduced, squared, cumulative, and filled values;
- vary final letters;
- add a kolel;
- select plene or defective spelling;
- transliterate a foreign name several ways;
- include or exclude prefixes and articles;
- search an entire corpus after seeing the target number.

A meaningful historical case is strengthened by an explicit instruction to calculate, a constrained name pool, contemporary spelling, textual variants predicted by the solution, or manuscript evidence of deliberate revision. Nero/666 is stronger than an unconstrained internet match because Revelation asks for a man’s number and the 616 variant corresponds to a spelling change—though it still remains a reconstruction.

Meir Bar-Ilan has called uncontrolled biblical number hunting “false science” because it ignores probability and textual flexibility. [Survey of the debate](https://jbqnew.jewishbible.org/assets/Uploads/524/jbq_524_goodfriendgematria.pdf)

### Scholarly assessments

- **Dornseiff**, *Das Alphabet in Mystik und Magie*, assembled Greek, Jewish, Christian, and magical evidence and remains foundational, though its diffusionist assumptions and source dating require updating.
- **Scholem** treated gematria as one technique within the historical growth of Kabbalah, not as a timeless key capable of mechanically decoding all Judaism.
- **Idel** shifted attention toward practices—permutation, recitation, mystical technique—and challenged parts of Scholem’s grand narrative.
- **Kieren Barry**, *The Greek Qabalah* (1999), emphasized the extent of Greek alphanumeric symbolism. The book is influential in esoteric circles but is not a substitute for critical editions of individual inscriptions and texts.
- **Aryeh Kaplan** presented *Sefer Yetzirah*, meditation, and letter symbolism sympathetically and accessibly; some historical syntheses are traditional or speculative rather than consensus datings.
- **Friedrich Weinreb** developed an elaborate twentieth-century symbolic reading of Torah through numbers. It is a modern theological system, not evidence for how biblical authors composed the text.

### Forgeries and invented antiquities

Common red flags include:

- a chart called “Chaldean” without a cuneiform witness;
- an English cipher called “Sumerian”;
- a claim that Pythagoras taught an A–Z table;
- an attribution to the Ari, Gra, or Baal ha-Turim without book, section, and wording;
- a “Sefer Raziel” diagram copied from a much later printed edition and labeled antediluvian;
- papal-tiara claims without a contemporary tiara, inventory, or image;
- an internet calculator’s name treated as proof that its method is medieval;
- a supposed ancient text whose earliest appearance is a nineteenth- or twentieth-century occult book.

Absence of early evidence does not prove that nobody ever performed the arithmetic. It does prevent a responsible historian from assigning the named method an ancient date.

## Open questions

1. What are the earliest manuscript occurrences of the exact labels מספר קדמי, מספר בונה, מספר מוספי, and מספר פרטי?
2. Which of the methods now attributed to Cordovero actually appear verbatim in the earliest *Pardes Rimmonim* manuscripts, rather than later indexes and glosses?
3. Can the competing totals 16,280 and 16,283 in Sargon’s inscriptions be resolved by inscriptional recension, unit, or modern editorial error?
4. When did the extended final values 500–900 first become a coherent named table?
5. When is 786 first demonstrably used as a written surrogate for the Basmala, rather than merely being a calculable total?
6. How early are Hebrew chronograms that encode a complete year rather than merely use alphabetic numeral notation?
7. Which Zoharic gematriot belong to the main Castilian strata and which to later additions?
8. Can corpus-based statistical controls distinguish deliberately constructed biblical or rabbinic equations from accidental collisions?
9. How much of the Bunian letter-science corpus is attributable to al-Būnī himself?
10. Which modern online cipher names can be traced to identifiable software authors rather than earlier printed occult literature?

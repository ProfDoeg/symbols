# Mesopotamian and Egyptian precursors of gematria: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary labels

This dossier concerns practices before, beside, and behind alphabetic gematria: Mesopotamian name-number constructions, divine numbers, numerical spellings and ciphers, and Egyptian number symbolism. Later Hebrew, Greek, Arabic, Latin, and English systems are included comparatively, not presented as one continuous tradition.

Claims are classified as follows:

- **Documented text:** stated or demonstrably used in a surviving ancient or medieval source.
- **Scholarly reconstruction:** a modern explanation inferred from surviving evidence.
- **Tradition:** an attribution made within a religious or esoteric tradition but not independently demonstrated.
- **Disputed:** supported by some scholarship but contested or underdetermined.
- **Legend:** a retrospective story without contemporary evidence.
- **Modern invention:** a demonstrably recent system marketed as ancient.

---

## 1. Basic identification

### Name

**Modern descriptive name:** Mesopotamian precursors of gematria.

There was no single Akkadian or Sumerian institution corresponding exactly to Hebrew גימטריא, Greek ἰσοψηφία, or Arabic حساب الجُمَّل. “Mesopotamian gematria” is therefore a convenient modern umbrella for several different practices:

1. giving gods characteristic numbers;
2. writing divine names with numeral signs;
3. pairing syllabic or logographic signs with numbers;
4. numerical cryptography;
5. extracting meanings from graphic or numerical features of cuneiform signs;
6. making a physical measurement correspond to the “number” or “name” of a king.

These overlap with later gematria but do not amount to a universal letter-value alphabet.

### Script

- Sumerian and Akkadian **cuneiform**, principally syllabic and logographic.
- Relevant royal name: Akkadian **Šarru-kīn**, “the king is legitimate/established,” conventionally Sargon.
- Common logographic writing: **LUGAL-GI.NA**, approximately 𒈗𒄀𒈾.
- Numerals were written with cuneiform numeral signs in decimal, sexagesimal, metrological, and contextual systems.

### Period

- Individual god-number writings reach back at least into the second millennium BCE.
- The canonical divine-number hierarchy is particularly visible in first-millennium BCE scholarly, astronomical, divinatory, and theological texts.
- Sargon II’s Dur-Šarrukīn construction inscriptions belong to approximately **713–706 BCE**, during his reign, 722–705 BCE.
- Numerical cryptography and number-syllabaries are especially well attested in Late Babylonian and Seleucid scholarship, roughly the first millennium through the last centuries BCE.

### Region

- Assyria: especially Kalḫu, Nineveh, and Dur-Šarrukīn/Khorsabad.
- Babylonia: Babylon, Uruk, Nippur and other scribal centers.
- Egypt is treated separately below: it possessed sophisticated numeral notation and pervasive number symbolism, but no demonstrated indigenous hieroglyphic letter-value gematria.

## 2. The “table of values”

### 2.1 No Mesopotamian alphabetic table

**Finding: absence of evidence.** There is no recovered Mesopotamian table in which every cuneiform phonetic sign has a stable additive value analogous to:

- Hebrew א = 1, ב = 2 … ת = 400;
- Greek α = 1, β = 2 … ω = 800;
- Arabic ا = 1, ب = 2 … غ = 1000.

Cuneiform was not an alphabet. It contained hundreds of polyvalent signs that could function as syllables, words, determinatives, or numbers. Accordingly, there was no natural one-to-one alphabetic series from which a standard gematria table could arise.

Late Babylonian **number-syllabaries** do pair numerals with signs, but these are special scribal lists, not a universal valuation of all written language. Laurie Pearce describes tablets whose entries follow the pattern “numeral—DIŠ—syllabic sign”; the known witnesses are late, several probably Seleucid, and come or probably come from Babylon. This is documented numerical substitution, but not Hebrew-style additive gematria. See [Pearce, “Cuneiform Number-Syllabaries”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iraq/article/abs/cuneiform-numbersyllabaries/525C866920A2AB09CA04191A77020606).

### 2.2 Canonical divine numbers

A recurrent first-millennium hierarchy is:

| Deity | Akkadian/Sumerian name | Characteristic number | Function |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Anu | An/Anu | 60 | supreme heaven god |
| Enlil | Ellil/Enlil | 50 | chief divine authority |
| Ea | Enki/Ea | 40 | wisdom, subterranean waters |
| Sîn | Nanna/Sîn | 30 | moon |
| Šamaš | Utu/Šamaš | 20 | sun and justice |
| Ištar | Inanna/Ištar | 15 | Venus, love and war |
| Adad | Iškur/Adad | 6 or 10 in different traditions | storm god |
| Marduk/Bēl | Marduk | commonly 10; other values occur | Babylonian state god |

The principal sequence Anu 60, Enlil 50, Ea 40, Sîn 30, Šamaš 20, Ištar 15 is documented in cuneiform lists and summarized by the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/essays/mesopotamian-deities). Variants are important: the assignments were not one immutable “Babylonian gematria table.” One tablet might use 60 as Anu’s name; another theological or cryptographic scheme might associate the same number or sign differently.

#### Interpretation

- **Documented:** numeral signs sometimes stand for particular gods.
- **Scholarly reconstruction:** the descending 60–50–40–30–20 hierarchy probably expresses rank within a sexagesimal intellectual system.
- **Astronomically transparent cases:** Sîn’s 30 corresponds naturally to the approximate lunar month; Šamaš’s 20 may be understood as one-third of the sexagesimal whole; Ištar’s 15 as one-quarter.
- **Disputed:** whether every assignment originated through astronomy, fractions of 60, theological hierarchy, scribal punning, or some mixture.
- **Not demonstrated:** that divine numbers were obtained by adding fixed numerical values of the syllables in the gods’ names.

### 2.3 How the system operated

In Mesopotamian scholarship a numeral could operate as:

1. a number in ordinary calculation;
2. a logographic or cryptographic spelling;
3. the emblematic number of a deity;
4. a substitute for a sign with the same graphic form;
5. a value explained through sign names, sign components, metrology, or homophony;
6. a key in a restricted scholarly commentary.

A late commentary on *Enūma Anu Enlil* contains apodoses written numerically, with a commentary serving as a key. Yale’s Cuneiform Commentaries Project calls it evidence for the sophistication of late Mesopotamian hermeneutics and notes its possible second- or first-century BCE date: [CCP 3.1.u7](https://ccp.yale.edu/P461117).

This culture treated cuneiform as polyvalent. A sign could be read according to its sound, Sumerian word value, Akkadian equivalent, visual components, or numerical identity. That resembles later multi-level letter interpretation, but it is structurally different from alphabetic addition.

## 3. The Sargon II wall inscription

### 3.1 The documented text

Sargon II created Dur-Šarrukīn, “Fortress of Sargon,” at modern Khorsabad near Mosul. Several construction inscriptions state, in substantially the same passage, that he made the city wall’s circuit correspond to his name:

> “16,283 cubits, the numeral of my name, I made the circuit of its wall.”

The Akkadian expression is commonly transcribed **nibīt šumīya**, literally something like “the naming/number-designation of my name.” The statement occurs in cylinder, bull, bronze, and display inscriptions. A convenient presentation of the sources and older bibliography appears in David Fouts’s dissertation, pp. 102–03: [The Use of Large Numbers in the Old Testament](https://godawa.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Fouts-Use-of-Large-Numbers-in-the-Old-Testament-Diss.pdf).

The current royal-inscription corpus is Grant Frame, *The Royal Inscriptions of Sargon II, King of Assyria (721–705 BC)*: [RINAP 2](https://build-oracc.museum.upenn.edu/rinap/downloads/Frame_RINAP_2.pdf).

### 3.2 The metrological arithmetic

An older edition gives the measurement as:

- 4 **šar**
- 3 **nēr**
- 1 **šūš**
- 3 **qānu**
- 2 cubits

Using:

- šar = 3,600 cubits
- nēr = 600
- šūš = 60
- qānu = 6 cubits

the calculation is:

\[
(4\times3,600)+(3\times600)+60+(3\times6)+2
\]

\[
14,400+1,800+60+18+2=16,280.
\]

If qānu is evaluated as seven rather than six cubits:

\[
14,400+1,800+60+(3\times7)+2=16,283.
\]

This explains the two totals in modern literature. Fritz Hommel discussed the six/seven-cubit issue in 1907: [“Die Zahl ‘meines Namens’ in Sargons Zylinderinschrift”](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1524/olzg.1907.10.16.113/html).

### 3.3 Does 16,283 equal the letters of Šarru-kīn?

The inscription itself says that the measurement corresponds to Sargon’s name, but it does not preserve the key.

Friedrich Delitzsch proposed in 1878 that the value came from counting or numerically interpreting the cuneiform wedges in the written name. He divided the written form into:

1. the personal-name determinative;
2. the sign for “king,” šarru;
3. the sign or signs representing kīn/kūn, “established.”

This is a **nineteenth-century scholarly reconstruction**, not arithmetic stated by Sargon’s inscription. Later writers often shortened it to “Sargon’s name added up to 16,283,” thereby making the mechanism sound more certain than it is.

Eckart Frahm’s assessment is decisive: the monument is a “famous early example of Mesopotamian gematriah,” but the riddle remains “essentially unsolved”; no consensus explains how the name and number correlate. See Frahm, [“Observations on the Name and Age of Sargon II”](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/saao/aebp/downloads/frahm_nabu2005_44.pdf).

### 3.4 What may safely be claimed

- **Documented:** the wall number was intentionally described as corresponding to Sargon’s name.
- **Documented:** the result was 16,280 or 16,283 depending on metrological interpretation.
- **Reconstruction:** wedge counting or decomposition of the written royal name.
- **Disputed:** the exact calculation.
- **Not documented:** a general alphabetic or syllabic table capable of deriving 16,283 from Šarru-kīn.
- **Misleading modern formulation:** “Sargon used standard Babylonian gematria exactly like Hebrew gematria.”

The episode is a genuine precursor because a name is assigned an operative number and embodied architecturally. It is not proof of a lost cuneiform equivalent of the Hebrew table.

## 4. Other Mesopotamian precursors

### 4.1 Gods written as numbers

The clearest proto-gematric practice is not word summation but identity between a deity and a numeral.

Examples include:

- **30 = Sîn**, the moon god;
- **20 = Šamaš**, the sun god;
- **15 = Ištar**;
- **40 = Ea**;
- **50 = Enlil**;
- **60 = Anu**.

The numeral could substitute for the divine name in astronomical or scholarly writing. This is closer to a cryptographic nomen sacrum than to an alphabet total.

### 4.2 Nabû and scribal number games

Nabû was the deity of writing, learned calculation, and scribal knowledge. His name derives from the Semitic root *nbʾ*, “to call/name/designate”; his emblem was the stylus. For historical background see [ORACC, “Nabu”](https://oracc2.museum.upenn.edu/amgg/listofdeities/nabu/index.html).

Late scholarly tablets contain number-name puzzles and number-syllabaries. One Seleucid-period Uruk puzzle reportedly gives a sequence of cuneiform signs with the numbers:

- 21, 35, 35, 26, 44
- “son of”
- 21, 11, 20, 42

The purpose is to encode a personal name through numerical substitutions. This is **documented numerical cryptography**, but the values belong to a specialist sign-key rather than an ordinary alphabet. Georges Ifrah reproduces such material in his discussion of “Codes and Ciphers in Cuneiform Numerals”: [Ifrah excerpt](https://www.ms.uky.edu/~sohum/ma330/files/Ifrah_numbers.pdf).

### 4.3 Cuneiform cryptography

Cryptography appears in:

- divinatory texts;
- medical and magical recipes;
- colophons;
- scholarly commentaries;
- “secret knowledge” texts restricted to trained scribes.

Techniques include rare sign values, numerical substitutions, reversed writings, sign splitting, rebus readings, and deliberate ambiguity. A modern overview of wordplay and restricted scholarly writing is Scott Noegel, ed., [*“Wordplay” in Ancient Near Eastern Texts*](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/9780884144762_OA.pdf).

### 4.4 Esarhaddon’s 70 reversed into 11

A royal account of Babylon’s restoration says Marduk reversed the written destiny of seventy years, turning the written numeral into eleven. The operation depends on the graphic orientation of cuneiform numeral signs, not addition of letter values.

- **Documented:** a number-sign transformation embedded in theological history.
- **Significance:** it demonstrates that Neo-Assyrian authors could build political and prophetic meaning from the visual and numerical properties of writing.
- **Not gematria narrowly defined:** no word is summed.

### 4.5 Relationship to later hermeneutics

Stephen J. Lieberman’s 1987 article, “A Mesopotamian Background for the So-Called Aggadic ‘Measures’ of Biblical Hermeneutics?”, compares Mesopotamian scribal interpretation with later rabbinic techniques. Bibliographic record: [Yale Cuneiform Commentaries Project](https://ccp.yale.edu/mesopotamian-background-so-called-aggadic-measures-biblical-hermeneutics); journal citation: *Hebrew Union College Annual* 58 (1987), 157–225.

Lieberman’s argument is best understood as a comparison of **hermeneutical environments and techniques**, including polysemy, graphic analysis, numerical interpretation, and learned wordplay.

- **Plausible reconstruction:** Jewish scribes lived within an ancient Near Eastern world where such operations had long precedents.
- **Not proved:** a direct line from a particular Babylonian tablet to a particular rabbinic rule.
- **Reception:** the article is frequently cited to establish a Mesopotamian background for aggadic techniques, but it has not produced a consensus that Hebrew gematria itself is a direct cuneiform inheritance.
- **Methodological problem:** analogous operations can arise independently wherever writing signs also function as numbers or have enumerable graphic components.

## 5. Egypt

### 5.1 Egyptian numerals

Ancient Egyptian possessed a decimal, additive numeral system. In hieroglyphic form:

| Value | Typical hieroglyph | Description |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 𓏺 | stroke |
| 10 | 𓎆 | heel-bone/cattle-hobble sign |
| 100 | 𓍢 | coil of rope |
| 1,000 | 𓆼 | lotus |
| 10,000 | 𓂭 | pointing finger |
| 100,000 | 𓆐 | tadpole/frog |
| 1,000,000 | 𓁨 | deity with raised arms |

Numbers were formed additively by repeating signs. Thus 23 could be written with two tens and three units. Hieratic and Demotic developed more compressed numeral signs.

### 5.2 Did hieroglyphs have letter values?

**No indigenous hieroglyphic gematria table is attested.**

Egyptian writing was a mixed system:

- uniliteral phonograms;
- biliteral and triliteral phonograms;
- logograms;
- determinatives;
- explicit numeral signs.

The roughly two dozen uniliteral signs sometimes called an “Egyptian alphabet” were not arranged as a historically attested sequence with values 1–9, 10–90, and 100–900. Egyptian scribes did not normally obtain the value of a word by adding its phonograms.

Consequently:

- **Documented:** Egyptian numeral notation.
- **Documented:** symbolic and ritual use of numbers.
- **Documented:** cryptographic, acrophonic, visual, and rebus manipulation of hieroglyphs.
- **Not documented:** a Pharaonic letter-number table.
- **Modern invention:** tables assigning Hebrew-, Greek-, or English-style values to Egyptian uniliterals and then calculating Egyptian words.
- **Disputed modern speculation:** claims that Greek isopsephy was secretly encoded in pyramids, cubit rods, temple dimensions, or the 28 chapters of Papyrus Leiden I 350.

Unicode’s current classification likewise distinguishes sign functions such as phonogram, logogram, determinative, and numeral rather than treating the script as an alphanumeric alphabet: [Unicode Technical Standard #57](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr57/).

### 5.3 Egyptian number symbolism

Egyptian texts and monuments attach significance to numbers without using gematria:

- **2:** duality and paired oppositions.
- **3:** plurality; three repeated signs can mark the plural.
- **4:** totality through four directions or supports.
- **7:** completion or heightened efficacy in some ritual contexts.
- **8:** the Hermopolitan Ogdoad.
- **9:** the Heliopolitan Ennead and an extended divine plurality.
- **10 and its powers:** administrative and monumental enumeration.
- **42:** the assessors or nomes in later systematizations.
- **365:** calendrical total.

These are genuine histories of symbolic number, but number symbolism is not automatically gematria.

### 5.4 Egyptian-Greek transition

Egypt later became an important region for alphabetic numeration because Greek was used under the Ptolemies and Romans. Greek alphabetic numerals, isopsephy, Coptic alphabetic numerals, Gnostic letter speculation, and the Greek magical papyri flourished in Egyptian settings.

This yields a necessary distinction:

- “Gematria practiced **in Egypt**” may be well documented in Greek, Coptic, Jewish, or Arabic texts.
- “An **indigenous Pharaonic hieroglyphic gematria**” is not documented.

## 6. Comparative alphabetic systems

## 6.1 Greek isopsephy

### Table

| Value | Letters |
|---:|---|
| 1–9 | α 1, β 2, γ 3, δ 4, ε 5, ϛ/ϝ 6, ζ 7, η 8, θ 9 |
| 10–90 | ι 10, κ 20, λ 30, μ 40, ν 50, ξ 60, ο 70, π 80, ϟ 90 |
| 100–900 | ρ 100, σ/ς 200, τ 300, υ 400, φ 500, χ 600, ψ 700, ω 800, ϡ 900 |

The three supplemental numeral letters are digamma/stigma 6, qoppa 90, and sampi 900. Diacritics, breathing marks, and accents do not count. Final sigma has the same value as sigma. Words have equal “pebbles,” ἴσος + ψῆφος, when their unreduced totals agree.

The earliest Greek alphabetic numerals are attested by the fifth century BCE, with their precise origin and earlier chronology disputed. For the documentary history see the accounting survey noting fifth-century Athenian and Halicarnassian examples: [Greek and Roman Accounting](https://www.mgh-bibliothek.de/dokumente/a/a146432.pdf).

### Pompeii, before 79 CE

CIL IV 1595 reads:

> Φιλῶ ἧς ἀριθμὸς φμεʹ  
> “I love her whose number is 545.”

The numeral is:

\[
\phi+\mu+\epsilon=500+40+5=545.
\]

The woman’s actual name is withheld. This is a securely documented social use of isopsephy, not a later theological interpretation.

### Jesus = 888

Irenaeus reports that the Marcosian Gnostics calculated Greek Ἰησοῦς:

\[
Ι+Η+Σ+Ο+Υ+Σ
\]

\[
10+8+200+70+400+200=888.
\]

### Dove = 801

Greek περιστερά, “dove”:

\[
Π+Ε+Ρ+Ι+Σ+Τ+Ε+Ρ+Α
\]

\[
80+5+100+10+200+300+5+100+1=801.
\]

Marcosian teaching used Alpha and Omega:

\[
Α+\Omega=1+800=801,
\]

making 801 signify both the dove and Alpha–Omega. Irenaeus reports and attacks these calculations in *Against Heresies* I.14–16: [online translation](https://openscriptorium.com/books/irenaeus-plain).

### Pistis Sophia

The Coptic *Pistis Sophia*, probably third–fourth century CE in its present form, contains elaborate numbered heavens, emanations, seals, names and sequences of vowels and letters. Some strings can be interpreted through Greek numerical values, but it does not preserve one simple new gematria table. The work belongs to the same late-antique milieu of alphanumeric cosmology, voces magicae, and numbered celestial structures. Text: [Coptic Scriptorium](https://data.copticscriptorium.org/texts/pistissophia/) and [English translation](https://thegnosticgospels.org/gospels/pistis-sophia-1/).

## 6.2 Hebrew gematria

### Standard table: מספר הכרחי, *mispar hekhreḥi*

| Value | Letters |
|---:|---|
| 1–9 | א 1, ב 2, ג 3, ד 4, ה 5, ו 6, ז 7, ח 8, ט 9 |
| 10–90 | י 10, כ/ך 20, ל 30, מ/ם 40, נ/ן 50, ס 60, ע 70, פ/ף 80, צ/ץ 90 |
| 100–400 | ק 100, ר 200, ש 300, ת 400 |

Rules:

- consonants count;
- vowel points and cantillation marks do not;
- ordinary final forms ך ם ן ף ץ retain 20, 40, 50, 80, 90;
- spaces and punctuation do not count;
- exact unreduced equality is the normal equivalence;
- spelling differences change the total.

In **mispar gadol**, the five finals may be extended:

- ך 500, ם 600, ן 700, ף 800, ץ 900.

Other methods include:

- **מספר קטן, mispar qaṭan:** reduce each letter to its unit digit;
- **מספר סידורי, mispar siduri:** alphabetic positions 1–22;
- **מספר קדמי, mispar qadmi:** cumulative values;
- **מילוי, millui:** spell out letter names and total them;
- **אתב״ש, atbash:** substitute first ↔ last, second ↔ next-to-last;
- **אלב״ם, albam:** substitute between two halves;
- **תמורה, temurah:** systematic letter permutation;
- **נוטריקון, notarikon:** acronymic or acrostic expansion, technically distinct from gematria.

### Eliezer = 318

Hebrew אליעזר:

\[
א+ל+י+ע+ז+ר
\]

\[
1+30+10+70+7+200=318.
\]

Genesis 14:14 says Abraham mobilized 318 retainers. Genesis Rabbah and Babylonian Talmud *Nedarim* 32a interpret the number as Eliezer, suggesting that Eliezer alone accompanied Abraham or was equivalent to all the retainers. Rashi repeats the interpretation. [Rashi on Genesis 14:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Genesis.14.14.3); [Nedarim 32a](https://www.sefaria.org/Nedarim.32a).

Ibn Ezra explicitly calls it midrash and says Scripture does not speak by gematria in its plain sense: [Ibn Ezra on Genesis 14:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.14.14-16?lang2=en&with=Ibn+Ezra).

### אהבה = אחד = 13

אהבה, *ahavah*, “love”:

\[
א+ה+ב+ה=1+5+2+5=13.
\]

אחד, *eḥad*, “one”:

\[
א+ח+ד=1+8+4=13.
\]

The equality is arithmetically exact. Its use as a theological statement joining love and unity is traditional homiletics, not proof that either word was coined to obtain 13.

### נחש = משיח = 358

נחש, *naḥash*, “serpent”:

\[
נ+ח+ש=50+8+300=358.
\]

משיח, *mashiaḥ*, “messiah”:

\[
מ+ש+י+ח=40+300+10+8=358.
\]

The equivalence became important in kabbalistic and later occult interpretations of redemption, transformation, and the “serpent.” It is an exact standard-gematria equality; the meanings assigned to it depend on the interpreter.

### Tetragrammaton = 26

יהוה:

\[
י+ה+ו+ה=10+5+6+5=26.
\]

This is among the most durable Jewish and Christian-kabbalistic divine numbers.

### Thirty-two rules

The *Baraita of the Thirty-two Rules*, traditionally attributed to the second-century tanna Eliezer ben Yose ha-Gelili, lists gematria as the twenty-ninth rule in common numbering. The received explanatory work is much later than the attributed authority; one modern dating places its compilation around the tenth century. Thus:

- **Tradition:** second-century attribution.
- **Documented manuscript tradition:** medieval work transmitted under his name.
- **Disputed:** whether the list or portions preserve early tannaitic material.
- **Incorrect simplification:** “a complete second-century book by Rabbi Eliezer survives.”

See [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Rules of Eliezer”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/12935-rules-of-eliezer-b-jose-ha-gelili-the-thirty) and [Posen Library, “Midrash of Thirty-Two Attributes”](https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/midrash-thirty-two-attributes).

### Chronology and origin

The earliest securely dated Hebrew use of decimal alphabetic numerals is generally placed in the late Hellenistic period, including Alexander Jannaeus’s coins of 78 BCE. Earlier Phoenician letters occur as ordinal or low-value marks, but whether they represent a complete decimal alphabetic system is debated. A recent review gives the present consensus and challenges to it: [“Counting on God’s Name”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0BCF68076A22C4DCD5AE71C9277D9D7E/S001781602510076Xa.pdf/div-class-title-counting-on-god-s-name-the-numerology-of-span-class-italic-nomina-sacra-span-div.pdf).

Claims of hidden gematria in the Hebrew Bible are therefore individually disputed. A proposed equality is not, by itself, evidence of intentional composition.

## 6.3 Revelation’s 666 and 616

The leading modern solution reads “Neron Caesar” in Hebrew:

נרון קסר:

\[
נ+ר+ו+ן+ק+ס+ר
\]

\[
50+200+6+50+100+60+200=666.
\]

The Latin-form “Nero Caesar,” without final *n*, gives נרו קסר:

\[
50+200+6+100+60+200=616.
\]

The 616 variant is ancient, appearing in witnesses including Papyrus 115 and Codex Ephraemi. Its agreement with the spelling difference is powerful evidence for the Nero interpretation.

But the history of the interpretation must be distinguished from the proposed ancient cipher:

- **Documented ancient text:** Revelation instructs the intelligent reader to “calculate” the beast’s number.
- **Documented ancient variants:** 666 and 616.
- **Scholarly reconstruction:** both encode Nero’s name through Hebrew letters.
- **Historical caution:** the explicit modern Nero-Caesar derivation first became prominent in 1831 through independent nineteenth-century proposals; no surviving first-century commentary gives this solution.
- **Alternative interpretations:** imperial symbolism, Solomon’s 666 talents, “beast” itself, and other imperial names.

See [JTS, “Concealed Claudian”](https://academic.oup.com/jts/article/76/1/109/8043235) and [Cambridge, “The Apocalyptic Number 616”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-ecclesiastical-history/article/abs/apocalyptic-number-616-and-the-donatist-church/0775DE1C87F329E3270E5A34AF356069).

## 6.4 Arabic abjad

### Eastern table

Mnemonic order: **أبجد هوز حطي كلمن سعفص قرشت ثخذ ضظغ**

| Value | Letters |
|---:|---|
| 1–9 | ا 1, ب 2, ج 3, د 4, ه 5, و 6, ز 7, ح 8, ط 9 |
| 10–90 | ي 10, ك 20, ل 30, م 40, ن 50, س 60, ع 70, ف 80, ص 90 |
| 100–1000 | ق 100, ر 200, ش 300, ت 400, ث 500, خ 600, ذ 700, ض 800, ظ 900, غ 1000 |

Names include:

- **حساب الجمل, ḥisāb al-jummal:** calculation of the total;
- **حساب الأبجد, ḥisāb al-abjad:** abjad calculation;
- **علم الحروف, ʿilm al-ḥurūf:** science of letters;
- **وفق / أوفاق, wafq / awfāq:** magic square(s);
- **مادة التاريخ, māddat al-tārīkh:** chronogrammatic date phrase.

The order inherits the ancient Northwest Semitic letter sequence, while Arabic script itself developed from Nabataean Aramaic. This does not mean that the Arabic numerical system simply copied Nabataean numeral signs: Nabataean notation used a separate additive system. Johannes Thomann distinguishes an older and a later/scientific abjad tradition: [“Scientific and Archaic Arabic Numerals”](https://www.sciamvs.org/files/SCIAMVS_19_167-200_Thomann.pdf). See also [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Abjad”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abjad/).

### The basmala = 786

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم

Ignoring short vowels and counting the written consonantal letters:

- بسم = ب 2 + س 60 + م 40 = 102
- الله = ا 1 + ل 30 + ل 30 + ه 5 = 66
- الرحمن = ا 1 + ل 30 + ر 200 + ح 8 + م 40 + ن 50 = 329
- الرحيم = ا 1 + ل 30 + ر 200 + ح 8 + ي 10 + م 40 = 289

Total:

\[
102+66+329+289=786.
\]

Using 786 as a substitute for the basmala is especially associated with South Asian Muslim practice. The equality is mathematically standard; treating 786 itself as scriptural or inherently sacred is a later customary or folk-religious development, and Muslim legal opinions disagree about its appropriateness.

### Chronograms

A poet composes a phrase whose total equals the Hijri year of the event. Persian terms include *tārīkh*, *mādda-ye tārīkh*, and *tārīkh-e maʿnawī*. Elaborate forms add or subtract words indicated by the poem.

Chronograms appear on:

- tombstones;
- mosques, fountains, bridges, palaces and restorations;
- manuscript colophons;
- title pages;
- accessions, births, deaths and military victories.

Poetic chronograms became a major Persianate and Ottoman art, particularly from the later medieval and early-modern periods. The earliest securely recorded poetic examples are much later than Islam’s beginnings—approximately the fourteenth century in one archaeological review. See [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Chronograms”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chronograms-pers/) and [Abjad numerals as a dating method](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/abjad-numerals-as-an-absolute-dating-method-forts-from-al-ain-uae/).

### Magic squares and letter magic

Islamicate practitioners converted names, Qurʾanic phrases, divine names, and planetary correspondences into numbers and arranged them in *awfāq*. The operation joined mathematical magic-square construction with astrology, talismanic design and *ʿilm al-ḥurūf*. It appears in Arabic and Persian occult literature, including traditions related to al-Būnī and in materials transmitted with or compared to the *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm/Picatrix*.

This is not one uniform doctrine. A magic square can be purely mathematical, astrologically consecrated, or filled with abjad-derived names. For the early Arabic literature see [Noah Gardiner/Hallum, “New Light on Early Arabic Magic Squares”](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/117867/1/WRAP-new-light-early-arabic-literature-Hallum-2020.pdf).

## 6.5 Agrippa’s Latin table

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia libri tres*, completed in an early form around 1510 and printed in its definitive three-book form in 1533, integrates Hebrew, Greek and Latin letters with planets, zodiacal signs and elemental correspondences.

His Latin numerical arrangement follows the alphabetic-numeral model rather than modern A1Z26:

| Value | Latin letters in a common Agrippan presentation |
|---:|---|
| 1–9 | A 1, B 2, C 3, D 4, E 5, F 6, G 7, H 8, I 9 |
| 10–90 | K 10, L 20, M 30, N 40, O 50, P 60, Q 70, R 80, S 90 |
| 100–500 | T 100, V/U 200, X 300, Y 400, Z 500 |

J is treated as a form of I; U and V are not fully separate letters in sixteenth-century typography. The table therefore cannot simply be applied to modern 26-letter English spelling without editorial decisions.

Edition: [1533 Latin scan](https://archive.org/details/DeOccultaPhilosophiaLoc1533/page/n15/mode/2up). English: Agrippa, trans. James Freake, *Three Books of Occult Philosophy* (1651), [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/occvltaphilosoph00herm/).

## 7. Uses through history

### Exegesis and mysticism

- **Rabbinic literature:** gematria operates mainly as an ancillary or mnemonic interpretation, seldom as an independent foundation for law.
- **Midrash:** numerical equalities connect verses, names and narrative numbers.
- **Zohar:** letter values join symbolic readings of divine names, sefirot and scriptural phrases.
- **Ḥasidei Ashkenaz:** Eleazar of Worms and related writers developed extensive numerical prayer and divine-name exegesis.
- **Abraham Abulafia, thirteenth century:** combined gematria with **tzeruf ha-otiot**, letter permutation, vocalization, breathing and meditation. Scholem describes gematria, acrostic and substitution as parts of Abulafia’s linguistic method: [“The Name of God and the Linguistic Theory of the Kabbala”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/039219217202008008).
- **Baal Shem traditions:** divine names, permutations, numerical correspondences and amulets are attributed to miracle-working masters; individual stories require separate textual dating.

### Magic and amulets

- **Greek magical papyri:** alphabetic sequences, vowel strings, characteres, numbered names and isopsephic constructions occur in ritual texts from Greco-Roman Egypt. They should not all be called gematria: many are voces magicae or alphabet magic.
- **Sefer Raziel:** a medieval compilation drawing on several earlier Jewish magical and cosmological works; printed at Amsterdam in 1701, though components are older. It uses divine names, angelology, alphabets, astrology and numerical interpretation.
- **Arabic awfāq:** transform words and names into numbers for talismanic grids.
- **Picatrix:** integrates astrology, talismans and magical correspondences; later readers sometimes combine its procedures with abjad or Hebrew tables even where a particular calculation is not in the Arabic original.

### Chronography

Hebrew, Arabic, Persian, Ottoman Turkish and Latin authors created chronograms for buildings, tombs, books and deaths.

- Hebrew chronograms commonly omit the thousands digit of an *anno mundi* date and signal calculation through a biblical phrase.
- Persian and Ottoman poets made the chronogram a recognized literary genre.
- Latin chronograms normally count only Roman numeral letters I, V, X, L, C, D and M, often highlighted typographically. This is not Agrippa’s full Latin table.

### Polemic and prophecy

Revelation 13 generated repeated identifications of the Antichrist. Medieval and Reformation polemicists manipulated Greek, Hebrew or Roman spellings to make opponents—emperors, popes, reformers, nations—equal 666. “Vicarius Filii Dei” became a famous anti-papal calculation by counting its Roman-numeral letters, but it is not a normal papal title in the form polemicists require.

Such results are historically real as polemic even when philologically contrived.

### Modern occultism

- The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn used Hebrew gematria within a larger correspondence system derived from Kabbalah, Renaissance magic and nineteenth-century occultism.
- Aleister Crowley’s *Sepher Sephiroth* is a numerical dictionary of Hebrew words; it appeared in *The Equinox* and became part of the material later collected with *777*. [*Sepher Sephiroth* scan](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/equinox_1_8_1912/equinox_v1_8_text.pdf).
- Crowley’s *777* tabulates correspondences rather than constituting a single new gematria alphabet.
- James Lees created the **English Qaballa/ALW cipher** in 1976, claiming to solve Crowley’s call for “the order & value of the English Alphabet.” Its order begins A-L-W-H-S-D-O-Z-K-V-G-R-C-N-Y-J-U-F-Q-B-M-X-I-T-E-P. This is a documented modern invention first publicized in 1979, not an ancient English system.

### Modern popular numerology

The now-familiar “Pythagorean” English table cycles A–Z through 1–9:

- 1: A J S
- 2: B K T
- 3: C L U
- 4: D M V
- 5: E N W
- 6: F O X
- 7: G P Y
- 8: H Q Z
- 9: I R

Its historical form comes principally from the American New Thought writer L. Dow Balliett, especially *How to Attain Success through the Strength of Vibration* (1904), and was systematized by later writers such as Juno Jordan.

The “Chaldean” table popularized by Cheiro assigns:

- 1: A I J Q Y
- 2: B K R
- 3: C G L S
- 4: D M T
- 5: E H N X
- 6: U V W
- 7: O Z
- 8: F P
- 9: ordinarily unassigned

Cheiro presented it as ancient Chaldean wisdom, especially in *Cheiro’s Book of Numbers* (1926). No cuneiform source contains this English-letter table.

Thus:

- **Ancient fact:** Mesopotamian number symbolism and numerical cryptography existed.
- **Modern invention:** this A–Z “Chaldean” assignment.
- **Legend/marketing:** an unbroken Babylonian transmission of Cheiro’s table.

### Internet gematria

Contemporary calculators commonly label several unrelated mappings:

- “Jewish gematria”: English letters assigned values modeled loosely on Hebrew magnitudes;
- “English gematria”: A = 6, B = 12 … Z = 156;
- “simple gematria”: A = 1 … Z = 26;
- reverse, reduction, prime-number and cipher variants.

“Gematrinator” and similar tools make hundreds of ciphers searchable at once. This changes the evidentiary environment: with enough spellings, punctuation rules, languages and tables, a desired equality is usually discoverable. The calculator demonstrates arithmetic, not historical intention.

### Literature

Borges, Joyce, Perec and Oulipo all engage numbers, combinatorics, Kabbalah or constrained writing, but these categories should not be collapsed:

- Borges wrote repeatedly about Kabbalah, infinite libraries and combinatorial language; not every significant number in Borges is gematria.
- Joyce built extensive numerical and verbal patterning into *Ulysses* and *Finnegans Wake*; many proposed hidden totals remain readerly reconstructions.
- Perec and Oulipo used explicit formal constraints, enumerations and combinatorics. Perec’s lipogram *La Disparition* is documented constraint writing, not gematria.
- Claims that a novel contains a numerical code require proof that the spelling, text edition, value table and selection rule were available to and consistently used by the author.

## 9. Controversies and disputes

### 9.1 Mesopotamia, Greece or Israel?

The question has several different answers because “priority” can mean different things.

1. **Earliest name-number claim:** Mesopotamia, Sargon II, eighth century BCE.
2. **Earliest standard alphabetic numeral system securely attested:** Greek, by the fifth century BCE.
3. **Earliest securely attested word-sum culture:** Greek isopsephy in Hellenistic and Roman evidence.
4. **Earliest securely attested Hebrew decimal letter numerals:** late Hellenistic period.
5. **Earliest extensive rabbinic gematria:** Roman and late-antique rabbinic literature.

Sargon cannot establish Mesopotamian priority for an alphabetic system, because neither an alphabet nor a complete additive key appears in his inscription.

### 9.2 Did Greece borrow alphabetic numerals from Semites?

Greeks borrowed the alphabet from a West Semitic source, but the numeral system need not have come with it. The present mainstream position holds that the complete Greek Milesian system is attested earlier than complete West Semitic decimal letter numerals. Phoenician letters used as low ordinal numbers do not necessarily demonstrate the whole 1–900 pattern.

The issue remains open to revision by new inscriptions, but current evidence favors a Greek development of the complete alphabetic numeral system.

### 9.3 The “Pythagorean” claim

Ancient Pythagoreans undeniably treated number as cosmologically significant. Evidence that Pythagoras himself assigned every Greek letter a fixed value and calculated names is much weaker. Much “Pythagorean” literature is late pseudepigrapha; some texts were composed to make Pythagoras anticipate Plato and Aristotle. See the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2022/entries/pythagoreanism/).

Therefore:

- **Documented:** Pythagorean number philosophy.
- **Documented later:** Greek isopsephy.
- **Disputed:** direct authorship or invention of isopsephy by Pythagoras.
- **Legend:** Pythagoras learned a complete gematria table from Egyptian priests.
- **Modern invention:** the repeating A–Z 1–9 table presented as Pythagoras’s own system.

### 9.4 Egypt as the source

Arguments for Egyptian priority usually combine:

- Egyptian uniliteral signs;
- Egyptian decimal numerals;
- 28-day lunar symbolism;
- temple dimensions;
- Greek reports that Pythagoras studied in Egypt;
- later Coptic alphabetic numerals.

None establishes a Pharaonic word-summing practice. Egyptian number symbolism could have influenced Greek religious thought, but influence in symbolism is not evidence for a missing letter table.

Online “Egypto-alphanumeric” systems assigning values such as A = 1 or R = 100 to hieroglyphic ancestors are modern reconstructions unsupported by a recovered Egyptian key or a reproducible corpus of ancient word totals.

### 9.5 The coincidence problem

If a script has \(n\) possible letters but ordinary words cluster within a limited numerical range, many words must share totals.

Coincidence grows rapidly when interpreters may vary:

- spelling;
- defective or plene writing;
- language and transliteration;
- inclusion of articles or titles;
- final-letter values;
- ordinal versus absolute values;
- reduction;
- word boundaries;
- one of hundreds of modern ciphers.

A persuasive historical case therefore needs more than equality. Strong evidence includes:

1. a text that explicitly instructs calculation;
2. a table demonstrably current in that culture;
3. a spelling independently attested;
4. multiple mutually reinforcing correspondences;
5. manuscript variants explained by the same hypothesis;
6. thematic relevance;
7. a result not selected after searching an enormous corpus.

The Nero 666/616 theory is stronger than most because one spelling change explains an ancient numerical variant. “Ahavah = echad” is an exact and meaningful equality, but by itself cannot prove authorial design.

### 9.6 Scholem, Idel, Dornseiff, Barry, Weinreb and Kaplan

- **Franz Dornseiff**, *Das Alphabet in Mystik und Magie* (1922; expanded edition 1925), remains a foundational comparative survey. It treats alphabet mysticism historically across Greek, Jewish and magical sources rather than assuming all systems descend from one revelation. [German text](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1922__dornseiff___das_alphabet_in_mystik_und_magie.pdf).
- **Gershom Scholem** treated gematria as one component of Kabbalistic linguistic mysticism, important but often secondary to divine names, sefirot, permutation and the ontological conception of Hebrew.
- **Moshe Idel** emphasized varieties of ecstatic, linguistic and practical Kabbalah and warned against forcing all Kabbalah into a single developmental model. His studies of Abulafia place numerical equivalence within an experiential technique.
- **Kieren Barry**, *The Greek Qabalah* (1999), amassed Greek isopsephic material and argued strongly for Greek influence on later Jewish Kabbalah. The documentary Greek evidence is valuable; the larger claim that Hebrew Kabbalah is fundamentally Greek remains controversial. [Book record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Greek_Qabalah.html?id=w70rWN_-ANAC).
- **Friedrich Weinreb** produced symbolically rich Torah-number interpretations. They belong to modern religious hermeneutics, not neutral demonstrations of ancient compositional procedure.
- **Aryeh Kaplan** made Kabbalistic texts and meditative practices accessible in English and discussed gematria within traditional interpretive frameworks. His reconstructions are influential in contemporary practice but do not erase the distinction between medieval tradition and historically documented antiquity.

### 9.7 Forgeries and invented antiquities

Common false or unsupported presentations include:

- Cheiro’s English “Chaldean” table as a recovered Babylonian priestly alphabet;
- the Balliett/Jordan table as the personal system of Pythagoras;
- A1Z26 as an ancient Hebrew or Greek cipher;
- “Jewish gematria” calculators multiplying English letters by six as an ancient Jewish method;
- the James Lees ALW order as a cipher known to Crowley before 1976;
- hieroglyphic alphabet-value tables advertised as Pharaonic;
- arbitrary temple dimensions asserted to equal divine names without a contemporary text or stable unit;
- modern Latin spellings forced into Hebrew or Greek without historical attestation;
- claims that every occurrence of 666 was consciously designed.

These are best called modern inventions or retrospective reconstructions, not necessarily deliberate frauds. The historical error occurs when a recent table is represented as a recovered ancient artifact.

## 10. Open questions

1. What calculation did Sargon II’s scribes actually use to connect 16,283 with Šarru-kīn?
2. Did the relationship involve wedge counts, sign names, divine numbers, metrology, the king’s age, or several simultaneous puns?
3. How early did the full divine-number hierarchy become standardized?
4. Were number-syllabaries teaching aids, cryptographic keys, theological tables, or all three?
5. How directly did Babylonian commentary practice influence Jewish scribal hermeneutics?
6. Can any proposed biblical gematria survive controls for spelling variation and coincidence?
7. Exactly when and where was the complete Milesian 1–900 system formulated?
8. Did Hebrew decimal alphabetic numerals arise by direct Greek borrowing, parallel adaptation, or through a mixed administrative environment?
9. When did abjad values first pass from notation into sustained mystical interpretation?
10. What are the earliest securely datable Arabic, Persian and Ottoman poetic chronograms?
11. Which numerical constructions in the Greek magical papyri are genuine isopsephy rather than generic alphabet or number magic?
12. Can corpus analysis estimate how often apparently meaningful equalities occur by chance in Hebrew, Greek and Arabic?
13. How much of the supposed numerical design in modernist literature is documented in manuscripts and authorial notes rather than inferred after publication?
14. How should scholarship archive rapidly proliferating internet ciphers whose rules and databases continually change?

## 11. Conclusions

Mesopotamia supplies the earliest securely documented declaration that a monumental measurement was made to correspond to a ruler’s name. It also supplies a rich culture of divine numbers, numerical spellings, cuneiform cryptography and learned manipulation of signs. These are authentic precursors of gematria.

They do not supply a recovered universal cuneiform letter-value table. Sargon’s 16,283 remains an unsolved calculation, and Delitzsch’s wedge-counting explanation is a modern hypothesis.

Egypt supplies ancient decimal numerals, sophisticated symbolic numbers and cryptographic writing, but no demonstrated Pharaonic word-summing system. Greek and Coptic alphabetic numeration practiced in Egypt belongs to later multilingual Egyptian history, not to indigenous hieroglyphic gematria.

The most economical historical reconstruction is therefore:

\[
\text{ancient number symbolism and scribal cryptography}
\]

\[
\longrightarrow \text{Greek alphabetic numerals and isopsephy}
\]

\[
\longrightarrow \text{Hebrew, Christian and Arabic letter-number interpretation}
\]

\[
\longrightarrow \text{medieval Kabbalah, letter magic and chronograms}
\]

\[
\longrightarrow \text{Renaissance occult tables}
\]

\[
\longrightarrow \text{modern numerology and digital cipher proliferation}.
\]

This is not necessarily one continuous chain of transmission. It is a history of related technologies repeatedly reinvented wherever writing, number, divine names and interpretive ingenuity met.

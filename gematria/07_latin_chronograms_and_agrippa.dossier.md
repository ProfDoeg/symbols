# Latin letter-number systems: chronograms and Agrippa's gematria: Research Dossier

## Basic identification

### Scope

This dossier concerns two historically distinct Latin-script letter–number practices:

1. **Roman-numeral chronograms**: inscriptions, mottos, verses, titles, or epitaphs in which selected—or, under stricter rules, all—letters that are also Roman numerals add to a date.
2. **Agrippa’s Latin arithmancy**: a complete Latin alphabetic valuation printed in Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia*, Book II, chapter 20, in 1533. It imitates the units–tens–hundreds structure of Greek alphabetic numerals and Hebrew gematria and assigns a number to every Latin letter or specially constructed letter-group.

They share the general idea that letters can carry numerical values, but they do not use the same table, rules, or historical lineage.

### Script, period, and region

| System | Script | Secure period | Principal regions |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Roman-numeral chronogram | Latin alphabet; numeral letters `I V X L C D M` | Medieval Europe; confirmed examples by the fourteenth century, with older examples or datings disputed | Holy Roman Empire, Low Countries, Habsburg lands, Bohemia, Poland, Hungary; less common in England, France, and Italy |
| Agrippan Latin arithmancy | Renaissance Latin alphabet expanded to 27 positions | Printed in Cologne, 1533; drafted within Agrippa’s larger project from c. 1510 onward | Germany, Italy, France; later British and continental ceremonial-occult traditions |
| Modern “Jewish/Latin gematria” | Modern English 26-letter alphabet, usually adapted from Agrippa | Internet-era label; individual mappings derive variously from Agrippa or later English ciphers | Primarily Anglophone internet culture |

### Table A: Roman-numeral chronogram values

| Letter | Value |
|---|---:|
| I | 1 |
| V | 5 |
| X | 10 |
| L | 50 |
| C | 100 |
| D | 500 |
| M | 1,000 |

All other letters normally have value zero.

The governing operation is addition, not ordinary positional Roman-numeral syntax. Thus `IV` embedded in a word normally contributes `I + V = 6`, not 4, unless a particular author explicitly adopts a different rule.

### Table B: Agrippa’s Latin alphabet

Agrippa divides the alphabet into three groups modeled on Greek and Hebrew alphabetic numeration:

| Units | Value | Tens | Value | Hundreds | Value |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| A | 1 | K | 10 | T | 100 |
| B | 2 | L | 20 | U / vocalic V | 200 |
| C | 3 | M | 30 | X | 300 |
| D | 4 | N | 40 | Y | 400 |
| E | 5 | O | 50 | Z | 500 |
| F | 6 | P | 60 | J / consonantal I | 600 |
| G | 7 | Q | 70 | V / consonantal U | 700 |
| H | 8 | R | 80 | HI | 800 |
| I / vocalic I | 9 | S | 90 | HU | 900 |

The 1651 English translation explains the last four positions:

> “Seeing in the Roman Alphabet there are wanting four to make up the number of twenty seven Characters, their places are supplied with I, and V simple consonants … and hi, and hu aspirate consonants…”

“Simple consonants” means consonantal `I` and `V`, corresponding approximately to later `J` and consonantal `V`. `HI` and `HU` are not ordinary independent Latin letters. They represent aspirated or consonantal sounds in names such as *Hierom* and *Huilhelme*. Agrippa remarks that Germans use double `vv` for the latter, while Italians and French may write `gu`, as in *Guilhelmus*. Modern tables consequently render the end of the series as:

`Z=500, J=600, V=700, HI=800, HU or W=900`.

This modernization conceals Agrippa’s historically important distinction between vocalic and consonantal `I/V`. The primary witnesses should therefore be consulted before calculating early-modern spellings. [Agrippa, II.20, 1651 English transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agrippa2.htm); [1533 Cologne scan](https://www.e-rara.ch/cgj/content/structure/1343192); [1651 scan](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/threebooksofoccu00agri/threebooksofoccu00agri.pdf).

## The system in detail

## 1. Roman-numeral chronograms

### Terminology

Documented synonyms include:

- **chronogram**, from Greek χρόνος, “time,” and γράμμα, “letter/writing”;
- **chronograph**;
- **chronicon**;
- **chronostichon**, a dated verse;
- **eteostichon**, “year verse”;
- **chronodistichon**, a chronogrammatic elegiac or other distich;
- elaborate formations such as **eteamenchemerodistichon**, intended to encode year, month, and day.

James Hilton’s nineteenth-century compilations preserve thousands of examples and much of this antiquarian terminology. The *Jewish Encyclopedia* likewise records the names, although its claim that a Latin chronogram dates to 1210 depends on Hilton and should not be treated as securely established without rechecking the object or manuscript. [Hilton, *Chronograms*, 1882](https://books.google.com/books?id=upQDAAAAYAAJ); [*Jewish Encyclopedia*, “Chronogram”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/4372-chronogram).

### Ordinary rule

1. Compose or identify a meaningful phrase referring to an event.
2. Select the letters `M D C L X V I`.
3. Add their numerical values.
4. The total should equal the year or other intended date.
5. The numeral letters are normally distinguished typographically: capitals among lowercase letters, enlargement, rubrication, gilding, spacing, or superior type.

Example invented purely to demonstrate the rule:

`paX nobIs data`

Counting the marked capitals:

- X = 10
- I = 1

Total: `10 + 1 = 11`.

This is not a historical chronogram; it merely illustrates the operation.

### Inclusive and selective counting

Two practices occur:

- **Strict or inclusive counting**: every occurrence of `I/V/X/L/C/D/M` in the wording must be included.
- **Selective typographic counting**: only enlarged, capitalized, rubricated, or otherwise marked numeral letters count.

The second permits a writer to ignore inconvenient numeral letters in the same sentence. Such examples were sometimes called impure, imperfect, or “unclean” by later collectors. Terminology is not perfectly consistent across Hilton, continental handbooks, and modern summaries.

### Additive treatment

Chronograms collect numeral values additively:

`M + D + C + L + X + V + I = 1000 + 500 + 100 + 50 + 10 + 5 + 1 = 1666`.

Embedded strings do not ordinarily use subtraction. Thus marked `IV` contributes 6, not 4.

### Pure chronogram

A **pure chronogram** has at least one numeral letter in every word. Purity concerns distribution across words, not necessarily correctness of Latin or chronological authenticity.

### Natural chronogram

A **natural chronogram** arranges the numeral letters in conventional descending Roman-numeral order. A modern example often given is:

`AMORE MATVRITAS`

If its numeral sequence is read as `MMVI`, it gives 2006:

- M = 1,000
- M = 1,000
- V = 5
- I = 1

Total: `1,000 + 1,000 + 5 + 1 = 2,006`.

This is useful for defining the form, but it is a modern specimen, not an ancient or medieval attestation.

### Double and multiple chronograms

A **double chronogram** may encode the same year twice, or produce twice the year so that the total must be divided by two. Hilton records still more elaborate multiple forms. These are collector’s categories rather than a single universally enforced medieval rule.

### Chronogrammatic verse

Latin hexameters, pentameters, elegiac couplets, epigrams, and hymnic lines could all be made chronogrammatic. The challenge was double: produce grammatical, metrically acceptable Latin and supply exactly the desired numeral total.

### Worked historical examples

#### Gustavus Adolphus medal

A medal associated with Gustavus Adolphus is conventionally transcribed:

`ChrIstVs DVX ergo trIVMphVs`

“Christ is leader, therefore triumphant.”

The highlighted numeral sequence is reported by Addison and later collectors as:

`M D C X V V V I I`

Arithmetic:

- M = 1,000
- D = 500
- C = 100
- X = 10
- V + V + V = 15
- I + I = 2

Total:

`1,000 + 500 + 100 + 10 + 15 + 2 = 1,627`.

This is the year assigned to the medal.

**Text-critical caution:** plain-text transcriptions often capitalize an additional `V` because early-modern `U/V` typography is normalized inconsistently. If four Vs are counted, the result is 1,632. The intended historical reading is established by the distinguished letters on the medal, not by an uncontrolled modern transcription. Joseph Addison described the device in *The Spectator* in 1711; Hilton later reproduced it. [Hilton scan and explanation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/Chronograms%2C_5000_and_more_in_number%2C_excerpted_out_of_various_authors_and_collected_at_many_places%3B_%28IA_chronograms5000m00hilt%29.pdf).

Status: **documented early-modern object and reception**, with a **typographical-transcription problem** in secondary reproductions.

#### Queen Elizabeth I

`My Day Closed Is In Immortality`

The initials are:

`M D C I I I`

Arithmetic:

`1,000 + 500 + 100 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 1,603`.

The phrase commemorates Elizabeth’s death in 1603.

It is often called a “perfect” chronogram because the relevant initials occur in descending Roman-numeral order. However, other numeral-capable letters inside the words must be ignored; “perfect” here is an antiquarian aesthetic judgment, not strict inclusive counting.

Status: **documented post-event chronogram**, not necessarily contemporary proof of a 1603 composition.

#### Malplaquet

`LILICIDIVM`

Literally a “slaughter of lilies,” alluding to the French Bourbon fleur-de-lis and the Battle of Malplaquet in 1709.

Arithmetic:

- L = 50
- I = 1
- L = 50
- I = 1
- C = 100
- I = 1
- D = 500
- I = 1
- V = 5
- M = 1,000

Total:

`50 + 1 + 50 + 1 + 100 + 1 + 500 + 1 + 5 + 1,000 = 1,709`.

Status: **well-attested early-eighteenth-century political wordplay**.

#### Innsbruck restoration inscription, 1671

A frequently cited inscription reads approximately:

`restaVror post horrenDos ContInVo anno et VLtra perpessos terrae MotVs`

“I am restored after the terrible earthquakes suffered continuously in this year and beyond.”

Counting its specially displayed letters yields 1671. Because online transcriptions vary in capitalization, the monument or a diplomatic transcription is preferable to recomputing from normalized lowercase text.

Status: **documented monumental chronogram**; exact arithmetic depends on the original display.

### What does not count?

- Arabic numerals inside a phrase do not automatically become part of a traditional Latin chronogram.
- Ordinary occurrences of `I V X L C D M` may be excluded only in selective forms.
- `U` does not count as `V` unless typography, orthography, or an author’s rule makes it a numeral `V`.
- `J` does not count as `I` in an ordinary Roman-numeral chronogram unless explicitly so treated.
- Lowercase versus uppercase is not an intrinsic numerical distinction; it is a display convention identifying intended letters.
- The total is not normally reduced to a digital root.
- Equal total does not imply semantic equivalence. A chronogram encodes a date; it does not by itself assert that all phrases of that total share an occult essence.

## 2. Agrippa’s complete Latin valuation

### Source and purpose

Agrippa’s heading is:

> *Quae literae quibus numeris dicantur, atque de divinatione per easdem.*

The 1651 translation gives:

> “What numbers are attributed to letters; and of divining by the same.”

Agrippa calls the operation **arithmancy**—*arithmantia*—and presents it as divination through the numbers of names. He attributes letter-number divination to “the Pythagorians,” Aristotle, Ptolemy, Pliny, Terentianus, and an otherwise problematic “Alexandrinus.” These are Agrippa’s authorities and traditional constructions; they do not establish that his 27-position Latin table was transmitted from Pythagoras.

### Basic calculation

1. Fix a spelling.
2. Distinguish vocalic `I/U` from consonantal `J/V` where Agrippa’s historical rule requires it.
3. Treat `HI` and `HU` as special consonantal units only in their intended phonetic circumstances.
4. Substitute the tabular values.
5. Add all values.
6. Do not reduce the total unless applying Agrippa’s separate planetary divination rule.

Example using an unproblematic Latin spelling:

`ROMA`

- R = 80
- O = 50
- M = 30
- A = 1

Total:

`80 + 50 + 30 + 1 = 161`.

This example is explanatory, not quoted from Agrippa.

### Planetary remainder method

Agrippa instructs the practitioner to calculate the names of a person and both parents, divide the result by nine, and assign the remainder to a planet:

| Remainder | Planet |
|---:|---|
| 1 or 4 | Sun |
| 2 or 7 | Moon |
| 3 | Jupiter |
| 5 | Mercury |
| 6 | Venus |
| 8 | Saturn |
| 9, i.e. no remainder | Mars |

This is not ordinary gematric equivalence. It is a second-stage astrological classification.

For the demonstration `ROMA = 161`:

`161 = 9 × 17 + 8`.

Remainder 8 corresponds to Saturn.

Agrippa also says name-number comparisons may predict which combatant will prevail or which spouse will die first. These are **documented claims made in the source**, not validated predictive methods. [Agrippa, II.20](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op2.htm).

### Vowels

Agrippa reports, through Pliny, an older onomantic doctrine in which an odd number of vowels portends defects on the right side of the body and an even number defects on the left. The operative feature there is the **count of vowels**, not their Agrippan numerical sum.

This is an example of Agrippa compiling multiple kinds of name divination under one heading.

### No automatic digital reduction

The full Agrippan sum is normally retained. Reduction modulo nine occurs only for the stated planetary rule. Modern calculators frequently add:

- repeated digit reduction;
- “master numbers” 11, 22, or 33;
- reverse values;
- triangular, prime, or square-number tables.

These are not rules in Agrippa II.20.

### Spelling instability

Agrippa’s system is unusually sensitive to orthography:

- `Iesus` and `Jesus` do not necessarily have the same value.
- vocalic `V/U` and consonantal `V` occupy different positions;
- `W` may be interpreted as `HU`, double `V`, or a modern independent letter;
- `HI` and `HU` are digraph-like sound units, not merely the sum of H+I or H+U.

Consequently, many modern “Agrippa” calculators are adaptations, not diplomatic implementations.

### Worked Agrippan example: a modernized form

Using a common modern table, `JESUS` becomes:

- J = 600
- E = 5
- S = 90
- U = 200
- S = 90

Total:

`600 + 5 + 90 + 200 + 90 = 985`.

This should not be confused with Greek `Ἰησοῦς = 888`. Different scripts and tables give different results.

## 3. Comparative classic equivalences

These examples belong to Hebrew, Greek, or Arabic systems. They explain the traditions Renaissance authors were imitating, but none is an Agrippan Latin calculation.

### Hebrew: the Tetragrammaton

`יהוה`, YHWH:

- י = 10
- ה = 5
- ו = 6
- ה = 5

Total:

`10 + 5 + 6 + 5 = 26`.

Status: **standard Hebrew gematria**.

### Hebrew: אהבה and אחד

`אהבה`, *ahavah*, “love”:

- א = 1
- ה = 5
- ב = 2
- ה = 5

Total: `1 + 5 + 2 + 5 = 13`.

`אחד`, *eḥad*, “one”:

- א = 1
- ח = 8
- ד = 4

Total: `1 + 8 + 4 = 13`.

The equality is traditional and widely used homiletically. Equal value licenses comparison within gematric interpretation; it is not lexical identity.

### Hebrew: נחש and משיח

`נחש`, *naḥash*, “serpent”:

- נ = 50
- ח = 8
- ש = 300

Total: `50 + 8 + 300 = 358`.

`משיח`, *mashiaḥ*, “messiah”:

- מ = 40
- ש = 300
- י = 10
- ח = 8

Total: `40 + 300 + 10 + 8 = 358`.

Status: **later Jewish and occult homiletic equivalence**; it is not an explicit equation in the biblical text.

### Hebrew: Eliezer and 318

`אליעזר`, *Eliʿezer*:

- א = 1
- ל = 30
- י = 10
- ע = 70
- ז = 7
- ר = 200

Total:

`1 + 30 + 10 + 70 + 7 + 200 = 318`.

Genesis 14:14 says Abraham mustered 318 trained retainers. *Nedarim* 32a records the interpretation that Abraham went with Eliezer alone, since Eliezer’s numerical value is 318; Rashi repeats it in his commentary.

Status: **documented rabbinic exegesis**, not evidence that Genesis was originally composed to encode Eliezer. [*Nedarim* 32a](https://www.sefaria.org/Nedarim.32a); [Rashi on Genesis 14:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Genesis.14.14).

### Greek: Jesus = 888

`Ἰησοῦς`, conventionally calculated without accents:

- Ι = 10
- Η = 8
- Σ = 200
- Ο = 70
- Υ = 400
- Σ = 200

Total:

`10 + 8 + 200 + 70 + 400 + 200 = 888`.

Irenaeus reports and criticizes the Marcosian use of Jesus’ number. Status: **documented second-century Christian/Gnostic numerical exegesis**. [Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* I.14–16](https://www.logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/114.html).

### Greek: dove = 801

`περιστερά`, “dove”:

- π = 80
- ε = 5
- ρ = 100
- ι = 10
- σ = 200
- τ = 300
- ε = 5
- ρ = 100
- α = 1

Total:

`80 + 5 + 100 + 10 + 200 + 300 + 5 + 100 + 1 = 801`.

The Marcosian argument connects the dove at Jesus’ baptism with alpha and omega: `Α + Ω = 1 + 800 = 801`.

Status: **documented Marcosian interpretation as reported by Irenaeus**.

### Nero Caesar = 666

Greek-form *Neron Kaisar* transliterated into Hebrew as `נרון קסר`:

- נ = 50
- ר = 200
- ו = 6
- ן = 50
- ק = 100
- ס = 60
- ר = 200

Total:

`50 + 200 + 6 + 50 + 100 + 60 + 200 = 666`.

Without the final nun, `נרו קסר`:

`50 + 200 + 6 + 100 + 60 + 200 = 616`.

This elegantly accounts for the textual variants 666 and 616. Nevertheless, no surviving ancient interpreter explicitly identifies the Beast by this Hebrew computation; the Nero solution appears in modern scholarship in 1831. It is therefore a **strong but disputed scholarly reconstruction**, not a documented ancient gloss. [Oxford, “Concealed Claudian”](https://academic.oup.com/jts/article/76/1/109/8043235); [Revelation study](https://www.tyndalebulletin.org/article/29329.pdf).

### Pompeii: 545

A Pompeian graffito reads:

`φιλῶ ἧς ὁ ἀριθμός φμε`

“I love her whose number is 545.”

The inscription supplies the number rather than the woman’s name. Because many Greek spellings can total 545, her identity cannot be securely recovered.

Status: **documented first-century graffito**, buried in 79 CE; a classic illustration of ordinary social isopsephy rather than esoteric theology.

### Arabic: the basmala = 786

`بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم`

Using the ordinary abjad values:

- `بسم`: ب 2 + س 60 + م 40 = 102
- `الله`: ا 1 + ل 30 + ل 30 + ه 5 = 66
- `الرحمن`: ا 1 + ل 30 + ر 200 + ح 8 + م 40 + ن 50 = 329
- `الرحيم`: ا 1 + ل 30 + ر 200 + ح 8 + ي 10 + م 40 = 289

Total:

`102 + 66 + 329 + 289 = 786`.

Status: **documented Islamic and South Asian convention**, but not a Qurʾanic statement that 786 substitutes doctrinally for the basmala. Whether the initial alif of *ism* is counted depends on orthographic convention.

## Origins

## 1. Alphabetic numerals before Latin gematria

Letter-number interpretation depends historically on letters already being usable as numerals. That must be distinguished from mystical or exegetical exploitation of equal totals.

### Mesopotamian precedent

A Sargon II inscription concerning Dūr-Šarrukīn says he made the city wall’s measure correspond to the numerical value of his name. Modern publications give approximately 16,280 Assyrian cubits; 16,283 is also frequently repeated.

Status: **documented royal claim**, but the precise computational mechanism is unresolved in popular accounts. It is not evidence for a Latin, Greek, or Hebrew alphabetic table. The inscription may employ Mesopotamian scribal number symbolism, cuneiform word-values, or a learned calculation not fully reconstructed. Eckart Frahm’s discussion is a necessary corrective to claims that Sargon used “gematria” in the later technical sense. [Dūr-Šarrukīn summary and Frahm reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dur-Sharrukin).

The conclusion warranted by the evidence is limited: a ruler in the late eighth century BCE claimed an architectural measurement equal to his name by some numerical procedure.

### Greek alphabetic numerals

The Milesian or Ionic system assigned:

- α–θ = 1–9;
- ι–ϟ = 10–90;
- ρ–ϡ = 100–900.

Its normal functions were accounting, measurement, pagination, astronomy, and calculation. Once words and numerals used the same signs, isopsephy became possible.

Secure literary, inscriptional, and graffito uses belong particularly to the Hellenistic and Roman periods. The Pompeian 545 graffito and Revelation 13:18 presuppose a culture in which “the number of a name” was intelligible.

### Hebrew alphabetic numerals and gematria

The earliest surviving Hebrew use of letters as numerals is considerably later than the alphabet itself. Claims of biblical-period gematria must distinguish:

- composition employing symbolic numbers;
- later rabbinic calculation of a biblical spelling;
- a demonstrable authorial alphabetic sum.

Rabbinic gematria becomes explicit in late antique texts. The attribution of gematria as the twenty-ninth of the thirty-two interpretive rules associated with Rabbi Eliezer ben Yose ha-Gelili belongs to the later transmitted list. The rules are traditionally placed in the second century, but the surviving textual history is later and composite.

## 2. Latin Roman numerals

Roman numerals originated as a restricted set of numeral signs, not a full alphabetic numeral series. Their overlap with ordinary letters created the raw material for chronograms, but ancient Romans are not securely known to have composed chronograms.

Reports of Roman imperial chronograms are retrospective or unconfirmed. The cautious finding is:

- **Roman numerical-letter overlap is ancient**.
- **Latin chronogram composition is medieval**.
- **confirmed Roman-numeral chronograms appear around the mid-fourteenth century**, while earlier examples require individual authentication.

The *Jewish Encyclopedia* and Hilton report an earliest Latin example dated 1210. More recent reference works commonly place the earliest confirmed examples in the mid-fourteenth century. This discrepancy remains an open provenance question, not a basis for silently choosing the older date.

## 3. Hebrew and eastern chronograms

Al-Ḥarizi’s Hebrew poetry provides a chronogram dated 1205 according to the *Jewish Encyclopedia*. Thirteenth-century German-Jewish epitaphs supply further examples.

Abraham Firkovich published supposed Crimean Jewish chronograms as early as 582. Contemporary and later scholarship identified numerous Firkovich inscriptions and dates as altered or forged. His 582 specimen is therefore **disputed and probably forged**, not secure evidence for sixth-century Hebrew chronograms. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*, “Chronogram”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/4372-chronogram).

Persian and Arabic chronograms employ abjad totals, usually producing the Hijri year. Persian calls the chronogram or its concluding date-phrase **tārīkh**; Ottoman Turkish uses **tarih düşürmek**, “to drop/compose a date.” These traditions reached extraordinary poetic refinement, but their precise relationship to the first Latin chronograms has not been demonstrated. “The Latin chronogram came from the East” remains a **plausible antiquarian hypothesis**, not established transmission history.

## 4. Christian alphabetic numerology

### Revelation

Revelation 13:18, written near the end of the first century CE under common datings, directs the knowledgeable reader to calculate the Beast’s number. This is a primary witness to name-number riddling in early Christianity.

Irenaeus, writing c. 180, knew both 666 and the variant 616. He rejected confident contemporary identification and proposed Greek names such as `ΤΕΙΤΑΝ` and `ΛΑΤΕΙΝΟΣ` that total 666.

### Marcosians

In *Against Heresies* I.14–16, Irenaeus describes followers of Marcus who analyzed:

- alphabetic totals;
- the 24 Greek letters;
- 30 aeonic entities;
- Jesus as 888;
- the dove as 801;
- letters, syllables, bodily proportions, and cosmological groups.

This is a hostile report, but it is early and detailed. Status: **documented polemical testimony**, with uncertainty about how faithfully Irenaeus reproduces Marcosian teaching.

### *Pistis Sophia*

The Coptic *Pistis Sophia*, probably composed in stages in the third or fourth century, retains Greek letters, numbered mysteries, names, seals, and numerical cosmology. It belongs to the same broad late-antique environment of alphabetic and cosmological speculation, but it does not transmit Agrippa’s Latin table. Claims of a single continuous “ancient qabalah” running unchanged from the Marcosians through *Pistis Sophia* to Agrippa are **later synthesis**.

## 5. Medieval Latin development

Chronograms emerged because Latin already had numeral letters and because medieval intellectual culture prized:

- acrostics;
- anagrams;
- computus;
- biblical numbers;
- riddling inscriptions;
- figured and constrained verse;
- mnemonic artifices.

The earliest surviving examples must be assessed object by object. A line stating an ancient date may be a much later retrospective composition.

By the fifteenth century the technique was established enough to appear in inscriptions and manuscript verse. Its great expansion, however, came after print.

## 6. Renaissance Christian Cabala

### Pico della Mirandola

Pico’s *Conclusiones* of 1486 publicly advanced Christian Cabala as a tool for confirming Christian doctrine. He did not print Agrippa’s Latin table. His importance lies in making Jewish kabbalistic techniques part of a Christian humanist program.

Status: **documented intellectual precursor**, not inventor of Latin gematria.

### Johannes Reuchlin

Reuchlin’s *De verbo mirifico* (1494) and *De arte cabalistica* (1517) presented Hebrew divine names, letter symbolism, gematria, notarikon, and permutation to Christian readers. He retained Hebrew as privileged sacred language.

Agrippa learned substantially from Reuchlin but did not simply reproduce him. The modern scholarly account emphasizes Agrippa’s broader synthesis of Hebrew names with Neoplatonic, astrological, and theurgical *barbara verba*. [Stanford Encyclopedia, Agrippa](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/).

### Johannes Trithemius

Agrippa sent an early draft of *De occulta philosophia* to Trithemius in 1510. Trithemius advised discretion. His *Steganographia* and *Polygraphia* combined angelic language, cryptography, artificial alphabets, and combinatorial tables.

Trithemius supplied an environment in which alphabets could be transformed systematically, but the evidence does not establish that he invented Agrippa’s 27-place numerical table. *Polygraphia* uses a 24-letter Latin order and elaborate cipher tables. [Trithemius Corpus](https://trithemius-corpus.github.io/Trithemius-Corpus/works/prdl-24390_polygraphiae-libri-vi_style-c-cipher-key.html).

### Christoph Rudolff

A classical-Latin letter valuation appeared in Christoph Rudolff’s *Behend und hübsch Rechnung* in 1525. This is important because it shows that complete Latin alphabetic valuations were not exclusively Agrippa’s occult invention. The exact relation between Rudolff’s mathematical table, contemporary reckoning practices, and Agrippa’s scheme deserves closer manuscript comparison.

### Agrippa, 1533

The complete *De occulta philosophia libri tres* appeared at Cologne in 1533. Book II, chapter 20:

- presents Greek and Hebrew alphabetic values as models;
- expands the Latin alphabet to 27 positions;
- supplies a full units–tens–hundreds table;
- gives onomantic and astrological procedures;
- invokes Pythagorean and classical authority;
- grounds meaningful names in a cosmos created “by number, measure, and weight.”

This is the earliest securely printed and influential statement of the particular system now called **Agrippan gematria**.

“Gematria” is a convenient modern label; Agrippa’s own operative term is *arithmancy*.

## 7. Seventeenth-century expansion

### Chronogram golden age

The seventeenth and eighteenth centuries were the chronogram’s great era, especially in:

- Jesuit colleges;
- Catholic festival books;
- episcopal and princely entries;
- church dedications;
- bells and fountains;
- medals;
- theses and academic congratulatory volumes;
- wars and peace treaties;
- epitaphs;
- title pages.

Germany, Austria, Bohemia, Hungary, Poland, Belgium, and the Netherlands produced vast quantities. Chronograms were ideally suited to Baroque occasional culture: they joined learned Latin, display typography, arithmetic ingenuity, and commemoration.

Michael Boym’s *Flora Sinensis* (1656) reportedly used page chronograms pointing to 1655, Leopold I’s coronation as king of Hungary. The political purpose was patronage as well as display.

### Jesuit practice

Jesuit schooling trained students in Latin verse, emblem, rhetoric, and ceremonial publication. Chronograms became part of the festival apparatus surrounding saints, rulers, buildings, and victories.

Calling the chronogram “a Jesuit invention” is incorrect: the technique predates the Society of Jesus. Calling the seventeenth-century Jesuits major cultivators is well supported.

### Delft and the Low Countries

The Low Countries preserve chronograms on buildings, maps, medals, printed broadsides, religious monuments, and civic inscriptions. Delft belongs to the wider Netherlandish epigraphic culture, but individual Delft attributions should be verified against municipal records and surviving stones. Plain-text local-history pages rarely preserve capitalization accurately enough for arithmetic.

The Dutch concentration reflects:

- dense urban inscriptional culture;
- Latin-educated civic elites;
- confessional commemoration;
- print and medal industries;
- Habsburg and later republican political messaging.

### Gustavus Adolphus

Chronograms were not exclusively Catholic. The Gustavus medal demonstrates Protestant and martial use. Chronogrammatic pamphlets and medals could praise or attack rulers with the same technique.

### Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54) and *Arithmologia* (1665) assembled Hebrew, Arabic, Greek, Egyptian, magical-square, and number-symbolic materials.

Kircher distinguished or at least reported gematria, notarikon, and temurah, but frequently subordinated them to his universal Christian reconstruction. He could describe Jewish kabbalistic procedures as part of a “bad” Cabala even while appropriating their diagrams and techniques. His Egyptian decipherments are now known to be erroneous.

Status:

- his books and tables are **documented**;
- his historical theories of primordial Egyptian wisdom are **obsolete reconstruction**;
- claims that his hieroglyphic readings preserve genuine pharaonic gematria are **false**.

[Study of Kircher’s Arabic and Hebrew number magic](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/228272931/arab-article-p381_1.pdf).

### Postel and later Christian Cabalists

Guillaume Postel integrated Hebrew learning into an expansive prophetic and universalist theology. Christian Cabala in the sixteenth century also involved Francesco Giorgi, Paulus Ricius, Egidio da Viterbo, Arcangelo da Borgonovo, and Johannes Pistorius.

Their common importance is intellectual transmission, not uniform use of Agrippa’s exact Latin table. [Schmidt-Biggemann, *Geschichte der christlichen Kabbala*](https://www.geisteswissenschaften.fu-berlin.de/forschung/publikationen/publikationen_dc_2012/schmidt-biggemann/index.html).

## 8. Eighteenth and nineteenth centuries

### Criticism

Joseph Addison’s *Spectator* essay of 1711 grouped chronograms with anagrams and acrostics as an overworked species of “false wit.” His criticism documents both the form’s visibility and the growing neoclassical impatience with Baroque verbal ingenuity.

### Continued inscriptional use

Despite criticism, chronograms continued on:

- churches and statues;
- bells;
- restoration tablets;
- tombstones;
- medals;
- commemorative books.

They survived particularly well in Central and Eastern Europe.

### Hilton

James Hilton published:

- *Chronograms, 5000 and More in Number* (1882);
- *Chronograms Continued and Concluded* (1885);
- *Chronograms Collected, More Than 4,000 in Number* (1895).

Together they contain well over 14,000 entries, repetitions, retrospective examples, and related materials. Hilton is indispensable as a locator of objects and texts, but not every item is independently authenticated. His datings and transcriptions must be checked against monuments or early editions. [Hilton 1882](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/Chronograms%2C_5000_and_more_in_number%2C_excerpted_out_of_various_authors_and_collected_at_many_places%3B_%28IA_chronograms5000m00hilt%29.pdf); [Hilton 1895](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/77/Chronograms_collected%2C_more_than_4%2C000_in_number_-_since_the_publication_of_the_two_preceding_volumes_in_1882_and_1885_%28IA_chronogramscolle00hiltrich%29.pdf).

## 9. Modern occult transmission

### Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, founded in Britain in the late 1880s, systematized:

- Hebrew letter values;
- Tree of Life paths;
- tarot;
- astrology;
- divine names;
- color scales;
- magical grades.

Its central gematria remained Hebrew or transliterated Hebrew, not Agrippa’s Latin table. Agrippa nevertheless belonged to the order’s wider Renaissance magical ancestry.

### Crowley

Aleister Crowley inherited Golden Dawn correspondences through Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers, Allan Bennett, George Cecil Jones, and others.

- *Liber 777* appeared in 1909.
- *Sepher Sephiroth*, compiled with Allan Bennett, organized Hebrew words by numerical value.
- “Gematria” appeared in *The Equinox* I.5 in 1911.
- *777 Revised* appeared posthumously in 1955.

Crowley normally transliterated into Hebrew or used Greek isopsephy for calculations such as `Τὸ Μέγα Θηρίον`, “the Great Beast,” = 666. His importance lies in transmitting gematria to twentieth-century ceremonial occultism, not in inventing a standard English alphabet.

[1909 bibliographical record and scan](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/777_1909/777_1909.htm); [*777 Revised*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf).

### Balliett and “Pythagorean” numerology

L. Dow Balliett’s *The Philosophy of Numbers* (1908) and related writings helped establish modern English name numerology. The familiar system cycles the English alphabet through 1–9:

`A=1, B=2 … I=9, J=1 … R=9, S=1 … Z=8`.

Names and birth dates are reduced, and numbers receive personality, color, vibration, or destiny meanings.

This is now marketed as “Pythagorean numerology.” No ancient text of Pythagoras gives this 26-letter English table. It is a **modern American occult construction**, given an ancient pedigree through the general prestige of Pythagorean number philosophy. [Balliett, 1908](https://archive.org/details/philosophyofnumb00ball).

### Cheiro and “Chaldean” numerology

William John Warner, “Cheiro,” popularized a different table in *Cheiro’s Book of Numbers* (1926):

| Value | Letters |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A I J Q Y |
| 2 | B K R |
| 3 | C G L S |
| 4 | D M T |
| 5 | E H N X |
| 6 | U V W |
| 7 | O Z |
| 8 | F P |
| 9 | generally unassigned |

Cheiro called it “Chaldean” or “Chaldean-Hebrew.” He did not produce a cuneiform, Babylonian, or late antique Chaldean source containing this English table. No such ancient witness is known.

Status: **documented twentieth-century table; claimed antiquity unsupported**. [Cheiro text](https://www.svpvril.com/SVP_Library/Book-of-Numbers-Cheiro.pdf).

### English Qabalah experiments

Crowley’s *Book of the Law* II.55 says:

> “Thou shalt obtain the order & value of the English Alphabet…”

After Crowley’s death, numerous systems were proposed:

- English ordinal: A=1 through Z=26;
- reduction: 1–9 repeated;
- reverse ordinal;
- primes, squares, and triangular numbers;
- Agrippa-derived “Latin” or “Jewish” tables;
- various Thelemic orders.

No single English system has historical or institutional priority over all others.

### James Lees and New Aeon English Qaballa

James Lees devised the system usually called English Qaballa, ALW, or New Aeon English Qabalah in November 1976. Ray Sherwin first reported it publicly in *The New Equinox* in 1979; further work appeared in 1981–82.

Its letter order follows an eleven-step cycle through the English alphabet:

`A L W H S D O Z K V G R C N Y J U F Q B M X I T E P`

Values run 1–26 along that sequence.

Status: **documented modern invention framed by practitioners as discovery or revelation**. It is not ancient Hebrew Kabbalah and is not Agrippa’s table. [Hadean Press history](https://hadean.press/products/the-new-equinox).

## Uses

## 1. Dating buildings, objects, and restorations

Chronograms provided dates while simultaneously offering:

- praise of a patron;
- prayer;
- a saint’s invocation;
- explanation of a restoration;
- political slogan;
- epitaph.

They occur on façades, cornerstones, altar furnishings, bells, bridges, fountains, statues, and sundials.

Their practical value to historians is conditional: the sum may date the event described, the composition of the inscription, a restoration, or a later commemoration.

## 2. Tombstones

Latin, Hebrew, Persian, and Arabic traditions all used letter totals on tombstones, but with different alphabets.

A grave chronogram may encode:

- year of death;
- year of burial;
- age;
- a biblical verse chosen for the deceased;
- a pun on the name.

Firkovich’s altered Crimean epitaphs demonstrate the danger of relying on chronograms without epigraphic and palaeographic verification.

## 3. Printed title pages

Chronograms could conceal or display the date of publication in:

- titles;
- imprints;
- dedicatory poems;
- printers’ devices;
- congratulatory collections.

Bibliographers must count from the original typography. Catalog records that normalize `U/V`, `I/J`, ligatures, capitalization, or long `ſ` can destroy the evidence.

## 4. Political praise and polemic

Chronograms celebrated:

- coronations;
- victories;
- princely births;
- marriages;
- peace treaties;
- papal elections;
- civic anniversaries.

They also satirized enemies. Reformation and Counter-Reformation polemic generated many calculations designed to make a pope, reformer, ruler, or confession equal 666.

A familiar anti-papal construction, `VICARIVS FILII DEI`, can be made to total 666 if Roman-numeral letters are extracted:

- V = 5
- I = 1
- C = 100
- I = 1
- V = 5
- I = 1
- L = 50
- I = 1
- I = 1
- D = 500
- I = 1

Total: `5+1+100+1+5+1+50+1+1+500+1 = 666`.

The arithmetic is correct for that phrase. The controversy concerns whether *Vicarius Filii Dei* was an official fixed papal title and whether Revelation intends this Latin extraction. Treating the numerical fit as historical proof is methodologically circular.

## 5. Magic and talismans

Agrippa places alphabetic values next to:

- planetary magic squares;
- divine names;
- angelic intelligences;
- planetary spirits;
- seals;
- astrological images.

His primary magical computations are frequently Hebrew. Latin arithmancy supplies name divination rather than replacing the Hebrew tables.

Later occultists used numerical totals to:

- select planetary timing;
- compare names;
- construct sigils;
- match divine or angelic names to magic-square totals;
- classify ritual words.

### *Sefer Raziel* and Hebrew magic

*Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* contains divine names, alphabets, seals, adjurations, and number symbolism accumulated from medieval Jewish magical traditions. Its Hebrew calculations are ancestral context, not evidence for an ancient Latin Agrippan table.

### Arabic *awfāq* and the *Picatrix*

Arabic letter science, *ʿilm al-ḥurūf*, used abjad values in talismanic names and magic squares, *awfāq*. The Arabic *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm*, translated into Latin as *Picatrix* in thirteenth-century Spain, transmitted astral magic to Latin readers.

However, the *Picatrix* does not contain Agrippa’s 1533 Latin alphabet as an ancient table. Later Renaissance magi combined Arabic astral magic, Hebrew Cabala, and Latin learning.

## 6. Poetry

### Persian *tārīkh*

A Persian chronogram poet seeks a word or hemistich whose abjad total gives the Hijri year. The date-bearing phrase usually also comments wittily on the event.

### Ottoman chronogram poetry

Ottoman poets called the practice *tarih düşürmek*. Techniques included:

- a complete phrase total;
- “full” or *tam* chronograms;
- adding or subtracting words signaled by the poem;
- using dotted or undotted letters;
- producing the date in the final hemistich;
- multiple verses yielding the same year.

Chronogram specialists composed dates for accessions, victories, buildings, births, and deaths.

### Latin verse

Latin chronograms similarly joined date, grammar, meter, and topical meaning. The difference is that Latin uses only seven Roman-numeral letters unless an Agrippan system is explicitly invoked.

## 7. Literature

### Oulipo

The Oulipo’s official catalogue recognizes **chronogramme** as a constraint: a text in which Roman-numeral letters total a designated year. This is direct modern literary continuation of the Latin device. [Oulipo catalogue](https://www.oulipo.net/fr/node/4954).

Georges Perec’s works use elaborate numerical, lipogrammatic, combinatorial, and spatial constraints. It is legitimate to place him in the broader history of letter-number constraint. It is not established that his major novels encode Agrippa’s table.

### Joyce

*Ulysses* and *Finnegans Wake* contain conspicuous recurring numbers, Kabbalistic references, alphabet play, and numerical structures. Scholars have proposed gematric readings, especially of `ALP`, 111, 1132, and the Wake’s cyclical architecture.

Status: **documented numerical and Kabbalistic interest**, but particular gematric decodings often remain interpretive hypotheses. There is no single “Joyce gematria table.” [Study discussing ALP and gematria](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/9/11/375).

### Borges

Borges repeatedly used infinity, enumeration, combinatorial libraries, divine names, Cabala, and the idea of a total language. These make gematria thematically relevant, especially in “The Aleph,” “The Writing of the God,” and essays on Kabbalah.

No securely documented evidence establishes that Borges systematically composed major texts by Agrippa’s Latin values. Internet lists of “Borges gematria” should therefore be labeled **reader-generated numerology**, unless supported by manuscripts or authorial testimony.

## 8. Internet gematria and conspiracy culture

Modern calculators commonly offer dozens of simultaneous ciphers:

- English ordinal;
- reverse ordinal;
- reduction;
- reverse reduction;
- Agrippa or “Jewish”;
- primes;
- squares;
- trigonal numbers;
- Satanic and custom offsets.

The label **“Jewish gematria”** is especially misleading when applied to English. Many sites use an Agrippa-derived Latin series, not Hebrew letters. Some now openly acknowledge this provenance. [Example of the modern label](https://gematriacalculator.org/gematria-calculators/jewish/).

Internet “decoding” commonly proceeds by:

1. choosing a phrase after an event;
2. varying spelling, articles, plurals, abbreviations, and transliteration;
3. testing many ciphers;
4. retaining only the striking match;
5. treating equality as evidence of intentional planning.

This is a **documented modern use**, particularly in conspiracy videos and social media. It is not evidence that political elites employ the calculations unless independent documents show prior selection of the words and cipher.

The “Gematrinator” ecosystem helped popularize multi-cipher English calculation in the 2010s. Its mixes of ordinal, reverse, reduction, primes, and Agrippan-derived tables are modern software taxonomies, not a single ancient tradition.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Mesopotamia, Greece, or Israel?

The priority question is often framed too simply.

### What is documented

- Mesopotamia had sophisticated numerical scribal symbolism and Sargon’s name-measure claim.
- Greeks developed or standardized an alphabetic numeral system and used isopsephy.
- Jews adopted alphabetic numerals and developed extensive gematric exegesis.
- Latin chronograms exploit Roman numeral letters.
- Agrippa deliberately constructs a Latin analogue modeled on Greek and Hebrew three-tier tables.

### What is not documented

- A single continuous secret school transmitting one table unchanged from Babylon through Pythagoras, Israel, Gnosticism, Kabbalah, and Renaissance magic.
- A cuneiform ancestor of Agrippa’s precise 27-position Latin table.
- biblical Hebrew authors using the full later rabbinic catalogue of gematric methods.
- Pythagoras using a modern 26-letter English reduction table.

The best reconstruction is one of **related affordances and multiple adaptations**, with some genuine transmission but substantial reinvention.

## 2. The word “gematria”

The etymology is disputed. Traditional scholarship often connects Hebrew גימטריא with Greek γεωμετρία, “geometry,” or γραμματεία/γραμματική, writing or letters. Etymology does not prove historical priority.

Latin Renaissance writers generally spoke of *arithmantia*, Cabala, numerical letters, or divination by names. Calling every system “gematria” is convenient but can erase native terminology.

## 3. Agrippa’s Pythagorean claim

Agrippa says the Pythagoreans, Aristotle, and Ptolemy held that letters possess divine numbers. This establishes his intellectual genealogy, not the antiquity of his table.

Problems include:

- no writing securely by Pythagoras survives;
- ancient Pythagoreanism accumulated pseudepigraphic texts;
- Greek alphabetic numerals are not the same as Agrippa’s reconstructed Latin alphabet;
- `J`, consonantal `V`, `HI`, and `HU` reflect medieval and Renaissance European orthography.

Verdict: **Agrippa’s appeal to Pythagorean antiquity is a documented Renaissance traditional claim; the table itself is a Renaissance construction.**

## 4. The “Chaldean” table

Cheiro’s English table has no demonstrated Chaldean-language original. Its phonetic groupings and omission of 9 belong to modern occult numerology.

Verdict: **modern system with legendary ancient attribution**.

## 5. Coincidence and multiple comparisons

If a table assigns positive integer values to letters, many distinct words must share totals. The number of collisions increases rapidly with:

- vocabulary size;
- phrase length;
- alternative spellings;
- transliterations;
- multiple languages;
- several ciphers;
- reductions;
- optional articles or titles;
- arithmetic after summing.

An equality therefore has weak evidentiary force unless:

1. the text fixes the cipher in advance;
2. the spelling is historically appropriate;
3. the target set is limited;
4. the calculation is reproducible;
5. the match is contextually indicated;
6. unsuccessful comparisons are reported;
7. authorial intention is independently supported.

Traditional exegesis does not necessarily claim statistical proof: it may use equal totals as mnemonic, homiletic, contemplative, or poetic connections. Problems arise when post hoc equivalences are presented as exclusive historical identification or scientific prediction.

A recent survey of biblical numerical approaches notes scholarly criticism that systems disregarding coincidence become “false science,” while allowing that tightly constrained textual patterns may sometimes be deliberate. [Goodfriend, “Gematria in the Hebrew Bible”](https://jbqnew.jewishbible.org/assets/Uploads/524/jbq_524_goodfriendgematria.pdf).

## 6. 666 and Nero

Strengths of the Nero hypothesis:

- correct total for `נרון קסר`;
- 616 follows naturally when the final `n` is omitted;
- Nero and Nero-redivivus traditions fit Revelation’s imperial setting.

Weaknesses:

- Revelation does not tell readers to transliterate a Greek imperial name into Hebrew;
- no ancient interpreter records this solution;
- multiple names can total 666;
- the first known explicit Nero-gematria proposal is modern, in 1831.

Verdict: **important and often persuasive scholarly reconstruction, not established ancient interpretation**.

## 7. Earliest Latin chronogram

Three propositions circulate:

- Roman imperial examples existed;
- a Latin chronogram dates to 1210;
- the earliest confirmed Roman-numeral examples are mid-fourteenth-century.

The first lacks confirmation. The second derives from Hilton’s retrospective catalogue and needs object-level authentication. The third is presently the safest general statement.

## 8. Firkovich’s inscriptions

Abraham Firkovich’s early Jewish inscriptions were used to support very early Karaite and Jewish settlement histories. Alteration, selective copying, and fabrication have been extensively alleged and demonstrated within his collections.

Verdict on the supposed 582 chronogram: **probably forged or altered; unusable as the earliest attestation**.

## 9. Agrippa’s “Fourth Book”

The *Fourth Book of Occult Philosophy* circulated under Agrippa’s name. Johann Weyer, Agrippa’s former associate, denied its authenticity. Modern scholarship generally treats it as pseudonymous.

Its contents must not be used uncritically to reconstruct Agrippa’s own rules. The authentic three books and their manuscript/print history remain the foundation.

## 10. Kircher’s ancient Egypt

Kircher’s Egyptian translations were ingenious but fundamentally wrong because hieroglyphs were not the symbolic universal language he imagined. His use of Cabala, number, and divine names documents seventeenth-century reception, not authentic pharaonic gematria.

## 11. Barry’s Greek-priority thesis

Kieren Barry’s *The Greek Qabalah* compiles a valuable corpus of Greek alphabet symbolism and isopsephy and argues that Hebrew Qabalah has Greek origins.

The evidence strongly supports Greek influence in the Hellenistic and late-antique environment. The stronger proposition—that Jewish Kabbalah as a whole originated in Greek isopsephy—is disputed because medieval Kabbalah incorporates biblical exegesis, rabbinic traditions, Hebrew linguistics, Jewish mysticism, philosophy, and multiple historical layers.

Verdict: **useful corpus and substantial influence thesis; totalizing priority thesis disputed**. [Barry bibliographical record](https://books.google.com/books?id=w70rWN_-ANAC).

## 12. Scholem and Idel

Gershom Scholem treated gematria as one technique within the history of Jewish mysticism, important but often capable of arbitrary expansion. His historical method rejected timeless occult genealogies.

Moshe Idel shifted attention toward diverse Kabbalistic techniques, experiences, and models. In Abraham Abulafia, letter permutation, pronunciation, breathing, writing, and numerical equivalence form part of an ecstatic discipline, not merely a search for clever equal sums.

Neither scholar supplies evidence that Agrippa’s Latin table was an ancient Jewish system. Their work instead helps explain which Hebrew materials Renaissance Christian Cabalists reinterpreted.

## 13. Dornseiff

Franz Dornseiff’s *Das Alphabet in Mystik und Magie* (1922) remains foundational because it assembles alphabetic symbolism across Greek, Jewish, Gnostic, magical, and related materials.

Its strengths are philological breadth and primary-text collection. Its limitations include the age of its archaeological chronology and occasional readiness to join phenomena through broad diffusionist narratives. It should be used alongside newer editions and specialized studies, not discarded or treated as final. [Dornseiff scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1922__dornseiff___das_alphabet_in_mystik_und_magie.pdf).

## 14. Kaplan and Weinreb

Aryeh Kaplan’s translations and popular studies present traditional Kabbalistic techniques sympathetically and often clearly, but they combine historical exposition with practitioner-oriented reconstruction. Friedrich Weinreb’s numerical Torah interpretations likewise belong primarily to modern theological and mystical reception.

They are sources for how gematria is practiced and interpreted in modern Judaism and esotericism, not neutral proof that every proposed numerical structure was intended by biblical authors.

## 15. Modern calculator names

“Jewish,” “English,” “Latin,” “Agrippa,” “Satanic,” and “Chaldean” are not standardized software terms. Two calculators with the same label may:

- order J differently;
- merge or separate U/V;
- assign W=900 or W=double V;
- omit `HI/HU`;
- reduce totals;
- ignore punctuation differently.

A result is meaningless as evidence unless the complete table and normalization rules are stated.

## Open questions

1. What physical or manuscript witness lies behind Hilton’s purported Latin chronogram of 1210?
2. Which mid-fourteenth-century Roman-numeral chronogram is currently the earliest independently authenticated object?
3. Can Sargon II’s name total be reconstructed from the original Akkadian wording without importing later alphabetic assumptions?
4. How did complete Latin alphabetic valuations circulate before Agrippa? A comparison of Rudolff, Agrippa’s manuscripts, Italian onomancy, and Latin translations of Arabic works is needed.
5. Did Agrippa intend `HI` and `HU` as indivisible graphemes in all calculations, or only as contextual signs for particular consonants?
6. How consistently did seventeenth-century users preserve Agrippa’s distinction between vocalic and consonantal `I/V`?
7. How many apparently early chronograms are retrospective compositions copied onto later restorations?
8. Can a full digital corpus of Hilton’s examples be linked to surviving monuments and first-edition title pages?
9. What routes connected Persian and Ottoman *tārīkh* composition with European chronogram culture, if any?
10. How widely was Agrippa’s full Latin table actually used before the nineteenth-century occult revival?
11. When did the label “Jewish gematria” first become attached to the English adaptation of Agrippa’s table?
12. Which modern English systems can be traced to a specific first publication, and which emerged anonymously through software?
13. Can statistical baselines be developed for each historical language, orthography, and period to measure how surprising a proposed equivalence actually is?
14. Which Joyce, Borges, or Perec numerical readings are supported by notebooks or authorial testimony rather than retrospective calculation?

## Conclusions

The Latin material is not one system but a family of historically layered practices.

- The **chronogram** is a medieval and early-modern European date-making constraint built from the seven Roman numeral letters. Its golden age was Jesuit, courtly, civic, and Baroque, especially in Central Europe and the Low Countries.
- **Agrippa’s table** is a Renaissance construction printed in 1533. It consciously imitates the units–tens–hundreds structure of Greek and Hebrew alphabetic numerals and expands the Latin alphabet artificially to 27 positions.
- Renaissance Christian Cabala supplied the intellectual setting through Pico, Reuchlin, Trithemius, Postel, Agrippa, Dee, and Kircher, but these thinkers did not all use the same numerical system.
- Golden Dawn and Crowleyan occultism mainly transmitted Hebrew and Greek calculations. Agrippa survived as an authority and, later, as the source of a Latin/English table.
- “Pythagorean” and “Chaldean” English numerologies are modern systems with unsupported ancient pedigrees.
- New Aeon English Qaballa is a documented 1976 invention or, in practitioners’ terms, discovery.
- Internet calculators have generated a new multi-cipher culture in which labels are unstable and post hoc coincidence is extremely easy.
- Legends, theological readings, magical practices, and modern inventions are historically real as beliefs and practices even when their asserted antiquity or predictive power is unsupported.

# The occult English Qabalahs: Crowley, the Golden Dawn and the New Aeon systems: Research Dossier

## Basic identification

**Subject:** A family of modern occult systems that apply numerical, combinatorial, and correspondence-based interpretation to English letters, especially in the Thelemic reception of Aleister Crowley’s *Liber AL vel Legis*.

**Script:** Modern Roman alphabet, normally the unaccented 26 letters **A–Z**. Case is ordinarily ignored. Spaces, punctuation, apostrophes, and typographic ligatures normally have no value unless a practitioner expressly assigns one.

**Period:**  

- Golden Dawn background: c. 1888–1903.
- Crowley’s Thelemic problem: 1904 onward.
- *Liber Trigrammaton*: 1907; letter attributions added later.
- *777* and *Sepher Sephiroth*: 1909 publication context.
- Frater Achad’s AL/LA key: 1918–1919.
- English Qaballa or ALW cipher: discovered/constructed by James Lees in November 1976; first publicly reported in 1979.
- Trigrammaton Qabalah: R. Leo Gillis, first published 1996; expanded as *The Book of Mutations* in 2002 and later editions.
- Cipher X: E. Joel Love, late twentieth century; public documentation is substantially later and less secure than for ALW or Gillis.
- Calculator culture: chiefly twenty-first century.

**Regions:** Britain, especially London and the English Midlands; later the United States and international Thelemic and Internet communities.

**Core finding:** There is no single “English Qabalah of Crowley.” There are at least four historically distinct things:

1. Golden Dawn and early Crowley use of **Hebrew and Greek** gematria, with English used largely for transliteration and explanation.
2. Crowley’s unpublished or incompletely realized attempt to assign English letters to the 27 figures of *Liber Trigrammaton*.
3. James Lees’s independent 1976 **English Qaballa**, whose order begins **ALW**.
4. Later constructions—including Gillis’s Trigrammaton Qabalah and Joel Love’s Cipher X—which solve the same Thelemic problem differently.

Treating them as one ancient or continuous cipher is a modern conflation.

The figures in *Liber Trigrammaton* are composed of three positions, each occupied by a solid line, a broken line, or a point: \(3^3=27\) possible figures. Gillis interpreted them as the ternary numbers 0–26. One figure remains pre-alphabetic or “Nothing”; the 26 others receive Crowley’s English letters. Hence TQ begins at zero and gives **I = 0**. This has the unusual result that adding or removing I does not change a word’s sum.

“LCH Kabbalah” is a calculator label derived from the first nonzero letters. Gillis’s historically proper name is **Trigrammaton Qabalah** or **TQ**. Gillis first published it in 1996 and issued *The Book of Mutations* in 2002; later editions develop tarot, astrology, the Cube of Space, divination, magic squares, and base-three arithmetic. ## The system in detail

### Common calculation rules

For ALW, Cipher X, and TQ:

1. Normalize to the intended 26-letter spelling.
2. Usually ignore capitalization, spaces, punctuation, apostrophes, hyphens, and digits.
3. Replace each A–Z letter by its table value.
4. Add the values.
5. Preserve the unreduced sum unless a source explicitly applies another operation.
6. Two expressions are “equivalent” in the minimal arithmetic sense if they have the same sum under the same table and normalization rules.

Reduction to a digital root is not intrinsic to any of the three principal Thelemic tables. Practitioners sometimes:

- add the digits;
- reduce repeatedly to 1–9;
- factor totals;
- reverse digits;
- treat a result as a tarot, Hebrew, Greek, or *777* reference;
- use theosophical extension, \(1+2+\dots+n\);
- count letters, words, lines, or punctuation;
- multiply or divide component totals;
- transliterate a result into Hebrew or Greek.

Those are supplementary interpretive moves. They must not be silently incorporated into the primary sum.

### English orthographic problems

Unlike Hebrew and Greek alphabetic numerals, the English systems do not rest on letters that historically served as the language’s everyday numeral signs. Consequently:

- **I/J and U/V:** Modern English treats these as separate, whereas early modern Latin often did not.
- **W:** Historically a doubled U or V, but all three modern Thelemic systems give it an independent value.
- **Ligatures:** Æ and Œ have no standard value. They are normally expanded to AE and OE.
- **Diacritics:** É is normally E; transliterated Hebrew or Greek accents are ignored.
- **&:** Usually ignored or expanded to AND, but the two choices produce different results.
- **Digits:** In ordinary word gematria they remain numbers rather than letters; *Liber AL* II:76 is exceptional because it deliberately interleaves both.
- **Spelling variants:** QABALAH, KABBALAH, CABALA, and QABALLA are distinct strings and therefore distinct numbers.
- **Inflection:** As in Greek, a changed spelling changes the total; translations do not preserve numbers.

These choices create a large “researcher degree of freedom.” A responsible calculation records the edition, spelling, treatment of punctuation, and every secondary operation.

### Named inherited methods

The English occult systems borrowed the vocabulary and prestige of older literal Qabalah:

- **Gematria** (Hebrew גימטריא): numerical comparison of words.
- **Notariqon** (נוטריקון, from Latin *notarius*): treating letters as initials or expanding a word into a phrase.
- **Temurah** (תמורה, “permutation/substitution”): rearrangement or systematic substitution of letters.
- **Atbash** (אתבש): first Hebrew letter exchanged with last, second with next-to-last.
- **Aiq Bekar** or “Qabalah of the Nine Chambers”: Hebrew letters grouped by digital class.
- **Mispar hechrechi:** ordinary absolute Hebrew value.
- **Mispar gadol:** a “great” value, often including 500–900 for final Hebrew forms.
- **Mispar katan:** reduced or “small” value.
- **Milui:** spelling each letter-name in full and enumerating the expansion.
- **Kolel:** adding one for the word or totality.
- **Isopsephy** (ἰσοψηφία): Greek “equality of pebbles,” the Greek counterpart of gematria.
- **Ḥisāb al-jummal** (حساب الجمل): Arabic “calculation of the total.”
- **Abjad** (أبجد): the inherited Semitic letter order and associated values.
- **Taʾrīkh / madda-ye tāriḵ:** Arabic/Persian/Ottoman chronogram.
- **Theosophical addition:** summing all integers from 1 through a number.
- **Theosophical reduction:** digit-summing to a small residue.

The English systems imitate these operations by analogy. They do not inherit an ancient English manuscript tradition for them.

## Central Thelemic calculations

### ABRAHADABRA = 418

Crowley’s characteristic eleven-letter formula is commonly transliterated into Hebrew as אבראהאדאברא:

\[
א(1)+ב(2)+ר(200)+א(1)+ה(5)+א(1)+ד(4)+א(1)+ב(2)+ר(200)+א(1)=418.
\]

Thus:

\[
1+2+200+1+5+1+4+1+2+200+1=418.
\]

This is Hebrew gematria, not English ALW. The spelling is selected to produce and express Crowley’s formula. It consists of eleven letters and is associated with the completion of the Great Work and the union of microcosm and macrocosm.

### THELEMA = 93

Greek θέλημα, normalized ΘΕΛΗΜΑ:

\[
\Theta(9)+Epsilon(5)+Lambda(30)+Eta(8)+Mu(40)+Alpha(1)
\]

\[
9+5+30+8+40+1=93.
\]

### AGAPE = 93

Greek ἀγάπη, normalized ΑΓΑΠΗ:

\[
Alpha(1)+Gamma(3)+Alpha(1)+Pi(80)+Eta(8)
\]

\[
1+3+1+80+8=93.
\]

Therefore θέλημα, “will,” and ἀγάπη, “love,” are isopsephic at 93. “93” consequently became a compact Thelemic sign for the formula “Love is the law, love under will.” This is a documented Greek calculation central to Crowley’s movement, not an English-gematria discovery.

### AL = 31: Frater Achad’s key

In Hebrew:

\[
אל = א(1)+ל(30)=31.
\]

But לא, *LA*, “not,” is:

\[
ל(30)+א(1)=31.
\]

Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, developed the theological polarity AL/LA—God/Not, manifestation/nothingness—in his 1918 diary, later called *Liber Thirty-One*. He planned to send it in November 1918 but sent it to Crowley in September 1919. Crowley replied that the key “opens Palace” and believed Achad had fulfilled the prophecy that “one cometh after him” who would discover a key. [Achad, *Liber Thirty-One*](https://hermetic.com/achad/misc/liber31); [dated text and transmission history](https://www.otohungary.org/documents/publication/english/Liber_XXXI.html).

**Status:** Documented Hebrew wordplay and documented reception by Crowley. It is not an English alphabet table and does not constitute a reproducible decipherment of every element of II:76.

### Crowley’s own “ALW”

Crowley later attached English letters to the figures of *Liber Trigrammaton*. Since that order contains an A, L, and W at specific positions, later writers sometimes speak imprecisely of “Crowley’s ALW.”

This must be distinguished from Lees’s ALW sequence:

- Crowley’s Trigrammaton order begins **I, L, C, H…**, not A, L, W.
- Lees’s table begins **A=1, L=2, W=3**.
- Crowley did not publish Lees’s values.
- Crowley’s own New Comment says his Trigrammaton attribution was theoretically good but that “no Qabalah of merit” had arisen from it. [Crowley’s commentary on II:55](https://hermetic.com/legis/new-comment/chapter-ii).

**Status:** “Crowley invented the ALW cipher” is a modern error. The available documentary chronology assigns ALW to James Lees in 1976.

## *Liber AL* II:55 and II:76

### The command

*Liber AL* II:55 reads:

> Thou shalt obtain the order & value of the English Alphabet; thou shalt find new symbols to attribute them unto.

Crowley’s Old Comment says: “Done. See Liber Trigrammaton, Comment.” His later New Comment is more cautious: the attribution was good theoretically, but no meritorious Qabalah had arisen from it; he considered Sanskrit roots and Enochian records possible avenues for further work. [Text and comments](https://hermetic.com/legis/new-comment/chapter-ii).

### The riddle

II:76 reads:

> 4 6 3 8 A B K 2 4 A L G M O R 3 Y X 24 89 R P S T O V A L

The verse continues by calling this a “sequence” and connecting its solution with the identity or accomplishment of a coming interpreter.

The manuscript matters because III:47 says that editions should preserve Crowley’s handwriting and that mysteries lie in “the chance shape of the letters and their position to one another.” [Manuscript and text portal](https://hermetic.com/legis/index); [facsimile](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib31al/index).

### What is securely known

**Documented:**

- The string occurs in the 1904 manuscript.
- It contains both digits and letters.
- Crowley did not leave an uncontested, mechanically demonstrable solution.
- He treated Achad’s 31 key as highly important.
- He regarded the manuscript’s arrangement as potentially meaningful.
- He later attached letters to *Liber Trigrammaton*.
- Competing interpreters have supplied mutually incompatible “solutions.”

### Principal solution families

#### Achad: AL/LA = 31

Achad’s insight explains AL/LA polarity and much of his own reading of *Liber AL*. Crowley praised it. It does not by itself supply a unique substitution rule converting the full II:76 string into an independently checkable plaintext.

**Status:** Documented key; disputed as a complete solution.

#### Crowley: Trigrammaton attribution

Crowley apparently regarded his attribution of letters to the 27 trigrams as fulfilling II:55. He did not publish a developed corpus of English numerical equivalences from it comparable to *Sepher Sephiroth*.

**Status:** Documented attribution; incomplete as a numerical English Qabalah.

#### James Lees: English Qaballa

Lees’s order assigns the 26 values 1–26 in the eleven-step ALW sequence. English Qaballa practitioners found patterns in the manuscript grid, in line and word counts, and in the corpus of *Liber AL*. One widely repeated argument observes 17 items in one segment and 11 in another, giving:

\[
17\times 11=187,
\]

and under ALW:

\[
ENGLISH = E25+N14+G11+L2+I23+S5+H4=84,
\]

\[
ALPHABET = A1+L2+P26+H4+A1+B20+E25+T24=103,
\]

\[
84+103=187.
\]

Hence **ENGLISH ALPHABET = 187**.

This is arithmetically correct under ALW. It does not prove that the author encoded that particular phrase: corpus searches produce many other expressions totaling 187. A community critic reports 134 phrases in *Liber AL* alone at that value, illustrating the collision problem. [Contemporary dispute](https://www.reddit.com/r/thelema/comments/191c8pe/new_aeon_english_qabala/).

**Status:** Reproducible arithmetic; intentionality disputed.

#### Gillis: Trigrammaton Qabalah

Gillis reads the 27 figures as base-three numbers 0–26 and assigns Crowley’s letters to 26 of them. This supplies a coherent mathematical reason for the range and for zero. Gillis connects the system with 26 components of a cube—six faces, twelve edges, eight vertices:

\[
6+12+8=26.
\]

**Status:** Coherent modern elaboration grounded in Crowley’s figures and letter annotations; not documented as Crowley’s own numerical solution.

#### E. Joel Love: Cipher X

Love’s table begins KFW and is related by its expositors to inversions and transformations of the same Thelemic alphabet problem. Publicly accessible provenance is thinner than for Lees and Gillis, and much depends on later accounts by associates.

**Status:** Modern Thelemic construction; date and original documentary form remain open research questions.

## Golden Dawn, *777*, and *Sepher Sephiroth*

### The Golden Dawn inheritance

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn incorporated:

- the ten Sephiroth and twenty-two paths;
- Hebrew divine, angelic, and qliphothic names;
- tarot trumps assigned to Hebrew letters;
- planets, zodiacal signs, elements, colors, weapons, perfumes, and deities;
- Greek, Coptic, Egyptianizing, Enochian, and Christian material;
- Agrippan and later ceremonial magic.

Its distinctive achievement was not an English letter-number cipher but a large syncretic correspondence structure. English words served as headings and translations while Hebrew letters remained the numerical skeleton.

### *Liber 777*

*Liber 777*, published in 1909, is a many-column table of correspondences keyed primarily to the Qabalistic Tree of Life:

- lines 1–10 correspond to the Sephiroth;
- lines 11–32 correspond to the twenty-two Hebrew-letter paths;
- columns juxtapose Hebrew, tarot, astrology, gods, colors, animals, plants, magical weapons, perfumes, and other symbolic classes.

The number 777 is itself a Hebrew-Qabalistic total and a title, but the book is not a dictionary in which A–Z receive English values. Its method is analogical alignment: items on the same row are treated as correspondences.

Editorial notes to modern editions reveal variant spellings, corrupted Hebrew, transliteration problems, and some entries evidently adjusted to yield desired totals. One editor wryly observes that sufficiently determined gematria can prove almost anything. [*777*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/777/index); [1909/1955 PDF](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777.pdf).

### *Sepher Sephiroth*

*Sepher Sephiroth*, compiled by Allan Bennett and Crowley, was published in *The Equinox* I.8 in 1912, although it belongs to the project surrounding the 1909 *777*. It is a numerical dictionary: Hebrew and some Greek expressions are placed under their totals so that a magician can compare terms sharing a number.

Bennett began the collection; Crowley expanded and edited it. Bennett’s “A Note on Genesis” says explicitly that the dictionary was begun by Bennett and continued by Crowley. [Bennett, “A Note on Genesis”](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib2261); [*Sepher Sephiroth*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib500).

The work’s statement that certain supplied vowels are omitted because they have “no importance in Gematria” concerns vowel points attached to Hebrew consonants—not English vowels. Hebrew matres lectionis such as ו and י remain letters when present in the spelling.

**Dating correction:** Calling *Sepher Sephiroth* simply “1909” conflates it with *777*. The dictionary was prepared in the same intellectual project but appeared in *The Equinox* I.8, dated 1912.

## Historical antecedents

These antecedents explain the models imitated by the English occult systems. They do not demonstrate an ancient English Qabalah.

### Alphabetic numerals

Gematria and isopsephy become possible when alphabet letters also function as numeral signs. In the standard Hebrew, Greek, and Arabic systems, letters cover units, tens, and hundreds. A written name can therefore be read as a sum without inventing a second cipher alphabet.

English A–Z did not retain such a conventional numeral function. English gematrias are assignments made by authors, not ordinary alphabetic numeration.

### Sargon II, c. 713–707 BCE

Several inscriptions of Sargon II associate the circumference of Dur-Šarrukin’s wall with “the reading” or “number” of his name. The cylinder inscription gives 16,280 cubits in the current RINAP translation; some older publications give 16,283, depending on textual reconstruction and interpretation.

The inscription itself documents a number-name association. Friedrich Delitzsch’s 1878 proposal that the number was calculated from the numerical values of the component cuneiform wedges is a scholarly reconstruction, not a table supplied by Sargon. [Israel Museum/RINAP translation](https://museum.imj.org.il/journal/archive/2018-2019/pdf/cylinder.pdf).

**Status:** Ancient documented claim that wall length corresponds to the royal name; exact computational key reconstructed and disputed. It is not securely the direct ancestor of Greek or Hebrew alphabetic gematria.

### Greek isopsephy, Hellenistic and Roman periods

The Greek alphabetic numeral system used 27 signs: 24 ordinary letters plus digamma/stigma, koppa, and sampi. Clear isopsephic wordplay is Hellenistic and Roman; claims for Pythagoras personally using later Milesian isopsephy are retrospective and chronologically problematic.

The Pompeii graffito, buried in 79 CE, reads:

> φιλῶ ἧς ἀριθμὸς φμεʹ  
> “I love her whose number is 545.”

The graffito gives the sum but not her name. Any proposed reconstruction is therefore non-unique. [Discussion and primary epigraphic context](https://www.tmin.org/pdfs/Interpreting_Number_Words.pdf).

### Marcosians and Irenaeus, c. 180 CE

In *Against Heresies* I.14–16, Irenaeus reports and attacks the numerical-letter speculations of Marcus’s Valentinian followers. Their systems interpret names through letters, syllables, vowels, the 24-letter alphabet, and numerical totals.

Irenaeus reports the number 888 for Jesus:

\[
ΙΗΣΟΥΣ=Ι(10)+Η(8)+Σ(200)+Ο(70)+Υ(400)+Σ(200)=888.
\]

He also reports the dove, περιστερά:

\[
Π(80)+Ε(5)+Ρ(100)+Ι(10)+Σ(200)+Τ(300)+Ε(5)+Ρ(100)+Α(1)=801.
\]

The dove’s 801 is interpreted through alpha and omega:

\[
Α(1)+Ω(800)=801.
\]

Irenaeus is a hostile witness, but his attack is excellent evidence that Christians or Christian Gnostics were actually practicing this sort of exegesis by the late second century. [Irenaeus I.14](https://www.logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/114.html); [complete translation](https://openscriptorium.com/books/irenaeus-plain).

### Pistis Sophia

The Coptic *Pistis Sophia*, preserved in the Askew Codex and probably compiled in the third or fourth century, contains numerical cosmologies, repeated groups of emanations, and mysterious names composed of vowels and letters. It belongs to the same broad late-antique environment of alphabetic and vocalic speculation.

**Caution:** Not every number in *Pistis Sophia* is gematria. Claims that every aeon-name or sequence has an isopsephic solution require the underlying Greek reconstruction, not merely the Coptic translation. [G. R. S. Mead translation/Greek materials](https://www.bahaistudies.net/asma/pistis_sophia-greek.pdf).

### Rabbinic gematria

The Babylonian Talmud, Nedarim 32a, identifies Abraham’s 318 retainers with Eliezer:

\[
אליעזר=א(1)+ל(30)+י(10)+ע(70)+ז(7)+ר(200)=318.
\]

Rashi repeats the interpretation at Genesis 14:14. [Nedarim tradition indexed by Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Nedarim.32a); [Rashi on Genesis 14:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Genesis.14.14.3).

The twenty-ninth of the “Thirty-Two Rules” attributed to R. Eliezer ben R. Jose ha-Gelili names gematria. Traditional attribution places him in the second century. The extant recension contains later material, and Moshe Zucker argued for a much later composition. Thus “a second-century fixed list” is tradition, not an uncontested manuscript fact. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Gematria”](https://sefaria.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria); [“Baraita of the Thirty-Two Rules”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/2500-baraita-of-the-thirty-two-rules).

### Standard Hebrew examples

#### Tetragrammaton = 26

\[
יהוה=י(10)+ה(5)+ו(6)+ה(5)=26.

#### אהבה = אחד = 13

\[
אהבה=א(1)+ה(5)+ב(2)+ה(5)=13
\]

\[
אחד=א(1)+ח(8)+ד(4)=13.
\]

This love/unity equivalence is popular in later Jewish teaching. The arithmetic is exact; a claim that it was deliberately encoded by a biblical author requires separate textual evidence.

#### נחש = משיח = 358

\[
נחש=נ(50)+ח(8)+ש(300)=358
\]

\[
משיח=מ(40)+ש(300)+י(10)+ח(8)=358.
\]

This serpent/Messiah equivalence became especially important in kabbalistic and later Christian occult readings. Again, numerical equality is fact; the theological conclusion belongs to the interpreting tradition.

### Nero Caesar = 666

A leading historical interpretation of Revelation 13:18 transliterates Greek Νέρων Καῖσαρ into Hebrew as נרון קסר:

\[
נ(50)+ר(200)+ו(6)+ן(50)+ק(100)+ס(60)+ר(200)=666.
\]

A Latinized form without final nun gives 616:

\[
נרו קסר=50+200+6+100+60+200=616.
\]

The existence of ancient manuscripts reading 616 strengthens, but does not absolutely prove, the Nero hypothesis. It is one reason modern scholars generally treat Nero as more historically grounded than later Antichrist identifications.

### Persian and Ottoman chronograms

A chronogram constructs a meaningful word or verse whose abjad total is a year. Persian examples are documented by the fourteenth century. The phrase *meyl-e behešt* yields 787 AH in a poem marking 3 April 1385. The tomb chronogram for Hafez uses *ḵāk-e Moṣallā* to yield 791 AH/1389. Later poets made elaborate “dotted,” “undotted,” connected, and disconnected chronograms. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Mādda Tāriḵ”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/madda-tarik-chronogram/); [“Chronograms”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chronograms-pers/).

Ottoman **tarih düşürme** became a cultivated poetic genre for accessions, births, deaths, buildings, fountains, fires, conquests, and books. The chronogram often appears as the closing line of a *qıṭʿa* and may be literally inscribed on the commemorated structure. [Ottoman study](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/iusarkiyat/article/11441); [survey](https://turkoloji.cu.edu.tr/ESKI%20TURK%20%20EDEBIYATI/vildan_serdaroglu_coskun_ottoman_poetry.pdf).

## Uses

### Exegesis

Rabbinic gematria is normally a supplementary *remez* or homiletic clue, not an unrestricted replacement for grammar and context. Talmudic examples support legal or narrative arguments; Midrash develops mnemonic and symbolic equations; the Zohar integrates numbers with divine names, sefirot, and cosmology.

Abraham Abulafia, thirteenth-century Spain and Italy, placed gematria within a larger meditative discipline of:

- letter permutation, *tzeruf*;
- vocalization;
- rhythmic breathing;
- visualization;
- combination of divine names.

For Abulafia, equality could serve as a bridge in a controlled sequence of associations rather than as a stand-alone proof. Moshe Idel’s studies emphasize the experiential and linguistic setting of these operations. [Idel, *Studies in Ecstatic Kabbalah*](https://maypoleofwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/studies-in-ecstatic-kabbalah-moshe-idel-z-lib.org_.pdf).

Later Hasidic teachers, including traditions around the Baal Shem Tov, used letter transformation and numerical equivalence devotionally. Specific attributions must be traced to dated Hasidic texts; generic statements that “the Baal Shem invented” a particular cipher are unsupported.

### Amulets and magic

Greek magical papyri combine letter-names, voces magicae, vowels, repeated characters, numerical totals, and geometric layouts. Not every magical name is decipherable as isopsephy.

*Sefer Raziel*, a medieval compilation with earlier components, circulates divine and angelic names, alphabets, seals, and numerical correspondences. Dating individual recipes to “biblical times” is legendary.

Islamicate **awfāq** convert names or phrases into numbers and distribute them in magic squares whose rows, columns, and diagonals share totals. Al-Būnī, d. 1225 or 1232, became the pre-eminent attributed authority, although current manuscript scholarship warns that the “Būnian corpus” includes later recensions and pseudepigrapha. [Study of the Būnian corpus](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-28419-8_9).

The *Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm*, Latin *Picatrix*, joins astrological elections, planetary images, names, suffumigations, and talismanic operations. Abjad and magic-square practices belong to its broader reception, but the blanket claim that every *Picatrix* image is an abjad cryptogram is false.

Golden Dawn and Crowley practitioners used gematria to select or analyze:

- divine names for ritual;
- grades and magical mottos;
- pentacles;
- invocations;
- talismanic numbers;
- correspondences in *777*;
- new ritual formulae.

### Chronograms

Hebrew chronograms date books, title pages, tombstones, synagogues, and dedications. Often only marked letters are counted; “major” and “minor” era conventions may omit the millennium.

Arabic, Persian, and Ottoman chronograms encode Hijri dates in words or verses. Latin chronograms normally highlight Roman numeral letters—I, V, X, L, C, D, M—within an inscription. These are narrower than Agrippan full-alphabet numeration.

### Prophecy and polemic

Revelation’s 666 generated identifications of Nero, later emperors, Muhammad, popes, reformers, kings, Napoleon, and modern politicians. Reformation polemic made sums from titles such as *Vicarius Filii Dei*. Such calculations commonly depend on:

- selecting a particular title;
- choosing Latin rather than the subject’s vernacular;
- counting only Roman numeral letters;
- accepting or rejecting U as V;
- ignoring inconvenient letters.

The arithmetic can be correct while the premise that the title is official, characteristic, or intended by Revelation is false or disputed.

### Poetry and literature

Persian and Ottoman chronogram poetry is the clearest literary genre founded directly on letter values.

Claims for gematria in Borges, Joyce, Perec, and Oulipo range from documented authorial constraint to reader-produced pattern hunting:

- Perec and Oulipo certainly practiced formal numerical and alphabetic constraints, but not every constraint is gematria.
- Joyce made deliberate and extensive numerical jokes; assigning a later English cipher to his text without manuscript or authorial evidence is speculative.
- Borges wrote about Kabbalah, divine names, infinite libraries, and combinatorial language; claims that particular English translations encode fixed gematria are normally modern readings.
- A genuine demonstration requires an authorial note, manuscript calculation, or a pattern too structurally constrained to be explained by ordinary coincidence.

### Popular numerology

The familiar “Pythagorean” English name-number table cycles A–I through 1–9 and repeats. No surviving source from Pythagoras assigns the modern 26-letter English alphabet this table. It is a modern numerological construction named retrospectively for the prestige of Pythagorean number philosophy.

The so-called “Chaldean” table assigns phonetic groups values 1–8, often reserving 9 as sacred. Its present English form is documented in nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century occult numerology, especially writers such as L. Dow Balliett and Cheiro. It is not a recovered cuneiform Chaldean name table.

Cheiro—William John Warner, 1866–1936—popularized a “Chaldean” or “Hebrew” name system through celebrity readings and mass-market books. His claim of Eastern initiation is part of his authorial legend; it is not equivalent to a datable Babylonian chain of transmission.

### Internet gematria

Sites such as Gematrinator automate dozens of ciphers, reverse ciphers, reductions, prime assignments, and databases of phrases. The site describes itself as online since 2017. It formerly labeled an English adaptation of Agrippa’s Latin table “Jewish” and now calls it “Latin.” [Gematrinator calculator](https://gematrinator.com/calculator); [FAQ](https://gematrinator.com/faq).

Internet usage differs from historical exegesis because it can:

- search huge corpora after the fact;
- change ciphers without a prior hypothesis;
- retain hits and discard misses;
- vary spelling and phrasing;
- generate database matches automatically;
- mix dates, arithmetic operations, coordinates, and word totals.

This greatly increases the number of striking-looking coincidences. It also makes the method useful for creative play, divination, or community myth-making—uses that need not claim historical proof.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Mesopotamian, Greek, or Jewish priority?

Three different questions are often confused:

1. Who first associated a name with a number?
2. Who first used alphabet letters as numerals?
3. Who first used equal word sums as an exegetical method?

Sargon supplies very early evidence for a name-number correspondence, but not a secure alphabetic table ancestral to later gematria. Greek isopsephy rests on a clearly documented alphabetic numeral system. Jewish gematria becomes explicit in rabbinic sources, while possible biblical examples are retroactive readings rather than labeled calculations.

Kieren Barry argues strongly for Greek priority and influence. Franz Dornseiff surveys the larger ancient world of alphabet mysticism. Gershom Scholem treats Jewish gematria within the development of Jewish mystical and exegetical traditions. The evidence supports contact and shared Hellenistic environments more securely than a single inventor or a simple one-way genealogy. [Barry](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Greek_Qabalah.html?id=w70rWN_-ANAC); [Dornseiff PDF](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1922__dornseiff___das_alphabet_in_mystik_und_magie.pdf).

### 2. The Pythagorean claim

**Tradition:** Pythagoras taught that number structures reality.

**Documented ancient fact:** Pythagorean schools cultivated numerical cosmology, ratios, musical harmony, and figured numbers.

**Unsupported extension:** Pythagoras used the modern 1–9 English name-number table.

The 26-letter Roman alphabet did not exist in its modern English form in Pythagoras’s lifetime, and no ancient Pythagorean text supplies the modern mapping. “Pythagorean numerology” is therefore a modern branding of a system inspired broadly by Pythagorean number mysticism.

### 3. The “Chaldean” claim

Ancient Mesopotamia possessed sophisticated mathematics, omen sciences, and scribal letter play. That does not authenticate the modern English Chaldean name table. The table’s recoverable history lies in modern occult numerology, especially nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century publications.

“Chaldean” functions largely as an authority claim: ancient, oriental, astrological, and priestly. A cuneiform tablet giving the standard modern English assignments has not been produced.

### 4. Did Crowley solve the English alphabet?

Crowley said “Done” in his Old Comment, pointing to *Liber Trigrammaton*, but later admitted that no worthwhile Qabalah had arisen from it. No surviving Crowley text lays out Lees’s A=1, L=2, W=3 table. The best evidence therefore supports:

- Crowley created the problem and one symbolic attribution;
- Lees created or discovered ALW independently in 1976;
- Gillis later supplied a base-three numerical interpretation of Crowley’s Trigrammaton order.

### 5. Is ALW “revealed” or invented?

Lees and his associates used discovery language. Historically, the neutral statement is that Lees first reported obtaining the table in November 1976. Whether it was supernaturally revealed is a theological or experiential claim, not one historical method can decide.

The eleven-step cycle is mathematically simple. Many analogous complete permutations exist: every step coprime to 26 generates one. Its privileged status therefore requires evidence from successful predictions or tightly constrained textual structures, not merely the existence of a pattern.

### 6. The manuscript-grid derivation

Later accounts derive the ALW order from a diagonal on the ruled manuscript page of *Liber AL* III:47. Some insiders dispute whether the grid actually caused Lees’s discovery, arguing that the eleven-step order came first and the manuscript alignment was noticed afterward.

The chronological question can be resolved only from dated notebooks, correspondence, and the original *New Equinox* record. Later retrospective diagrams do not by themselves prove the order of discovery.

### 7. Coincidence and multiple comparisons

For an alphabet of 26 positive values and ordinary English word lengths, totals cluster in a comparatively narrow range. Many unrelated words must share values.

If researchers may also choose among:

- ALW, TQ, Cipher X, ordinal, reverse, reduction, Agrippa, “Chaldean,” and prime tables;
- alternative spellings;
- initials and expansions;
- additions, products, factors, reversals, and digital roots;
- arbitrary word or line boundaries;

then the number of possible tests becomes enormous. A hit selected afterward has little evidential weight without disclosure of how many alternatives were tried.

A strong claim would specify in advance:

- one source text and edition;
- one table;
- one normalization rule;
- one target;
- one permitted operation;
- a result unlikely under an appropriate comparison corpus.

Gematria traditionally functions as associative exegesis or divination; it need not meet this statistical standard unless it is offered as historical or scientific proof.

### 8. *Sepher Sephiroth* as evidence

*Sepher Sephiroth* is invaluable evidence for Bennett’s and Crowley’s actual practice. It is not a critical lexicon of biblical Hebrew. Problems include variant transliterations, nonstandard spellings, mixed languages, editorial errors, occult coinages, and entries chosen precisely because they support magical equations.

The correct scholarly use is to ask, “What did Bennett and Crowley count, and why?” rather than “What is the definitive ancient value of this translated English phrase?”

### 9. Forgeries and false antiquity

No ancient manuscript is known for:

- ALW/NAEQ;
- Cipher X;
- Gillis’s TQ numerical table;
- English Ordinal A=1…Z=26 as a sacred system;
- “English Sumerian” A=6…Z=156;
- the modern English “Jewish gematria” calculator cipher;
- the modern 1–9 “Pythagorean” English table;
- the modern “Chaldean” English name table.

These are modern inventions or adaptations. Calling them “ancient” without qualification is false even if their authors drew inspiration from genuinely ancient number mysticism.

The label “English Sumerian” is particularly misleading: multiplying English ordinal values by six evokes sexagesimal associations but is not a recovered Sumerian orthographic practice.

### 10. Scholarship versus insider literature

- **Dornseiff** supplies historical-philological comparison, though his 1922 conclusions require updating.
- **Scholem** situates gematria within Jewish mysticism and warns against flattening Kabbalah into universal occultism.
- **Idel** emphasizes technique, experience, language, and the diversity of Kabbalistic schools.
- **Barry** assembles a valuable Greek corpus but advances a strong Greek-priority thesis that exceeds scholarly consensus.
- **Aryeh Kaplan** offers accessible traditional and meditative reconstructions; his historical datings and experiential syntheses should be separated.
- **Friedrich Weinreb** presents spiritually creative numerical Torah readings, not conventional historical-critical demonstrations.
- **Thelemic authors** are indispensable primary witnesses to belief and practice but are not independent confirmation that a cipher was intended by the 1904 text.
- **Calculator sites** accurately implement tables when coded correctly but are generally poor authorities for provenance.

## Open questions

1. Where are James Lees’s earliest dated notebooks showing the November 1976 construction?
2. Does the manuscript evidence establish that the ruled-page diagonal generated ALW, or was it a later confirmation?
3. What precisely appeared in Ray Sherwin’s 1979 editorial, and how did the 1981–82 articles formulate the rules?
4. Can Trevor Langford’s 1984–85 software, source code, or output be recovered?
5. What is the earliest primary document for E. Joel Love’s Cipher X?
6. When did the names “ALW cipher,” “NAEQ,” “KFW Kabbalah,” and “LCH Kabbalah” first appear in print?
7. Which English letters in Crowley’s annotated *Liber Trigrammaton* are autograph, when were they entered, and what changes appear across witnesses?
8. Did Crowley intend numerical values for the trigrams, or only qualitative symbols?
9. Can any proposed solution of II:76 decode the entire sequence by one rule without discretionary spelling, segmentation, or post-hoc operations?
10. How many apparent “hits” in *Liber AL* occur under each cipher relative to matched English control corpora?
11. How sensitive are celebrated equivalences to punctuation, manuscript spelling, editorial corrections, and inclusion of titles?
12. Which entries in *Sepher Sephiroth* derive from Bennett, Crowley, Golden Dawn manuscripts, older Jewish sources, or later editorial repair?
13. How far did Agrippa’s Latin table influence nineteenth-century English numerology directly?
14. What are the first printed witnesses for the currently called Pythagorean and Chaldean English tables?
15. Which literary claims about Borges, Joyce, Perec, and Oulipo rest on authorial evidence rather than retrofitted calculator searches?

## Conclusions

The occult English Qabalahs are modern descendants by analogy—not direct survivals—of Hebrew gematria, Greek isopsephy, Arabic abjad, Renaissance Latin letter-number tables, and Golden Dawn correspondence-making.

The documentary sequence is:

1. Ancient cultures used letters or signs numerically.
2. Jewish, Greek, Christian, and Islamicate interpreters developed exegetical, magical, and chronographic uses.
3. Agrippa deliberately adapted the units–tens–hundreds model to Latin in 1533.
4. The Golden Dawn organized Hebrew Qabalah and comparative occult correspondences but did not leave a standard 26-letter English gematria.
5. Crowley made Hebrew and Greek number-symbolism central to Thelema, posed the English-alphabet problem in 1904, and attached English letters to *Liber Trigrammaton* after 1907.
6. Achad discovered the Hebrew AL/LA = 31 key in 1918.
7. James Lees supplied the first well-documented modern English Qaballa table, ALW, in 1976.
8. Gillis, Love, and others created alternative solutions.
9. Internet calculators detached these tables from their source traditions, renamed them, multiplied the number of searchable ciphers, and enabled both creative exploration and uncontrolled coincidence hunting.

The arithmetic of these systems is objective once a table and spelling are fixed. Their historical provenance, intended textual meaning, supernatural authority, and evidential force are separate questions. Confusing those levels is the principal source of both occult legend and modern misinformation.

# Gematria as mystical practice: Abulafia, the Names and the letter-permutation: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary key

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of claim:

- **[D: documented]** directly attested in a surviving primary text, inscription, manuscript, or early printed edition.
- **[R: reconstruction]** a modern scholarly inference from dated evidence.
- **[T: tradition]** a claim transmitted within a religious or esoteric community, whether or not historically demonstrable.
- **[X: disputed]** a claim for which competing scholarly readings or uncertain textual evidence exist.
- **[L: legend]** a retrospective story not supported by contemporary evidence.
- **[M: modern invention]** a system or attribution demonstrably created in modern times but often advertised as ancient.

“Gematria” is used here narrowly for numerical interpretation of letters and words, but the dossier also covers its historically entangled neighbors: alphabetic numerals, Greek isopsephy, Arabic *ḥisāb al-jummal*, permutation (*ṣeruf*), substitution (*temurah*), acronymic interpretation (*notariqon*), divine-name construction, chronograms, magic squares, and modern name numerology.

# Basic identification

## Name

- Hebrew: גימטריא / גימטרייה, *gimaṭriyya*.
- Greek analogue: ἰσοψηφία, *isopsēphia*, literally “equality of pebbles/count.”
- Arabic and Persian: حساب الجُمَّل, *ḥisāb al-jummal*, “calculation of the total”; حساب أبجد, *ḥisāb abjad*.
- Ottoman Turkish: *ebced hesabı*.
- Chronogram: Hebrew גימטריא של תאריך; Persian مادّهٔ تاریخ, *mādda-ye tārīkh*; Ottoman *tarih düşürme*.
- Letter combination: חכמת הצירוף, *ḥokhmat ha-ṣeruf*, “science/wisdom of combination”; צירוף אותיות, *ṣeruf otiyyot*.
- Letter substitution: תמורה, *temurah*.
- Acronym or expansion: נוטריקון, *notariqon*.

The Hebrew term is normally explained as a borrowing from Greek γεωμετρία, “geometry,” although the semantic route is not completely transparent. A once-popular derivation from Greek γραμματεία, “writing/learning,” is linguistically tempting but less securely attested.

## Principal script, period, and region

**Core system:** Hebrew consonantal alphabet.

**Values:** alphabetic decimal series: 1–9, 10–90, 100–400.

**Period securely attested as word-number interpretation:** Roman-period rabbinic Judaism, especially second-century tannaitic traditions as preserved in later compilations and the Babylonian Talmud.

**Region:** Roman Palestine and Babylonia; transformed into a major mystical practice in medieval Germany, Spain, Italy, Byzantium, and Safed; transmitted into Christian Kabbalah and European ceremonial magic.

**Abulafian form:** late thirteenth century, chiefly Spain, Italy, Sicily, Greece, and the eastern Mediterranean. Abraham Abulafia lived c. 1240–after 1291.

### Final letters

In ordinary or “necessary” gematria the five final forms have the same values as their medial forms:

- ך = כ = 20
- ם = מ = 40
- ן = נ = 50
- ף = פ = 80
- ץ = צ = 90

In *mispar gadol* or certain “large-number” schemes, the final forms continue the hundreds:

- ך = 500
- ם = 600
- ן = 700
- ף = 800
- ץ = 900

These enlarged finals are a secondary convention, not the ordinary basis of classical rabbinic examples. Thus מטטרון, Metatron, ends in ordinary nun = 50, not final nun = 700.

### Vowels, points, accents, and punctuation

Standard Hebrew gematria counts written consonant letters:

- Masoretic vowel points do not normally count.
- Cantillation marks do not count.
- Apostrophe-like marks, quotation marks, maqaf, spaces, and punctuation do not count.
- *Matres lectionis*—א ה ו י when actually written—do count because they are letters.
- Defective and plene spelling can therefore change the result.
- A supplied but unwritten vowel does not count.
- A ligature or scribal abbreviation must be expanded only if the source or practitioner says to expand it.

There is no universal rule allowing one to modernize spelling while retaining the claimed ancient sum. A valid historical argument must specify the actual textual spelling being counted.

# The system in detail

## 1. Ordinary addition

For a word \(L_1L_2\ldots L_n\):

\[
G(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v(L_i)
\]

A phrase is normally the sum of all its written letters, ignoring spaces and punctuation. Whether articles, conjunctions, divine-name substitutions, abbreviations, or honorifics are included depends on the source.

Two expressions are “equivalent” in the minimal numerical sense when their totals match. Kabbalists may regard the equality as evidence of analogy, shared root, corresponding power, or exegetical permission—but equal totals alone do not specify what kind of relationship is intended.

## 2. Reduction is optional, not standard

Ordinary Hebrew gematria does not automatically reduce a total to one digit. Reduction belongs to named sub-methods. Thus 358 remains 358 unless the interpreter explicitly invokes a reduced method.

## 3. Major named methods

Terminology fluctuates between manuscripts and later handbooks, but the following are widely attested.

### מספר הכרחי — *mispar hekhreḥi*

“Necessary” or standard value: the table above.

### מספר סידורי — *mispar siduri*

Ordinal value: א = 1 through ת = 22.

Example:

- תורה, *Torah*: ת22 + ו6 + ר20 + ה5 = **53** ordinally.
- Standard value: 400 + 6 + 200 + 5 = **611**.

### מספר קטן — *mispar qaṭan*

“Small number”: discard place value, normally treating tens and hundreds cyclically:

- א י ק = 1
- ב כ ר = 2
- ג ל ש = 3
- ד מ ת = 4
- ה נ = 5
- ו ס = 6
- ז ע = 7
- ח פ = 8
- ט צ = 9

A word may then remain a multi-letter sum or be reduced again to a digital root. Sources do not always distinguish these two operations.

### מספר גדול — *mispar gadol*

“Large number” may mean either:

1. using final forms as 500–900; or
2. counting the fully spelled names of letters.

Because both uses occur, a writer must define the method.

### מילוי — *millui*

“Filling”: spell each letter’s name and count the spelling.

Example: יוד, the name of yod:

- י10 + ו6 + ד4 = **20**.

Different spellings—הא/הה/הי for *he*, ואו/ויו/וו for *vav*—produce different totals and became fundamental in Lurianic Kabbalah.

### מספר קדמי — *mispar qadmi*

“Preceding” or cumulative value: each letter receives the sum of values up to itself. Later sources systematize several variants.

### מספר פרטי — *mispar peraṭi*

The square of each letter’s ordinary value before summing.

### מספר משולש — *mispar meshullash*

“Triangular” or sometimes “cubed” value, depending on the author. The term is not safe without a formula.

### כולל — *kolel*

Add one for the word or total unit, occasionally one for each word. Example: if two totals differ by one, an interpreter may invoke “with the *kolel*.” This is a historically used convention, but also one of the easiest ways to manufacture correspondences.

### עם האותיות — “with the letters”

Add the number of written letters to the numerical total.

### חילופי אותיות / תמורה — substitution

Not numerical addition strictly speaking, but treated beside gematria in many kabbalistic classifications.

- אתב״ש, *Atbash*: א↔ת, ב↔ש, ג↔ר …
- אלב״ם, *Albam*: the alphabet divided into two groups of eleven.
- אי״ק בכ״ר, *Aiq Bekar*: the “nine chambers,” grouping 1/10/100, 2/20/200, etc.
- אבג״ד: alphabetic displacement or paired alphabets in later taxonomies.

Cordovero carefully distinguishes *ṣeruf*, rearranging the same letters, from *temurah*, replacing letters by a cipher alphabet. [Pardes Rimmonim 30:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.30.5)

### צירוף — permutation

Letters remain themselves but change order. For three distinct letters ABC there are \(3!=6\) permutations:

ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA.

In ecstatic Kabbalah permutation is not merely a cryptographic device. It is a sequence of writing, seeing, chanting, breathing, moving, and mentally recombining letters intended to loosen ordinary linguistic associations.

### נוטריקון — *notariqon*

A word’s letters become initials of a sentence, or initial letters are assembled into a word. This is not arithmetic, though traditional handbooks frequently place it beside gematria.

## 4. Cordovero’s hierarchy

**[D]** Moses Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim*, composed in Safed in 1548, devotes Gate 30 to letters, permutations, substitutions, and gematria.

In 30:8 Cordovero calls a particularly exact form “weight” (*mishqal*): two words should share not only a total but the same number of letters. His example is:

- רזיאל, Raziel = ר200 + ז7 + י10 + א1 + ל30 = **248**
- אברהם, Abraham = א1 + ב2 + ר200 + ה5 + ם40 = **248**

Both have five letters, so Cordovero regards the equation as stronger than an equality achieved by differently sized words. [Pardes Rimmonim 30:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.30.8.3)

This is important evidence that historical practitioners recognized degrees of constraint rather than treating every equal sum as equally persuasive.

# Worked examples

## Hebrew examples

### Eliezer = 318

**[D]** Genesis 14:14 says Abraham mobilized 318 trained retainers. Babylonian Talmud, Nedarim 32a, identifies them homiletically with Eliezer alone:

אליעזר:

- א = 1
- ל = 30
- י = 10
- ע = 70
- ז = 7
- ר = 200

\[
1+30+10+70+7+200=\mathbf{318}
\]

Rashi repeats this on Genesis 14:14: “Eliezer alone, for the numerical value of his name is 318.” See [Nedarim 32a](https://www.sefaria.org/Nedarim.32a) and [Rashi on Genesis 14:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Genesis.14.14.3).

**Status:** the numerical equivalence is exact. The historical claim that Abraham literally took only Eliezer is midrashic interpretation, not recoverable military history. Ibn Ezra explicitly treats it as *derash* rather than the verse’s plain sense. [Genesis 14:14 with Ibn Ezra](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.14.14-16?with=Ibn_Ezra)

### אהבה = אחד = 13

אהבה, *ahavah*, “love”:

\[
א1+ה5+ב2+ה5=\mathbf{13}
\]

אחד, *eḥad*, “one”:

\[
א1+ח8+ד4=\mathbf{13}
\]

**[T]** Kabbalistic and Hasidic teaching frequently interprets the equality as “love is unity” or as a doubled 13 yielding 26, the Tetragrammaton. The arithmetic is exact; the theological relation is interpretive.

### נחש = משיח = 358

נחש, *naḥash*, “serpent”:

\[
נ50+ח8+ש300=\mathbf{358}
\]

משיח, *mashiaḥ*, “messiah”:

\[
מ40+ש300+י10+ח8=\mathbf{358}
\]

**[T]** Later kabbalistic and Christian-esoteric writers use the equality for a polarity in which the messianic force heals or rectifies the serpent-force. The equality is real, but it is not a biblical statement and should not be projected into the composition of Genesis without independent evidence.

### The Tetragrammaton = 26

יהוה:

\[
י10+ה5+ו6+ה5=\mathbf{26}
\]

This is one of the most persistent values in Jewish and Christian Kabbalah.

### Metatron = Shaddai = 314

מטטרון:

\[
מ40+ט9+ט9+ר200+ו6+ן50=\mathbf{314}
\]

שדי:

\[
ש300+ד4+י10=\mathbf{314}
\]

**[D/T]** Rashi on Exodus 23:21 explains “My name is in him” by saying Metatron’s name equals that of his Master, Shaddai. The same equation recurs in later kabbalistic literature. See the references collected in the [Pritzker Zohar edition](https://big.shi.yt/~mac/zohar/Zohar_Vol_10.pdf) and the historical [Jewish Encyclopedia entry on Metatron](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10736-metatron).

**Caution:** the computation uses the ordinary final nun value 50. A nineteenth-century Christian critic objected that ן “must” be 700, but that confuses optional *mispar gadol* with standard gematria.

### Jesus = 888

ΙΗΣΟΥΣ:

\[
Ι10+Η8+Σ200+Ο70+Υ400+Σ200=\mathbf{888}
\]

**[D]** Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.15, reports and attacks the Marcosian use of 888 for Jesus. His testimony, c. 180 CE, shows that the equation was already circulating among Valentinian Christians. [Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.14–16](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103114.htm)

### Dove = 801

περιστερά, “dove”:

\[
π80+ε5+ρ100+ι10+σ200+τ300+ε5+ρ100+α1=\mathbf{801}
\]

**[D]** Irenaeus reports Marcosian interpretation of the dove descending at Jesus’s baptism. 801 was symbolized as alpha plus omega:

\[
α1+ω800=\mathbf{801}
\]

The arithmetic connected the dove with the beginning and end of the alphabet. The Marcosians combined numerical values, letter counts, syllables, and a cosmic system of 24 or 30 aeons; Irenaeus preserves the material polemically, so the report is indispensable but hostile.

### Nero Caesar = 666

The familiar equation uses a Hebrew/Aramaic spelling of the Greek name:

נרון קסר, *Neron Qesar*:

\[
נ50+ר200+ו6+ן50+ק100+ס60+ר200=\mathbf{666}
\]

**[R, strong]** This is a leading historical explanation of Revelation 13:18. A Latinized spelling lacking the final nun gives:

נרו קסר:

\[
נ50+ר200+ו6+ק100+ס60+ר200=\mathbf{616}
\]

The early textual variant 616 therefore materially strengthens the Nero hypothesis. It does not prove that every later 666 calculation is legitimate; rather, it shows how spelling and language were integral to the original riddle.

### Pompeii: “I love her whose number is 545”

The graffito reads:

> φιλῶ ἧς ἀριθμὸς φμεʹ  
> “I love her whose number is 545.”

Here φμεʹ is a numeral, not the woman’s written name:

- φ = 500
- μ = 40
- ε = 5

\[
500+40+5=\mathbf{545}
\]

**[D]** Buried by the eruption of 79 CE, it demonstrates ordinary social use of isopsephic anonymity: readers could identify the beloved if they knew a name with that sum. [Discussion and transcription](https://www.tmin.org/pdfs/Interpreting_Number_Words.pdf)

I/J and U/V were not separate modern letters; W is absent. Therefore a claim to apply “Agrippa’s ancient Latin gematria” to a modern 26-letter English spelling without declaring rules is anachronistic.

# Origins: a dated reconstruction

## Before alphabetic gematria

### Mesopotamian precedent: Sargon II, c. 713–706 BCE

**[D]** An inscription of Sargon II says that he made the circuit of the wall of Dūr-Šarrukīn correspond to “the reading/number of my name.”

One edition gives:

\[
4\times3600+3\times600+1\times60+3\times6+2
=14{,}400+1{,}800+60+18+2
=\mathbf{16{,}280}
\]

Other publications give **16,283**, arising from a different textual reading or edition.

**[X]** This is the earliest frequently cited name-number construction, but it is not demonstrably gematria in the later Hebrew or Greek sense. Akkadian cuneiform signs possessed polyvalent syllabic, logographic, and numerical readings; no stable alphabetic table “A = 1, B = 2…” has been recovered that straightforwardly yields the wall number. Friedrich Delitzsch attempted a wedge-count reconstruction in 1878, but the method is not equivalent to later alphabetic summation.

Therefore:

- “Sargon intentionally related a monumental measurement to his royal name”: documented.
- “Sargon used Hebrew-style gematria”: unsupported.
- “Gematria was invented in Mesopotamia”: possible broad ancestry, not demonstrated transmission.

See the [Israel Museum publication of the cylinder inscription](https://museum.imj.org.il/journal/archive/2018-2019/pdf/cylinder.pdf).

## Alphabetic numerals

The indispensable precondition was the assignment of numerical values to letters. Greek Milesian/Ionic numerals used 27 signs for units, tens, and hundreds. Hebrew and Aramaic later used related alphabetic notation, while Arabic inherited the old Semitic alphabetic order even after reshaping and reordering its everyday alphabet.

Alphabetic numeration and mystical word-equation are not identical:

1. Letters can serve as numerals without words being interpreted by their totals.
2. Once the same sign is both phoneme and numeral, summing names becomes possible.
3. Mystical significance is a further cultural step, not an automatic property of the notation.

## Greece: Hellenistic and Roman isopsephy

**[D]** Greek alphabetic numerals are securely established before the common era. Word equations and isopsephic compositions are documented by the Hellenistic/Roman period, then abundantly in inscriptions, riddles, graffiti, magical texts, Gnostic speculation, and Christian exegesis.

The Pompeii graffito proves a socially intelligible practice before 79 CE. Irenaeus proves elaborate theological numerical exegesis by the later second century.

**[X]** Claims that Pythagoras personally invented isopsephy are not supported by contemporary Pythagorean texts. Ancient Pythagoreans unquestionably developed number symbolism, harmony, ratios, and cosmological arithmology. But modern “Pythagorean numerology” often conflates those doctrines with later alphabetic ciphers. The surviving Pythagorean record is late, layered, and heavily pseudepigraphic. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Pythagoreanism”](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoreanism/)

## Early Judaism

### Possible biblical composition

**[X]** Scholars and religious interpreters have proposed intentional numerical structures in the Hebrew Bible: divine-name totals, alphabetic counts, acrostics, the 318 retainers, the 153 fish of John, and many others.

Some textual numbers may be deliberate literary structures. However, an equation discovered after the fact is not evidence of authorial intent unless reinforced by:

- a contemporary counting convention;
- a controlled textual spelling;
- multiple structurally linked examples;
- an explicit numerical cue;
- manuscript stability;
- or an early interpretive witness.

There is no scholarly consensus that the Torah was composed throughout by a systematic gematria code.

### Rabbinic evidence

**[D]** Tannaitic and amoraic texts employ numerical interpretation. Examples include:

- Eliezer = 318, Nedarim 32a.
- יהיה, “he shall be,” as 30 in discussions of a Nazirite vow: י10 + ה5 + י10 + ה5 = 30.
- השטן, “the satan,” as 364, producing the homily that the accuser has authority 364 days but not on Yom Kippur:

\[
ה5+ש300+ט9+ן50=\mathbf{364}
\]

The Baraita of the Thirty-Two Rules attributes a rule of gematria to R. Eliezer ben Yose ha-Gelili, a second-century tanna.

**[X]** Numbering differs: often rule 29, sometimes 28 in modern summaries. More importantly, the surviving expanded text passed through later transmission and contains material later than its attributed author. It is evidence for an established rabbinic category, not a securely autographic second-century handbook. See [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Gematria”](https://sefaria.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/6571-gematria) and [the Baraita introduction on Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Otzar_Midrashim%2C_Baraita_of_The_32_Midot_of_Aggadah%2C_Introduction).

## Gnostic and early Christian systems

### Marcosians, c. 150–180 CE

**[D]** Irenaeus’s *Against Heresies* 1.14–16 preserves the most extensive early report of a Christian group combining:

- Greek letter values;
- the 24-letter alphabet;
- voiced, unvoiced, and mute letters;
- 30 aeons;
- 888 for Jesus;
- alpha and omega;
- syllable and name counts;
- liturgical invocation.

Irenaeus is an adversarial witness, calling the reasoning arbitrary. Nevertheless, his detailed refutation establishes the existence of such practice.

### Pistis Sophia, third–fourth century

**[D]** The Coptic *Pistis Sophia* and related Books of Jeu contain elaborate arrangements of names, seals, repeated vowels, numbers, and mysteries.

**[X]** Modern occult writers often translate every repeated letter-string into Greek gematria and then claim a comprehensive number code. The surviving work unquestionably practices alphabetic and numerical mysticism, but not every sequence is explicitly said by the text to be an isopsephic equation. It is safer to distinguish:

- explicit numbered ranks, emanations, or mysteries;
- written magical names and vowels;
- modern numerical decoding of those strings.

The *Pistis Sophia* is not among the Nag Hammadi codices; it survives chiefly in the Askew Codex. Conflating it with “the Nag Hammadi text Pistis Sophia” is a modern bibliographical error.

## Late antique magic

**[D]** Greek and Coptic magical papyri, chiefly Roman Egypt, preserve divine names, *voces magicae*, vowel sequences, character-signs, palindromes, and number symbolism. The collection called the PGM spans materials roughly from the second century BCE to the fifth century CE.

**[X]** Alphabetic mysticism is central to many rites, but it is misleading to call every magical word “gematria.” Many names function by sound, foreignness, written shape, ritual authority, or claimed revelation rather than numerical equality. See [Betz’s PGM bibliographical context](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Magical_Papyri) and the study of [magical names in Greek and Coptic texts](https://doi.org/10.1515/arege-2024-0005).

# Medieval Jewish developments

## Sefer Yeṣirah as background

**[D/X]** *Sefer Yeṣirah*, in forms produced and transmitted in late antiquity and the early medieval period, treats the 22 Hebrew letters as formative elements of creation and describes combinations or “gates.”

For 22 letters, the unordered pairs number:

\[
\binom{22}{2}=\frac{22\times21}{2}=\mathbf{231}
\]

Hence the “231 gates,” רל״א שערים. Counting both directions yields 462 ordered pairs. This combinatorial cosmology is fundamental background to Abulafia, though *Sefer Yeṣirah* is not simply a manual of ordinary gematria.

## The Divine Names

### The 72-fold Name

**[D/T]** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three consecutive Hebrew verses traditionally counted as 72 letters each. They are arranged:

1. first verse forward;
2. second backward;
3. third forward.

Reading vertically produces 72 three-letter units. This boustrophedon construction is documented in medieval Jewish sources and was known by the geonic period.

It is called the “Name of seventy-two,” but that can mean:

- 72 triplets;
- a 216-letter construction;
- or, in other contexts, a Name numerically equal to 72.

Those should not be confused.

### The 42-letter Name

**[D/T]** Rabbinic sources know a “Name of forty-two letters,” but do not give one universally accepted public spelling. Medieval traditions connect it with:

- the initial letters of the liturgical poem אנא בכח, *Ana be-Khoaḥ*, yielding seven groups of six letters;
- letter sequences such as אבג יתץ / קרע שטן / נגד יכש / בטר צתג / חקב טנע / יגל פזק / שקו צית;
- expansions or transformations of divine names.

The equation “the 42-letter Name simply is *Ana be-Khoaḥ*” is traditional and influential, but historically the rabbinic expression may predate the poem and could have referred to a different secret sequence.

### The 22-letter Name

**[T/X]** Medieval magical and kabbalistic texts preserve several sequences called a 22-letter Name, including forms built from the priestly blessing or other transformations. There is no single universally authoritative 22-letter string across manuscripts. Popular lists often collapse manuscript variants into a synthetic standardized name.

## Hasidei Ashkenaz

### Judah the Pious and Eleazar of Worms

**[D]** The German Pietists, especially Judah he-Ḥasid (d. 1217) and Eleazar ben Judah of Worms (c. 1165–1238), gave numerical, orthographic, and divine-name speculation an extraordinary role.

Eleazar’s writings include or are associated with:

- *Sefer ha-Ḥokhmah*;
- *Sefer ha-Shem*;
- *Sode Razayya*;
- *Sefer ha-Roqeaḥ*;
- extensive prayer and biblical calculations.

A celebrated authorial wordplay is:

רקח, *Roqeaḥ*:

\[
ר200+ק100+ח8=\mathbf{308}
\]

אלעזר, *Eleazar*:

\[
א1+ל30+ע70+ז7+ר200=\mathbf{308}
\]

**[D/T]** The Ashkenazi pietists counted words and letters of prayers, interpreted anomalous spellings, correlated divine names, and treated inherited liturgical forms as precisely structured.

### The “73 gates”

**[D/X]** Joseph Dan identified a set of 73 Ashkenazi “gates of wisdom” in Eleazar’s materials. These are diverse interpretive operations, not simply 73 varieties of arithmetic gematria; surviving lists are examples and taxonomies rather than proof of a universally fixed ancient canon. A scholarly preview notes that the 73 are themselves examples within a wider combinatorial culture. [Study preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783110871753_A20711797/preview-9783110871753_A20711797.pdf)

## Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh

**[D]** The printed *Sefer Raziel* is a medieval compilation, not a book written by the angel Raziel or by Adam. Its components draw on Ashkenazi esotericism, magical uses of Torah and Psalms, Hekhalot materials, astrology, angelology, divine names, seals, and amulets.

**[T/L]** Its framing legend says Raziel transmitted heavenly secrets to Adam after the expulsion from Eden; other traditions connect the book to Noah and Solomon.

**[R]** The compilation took shape over time, and different recensions contain materially different names and rites. Its magical purposes include protection, healing, victory, love, averting danger, and knowledge of celestial powers. [University of Pennsylvania manuscript description](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002/html/mscodex1674.html)

The 72-fold Name is treated as exceptionally powerful. Manuscripts disagree about vocalization, which is decisive evidence against claims that a single universally preserved pronunciation survived unchanged from Moses. Joshua Trachtenberg’s discussion notes both the power attributed to the Name and the uncertainty of its vowels. [Jewish Magic and Superstition, “In the Name of…”](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm)

# Abraham Abulafia

## Life and project

**[D/R]** Abraham ben Samuel Abulafia was born in Zaragoza in 1240. He traveled across the Mediterranean, studied philosophy and Kabbalah, attempted to meet Pope Nicholas III in 1280, wrote extensively in Italy and Sicily, and disappears from secure documentation after the early 1290s.

He called his approach:

- קבלה נבואית, “prophetic Kabbalah”;
- קבלת השמות, “Kabbalah of Names”;
- חכמת הצירוף, “science of combination.”

Its stated end was prophecy, intellectual conjunction, revelation, and transformation of consciousness—not primarily fortune-telling.

## Principal manuals

### Ḥayyei ha-Olam ha-Ba — חיי העולם הבא

“The Life of the World to Come,” composed c. 1280.

**[D]** A practical manual centered on divine-name recitation and combination, especially the 72-fold Name. It describes bodily preparation, seclusion, breath, voice, visualization, and movement.

### Or ha-Sekhel — אור השכל

“Light of the Intellect,” c. 1285.

**[D]** Gives theoretical and practical treatment of letters, divine names, vowels, respiration, and head motion.

### Sefer ha-Ot — ספר האות

“The Book of the Sign/Letter,” composed in the 1280s.

**[D]** A visionary-prophetic work using dense letter and name symbolism. It is not simply a table of gematria; it stages revelatory authority through alphabetic signs and self-referential prophecy.

Other relevant works include *Oṣar Eden Ganuz*, *Sitrei Torah*, *Mafteaḥ ha-Shemot*, *Mafteaḥ ha-Ḥokhmot*, *Imrei Shefer*, and the autobiographical-prophetic *Sefer ha-Edut*.

## Abulafia’s operational sequence

Moshe Idel reconstructs three linked levels from Abulafia and his school:

1. **Written combination:** write letters and permutations with pen, ink, and parchment.
2. **Oral-physical combination:** chant or sing the letters with vowels, regulated respiration, and head movements.
3. **Mental combination:** internalize the sequences until letters and meanings recombine in thought.

Abulafia writes, in translation, “Take the pen and parchment and ink, and write and combine Names.” The disciple’s report in *Shaʿarei Ṣedeq* speaks of working after midnight “with the quill in my hand and paper on my knees.” Idel emphasizes that written, spoken, and mental combination form stages of one discipline. [Idel, *The Mystical Experience in Abraham Abulafia*](https://www.magnespress.co.il/en/book/The_Mystical_Experience_in_Abraham_Abulafia-3212)

## Breath and pronunciation

**[D]** The practitioner:

- withdraws to a private, clean place;
- wears clean garments;
- prepares psychologically as if separating from ordinary worldly life;
- writes or visualizes the designated letters;
- inhales before a letter;
- pronounces letter and vowel during exhalation;
- pauses for prescribed intervals;
- does not speak while inhaling;
- keeps a controlled rhythm;
- proceeds through columns or cycles of combinations.

The manuals differ on exact pauses—two, three, or up to five breaths at certain boundaries. That variation matters: there was no single mechanically uniform “Abulafia breath.”

Idel compares Abulafia’s intake, emission, and pause with yogic *pūraka*, *recaka*, and *kumbhaka*, but notes that the systems are not exact parallels. A similarity of respiratory analysis does not demonstrate direct borrowing from yoga. [Idel text excerpt](https://pdfcoffee.com/1988-the-mystical-experience-in-abraham-abulafia-moshe-idel-pdf-free.html)

## Head movements and vowel signs

**[D]** The head traces or gestures the spatial form associated with the vowel being sounded. In commonly transmitted descriptions:

- ḥolem: upward movement;
- ḥiriq: downward;
- ṣere: horizontal movement;
- qameṣ: opposing horizontal movement;
- shuruq: forward or central motion.

Details and starting directions differ by text and edition. The point is not merely mnemonic: the written vowel is enacted through the body.

Abulafia’s technique joins:

\[
\text{letter}+\text{vowel}+\text{breath}+\text{voice}+\text{gesture}+\text{intention}
\]

The resulting unit is closer to ritualized phonetic meditation than to silently adding numbers.

## Circles and wheels

**[D]** Abulafian manuscripts organize combinations in tables, columns, circular figures, or rotating “wheels.” Such diagrams manage:

- direct and reverse combinations;
- divine letters paired with alef;
- permutations of consonants;
- changes of vowels;
- transitions between written and vocal performance.

The broader concept is indebted to *Sefer Yeṣirah*’s gates and rotations but reshaped into a technique for ecstatic-prophetic attainment.

## Gematria’s role in Abulafia

Abulafia uses numerical equivalence extensively, but his “science of combination” is not reducible to gematria. Gematria serves several functions:

- link words that disclose a hidden conceptual identity;
- validate an interpretation;
- connect philosophical terminology with biblical language;
- map parts of the human being to names and cosmic structures;
- generate further letter combinations;
- authorize autobiographical and messianic claims;
- correlate breath, intellect, prophecy, Torah, and Name.

Scholem described this as a “mystical logic”: the breaking and recombination of ordinary language discloses a more primordial world of letters. [Scholem, *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism*, Lecture IV](https://www.bard.edu/library/pdfs/archives/Scholem-Major_Trends_in_Jewish_Mysticism.pdf)

Idel’s corrective is that Abulafia’s system is not merely abstract letter play. It is embodied, musical, respiratory, intellectual, and experiential. Ordinary semantic language is dismantled so that the practitioner may reach what Abulafia understood as the divine or universal language.

## Psychological interpretation

### Scholem

**[R]** Scholem interpreted Abulafia as the chief representative of ecstatic or prophetic Kabbalah, distinct from the theosophical-theurgical Kabbalah of the sefirot. Letter combination releases consciousness from ordinary forms and produces visionary experience.

### Idel

**[R]** Idel stresses technique, experience, and the diversity of Abulafia’s sources: Maimonidean psychology, *Sefer Yeṣirah*, divine-name traditions, music, controlled breathing, and the possibility of Sufi parallels. He criticizes any account that makes Abulafia a marginal curiosity rather than a major mystical tradition.

### Wolfson

**[R]** Elliot Wolfson emphasizes the linguistic embodiment of vision, the erotic and gendered structure of mystical union, imagination, and the complex relation between letter, voice, body, and divine form. His work complicates a simple claim that Abulafia sought disembodied rational union.

## The visions

**[D]** Abulafia and disciples describe:

- trembling and bodily shaking;
- heat or altered bodily sensation;
- lights;
- intensified voice;
- the appearance of a human form;
- the experience of hearing an answer;
- sometimes seeing one’s own likeness.

Modern scholars have discussed these as autoscopy, ecstatic physiology, imagination, or culturally structured altered states. That does not decide the practitioner’s theological interpretation.

**[T]** For Abulafia, such experiences could be stages toward prophecy and conjunction with the Active Intellect.

**[M]** Claims that his method has been scientifically proven to activate specific chakras, DNA codes, or quantum fields are modern overlays without evidence in his writings.

# Zohar, Cordovero, Luria, and Hasidism

## The Zohar

**[D]** The Zoharic corpus, mostly produced in late thirteenth-century Castile with later strata, uses gematria among many hermeneutic devices. It correlates words, divine names, sefirot, biblical numbers, and ritual structures.

**[T]** Traditional attribution assigns the Zohar to the second-century R. Simeon bar Yoḥai.

**[R, dominant academic view]** Linguistic, historical, and documentary evidence connects the main body to Moses de León and a Castilian circle around the 1280s–1290s.

The Zohar does not present one closed gematria manual. Its arguments combine numerical equivalence with symbolism, wordplay, verse linkage, and theosophy.

## Cordovero

As noted, Gate 30 of *Pardes Rimmonim* systematizes:

- permutation;
- substitution alphabets;
- numerical equivalence;
- letter-count equivalence;
- “weighted” comparisons;
- cumulative and expanded calculations.

Cordovero helped integrate earlier ecstatic, Ashkenazi, and theosophical practices into an encyclopedic Safedian Kabbalah.

## Lurianic four fillings: 72, 63, 45, 52

Lurianic Kabbalah associates four spellings of יהוה with cosmic configurations known by their numerical names:

- ע״ב, AV = 72
- ס״ג, SAG = 63
- מ״ה, MAH = 45
- ב״ן, BAN = 52

### 72: filling with yods

יוד הי ויו הי

- יוד = 10 + 6 + 4 = 20
- הי = 5 + 10 = 15
- ויו = 6 + 10 + 6 = 22
- הי = 15

\[
20+15+22+15=\mathbf{72}
\]

### 63: mixed filling

יוד הי ואו הי

- יוד = 20
- הי = 15
- ואו = 6 + 1 + 6 = 13
- הי = 15

\[
20+15+13+15=\mathbf{63}
\]

### 45: filling with alefs

יוד הא ואו הא

- יוד = 20
- הא = 5 + 1 = 6
- ואו = 13
- הא = 6

\[
20+6+13+6=\mathbf{45}
\]

### 52: filling with hes

יוד הה וו הה

- יוד = 20
- הה = 5 + 5 = 10
- וו = 6 + 6 = 12
- הה = 10

\[
20+10+12+10=\mathbf{52}
\]

**[D/T]** These values are central in the system transmitted through Isaac Luria’s disciples, especially Ḥayyim Vital. They correspond to divine configurations, worlds, aspects of the primordial human, and processes of emanation and repair. They are not four alternative ordinary spellings of the biblical name; they are technical *millui* constructions. [Worked discussion of SAG](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/379239/jewish/The-Strength-of-Sarah.htm)

## Baal Shem Tov and Hasidism

**[D/T]** Teachings attributed to Israel ben Eliezer, the Baal Shem Tov (c. 1700–1760), emphasize that the letters of divine speech continually sustain creation and that prayer requires attachment to the vitality within letters.

Gematria appears in early Hasidic homily, but several cautions apply:

- many Baal Shem Tov sayings were written down by disciples after his death;
- attribution chains vary;
- later Chabad and Hasidic writers elaborated letter meditation far beyond securely recoverable first-generation teachings;
- “Baal Shem” means a master of the divine Name and has a pre-Hasidic magical history.

Thus the general importance of letters and names is documented in early Hasidism; any precise technique advertised as “the Baal Shem Tov’s ancient gematria meditation” requires a cited early collection.

# Uses

## Exegesis

Gematria was used to:

- harmonize distant verses;
- explain biblical numbers;
- support aggadic identifications;
- attach theological meaning to names;
- interpret unusual spellings;
- correlate commandments with symbolic totals;
- connect divine names with angels, sefirot, and human faculties.

Rabbinic authorities generally did not treat an unconstrained numerical coincidence as sufficient to overturn plain meaning or established law. Gematria’s prestige varied from suggestive embellishment to claimed received tradition.

## Divine-name mysticism

Names were:

- counted;
- filled out;
- permuted;
- substituted;
- vocalized;
- arranged in grids or circles;
- written on amulets;
- recited in prescribed cycles;
- correlated with angels or cosmic levels.

For Abulafia, the Name was primarily a vehicle of prophetic-intellectual transformation. In practical Kabbalah, names could be operative powers used for protection, healing, coercion, knowledge, or vision. The boundary was porous.

## Amulets and Jewish magic

**[D]** Medieval and early modern Hebrew amulets combine:

- biblical verses;
- divine names;
- angel names;
- letter squares;
- seals;
- abbreviations;
- gematria;
- adjurations;
- diagrams.

Gematria might establish that one protecting name is equivalent to another, determine repetitions, or translate a word into a numerical grid. Not every amulet uses gematria, and not every meaningless-looking string is a cipher.

## Greek and Egyptian magic

The magical papyri use alphabetic series, vowel arrangements, names, numbers, geometrical writing, and repeated sounds. Isopsephy is one technique among many. Claims that the entire PGM is “Greek Qabalah” flatten Egyptian, Greek, Jewish, and other ritual traditions into one retrospective system.

## Islamicate letter science

### ʿIlm al-ḥurūf

**[D]** The “science of letters” treated Arabic letters as cosmic entities related to:

- elements;
- planets;
- zodiacal signs;
- divine names;
- Qurʾanic mysteries;
- numerical values;
- shapes and articulation points.

Prominent figures include Jābirian authors, Ibn ʿArabī, and Aḥmad al-Būnī (fl. c. 1225), though their systems differ fundamentally.

### Awfāq

A *wafq*, plural *awfāq*, is a magic square: numbers are arranged so rows, columns, and main diagonals share a sum. Letters may be converted to abjad values, placed in the square, or recovered from numbers.

**[D]** Magic squares became fundamental to Islamicate occult sciences. A recent study warns that the familiar printed *Shams al-maʿārif al-kubrā* is later than al-Būnī and cannot automatically document what al-Būnī himself wrote in the thirteenth century. [Study of the Būnian corpus](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-28419-8_9)

### Picatrix

**[D]** The Arabic *Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm*, translated into Castilian and Latin as *Picatrix* in the thirteenth century, joins astral magic, talismans, names, suffumigations, images, and numerical correspondences.

**Caution:** *Picatrix* is not primarily an abjad-gematria handbook. Later magicians read it alongside letter-number and magic-square traditions; that association should not be back-projected indiscriminately into every passage.

## Chronograms

A chronogram is a word, phrase, or verse whose letter values yield a date.

### Hebrew

**[D]** Hebrew chronograms occur on:

- tombstones;
- synagogue inscriptions;
- marriage documents;
- manuscripts;
- title pages and colophons;
- commemorative poems.

An early literary Hebrew example is associated with Judah al-Ḥarizi in 1205. Hebrew printers often highlighted the counted letters with enlarged type, dots, or marks. [“It All Adds Up: Chronograms in Hebrew Books”](https://jewishlibraries.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/SchiffmanText2019.pdf)

A frequent practical difficulty is the millennium: a title page might give only the hundreds and tens under the convention לפ״ק, “according to the minor era,” requiring the reader to restore the millennium from context.

### Persian

**[D]** Persian *mādda-ye tārīkh* became an important poetic genre. A final hemistich could encode a death, accession, construction, victory, or literary event. Poems include:

- plain total dates;
- dates requiring addition or subtraction of a clue word;
- riddling *taʿmiya* chronograms;
- letters explicitly named by their abjad values.

The genre remained active into Qajar Iran and the twentieth century. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Mādda Tārīkh”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/madda-tarik-chronogram/)

### Ottoman Turkish

**[D]** Ottoman poets cultivated *tarih düşürme* for:

- births and deaths;
- appointments and dismissals;
- circumcisions and marriages;
- mosques, fountains, schools, and palaces;
- fires, floods, wars, and treaties;
- book composition and printing.

Chronogram poems served as both ornament and historical record. [Study of Ottoman chronogram poetry](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/iusarkiyat/article/11441)

### Latin

Latin chronograms usually count visible Roman numeral letters—M, D, C, L, X, V, I—rather than Agrippa’s full alphabetic table. Highlighted capitals in inscriptions and title pages yield the date.

This is a distinct convention. Calling every Latin chronogram “Agrippan gematria” is incorrect.

## Prophecy and polemic

### 666 and Antichrist identification

From antiquity onward, interpreters tried candidate names for Revelation’s beast. Irenaeus mentions solutions such as Lateinos but counsels caution because many names produce 666.

During the Reformation and confessional wars, Protestants calculated papal titles; Catholics and anti-Protestant writers returned the method against Luther and others.

**[D]** Such calculations are part of polemical history.

**[X]** The famous *Vicarius Filii Dei* calculation depends on treating selected letters as Roman numerals. The phrase’s historical status as an official unique papal title is disputed, and numerous names can be made to total 666 with selective spelling.

## Poetry

Greek isopsephic epigrams could construct lines with equal totals. Persian and Ottoman chronograms made letter-number craft a recognized poetic accomplishment. Hebrew poets and printers used it in acrostic and commemorative design.

## Modern occultism

### Christian Kabbalah

Pico della Mirandola (1463–1494), Johannes Reuchlin (1455–1522), Francesco Giorgi, Agrippa, and others transmitted Hebrew letter mysticism into Christian theological and magical systems.

Reuchlin’s *De verbo mirifico* (1494) and *De arte cabalistica* (1517) interpreted Hebrew names Christologically. He connected the Tetragrammaton with Pythagorean tetractys symbolism and treated the 72 triplets as angelic names when supplied with suffixes such as -אל or -יה.

**[R]** Christian Kabbalah was creative appropriation and transformation, not a transparent reproduction of rabbinic practice.

### Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, founded in Britain in 1888, incorporated:

- the Hebrew alphabet;
- Tree of Life paths;
- tarot;
- astrology;
- divine-name tables;
- gematria;
- notarikon;
- Aiq Bekar;
- 72 Shem angels.

Its system synthesized Jewish Kabbalah, Christian Cabala, Renaissance magic, Rosicrucianism, Freemasonry, Egyptianizing symbolism, and nineteenth-century occultism.

### The 72 angels and 72 Goetic spirits

**[D/R]** Reuchlin and Blaise de Vigenère transmitted 72 angel names formed by adding divine suffixes to the Exodus triplets. Thomas Rudd’s seventeenth-century manuscripts paired controlling angels with Goetic spirits. Golden Dawn and modern ceremonial-magic authors further regularized the pairings.

**[M if claimed ancient]** The one-to-one angel/demon opposition is not an ancient Israelite system and is not found in Exodus. It belongs to the early modern Solomonic and later Hermetic tradition. The mere fact that both lists contain 72 members does not prove an older correspondence. [Skinner and Rankine’s historical description](https://www.sskinner.com/), [summary of the transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shem_HaMephorash)

### Crowley

Aleister Crowley (1875–1947) inherited Golden Dawn Qabalah.

- *Liber 777* first appeared in 1909, substantially drawing on Golden Dawn correspondence tables.
- *Sepher Sephiroth* was published in *The Equinox* I.8 in 1912.
- The latter describes itself as begun by Allan Bennett and enlarged from earlier kabbalistic dictionaries, *777*, biblical searches, and contributions by Crowley and colleagues.

It is a numerical dictionary arranging Hebrew words by total, not an ancient *sefer*. [Facsimile of *Sepher Sephiroth*](https://keepsilence.org/the-equinox/1.8/sepher-sephiroth_low.pdf), [*777* publication history](https://hermetic.com/hermeneuticon/777-vel-prolegomena-symbolica-ad-systemam-sceptico-mysticae-viae-explicandae-fundamentum-hieroglyphicum-sanctissimorum-scientiae-summae)

Crowley did not establish one universally accepted English gematria. Later Thelemites created competing English systems in response to *Liber AL* II.55.

## Popular numerology

### L. Dow Balliett

**[D/M]** L. Dow Balliett (1847–1929) was a foundational American popular numerologist. Her books combined musical vibration, letters, biblical symbolism, color, and character analysis.

The familiar modern “Pythagorean” table cycles the modern English alphabet through 1–9:

- A/J/S = 1
- B/K/T = 2
- C/L/U = 3
- D/M/V = 4
- E/N/W = 5
- F/O/X = 6
- G/P/Y = 7
- H/Q/Z = 8
- I/R = 9

**[M]** No evidence shows Pythagoras using this 26-letter English table. The name is a branding genealogy through generalized Pythagorean number mysticism, not a historical attribution.

### Cheiro’s “Chaldean” system

Count Louis Hamon, “Cheiro” (1866–1936), popularized a different English name-number table, generally assigning letters to 1–8 and leaving 9 sacred or unassigned.

**[M]** Ancient Chaldeans did not use the modern English alphabet. Cheiro’s table is an early modern/modern occult construction advertised under Chaldean prestige. Its exact pre-Cheiro genealogy requires further bibliographical work, but no cuneiform original has been produced.

### “Numerology”

The modern personality-reading discipline—life path, destiny number, soul urge, name correction—is largely a late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century synthesis, later systematized by writers such as Balliett, Julia Seton, Juno Jordan, and Cheiro. It should not be conflated with ancient Greek arithmology or rabbinic gematria.

## English Qabalah experiments

### Early experiments

John Skelton’s *Garland of Laurel* (1523) contains alphabetic-number play. Willis F. Whitehead’s *Mystic Thesaurus* (1899) described an “English Cabala.” Later occultists devised many ordinal, reduced, reverse, prime, and transliteration systems.

### New Aeon English Qaballa

**[D/M]** James Lees devised the ALW or New Aeon English Qaballa in November 1976. Ray Sherwin first publicly reported it in *The New Equinox* in 1979. Its letter order advances by eleven positions:

A, L, W, H, S, D, O, Z, K, V, G, R, C, N, Y, J, U, F, Q, B, M, X, I, T, E, P

assigned values 1–26 in that order.

It was promoted as fulfillment of *Liber AL* II.55, “Thou shalt obtain the order & value of the English Alphabet.”

- The date and human discoverer are documented.
- The claim that the order was supernaturally encoded by Crowley is a Thelemic interpretation.
- It is not an ancient cipher.
- Competing Thelemic English systems demonstrate the underdetermination of the prompt. [History of English Qaballa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Qaballa)

## Internet gematria

Online calculators make it possible to search many ciphers simultaneously. Gematrinator and related sites offer:

- English ordinal;
- reverse ordinal;
- reduced values;
- “Jewish gematria” applied to Latin letters;
- primes;
- squares;
- custom ciphers;
- date arithmetic.

**[M]** The calculator label “Jewish gematria” often refers to a Latin-letter cipher, not standard Hebrew gematria. Gematrinator itself now notes that this cipher has been relabeled “Latin.” [Gematrinator FAQ](https://gematrinator.com/faq)

Internet conspiracy practice commonly:

1. searches many spellings;
2. tests multiple ciphers;
3. includes or excludes articles and middle names;
4. translates selectively;
5. adds dates;
6. reports only successful matches.

This is an extreme multiple-comparisons environment. Its results are culturally significant evidence of modern mythmaking but rarely establish historical intention.

## Literature

### Borges

Borges repeatedly used Kabbalah, infinite libraries, divine writing, secret names, permutations, and total languages. “The Aleph,” “The Library of Babel,” “The Writing of the God,” and essays on the Kabbalah belong to this field.

**Caution:** Borges’s literary Kabbalah is not a consistent operational gematria system. Numerical and alphabetic infinity function as metaphysical fiction.

### Georges Perec and Oulipo

Perec and Oulipo used combinatorics, lipograms, palindromes, grids, mathematical architecture, and exhaustive permutation. *La Disparition* omits the letter *e*; *Life A User’s Manual* is structured by constrained movement and combinatorial plans.

These practices are legitimately comparable to *ṣeruf* as formal recombination, and Perec’s Jewish biography makes Kabbalistic readings fruitful. But a comparison is not evidence that his works were composed by traditional Hebrew gematria unless a specific numerical construction is demonstrated. [Study of letter metamorphosis in Perec](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstreams/6e3b52be-0c0d-40e2-b962-6a803458438d/download)

### Joyce

Joyce uses conspicuous numbers, cyclic structures, multilingual puns, letter play, and numerological motifs in *Ulysses* and *Finnegans Wake*. Later interpreters have proposed gematrias for names and phrases.

**Open finding:** no single documented Joycean gematria table comparable to Abulafia’s Hebrew system or Lees’s ALW cipher has achieved scholarly consensus. Broad claims that “Joyce encoded the book in gematria” need passage-by-passage manuscript evidence.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Mesopotamia, Greece, or Israel?

### Mesopotamian priority

- Strong evidence: Sargon’s inscription explicitly relates a measure to his name.
- Weak point: no demonstrated alphabetic summation table produces it.
- Best conclusion: a genuine pre-Greek name-number analogy, not proven direct ancestor of gematria.

### Greek priority

- Strong evidence: the Greek alphabetic numeral system and extensive early isopsephy are securely documented.
- Greek cultural vocabulary plausibly influenced the rabbinic name *gematria*.
- Weak point: secure Greek evidence does not by itself prove that every Jewish use was borrowed rather than developed within a shared eastern Mediterranean scribal environment.

### Israelite priority

- Traditional claims place letter secrets in creation, Sinai, Abraham, or Moses.
- The Hebrew Bible contains alphabetic acrostics and sophisticated numerical structures.
- Weak point: explicit Hebrew word-value exegesis is securely attested later than the biblical texts.

### Judgment

**[R]** The safest historical model is not a single invention but a sequence:

1. ancient Near Eastern name and number symbolism;
2. alphabetic numeral notation;
3. Greek and Semitic experimentation with word totals;
4. rabbinic codification as hermeneutics;
5. medieval Jewish, Christian, and Islamic mystical expansion.

Kieren Barry argues strongly for Greek precedence and influence; his useful comparative collection should not be treated as proof that the entire Hebrew Kabbalah is a Greek derivative. [Barry, *The Greek Qabalah*](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Greek_Qabalah.html?id=w70rWN_-ANAC)

## 2. Was gematria Pythagorean?

**[T/L/M]** Renaissance writers portrayed Kabbalah as the primordial wisdom from which Pythagoras learned. Reuchlin even presented Christian Cabala as “Pythagoras reborn.”

Ancient Pythagorean number metaphysics is real. Yet:

- no contemporary text shows Pythagoras using the modern 1–9 English table;
- much Pythagorean literature is late and pseudonymous;
- alphabetic isopsephy and philosophical arithmology are related but not identical;
- “Pythagorean numerology” as personality analysis is modern.

The Pythagorean label is therefore partly intellectual genealogy, partly legend, and—in modern advertising—often historical fiction.

## 3. The coincidence problem

For an alphabet with many common letters and a limited range of totals, collisions are inevitable.

Suppose a corpus has 50,000 distinct words and most totals fall between 1 and 1,500. Even if perfectly uniform, the average is:

\[
50{,}000/1{,}500\approx33
\]

words per value.

They are not uniform: word lengths and letter frequencies cluster totals, so some values have far more collisions. If an investigator also allows:

- variant spelling;
- singular/plural;
- prefixes;
- translation;
- acronyms;
- *kolel*;
- reduced value;
- final-letter values;
- ordinal value;
- full spelling;
- multiple languages;

the search space becomes enormous.

This does not prove that all historical gematria is accidental. It means intentionality requires controls:

- Was the method stated beforehand?
- Is the spelling textually secure?
- Is the relation contextually relevant?
- Does the source signal counting?
- Are failed comparisons reported?
- Is the equality rare under the selected rules?
- Does a manuscript or early commentary confirm it?

An ancient author can intentionally exploit a system that also generates many accidental matches.

## 4. Textual instability

Gematria is unusually sensitive to scribal variation. One added vav changes a sum by 6; one yod by 10. Plene and defective spelling, final letters, Greek name endings, Arabic hamza conventions, and abbreviations all matter.

The 666/616 pair is a model of responsible analysis because the textual variant corresponds to a linguistically predictable spelling change. By contrast, silently changing spelling until a desired total appears is circular.

## 5. Scholem versus Idel

Scholem’s great contribution was to establish Abulafia as the representative of prophetic/ecstatic Kabbalah and to analyze permutation as mystical logic.

Idel argued that Scholem:

- separated schools too sharply;
- underweighted concrete technique;
- treated Abulafia as more isolated than transmission evidence permits;
- did not sufficiently integrate breathing, music, embodiment, and experience;
- sometimes forced diverse Kabbalists into a single historical morphology.

Wolfson further complicates both by emphasizing visionary embodiment, gender, eroticism, and the linguistic form of revelation.

These are scholarly disagreements over emphasis and phenomenology, not a simple “Scholem was wrong, Idel right.”

## 6. Weinreb and Kaplan

Friedrich Weinreb developed expansive symbolic readings of Torah numbers and Hebrew structures. His work belongs to modern religious interpretation, not mainstream historical-critical proof of ancient encoding.

Aryeh Kaplan translated and popularized *Sefer Yeṣirah*, meditation, divine-name traditions, and Abulafian materials. He made difficult practices accessible but sometimes combined historical scholarship, traditional synthesis, and practical reconstruction. Readers should distinguish his explicitly sourced translations from proposed operational restorations.

## 7. Dornseiff

Franz Dornseiff’s *Das Alphabet in Mystik und Magie*, first published in the 1920s, remains a pioneering comparative study of alphabetic symbolism, vowels, isopsephy, Jewish and Christian letter mysticism, and magic.

Its strengths are philological breadth and collection of primary evidence. Its limitations are those of its period: diffusionist assumptions, dated editions, and insufficient distinction in places between parallelism and transmission. [German scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1922__dornseiff___das_alphabet_in_mystik_und_magie.pdf)

## 8. Barry

Kieren Barry’s *The Greek Qabalah* gathers Greek alphabetic mysticism, magic, Gnosticism, philosophy, and early Christian evidence. Its conclusion that Hebrew Qabalah has Greek origins is broader than the evidence securely establishes. It is valuable as a comparative compendium, but “Greek influence” and “Greek origin of Kabbalah as a whole” are different propositions.

## 9. Forgeries, pseudepigraphy, and false antiquity

### Sefer Raziel

The book’s Adamic authorship is legend. The surviving compilation is medieval and composite.

### Zohar

Traditional second-century attribution is not the dominant historical conclusion. The main corpus is medieval Castilian.

### Pythagorean name chart

The modern English 1–9 chart is not attested to Pythagoras.

### “Chaldean” English numerology

It is not recovered from ancient Babylonian tablets.

### Shams al-Maʿārif

Modern printed *Shams* editions contain later strata and cannot be cited indiscriminately as al-Būnī’s own thirteenth-century words.

### Goetia–Shem pairings

The paired 72 angel/demon system is early modern and Hermetic, not Mosaic antiquity.

### English Qabalah

ALW dates to James Lees in 1976. Other English systems have their own modern inventors.

### “Jewish gematria” calculators

Many use Latin-letter ciphers absent from Jewish textual history.

### “Metatron’s Cube”

The modern geometric emblem called “Metatron’s Cube” is not a standard diagram of classical rabbinic or medieval Jewish Kabbalah. Its attachment to Metatron belongs to modern sacred-geometry culture.

# Open questions

1. What exact cuneiform operation did Sargon’s scribes intend by “the reading/number of my name,” and which reading—16,280 or 16,283—best reflects each inscriptional witness?
2. When is the earliest securely dated Greek inscription that equates two complete lexical expressions rather than merely concealing a name by its numeral?
3. Which strata of the Thirty-Two Rules are tannaitic, and when was gematria assigned its present position in the list?
4. How early is the exact boustrophedon construction of the 72 Exodus triplets?
5. What was the rabbinic 42-letter Name before its medieval identification with the initials of *Ana be-Khoaḥ*?
6. Can the competing medieval 22-letter Names be stemmatically reconstructed?
7. How do surviving manuscripts of *Ḥayyei ha-Olam ha-Ba* and *Or ha-Sekhel* differ in breath counts, vowels, and bodily directions?
8. To what extent did Abulafia know Sufi respiratory or musical practices directly, rather than through a shared Mediterranean contemplative environment?
9. Which gematrias in the Zohar belong to its earliest Castilian strata, and which entered later recensions?
10. What is the earliest dated evidence for devotional use of 786 as a substitute for writing the basmala?
11. Which *awfāq* texts genuinely predate al-Būnī, and which works attributed to him are later compilations?
12. What is the exact manuscript history from Vigenère and Rudd to Golden Dawn angel–demon tables?
13. Who first devised the table later marketed by Cheiro as “Chaldean”?
14. Which specific English cipher did Willis F. Whitehead use in 1899, and how much did it influence later systems?
15. Can statistical models distinguish intentional gematria from coincidental collision in a way appropriate to historically variable spelling?
16. Which purported gematrias in Borges, Joyce, and Perec can be supported by drafts, notebooks, or explicit authorial statements?

[NAME]
Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this gematria or letter-number
system, its rules, its history and its uses. I want the fullest reconstructable account, from
the earliest attestation to the present, chronologically and in detail. Include, wherever
available:

- THE SYSTEM ITSELF: the exact table of letter values (in the script's own letters, Unicode
  where possible), the rules (which letters count, finals, ligatures, vowels or not, how
  words and phrases are summed, whether the sum is reduced, what counts as equivalence), the
  named sub-methods and their Hebrew, Greek, Arabic or Latin terms; worked examples from the
  sources (the classic equivalences: Eliezer = 318 in Genesis 14:14 per Rashi and Nedarim
  32a; ahavah = echad = 13; nachash = mashiach = 358; the 26 of the Tetragrammaton; Jesus =
  888 and the dove = 801 in Irenaeus; Nero Caesar = 666; the Pompeii "I love her whose number
  is 545"; the bismillah's 786; the abjad chronograms; Agrippa's Latin table), each with its
  arithmetic shown
- ORIGINS: the earliest surviving uses with dates and sources (the Sargon II inscription
  claiming the wall's length equals his name's value, c. 720 BCE; the Greek isopsephy of the
  Hellenistic period; the Talmudic gematria as the twenty-ninth of Rabbi Eliezer's
  thirty-two rules; the Marcosians in Irenaeus I.14-16; the Pistis Sophia; the Arabic
  abjad's Nabataean-Aramaic inheritance and the first chronograms; Agrippa 1533; the
  eighteenth-century English numerology; Cheiro; Crowley); the relation to the alphabetic
  numerals it rests on
- USES: exegesis (Talmud, Midrash, Zohar, Abulafia's letter-permutation, the Baal Shem),
  amulets and magic (the magical papyri, the Sefer Raziel, the Arabic awfaq magic squares,
  the Picatrix), chronograms on buildings, tombstones and title pages (the Hebrew, Arabic,
  Persian and Latin traditions), prophecy and polemic (666 and the Antichrist, the Reformation's
  papal sums), poetry (the Persian ta'rikh, the Ottoman chronogram poets), the modern
  occult (the Golden Dawn, Crowley's Sepher Sephiroth and 777, the English Qabalah
  experiments, the New Aeon English Qabalah), popular numerology (Pythagorean and Chaldean
  name-numbers), conspiracy and internet gematria (the gematrinator, the "Jewish gematria"
  of the calculators), gematria in literature (Borges, Perec, the Oulipo, Joyce's numbers)
- PEOPLE: the rabbis, church fathers, Sufis, magicians, poets, calculators and cranks who
  used it, with dates and their key texts
- CONTROVERSIES: the priority question (Mesopotamia, Greece or Israel), the Pythagorean
  claim, the reliability of the traditional derivations, the coincidence problem (how many
  words share a sum), the scholarly assessments (Scholem, Idel, Dornseiff's Das Alphabet in
  Mystik und Magie 1922, Barry's The Greek Qabalah 1999, Weinreb, Kaplan), the forgeries and
  the modern inventions presented as ancient

Include the good and the bad without judgment, and legend as fully as fact.
For every significant claim distinguish documented text, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed claim, legend and modern invention, and say which sources carry it, when
the reading first appears, who spread it, and what evidence exists.
Do not use fact-checking summaries as substitutes for the sources; trace to the texts
(sefaria.org for the Talmud, Midrash and Zohar; the Nag Hammadi and Irenaeus in standard
translations; Dornseiff and Barry; archive.org for Agrippa and Crowley; the Ottoman and
Persian chronogram studies).

Structure the dossier as: Basic identification (name, script, period, region, the table);
The system in detail (rules, sub-methods, worked examples); Origins (dated, placed); Uses;
People; Controversies and disputes; Open questions; Sources (full list of URLs and editions
consulted).
Length: as long as the material requires; for the Hebrew standard, the Greek isopsephy or
the abjad a thousand lines is not too many.

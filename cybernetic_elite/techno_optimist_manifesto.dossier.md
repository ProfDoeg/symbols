## 1. Identification

*The Techno-Optimist Manifesto* is a roughly 5,200-word polemical essay by American software entrepreneur and venture capitalist Marc Andreessen, published on the Andreessen Horowitz website and Andreessen’s Substack on 16 October 2023. It is divided into fifteen movements: “Lies,” “Truth,” “Technology,” “Markets,” “The Techno-Capital Machine,” “Intelligence,” “Energy,” “Abundance,” “Not Utopia, But Close Enough,” “Becoming Technological Supermen,” “Technological Values,” “The Meaning of Life,” “The Enemy,” “The Future,” and “Patron Saints of Techno-Optimism.” It argues that technology, free markets, economic growth, artificial intelligence, abundant energy and deliberate acceleration are morally desirable; that most material problems can be solved by more technology; and that environmental, regulatory, safety and anti-growth doctrines form a six-decade “demoralization campaign.” It ends with 56 “patron saint” entries offered in place of detailed citations. ([Manifesto](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/), 16 October 2023; **Primary record**.)

Andreessen was then a cofounder and general partner of Andreessen Horowitz, or a16z, the venture-capital firm he and Ben Horowitz founded in 2009. The firm published the essay on its corporate site but attaches a disclaimer to its editorial material saying that contributors’ views do not necessarily represent AH Capital Management and that investments discussed are not recommendations. ([a16z About](https://a16z.com/about/); [a16z disclosures](https://a16z.com/disclosures/); **Primary corporate records**.)

The essay is simultaneously:

- an ideological manifesto;
- a defense of technological investment and capital formation;
- a contribution to the 2022–23 accelerationist and AI-regulation debate;
- a corporate-media product hosted by a large technology investor;
- and a canon-building document whose “patron saints” connect classical economics, libertarianism, futurism, cybernetics, innovation studies and contemporary e/acc.

It is not a corporation, fund, foundation, government program or experimental protocol. No separate budget, staff, tax filing, security clearance, grant, court docket or archive belonging to the text could be identified.

### Core propositions

The manifesto makes the following principal claims:

1. Growth is necessary for social vitality; stagnation leads to zero-sum conflict and decline.
2. Population, natural resources and technology are the only sources of growth, with technology the only indefinitely renewable one.
3. Free markets are decentralized information-processing systems and, with technology, form the “techno-capital machine.”
4. Intelligence includes people, organizations, human-machine “cybernetic systems” and artificial intelligence.
5. AI is a universal problem solver; slowing it will prevent life-saving advances. Andreessen calls preventable deaths caused by foregone AI “a form of murder.”
6. Cheap, abundant energy—especially nuclear fission and fusion—should be expanded radically.
7. Earth can support vastly more people, potentially 50 billion, followed by settlement beyond Earth.
8. Humans should become “technological supermen,” embrace ambition and accept struggle and risk.
9. Technology and markets are liberatory rather than exploitative.
10. Precaution, degrowth, limits-to-growth arguments and much technology ethics constitute obstacles to abundance.
11. Liberal-democratic economic, cultural and military power depends on technological superiority.
12. Although the essay calls itself “material” rather than political, it endorses free markets, rejects socialism and central planning, condemns universal basic income, prescribes national policy and identifies political doctrines as enemies. The “not political” characterization is therefore Andreessen’s own limiting definition, not a neutral description. ([Manifesto](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/), 16 October 2023; **Primary record and subject claims**.)

### The complete enemies catalogue

The manifesto first identifies a “demoralization campaign” conducted under these labels:

- “existential risk”;
- “sustainability”;
- “ESG”;
- “Sustainable Development Goals”;
- “social responsibility”;
- “stakeholder capitalism”;
- “Precautionary Principle”;
- “trust and safety”;
- “tech ethics”;
- “risk management”;
- “de-growth”;
- “the limits of growth.”

It then names as enemies:

- stagnation;
- anti-merit, anti-ambition, anti-striving, anti-achievement and anti-greatness;
- statism, authoritarianism, collectivism, central planning and socialism;
- bureaucracy, vetocracy, gerontocracy and blind deference to tradition;
- corruption, regulatory capture, monopolies and cartels;
- formerly vital institutions now described as compromised, corroded and collapsing;
- the “ivory tower,” expert credentialism, luxury beliefs and social engineering;
- speech control and thought control, associated with George Orwell’s *1984*;
- Thomas Sowell’s “Unconstrained Vision”;
- Alexandre Kojève’s “Universal and Homogeneous State”;
- Thomas More’s *Utopia*;
- the Precautionary Principle;
- deceleration, degrowth and depopulation;
- Friedrich Nietzsche’s “Last Man”;
- and, finally, “that”—the resigned, derisive, complacent disposition represented by the Last Man.

Andreessen says these are “bad ideas,” not bad people, but also says persons “captured” by them suffer from *ressentiment* and need to be led out of a “self-imposed labyrinth of pain.” ([Manifesto](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/), 16 October 2023; **Primary record**.)

### Documentary stress tests

Several empirical assertions are advocacy claims rather than findings demonstrated in the essay:

- **World population.** Andreessen wrote that total human population “may already be shrinking.” The United Nations’ then-current 2022 projection instead anticipated continuing growth from eight billion to approximately 10.4 billion in the 2080s. Some countries were shrinking, but the global claim was inconsistent with the principal contemporary demographic estimate. ([UNFPA/UN World Population Prospects](https://www.unfpa.org/press/world-set-reach-8-billion-people-15-november-2022), 2022; **Contradicted as a global claim**.)

- **Precautionary Principle.** Andreessen says it was “invented to prevent large-scale civilian nuclear power.” Germany’s Federal Environment Agency traces the modern *Vorsorgeprinzip* to broad 1970s environmental policy concerned with preventing environmental and health damage, not exclusively to opposition to nuclear power. ([German Federal Environment Agency](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/en/precautionary-principle); **Official history contradicting the narrow origin claim**.)

- **“300 years of history” proving that technology raises wages.** This is asserted without a cited dataset or defined measure. It compresses substantial disputes concerning distribution, bargaining power, displacement and time horizons. **Claimed by the subject; not demonstrated in the text.**

- **AI delay as murder.** This is a moral and counterfactual assertion. The essay supplies no mortality model, causal estimate or legal argument. **Claimed by the subject; not an adjudicated or empirical finding.**

### Preservation and archive

The operative copies remain on the a16z and Substack websites. No evidence was located that Andreessen deposited drafts or correspondence in a public archive. The text has also been preserved through quotation, criticism, PDFs and web archiving, but no authoritative critical edition exists.

### Conversion into political power

On 14 December 2023, Ben Horowitz announced that a16z would, for the first time, become directly involved in candidate politics, supporting candidates aligned with an “optimistic technology-enabled future,” particularly in AI, cryptocurrency and biotechnology. The statement promised to oppose candidates whose policies the firm believed would damage those sectors. ([“Politics and the Future”](https://a16z.com/politics-and-the-future/), 14 December 2023; **Primary corporate record**.)

A16z subsequently announced contributions to Fairshake, a cryptocurrency-focused political action committee. The firm said it would not control Fairshake’s independent spending. ([a16z crypto statement](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/contributing-to-fairshake-pac/); **Primary corporate record/firm claim**.)

Thus the documented sequence is:

**investment capital → corporate manifesto and media → public policy argument → declared electoral spending strategy.**

That sequence demonstrates institutional power and aligned interests. It does not establish an illegal coordination scheme or that the manifesto itself was purchased by portfolio companies.

## 5. Ideas and Programs

### Technology and markets as a single system

The manifesto treats technology and competitive markets as mutually reinforcing information-processing systems. Hayek supplies dispersed knowledge; Ricardo comparative advantage; Smith exchange; Nordhaus social returns to innovation; Land the self-accelerating “techno-capital machine.” Companies and networks are expressly called “cybernetic systems.” ([Manifesto](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/); **Primary record**.)

This is technological determinism qualified by voluntarism: technological growth is presented as civilization’s fundamental driver, but people are commanded consciously to accelerate it.

### Effective Accelerationism

The early e/acc statement published under @BasedBeffJezos and @bayeslord describes markets, thermodynamics, decentralized adaptation and the “technocapital singularity” as forces to be accelerated rather than restricted. ([e/acc principles](https://effectiveaccelerationism.substack.com/p/repost-notes-on-eacc-principles-and), 31 October 2022; **Movement self-publication**.)

Andreessen participated in a 3 March 2023 online discussion with @bayeslord and others in which he spoke approvingly of the techno-capital machine and opposed centralized AI gatekeeping. ([Contemporaneous transcript](https://diyhpl.us/wiki/transcripts/2023-03-03-twitter-bayeslord-pmarca/); **Unofficial transcript of public discussion**.)

The manifesto never uses the label “e/acc,” but the patron list and preceding discussion establish more than a coincidental resemblance. The most accurate classification is:

- **documented:** Andreessen engaged with leading e/acc accounts and named two as patron saints;
- **supported interpretation:** the manifesto is a major, investor-authored restatement of e/acc-adjacent arguments;
- **not established:** that Guillaume Verdon or @bayeslord wrote, edited or funded the manifesto.

### Nick Land’s accelerationism

Andreessen expressly attributes the “techno-capital machine” to Nick Land. Academic analysis characterizes Land’s version as cybernetic, anti-anthropocentric and increasingly autonomous from human purposes: capital and intelligence accelerate beyond political command. ([Manifesto](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/), **Primary record**; [Tuomas Tahko-era philosophical analysis of Land](https://www.journals.vu.lt/problemos/en/article/view/25345), 2022, **Scholarly interpretation**.)

Andreessen domesticates that concept. He insists that humans remain masters and beneficiaries of technology, whereas Land’s writings frequently undermine confidence in human mastery. *Fanged Noumena*, covering Land’s writings from 1987–2007, supplies the relevant textual corpus. ([MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780983216940/fanged-noumena/); **Publisher record**.)

### Cybernetic lineage

The text’s “human-machine cybernetic systems” connect it explicitly to cybernetic language. Its patron list then supplies three major nodes:

1. **John von Neumann** — automata, computing, game theory and military systems;
2. **Douglas Engelbart** — interactive computing and augmented human intellect;
3. **Stewart Brand** — presentation and cultural circulation of Engelbart’s system.

That is a genuine intellectual genealogy. It is not evidence that the manifesto originated in a covert cybernetic program.

### Marinetti and political futurism

The “violent assault” sentence is an acknowledged paraphrase of Filippo Tommaso Marinetti’s 1909 *Futurist Manifesto*. The original movement exalted machinery, speed, aggression and rupture. Marinetti later merged his political organization with Benito Mussolini’s Fascist movement and became a fascist cultural advocate. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021667099/); [TIME historical account](https://time.com/10217/the-guggenheim-goes-back-to-the-futurists/); **Archival and historical reporting**.)

Andreessen does not reproduce the 1909 text’s praise of war, militarism or contempt for women. Listing Marinetti and adapting one sentence documents an aesthetic and rhetorical appropriation; it does not alone prove endorsement of fascism. Criticism that ignores the source’s politics is incomplete, but labeling Andreessen a fascist solely from the citation exceeds the evidence.

### The Kaczynski comparison

Theodore Kaczynski’s *Industrial Society and Its Future* was published by *The Washington Post* and *The New York Times* on 19 September 1995 after the FBI and Department of Justice agreed that publication might identify the Unabomber. Kaczynski’s brother David recognized the writing, contributing to Kaczynski’s identification and arrest. ([1995 text](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1995/09/19/industrial-society-and-its-future-part-1-of-10/c5646f84-a83c-4934-8923-b37d41d163d3/); [FBI case history](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/unabomber); **Contemporaneous publication and official record**.)

The “photographic negative” comparison is analytically plausible:

| Kaczynski | Andreessen |
|---|---|
| Industrial technology progressively destroys autonomy. | Technology expands agency and freedom. |
| Large technological systems require control and centralization. | Markets decentralize knowledge and power. |
| Further technological development deepens the problem. | Technology-generated problems should be solved with more technology. |
| The industrial system should ultimately be overthrown. | The techno-capital system should be deliberately accelerated. |
| Technological progress alienates people from nature. | Humanity should overcome nature and make it serve human ends. |

They also share rhetorical architecture: civilizational diagnosis, technological determinism, a manifesto form, generalized social enemies and a binary choice between competing futures.

But:

- Andreessen never cites Kaczynski.
- No draft, interview or correspondence located says the essay was written as a response to Kaczynski.
- No close verbal borrowing was identified.
- The “photographic negative” formulation remains a critic’s interpretation, not documented authorial intent.

### Human-subject research

The manifesto proposes no experiment involving human subjects, contains no protocol, and identifies no medical or behavioral research grant. Searches found no evidence that the text was funded through MKUltra, a successor behavioral program or any intelligence-sponsored human-subject project.

Kaczynski’s participation in Henry Murray’s Harvard stress research is frequently folded into online MKUltra narratives. That history does not establish an MKUltra connection to Andreessen’s text. **Any such extension is unsupported.**

## 6. Scandal, Controversy and Contest

### Regulation and conflicts of interest

Critics argued that the essay converts the financial interests of a venture capitalist into a universal moral philosophy. Steven Levy observed that many foundational technologies, including the internet, arose from public funding and that the essay underplays inequality and institutional responsibility. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/plaintext-marc-andreessen-techno-billionaire-wrong-techno-optimism/), 20 October 2023; **Named criticism**.)

Adam Lashinsky argued that the manifesto was self-serving in light of a16z’s stakes in technology, cryptocurrency and platform companies. ([Washington Post opinion](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2023/10/19/marc-andreessen-manifesto-silicon-valley/), 19 October 2023; **Named commentary**.)

Axios separated Andreessen’s legitimate case for innovation from the “enemies” section, noting the absence of terms such as “unintended consequences” and “climate change.” ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/10/17/marc-andreessens-ai-manifesto-hurts-his-own-cause), 17 October 2023; **Named reporting/commentary**.)

These sources document conflicts and omissions. None presents evidence of fraud in the manifesto’s production.

### Expertise, safety and precaution

Nitin Pai, writing as a self-described techno-optimist, argued that technological development requires steering rather than acceleration alone and that rejecting precaution and ethics is irresponsible. ([Pai](https://www.nitinpai.in/2023/10/23/a-techno-optimist-rejoinder-to-marc-andreessens-manifesto), 23 October 2023; **Named criticism**.)

Henry Farrell counted the repeated “we believe” construction and interpreted the essay as a religious-style engineers’ creed whose appeal to Hayek conflicts with its certainty about technology’s direction. ([Crooked Timber](https://crookedtimber.org/2023/11/13/the-religion-of-the-engineers-and-hayek-its-true-prophet/), 13 November 2023; **Named scholarly commentary**.)

### Elitism and rule by technologists

Elizabeth Spiers characterized the manifesto as a case for rule by technology billionaires and compared its pathos to the Unabomber’s manifesto while finding it less coherent. This was an opinion comparison, not a claim of collaboration or ideological identity. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/10/28/opinion/marc-andreessen-manifesto-techno-optimism.html), 28 October 2023; **Named commentary**.)

TechCrunch similarly questioned whether its universal claims reflected the experience of people without Andreessen’s wealth or institutional power. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/17/when-was-the-last-time-marc-andreessen-talked-to-a-poor-person/), 17 October 2023; **Named commentary**.)

### Dedicated contested-material pass

#### Jeffrey Epstein

A March 19, 2012 email from Lesley Groff to Jeffrey Epstein contains “Marc Andreesen” on a proposed invite list for a seminar titled “MONEY.” The record says “possible Invite List.” It does not show that an invitation was sent, that Andreessen accepted, that a meeting occurred, or that money or information passed between them. ([House Oversight production PDF](https://media-cdn.rollcall.com/epstein-files/House_Oversight_028784-028784.pdf), 19 March 2012; **Primary correspondence**.)

Accordingly:

- **Documented:** Epstein’s assistant placed Andreessen’s misspelled name on a possible-invite list.
- **Not documented by that record:** attendance, correspondence with Andreessen, friendship, funding, travel, protection, employment or involvement in the manifesto.
- **No manifesto connection:** the email predates the text by eleven years and does not concern its ideas or production.

No credible record was located showing that Epstein funded the manifesto, a16z’s publication of it, e/acc, or the four highlighted patron saints as a group.

#### CIA and intelligence agencies

No record was located showing CIA authorship, review, funding or direction of the manifesto. A16z later published a March 2024 conversation with CIA Chief Technology Officer Nand Mulchandani about AI and intelligence. That is a documented post-publication corporate contact, not evidence about the manifesto’s origin. ([a16z/CIA CTO podcast](https://a16z.com/podcast/intelligence-in-the-age-of-ai-with-new-cto-of-the-cia/), 11 March 2024; **Primary corporate record**.)

The firm’s investment in Anduril establishes a direct financial relationship with a defense contractor serving military and intelligence missions. It does not establish CIA control of Andreessen’s writing.

#### MKUltra

Targeted searches for the manifesto with “MKUltra,” “human subjects,” “CIA funding” and related terms found no court record, declassified document, grant or contemporaneous correspondence connecting them. Online narratives generally reach the manifesto by chaining:

Kaczynski → Harvard experiments → disputed MKUltra association → cybernetics → internet institutions → Andreessen.

Each arrow requires separate proof. No evidence was found for the final connection to the 2023 text. **Rumor/conspiracy material; unsupported.**

#### Lawsuits, indictments and depositions

No lawsuit, indictment, prosecution, settlement, deposition or sealed docket concerning the manifesto itself was located. It has no separate legal identity or assets. Litigation involving Andreessen, a16z or portfolio companies cannot be imputed to the text without a documentary nexus; none was found.

#### Foundations and covert funding

No foundation grant, government contract, donor-advised fund, offshore entity or tax-exempt vehicle was identified as financing the essay. The observable institutional support is a16z’s website, production staff and podcast infrastructure. **Absence of evidence after targeted searching; not proof that no unrecorded assistance existed.**

#### Conspiracy narratives

Online conspiracy material recasts the manifesto as a published blueprint for oligarchic or technocratic rule and links it to a “progress cult,” transhuman religion or unified corporate-government elite. One representative page calls it part of an openly published political “plan,” while supplying interpretation rather than evidence of common command. ([“They Published the Plan”](https://www.griftgrinders.com/investigations/they-published-the-plan); **Rumor/political-conspiracy interpretation**.)

The documented basis underneath these narratives is narrower:

- a powerful investor wrote a political-economic manifesto;
- his firm invests in AI, defense, nuclear technology and cryptocurrency;
- he cites Land, von Neumann, Engelbart, Brand and Marinetti;
- the firm later announced electoral spending and maintains contacts with defense and intelligence officials.

Those facts justify scrutiny of concentrated capital and influence. They do not, without additional records, prove a covert coordinating organization, intelligence authorship or an Epstein-directed network.

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

### Immediate reception

- **17 October 2023 — Axios:** described the essay as an unusually blunt anti-regulatory intervention in the AI-safety debate, noted Andreessen’s financial exposure and criticized its minimization of harms. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/10/17/marc-andreessen-ai-manifesto-techno-optimist); **Named reporting**.)

- **17 October 2023 — Andrew Kelley:** published an alternative manifesto advocating technology while rejecting religion, war and concentrations of control. ([Kelley](https://andrewkelley.me/post/the-techno-optimist-manifesto.html); **Primary response text**.)

- **17 October 2023 — TechCrunch:** treated the essay as an expression of billionaire distance from poverty and technological harm. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/17/when-was-the-last-time-marc-andreessen-talked-to-a-poor-person/); **Named commentary**.)

- **19 October 2023 — Adam Lashinsky:** characterized it as self-interested Silicon Valley ideology. ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2023/10/19/marc-andreessen-manifesto-silicon-valley/); **Named commentary**.)

- **20 October 2023 — Steven Levy:** accepted technology’s potential while disputing Andreessen’s identification of capitalism and investor self-interest with general progress. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/plaintext-marc-andreessen-techno-billionaire-wrong-techno-optimism/); **Named commentary**.)

- **23 October 2023 — Nitin Pai:** supplied a techno-optimist rejoinder centered on governance and steering. ([Pai](https://www.nitinpai.in/2023/10/23/a-techno-optimist-rejoinder-to-marc-andreessens-manifesto); **Named commentary**.)

- **25 October 2023 — Andreessen and Ben Horowitz:** offered the principal authorial afterword through a16z’s podcast. ([a16z podcast](https://a16z.com/podcast/the-techno-optimist-manifesto-with-marc-andreessen-and-ben-horowitz/); **Claimed by the subject/primary corporate record**.)

- **28 October 2023 — Elizabeth Spiers:** emphasized oligarchic implications and compared the rhetoric with the Unabomber’s manifesto. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/10/28/opinion/marc-andreessen-manifesto-techno-optimism.html); **Named commentary**.)

- **10 November 2023 — James Pethokoukis interview:** Pethokoukis broadly endorsed the text. Andreessen said it arose from a conviction that the United States had been demoralized against technology since the 1960s and that he wrote when those frustrations reached a “boiling point.” ([Faster, Please](https://fasterplease.substack.com/p/my-long-read-q-and-a-with-marc-andreessen); **Named interview and subject account**.)

- **13 November 2023 — Henry Farrell:** analyzed it as a creed combining engineers’ religion with a selective use of Hayek. ([Crooked Timber](https://crookedtimber.org/2023/11/13/the-religion-of-the-engineers-and-hayek-its-true-prophet/); **Named commentary**.)

- **21 November 2023 — Foreign Policy:** situated the essay within American elite faith in progress and Silicon Valley’s political economy. ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/11/21/silicon-valley-tech-facebook-twitter-venture-capital-optimism/); **Named commentary**.)

### Subsequent afterlife

The manifesto quickly became a canonical reference in discussions of e/acc, AI safety, venture-capital politics and “techno-optimism.” Its slogans—especially “AI deceleration” as preventable killing, the enemies list and “It’s time to build”—were more influential than its economic proofs.

Ben Grosser produced an artistic redaction of the text, treating omission and visual suppression as critique. ([Redacted manifesto PDF](https://bengrosser.com/files/Techno-Optimist-Manifesto-Andreessen-redacted-by-Grosser.pdf); **Primary artistic response**.)

The December 2023 a16z political announcement gave the essay a more concrete institutional afterlife: “techno-optimism” moved from philosophical declaration toward a criterion for campaign support.

The Hidden AmuraKa 2026 framing extends a longer documentary tradition exemplified by Lutz Dammbeck’s 2003 film *The Net*, which juxtaposed Kaczynski, cybernetics, military research and network intellectuals. The 2026 claim should therefore be understood as a later network narrative imposed on the 2023 text. Its most specific textual observation—the inclusion of von Neumann, Engelbart, Brand and Land—is verified; its stronger implication of covert continuity is not.

# Program for Evolutionary Dynamics (Harvard, 2003-2020): Research Dossier

## Evidentiary key and scope

- **Tier A — documented in primary or institutional records:** university reports and correspondence, grant and tax records, contemporaneous program documents, published papers, or records released by government bodies.
- **Tier B — reported:** reporting by named journalists, normally based on documents or interviews.
- **Tier C — allegation:** claim in litigation, by a named accuser, or in a formal complaint, not adjudicated as fact.
- **Tier D — subject’s account:** statement, memoir, interview, promotional text, or retrospective claim by a participant.
- **Tier E — rumor/conspiracy:** unsupported inference or narrative circulated by online or popular accounts.
- **Tier F — disproved, retracted, or contradicted:** claim contradicted by stronger records or removed/retracted by its publisher.

The principal institutional source is Harvard’s May 2020 report, written by Harvard general counsel Diane E. Lopez, university attorney Ara B. Gershengorn, and Foley Hoag partner Martin F. Murphy. It drew on more than forty interviews and targeted searches of more than 250,000 pages, but it was commissioned and published by Harvard rather than produced through judicial discovery or an independent public inquiry. Its conclusions are consequently institutional findings, not court judgments. ## 1. Identification

The Program for Evolutionary Dynamics, commonly abbreviated **PED**, was an interdisciplinary research program within Harvard University’s Faculty of Arts and Sciences. It was founded in 2003, directed throughout its existence by Austrian-born mathematical biologist **Martin A. Nowak**, and associated institutionally with Harvard’s Departments of Mathematics and Organismic and Evolutionary Biology. Its offices occupied sixth-floor space at **One Brattle Square, Cambridge, Massachusetts**—space leased by the Harvard Kennedy School and subleased to PED. It was not a separately incorporated charity or company and therefore had no independent nationality, shareholders, spouse, estate, or criminal personality.

PED applied mathematical and computational models to mutation, selection, genetic drift, population structure, evolutionary games, cooperation, language, viruses, cancer, and the origin of life. An internal 2010 report described it as a multidisciplinary group spanning biology, mathematics, physics, economics, and computer science. Its blog later included eusociality, culture, scientific publishing, social contagion, and banking among its subjects. [PED blog, March 2011](https://archive.blogs.harvard.edu/pedblog/)

The program was made possible by an **unrestricted $6.5 million gift from Jeffrey E. Epstein in 2003**, the largest of his gifts to Harvard. Contemporary reports described a possible or pledged total of $30 million, but Harvard’s 2020 investigators found no institutional record of a $30 million pledge. PED ceased operating as a distinct program after Harvard’s 2020 review and Martin Nowak’s administrative leave; its formal closure was announced in March 2021. Thus “2003–2020” describes its practical operating life, while the formal closure decision belongs to 2021.

## 2. The Record

### Origins, recruitment, and the 2003 gift

Before Harvard, Martin Nowak directed the Institute for Advanced Study’s theoretical-biology program in Princeton. Harvard’s review found that Epstein had already supported Nowak’s work there. A later statement attributed to Nowak put that support at $500,000 and characterized both it and the subsequent Harvard gift as unrestricted support for basic research; that is Nowak’s account, not the original gift agreement.

In 2002, Harvard recruited Nowak into a rare joint appointment in mathematics and biology. A contemporaneous Harvard Crimson account said that then-Mathematics Department chair **Benedict H. Gross** advocated his recruitment and that Nowak received a tenured appointment in August 2002. Discussions about Epstein’s money occurred in parallel. The earliest PED website, quoted in Harvard’s review, said the program was established by President **Lawrence H. Summers** following a proposal by Epstein and Gross, operated under Faculty of Arts and Sciences dean **William C. Kirby**, and had Nowak as faculty director. [The Crimson’s May 2003 account](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2003/5/1/mogul-donor-gives-harvard-more-than/)

The documentary record establishes:

- Epstein gave Harvard **$6.5 million in 2003** to create or help create PED. **[Tier A: Harvard development records summarized May 2020.]**
- The money was unrestricted and paid, among other things, for a separate research facility and its rent at One Brattle Square. **[Tier A: Harvard report, May 2020.]**
- A 2006 Crimson account said Epstein had agreed to *consider* increasing his contribution to $30 million following a review of the program; that review had not occurred. **[Tier B: contemporaneous reporting, September 2006.]**
- Harvard’s investigators found **no Harvard record of a binding $30 million pledge**. **[Tier A/F: May 2020 report, contradicting contemporary and promotional descriptions.]**
- A later Epstein public-relations page escalated the claim to **$35 million**. Harvard expressly found that statement false. **[Tier F: Harvard report, May 2020.]**
- No public copy of the original executed gift instrument was located, so its complete legal terms cannot be independently reproduced. Later records establish that the usable gift was unrestricted and assigned to PED, but not every clause or payment date.

The initial money supported rent, space and occupancy, equipment, supplies, administrative and research staff, students, visitors, events, and research activity. It did not purchase a named Harvard building or create a separately endowed legal entity.

### Epstein’s arrest, Harvard’s decision, and continuing contact

Epstein was arrested in Florida in 2006. Harvard accepted four further Epstein gifts totaling $736,000 between that arrest and July 2007, but those were not identified as additional PED gifts. Harvard publicly declined to return the already-spent PED money, saying it funded legitimate research into evolution, viruses, and cancers. [The Crimson, September 2006](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2006/9/13/harvard-to-keep-epstein-gift-after/)

After Epstein’s June 2008 guilty plea and sentence for state offenses involving solicitation of a minor, Harvard president **Drew G. Faust** decided no later than November 2008 that Harvard would accept no further gifts from him. Harvard’s 2020 review found no post-conviction direct gift from Epstein to Harvard.

Communication of the ban was defective. It was not formally recorded or uniformly explained to faculty. Martin Nowak acknowledged knowing Faust had reservations but told reviewers he considered the situation unclear or potentially temporary. Emails show that Nowak, Benedict Gross, science dean **Jeremy Bloxham**, and others sought clarification or reconsideration between 2010 and 2014. In December 2013, FAS dean **Michael D. Smith**, after learning of Epstein’s conviction, independently refused to submit a proposed gift to Harvard’s Gift Policy Committee. Harvard’s reviewers found no evidence that Faust or Smith ever intended their decisions to be temporary.

### Third-party money facilitated by Epstein

PED needed unrestricted money to keep paying rent at One Brattle Square, because ordinary sponsored-research grants generally could not pay that expense. Harvard’s report documented four gifts, totaling **$7.5 million**, from donors Epstein introduced to Nowak:

| Date | Donor | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| September 10, 2010 | Falconwood Foundation | $500,000 |
| November 22, 2011 | Leon D. Black and Debra R. Black | $2,000,000 |
| December 29, 2014 | Leon D. Black and Debra R. Black | $4,000,000 |
| October 30, 2015 | Black Family Foundation | $1,000,000 |

Harvard found no evidence that these funds secretly belonged to Epstein. Falconwood and the Blacks said the money was their own; contemporaneous records corroborated the source of at least one Black gift. Epstein and his staff nevertheless made introductions, transmitted proposed wording, and helped ensure that donation letters permitted PED to use the money for rent. **Documented facilitation is not documentary proof of laundering or concealed ownership.**

Falconwood Foundation tax filings identify psychiatrist and commodities trader **Henry G. Jarecki** as its president/treasurer. [Falconwood Foundation IRS filings](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/133456475)

### Templeton matching-funds episode

In 2014 PED sought $3 million from the **John Templeton Foundation**. Templeton was prepared to fund half if PED could show that the other half had been secured. PED already possessed usable Black money. Instead of obtaining the required letter from Harvard or Leon Black, Nowak asked Epstein.

A November 24, 2015 letter on **Enhanced Education** letterhead, signed by **Darren K. Indyke**, represented that Enhanced Education had established a PED gift in 2003 and that $1,500,328 from Harvard fund 347150 would be applied to the 2016–2018 project. [Released letter](https://efta.threats.watch/documents/efta01102163)

Harvard found those statements false in two respects:

1. Enhanced Education had never funded PED.
2. Epstein’s 2003 gift had already been spent and could not fund the 2016–2018 project.

In PED’s 2018 grant close-out process, the chief administrative officer instructed a researcher to identify Enhanced Education as the source of additional funds. Harvard again found that statement false. Templeton staff knew Enhanced Education was connected to Epstein and knew the application had substituted Enhanced Education for “Jeffrey Epstein and friends,” but Harvard could not determine whether Templeton knew the original money was gone or whether the exact source, as opposed to availability of matching funds, would have affected its award.

This was an institutional finding of knowingly supplied false grant information, not a criminal fraud conviction. No public indictment or civil judgment against PED or Nowak over the Templeton grant was located.

PED supplied Epstein and his assistants with:

- PED’s keypad code;
- a key to Office 610;
- an office informally called **“Jeffrey’s Office”**;
- a Harvard telephone line identified as Epstein’s until 2017.

Epstein supplied a rug and photographs for Office 610. Nowak told investigators it was really a visitor’s office only nominally associated with Epstein; other PED personnel said scarce office space was understood to be reserved for him when he came. Both versions appear in Harvard’s report.

When Harvard installed a more restrictive identification system in 2017, PED’s chief administrative officer obtained “unassigned” visitor cards and mailed two to an Epstein assistant, saying that doing so was easier than arranging annually renewed photographic identification. Harvard found that this, with Nowak on notice, circumvented the new security procedures and gave Epstein effectively unlimited access.

Inviting Epstein as a guest was not itself prohibited, even though he was a registered sex offender. The policy issue was permanent, unaccompanied access for a person with no Harvard affiliation after 2006.

Epstein generally selected dates and guest lists and used PED to meet prominent academics, occasionally political figures, and sometimes to hold dinners. He was routinely accompanied by young women described as assistants in their twenties. Harvard found:

- no evidence that Epstein engaged with PED graduate or undergraduate students during these meetings;
- one instance in which he attended part of a Nowak undergraduate mathematics class, leaving without student interaction;
- no finding that trafficking or sexual abuse occurred at PED;
- no basis for identifying the accompanying women as victims merely from their presence.

Visits stopped in 2018 only after PED researchers complained to Nowak about Epstein’s presence and relationship with the program.

### Website and reputation laundering

Beginning in 2013, Epstein’s publicist sought links from PED’s `harvard.edu` domain to Epstein foundation sites, explaining that the Harvard link would improve Epstein’s web visibility. With Nowak’s approval, PED added the links.

In 2014 PED created a full page for Epstein containing his photograph, biography, and links to his websites. It was reached through a “Friends” tab; Epstein was the only friend listed. Harvard found that the linked material contained false $35 million claims and formed part of an attempt to rehabilitate Epstein’s public image.

The page was removed after sexual-assault survivor advocates complained to PED and President Faust’s office in October 2014. An article prepared by Epstein’s publicist and published by *National Review* was also linked by PED; *National Review* later removed it. **The removal did not amount to a retraction of PED’s science, but the promotional donation figures were false or unsupported.**

### 2019–2021 review, leave, sanctions, and closure

After Epstein’s July 2019 federal arrest and August death, Harvard president **Lawrence S. Bacow** announced a review. The May 2020 report found substantial individual and institutional failures involving office access, web promotion, gift-policy communication, and grant representations. Harvard separately identified **$200,937 in unspent Epstein gifts across the university** and divided that money equally between My Life My Choice and Girls Educational & Mentoring Services. It did not identify those residual funds as PED-specific. [President Bacow’s May 2020 statement](https://www.harvard.edu/president/news-and-statements-by-president-bacow/2020/report-regarding-jeffrey-epstein-s-connections-to-harvard/)

Nowak was placed on paid administrative leave in 2020. On March 25, 2021, FAS dean **Claudine Gay** announced that PED would be closed and that Nowak had violated professional-conduct, campus-access, and endorsement-related policies. For two years he could not:

- serve as principal investigator on new grants or contracts;
- take new postdoctoral researchers, graduate students, or other researchers;
- advise undergraduate theses;
- supervise existing graduate students without a co-adviser.

He remained permitted to teach. His research was transferred to the Mathematics Department. [Harvard Magazine, March 25, 2021](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2021/03/martin-nowak-sanctioned-for-jeffrey-epstein-involvement)

Nowak stated that he regretted helping foster the connection between Harvard and Epstein and the hurt it caused. **[Tier D: Nowak’s public response, March 2021.]**

### After closure and records

Harvard restored Nowak’s advising and research privileges in March 2023, according to Organismic and Evolutionary Biology chair **Scott V. Edwards**; Harvard spokespeople declined detailed comment. PED itself was not reopened. [The Crimson, May 22, 2023](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2023/5/22/nowak-sanctions-lifted/)

In February 2026 Harvard placed Nowak on paid administrative leave again after newly released Epstein records prompted a further FAS inquiry. That inquiry concerns Nowak, not the revival of PED, and was unresolved in the sources reviewed. [Harvard Magazine, February 2026](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/university-news/martin-nowak-harvard-administrative-leave-epstein)

No public finding describes what happened to all PED paper files, research data, or administrative correspondence. Harvard University Archives is the repository for institutional records generally, while publications remain in journal repositories, Harvard DASH, the archived PED website/blog, and government-released document collections. No dedicated, publicly catalogued “Program for Evolutionary Dynamics records” collection was located.

## 5. Ideas and Programs

PED’s central theoretical claim was that evolution can be treated as a mathematical dynamical system. Mutation, selection, stochastic drift, population structure, reproduction, and interaction rules could be expressed as equations or computational processes applicable to genes, cells, viruses, organisms, behaviors, and languages.

Its principal programs included:

1. **Evolutionary game theory:** finite-population versions of cooperation/defection games, punishment, reciprocity, public goods, and strategic updating.
2. **Evolutionary graph and set theory:** how network or set structure changes the probability that advantageous traits spread.
3. **Cooperation:** Nowak’s five mechanisms—kin selection, direct reciprocity, indirect reciprocity, network reciprocity, and group selection—and later formal work on structured populations.
4. **Cancer evolution:** mathematical models of tumor-suppressor loss, chromosomal instability, clonal expansion, driver and passenger mutations, treatment resistance, and somatic selection.
5. **Virus and infectious-disease dynamics:** HIV, hepatitis, transmission, immune escape, resistance, and treatment.
6. **Language:** grammar acquisition, irregularization/regularization, peer learning, indirect speech, language corpora, and evolutionary models.
7. **Origins of life:** “prelife,” protocells, template replication, chemical information, and transitions from chemistry to Darwinian evolution.
8. **Behavioral economics and cultural evolution:** risk-taking, punishment, altruism, reputation, group identity, emotion contagion, and international cooperation.
9. **Computation and AI-adjacent work:** graph complexity, neural networks, asynchronous computation, and Joscha Bach’s cognitive architectures.

### Human-subject research

The 2010 report expressly lists experimental studies involving:

- financial and social risk-taking;
- costly punishment and public-goods games;
- reputation and indirect reciprocity;
- altruism among children and churchgoers;
- genetic correlates of economic behavior;
- testosterone or other biological correlates of financial decisions;
- neuroimaging of grammatical processing.

These are documented as PED-affiliated human-subject research topics. The activity report does not reproduce consent forms, IRB approvals, recruitment procedures, or adverse-event records. Individual published studies sometimes identify their participants and funding, but the public sources examined do not establish unethical experimentation, non-consensual experimentation, intelligence sponsorship, or research performed on Epstein’s victims. Claims that PED conducted covert human experimentation are therefore **unsupported [Tier E]**.

### Use by others

PED’s mathematical methods were taken up in theoretical biology, economics, network science, cancer research, infectious-disease modeling, language evolution, and algorithms. Some work received defense-agency grants or fellowships. That fact documents public military research support; it does not itself establish surveillance, psychological warfare, intelligence-tasking, or operational social control.

## 6. Scandal, Controversy and Contest

### Established institutional findings

Harvard found that:

- Epstein’s post-conviction invitations were not themselves prohibited.
- His permanent office, key, codes, and unrestricted cards implicated or violated access policies.
- The 2017 visitor-card workaround circumvented new security procedures.
- PED’s website assisted Epstein’s reputation-rehabilitation campaign and transmitted false giving figures.
- Nowak and PED’s chief administrative officer supplied false information in the Templeton matching-funds matter.
- Harvard’s own gift-ban communication and development practices were inconsistent.
- Development personnel knew and sometimes encouraged Epstein’s role in securing other donors.
- PED researchers’ objections, not senior administration, finally stopped his visits.

### What was not established

- Harvard found no post-2008 direct gift from Epstein to Harvard.
- It found no evidence that the Black or Falconwood gifts were secretly Epstein’s money.
- It found no student contact during the documented PED meetings beyond Epstein’s partial attendance at one class.
- It did not find that abuse or trafficking occurred in PED offices.
- PED, as an institution, was not indicted or prosecuted.
- No located civil judgment held PED liable for Epstein’s offenses.
- No located record shows PED knowingly funding or protecting trafficking.
- No evidence located connected PED to organized crime.
- No public record located establishes CIA, FBI, NSA, Mossad, MKUltra, or other intelligence-agency direction of PED.

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

Before 2006, PED was portrayed as an unusually ambitious center bringing mathematics into biology. Its research on cancer, viruses, cooperation, graphs, and language received prominent academic and popular coverage. Epstein’s financial role was openly acknowledged in numerous papers.

After Epstein’s first arrest, the public story split:

- Harvard emphasized the legitimate scientific uses of an already accepted gift.
- Epstein and his publicists portrayed him as a transformative Harvard science patron, inflating $6.5 million into $30 million or $35 million.
- PED provided a venue and Harvard-domain credibility that assisted that portrayal.
- After 2019, journalism reframed the program as a central case study in donor access, institutional prestige laundering, and weak university controls.
- Harvard’s 2020 report converted many journalistic claims into documented institutional findings.
- The 2021 closure made PED one of the clearest institutional consequences of the Epstein academic network.
- The restoration of Nowak’s privileges in 2023 produced little public explanation.
- Government releases in 2025–2026 renewed examination of meetings and correspondence and led Harvard to place Nowak on leave again, without reopening PED.

The scholarly afterlife is separate from the institutional scandal: PED-affiliated papers remain part of mathematical biology, cancer modeling, and evolutionary game theory. No wholesale retraction of its scientific output occurred. The central reputational dispute concerns patronage, access, truthfulness, and governance, not a demonstrated fabrication of its mathematical results.

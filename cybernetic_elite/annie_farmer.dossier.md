# Annie Farmer: Research Dossier

## Evidentiary key and scope

This dossier distinguishes the status of each claim:

- **[P—primary record]** Court filing, sworn testimony, official transcript, government record, or contemporaneous document. This proves that the statement or event is in the record; it does not necessarily prove every underlying allegation.
- **[A—allegation]** Alleged in litigation or by a named accuser and not separately adjudicated.
- **[J—reported]** Reported by a named journalist or established news organization.
- **[S—subject’s account]** Claimed by Annie Farmer in testimony, interviews, or professional materials.
- **[W—witness account]** Claimed by another identified witness.
- **[D—denial/dispute]** Denied or materially disputed by the accused or another source.
- **[R—rumor/conspiracy]** Circulated without adequate documentary support.
- **[N—negative finding]** No substantiating record was located in the materials searched.

“Annie Farmer” is sometimes rendered “Ann Farmer” in indexes or redacted government records. “Minor Victim 2,” “Victim 2,” and similar courtroom identifiers are litigation designations, not aliases. This dossier does not disclose private residential or contact information.

## 1. Identification

Annie Farmer is an American psychologist, therapist, survivor advocate, civil plaintiff, and government witness, born July 2, 1979, in Missouri. She lived in Arizona from approximately age eight and attended Xavier College Preparatory in Phoenix. She later earned a B.A. in anthropology from the University of Pennsylvania and a doctorate in counseling or educational psychology from the University of Texas at Austin; her professional biography says her doctoral work concerned the effects of post-traumatic stress disorder on female partners of military veterans. She has practiced psychology in Texas and co-founded Pivot Psychology in Austin. **[P/S]** Her birth date, educational history, and occupation appear in her civil complaint and sworn testimony; her fuller clinical history comes from her professional biography. [Farmer’s 2019 complaint](https://ia803106.us.archive.org/20/items/gov.uscourts.nysd.526309/gov.uscourts.nysd.526309.1.0.pdf), [December 10, 2021 trial transcript](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/USA_vs._Maxwell_jury_trial_transcript_-_volume_10.pdf), [professional biography](https://www.anniefarmer.com/about-dr-farmer).

Farmer is publicly known principally for her account that Jeffrey Edward Epstein and Ghislaine Noelle Marion Maxwell groomed and sexually abused her when she was sixteen, particularly during an April 1996 visit to Epstein’s New Mexico property known as Zorro Ranch. **[P/A/S]** She sued Epstein’s estate and Maxwell in 2019, accepted $1.5 million from the Epstein Victims’ Compensation Program in 2020, dismissed the suit with prejudice pursuant to a release in 2021, testified for the United States at Maxwell’s criminal trial in December 2021, and addressed the court at Maxwell’s June 2022 sentencing. **[P]** The settlement resolved Farmer’s civil claims without an adjudication of their truth or falsity; Maxwell’s criminal jury did not return a special verdict establishing every act Farmer described.

Farmer is Maria Farmer’s younger sister. Maria worked for Epstein in the mid-1990s and has separately alleged that Epstein and Maxwell assaulted her and that she reported them to the New York Police Department and Federal Bureau of Investigation in 1996. The sisters’ experiences overlap in the documentary record but must not be conflated: the available 1996 FBI report was Maria Farmer’s report, whereas Annie Farmer’s own first documented FBI interview occurred in November 2006. **[P/A]**

No public record reviewed identifies Annie Farmer as an intelligence officer, financier, corporate director, political official, cybernetician, intelligence asset, participant in MKUltra, or associate of Theodore John Kaczynski. **[N]** Her defensible place in the Epstein network is as a minor whom Epstein and Maxwell cultivated, a recipient of Epstein-financed travel, Maria Farmer’s sister, and later an accuser, civil claimant, trial witness, and transparency advocate.

## 2. The Record

### Childhood, education, and professional life

Farmer testified that she was born in Missouri and moved repeatedly before living in Arizona from about age eight. Her parents divorced; her 2019 complaint says her mother raised three daughters while facing financial strain. Farmer attended Xavier College Preparatory during the 1995–96 academic year and described herself as a high-achieving student interested in attending an elite university. **[P/S]**

She later attended the University of Pennsylvania, earning a bachelor’s degree in anthropology. Her professional biography says her honors research addressed women’s access to health care. She earned a doctorate at the University of Texas at Austin and completed clinical training that included the Michael E. DeBakey VA Medical Center in Houston, where she worked with trauma-related disorders. **[S]** Her publicly described clinical experience includes inpatient psychiatry, college counseling, primary-care settings, veterans, trauma survivors, and private practice. [Professional biography](https://www.anniefarmer.com/about-dr-farmer).

World Without Exploitation’s biography says that in 2003 Farmer worked with the Tundra Women’s Coalition in Alaska, the Alaska Network on Domestic Violence and Sexual Assault, and Yup’ik youth on *Let’s Talk About It*, a youth-produced film concerning intimate-partner violence and adolescent relationships. **[S—organizational biography]** [World Without Exploitation biography](https://www.worldwithoutexploitation.org/bios/annie-farmer).

Public professional pages identify her as a co-founder of Pivot Psychology in Austin. The practice listed a Texas license number and a $200 standard session fee, but these pages do not establish income, wealth, ownership percentages, or present license status after the displayed expiration date. **[S/N]** [Pivot Psychology clinicians](https://www.pivotpsychologyatx.com/clinicians).

No reliable public source reviewed identifies a spouse or marriage, children, inherited wealth, trusts, securities holdings, offshore entities, political donations, corporate boards, security clearance, or government advisory position. **[N]**

### Initial contact with Jeffrey Epstein: New York, late 1995 or January 1996

Farmer’s connection to Epstein arose through Maria Farmer, who was then working for him. Farmer testified that Maria described Epstein as a wealthy patron interested in education and young people. Epstein expressed an interest in helping Annie’s academic prospects and suggested international volunteer experience would strengthen her college applications. **[P/S]**

Farmer’s testimony placed her first New York visit in late 1995 or around January 1996, when she was sixteen. She stayed in Epstein’s Manhattan townhouse, met with him, and attended *The Phantom of the Opera*. Maxwell was not present during the townhouse or theater events Farmer described. **[P/S]**

Farmer testified that during the theater outing Epstein held her hand, rubbed her arm, touched her shoe or foot, and behaved in a way she found “weird” but initially rationalized as affection from a parental or mentor figure. Her January 7, 1996 journal entry described the trip positively and said it changed her outlook. **[P/S]** At Maxwell’s trial the defense used that entry to argue that Farmer did not perceive the encounter as abuse at the time. Farmer answered that she had rationalized the behavior because Epstein had presented himself as a caring adult and benefactor. **[P/D]**

Farmer did not testify that Epstein had sexual contact with her inside the townhouse, and she confirmed Maxwell was not present during this New York visit. **[P]** That distinction narrows, rather than contradicts, her subsequent allegations.

### Zorro Ranch, New Mexico: April 1996

Epstein subsequently invited Farmer to his New Mexico property. Farmer’s complaint says the invitation was represented as an educational or school-related visit with adult supervision and possibly other students. Farmer testified that she spoke to Epstein by telephone several times, flew commercially to New Mexico alone, and did not pay for the ticket. A driver collected her and took her to the isolated ranch. **[P/S]**

The ranch visit occurred in April 1996. Because Farmer was born July 2, 1979, she was sixteen. Some early press accounts and Maria Farmer’s descriptions rendered Annie as fifteen; Farmer’s dated complaint and sworn trial testimony consistently say sixteen. **[P/D]**

Farmer testified that only Epstein and Maxwell were present as hosts. Maxwell welcomed her and discussed school. During a shopping excursion Maxwell bought henna or skin cream, while Epstein and Maxwell bought Farmer cowboy boots. **[P/S]**

Farmer described the following sequence under oath:

1. Maxwell showed Farmer how to massage Epstein’s bare feet and directed her to do so. Farmer testified that Epstein groaned and that she felt uncomfortable. **[P/S]**
2. During a movie, Epstein and Maxwell behaved sexually with one another. Farmer said Epstein again caressed her hand or leg. Her civil complaint alleged more specifically that Maxwell pulled down Epstein’s sweatpants during the movie. **[P/A/S]**
3. Maxwell repeatedly urged Farmer to receive a massage, instructed her to undress, and placed her naked under a sheet. Farmer testified that Maxwell massaged her back and legs, told her to turn over, pulled down the sheet, and rubbed her chest and upper breasts. Farmer described freezing and not knowing how to respond. **[P/A/S]**
4. The next morning, Farmer testified, Epstein entered her bedroom, said he wanted to cuddle, got into bed behind her, put his arms around her, and pressed his body or genitals against her. She escaped by saying she needed to use the bathroom. **[P/A/S]**

Farmer testified that the episode changed her understanding of the invitation: what had been presented as mentoring and educational assistance now appeared to her to have been sexual grooming. She did not write about the ranch incident in her journal, which she said reflected avoidance rather than absence of the event. **[P/S]**

The 2019 complaint additionally alleged that Farmer had no independent transportation from the remote property and felt unable to leave. That assertion supported a civil false-imprisonment claim but was never adjudicated. **[P/A]**

### Disclosure in 1996 and the Global Routes trip

Farmer testified that after returning home she was withdrawn. Her mother, Janice Swain, testified that Farmer seemed quiet and unlike herself. Later that summer Farmer told her mother that something had happened but added that she had not been raped and did not want to discuss it. **[P/W]**

Farmer also testified that she told her high-school boyfriend, David James Mulligan, during the fall of 1996. Mulligan testified that she described Maxwell touching her breasts at the ranch and that Farmer appeared fearful, awkward, and helpless when recounting it. **[P/W]**

Epstein subsequently paid for Farmer to participate in a roughly six-week summer service and cultural program in Thailand and Vietnam operated by Global Routes. At trial Farmer said she turned seventeen during the trip. **[P/S]** A redacted FBI interview memorandum dated November 15, 2006, says Epstein and Maxwell mailed the interviewee a ticket and several hundred dollars for the Global Routes trip. The memorandum identifies Global Routes as an organization through which North American youth performed overseas community service. **[P]** The released record is redacted, but its account corresponds closely to Farmer’s trial testimony. [FBI investigative file, EFTA00162520](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00162520.pdf).

Farmer testified that she did not maintain contact with Epstein after the trip. She also confirmed that Epstein did not pay her University of Pennsylvania tuition. **[P]**

### The 1996 police and FBI reporting question

Maria Farmer’s 2019 affidavit alleged that after an assault at Leslie H. Wexner’s Ohio property, she sought help from New York’s Sixth Precinct and reported Epstein and Maxwell to the FBI in 1996. Annie’s civil complaint alleged that Maria told law enforcement what had happened and telephoned Annie while Annie was abroad. **[P/A]**

Files released in December 2025 corroborated that an FBI complaint connected to Maria Farmer was recorded in September 1996. Reporting on the document says it described Epstein’s possession or theft of nude and partially nude photographs of the sisters, threats, and concerns about exploitation. **[P/J]** The released record does not demonstrate that the FBI interviewed Annie in 1996 or opened a documented investigation specifically into the Zorro Ranch massage and bedroom allegations. [TIME report on the released 1996 file](https://time.com/7342045/epstein-files-release-trump-bill-clinton-maria-farmer-1996-redactions/).

Annie Farmer has subsequently characterized Maria’s 1996 report more broadly, saying it warned the government about Maria’s experience in Ohio and Annie’s experience at Zorro Ranch. **[S]** Her May 2026 statement entered in the Congressional Record says the FBI failed to heed Maria’s report and that decisive action could have prevented later abuse. [Congressional Record, May 26, 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-05-26/pdf/CREC-2026-05-26-pt1-PgE493-2.pdf).

The reconciled finding is therefore:

- A 1996 FBI contact by Maria Farmer is now documented. **[P]**
- Annie Farmer’s own first located FBI interview is dated November 15, 2006. **[P]**
- Whether Maria’s 1996 report gave the FBI a detailed, independently actionable account of the entire Zorro Ranch episode remains disputed by how later accounts summarize the redacted and incomplete record. **[D]**

### Vanity Fair, 2002–03

In 2002 journalist Vicky Ward interviewed Maria Farmer, Annie Farmer, and their mother while reporting a profile of Epstein for *Vanity Fair*. Annie was by then an undergraduate at the University of Pennsylvania. The sisters spoke on the record and allowed their names to be used. **[J/W]**

The published March 2003 article, “The Talented Mr. Epstein,” described Epstein’s money, social circle, and access to young women but omitted the Farmer sisters’ accusations. [Ward’s published 2003 article](https://www.vanityfair.com/news/2003/03/jeffrey-epstein-200303).

The reason for the omission is disputed:

- Ward has said she reported the allegations and that Epstein pressured editor Graydon Carter, including through threats directed at Ward. **[J/S]**
- Carter has said the allegations did not meet *Vanity Fair*’s legal and evidentiary threshold and disputed Ward’s later account of when and how she presented them. **[D]**
- A 2022 *New Yorker* investigation by Isaac Chotiner found emails showing that Ward raised the allegations before publication, but also found inconsistencies between Ward’s later public narrative and a 2011 draft account. The draft reportedly minimized Annie Farmer’s allegations and did not fully recount the massage and bedroom episodes. Ward acknowledged uncertainty in her memory. **[J/D]**
- Annie and Maria Farmer said the omission erased their warnings and left them less safe. **[S]**

The most defensible conclusion is that Ward did interview both sisters in 2002 and the allegations were omitted in 2003; the internal editorial reason and the degree of Epstein’s influence remain contested. [GBH/NPR investigation](https://www.wgbh.org/news/national/2019-08-22/a-dead-cat-a-lawyers-call-and-a-5-figure-donation-how-media-fell-short-on-epstein), [2022 *New Yorker* investigation](https://www.newyorker.com/news/annals-of-communications/why-didnt-vanity-fair-break-the-jeffrey-epstein-story).

Ward later released audio from her 2002 interview with Maxwell. Maxwell denied giving Annie a massage, called the Farmer sisters’ accounts false, and asserted that they had benefited from Epstein. **[D—accused’s recorded denial]** [Ward’s released interview material](https://www.vickywardinvestigates.com/p/maxwell-unfiltered).

### FBI interview, 2006

Farmer testified in 2021 that the FBI first interviewed her in 2006. A released investigative file includes a redacted November 15, 2006 interview describing the New Mexico trip, the massage setting, Epstein entering the bedroom, the Global Routes payment, and the interviewee’s distress. **[P]** The memorandum belongs to the federal investigation opened after Palm Beach police investigated Epstein between 2005 and 2006. [FBI investigative file](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00162520.pdf).

This establishes that Farmer’s account was in federal investigative files approximately thirteen years before Epstein’s 2019 arrest and fifteen years before Maxwell’s trial. It does not establish what charging decisions prosecutors considered in relation to Farmer or why her account did not produce a charge at that time. **[P/N]**

### Epstein proceedings, 2019

On July 15, 2019, Farmer appeared through attorney David Boies at Epstein’s federal bail hearing in the Southern District of New York. She identified herself by name, said she had been sixteen when Epstein flew her to New Mexico, and urged Judge Richard M. Berman to deny bail. She argued that Epstein’s wealth and notoriety discouraged victims from coming forward. **[P/S]** [July 15, 2019 bail transcript](https://epstein-docs.github.io/document/36/).

Judge Berman denied bail on July 18, citing danger to the community among the central considerations and expressly referencing statements from Farmer and Courtney Wild. **[P/J]**

After Epstein died in federal custody on August 10, 2019, Farmer spoke at the September 3 hearing at which the government sought dismissal of the criminal indictment. She said she was sorry other survivors could no longer confront Epstein in court, spoke for Maria, criticized authorities’ failure to respond to the 1996 warning, and asked that investigations into Epstein’s associates continue. **[P/S]** [September 3, 2019 hearing transcript](https://epstein.kevinchamplin.com/serve.php?id=4378).

### Civil suit, 2019–21

On November 12, 2019, Farmer sued Darren K. Indyke and Richard D. Kahn, as executors of the Estate of Jeffrey Edward Epstein, and Ghislaine Maxwell in the Southern District of New York. The complaint pleaded battery, false imprisonment, and intentional infliction of emotional distress and requested compensatory and punitive damages. **[P/A]** [Civil complaint](https://ia803106.us.archive.org/20/items/gov.uscourts.nysd.526309/gov.uscourts.nysd.526309.1.0.pdf).

The complaint’s factual allegations were never tried. On June 2, 2020, the Virgin Islands probate court authorized the Epstein Victims’ Compensation Program. Farmer submitted a claim on June 26 and accepted an offer on October 5, 2020. The release covered Epstein’s estate and related individuals, including Maxwell, and required dismissal of the civil suit. **[P]**

Judge Lorna G. Schofield allowed Farmer to dismiss the action with prejudice without publicly filing an unredacted compensation release. Maxwell had argued she should receive information concerning the payment; the court rejected that demand as unnecessary to dismissal. **[P]** [February 10, 2021 dismissal order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2019cv10475/526309/118/).

At Maxwell’s trial Farmer disclosed that the award was $1.5 million, wired to her after she accepted the offer. She testified that Boies Schiller Flexner represented her pro bono and took no portion of the award. **[P]**

The compensation was a claims-program resolution, not a judicial finding that Maxwell or Epstein’s estate admitted each allegation. The dismissal “with prejudice” prevents Farmer from refiling the released claims; it does not mean the allegations were dismissed as false.

### Maxwell detention hearing and trial

At Maxwell’s July 14, 2020 arraignment and bail hearing, Farmer said she met Maxwell at sixteen and accused her of grooming and abusing Farmer and other young women. Farmer urged detention, citing Maxwell’s resources and international connections. **[P/A/S]** Judge Alison J. Nathan detained Maxwell based on flight risk; the government expressly said it was not asking for detention on dangerousness grounds at that hearing. [July 14, 2020 transcript](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United_States_v._Ghislaine_Maxwell_(Arraignment_Hearing_Transcript)).

Maxwell’s attorneys subpoenaed material including Farmer’s journal, photographs, cowboy boots, communications, and Victims’ Compensation Program submissions. Portions were used in cross-examination at trial. **[P]**

Farmer testified on December 10, 2021. She was the fourth accuser to testify and the only one to use her full name. She identified Maxwell in court, described both the New York and New Mexico episodes, and recounted disclosures to her mother and boyfriend. Janice Swain and David James Mulligan then supplied limited corroboration concerning Farmer’s condition and contemporaneous disclosure. **[P/W]** [Trial transcript](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/USA_vs._Maxwell_jury_trial_transcript_-_volume_10.pdf).

The defense elicited several differences in wording among Farmer’s journal, FBI interview, civil complaint, compensation submission, and direct testimony:

- Her January 1996 journal was positive and recorded that she rationalized Epstein’s touching. **[P/D]**
- A compensation-program characterization called the theater conduct sexual abuse, while the detailed narrative described caressing rather than touching private parts. **[P/D]**
- Different documents used “groped,” “rubbed,” or “touched” concerning Maxwell’s contact with Farmer’s chest. **[P/D]**
- The compensation narrative referred to Epstein rubbing or pressing his genitals against Farmer; at trial she described him pressing his body or genitals against her from behind. **[P/D]**
- Farmer used records to refresh dates but testified that she remembered the traumatic events themselves without those records. **[P/S]**

These were impeachment points placed before the jury, not judicial findings that she lied.

Judge Nathan instructed the jury that the alleged physical contact with Farmer was not itself the “illegal sexual activity” charged under the statutes before the jury. Her testimony was admitted for narrower purposes concerning the alleged conspiracy, grooming methods, intent, and relationship among the participants; it was not permission to convict Maxwell merely because jurors believed Maxwell had harmed Farmer. **[P]**

On December 29, 2021, the jury found Maxwell guilty on five of six counts. The verdict established Maxwell’s criminal participation in a scheme involving sexual exploitation of minors, but it did not return act-by-act findings on Farmer’s ranch allegations. Any statement that Maxwell was convicted specifically of assaulting Annie Farmer would therefore overstate the verdict.

### Sentencing and subsequent advocacy

Farmer submitted a victim-impact statement and spoke at Maxwell’s June 28, 2022 sentencing. She described lasting effects including distrust, hypervigilance, and the burden of repeatedly recounting the abuse. She argued that Maxwell had manipulated and bullied victims and shown no genuine remorse. **[P/S]** [Filed victim-impact statements](https://www.justice.gov/multimedia/Court%20Records/United%20States%20v.%20Maxwell%2C%20No.%20120-cr-00330%20%28S.D.N.Y.%202020%29/672.pdf), [sentencing transcript](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United_States_v._Ghislaine_Maxwell_(Sentencing_Transcript)).

Judge Nathan sentenced Maxwell to 240 months’ imprisonment. Farmer later described the sentence as a “tremendous relief” but said she did not regard Maxwell’s courtroom remarks as an apology. **[J/S]**

Farmer continued advocating for release of Epstein-related records and accountability for institutional failures. In February 2026 Representative Robert Garcia invited her as his guest to the State of the Union, describing her as an advocate for survivors and transparency. **[P—congressional release]** [House Oversight Democrats announcement](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/ranking-member-robert-garcia-announces-epstein-survivor-annie-farmer-as-state-of-the-union-guest).

In May 2026 Farmer’s statement was entered into the Congressional Record. She asked Congress to protect survivor identities, investigate the failure to act on Maria Farmer’s 1996 report, release records responsibly, hold alleged enablers accountable, and oppose any pardon of Maxwell. **[P/S]**

In July 2026 Farmer and other survivors met Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche. Farmer described the meeting as abrasive, condescending, and noncommittal; the Justice Department characterized it as productive and said survivors were encouraged to provide information. These are conflicting accounts of the meeting, not reconcilable findings. **[J/S/D]** [Associated Press report](https://apnews.com/article/3a5877e7cd70bf545fbf2d318188b0d9).

Farmer is alive. There is no estate or publicly identified personal archive. Her journal, photographs, cowboy boots, FBI interviews, compensation materials, and litigation files constitute the principal known documentary corpus; some compensation and investigative records remain sealed, redacted, or protected.

### The underlying accusations

### Maxwell’s denials

In Ward’s 2002 recording Maxwell denied massaging Farmer and disparaged the sisters’ truthfulness and motives. Maxwell pleaded not guilty, challenged Farmer’s evidence, and contested the government’s use of it. Her eventual conviction disproved her blanket position that she bore no criminal responsibility in Epstein’s exploitation scheme, but it did not adjudicate every statement she made about Farmer.

### Variations in Farmer’s accounts

The defense identified variations in chronology and wording across Farmer’s journal, FBI statements, complaint, compensation application, and testimony. The record supports both propositions:

- Farmer’s central narrative—New York contact followed by an April 1996 New Mexico trip, Maxwell’s massage, Epstein entering her bed, later overseas travel, and delayed fuller disclosure—appears repeatedly over many years. **[P]**
- Details and labels differed: “caressing” versus “sexual abuse,” “rubbed” versus “groped,” and “pressed” versus “rubbed his genitals.” **[P/D]**

No court found Farmer committed perjury. The jury heard the discrepancies and convicted Maxwell on five counts, but that verdict cannot be converted into a finding on every disputed detail.

Farmer’s birth date and her testimony establish that she was sixteen in April 1996. Accounts describing her as fifteen appear to be secondary or familial dating errors. The primary record controls. **[P/D]**

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

Farmer’s public image changed in several stages:

1. **1996–2002: private or limited disclosure.** Her mother, boyfriend, sister, and later the FBI received all or part of the account. She was not yet a public figure.
2. **2002–03: suppressed or omitted journalism.** Ward interviewed her by name, but *Vanity Fair* omitted the allegations. The resulting profile enhanced Epstein’s public mystique while excluding the Farmer account.
3. **2006–18: investigative-record presence without broad public recognition.** Her account entered the FBI’s Epstein file. She pursued education and clinical work.
4. **2019: named public accuser.** She addressed Epstein’s bail hearing, spoke at the post-death hearing, and filed a civil action.
5. **2020–21: documentary subject, compensated claimant, and criminal witness.** Streaming documentaries and extensive reporting circulated her account; she accepted compensation and testified at Maxwell’s trial.
6. **2022 onward: survivor advocate and institutional critic.** Her sentencing statement and later congressional activity emphasized both individual wrongdoing and government failure.

The central works and records shaping her public image include:

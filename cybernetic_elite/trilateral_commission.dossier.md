# Trilateral Commission: Research Dossier

## Evidentiary key and scope

- **[P—primary]** Contemporary organizational records, government documents, court filings, tax returns, archival finding aids, published rosters, correspondence, or recordings.
- **[R—reported]** Reporting by an identified publication or journalist.
- **[A—allegation]** A claim in litigation or by a named accuser, not established as fact.
- **[S—subject claim]** A statement by the Commission, a member, or another directly involved person, not independently proved unless paired with **[P]**.
- **[C—conspiracy/rumor]** A circulating narrative for which the searched record supplies insufficient evidence.
- **[D—disproved, contradicted, or misattributed]** A claim contradicted by dated records or unsupported by the source to which it is attributed.

“Every tie” cannot literally encompass every person who has appeared on every roster during more than fifty years. The network register below covers the founders, officers, documented funders, government/intelligence/finance overlaps, the Epstein chain, current leadership, and the named people most relevant to the requested cybernetics–computing–intelligence–finance network. The complete June 12, 1995 roster is linked so every remaining contemporaneous co-membership can be checked directly.

## 1. Identification

**[P/S]** The Trilateral Commission is a private, nongovernmental international policy-discussion and membership organization founded in July 1973. Its original geographic “triad” was North America, Western Europe, and Japan; its present structure comprises North America, Europe, and Asia Pacific. It convenes regional and plenary meetings and commissions reports intended to shape discussion among political, corporate, financial, academic, labor, media, and nonprofit leaders. It is not a treaty organization, legislature, intelligence agency, government commission, bank, grant-making foundation, or regulatory authority. Its current description calls it a “global membership organization” founded by David Rockefeller; its archival history also identifies Zbigniew Brzezinski as the principal intellectual organizer and first director. ([Commission, current “About” page](https://www.trilateral.org/about/); [Rockefeller Archive Center finding aid, 2011 copy](https://statemilitiaintel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/trilateral-commission.pdf))

**[P]** The North American entity is **Trilateral Commission (North America)**, a District of Columbia corporation with EIN **23-7309933**, formed in 1973 and recognized as tax-exempt under IRC §501(c)(3) in August 1976. Its most recently accessible filings classify it as an international-affairs/public-policy research organization. The European and Asia-Pacific groups have their own secretariats and fundraising arrangements; the North American Form 990 is therefore not a consolidated worldwide account. ([2023 Form 990, filed 2025](https://pdf.guidestar.org/PDF_Images/2024/237/309/2024-237309933-202511359349311496-9.pdf); [ProPublica IRS-derived record](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/237309933))

**[P]** Current published leadership identifies **Meghan L. O’Sullivan** as North American chair, **Axel A. Weber** as European chair, **Takeshi Niinami** as Asia-Pacific chair, and **Richard Fontaine** as North American executive director. The organization reports 135 North American, 184 European, and 96 Asia-Pacific members, plus fellows and global members; its Executive Committee has 74 members. Members entering national-government roles are supposed to rotate off, while selection procedures vary by national group. ([current leadership and membership page](https://www.trilateral.org/about/members-fellows/))

## 2. The Record

### Origins, 1970–73

**[P]** Brzezinski’s 1970 book *Between Two Ages: America’s Role in the Technetronic Era* argued that technological and economic interdependence was weakening the ability of nation-states to act alone. The Commission’s archival history cites pages 295–97 of that book as part of the intellectual background. “Technetronic” referred to a society transformed by electronics, communications, computing, and knowledge production; it does not establish that the later Commission was itself a cybernetics or computing laboratory. ([RAC finding aid](https://statemilitiaintel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/trilateral-commission.pdf))

**[P]** Archival reconstruction records an April 1972 discussion in which Rockefeller advanced the idea of a North American–European–Japanese body. His consultations with Brzezinski, former Council on Foreign Relations executive director **George S. Franklin Jr.**, and contacts in Europe and Japan culminated in a meeting at the Rockefeller family estate at Pocantico Hills on **July 23–24, 1972**. Participants included C. Fred Bergsten, Robert Bowie, Brzezinski, McGeorge Bundy, Karl Carstens, Guido Colonna di Paliano, François Duchêne, George Franklin, Max Kohnstamm, Bayless Manning, Kiichi Miyazawa, Saburo Okita, Henry Owen, Rockefeller, and Tadashi Yamamoto. They agreed to proceed with the project. The underlying archive citation is “The Trilateral Commission: A Proposal,” March 15, 1973, in the Rockefeller Brothers Fund records. **[P]** This establishes organized elite planning; it does not establish a covert governmental charter. ([RAC finding-aid text](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=8e970b338fcae64177e8030d5723bff8d0165081&repid=rep1&type=pdf))

**[P]** At a Tokyo planning meeting in January 1973, **Gerard C. Smith**, **Max Kohnstamm**, and **Takeshi Watanabe** were chosen as the initial North American, European, and Japanese chairs; Brzezinski was confirmed as director. A March 1973 constitution envisioned approximately 180 members, an Executive Committee, three regional chairs, and a director. The Commission formally began in July, and its founding Executive Committee session took place in Tokyo on **October 20–22, 1973**. ([historical study based on the archives](https://www.issuelab.org/resources/27799/27799.pdf); [RAC finding aid](https://statemilitiaintel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/trilateral-commission.pdf))

### Structure and method

**[P/S]** Its early published formulation said that private citizens from North America, Western Europe, and Japan would foster cooperation, improve public understanding, support joint proposals, and cultivate habits of working together. Commission-sponsored reports explicitly stated that authors wrote in their personal capacities and did not necessarily represent their employers. ([1976 report preserved in the World Bank Archives](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/077070325155065522-0560011976/original/WorldBankGroupArchivesfolder1056373.pdf))

**[P]** Initial membership was approximately 190. Regional ceilings appeared around 1980. By 2011 the ceilings were 120 in North America—87 United States, 20 Canada, 13 Mexico—160 in Europe, and more than 100 in Pacific Asia. The current published totals are higher. ([RAC finding aid](https://statemilitiaintel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/trilateral-commission.pdf); [current membership page](https://www.trilateral.org/about/members-fellows/))

**[P/S]** Meetings generally combine annual plenaries, regional meetings, private discussion, invited officials, and commissioned reports. A 1977 account entered in the Congressional Record said selected reporters could attend but discussions were private and attributable only under agreed background rules. That combination—published rosters and reports but nonpublic deliberation—is the factual basis for describing the organization as private or opaque; “secret society” is an interpretive label, not its legal form. ([Congressional Record, January 19, 1977](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1977-pt2/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1977-pt2-2-3.pdf))

### Carter-era political influence

**[P]** Jimmy Carter belonged from 1973 until entering the presidency. The State Department’s official historical account says Carter served from 1973–76 and Brzezinski became his national security adviser. Carter met Brzezinski through Commission work and drew numerous foreign-policy officials from the same professional network. ([State Department historical account](https://2001-2009.state.gov/r/pa/ho/time/qfp/103525.htm))

**[P]** A January 1977 State Department diplomatic record shows Chinese ambassador Huang Chen discussing Rockefeller’s introductions of Brzezinski, Cyrus Vance, and W. Michael Blumenthal. Henry Kissinger jokingly called the Commission a “government in exile,” while Vance remarked that so many members had entered the Carter administration that few remained for the Tokyo meeting. The exchange documents perceived personnel overlap, but the wording was humorous conversation, not proof that the Commission exercised governmental command. ([FRUS diplomatic conversation, January 17, 1977](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v13/d2))

**[P]** A 1977 foreign-government assessment listed Carter, Walter Mondale, Brzezinski, Vance, Warren Christopher, Richard Cooper, Richard Holbrooke, and Blumenthal among Commission members in senior administration posts. **Finding:** this was a real concentration of a small elite network. **Non-finding:** the records reviewed do not show that the Commission legally appointed them, issued binding instructions, or constituted a parallel government. ([FRUS document](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v22/d233))

### Later development

**[P/S]** In 2000 Mexico entered the North American group and the Japanese group expanded into the Pacific Asian group, initially adding South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, and ASEAN representation. China and India joined the regional framework in 2009. ([Asia-Pacific history](https://www.trilateral.org/regions/asia-pacific-group/))

**[P/S]** The organization now runs **David Rockefeller Fellows** programs for younger participants. North American fellows must be 35 or younger, obtain a nomination from a current member, and attend regional and global meetings; the Commission may cover their travel. ([North American fellows rules](https://www.trilateral.org/about/david-rockefeller-fellows-north-america/))

**[P]** The institution remains active. Its 50th-anniversary plenary in New Delhi, March 10–12, 2023, reportedly brought more than 260 participants from 46 countries. ([Japan Center for International Exchange meeting record](https://jcie.org/programs/trilateral-commission/trilateral-commission-2023-plenary-meeting-delhi/))

### Archives and surviving records

**[P]** The North American group donated records to the Rockefeller Archive Center in accessions received in 1985 and 2002–03. The collection includes planning files, minutes, correspondence, membership records, task-force material, meeting files, *Trialogue*, and financial records through the 1990s. Related microfilm is held at the Jimmy Carter Library; Brzezinski’s later papers are also at the Library of Congress. ([RAC finding aid](https://statemilitiaintel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/trilateral-commission.pdf); [Library of Congress Brzezinski collection](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/4723))

**[P]** The finding aid states plainly that much day-to-day financial material was discarded before transfer. Surviving financial records cover 1972–97, including selected statements, fundraising correspondence, foundation files, corporate solicitations, and “balancing of accounts” among the regions for 1976–88. Thus the archive is substantial but not a complete transactional ledger. This is an important documented absence, not proof that the missing material concealed illegality. ([RAC finding-aid financial-series description](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=8e970b338fcae64177e8030d5723bff8d0165081&repid=rep1&type=pdf))

### Power

**[P/inference]** The Commission’s power is primarily:

1. Selection and convening power: deciding which leaders enter a recurring private forum.
2. Agenda power: choosing report topics and meeting programs.
3. Network power: repeated contact among people who later enter governments, central banks, corporations, universities, media, and international institutions.
4. Reputation power: membership can function as elite credentialing, as the Epstein record illustrates.
5. Policy-discourse power: reports and personal relationships can influence debate.

It has no documented power to enact laws, appropriate taxes, issue currency, command intelligence services, prosecute cases, or bind member governments. The stronger claim that it secretly exercises those functions remains **[C]** absent operational records.

## 5. Ideas and Programs

### Trilateralism and interdependence

**[P/S]** The founding thesis was that postwar U.S. predominance had weakened while North America, Western Europe, and Japan had become economically interdependent. Energy shocks, monetary instability, trade disputes, multinational corporations, and relations between industrial and developing states therefore required recurring coordination outside slow formal diplomacy.

**[P]** Early “Triangle Papers” addressed monetary reform, trade, energy, aid to resource-poor countries, multinational corporations, and reform of international institutions. Their recommendations were not treaties, but the authors and audience occupied positions capable of translating ideas into national or corporate policy.

### *The Crisis of Democracy*

**[P]** Michel Crozier, Samuel P. Huntington, and Joji Watanuki’s 1975 report *The Crisis of Democracy* argued that Western governments faced overload, weakened authority, expanding demands, and—in Huntington’s U.S. chapter—an “excess of democracy.” The publication is genuine and remains available through the Commission. ([official publication page](https://www.trilateral.org/publications/task-force-report-8-the-crisis-of-democracy/))

**[R/interpretation]** Critics including Noam Chomsky and left-wing contributors to Holly Sklar’s *Trilateralism* treated it as evidence that corporate and governmental elites sought to discipline mass participation after the civil-rights, labor, antiwar, feminist, and student movements. That is a serious textual and political critique, distinct from claims that the Commission possessed a hidden world-government chain of command.

### Cybernetics, computing, behavioral science, and human subjects

**Finding:** **[P]** Brzezinski’s “technetronic” analysis and later Commission work on communications, information trade, technology, AI, and economic systems place it within the history of elite thinking about networked technological society.

**Non-finding:** No searched roster, task-force record, tax filing, archival description, or government file shows that the Commission:

- operated a cybernetics laboratory;
- conducted experiments on human subjects;
- administered psychiatric, drug, mind-control, or behavioral-modification research;
- funded MKUltra;
- employed Ted Kaczynski;
- directed ARPANET, the Internet, AI laboratories, or intelligence research programs;
- ran surveillance or blackmail operations.

Individuals on its rosters had separate ties to defense, intelligence, computing, universities, and foundations. Treating every activity of every member as an institutional Commission program would be a composition error.

## 6. Scandal, Controversy and Contest

### Elite access and democratic accountability

**[P/R]** The strongest substantiated controversy is structural rather than clandestine: a self-selecting private organization joined senior corporate, financial, media, academic, labor, and political leaders; held important discussions mainly off the record; published policy recommendations; and supplied a conspicuous number of Carter administration officials. Critics reasonably described this as elite agenda formation outside electoral accountability. The documentary record supports that critique without proving a centrally commanded conspiracy.

### Right-wing conspiracy literature

**[C]** From the mid-1970s, Gary Allen, the John Birch milieu, Antony C. Sutton, and later Pat Robertson portrayed the Commission, CFR, and Bilderberg as an “Insider” apparatus seeking world government, surrender of U.S. sovereignty, collectivism, or banker rule. Ronald Reagan and John Connally used Commission affiliation against George H. W. Bush and Carter during the 1980 campaign, although Reagan said he did not consider it literally conspiratorial. Contemporary reporting documented the political controversy. ([Christian Science Monitor, April 17, 1980](https://www.csmonitor.com/1980/0417/041760.html))

**[C]** Lyndon LaRouche’s publications attributed drug trafficking and a variety of geopolitical plots to a Rockefeller–Rothschild–Trilateral apparatus. The searched record contains no primary documentation connecting the Commission institutionally to organized crime or drug trafficking.

**[D/C]** The often-circulated quotation that David Rockefeller thanked *The Washington Post*, *The New York Times*, and *Time* for keeping a plan for world government secret, and praised the “supranational sovereignty of an intellectual elite and world bankers,” is not supported by a located transcript of a 1991 Commission or Bilderberg speech. Its versions disagree about venue, and the “almost forty years” wording cannot refer to a Commission then only eighteen years old. A source-tracing article concluded it was probably fabricated. ([source investigation](https://www.lobster-magazine.co.uk/article/issue/46/the-view-from-the-bridge-10/))

**[S, often stripped of context]** Rockefeller really did write in his 2002 memoir that if supporting a more integrated global political and economic structure constituted the alleged charge of conspiracy, he stood “guilty” and proud. That is evidence of avowed internationalism, not a confession to crimes or secret world government.

### Left critique

**[R/analysis]** Holly Sklar’s 1980 edited volume *Trilateralism* and Stephen Gill’s later academic work analyzed the Commission as an institution of transnational capitalist-class formation and U.S.-centered hegemony. These works emphasize documented membership, corporate funding, and policy consensus rather than supernatural or all-controlling claims.

**[R/analysis]** Chomsky’s critique of *The Crisis of Democracy* argues that its concern was not insufficient democracy but too much participation by previously passive populations. Whatever judgment is made about that interpretation, it proceeds from an authentic Commission report.

### CIA and MKUltra claims

**[D/C]** The following facts are frequently collapsed:

1. David Rockefeller founded the Trilateral Commission.
2. His brother Nelson Rockefeller chaired the distinct 1975 presidential commission investigating CIA domestic abuses.
3. That presidential commission examined CIA drug testing and behavior-control programs.
4. Various former intelligence officials later joined the Trilateral Commission.

None of those facts proves that the Trilateral Commission funded or operated MKUltra. The named institutions and Rockefellers must not be conflated.

**[P]** The FBI has a FOIA file on the Commission, and State/CIA archives contain records of official contacts or discussions involving members. Government possession of documents about an organization is not proof that the organization was an intelligence front.

### Litigation and criminal record

**[P]** The institution itself was not found to have been indicted, convicted, or subjected to a major enforcement action. It has been named in scattered pro se conspiracy suits. In *Kurkowski v. Volcker*, counsel invoked an alleged international banking conspiracy rooted in the Federal Reserve and Trilateral Commission; the Eighth Circuit affirmed Rule 11 sanctions because the underlying repetitive lawsuit was frivolous. **[D as judicial outcome]** [819 F.2d 201](https://openjurist.org/819/f2d/201).

**[P]** A 2025 pro se civil-rights complaint also named the Commission alongside the UN, WTO, International Court of Justice, and embassies. Being named is not an adjudicated finding; the docket supplied no substantiated institutional misconduct. **[A only]**

### Epstein scandal: what is proved and what is not

**Proved [P]:**

- Epstein was on the June 12, 1995 roster.
- He remained publicly described and listed as a member in 2002–08.
- An Epstein-associated ledger records a $50,000 Commission payment in 2006.
- He received a 2008 plenary invitation as a member/participant.
- He disappeared from the located roster by 2010.
- He later claimed Rockefeller invited him.

**Not traced:**

- His original application or nomination.
- The membership committee vote.
- The identity of any formal sponsor.
- The date of admission.
- Whether the $50,000 was dues, a donation, meeting support, or another category.
- Complete attendance records.
- The date and reason his membership ended.
- Any public Commission inquiry into why he remained a member during the Palm Beach investigation.
- Any Commission statement accounting for his selection or removal.

**Unsupported [C]:**

- That Commission membership proves Epstein was a CIA, Mossad, MI6, or other intelligence asset.
- That the Commission participated in trafficking or blackmail.
- That its members collectively knew of his abuse.
- That it influenced the 2007 federal non-prosecution agreement or his 2008 work-release conditions.
- That every person on the same roster knew, met, financed, or was compromised by him.

**Conflict [P/R/S]:** The best minimum membership window is **June 1995 through April 2008**. Epstein’s 2019 memory placed entry in the early 1990s; no earlier roster was located to confirm it. *Le Monde*’s 1998 entry date is contradicted by the 1995 roster. The cessation date is bounded only by his 2008 inclusion and 2010 absence.

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

- **1977:** Washington reporting and Congressional Record insertions made the Carter personnel overlap nationally visible; commentary ranged from measured elite-network analysis to claims of a Rockefeller coup. **[R/P]**
- **1980:** The Commission became a presidential-campaign issue because Carter, George H. W. Bush, and John Anderson had affiliations. Contemporary reporting asked whether membership caused appointment or merely reflected the same elite recruitment pool. **[R]**
- **1980:** Holly Sklar’s *Trilateralism: The Trilateral Commission and Elite Planning for World Management* consolidated the left critique.
- **1981/1995 editions:** Antony Sutton’s *Trilaterals Over America* consolidated the conspiratorial New Right account.
- **1990:** Stephen Gill’s *American Hegemony and the Trilateral Commission* reframed the subject within academic international political economy.
- **1992:** *The Washington Post* surveyed claims that the organization was anti-American, anti-Christian, anti-worker, or planning world government, treating them as conspiracy narratives while recognizing the Commission’s real elite composition. ([Washington Post, April 25, 1992](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1992/04/25/beware-the-trilateral-commission/59c48198-9479-4c80-a70a-a1518b5bcfff/))
- **2002–03:** Profiles in *New York Magazine*, *Vanity Fair*, and the *Harvard Crimson* made Epstein’s membership part of his carefully cultivated establishment image.
- **2016:** Dino Knudsen’s archival history, *The Trilateral Commission and Global Governance*, supplied a less speculative account based on internal records.
- **2019–26:** Epstein reporting, document releases, podcasts, and videos revived the Commission as a network node. The new evidence strengthens the membership-and-money story—especially the 1995 roster and 2006 payment—but still does not prove Commission participation in Epstein’s crimes.
- **2023:** The fiftieth anniversary presented the Commission’s own afterlife narrative: a continuing venue for democratic-market cooperation, now expanded beyond the original industrial triad.
- **2026:** The released Epstein–Bannon recording added Epstein’s own account of recruitment by Rockefeller, while simultaneously revealing chronological unreliability.

## 9. Findings

1. **The video’s specific roster claim is true.** Epstein is unmistakably listed on the June 12, 1995 roster.
2. **The tie was neither nominal nor confined to one stray list.** Contemporary journalism, later rosters, a 2006 $50,000 payment, and a 2008 member invitation establish a sustained relationship.
3. **The admission story remains incomplete.** Epstein said Rockefeller invited him, but his chronology is unreliable and no application, sponsorship record, or membership vote was located.
4. **The 1998 entry date reported by *Le Monde* is contradicted by the 1995 roster.**
5. **Membership did not itself establish criminal or intelligence collaboration.** No located primary record connects the Commission as an institution to Epstein’s abuse, trafficking, non-prosecution agreement, or alleged intelligence role.
6. **The Commission is a real elite policy network with real political effects.** Its funders, membership, private deliberations, reports, and Carter-era personnel overlap are extensively documented.
7. **“Private” does not mean powerless, but “influential” does not mean omnipotent.** Its demonstrated power is convening, credentialing, agenda formation, and network reproduction.
8. **The CIA/MKUltra claim fails at the institutional level.** It commonly confuses David Rockefeller’s Trilateral Commission with Nelson Rockefeller’s separate presidential CIA commission and converts individual membership overlaps into an unsupported agency relationship.
9. **Financial transparency is partial.** Historic funders and aggregate modern accounts are recoverable, but donor-level continuity is obstructed by destroyed routine records, decentralized regional finances, and nondisclosure of modern donor names.
10. **No substantial institutional indictment, conviction, dissolution, human-subject experiment, organized-crime relationship, or intelligence charter was found.** Those absences are findings, not declarations that undisclosed records cannot exist.

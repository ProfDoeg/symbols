# The Long Now Foundation: Research Dossier

## Evidentiary key and scope

- **[P] Primary record:** government filing, tax return, court record, contemporaneous organizational document, technical paper, or released document.
- **[I] Institutional account:** The Long Now Foundation’s own website, archive, announcement, or project publication. Useful for organizational facts and self-description, but not independent verification.
- **[J] Journalism:** reporting by an identified publication or journalist.
- **[A] Academic or archival secondary source.**
- **[L] Litigation/accusation:** an allegation in litigation or by a named accuser, not an adjudicated fact unless stated.
- **[C] Personal claim:** memoir, interview, correspondence, or account by an involved person.
- **[R] Rumor/conspiracy material:** a claim circulated without adequate documentary support.
- **[D] Disproved, corrected, or retracted.**
- **[N] Negative finding:** no supporting record was located in the sources searched. This is not proof that no record exists.

“Every tie” below means every material governance, employment, funding, project, institutional, legal, or controversy tie identifiable in the reviewed records. Thousands of seminar guests, ordinary members, bar customers, newsletter subscribers, and one-time event attendees are not treated as organizational ties unless another relationship is documented. Live webpages were checked on September 28, 2026.

## 1. Identification

The Long Now Foundation is a California nonprofit public-benefit corporation and federally tax-exempt 501(c)(3), EIN 68-0384748, headquartered at Fort Mason Center in San Francisco. The corporation was filed on January 4, 1996, initially described within the group as the “Clock/Library”; the name “The Long Now Foundation” was adopted in 1997. Federal exemption was issued in March 1998. Its IRS classification is educational institutions and related activities/libraries. **[P/I]** [California registry-derived record](https://www.bizprofile.net/ca/san-francisco/the-long-now-foundation); [IRS-derived Form 990 archive](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/680384748); [institutional timeline](https://longnow.org/ideas/long-now-timeline-current-status/).

The foundation defines its purpose as fostering thinking and responsibility on a 10,000-year horizon. Its best-known work comprises the Clock of the Long Now, the Rosetta Project language archive, Long Bets, public talks and podcasts, the Interval, long-term libraries and archives, the Pace Layers framework, and a Nevada bristlecone-pine preserve. **[I]** [Long Now home page](https://longnow.org/); [projects page](https://longnow.org/projects/).

Three different descriptions of its origin need to be kept distinct:

1. Long Now’s current account calls Stewart Brand, William Daniel “Danny” Hillis, and Brian Eno its founders. **[I, current institutional formulation]**
2. Its detailed timeline says Hillis began circulating the clock idea in 1994; Brand proposed an institution and library; Eno supplied “The Clock of the Long Now”; Peter Schwartz suggested expanding the horizon from 1,000 to 10,000 years; and Brand incorporated the nonprofit in 1996. **[I]**
3. In a 1998 interview, Brand named a nine-person “founding board”: Douglas Carlston, Paul Saffo, Peter Schwartz, Stewart Brand, Danny Hillis, Kevin Kelly, Brian Eno, Esther Dyson, and Mitchell Kapor. **[C]** [Brand’s 1998 Edge interview](https://www.edge.org/conversation/stewart_brand-the-clock-of-the-long-now).

Thus, saying simply “Brand and Hillis’s foundation,” as the 2026 Hidden AmuraKa framing reportedly does, is incomplete. Brand and Hillis were central initiators, but Eno was also credited as a founder, and six other people comprised the first board.

The organization remains active. Its live board page lists Stewart Brand, Brian Eno, Mick Costigan, Danica Remy, Joe Speicher, Patrick Collison, David Eagleman, Kevin Kelly, and David Rumsey. Mick Costigan is identified as chair, Remy as secretary, and Speicher as treasurer. Josette Melchor is listed as interim executive director following Rebecca Lendl’s announced September 2026 departure. **[I, live pages retrieved 2026-09-28]** [board](https://longnow.org/people/board/); [staff](https://longnow.org/people/staff/); [Lendl transition announcement, July 14, 2026](https://longnow.org/ideas/how-institutions-learn/).

## 2. The Record

### 2.1 Intellectual and institutional origins, 1994–1998

In 1994, Danny Hillis circulated an email proposing a clock that would tick once a year, chime once a century, and produce a cuckoo once a millennium. Stewart Brand proposed surrounding the clock with an institution and library; Brian Eno proposed the name “Clock of the Long Now.” **[I]** Long Now’s timeline says Peter Schwartz then proposed a 10,000-year scale. Hillis presented the concept publicly in a 1995 *Wired* essay. **[I]** [institutional timeline](https://longnow.org/ideas/long-now-timeline-current-status/).

Brand incorporated a California nonprofit in January 1996. Its first organizational meeting was held in Sausalito in June 1996. It initially used “Clock/Library” language; the name became The Long Now Foundation in 1997. Alexander Rose became the first employee and project manager that year. **[P/I]**

In 1998, the organization co-produced the “Time and Bits” conference with the Getty Information Institute. It began the Rosetta Project, inspired in part by the problem of preserving information across changes in media and language. California and the IRS recognized its nonprofit status, with federal exemption dated March 1998. **[P/I]**

### 2.2 The first clock and Nevada property, 1999–2002

The foundation purchased mountain property in eastern Nevada for a possible full-scale clock. Its later timeline says it owned 180 acres by 1999. A contemporaneous archived account describes an initial 80-acre, approximately $140,000 purchase financed by Mitchell Kapor, Jay Walker’s family, and Bill Joy. The likely explanation is that the holding expanded, but the records reviewed do not provide a complete parcel-by-parcel history. **[I/C]** [timeline](https://longnow.org/ideas/long-now-timeline-current-status/); [archived contemporaneous account](https://archive.longnow.org/www.longnow.org/01999-02005/about/articles/ArtInformationOverlord.html).

The first working clock prototype was completed for New Year’s Eve 1999. It was funded by banker and film producer Jacqui Safra and subsequently loaned to the Science Museum in London. The foundation’s current FAQ says Long Now did not finance this construction from its operating funds. **[I]** [Clock FAQ](https://longnow.org/clock/faq/).

In 1999, Long Now also moved into a Presidio building shared with the Internet Archive. Roger Kennedy and Stanford librarian Michael Keller joined the board. **[I]**

The clock prototype appeared at the TED conference in 2000. Long Now held a Stanford conference on long-lived institutions and launched the All Species Inventory initiative; that project was later spun out and became dormant. **[I]**

Long Bets was developed in this period and publicly launched in 2002, although its surviving “About” page says “started in 2003.” This is a minor date discrepancy between two institutional accounts. Long Now’s timeline and retrospective history use 2002; Long Bets’ own page uses 2003. **[I, conflicting institutional versions]** [Long Bets history](https://longnow.org/ideas/how-warren-buffett-won-his-multi-million-dollar-long-bet/); [Long Bets About](https://longbets.org/about/).

### 2.3 Rosetta, seminars, and expansion, 2003–2008

In 2003, Long Now started its Seminars About Long-term Thinking, later a large archive of talks and podcasts. The Rosetta Project received an approximately $1 million National Science Foundation grant to collect materials covering roughly 2,500 languages. **[I/P]** [Rosetta project history](https://media.longnow.org/files/2/Rosetta_Disk_Story.pdf).

The Rosetta Disk was designed as a three-inch nickel micro-etched archive readable with optical magnification rather than proprietary digital hardware. The project reports nearly 14,000 pages of linguistic material on the current disk design, with a broader collection exceeding 100,000 pages and recordings from more than 2,500 languages. **[I]** [Rosetta About](https://rosettaproject.org/about/); [Rosetta project site](https://rosettaproject.org/).

A Rosetta Disk was placed aboard the European Space Agency’s Rosetta spacecraft in 2004. The mission reached comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko in 2014. David Rumsey joined the foundation’s board in 2004. **[I]**

A mechanical orrery commissioned for Long Now was completed in 2005. Nathan Myhrvold paid for two examples and donated one back to the foundation. **[I]** [Clock FAQ](https://longnow.org/clock/faq/).

Long Now moved to Fort Mason Center in 2006. Membership began in 2007. Kim Polese and Chris Anderson joined the board that year. **[I]**

In 2008, the foundation administered Warren Buffett’s wager with Protégé Partners over whether a low-cost S&P 500 index fund would outperform selected hedge funds over ten years. Each side also made a $20,000 charitable contribution to Long Now. The wager eventually generated more than $2 million for Girls Inc. of Omaha after Buffett’s side won. **[I/P]** [contemporaneous Fortune/Loomis document](https://static.longnow.org/media/djlongnow_media/press/pdf/020080609-Loomis-BuffettsBigBet.pdf); [Long Now retrospective](https://longnow.org/ideas/how-warren-buffett-won-his-multi-million-dollar-long-bet/).

### 2.4 The Texas clock, 2009–present

Jeff Bezos agreed to finance a monumental clock inside a mountain on land he controlled in West Texas. Drilling began in 2009 and construction in 2010. The clock is designed to operate mechanically over 10,000 years, using thermal differences between day and night for power and solar noon for correction. **[I]** [Clock FAQ](https://longnow.org/clock/faq/); [legacy project description](https://legacy.longnow.org/clock/).

The frequently repeated figure that Bezos committed **$42 million** is not disclosed in Long Now’s audited returns or clock FAQ. It was reported by *Wired* and repeated by *The Washington Post* and IEEE Spectrum. IEEE expressly noted that the budget had not been officially disclosed. Long Now itself confirms Bezos’s funding and land but says the final cost is unknown and not public. **[J/I]** [Washington Post, August 5, 2013](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national/washington-post-to-be-sold-to-jeff-bezos/2013/08/05/ca537c9e-fe0c-11e2-9711-3708310f6f4d_story.html); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/engineering-the-10-000year-clock); [Clock FAQ](https://longnow.org/clock/faq/).

A 2024 reported reconstruction says the Texas installation is owned through Clock One LLC and that Bezos’s spending has likely exceeded the original reported commitment. This is journalistic reporting, not a Long Now financial disclosure. **[J]** [Asterisk, “Chimes at Midnight,” 2024](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/08/chimes-at-midnight).

Long Now’s FAQ makes two financially significant statements: the foundation has never used its own funds to construct the large clocks, and it neither owns nor maintains them, except for the smaller display mechanism at the Interval. This means the Texas work should not be represented as a $42 million donation passing through Long Now’s Form 990 accounts. **[I]**

As of the latest reviewed sources, the Texas clock is not publicly documented as complete or open to visitors. Bezos posted installation footage in 2018, but the foundation continued to describe completion as prospective. **[I/J]** [2018 installation report](https://www.businessinsider.de/tech/jeff-bezos-shares-video-10000-year-clock-texas-2018-2/).

### 2.5 New projects and institutional transitions, 2010–2026

- David Eagleman joined the board in 2010. **[I]** [announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/david-eagleman-joins-long-now-board/).
- PanLex, an effort to construct a broad lexical translation database, joined the Rosetta Project in 2012 and later became institutionally distinct. **[I]** [announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/panlex-joins-rosetta-at-long-now/).
- Ping Fu joined the board in 2012. **[I]** [announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/ping-fu-joins-long-now/).
- Revive & Restore began at Long Now in 2012–2013 and later spun out as an independent nonprofit. **[I]**
- The Interval, Long Now’s public bar, café, museum, library, and event venue at Fort Mason, opened in 2014 following a crowdfunding campaign. **[I]** [launch announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-interval-at-long-now-our-new-venue/).
- The Fund of the Long Now was announced in 2015 as a long-horizon investment vehicle intended to support operations; it was not to begin active management until it reached $500,000. **[I]** [fund announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/fundofthelongnow/).
- Katherine Fulton joined the board in 2017. **[I]** [announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/katherine-fulton-joins-long-now-board/).
- Patrick Collison joined in 2019; the announcement also says Stripe had sponsored Long Now talks since 2017. **[I]** [announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/patrick-collison-joins-long-now-board/).
- Alexander Rose joined the board in 2020 after serving as executive director. **[I]** [announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/alexander-rose-joins-long-now-board/).
- Rose stepped down from operational leadership in 2023 after 26 years. **[I]** [institutional tribute](https://longnow.org/ideas/honoring-alexander-rose/).
- Rebecca Lendl, interim executive director from early 2023 and then executive director, announced that she would leave the role in September 2026 and join the Long Now Council. **[I]**
- As of September 28, 2026, Josette Melchor was listed as interim executive director, while Mick Costigan had succeeded Patrick Dowd as board chair. **[I]**

The foundation has not dissolved. Its digital archive remains online; Rosetta materials are also hosted through the Internet Archive. No disposition of institutional papers or an endowment upon dissolution is therefore applicable.

## 5. Ideas and Programs

### 5.1 The Clock of the Long Now

The clock is intended as both a functioning mechanical system and a symbolic intervention: it makes a 10,000-year future perceptible and asks present institutions to consider consequences beyond quarterly, electoral, or human-lifetime cycles. Hillis supplied the engineering premise; Brand developed the institutional and rhetorical program; Eno named it and contributed a chime concept. **[I/C]**

The Texas mechanism uses slow mechanical computation, astronomical correction, thermal power, and a chime generator intended to avoid repeating combinations over long periods. A technical description was published by Hillis and collaborators. **[P]** [technical paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.3004).

Critics argue that a remote, billionaire-financed monumental clock is inaccessible and embodies elite control of “the future.” *Wired* in 2020 called it art “of, by, and for the ultrarich.” That is a critical interpretation, not evidence of fraud or covert function. **[J]** [Wired, 2020](https://www.wired.com/story/the-10000-year-clock-is-a-waste-of-time/).

### 5.2 Rosetta Project

Rosetta seeks to preserve linguistic knowledge against extinction, technological obsolescence, and institutional neglect. Its physical disk uses micro-etched text legible through magnification. Its broader archive contains language documentation, parallel texts, recordings, and descriptive material. **[I]**

The project received NSF and private funding and worked with language archives and academic institutions. No evidence indicates experimentation on human subjects. Collecting or archiving language material can raise questions of community consent, Indigenous ownership, or archival governance, but the reviewed records did not identify a lawsuit, formal ethics finding, or named accuser alleging abuse by Long Now’s Rosetta Project. **[N]**

### 5.3 Long Bets

Long Bets was designed to make predictions accountable by recording claims, reasoning, dates, and eventual outcomes, often with charitable stakes. Jeff Bezos funded web development; Capital Research and Management Company managed the wager pool. **[I]**

Its best-publicized case was the Buffett–Protégé wager. The platform’s epistemic premise—requiring named forecasters to commit publicly—later overlapped thematically with Edge discussions of forecasting, but no evidence shows that Long Bets was an intelligence-agency prediction market or intelligence-funded program. **[N]**

### 5.4 Seminars, talks, and publications

The seminars began in 2003 and became a large archive of public intellectual programming. Speakers range across science, history, technology, ecology, economics, art, and governance. Participation as a speaker or attendee is not equivalent to board membership, funding, or institutional endorsement.

### 5.5 The Interval

The Interval combines public programming with hospitality revenue and physical exhibition. It also functions as a social convening space for the foundation’s network. Its 2014 launch was supported through a “brickstarter” crowdfunding effort and donor recognition program. **[I]** [funding announcement](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-interval-brickstarter-funded-support-the-robot-stretch-goal/).

### 5.6 Pace Layers

Brand’s Pace Layers model describes civilization as interacting strata moving at different speeds—fashion and commerce changing rapidly, governance and infrastructure more slowly, culture and nature slowest. The model is an analytical metaphor, not an experimental scientific law. **[C/I]**

### 5.7 Nevada long science

The Nevada holding evolved from a proposed clock site into a site for bristlecone preservation, ecological monitoring, astronomy, and long-duration science. Long Now worked with university and climate-monitoring institutions. **[I/academic-institutional]** [Nevada long-science account](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-long-now-foundation-and-a-great-basin-mountain-observatory-for-long-science/).

### 5.8 Human-subject research

No Long Now program involving biomedical, psychological, coercive, or intelligence-funded experimentation on human subjects was identified. Rosetta’s collection of linguistic documentation is not evidence of an experimental human-subject program. No Institutional Review Board complaint, federal human-research sanction, MKUltra file, or named subject allegation involving the foundation was found. **[N]**

## 6. Scandal, Controversy and Contest

### 6.1 Nevada groundwater litigation and political contest

Long Now opposed the Southern Nevada Water Authority’s proposal to export groundwater from eastern Nevada, where the foundation owns land and monitors ecological conditions. A 2011 administrative/court record lists Long Now among project protestants seeking rehearing. The *Los Angeles Times* reported in March 2012 that it submitted a statement against the pipeline. **[P/J]** [litigation record](https://greatbasinwater.org/archives/doc/legal_9-2-14_answer_to_petitions_final_with_exhibits.pdf); [Los Angeles Times, March 11, 2012](https://www.latimes.com/world/la-xpm-2012-mar-11-la-na-hometown-spring-valley-20120311-story.html).

Nevada’s water authority denied the applications in 2018, stating that it could not approve them under the governing court directions and statutory standards. Later state material records that the denial was upheld and that SNWA withdrew or deferred the project. **[P]** [Nevada decision announcement](https://dcnr.nv.gov/news/nevada-division-of-water-resources-issues-snwa-water-rights-ruling); [state publication](https://epubs.nsla.nv.gov/statepubs/epubs/784718-2020.pdf).

This was a documented environmental and property-rights contest. It was not a prosecution of Long Now, nor did the reviewed record accuse Long Now of criminal conduct.

### 6.2 Accessibility, billionaire patronage, and “elite futurism”

Critics have argued that the Texas clock’s isolation, billionaire funding, and monumental scale undermine its public-minded rhetoric. *Wired*’s 2020 criticism and subsequent essays frame it as an elite art object rather than practical future stewardship. **[J]** [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/the-10000-year-clock-is-a-waste-of-time/); [Noema, 2022](https://www.noemamag.com/keeping-time-into-the-great-beyond/).

The underlying facts—Bezos’s funding, private Texas land, and limited public access—are documented. Claims that these facts prove a secret political program are interpretations for which no operational record was found.

### 6.3 Global Business Network

**Documented:** GBN donated money and services to Long Now; Brand and Schwartz cofounded GBN; Fulton and Costigan worked within the GBN/Monitor scenario-planning world; Rose described common origins. **[I/C]**

**Not documented:** that GBN was an intelligence front, that it transferred classified information to Long Now, or that Long Now performed intelligence work through GBN. Scenario planning has been used by corporations and governments, but that general history does not establish an agency relationship in this case. **[N]**

### 6.4 Edge, Jeffrey Epstein, and the limits of association

This is the most consequential contested-material issue.

**Documented about Edge:** Jeffrey Epstein-linked foundations were major donors to John Brockman’s Edge Foundation. A *New Republic* investigation based on Edge’s nonprofit filings reported approximately $638,000 from Epstein-linked foundations out of roughly $857,000 in disclosed donations during the examined 2001–2015 period. Edge’s Form 990 archive independently establishes its nonprofit filings, though the interpretation and aggregation are journalistic. **[P/J]** [Edge Form 990 archive](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/133528667); [New Republic investigation copy](https://herakleskonzept.de/material/tl_files/herakleskonzept/pdf/netz/EDGE_Jeffrey_Epstein%E2%80%99s_Intellectual_Enabler.pdf).

**Documented social overlap:** A June 27, 2015 email released in the Epstein records and reproduced by an independent document mirror concerned an Edge “Superforecasting” event. Stewart Brand and Danny Hillis appeared among RSVP names. The email also proposed press coverage that would not mention Epstein. An RSVP list is evidence of intended participation, not proof of attendance, knowledge of Epstein’s crimes, or organizational complicity. **[P, released-document mirror]** [EFTA00853254](https://epstein-data.com/EFTA00853254?nav=search).

An August 2013 Brockman mailing reproduced from the same released corpus included Jeff Bezos, Brian Eno, Jeffrey Epstein, Danny Hillis, Nathan Myhrvold, and numerous other technology and science figures. Being on a mass or group distribution list documents common circulation, not friendship, funding, conspiracy, or criminal knowledge. **[P, released-document mirror]** [EFTA02396208](https://epstein-data.com/EFTA02396208).

**Not located:** any record showing that Epstein:

- donated to The Long Now Foundation;
- served on its board or Council;
- employed its staff on behalf of the foundation;
- funded the Clock, Rosetta, Long Bets, the Interval, or the Nevada property;
- attended a Long Now event in an identified institutional capacity;
- held an account, contract, ownership interest, or advisory role with Long Now.

The reviewed early donor roll, current supporter roll, Form 990 archive, institutional histories, and contested-material searches do not identify him. **[N]**

**Conclusion:** Edge and Long Now occupied overlapping intellectual and technology networks, and Edge itself had a substantial Epstein funding relationship. That does not document an Epstein–Long Now institutional relationship. Claims that Epstein funded or controlled Long Now are presently **unsupported rumor [R]**, unless a further primary record is produced.

### 6.5 CIA and MKUltra

Dedicated searches did not locate a CIA contract, declassified file, grant, officer relationship, or MKUltra subproject involving The Long Now Foundation. **[N]**

A recurrent online narrative links Stewart Brand, the Merry Pranksters, 1960s psychedelic culture, cybernetics, and CIA LSD research, then projects that chain forward onto Long Now. The components have different evidentiary status:

- CIA MKUltra experimentation is historically documented in general.
- Brand’s participation in Bay Area counterculture and his later technology networks is documented.
- Those two facts do not establish that Brand organized the Acid Tests for the CIA or that Long Now continued MKUltra. No supporting primary record was located. **[R/N]**

### 6.6 Ted Kaczynski and the Harvard experiments

Ted Kaczynski underwent psychologically stressful research conducted by Henry A. Murray’s Harvard group between 1959 and 1962. Journalist Alston Chase reported that Kaczynski, coded “Lawful,” was among 22 student subjects and emphasized Murray’s earlier wartime Office of Strategic Services work. Chase speculated about the experiment’s possible effects on Kaczynski. **[J]** [The Atlantic, June 2000](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2000/06/harvard-and-the-making-of-the-unabomber/378239/).

The stronger internet claim that Kaczynski was definitively an “MKUltra subject” is not established by the reviewed record. Kaczynski himself later disputed the popular story that the Harvard experiment transformed his personality or explained his crimes. **[C]** [Kaczynski correspondence archive](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/ted-kaczynski-ted-argues-the-harvard-psych-experiments-had-little-effect-on-his-personality).

No connection between Murray’s study and The Long Now Foundation was found. Long Now was incorporated in 1996, the year of Kaczynski’s arrest, decades after the Harvard study. **[N]**

### 6.7 *Das Netz* and the “cybernetic elite” narrative

Lutz Dammbeck’s 2003 documentary *Das Netz (The Net: The Unabomber, LSD and the Internet)* linked cybernetics, counterculture, computing, John Brockman’s intellectual network, Brand, and Kaczynski’s anti-technology critique. Brockman’s own archive describes Dammbeck’s theory that Kaczynski sought to attack the network Brockman represented and notes bomb victim David Gelernter. This is a documentary-maker’s interpretive thesis, not a judicial or archival finding. **[C/R]** [Brockman archive](https://www.edge.org/jb/online.digital.archive.html).

The 2026 Hidden AmuraKa video appears to extend this framework to Long Now. The exact video or a stable transcript was not available in the indexed sources reviewed, so its individual statements could not be audited word for word. Its known Long Now elements divide as follows:

- Brand, Hillis, Kelly, Eno, Rose, Bezos, GBN, and Edge overlaps: substantially documented.
- The characterization of Long Now as a conspiratorial “hub”: interpretive.
- Epstein institutional funding or control: unsupported.
- CIA/MKUltra continuation: unsupported.
- A Kaczynski–Long Now tie: unsupported.

Hidden AmuraKa describes its own output in esoteric, technocracy, and memetic terms. That makes the video a source for a contemporary conspiracy or counter-history narrative, not primary evidence for the historical claims it makes. **[R]** [publisher’s Patreon description](https://www.patreon.com/cw/HiddenAmuraka).

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

- **Stewart Brand, *The Clock of the Long Now* (1999):** the founding manifesto, presenting long-term responsibility as an institutional and cultural problem. **[C]**
- **Brian Eno, *January 07003* (2003):** music associated with the Long Now clock’s chime concept. **[I/C]**
- **Lutz Dammbeck, *Das Netz* (2003):** placed Brand and the computing/counterculture milieu inside a critical history connecting cybernetics and Kaczynski. Long Now itself was not thereby shown to have a Kaczynski relationship. **[C/R]**
- **Fred Turner, *From Counterculture to Cyberculture* (2006):** influential academic history of how the Whole Earth network helped connect counterculture, systems thinking, and Silicon Valley. It supplies historical context for Long Now’s personnel lineage, not proof of covert coordination. **[A]**
- **Neal Stephenson, *Anathem* (2008):** Long Now’s timeline reports that the clock and long-duration institutional thinking influenced the novel. Stephenson was also an early donor. **[I]**
- **Warren Buffett–Protégé wager (2008–2017):** gave Long Bets its largest mainstream financial story and shifted public reception from speculative futurism toward demonstrable forecasting accountability. **[P/J]**
- **The Interval (2014–present):** made the foundation a physical cultural institution rather than only a clock project or web network. **[I]**
- **The Texas clock media cycle (2011–present):** coverage increasingly centers on Bezos, cost, private access, and the symbolism of billionaire futurism. **[J]**
- **Edge interviews (1998, 2019 and intervening years):** preserved the participants’ own accounts of how the foundation arose from the Whole Earth/GBN network. These interviews also helped later critics map the social network. **[C]**
- **Alexandra Rose transition, 2023:** institutional memory shifted from its 26-year founding executive toward a second generation of leadership. **[I]**
- **Rebecca Lendl and Patrick Dowd transition, 2023–2026:** Long Now publicly framed the change as an effort to make the organization outlast its founders. **[I]**
- **Hidden AmuraKa, “The Unabomber & the Net: Kaczynski, Epstein and the Cybernetic Elite” (2026):** represents the most recent synthesis of older *Das Netz*, Epstein/Edge, Silicon Valley, and cybernetics narratives. Its network overlaps are partly real; its implied institutional bridges exceed the current evidence. **[R/N]**

The public image has consequently moved through four stages: eccentric clock proposal; nonprofit language/archive and forecasting laboratory; prestigious Silicon Valley cultural institution; and, increasingly, an object of criticism about billionaire patronage, elite networks, and longtermist politics.

## 9. Findings in brief

The documentary record supports describing The Long Now Foundation as an institutional outgrowth of the Whole Earth, Global Business Network, early internet, scenario-planning, and Silicon Valley philanthropic milieu. Its founders and directors repeatedly crossed among those networks; GBN donated directly; John Brockman donated; Edge supplied a publishing and convening forum; and technology financiers funded particular projects.

The record does **not** presently support collapsing those ties into a single covert structure. In particular:

- Bezos’s clock funding is real, but $42 million is a reported estimate and the money is not shown as passing through Long Now’s ordinary accounts.
- GBN overlap is extensive and documented.
- Edge overlap is documented.
- Edge’s Epstein funding is documented and material.
- Some Long Now-associated individuals appeared in Edge/Epstein correspondence or event lists.
- No Epstein donation, position, contract, or project relationship with Long Now was located.
- No CIA or MKUltra relationship with Long Now was located.
- No Ted Kaczynski relationship with Long Now was located.
- No criminal prosecution, trafficking allegation, human-subject experiment, intelligence contract, fraud judgment, or offshore vehicle involving the foundation was located.
- The major documented legal-political contest was Long Now’s opposition to Nevada groundwater export.

# Martin A. Nowak: Research Dossier

**Research cutoff:** September 28, 2026.

## Evidentiary key and scope

- **[PR] Primary record:** government or university record, court exhibit, grant record, contemporaneous correspondence, or scholarly publication.
- **[IR] Institutional finding:** conclusion of a formally commissioned review. Harvard’s May 2020 report was an internal institutional investigation—not a court judgment—with access to roughly 250,000 pages and interviews with more than 40 people.
- **[JR] Reported:** reporting by named journalists or established publications.
- **[SA] Subject account:** statement by Martin A. Nowak, not independently established merely because he said it.
- **[AL] Allegation:** assertion by a legislator, litigant, accuser, or investigator that has not been adjudicated.
- **[RU] Rumor/conspiracy:** circulating interpretation unsupported by adequate documentary evidence.
- **[RF] Refuted, retracted, or contradicted:** contradicted by stronger evidence or formally withdrawn.

A name appearing in the Epstein files does not itself establish misconduct. The Justice Department warned in January 2026 that its approximately 3.5-million-page release was over-collected, contained duplicates, and could include false public submissions. Accordingly, this dossier distinguishes an original email or itinerary from interpretations imposed on it. [PR; DOJ release, January 30, 2026.](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-publishes-35-million-responsive-pages-compliance-epstein-files)

## 1. Identification

Martin Andreas Nowak is an Austrian-born mathematical biologist, born in Vienna in 1965, whose work applies evolutionary game theory and mathematical modeling to cooperation, infectious disease, cancer, language and the origin of life. He studied biochemistry and mathematics at the University of Vienna, worked at Oxford from 1989 to 1998, led the Institute for Advanced Study’s theoretical-biology initiative in Princeton from 1998 to 2003, and joined Harvard in 2003 as professor of mathematics and biology. He directed Harvard’s Program for Evolutionary Dynamics, or PED, from 2003 until Harvard closed it in connection with disciplinary action announced in March 2021. [PR/SA; Harvard biography and 2023 CV.](https://sites.harvard.edu/martin-nowak/) [PR; Harvard Magazine, March 25, 2021.](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2021/03/martin-nowak-sanctioned-for-jeffrey-epstein-involvement)

The central scandal is documented. Jeffrey Epstein supported Nowak’s work before Harvard, then gave Harvard $6.5 million in 2003 to establish PED and finance separate premises at One Brattle Square. After Epstein’s 2008 conviction, Nowak continued to associate with him. Epstein visited PED more than 40 times from 2010 through 2018, had an office, telephone, keys and access cards there, and frequently arrived with young women described as assistants. Epstein also introduced donors who subsequently gave $7.5 million to Nowak’s program. Harvard’s May 2020 review found professional-conduct and campus-access failures; Harvard imposed sanctions in March 2021, lifted them in March 2023, and placed Nowak on paid leave again in February 2026 after the release of additional Epstein documents. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.](https://ogc.harvard.edu/file_url/208) [JR; Harvard Crimson, May 22, 2023.](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2023/5/22/nowak-sanctions-lifted/) [JR; Harvard Crimson, February 25, 2026.](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2026/2/25/nowak-leave-epstein/)

No reviewed court record accuses Nowak of participating in Epstein’s sexual abuse or trafficking. He has not been charged with an Epstein-related crime. The reopened Harvard inquiry is administrative, and no final public determination from it was located by the research cutoff.

## 2. The Record

### Origins, education and early research

Nowak attended Vienna’s Albertus Magnus School. At the University of Vienna he completed studies in biochemistry and mathematics and worked in Peter Schuster’s theoretical-chemistry group and Karl Sigmund’s mathematics group. His 1989 doctorate was completed *sub auspiciis Praesidentis*, an Austrian distinction for sustained academic excellence. In 1988 he also worked with Manfred Eigen at the Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry in Göttingen. He habilitated at Vienna in 1993. [PR/SA; 2023 curriculum vitae.](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.harvard.edu/dist/c/619/files/2024/02/Nowak-CV-2023-b3229c1b7ca44bae.pdf)

An Erwin Schrödinger fellowship took him to Oxford in 1989 to work with Robert May. He was associated with Wolfson College and later Keble College, became a Wellcome Trust senior research fellow in 1992, headed Oxford’s mathematical-biology group from 1995 to 1998, and was appointed professor of mathematical biology in 1997. [PR/SA; CV, 2023.]

His early work with Karl Sigmund treated reciprocity and iterated games as evolutionary processes. His Oxford-era research with Robert May helped establish mathematical models of HIV and other infections. *Virus Dynamics*, written with May and published in 2000, synthesized the use of dynamical systems in viral evolution and treatment.

### Institute for Advanced Study and Princeton

In 1998 Nowak was recruited to the Institute for Advanced Study to lead its first program in theoretical biology. The five-year initiative was established under IAS director Phillip A. Griffiths and supported by trustee Leon Levy. Nowak also held associated-faculty relationships with Princeton programs in ecology and evolutionary biology and applied mathematics. [PR; IAS profile.](https://www.ias.edu/scholars/martin-nowak)

IAS reports document collaborations or exchanges with David Krakauer, Linda Wahl, Dominik Wodarz, Yoh Iwasa, Natalia Komarova, Alun Lloyd, Steven Frank, Franziska Michor, Garrett Mitchener and Joshua Plotkin, and experimental contacts with Charles Bangham, Jeff Lifson, Andrew McMichael, Alan Thomsen, Bruce Walker and Rolf Zinkernagel. His cancer-modeling work involved Bert Vogelstein and Christoph Lengauer. These are scholarly ties, not evidence that those scientists participated in his later relationship with Epstein.

Epstein began financing Nowak during this period. In May 2003 Nowak told the *Harvard Crimson* that Epstein had supplied about $500,000 since they met around 2000. A 2003 profile reported weekly discussions, travel to Epstein’s properties and impromptu lectures. Because those descriptions came from Nowak and Epstein-friendly contemporaneous reporting, they establish that the association was openly acknowledged, but not every self-aggrandizing detail in the articles. [SA/JR; *Harvard Crimson*, May 1, 2003.](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2003/5/1/mogul-donor-gives-harvard-more-than/) [JR; *Vanity Fair*, March 2003, reproduced as a federal-court exhibit.](https://www.justice.gov/multimedia/Court%20Records/Government%20of%20the%20United%20States%20Virgin%20Islands%20v.%20JPMorgan%20Chase%20Bank%2C%20N.A.%2C%20No.%20122-cv-10904%20%28S.D.N.Y.%202022%29/238-24.pdf)

### Recruitment to Harvard and the 2003 gift

Harvard began recruiting Nowak in 2002 as part of then-president Lawrence H. Summers’s effort to expand interdisciplinary science. Benedict H. Gross participated in the recruitment; Nowak met Gross in spring 2002 and received a tenure offer that August. [JR; *Harvard Crimson*, October 1, 2002.](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2002/10/1/university-lures-specialist-in-interdisciplinary-science/)

Harvard’s subsequent investigation found that Epstein had already supported Nowak at IAS and that his $6.5 million gift in 2003 established PED under Nowak. The money paid for research and a separate facility at One Brattle Square, leased through the Harvard Kennedy School. The evidence therefore supports the video’s core claim that Epstein’s $6.5 million gift funded PED. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

Early public accounts said Epstein had pledged $25 million, $30 million or even $35 million. Harvard’s financial records show no such pledge and only $6.5 million for PED. The often-repeated $30-million figure is therefore contradicted by the institutional ledger and review. A PED webpage later repeated a $35-million figure; Harvard investigators found that claim false. [RF/IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

The review found that Epstein gave Harvard $9,179,000 through 22 gifts between 1998 and 2007. Of this, $8,443,000 arrived before his July 2006 arrest and $736,000 between the arrest and his 2008 conviction. The $6.5-million PED gift was the largest component, not an additional sum on top of a $30-million commitment.

### PED’s work and institutional structure

PED was a theoretical and mathematical-biology program. Its published work addressed evolutionary games, graph theory, cancer progression, viral dynamics, language, cooperation and prebiotic evolution. Calling it a “genetics lab” is imprecise: it modeled evolutionary and genetic processes, but the program’s defining activity was mathematical and computational research.

The separate Brattle Square site produced a governance problem. It was Harvard space, but its location and relatively autonomous administration allowed Epstein to use it as a meeting venue after Harvard’s senior leadership had stopped accepting his money.

### Epstein after arrest and conviction

In 2006 Harvard decided to retain Epstein’s already-spent or committed gift. Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida in 2008 to state prostitution offenses, including solicitation involving a minor, served approximately 13 months under a controversial work-release arrangement, and registered as a sex offender.

By November 2008 Harvard president Drew Gilpin Faust had determined that Harvard should no longer accept gifts from him. The 2020 review found that Nowak understood in early 2008 that Faust had misgivings, although he described the situation as “unclear.” Emails from 2010 through 2014 show that he and others sought either clarification or reconsideration. In 2010 he wrote that he was trying to find a way for Epstein to give PED money; in December 2014 he asked whether Epstein had become an “acceptable donor.” The investigators found no evidence Faust had suggested that her position might change. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

In 2013 Benedict Gross and Jeremy Bloxham argued internally that the benefit to Nowak’s research outweighed the reputational risk. Dean Michael D. Smith rejected renewed direct funding from Epstein. This is evidence of an internal Harvard dispute, not of a secret government or intelligence intervention.

### The office, cards and visits

Harvard’s review established that Nowak permitted PED to provide Epstein:

- A dedicated office, Room 610, commonly called “Jeffrey’s Office.”
- A telephone line.
- A physical key.
- Proximity-card access.
- Later, generic visitor-access cards obtained by PED’s chief administrative officer despite tighter identification rules.

The office contained an Epstein-branded rug and photographs. In 2017 the administrator mailed two generic access cards to Epstein’s assistant and informed Nowak; investigators found no response from him. They nevertheless assigned ultimate responsibility to Nowak as PED director and emphasized that he knew Epstein was a registered sex offender. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

The available schedules establish at least the following likely PED visits: four in 2010, five in 2011, two in 2012, three in 2013, four in 2014, eight in 2015, nine in 2016, five in 2017 and six in 2018. The report cautioned that its records might be incomplete, making “more than 40” the defensible formulation.

Epstein commonly visited on weekends and supplied or influenced guest lists. Young women in their twenties routinely accompanied him as purported assistants. Harvard found no evidence that he interacted with Harvard students except that he sat in on part of one undergraduate class without participating. The visits ended after PED researchers complained. At least twelve Harvard faculty members knew he was using PED, so the arrangement was not literally secret inside the academic circle. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

### Templeton funding and the false matching-funds description

Nowak had a longstanding relationship with the John Templeton Foundation, had received two significant earlier grants and served on an advisory board. In 2014 he sought another $3 million. Templeton offered half if matching support could be documented.

Although PED had Black-derived funds available, Nowak sought a letter from Epstein. In 2015 Epstein’s Enhanced Education foundation supplied a letter falsely saying that it had established funding in 2003 that would be applied to the match. Enhanced Education had never funded PED, and Epstein’s original $6.5 million had already been spent. In 2018 PED’s administrator again falsely told a researcher that Enhanced Education funds had been used. Harvard concluded that Nowak and the administrator supplied false information concerning the source of the match; it could not determine whether Templeton knew the precise provenance mattered. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

Templeton’s public grant database separately identifies a $1,499,672 Harvard project led by Nowak on morality and evolutionary game theory and a later $2,091,121 project led by Nowak and Benjamin Allen on time in evolution. [PR; Templeton grant records.](https://www.templeton.org/ar/grant/why-we-care-about-more-than-just-the-consequence-of-peoples-actions-insights-from-evolutionary-game-theory-on-virtue-character-morality-and-norms-2) [PR; Templeton grant record.](https://www.templeton.org/grant/understanding-time-in-evolution)

### Epstein’s webpage and reputation management

PED’s early website credited Epstein and Benedict Gross with the program’s creation. In 2013 an Epstein publicist sought Harvard-domain links to Epstein-controlled foundations because links from Harvard.edu would improve search-engine placement. Nowak approved them.

A later “Friends” page presented Epstein as PED’s sole “Friend,” with a photograph, biography and favorable article. It repeated the false $35-million figure. Survivor advocates complained in October 2014, after which the page was removed. The favorable *National Review* article linked there was later withdrawn or retracted. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

This was not simply passive acceptance of a historic gift. The documentary record shows that Nowak allowed a Harvard webpage and facilities to confer status on Epstein after his conviction.

### Joscha Bach

Epstein introduced artificial-intelligence researcher Joscha Bach to Nowak in 2014. Bach was at the MIT Media Lab from 2014 to 2016 and was supported by Epstein there in 2014–2015. PED listed Bach as a research scientist from 2014 through 2019, gave him office access without a normal visitor agreement, and hosted meetings between him and Epstein.

Harvard found that it neither paid Bach nor received Epstein funds for him, and could not determine how Bach was supported after MIT. Two later papers acknowledged Epstein and PED. This is a documented bridge between Epstein’s MIT and Harvard academic networks, but not proof of intelligence work. [IR; Harvard review, May 2020.]

### 2020 investigation and 2021 sanctions

After Epstein’s July 2019 federal arrest, Harvard commissioned the review led by general counsel Diane E. Lopez, with Ara B. Gershengorn and Martin F. Murphy of Foley Hoag. The report was released May 1, 2020. President Lawrence S. Bacow said Harvard would redirect $200,937 in unspent Epstein-derived funds to My Life My Choice and Girls Educational and Mentoring Services. [PR; Harvard president’s statement, May 1, 2020.](https://www.harvard.edu/president/news-and-statements-by-president-bacow/2020/report-regarding-jeffrey-epstein-s-connections-to-harvard/)

Nowak was placed on paid leave while FAS reviewed personnel consequences. On March 25, 2021, Dean Claudine Gay announced findings that he violated Harvard’s professional-conduct, campus-access and endorsement rules. PED was closed. For two years:

- He could not serve as principal investigator on new grants.
- His role on existing grants was restricted.
- He could not recruit new postdoctoral researchers.
- Existing graduate advisees required co-advisers, and he could not take new graduate students.
- He could teach but could not supervise undergraduate research or theses.

He retained his appointments in mathematics and organismic and evolutionary biology. Thus the video’s formulation that “Harvard’s 2020 review sanctioned him” is broadly but chronologically imprecise: the report and initial leave were in May 2020; the formal sanctions and PED closure were announced in March 2021. [PR/JR; Harvard Magazine, March 25, 2021.]

Lawrence Lessig publicly called Nowak a scapegoat and argued that Harvard leaders, particularly Lawrence Summers, had made the Epstein relationship part of Nowak’s institutional role. That is Lessig’s interpretation, not an official finding. A Summers representative said established procedures had been followed and that Summers’s involvement belonged to the pre-arrest period.

### Sanctions lifted, then a new investigation

Harvard lifted the restrictions in March 2023. Department chairs Scott V. Edwards and Michael J. Hopkins, acting at Dean of Science Christopher W. Stubbs’s direction, told colleagues to welcome Nowak back. Harvard did not publish a substantive reconsideration of the 2021 findings. [JR; *Harvard Crimson*, May 22, 2023.]

In January 2026 the Justice Department released millions of additional records. On February 25 Harvard confirmed that Nowak had again been placed on paid administrative leave pending a formal FAS inquiry. Dean Hopi Hoekstra said new material had prompted the review; the faculty conduct committee recommended a panel investigation. Harvard’s current mathematics and biology profiles marked him “On Leave.” [JR; Harvard Magazine, February 27, 2026.](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/university-news/martin-nowak-harvard-administrative-leave-epstein) [PR; current Harvard Mathematics profile.](https://www.math.harvard.edu/people/nowak-martin/)

The *Harvard Crimson* reported at least 150 post-conviction communications and an in-person meeting as late as May 2019, as well as travel to Epstein’s island and New Mexico ranch. The volume indicates a sustained personal and professional relationship, not merely a historic donor contact. It does not, standing alone, establish participation in Epstein’s crimes. [JR; *Harvard Crimson*, February 25, 2026.]

### Congressional scrutiny in 2026

On June 17, 2026, Representative Jamie Raskin, ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee, wrote Harvard president Alan M. Garber demanding records concerning Harvard’s handling of Epstein-linked faculty. The letter characterized several Nowak communications as potentially “sinister” and said:

- Epstein appeared to channel $15,000 through Nowak and a Romanian graduate student for two Romanian women.
- Epstein’s staff booked Nowak and that student on a 2009 Boston–Palm Beach trip and arranged a Ritz-Carlton room.
- Nowak visited Epstein’s New Mexico ranch and island, the latter with a female PhD candidate.
- A 2014 email said “our spy was captured after completing her mission”; Epstein answered, “Did you torture her.”
- Nowak sent Epstein a photograph labeled “my Korean friend.”

These are primary email or travel references embedded in a congressional demand, but Raskin’s interpretation is an **allegation**, not an adjudicated finding. The letter asked Harvard to report by July 1 and complained that its earlier investigation might not have obtained the newly released documents. [PR/AL; Raskin letter, June 17, 2026.](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2026-06-17-raskin-to-garber-harvard-re-epstein.pdf)

No final public report from Harvard’s reopened inquiry was located as of September 28, 2026.

### Austrian Academy resignation

The Austrian Academy of Sciences opened an ethics review after the 2026 files appeared. Nowak resigned his membership on March 26, shortly before its ethics committee expected to complete its inquiry. The Academy said the resignation preempted the planned hearing, recommendation and possible exclusion procedure. It announced no substantive finding. [PR; Austrian Academy, March 26, 2026.](https://www.oeaw.ac.at/en/news/martin-nowak-resigns-from-austrian-academy-of-sciences-membership)

### Personal, legal and archival record

Public biographies establish that Nowak is Austrian-born and professionally based in the United States. A 2003 profile mentioned a spouse and child but the reliable sources reviewed did not establish their identities; they are therefore not named here.

No Epstein-related indictment, civil complaint against Nowak, criminal prosecution, SEC action or sworn accuser allegation of sexual abuse by him was located. Court and nonprofit searches produced numerous unrelated people with the same name; those records should not be attributed to the Harvard biologist.

Nowak is alive. There is consequently no personal estate or deposited archival collection to account for. His academic record remains distributed among Harvard, IAS, Oxford-era institutional records, publications, and the correspondence incorporated into Harvard’s and DOJ’s Epstein collections.

## 5. Ideas and Programs

### Evolutionary dynamics

Nowak’s central method treats mutation, selection, reproduction and strategic interaction as dynamical systems. This allowed him to work across viruses, cancer, language and social cooperation without claiming that all such systems were biologically identical.

His 2004 review with Karl Sigmund described evolutionary game theory as a mathematical and computational approach to biological interactions. His work helped move game theory beyond economics into population biology.

### Cooperation

The 2006 *Science* article “Five Rules for the Evolution of Cooperation” identified:

1. Kin selection.
2. Direct reciprocity.
3. Indirect reciprocity.
4. Network reciprocity.
5. Group selection.

[SC; *Science*, December 8, 2006.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3279745/)

The 2006 *Nature* paper with Hisashi Ohtsuki, Christoph Hauert and Erez Lieberman derived a simple graph-theoretic condition—often rendered as benefit-to-cost ratio exceeding average degree—for cooperation to be favored on certain networks. [SC; *Nature*, May 2006.](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04605)

*SuperCooperators*, written with Roger Highfield and first published in 2011, popularized the claim that cooperation is a major constructive force in evolution alongside mutation and selection. It mixed mathematical biology, intellectual autobiography and broader social applications. [PR; publisher record.](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/SuperCooperators/Martin-Nowak/9781451626636)

### Inclusive-fitness dispute

In 2010 Nowak, Corina Tarnita and Edward O. Wilson argued that inclusive-fitness theory was unnecessary or inadequate as a general account of eusocial evolution. The article prompted a response signed by 137 researchers defending inclusive fitness. Nowak and colleagues rejected the criticism.

This was a genuine scientific controversy over modeling and explanatory scope. No evidence located indicates that the paper was retracted or found fraudulent. It should not be merged with the Epstein misconduct issue.

### Cancer, viruses and language

Nowak’s collaborations used evolutionary dynamics to model:

- HIV infection and treatment resistance.
- Somatic evolution and cancer progression.
- The emergence of genetic instability.
- Evolution of grammar and language-learning systems.
- Prebiotic replication and the transition to biological evolution.

These are mathematical models and collaborations with experimental groups. No documented research program sought to engineer human populations for political or intelligence purposes.

### Human-subject experiments

A 2012 paper, “Civic Capital in Two Cultures,” involved approximately 200 Boston participants and 208 participants in Iași, Romania, performing computerized economic games. Boston participants received a $15 show-up fee plus experimental earnings; Iași participants received 10 Romanian lei plus earnings. The paper thanks local organizers and reports Templeton support for Nowak and David Rand. [PR/SC; working paper, 2012.](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp4042.pdf)

The accessible paper does not state an institutional-review-board number or use the word “consent.” That omission does not prove that no review or consent occurred. No complaint by a participant, regulatory finding of nonconsensual experimentation, or connection between these subjects and Epstein’s abuse was located.

### Cybernetics, AI and “the Net”

Nowak’s use of dynamical systems, information, games and networks places his work near the broad intellectual territory sometimes grouped with cybernetics. But he did not found a historical cybernetics program, and the reviewed sources do not connect him to canonical cybernetics institutions, ARPA’s early networking programs or Kaczynski.

Joscha Bach provides a documented AI-related connection, mediated by Epstein. It does not establish that PED was an intelligence or surveillance program.

### Matters documented but contextually unresolved

#### The “spy” email

The exchange exists. Nowak wrote that “our spy was captured after completing her mission”; Epstein replied, “did you torture her.” Representative Raskin called the exchange ominous.

Nowak says his sentence was a remembered line from *Civilization II*, used privately to mean a successful result, and that Epstein’s response made no sense to him. He says there was no spy, capture, mission or torture. [SA; Nowak’s public explanation, 2026.](https://www.martinnowak.com/confusion)

The surrounding record available here does not independently prove either a literal or coded criminal meaning. Online statements that the email “proves torture” are therefore unsupported. The appropriate status is **authentic exchange; meaning disputed; no identified victim or corroborating criminal event**.

#### Romanian payments and travel

The $15,000 in transfers and related travel arrangements are documentary matters. The proposition that the payments were scholarships or assistance is reported but incompletely documented; the proposition that they were recruitment payments for abuse or experimental subjects is likewise unproved.

#### “Epstein gave PED $30 million” or “$35 million”

**Status: contradicted.** Harvard found $6.5 million and no record of the larger pledge. The inflated numbers originated in 2003 publicity and Epstein-promoting web material.

**Status: unsupported.** Epstein facilitated the gifts, but Harvard found no evidence that he supplied the funds. The donors denied it.

**Status: unsupported.** A trust named a misspelled version of his name, but no payment was traced.

**Status: unsupported.** Epstein expressed eugenic fantasies to scientists and journalists, but no record shows PED implementing them. Mathematical work on genetics, cancer or cooperation is not evidence of a human-breeding program.

**Status: unsupported.** The Romanian economic-game study is real, and Epstein-linked payments to Romanian women are real, but no primary record connects the two populations or documents nonconsensual experimentation.

**Status: no evidence located.** Searches combining Nowak’s full name with CIA, MKUltra, intelligence, Mossad, surveillance and related terms returned conspiracy compilations, name collisions and material about allegations concerning Epstein—not a primary record tying Nowak to an intelligence agency. No employment, contract, clearance, handler, declassified file or congressional testimony establishing such a tie was found.

**Status: no evidence located.** No correspondence, common program, intermediary, citation trail or meeting links Nowak to Theodore Kaczynski. The connection in *The Unabomber & the Net* is a narrative juxtaposition involving technology, networks and elite science.

**Status: false.** The report deferred personnel decisions but documented serious failures; the later FAS process found rule violations and imposed sanctions.

**Status: false.** Harvard found access, fundraising, misrepresentation and professional-conduct failures. It did not find that Nowak committed trafficking or sexual abuse.

These facts support criticism of institutional failure. They do not, without further evidence, prove a criminal cover-up, prosecutorial protection or intelligence-directed immunity.

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

### 2003: patron-scientist success story

The earliest profiles presented Epstein as an unusually intelligent benefactor and Nowak as the brilliant scientist liberated by private patronage. Nowak said Epstein changed his life and enabled intellectual freedom. The $25–30-million figures and Harvard prestige helped construct Epstein’s public identity as a scientific philanthropist.

### 2006–2008: criminal case but retained prestige

After Epstein’s arrest, the *Boston Globe* reported that Harvard would keep the $6.5-million gift because it supported valuable research. Epstein’s conviction changed Harvard’s formal donation policy, but not his access to PED.

### 2011: *SuperCooperators*

The book made Nowak a public intellectual beyond mathematical biology. Its language of cooperation, altruism and moral progress later created a striking contrast with the institutional failures surrounding Epstein, though that contrast is rhetorical rather than evidence concerning the science.

### 2014: survivor criticism and webpage removal

Public complaints forced removal of PED’s Epstein “Friends” page. This was an early visible challenge to the reputational service Harvard branding provided him.

### 2019–2021: investigative reconstruction and sanctions

After Epstein’s 2019 arrest, reporting by the *Boston Globe*, *New York Times*, *Harvard Crimson* and others reconstructed his Harvard access. Harvard’s 2020 report converted much of that reporting into an institutionally documented chronology. Sanctions followed in 2021.

### 2023: reintegration

The lifting of sanctions shifted the institutional posture toward closure, although Harvard provided few details about rehabilitation, monitoring or why two years was considered sufficient.

### 2026: renewed documentary exposure

The DOJ release broadened the visible record from donations and office access to extensive correspondence, travel and personal messages. Harvard reopened its inquiry; Congress demanded documents; and Nowak resigned from the Austrian Academy before its ethics review finished.

Nowak’s current public account is that he was urged to cultivate Epstein, was impressed by wealth and elite connections, believed Epstein had served his sentence, did not know the full scale of his crimes until 2019, and now deeply regrets the association. [SA/JR; *Concord Bridge*, February 26, 2026.](https://concordbridge.org/index.php/2026/02/26/epstein-was-invited-to-suspended-profs-concord-home-documents-show/)

That account explains how Nowak now frames his conduct. It does not erase that he knew Epstein was a convicted sex offender, gave him continuing Harvard access and repeatedly pursued Epstein-mediated support.

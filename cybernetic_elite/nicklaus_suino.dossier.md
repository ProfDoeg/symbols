## 1. Identification

Nicklaus “Nick” Suino is a living, U.S.-born attorney, martial-arts instructor, author, publisher and small-business operator, born September 17, 1960, in Ann Arbor, Michigan, according to his Martindale/Lawyers.com directory record. That directory lists a University of Michigan B.A. in 1985, an M.F.A. in 1988 and admission to the Michigan bar in 1998. It calls his law credential an “LL.D.”; alumni and autobiographical materials describe a J.D./law degree from Thomas M. Cooley Law School, making “LL.D.” likely a directory error rather than a separate doctorate. **[Reported directory; credential conflict explicitly unresolved.]** [Lawyers.com, current profile](https://www.lawyers.com/ann-arbor/michigan/nicklaus-suino-718151-a/)

His historical importance here is narrow but well documented: while employed by University of Michigan psychologist James V. McConnell, Suino opened a parcel addressed to McConnell at McConnell’s Scio Township home on November 15, 1985. The concealed bomb exploded, injuring Suino. Theodore John Kaczynski later admitted in federal court that he had sent it. Suino was the unintended opener, not the named addressee, and no source found says Kaczynski knew him beforehand or selected him personally. **[P—primary court record; J—contemporaneous reporting.]** [January 22, 1998 plea transcript](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/jury-trial-day-11); [Michigan Daily, November 18, 1985](https://teannuals.club/nov1885/)

His name appears as **Nicklaus Suino**, **Nick Suino**, and, in newspaper OCR or reporting, **Nicklas**, **Nickas** and occasionally **Niklaus Suino**. The shortened and misspelled forms are not documented aliases.

### Employment by James V. McConnell

An August 1996 *Ann Arbor News* report said Suino had worked for McConnell for eight years by the time of the 1985 bombing and that one duty was opening mail for McConnell’s publishing business. If accurately reported, employment began around 1977, but no payroll, appointment letter or university personnel file was located online. Contemporary accounts alternately called him McConnell’s “assistant,” “research assistant,” “employee,” “graduate assistant,” and—in one police-description quotation—“servant.” **[J; exact formal job title not established by a personnel record.]** [Ann Arbor News collection](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/ann-arbor-news-stories-on-ted-kaczynski)

Suino himself recently called McConnell his “old mentor,” supporting a relationship broader than routine mail handling, but this remains the subject’s characterization. **[S.]** [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nicklaussuino)

No source located shows that Suino designed, conducted, funded or served as a human subject in McConnell’s behavior-modification or memory-transfer research. McConnell’s famous experiments were principally planarian-learning and animal-memory research. The public record establishes employment and mentorship, not scientific co-authorship.

### November 15, 1985 bombing

#### Preparation and delivery

The federal plea colloquy records the government’s evidence as follows:

- Kaczynski mailed the package on November 12, 1985.
- It was addressed to James V. McConnell and purported to come from “Ralph Kloppenburg,” a fictitious University of Utah history doctoral student.
- The accompanying letter asked McConnell to review a manuscript about the history of twentieth-century behavioral science.
- What appeared to be a ream of paper had been hollowed out to conceal the bomb.
- An end plug bore “FC.”
- The mailing label, envelope and accompanying letter were typed on the typewriter later recovered from Kaczynski’s cabin; investigators also recovered a carbon copy of the Kloppenburg letter and Kaczynski’s construction notes.
- Kaczynski’s coded journal called McConnell a University of Michigan “behavior modification researcher,” recorded that only the assistant suffered minor injury, and judged the device “A total failure” because it deflagrated rather than detonated.

Kaczynski agreed under oath that this factual account was correct. **[P—federal plea transcript, January 22, 1998.]** [Plea transcript](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/jury-trial-day-11)

A federally funded National Institute of Justice study independently records the Salt Lake City mailing, the fictitious sender, the lead fishing sinkers used as shrapnel and the “FC” marking. Its OCR/text incorrectly calls Suino “Kloppenberg’s assistant”; the court transcript and every contemporary report establish that he was McConnell’s assistant. **[P with D—isolated textual error.]** [NIJ/OJP study, 2006](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/grants/214217.pdf)

#### Explosion

The bomb exploded at approximately 2:51 p.m. at McConnell’s home. The contemporary *Michigan Daily* said it exploded while Suino was opening the package “in his arms”; later *Washington Post* reporting said he was wrestling with it on a kitchen counter, where the blast left a six-inch hole. These descriptions are compatible but not identical, and the precise position of the package cannot be fixed more closely from the available records. **[J—November 18, 1985; J—April 14, 1996.]** [Michigan Daily](https://teannuals.club/nov1885/); [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1996/04/14/to-unabomb-victims-a-deeper-mystery/a3147391-bed8-4ebc-a94c-819b255eaf7d/)

Dimensions also vary:

- Contemporary *Ann Arbor News* sources described an approximately 8-by-10-by-2-inch, unusually heavy, book-like package.
- The 1996 civil-suit report gave 10 by 12 by 3 inches.
- The FBI’s later summary describes a three-ring binder.

The variations likely reflect different witnesses or approximations; none changes the identity of the incident.

#### Injuries: versions by date

- **November 18, 1985:** *Michigan Daily* reported flesh wounds to the arm and superficial abdominal cuts; Suino was treated at University Hospital and released the following day. **[J—contemporaneous.]**
- **April 14, 1996:** *Washington Post* reporters George Lardner Jr. and Lorraine Adams described shrapnel wounds and powder burns on the arms and legs. **[J—later reconstruction.]**
- **August 1996 complaint reporting:** The civil complaint alleged temporary severe hearing loss, blurred vision, burns, bruises and lacerations to the left arm and abdomen, permanent scars, and emotional distress. These were allegations in Suino’s pleading at that point. **[L.]**
- **May 4, 1998:** In his federal victim-impact statement, Suino said his left arm and abdomen were cut, bruised and burned; his hearing was almost entirely lost on the day and remained abnormal for roughly three months; hospital staff flushed gunpowder and paper particles from his eyes; package-opening fear persisted, while sensitivity to loud noises and camera flashes faded over several years. He said the physical wounds had healed and characterized himself as comparatively fortunate. **[P—statement in open court; claimed by subject under courtroom conditions.]** [Sentencing transcript](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/catherine-e-f-bodene-u-s-v-kaczynski-sentencing)

Accounts also differ about McConnell. Initial reports said he was uninjured or merely “fine”; later accounts report slight or temporary hearing loss. This is a source variation, not evidence that the attack itself is doubtful.

#### Investigation and attribution

The immediate investigation involved the Washtenaw County Sheriff’s Department, Detroit ATF office and U.S. Postal Inspection Service. The FBI subsequently incorporated it into UNABOM. **[J—November 1985; P—FBI case history.]** [Michigan Daily](https://teannuals.club/nov1885/); [FBI case history](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/unabomber)

A sealed 1990 Michigan “John Doe” indictment charged the mailing of the McConnell bomb. It was unsealed in June 1996 after Kaczynski’s arrest and identified “FC” and “Ralph C. Kloppenburg” among the alleged sender’s names. At that stage this was an accusation, not a conviction. **[P as to indictment; J as to unsealing.]** [Los Angeles Times, June 29, 1996](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-06-29-mn-19688-story.html)

On January 22, 1998, Kaczynski pleaded guilty to charged California and New Jersey offenses and, as part of the same federal colloquy, expressly accepted the government’s factual account of all sixteen UNABOM devices, including the McConnell/Suino bomb. The Michigan bombing was part of the allocution, although not one of the California/New Jersey counts on which judgment was entered. **[P.]** [Plea transcript](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/jury-trial-day-11); [federal docket](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/united-states-v-kaczynski-2-96-cr-00259)

This establishes Kaczynski’s responsibility far beyond the earlier “suspect” status. Assertions that the sender remains unidentified are contradicted by his judicial admission and the physical and documentary evidence recited in court. **[D when circulated as continuing uncertainty.]**

#### Was Suino targeted?

No. The parcel was addressed to McConnell, and every primary account identifies Suino as the person who opened it. No document found indicates Kaczynski knew Suino, researched him, corresponded with him or meant to injure him specifically.

Why Kaczynski chose McConnell remains partly interpretive:

- Kaczynski’s own coded notes described McConnell as a behavior-modification researcher. **[P.]**
- The FBI’s retrospective history says UNABOM victims were selected through library research and effectively at random within the types Kaczynski targeted. **[P—agency account.]**
- Psychologist Edward K. Morris argued in 2023 that McConnell’s public behavior-modification advocacy, Kaczynski’s anti-technological politics and Kaczynski’s Harvard experience formed a “perfect storm.” That is an academic interpretation, not an admission by Kaczynski. **[J/interpretation.]** [Cambridge Center for Behavioral Studies, 2023](https://behavior.org/unabomer-extreme-reaction-behavior-modification/)
- No evidence shows that Kaczynski studied under McConnell. They were at the University of Michigan during overlapping years, but in different departments.

### Civil suit against Kaczynski

In August 1996 Suino filed a negligence and intentional-infliction-of-emotional-distress action in Washtenaw County Circuit Court. The contemporaneous reports say he sought more than $10,000. His attorneys were Michael A. Hettinger and Kerry Hettinger; the case was assigned to Judge Melinda Morris. Suino said compensation was a normal part of justice but doubted he would collect unless Kaczynski later earned money from a book or film. **[L for complaint allegations; J for statements.]** [Ann Arbor News collection](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/ann-arbor-news-stories-on-ted-kaczynski); [UPI, August 22, 1996](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1996/08/22/Michigan-man-sues-UNABOMber-suspect/1711840686400/)

Kaczynski’s attorney Thomas H. Speedy Rice filed to remove the case to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan on September 18, 1996. Rice invoked diversity jurisdiction and asserted that more than $50,000 was at issue; Suino’s counsel said the demand remained $10,000. The reports do not resolve that jurisdictional amount conflict. **[J describing filed notice.]**

No accessible final order, dismissal, settlement or damages judgment in Suino’s civil action was located. It would therefore be improper to claim that Suino won, lost, settled or collected. The later federal $15,026,000 restitution judgment was for the specifically identified UNABOM victims collectively; the public appellate opinions located do not state an individual amount allocated or paid to Suino. **[P—federal judgment; individual outcome unknown.]** [Ninth Circuit opinion, 2005](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/416/971/480866/)

### Victim-impact statement

At Kaczynski’s May 4, 1998 sentencing, Assistant U.S. Attorney Steven Freccero introduced Suino. Suino said a death sentence would not have made him grieve, but that his central objective was preventing more bombings and that he did not personally wish Kaczynski dead. His statement rejected revenge, urged other victims not to remain psychologically imprisoned by the crime, and described ordinary work, family and disciplined living as the route forward. **[P—sentencing transcript.]** [Sentencing transcript](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/catherine-e-f-bodene-u-s-v-kaczynski-sentencing)

## 5. Ideas and Programs

Suino is not documented as a scientist, cyberneticist or intelligence theorist. His principal body of work concerns Japanese martial arts, discipline, skill acquisition and personal development.

### Martial-arts program

His books and institutional teaching emphasize:

- technical iaido practice and sword handling;
- timing, distance, perception and reading an opponent’s intention;
- etiquette and responsibility toward training partners;
- the use of sustained practice to build character;
- “budo” as physical, mental and ethical development.

The publisher describes *Strategy in Japanese Swordsmanship* as pursuing two linked objectives: avoiding being cut while cutting the opponent, and moving toward “perfection of character.” **[P—publisher description, 2020.]** [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/175148/strategy-in-japanese-swordsmanship-by-nicklaus-suino/)

### The bombing as an ethical reference point

His 1998 victim statement is his clearest public philosophical response to the attack. He rejected revenge as a durable source of meaning, stressed ordinary constructive activity, and refused to center his later identity on victimhood. **[P.]**

Suino’s recent social-media writing calls McConnell his old mentor and analogizes research about hidden behavioral signals in language-model data to McConnell’s memory-transfer work. ## 6. Scandal, Controversy and Contest

### The bombing

Suino was the victim of a criminal attack, not a participant in it. Kaczynski’s 1998 allocution and the evidence recovered from his cabin establish responsibility. **[P.]**

### Injury descriptions

“Severely injured” in some later summaries overstates Suino’s own courtroom description of comparatively minor, healed physical injuries, though it may reflect the temporary hearing loss and psychological effects. The more precise record is cuts, burns, bruising, temporary near-total hearing loss, eye contamination and lasting package anxiety. **[D—overbroad wording corrected by primary testimony.]**

### Civil litigation

The complaint’s physical and emotional injury claims were allegations when filed in 1996. Much was later supported by Suino’s courtroom account, but no civil disposition was found. **[L/P.]**

### Motivation dispute

There is no direct Kaczynski statement saying, in full, why McConnell was chosen. His notes identify McConnell as a behavior-modification researcher. Later writers infer an ideological motive; the FBI emphasizes library-based victim selection. Those versions should remain separate.

### MKUltra/CIA narrative

A recurring online narrative says Henry Murray’s Harvard stress study of Kaczynski was part of MKUltra and then draws lines from Murray to Kaczynski, McConnell, cybernetics and Suino. Even if one accepts or rejects the separate MKUltra claim, it supplies no documented relationship between Suino and the CIA. One conspiracy-oriented 2023 page mentions Suino solely as the assistant injured by McConnell’s bomb. **[R as to Suino.]** [Sott.net, 2023](https://www.sott.net/article/481200-Ted-Kaczynskis-connection-to-MK-Ultra-explained)

### Epstein narrative

The documented “Epstein” in the UNABOM record is Charles Epstein, another bombing victim. No record found connects Suino with Jeffrey Epstein, his companies, foundations, aircraft, residences, staff, prosecutions or financial network. **[No evidence; name collision identified.]**

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

- **1985:** Local coverage treated Suino as McConnell’s injured assistant and speculated, without evidence, that controversy over McConnell’s textbook or behavior-modification views might explain the bombing.
- **1995–96:** As UNABOM attention intensified, Suino was quoted condemning political violence; after Kaczynski’s arrest, national reporting reconstructed the McConnell attack.
- **1996:** His civil suit made him a more visible claimant and potential creditor of Kaczynski.
- **1998:** His sentencing statement gave him a public identity distinct from simple victimhood: physically recovered, cautious about packages, critical of Kaczynski but opposed to living through revenge.
- **2000s onward:** Martial-arts publishing, law and business profiles largely displaced the bombing from his public biography. His own current professional sites barely center it.
- **2003, Lutz Dammbeck’s *The Net*:** This essay film connected Kaczynski, cybernetics, LSD, the CIA and the development of network technology. It is a work of interpretive documentary montage, not proof that each named person belonged to one coordinated organization. No evidence located shows Suino participated in making it.
- **2026, Hidden AmuraKa:** The title explicitly adds Epstein and a “cybernetic elite.” Its narrow Suino statement is accurate; extending Suino’s role beyond being McConnell’s employee and the injured opener lacks support.
- No memoir by Suino devoted to the bombing was found. His fullest first-person account remains the 1998 sentencing statement.

## 9. Sources

### Source assessment

The strongest evidence for the attack is the combination of the November 1985 local report, Kaczynski’s January 1998 plea colloquy and Suino’s May 1998 courtroom account. The federal transcript is decisive on authorship and construction; Suino’s statement is strongest on his injuries and psychological aftermath.

The *Ann Arbor News* material is accessible through a transcription/reprint collection that names the original article dates and writers. The underlying Ann Arbor District Library pages were unavailable through the research interface, so the reprint should be checked against scans before quotation in formal publication.

Suino’s, JMAC’s and SMAA’s pages are appropriate for his own account, organizational positions and claimed ranks. They are not independent verification of competition results, sales totals or business success.

The contested-claim search produced adjacency and name confusion, not evidence connecting Suino to Jeffrey Epstein, the CIA or MKUltra.

# Cammie (Kamie) Clark: Research Dossier

*Research cut-off: September 28, 2026. This dossier concerns a living private individual. It reports only public records, archived materials, and attributable journalism; private contact information appearing in released emails has been omitted.*

## Evidentiary key

- **[P—Primary record]** Contemporaneous email, government release, archived website, official corporate statement, or comparable documentary record.
- **[R—Reported]** Reporting by identified journalists, sometimes relying on records or unnamed sources that are not public.
- **[S—Subject claim]** A statement made by Clark or published on a profile apparently controlled by her.
- **[A—Allegation/rumor]** A lawsuit allegation, named accusation, or unsupported online assertion—not established fact.
- **[N—Negative finding]** No substantiating record was located in the searched public sources. This means “not established,” not metaphysical proof that no such record exists.

## 1. Identification

The subject is best identified as **Camilla “Cami” Clark**, an American entrepreneur and business-development executive reportedly born in Reno, Nevada, in 1979. The released Jeffrey Epstein correspondence uses **“Cami Clark”** and an address associated with `camiclark.com`; later reporting uses both Cami and Camilla. No primary record located uses **“Cammie Clark”** or **“Kamie Clark.”** Those appear to be audio transcriptions or phonetic variants, not documented aliases. **[P/R]** ([March 2011 email](https://filestein.com/Document/EFTA02358805); [Wall Street Journal, August 13, 2026](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/claude-dario-amodei-wife-anthropic-e1eeda7d))

Clark’s documented or reported roles include work at Herman Miller and mortgage company OLG Financial; co-founder of the women-oriented adult-media venture **Eddice**; founder of **The Hormone Project** and **Female Algorithm Technologies**; head of revenue at the motion-control company **Leap Motion**; and, according to her website, adviser or mentor to Pelagius and IndieBio. She is reported to have dated former Google chief executive **Eric Schmidt** from approximately 2011 to 2014, to have begun a relationship with artificial-intelligence researcher **Dario Amodei** in 2014, and to have married Amodei in 2022. No marriage certificate or official employment history was retrieved; these biographical facts therefore remain reported rather than independently established through primary civil or personnel records. **[R/S]** ([The Information, August 13, 2026](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropics-first-lady-took-winding-road-top); [Clark’s website](https://www.camiclark.com/))

The second woman introduced to Epstein in 2011 was **Michelle Capocefalo**. The email’s address header supplies that spelling. Brockman’s prose spells her first name “Michele,” while some later captions incorrectly render the surname “Capocetalo” or “Cappafello.” A New York business-directory transcription also lists **Michelle Capocefalo** as president of Eddice, Inc. **[P/R]** ([EFTA01837815](https://assets.getkino.com/documents/vol00010-efta01837815-pdf.pdf); [New York company-directory transcription](https://bizstanding.com/directory/NY/ED/110/))

Clark is not documented as a scientist, intelligence officer, Anthropic employee, or AI researcher. Her relevance to the requested network is entrepreneurial and relational: Brockman introduced her to Epstein while she was raising money; she later had relationships with Schmidt and Amodei; and journalists describe her as an informal adviser and connector around Amodei and Anthropic. **[P/R]**

## 2. The Record

### Origins, education, first marriage, and early employment

The *Wall Street Journal* reported from public records that Clark was born in Reno in 1979. *The Information* added that she described a blue-collar upbringing and work, beginning at about fourteen, at a pipefitters’ union where her grandmother and great-aunt worked. The specific union was not named and no personnel record was published. **[R]**

Clark reportedly studied architecture at the California College of the Arts but did not obtain a degree. An archived personal biography said she left architecture school in 1999. She moved to San Francisco that year. **[R/S]**

The *Journal* reported that, at twenty, Clark married 64-year-old architect **Waldemar Eklof III** in 1999 and divorced him approximately three years later. Eklof was associated with the design of Reno’s Atlantis Casino Resort. This account was said to derive from marriage records, but the certificate itself was not made public in the sources reviewed. **[R]**

Clark subsequently worked for office-furniture company **Herman Miller** and mortgage business **OLG Financial**. She later described herself as an OLG co-founder; the *Journal* reported that 2005 incorporation records it reviewed did not name her. That discrepancy does not by itself prove that she had no operational or informal founding role, but the documentary record reviewed by the newspaper did not substantiate the co-founder title. **[R/S; contested]**

The *Journal* also reported that a San Francisco apartment owned by Clark entered foreclosure in 2007 and that she filed for bankruptcy in 2009. The underlying property instrument and bankruptcy docket number were not published or independently retrieved for this dossier. These should therefore be treated as record-based journalism, not as independently inspected court records. **[R]**

### Eddice

*The Information* dates the beginning of Eddice to 2009; the *Journal* describes it as beginning around 2010. A secondary transcription of New York corporate records gives **October 20, 2010** as Eddice, Inc.’s registration date and lists Michelle Capocefalo as president. The company is listed as inactive. The actual state filing was not recovered. **[R]**

Eddice presented itself as a women-oriented, sex-positive adult-media and commerce venture, using language such as “intellectually promiscuous.” Its name reportedly came from Clark’s maternal grandmother, **Eddice Munson**. Clark and Capocefalo were identified in contemporary and later sources as co-founders. **[R/S]**

A February 9, 2011, archived Eddice page publicly sought **$350,000** through crowdfunding to build the site and produce four films under the working title *American Girl in Paris*. The page displayed six backers and **$710** contributed at the time of capture. Those figures are Eddice’s own website representations, not an audited account. **[P/S]** ([archived Eddice fundraising page](https://web.archive.org/web/20110209060725/http://eddice.com/))

The *Journal* reported that PayPal froze an account associated with the campaign. No PayPal record or litigation concerning the freeze was located. *The Information* later reported that Eddice raised roughly hundreds of thousands of dollars, but neither publication supplied an investor list or ledger. **[R]**

Archived September 2011 and May 2012 versions show that the Eddice website continued operating as a sex, culture, and design publication. The public record does not establish that the proposed four-film series was completed or commercially released. By 2013, according to *The Information*, the project had become inactive. **[P/R]** ([September 2011 archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20110927101814/http://www.eddice.com/); [May 2012 archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20120530000654/http://eddice.com/))

### The Epstein correspondence, 2011–2013

#### Introduction and meeting

On March 3, 2011, literary and technology agent **John Brockman** emailed **Jeffrey Epstein**, copying Clark and Capocefalo. Brockman proposed that Epstein meet “my girls—Cami and Michele,” whom he said he had met the previous month in Munich. He described them as being in Los Angeles raising money for a pornographic film aimed at women and supplied the Eddice URL. He also said they were watching TED at **Danny Hillis’s** house and would attend **Jason Calacanis’s** birthday dinner. **[P]** ([EFTA01837815](https://assets.getkino.com/documents/vol00010-efta01837815-pdf.pdf); [official DOJ copy, EFTA01794534](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%2010/EFTA01794534.pdf))

Clark replied at 11:41 p.m., copying Epstein and Capocefalo, saying they would like to have dinner with him that evening. The released record omits any reliable description of the ensuing encounter, but a subsequent message says a meeting occurred. **[P]** ([EFTA02358805](https://filestein.com/Document/EFTA02358805))

The Munich reference is not conclusive proof that Brockman met them at the DLD conference. Later journalists identify DLD, where Schmidt spoke in January 2011, but “last month” in a March 3 email would ordinarily indicate February. The place is documented; the precise event is reported and chronologically uncertain. **[P/R; unresolved]**

#### Funding proposal

On March 4, a message signed **“C+M”** thanked Epstein for the previous night’s meeting, described it as “FANTASTIC,” and referred to an attached breakdown of **$2.5 million** in funding needs. The surviving copy’s sender metadata is incomplete or redacted; the collective signature and Eddice details support attribution to Clark and Capocefalo jointly, not necessarily to Clark alone. **[P]** ([EFTA02688552](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%2011/EFTA02688552.pdf))

A separately released Eddice budget dated March 4 totals $2.5 million:

- **$1 million** for a sixteen-week launch;
- **$1.5 million** from launch through the end of year one;
- **$100,000** for film production before launch and **$500,000** afterward;
- **$300,000** for technology before launch and **$250,000** afterward;
- remaining categories including salaries, freelancers, legal costs, office expenses, public relations, travel, and business development.

The timing and figures make it highly likely that this was the referenced attachment, although a complete native-email attachment chain was not recovered. A budget is a request, not evidence that the money was raised or transferred. **[P; attachment relationship strongly inferred]** ([EFTA00599134](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00599134.pdf))

On March 7, a message signed “C” sent Epstein what was described as the script or treatment for the first four *American Girl in Paris* films. The treatment itself was not located in the reviewed release, so its contents, performers, production status, and handling of consent cannot be assessed. **[P/N]** ([EFTA00702664](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00702664.pdf))

These documents establish that Eddice’s founders met Epstein and solicited his interest in a $2.5 million plan. They do **not** establish that Epstein invested, loaned money, received equity, or participated in production. Searches across the indexed released records found no agreement, wire, check, capitalization table, or payment record. The indexes are incomplete, so this is a negative finding rather than proof that no transaction ever occurred. **[P/N]**

#### Renewed contact in 2012

On March 8–9, 2012, Clark recontacted Epstein, offered to introduce him to lingerie entrepreneur **Jennifer Zuccarini**, and said she and Capocefalo had moved back to New York. When Epstein asked who she was, Clark answered that Brockman had introduced them at TED the previous year and referred to Eddice as the “free luxury porn company.” **[P]** ([EFTA00718989](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%209/EFTA00718989.pdf); [searchable copy](https://efta.threats.watch/documents/EFTA00718989?fe=BodyMedia&fid=766652))

Asked what happened to Eddice’s plans, Clark responded that the project was proceeding and said the company had hired a head of programming and development who had worked for Oprah Winfrey’s network and Ashton Kutcher’s Katalyst. The person was not named in the thread. Clark asked whether Epstein remained interested and mentioned another financing round. Epstein answered that he could not do television sex. No transaction followed in the located records. **[P]**

Clark then described a prospective social-dieting application and website for women. The concept would combine data from Fitbit, Jawbone UP, BodyMedia Fit Core, Nike FuelBand, a Withings scale, MC10 devices, nutrition, medications and supplements, menstrual cycles, GPS and mood, with Facebook-style sharing. She cited a $61 billion American and $586 billion global diet market and asked whether the project interested Epstein. The email documents a pitch, not a launched system, data partnership, research protocol, or investment. **[P]**

On March 13–14, Clark introduced Zuccarini to Epstein by email, describing her work at Kiki de Montparnasse and Victoria’s Secret and her plan for a new lingerie line. Epstein forwarded the introduction to his assistant **Lesley Groff** for scheduling. **[P]** ([EFTA01845548](https://efta.threats.watch/documents/EFTA01845548))

In 2013, automated LinkedIn notifications received by Epstein reported that Clark had updated her profile and identified her as founder of **The Hormone Project**. These messages show that his account received network updates; they do not prove direct 2013 correspondence or another meeting. **[P]** ([EFTA01197491](https://efta.threats.watch/documents/EFTA01197491?q=%22Leases%22); [EFTA02357233](https://efta.threats.watch/documents/EFTA02357233?fe=8lola+Capital&fid=152608))

The *Journal* and *Information* reported that Clark and Capocefalo invited Epstein to a 2012 Lower East Side housewarming and that he did not attend. No invitation email was independently retrieved. No documented in-person contact after the 2011 meeting was found. **[R/N]**

The timing matters: Epstein had pleaded guilty in Florida on June 30, 2008, to felony solicitation and procurement of minors to engage in prostitution, served less than thirteen months, and was released in July 2009 under community control and sex-offender registration requirements. Thus Clark’s documented contact came after the conviction. Neither the emails nor the reporting establishes what Clark or Capocefalo knew about Epstein’s criminal history when Brockman introduced them. **[P/N]** ([DOJ Office of Professional Responsibility report](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/press-release/file/1336416/dl))

### Eric Schmidt and women’s-health ventures

The *Journal* and *Information* report that Clark dated **Eric Schmidt**, then a senior Google executive, for approximately three years beginning in 2011. Schmidt was married; the reporting describes his marriage as open. No statement from Clark or primary correspondence documenting the relationship was located. **[R]**

In 2013 Clark developed **Female Algorithm Technologies**, described as a prospective network of high-end women’s-health clinics. Reporting based on a pitch deck says Schmidt invested a “modest” amount, without disclosing a figure, terms, or date. The same materials reportedly listed **“Dr. Dario Amodei”** as an adviser. **[R]**

The clinics were proposed for Los Angeles and New York, with annual memberships reportedly ranging from **$9,000 to $20,000**. Architect **David Adjaye** was reportedly expected to design the facilities. No clinic license, clinical-trial registration, opening record, audited capitalization, or proof that the facilities became operational was found. *The Information* says the project was abandoned by 2016. **[R/N]**

A 2016 Arc Fusion presentation attributed to Clark proposed using artificial intelligence to combine conventional medical data with information from selfies, online activity, credit-card transactions, GPS and phone records. That establishes a proposed data-intensive health model, not that patient data were actually collected or that human-subject research occurred. No protocol, institutional-review-board approval, informed-consent form, trial result, or government contract was located. **[R/N]** ([identified presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OVpeo7LTQ0A))

Clark’s present website still calls her founder and chief executive of Female Algorithm Technologies. Because the page is undated and may be a legacy biography, it does not resolve the journalistic report that the operating project ended in 2016. **[S/R; unresolved]** ([Clark website](https://www.camiclark.com/home-page-full-scroll))

### Dario Amodei, Leap Motion, and the AI network

*The Information* reports that Clark met Amodei in 2013, while he was a Stanford postdoctoral researcher, and began dating him in 2014. The *Journal* and *El País* compress the account to meeting and beginning the relationship in 2014. They reportedly married in Italy in 2022. No civil certificate was retrieved. **[R; minor date conflict]**

Clark reportedly spent time at a San Francisco group house occupied by Amodei, his sister **Daniela Amodei**, **Holden Karnofsky**, and others involved in effective altruism and AI safety. Daniela Amodei later married Karnofsky. Clark and Dario Amodei also reportedly attended Burning Man and on one occasion camped with **Dustin Moskovitz** and **Cari Tuna**. These are reported social ties, not evidence of a shared organization or coordinated financial enterprise. **[R]**

In 2016 Clark became head of revenue at **Leap Motion** and left in 2017. Board member **Peter Bell** told *The Information* that she had less directly relevant experience than some candidates but performed well. At Leap Motion she became friendly with **Mira Murati**, then a product and engineering executive. **[R]**

Journalists report that Clark later introduced Murati to OpenAI co-founder **Greg Brockman**; Murati joined OpenAI in 2018. No introduction email was published. Clark also attended OpenAI-related events and circulated Amodei’s research to wealthy technology contacts while he was working there, according to unnamed sources quoted by the *Journal*. She never held a documented OpenAI position. **[R/N]**

The *Journal* reports that Clark introduced Amodei to Schmidt. In 2018 Schmidt visited Clark and Amodei at their San Francisco apartment; Schmidt recalled the visit and his favorable impression of Amodei in a 2025 Bloomberg profile. **[R]** ([Bloomberg, May 19, 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-05-19/anthropic-ceo-amodei-steers-61-billion-ai-powerhouse))

There is a source conflict. *El País* wrote in August 2026 that Schmidt introduced Clark and Amodei to each other, whereas the *Journal* says Clark brought Schmidt into Amodei’s and later Anthropic’s orbit. The reported 2013/2014 start of Clark’s relationship with Amodei, combined with the documented 2018 apartment visit, is compatible with Clark introducing Amodei to Schmidt; it does not support the reverse formulation. Without primary correspondence or on-record testimony addressing the first introduction, the discrepancy remains unresolved. **[R; conflicting versions]** ([El País, August 14, 2026](https://elpais.com/tecnologia/2026-08-14/fundadora-de-una-empresa-de-porno-para-mujeres-y-contacto-de-epstein-sale-a-la-luz-la-historia-de-la-misteriosa-mujer-de-dario-amodei.html))

After Dario and Daniela Amodei and five other employees left OpenAI, they founded Anthropic in December 2020. In early 2021, Clark reportedly prepared a forty-page proposal for a **“Mother of AGI Fund.”** According to documents described by the *Journal* and *Information*, it proposed formalizing her involvement in Dario Amodei’s company, managing Schmidt’s investment, and investing more broadly in artificial intelligence. Other Anthropic founders reportedly opposed the proposal as a personal conflict, and it did not proceed. The proposal itself is not public. **[R]**

Anthropic announced a **$124 million Series A** in May 2021, led by **Jaan Tallinn**, with participation from **James McClave, Dustin Moskovitz, the Center for Emerging Risk Research, Eric Schmidt**, and others. The official announcement does not name Clark. The *Journal*, relying on people close to the company, says Clark brought Schmidt in as an early investor. **[P/R]** ([Anthropic announcement, May 28, 2021](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-124-million-to-build-more-reliable-general-ai-systems))

Clark does not work for Anthropic and has no disclosed board, executive, equity, or advisory title. The *Journal* and *Information* nevertheless describe her as Amodei’s private sounding board, strategic adviser, public-image adviser, social connector, and participant in the family’s investment decisions. Those characterizations rely principally on unnamed company associates and acquaintances rather than corporate filings. **[R]**

She has accompanied Amodei at Davos, the Allen & Company conference in Sun Valley, and a 2026 AI summit in New Delhi where invited executives could bring one guest. She reportedly has social contact with **Ivanka Trump**, including a lunch, and spoke with **Jared Kushner** at Sun Valley. Dario Amodei had separately approached Kushner about investment, according to the *Journal*. None of these contacts establishes a governmental appointment, lobbying registration, campaign role, or investment by Trump or Kushner. **[R/N]**

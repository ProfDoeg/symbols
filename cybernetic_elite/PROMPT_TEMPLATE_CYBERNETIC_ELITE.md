# PROMPT_TEMPLATE_CYBERNETIC_ELITE.md

The dossier prompt for the "cybernetic elite" set (Anthony, 2026-09-28, from the video "The Unabomber & the Net: Kaczynski, Epstein and the Cybernetic Elite", Hidden AmuraKa): "create dossiers for all the characters mentioned (that we don't already have), separate from the atlas; map the social / power / financial network detailed in the video". The subjects are historical and living persons, institutions and programs in the twentieth- and twenty-first-century history of cybernetics, computing, intelligence, finance and their scandals. tools/codex_dossier_set.py sends the part after the first `---` with `[NAME]` substituted, then the per-item brief, then DELIVER.txt.

---

[NAME]

Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this person, institution or program. The subject belongs to the documented history of cybernetics, computing, intelligence agencies, elite finance and the scandals that connect them; what I want is the complete documented record: who the subject is, what they did, whom they funded, employed, married, advised, prosecuted or protected, and how money, positions and information moved through them. The network is the subject as much as the biography: every documented tie to another named person, institution, fund, foundation, agency, company or program matters.

Hold the evidentiary discipline throughout, and hold it harder because some subjects are alive and some claims are litigated. For every significant claim say which source carries it and from when. Distinguish: documented in primary records (court filings, depositions, flight logs, SEC and tax filings, congressional testimony, declassified files, contemporaneous correspondence, corporate registries, obituaries); reported by named journalists with sources; alleged in lawsuits or by named accusers; claimed in memoirs or interviews by the subject; circulated as rumor or in online conspiracy material; and disproved or retracted. Where sources conflict give the versions with their dates rather than harmonizing them. Nothing is discarded for being unprovable, but everything unprovable is labeled as such, with who says it and where; a rumor is reported as a rumor, not as fact and not omitted. Do not sanitize: abuse, trafficking, fraud, surveillance, funding of research on human subjects, ties to intelligence agencies and to organized crime, and the political uses of the subject are part of many of these records and must be stated plainly where documented, with the documentation. Where a claim would be defamatory if false, state the evidence tier explicitly and name the source.

Organize the dossier along these lines, adapting as the subject requires:

1. **Identification.** Who or what the subject is in one dense orienting passage; names, aliases, dates, nationality, positions held, and the subject's class: scientist, financier, intelligence officer, politician, institution, program, foundation, company, event.

2. **The Record.** The subject's life or history told fully and in order: origins, education, career, the institutions passed through, the money (salary, wealth, donations received and given, funds managed, with figures and dates where documented), the legal record (indictments, convictions, settlements, sealed matters), and the death or dissolution where applicable, with what happened to the papers, the estate, the archives.

3. **The Network.** A dedicated section listing EVERY documented tie between this subject and any OTHER named person, institution, fund, agency, company or program, one entry each: the other party, the nature of the tie in one line (funded, employed, advised, married, sat on the board of, was investigated by, testified about, flew with, donated to, was introduced by, co-authored with, prosecuted, protected, recruited), the dates, the source with its date, and the evidence tier. Discrete, checkable claims, not narrative prose. Include the ties that the video or popular accounts assert and mark plainly which are documented and which are alleged or unsupported.

4. **Money and Power.** Where the subject's money came from and went, in a compact accounting: employers, patrons, foundations, funds, government contracts, tax-exempt vehicles, real estate, offshore entities, with figures and sources; and the positions of power held or influenced (boards, advisory roles, security clearances, editorial control).

5. **Ideas and Programs.** For scientists, institutions and programs: what the subject built or argued (cybernetics, behavioral science, artificial intelligence, mirror worlds, technological determinism, the anti-technology critique), the key texts with dates, the experiments including those on human subjects (consent, funding, outcomes), and how the ideas were used by others.

6. **Scandal, Controversy and Contest.** The accusations, investigations, trials, cover-ups and public fights, each with its documentation and outcome; the conspiracy narratives that attach to the subject, stated as narratives with their origin, spread and evidentiary status; what remains sealed, unknown or disputed.

7. **Reception and Afterlife.** Books, films, documentaries, podcasts, court records and journalism that shaped the public image, with dates; the subject's own account where one exists; how the image has changed.

8. **Chronology.** A compact dated timeline anchored to evidence.

9. **Sources.**

Search broadly and deeply: court dockets and filings, government archives and FOIA releases, congressional records, corporate and nonprofit registries, university archives, contemporaneous newspapers, the named investigative journalists on the subject, academic history of science, and the primary documents behind popular accounts. Do not rely on the video that named the subject; verify or contradict it. Write without editorial overlay; report what is attested, by whom and when. Favor documentary presentation. Detail over brevity.

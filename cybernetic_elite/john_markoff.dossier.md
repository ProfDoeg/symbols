# John Markoff: Research Dossier

*Research cutoff: September 28, 2026.*

## Evidentiary key

- **[P] Primary record:** government document, released correspondence, contemporaneous article, institutional record, book credits, or film transcript/credits.
- **[R] Reported:** named journalism, historical scholarship, or a signed review relying on identified evidence.
- **[S] Subject claim:** John Markoff’s own account, including authorized biographies and interviews.
- **[A] Allegation:** assertion by a named accuser, litigant, or critic not established by adjudication.
- **[U] Unsupported/rumor:** conspiracy material or an assertion for which no adequate underlying record was found.
- **[C] Corrected, contradicted, or disproved:** contradicted by the cited record or later correction.

The surviving record is much stronger concerning Markoff’s journalism, books, employers, and institutional affiliations than concerning personal finances or private relationships. A name on an invitation list establishes an invitation—not attendance; an email proposing a call establishes contact—not necessarily that the call occurred; and membership in the same professional network does not establish financing, complicity, or friendship.

## 1. Identification

John Gregory Markoff is an American technology journalist and author, born in Oakland and raised in Palo Alto, whose principal institutional position was national computer and technology reporter for *The New York Times* from March 1988 until his full-time retirement on December 1, 2016. He subsequently worked as a Computer History Museum staff historian and held fellow or affiliate positions at the Simons Institute, the Center for Advanced Study in the Behavioral Sciences, the Berggruen Institute, and Stanford’s Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence. He is best known for reporting on computer security, Silicon Valley, the World Wide Web, surveillance, artificial intelligence and robotics, and for the books *The High Cost of High Tech* (1985), *Cyberpunk* (1991), *Takedown* (1996), *What the Dormouse Said* (2005), *Machines of Loving Grace* (2015), and *Whole Earth* (2022). His published name is John Markoff; no journalistic alias was found. [Markoff’s own biography](https://johnmarkoff.net/about), [Columbia University biography](https://precollege.sps.columbia.edu/person/john-gregory-markoff) **[S/P]**

His exact birthday is unresolved in public sources. Numerous derivative reference records give **October 24, 1949**, while the biographical statement attached to Markoff’s Harvard/Shorenstein oral history and the Simons Institute biography give **October 29, 1949**. Both agree on Oakland, California, and 1949. The latter profiles appear to derive from biographical information supplied by Markoff, but no birth certificate or equivalent primary civil record was located. [Riptide biography and oral history](https://www.digitalriptide.org/person/john-markoff/), [Simons Institute biography](https://simons.berkeley.edu/people/john-markoff) **[S/P; disputed date]**

Identity control matters here: this John Markoff is not John Markoff, the University of Pittsburgh sociologist and historian of democratization. Searches of litigation and corporate records also produced unrelated people with the same or similar names.

## 2. The Record

### Origins, education, and political formation

Markoff graduated from Whitman College in 1971 and earned an M.A. in sociology from the University of Oregon in 1976. At Oregon he edited the student newspaper, participated in antiwar and “power-structure research,” and worked with the Pacific Northwest Research Center and opposition to the B-1 bomber. He told the University of Oregon that he had then considered himself a democratic socialist; his later politics became less doctrinaire. [University of Oregon profile](https://news.uoregon.edu/oq/robots-among-us), [Whitman class record](https://www.whitman.edu/alumni/attend-events/photo-galleries-reunions/class-of-1971-40th-reunion-spring-2011) **[S/P]**

In his Riptide oral history, Markoff named I. F. Stone, Lincoln Steffens, C. Wright Mills, and G. William Domhoff as early influences. He described working at the Pacific Study Center and Pacific Northwest Research Center, writing a roughly seventy-page report on corporate farming, and deciding after graduate school to become a reporter. These are autobiographical claims, not independently audited employment records. [Riptide oral history, recorded for the 2013 project](https://www.digitalriptide.org/person/john-markoff/) **[S]**

### Pacific News Service and early technology journalism, 1977–1988

Markoff reported on technology and the defense industry for Pacific News Service from approximately 1977 to 1981. He said an unnamed Quaker family supplied about **$6,000** for investigative reporting on military activity in Silicon Valley; Pacific News Service administered the money without overhead and paid him another **$100 per story**. He recalled living on about **$3,000 a year** during part of this period. These figures come only from Markoff’s oral history; the donor was not identified and no grant file was located. [Riptide oral history](https://www.digitalriptide.org/person/john-markoff/) **[S]**

He joined *InfoWorld* in 1981, wrote a personal-computer column for the *San Jose Mercury News* from 1983 to 1985, served as *Byte*’s West Coast editor in 1984–85, and worked for the *San Francisco Examiner* from 1985 to 1988. His accounts from this period already joined technical reporting to workplace safety, military procurement, pollution, and surveillance. [Riptide biography](https://www.digitalriptide.org/person/john-markoff/), [Markoff biography](https://johnmarkoff.net/about) **[P/S]**

With Lenny Siegel, he wrote *The High Cost of High Tech: The Dark Side of the Chip* (Harper & Row, 1985), covering occupational hazards, toxic contamination, offshore assembly, military dependence, and surveillance in the electronics industry. Contemporary reviews treated it as an explicitly critical account of the costs hidden by Silicon Valley’s clean-industry image. [*Publishers Weekly* review](https://www.publishersweekly.com/978-0-06-039045-7), [contemporaneous excerpt/review material](https://content.library.pdx.edu/files/PDXScholar/CSQ-SEA-Fall1985/) **[P/R]**

### *The New York Times*, 1988–2016

Markoff joined the *Times* business section in March 1988 as its national computer reporter, moved to the San Francisco bureau in 1992, later wrote for the Science section, and returned to the Business section in 2016. He retired from the full-time staff on December 1, 2016, while retaining the possibility of contract work. His website rounds the tenure to 1988–2017; his contemporaneous retirement essay gives the more precise December 2016 date. [Markoff’s December 8, 2016 retirement account](https://www.wired.com/2016/12/i-covered-tech-for-the-times-for-28-years-and-now-my-time-is-over/) **[S/P]**

Major episodes included:

- **The Morris worm, 1988.** Markoff identified Robert Tappan Morris as the author of the Internet worm. Morris’s father, Robert Morris Sr., was an NSA computer-security scientist. That family fact does not make Markoff an NSA associate. A later account by Jonathan Littman claimed Robert Morris Sr. was Markoff’s personal friend and helped steer him to the story; this remains a hostile author’s account without independent documentary confirmation. [Jonathan Littman, *The Fugitive Game*, 1996](https://www.gbppr.net/2600/The_Fugitive_Game.pdf) **[A/R]**

- **The early Web, 1993.** “A Free and Simple Computer Link,” published December 8, 1993, was an early mass-newspaper explanation of the World Wide Web. [Archived article](https://www.links.net/vita/web/start/markoff.html) **[P]**

- **David Gelernter and the Unabomber, 1992–93.** Markoff published the substantial profile “David Gelernter’s Romance With Linda” on January 19, 1992. Theodore Kaczynski mailed Gelernter a bomb in June 1993; Gelernter survived with severe injuries. Markoff subsequently reported on his recovery. The timing is documented; causation between the profile and Kaczynski’s selection of Gelernter is not. [Reproduction of the 1992 profile](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/john-markoff-david-gelernter-s-romance-with-linda), [1996 federal indictment](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/1996/Jun96/283crm.htm) **[P]**

- **Kevin Mitnick, 1994–96.** Markoff’s July 4, 1994 “Cyberspace’s Most Wanted” article portrayed fugitive hacker Kevin Mitnick as a persistent and unusually capable intruder. Markoff followed the search and was present during the 1995 arrest proceedings. He then co-wrote *Takedown* with Tsutomu Shimomura. The Justice Department credited the FBI and a multiagency investigative team, assisted by Shimomura and the San Diego Supercomputer Center; it did not identify Markoff as an investigator or government agent. [DOJ arrest announcement, February 16, 1995](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/Pre_96/February95/89.txt.html) **[P]**

- **Total Information Awareness, 2002.** Markoff reported John Poindexter’s return to government and DARPA’s Information Awareness Office. The November 15, 2002 Congressional Record credited reporting by Markoff and *Washington Post* journalist Robert O’Harrow Jr. with exposing the breadth of the program. This is evidence of scrutiny of a defense-surveillance program, not employment by it. [Congressional Record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRECB-2002-pt17/pdf/CRECB-2002-pt17-issue-2002-11-15.pdf) **[P]**

- **Robotics and labor, 2012–13.** His August 2012 robotics reporting formed part of the *Times* staff’s “iEconomy” work, which won the 2013 Pulitzer Prize for Explanatory Reporting. The award citation concerned changing labor practices at Apple and other technology companies. [Pulitzer record and winning work](https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/staff-74) **[P]**

He also shared the 2005 Gerald Loeb Deadline Writing Award with Andrew Ross Sorkin, Steve Lohr, David Barboza, and Gary Rivlin for “End of an Era.” [UCLA Loeb Award archive](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/signature-events/gerald-loeb-awards/winners/historical-winners) **[P]**

### Books after *Cyberpunk*

*Cyberpunk: Outlaws and Hackers on the Computer Frontier* (Simon & Schuster, 1991), co-written with Katie Hafner, presented case studies of Kevin Mitnick, German computer intruders, and Robert Tappan Morris. In a contemporary interview, the authors said they were describing the intersection of advanced technology and outlaw behavior, not producing a hacking manual or endorsing the subjects. [1991–92 *Compute!* interview](https://www.atarimagazines.com/compute/issue136/160_Cyberpunk.php) **[S/P]**

*Takedown: The Pursuit and Capture of America’s Most Wanted Computer Outlaw* (Hyperion, January 1996), co-written with Tsutomu Shimomura, converted the Mitnick pursuit into a first-person narrative. Its production credits name Markoff and Shimomura as writers, John Brockman and Katinka Matson as literary agents, and Leslie Terzian among those thanked. [Official book-project credits](https://www.takedown.com/credits.html) **[P]**

*What the Dormouse Said: How the Sixties Counterculture Shaped the Personal Computer Industry* (Viking, 2005) connected psychedelic experimentation, communal and antiwar politics, Douglas Engelbart’s augmentation program, John McCarthy’s artificial-intelligence laboratory, Stewart Brand, SRI, Xerox PARC, and the Homebrew Computer Club. It argues that the personal-computer culture emerged partly from a meeting of government-funded research and Bay Area counterculture—not that counterculture alone invented computing. [Publisher record](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/291224/what-the-dormouse-said-by-john-markoff/) **[P/S]**

*Machines of Loving Grace* (Ecco, 2015) organized modern AI history around a tension between autonomous systems that replace human work and “intelligence augmentation” systems intended to extend human capability. John McCarthy and Douglas Engelbart function as the book’s representative poles. [Contemporaneous review](https://www.sfgate.com/books/article/Machines-of-Loving-Grace-by-John-Markoff-6454287.php) **[R]**

*Whole Earth: The Many Lives of Stewart Brand* (Penguin Press, March 22, 2022) was written with access to Brand, his journals, and previously unpublished material. This access made it unusually rich but also generated criticism that the biography was too sympathetic. [Publisher record](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/554161/whole-earth-by-john-markoff/), [2022 *Nation* review](https://www.thenation.com/article/society/stewart-brand-whole-earth/tnamp/) **[P/R]**

### Later appointments and present work

Markoff joined the Computer History Museum as staff historian in January 2017, undertaking oral histories and historical projects. He was also a science communicator in residence at the Simons Institute and a 2017–18 Berggruen Fellow at Stanford’s Center for Advanced Study in the Behavioral Sciences, studying robotics, aging, and social isolation. [Computer History Museum announcement](https://computerhistory.org/press-releases/markoff-joins-chm/), [Berggruen announcement](https://berggruen.org/news/berggruen-institute-announces-2017-2018-class-of-fellows), [CASBS class announcement](https://casbs.stanford.edu/news/meet-2017-18-casbs-class) **[P]**

Stanford’s 2019 HAI launch described him as an inaugural Distinguished Fellow; current Stanford material uses affiliate or network-affiliate language. This appears to be a change in appointment nomenclature, not evidence that either record was false. [Stanford HAI launch announcement](https://neuroscience.stanford.edu/news/stanford-university-launches-institute-human-centered-artificial-intelligence) **[P]**

As of 2026, the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation’s grant database identifies a **$55,000 grant** supporting Markoff’s proposed book *The Edge of Chaos: Douglas Engelbart, John McCarthy, and the Decade that Made Silicon Valley*, to be published by MIT Press. [Sloan public-understanding grants](https://sloan.org/programs/public-understanding/books) **[P]**

No comprehensive Markoff personal-papers finding aid was located. The Computer History Museum preserves oral histories he conducted, including work connected to Douglas Engelbart and the “Mother of All Demos”; the Riptide project preserves his journalism oral history. [Computer History Museum account](https://computerhistory.org/blog/net-50-did-engelbarts-mother-of-all-demos-launch-the-connected-world/) **[P]**

### Edge, Epstein, and the “cybernetic elite” claim

## 5. Ideas and Programs

### Technology’s hidden costs

*The High Cost of High Tech* treated Silicon Valley as an industrial and political system rather than a collection of ingenious products. It foregrounded toxic work, environmental damage, military demand, globalized assembly, and workplace surveillance. This early work complicates depictions of Markoff as simply a promoter of the technology elite.

### Hackers and computer crime

*Cyberpunk* and *Takedown* helped establish the narrative conventions through which 1990s computer intruders were presented to general readers: technically gifted outsiders, cross-border pursuit, law-enforcement vulnerability, and dramatic conflict between hacker and security expert. The factual criminal cases were real; critics disputed the degree of danger and the books’ narrative magnification.

### Counterculture and personal computing

*What the Dormouse Said* argues that Bay Area personal computing emerged through the interaction of several streams:

- federally financed computing research;
- Douglas Engelbart’s human-augmentation program;
- John McCarthy’s artificial-intelligence work;
- psychedelic and communal experimentation;
- Stewart Brand’s publishing and convening;
- SRI, Stanford, Xerox PARC, and hobbyist institutions.

Markoff did not conduct the LSD or psychology experiments he describes. No record was found of his funding or administering human-subject research. The book is a work of journalism/history about research cultures.

### AI versus intelligence augmentation

In *Machines of Loving Grace*, Markoff emphasizes that automation is not technologically predetermined. System designers choose whether computers displace human judgment or increase human capability. His contrast between McCarthy and Engelbart is interpretive rather than a claim that either man alone founded AI or augmentation.

### Human-centered AI, aging, and isolation

The Berggruen/CASBS fellowship extended that inquiry to robots and elder care. The available institutional descriptions indicate research and writing, not a registered intervention, clinical trial, or human-subject experiment. No protocol, participant cohort, consent dispute, or research outcome attributable to Markoff was found.

### Stewart Brand and institutional memory

*Whole Earth* follows Brand across the Merry Pranksters, *Whole Earth Catalog*, environmentalism, network culture, and later technological and ecological projects. Its evidentiary strength—access to Brand and his journals—is also the basis of the principal criticism: reviewers argued that closeness to the subject may have softened scrutiny.

## 6. Scandal, Controversy, and Contest

### The Mitnick reporting dispute

The most substantial controversy involving Markoff concerns Kevin Mitnick.

Mitnick alleged that Markoff exaggerated his abilities and dangerousness, approached him for *Cyberpunk*, became hostile when Mitnick would not cooperate without payment, and later profited from a narrative Markoff had helped intensify. Mitnick also alleged that a proposed film-consulting arrangement contemplated $50,000 and paid only $5,000. These are Mitnick’s allegations, published by named journalists; they were not adjudicated as factual findings against Markoff. [*Wired*, November 5, 2002](https://www.wired.com/2002/11/mitnicks-lost-chapter-found/), [published chapter](https://www.theregister.com/security/2003/01/13/chapter-one-kevin-mitnicks-story/943312) **[A/R]**

Jonathan Littman similarly accused Markoff of conflicts and sensationalism. Robert Wright’s 1996 *New Yorker* essay criticized the commercial convergence of Markoff’s reporting, Shimomura’s participation in the pursuit, and the rapid book/movie deal. Simson Garfinkel reported that book rights were sold in mid-March 1995 and film rights went to Miramax. [*New Yorker*, January 29, 1996](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1996/01/29/hackwork), [*Christian Science Monitor*, January 4, 1996](https://www.csmonitor.com/1996/0104/04132.html) **[R/A]**

Markoff responded in a December 30, 1995 interview that Mitnick was not a terrorist or national menace but was a persistent criminal. He acknowledged that he should have disclosed earlier that Mitnick had accessed his email; he said the *Times* then removed him from further direct reporting on the case. He also distanced himself from Miramax’s film development. [Markoff interview](https://www.salon.com/1995/12/30/qamarkoff/) **[S]**

Mitnick’s criminal convictions establish that his conduct was not invented, but they do not independently validate every adjective, technical claim, anonymous-source assertion, or commercial decision in Markoff’s coverage. Conversely, the critical books and interviews do not establish that Markoff fabricated the underlying crimes.

### *Takedown* film litigation

The 2000 film, variously released as *Takedown* or *Track Down*, was based on the Shimomura-Markoff book but dramatized events beyond it. Markoff himself called the movie fundamentally dishonest. Kevin Mitnick settled a defamation-related claim with Miramax on undisclosed terms, and Jonathan Littman pursued separate claims concerning the use of his material. Available reporting does not identify Markoff as the defendant in either reported resolution. [*SFGate*, May 4, 2000](https://www.sfgate.com/business/article/movie-about-notorious-hacker-inspires-a-tangle-of-suits-and-subplots-2782693.php) **[R/S]**

### Hewlett-Packard pretexting

HP’s 2005–06 attempt to identify board leaks included the acquisition of telephone records belonging to nine journalists and some family members through false pretenses. Markoff was among those targeted. HP’s SEC filing and the congressional investigation document the operation; later press-freedom reporting names Markoff. [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47217/000110465906063490/a06-20344_18k.htm), [House hearing](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-109hhrg31472/context), [RCFP](https://www.rcfp.org/hp-obtained-reporters-phone-records-find-sources/) **[P/R]**

Sources conflict about the resulting legal mechanism. The Reporters Committee described the 2008 payment as a negotiated settlement “not the result of a lawsuit,” whereas Berkeley later wrote that Markoff and the *Times* sued. No complaint or docket sufficient to reconcile those descriptions was located. The settlement amount was undisclosed, and some or all of the money was directed to investigative-journalism organizations. **[R; unresolved procedural conflict]**

### The Unabomber, *The Net*, and alleged target profiling

The 2003 Lutz Dammbeck documentary *The Net* examines cybernetics, counterculture, computing, Cold War psychology, and Theodore Kaczynski. Its documented interview subjects include John Brockman, Stewart Brand, Robert W. Taylor, Heinz von Foerster, Chris Waits, Butch Gehring, and David Gelernter. The credits thank John Markoff, but the film record does **not** list him as an interviewee. [UMass DEFA film record](https://www.umass.edu/defa/film/37596/), [contemporary credits review](https://leonardo.info/reviews_archive/nov2006/net_mosher.html), [film transcript and Kaczynski correspondence](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/correspondence-between-theodore-ted-john-kaczynski-and-lutz-dam) **[P/R/C]**

Therefore, the 2026 Hidden AmuraKa video’s claim that Markoff was “interviewed in *The Net*” is **contradicted by the film’s credits and transcript**. John Brockman—not Markoff—is the speaker who asserts in the documentary that Kaczynski probably read the *Times*, that bombing targets had been profiled by Markoff, and that Markoff’s large Gelernter profile likely influenced the bombing. Brockman phrases the causal claim as his belief; no Kaczynski document in the record cited by the film proves it. **[C for interviewer identification; A/U for causation]**

The underlying Gelernter chronology is real:

1. Markoff profiled David Gelernter on January 19, 1992.
2. Kaczynski mailed Gelernter a bomb in June 1993.
3. An official victimology report noted the Markoff article.

But the same official case material did not establish that the article caused the selection. The FBI’s retrospective history says victims were selected through library research and describes the case’s broader pattern; it does not credit Markoff’s profile as a causal trigger. [FBI/ATF/Postal Inspection victimology material](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/batf-fbi-uspis-unabom-major-case-75-00-san-francisco), [FBI history](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/unabomber) **[P]**

A systematic exact-name search for the other identified Unabomber victims—including Buckley Crist Jr., Percy Wood, Diogenes Angelakos, John Hauser, Hugh Scrutton, Gary Wright, James McConnell, Charles Epstein, Thomas Mosser, and Gilbert Murray—did not locate comparable pre-attack Markoff profiles. This is an absence-of-evidence finding, not proof that no article, mention, or unpublished research ever existed. On the record found, **Gelernter is the sole verified example** supporting the plural claim that Markoff profiled Kaczynski’s targets.

Brockman’s later online archive also claimed Markoff deduced that Kaczynski lived in Montana and drew a relationship between targets and *Wired* readership. No independent contemporaneous record was found confirming that Markoff made or substantiated such a deduction. [Brockman archive](https://www.edge.org/jb/online.digital.archive.html) **[A/U]**

A further source of distortion is the name **Charles Epstein**, a University of California geneticist injured by a Kaczynski bomb in June 1993. He was not Jeffrey Epstein. The federal indictment names Charles Epstein and David Gelernter as separate bomb recipients. No familial or operational link between Charles Epstein and Jeffrey Epstein was found. [DOJ indictment](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/1996/Jun96/283crm.htm) **[P/C]**

## 7. Reception and Afterlife

Markoff’s public image has moved through several phases:

1. **Labor and military-industry critic, 1970s–80s.** His early work emphasized surveillance, defense dependence, pollution, and worker health.

2. **Interpreter of computer outlaws, 1988–96.** The Morris and Mitnick stories made him a central mass-media narrator of computer intrusion. *Cyberpunk* was generally treated as accessible social reporting; *Takedown* became the enduring conflict point because the reporter, security expert, criminal pursuit, and media-rights market converged.

3. **Historian of network culture, 1993–2005.** His early Web reporting and *What the Dormouse Said* helped popularize a genealogy connecting defense-funded research, counterculture, and personal computing.

4. **Critic of surveillance and automation, 2002–15.** His Total Information Awareness and robotics reporting complicated any portrayal of him as an uncritical insider. *Machines of Loving Grace* recast AI as a field of political and design choices.

5. **Institutional historian and biographer, 2017 onward.** Museum oral histories and *Whole Earth* moved him from daily reporting toward preservation and synthetic history. Reception of the Brand biography divided between praise for access and criticism of sympathy toward its subject.

6. **Conspiracy-network node, especially after Epstein releases.** Later online narratives treat Edge as a single “cybernetic elite” and infer covert coordination from invitations, shared dinners, agent relationships, and correspondence. The released documents do establish real network adjacency and limited Epstein contact; they do not establish the larger covert program alleged.

The 2000 *Takedown* film strengthened Mitnick’s counter-narrative while alienating Markoff, who repudiated the adaptation’s honesty. Dammbeck’s *The Net* later drew his Gelernter article into a Kaczynski/cybernetics argument while thanking—but not interviewing—him. The Hidden AmuraKa video subsequently appears to have converted that credited assistance and Brockman’s onscreen statements into the inaccurate assertion that Markoff himself was interviewed.

No retraction of Markoff’s Gelernter profile, Morris-worm identification, or core Mitnick reporting was found. The record does contain Markoff’s acknowledgment that he should have disclosed sooner that Mitnick had read his email, and his own denunciation of the film adaptation.

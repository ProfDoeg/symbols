# The Four Luminaries (Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, Eleleth): Research Dossier

## Evidentiary synopsis

**Class:** A collective of four superhuman powers—Greek *phōstēres*, “luminaries/lights”—classified variously as aeons, great lights, angels, celestial places, or rulers of aeonic regions. In the texts conventionally classified as “Sethian,” they belong to the divine Fullness (*Pleroma*) and attend the Self-Generated One (*Autogenes*), often identified with Christ. In later Egyptian ritual and homiletic texts, some or all become powerful Christian angels detached from the complete Sethian system.

**Earliest secure attestation:** Irenaeus of Lyons, *Against Heresies* 1.29, written approximately **174–189 CE**, preserves a hostile but recognizably cognate account in which four luminaries attend Autogenes. The surviving Latin tradition gives their names as **Harmogenes/Armogenes, Raguel, David, and Eleleth**. This is earlier than every extant manuscript containing their standard Coptic names. Irenaeus says the information came from people he calls “Gnostics,” but his report does not establish what they called themselves or whether they possessed the surviving *Apocryphon of John* in its present form. [Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.29](https://openscriptorium.com/books/irenaeus-plain)

**Earliest physical witnesses:** Fourth-century Coptic papyrus manuscripts in the Nag Hammadi codices, especially NHC II, III, IV, VIII, and XIII. The codices were discovered near Nag Hammadi in 1945 and are now principally in Cairo’s Coptic Museum. The underlying works were translated from Greek and are normally dated substantially earlier than the surviving copies. [Coptic Museum](https://egymonuments.gov.eg/en/subportals-group/coptic-museum/coptic-museum/manuscripts)

### Evidentiary labels

- **[EA] Secure early attestation:** second- or early-third-century testimony or a securely relevant early manuscript.
- **[CE] Later canonical elaboration:** “canonical” only within the reconstructed Sethian literary dossier, not within catholic Christianity.
- **[RV] Regional or textual variant.**
- **[LS] Learned systematization:** an organized cosmological, philosophical, or ritual interpretation.
- **[SI] Syncretistic identification or borrowing.**
- **[LA] Late antique or medieval accretion.**
- **[FR] Folklore or popular ritual transmission.**
- **[MSR] Modern scholarly reconstruction.**
- **[MI] Modern invention or modern religious reception.**
- **[NE] No evidence located.**

“Gnostic” and “Sethian” are retained as practical scholarly categories. They are not treated as demonstrated ancient self-designations for every author or manuscript owner.

## 1. Identification, names, and epithets

### 1.1 Collective identity

The Four Luminaries occupy the lowest major division of the transcendent divine world beneath, or within the manifestation of, the Invisible Spirit, Barbelo, and Autogenes. They “stand before” Autogenes; organize the Pleroma into four ordered stations; receive Adamas, Seth, Seth’s incorruptible offspring, and other saved or eventually repentant souls; and in several texts provide the architectural setting for ascent, baptism, and postmortem transformation. They are therefore simultaneously:

1. personal superhuman beings;
2. aeons or divine attributes;
3. rulers of aeons;
4. the aeonic places themselves.

The texts do not consistently distinguish these senses. Translating them simply as “four angels” loses their spatial and metaphysical character; translating them simply as “realms” loses their speech and agency.

### 1.2 Collective terminology

- Greek **φωστήρ**, *phōstēr*, plural *phōstēres*: luminary, light-giver, celestial light. This is the term behind the Coptic usage.
- Coptic forms conventionally translated **“great lights”** or **“luminaries.”**
- **Aeons:** especially where each luminary heads, inhabits, or is identified with an eternal realm.
- **Angels:** Irenaeus presents attendant emanations, while the *Apocryphon of John* calls Harmozel the “first angel.” The *Gospel of Judas* describes four angels attending the angelic Autogenes.
- **Commanders-in-chief:** *Melchizedek* reportedly applies Greek *archistratēgos* to them, language later common for archangels such as Michael. This is a late-antique angelological accommodation, not evidence that the earliest system called all four archangels.

### 1.3 Individual names and variants

The original Greek spellings are lost; the names survive through Coptic transliteration and Irenaeus’s Greek work as transmitted principally in Latin.

| Standard scholarly form | Important variants | Earliest form | Observations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harmozel** | Armozel, Harmozēl, Harmosiel, Harmogenes, Armogenes; perhaps Hermosel in ritual texts | Irenaeus’s *Harmogenes/Armogenes*, c. 180 | First luminary. Modern derivations such as “high fortress of God” are speculative and should not be presented as translations. |
| **Oroiael** | Oroiel, Oriael, Oriel, Oraiael; Irenaeus’s Raguel | Irenaeus’s *Raguel*, c. 180 | Second luminary. Assimilation to **Uriel/Oriel** is plausible in transmission but not a secure original identity. |
| **Daveithai** | Daveithe, Davithe, Daviethe, Daueithel, Taveithe; Irenaeus’s David | Irenaeus’s *David*, c. 180 | Third luminary. Relation to biblical **David** is probable at least in reception: later ritual texts give Daveithe a lyre and musical/heavenly-concert functions. Whether the Sethian name originated from David remains disputed. |
| **Eleleth** | Elēlēth, Elelēth, Elethe; proposed restoration “[Eleleth]” in *Gospel of Judas* | Irenaeus’s *Eleleth*, c. 180 | Fourth luminary and by far the most narratively active. Semitic-looking repetition of *El* has encouraged etymologies, but no ancient source supplies a translation. |

The shared or frequent **-el** ending suggests Semitic angelic nomenclature, but the names are “not perfectly translucent to etymological analysis.” Modern internet glosses—Harmozel as “fortress of God,” Oroiael as “light of the Lord God,” Daveithai as “beloved of Yah,” and Eleleth as “God first and last”—lack ancient lexical testimony and are **[MI/MSR, unestablished]**. The only defensible claim is that several names were formed, altered, or heard within a Semitic and biblical angelological environment.

### 1.4 Names in Irenaeus and the problem of identity

Irenaeus’s **Harmogenes, Raguel, David, Eleleth** closely match the later sequence, functions, and placement. The correspondence is too exact to dismiss, but it raises textual questions:

- Harmogenes may be corruption, translation, or adaptation of Harmozel.
- Raguel is a familiar Jewish angelic name and may have replaced an unfamiliar Oroiael.
- David may be the transparent form behind Daveithe/Daveithai.
- Eleleth remains stable.

Accordingly, Irenaeus is the earliest secure witness to the **collective pattern**, even though three names differ from their later standard spellings.

## 2. Earliest attestation and textual corpus

### 2.1 Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.29

**Language and date:** Composed in Greek, c. 174–189 CE; complete chiefly in an ancient Latin translation.

**What it says:** Light/Christ and Incorruptibility produce four luminaries to attend Autogenes. Four further powers—Grace, Will, Understanding, and Prudence—serve them. Autogenes produces the perfect Human, Adamas, who is placed with the first luminary. Irenaeus’s surviving names are Harmogenes, Raguel, David, Eleleth.

**What it does not establish:** It does not prove that Irenaeus read one of the four surviving recensions of the *Apocryphon of John*. His chapter is an external polemical digest and may represent a related mythic source. **[EA]**

### 2.2 *Apocryphon/Secret Book of John*

**Witnesses:** Four Coptic manuscripts:

- NHC II,1 and IV,1: long recension;
- NHC III,1 and Berlin Codex 8502,2: short recension.

The short recension survives in two different Coptic translations; the two long witnesses represent essentially the same translation. The Coptic titles also differ, showing genuine textual plurality rather than mere scribal noise. [Four-witness collection](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/nhl_sbj.htm); [study of Coptic titles](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/13/9/796)

**Composition:** Normally placed in the second century or early third century in stages; all physical witnesses are fourth- or early-fifth-century Coptic copies of Greek originals. Whether the long recension expanded a shorter work or the short form abridged a longer one remains debated.

**Luminary system:** The fullest surviving basic arrangement is:

| Luminary | Associated triad | Inhabitant/class |
|---|---|---|
| Harmozel | Grace, Truth, Form | Geradamas/Adamas, the perfect Human |
| Oroiael | Afterthought/Conception, Perception, Memory | Seth |
| Daveithai | Understanding, Love, Idea | Seed of Seth; souls of the saints |
| Eleleth | Perfection, Peace, Sophia/Wisdom | Those initially ignorant of the Pleroma who later repent |

These are “the twelve aeons” before the child of Autogenes: four luminaries, each associated with three aeonic powers. The text can count the head luminary together with its triad as a fourfold aeon, while also calling the twelve subordinate qualities aeons. [Marvin Meyer translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/apocjn-meyer.html); [short recension](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/apocjn-short.html)

### 2.3 *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit* / Coptic *Gospel of the Egyptians*

**Witnesses:** NHC III,2 and IV,2, fourth-century Coptic manuscripts of a Greek work usually dated to the second or third century. This is not Clement of Alexandria’s “Greek Gospel of the Egyptians.”

**Role:** The text greatly elaborates the celestial administration. The four lights are born within the Autogenes complex and acquire consorts and ministers:

- Harmozel — minister **Gamaliel**;
- Oroiael — minister **Gabriel**;
- Daveithe — minister **Samblo/Samlo**;
- Eleleth — minister **Abrasax**.

Memory, Love, Peace, and Eternal Life also emerge within this expanded scheme. Adamas belongs in Harmozel, Seth in Oroiael, Seth’s offspring in Daveithe, and the souls of the offspring rest in Eleleth.

After five thousand years, Eleleth says, in effect, “Let one reign over Chaos and Hades”; the damaged sequel produces **Sakla** and **Nebruel**. This shifts demiurgic causation away from a straightforward solitary fault of Sophia. [Text](https://tianmu.org/good-work-library/gnostic/nag-hammadi-library/codex-iv/the-gospel-of-the-egyptians)

### 2.4 *Zostrianos*

**Witness:** NHC VIII,1, a badly damaged Coptic manuscript of a Greek ascent apocalypse. The work is commonly dated c. 200–300, with substantial debate over layers and its relationship to Platonism.

**Role:** The visionary ascends through the Autogenes aeon and the higher Barbelo configurations. The four luminaries appear with expanded compound names:

- Harmozel and associated names now partly lost;
- Oroiael, likewise damaged;
- Daveithe–Laraneus–Epiphanios–Eideos;
- Eleleth–Kodere–Epiphanios–Allogenios.

They interact with different classes of ascending or postmortem souls. *Zostrianos* turns the earlier genealogical map into a highly technical metaphysics of ascent and contemplative transformation. [John Turner translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/zostr-jdt.html)

### 2.5 *Trimorphic Protennoia* / *Three Forms of First Thought*

**Witness:** NHC XIII,1, preserved by leaves inserted into another codex; Coptic translation of a Greek work, normally dated to the late second or third century.

**Role:** The luminaries occur in expanded triadic nomenclature:

- Armedon–Nousanios–Harmozel;
- Phaionios–Ainios–Oroiael;
- Mellephaneus–Loios–Daveithai;
- Mousanios–Amethes–Eleleth.

The text then says that a word came from Eleleth: “I am King! Who belongs to Chaos and who belongs to the underworld?” The radiance of Eleleth appears, and the formless demon Saklas/Samael/Yaldabaoth manifests. Sophia or the Epinoia of light has previously descended and is described as the source from which Yaldabaoth came. Thus it is too simple to say that Eleleth single-handedly “created” the demiurge: Eleleth’s word precipitates or authorizes his appearance, while the text retains Sophia/Epinoia as his prior source. [Annotated translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/trimorph-JDT.html)

### 2.6 *Hypostasis of the Archons* / *Reality of the Rulers*

**Witness:** NHC II,4, c. fourth century; Greek composition probably second or third century.

**Role:** Norea resists an attempted sexual assault by the archons and invokes the supreme God. Eleleth is sent, rescues her, identifies himself as “the great angel” or sagacity standing before the Holy Spirit, and reveals the origin and fate of the rulers. He explains Sophia, Yaldabaoth, the repentance and enthronement of Sabaoth, Norea’s true kinship with the upper world, and the eventual coming of the True Human.

The work does not deploy the complete fourfold system in the way *Apocryphon of John* does. Dylan Burns argues that references connecting Eleleth explicitly to the Four Luminaries and Invisible Spirit may be glosses added to an originally independent Norea–angel revelation. The work’s classification as “Sethian” is consequently disputed; Tuomas Rasimus emphasizes its “Ophite” affinities. [Claremont Coptic Encyclopedia](https://ccdl.claremont.edu/digital/api/collection/cce/id/1001/download)

### 2.7 *Gospel of Judas*

**Witness:** Codex Tchacos 3, a Coptic manuscript probably copied in the fourth century; the Greek work existed by c. 180, when Irenaeus mentions a Gospel of Judas, although identity with the surviving text is highly probable rather than mechanically provable.

Four angels attend the Self-Generated One. On the damaged page 51, **Eleleth** has been proposed as a restoration; the speaker calls twelve angels to rule Chaos and Hades, followed by Nebro and Saklas. Because the name is restored in a lacuna, Eleleth’s presence must be labeled **[RV, probable but not certain]**. Scholars disagree whether the work represents primitive Sethian material, a late deformation of it, or a neighboring but distinct tradition. [Discussion summarized in Burns](https://libraryoflilith.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Magical_Coptic_Christian_The_Great_Angel.pdf)

### 2.8 *Melchizedek*

NHC IX,1, third-century composition in a fourth-century manuscript. The fragmentary work names the four and calls them *archistratēgoi*, “commanders-in-chief.” They participate in the heavenly and baptismal administration. **[CE/LA]**

### 2.9 Texts sometimes grouped nearby but without secure appearances

- *On the Origin of the World* parallels the cosmology behind *Hypostasis*, including Sophia, Yaldabaoth, and Sabaoth, but does not provide a secure complete Four-Luminary list. **[NE for the collective]**
- *Gospel of Truth*, *Tripartite Tractate*, and Clement’s *Excerpta ex Theodoto* are primarily Valentinian witnesses and do not attest this named tetrad. **[NE]**
- *Allogenes* shares the transcendent Barbelo/Triple-Powered metaphysics but does not securely name the four standard luminaries. **[NE]**
- *Eugnostos*, *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth*, and *Corpus Hermeticum* I (*Poimandres*) supply comparisons for heavenly humanity, intellect, generation, and ascent, not direct attestations. **[NE]**
- *Pistis Sophia* and the *Books of Jeu* preserve different late-antique celestial and ritual systems. Similar names or ritual structures do not establish the standard tetrad there. **[NE unless a specific manuscript reading is demonstrated]**
- Hippolytus, Epiphanius, and Tertullian transmit valuable polemical information about groups called Gnostics or Valentinians, but they do not improve upon Irenaeus’s direct evidence for this collective.
- Mandaean and Manichaean scriptures contain structurally comparable worlds of light, savior powers, and ordered celestial companies but do not attest Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, and Eleleth as their own tetrad.

## 3. Domain, attributes, and iconography

### 3.1 Domains

The luminaries have no terrestrial portfolios comparable to a civic god of agriculture or war. Their domains are metaphysical:

- ordering and illuminating the Pleroma;
- attending and praising Autogenes;
- housing archetypal humanity;
- receiving classes of saved souls;
- mediating ascent, baptism, and revelation;
- in Eleleth’s exceptional case, rescuing Norea and participating in the manifestation of lower rulers.

Their fixed order is one of their strongest attributes. Harmozel is highest and closest to Adamas; Eleleth is fourth and closest to Sophia, repentance, and the boundary at which the deficient cosmos appears.

### 3.2 Attributes by luminary

**Harmozel:** Grace, Truth, and Form; dwelling of Adamas; first angel/light. Later Coptic ritual art makes Harmozel a winged trumpet-bearing angel and calls him a great ruler. [Yale Peabody Museum](https://echoesofegypt.peabody.yale.edu/overview/coptic-magical-papyrus)

**Oroiael:** Afterthought or conceptualization, perception, memory; dwelling of Seth. No secure ancient individual iconography survives.

**Daveithai:** Understanding, love, idea; dwelling of Seth’s seed. Later texts give Daveithe golden hair, lightning eyes, keys of divinity or heaven, a golden cup or palm, trumpet, ring, and spiritual lyre. These are **late Egyptian Christian ritual developments**, not attributes stated in the early Sethian cosmogonies.

**Eleleth:** Perfection, peace, Sophia; refuge of late repenters or resting souls. Eleleth is a revealer and rescuer in *Hypostasis* and a causal or authorizing speaker at the manifestation of Saklas in other texts. Later amulets treat Eleleth as a healing or protective angel.

### 3.3 Iconography

**[NE]** No surviving ancient Sethian painting, statue, relief, or temple image can be securely identified as a depiction of the four luminaries. Nag Hammadi manuscripts are textual codices, not illustrated cult books.

The first secure image connected with an individual appears in later Coptic ritual material. A Coptic magical papyrus now interpreted by the Yale Peabody Museum depicts **Harmozel as a winged angel blowing a trumpet**, with Coptic letters issuing from the instrument. This belongs to Christian ritual power, not demonstrably to a second-century Sethian cult.

No securely ancient colors, zodiacal signs, planets, weekdays, plants, animals, weapons, or elemental/directional correspondences are assigned to the tetrad. Modern assignments to the four directions or four elements are **[MI]**. A later Coptic prayer places luminaries at the four corners of heaven, but that is not equivalent to the complete modern occult correspondence system.

## 4. Genealogy and relations

### 4.1 Basic hierarchy

The most stable reconstructed hierarchy is:

**Invisible Spirit → Barbelo → Autogenes/Christ → Four Luminaries and their aeonic associates → Adamas, Seth, Seth’s seed, and saved humanity**

This is not biological genealogy. “Emanation,” “production,” “appearance,” “establishment,” and “dwelling” vary among texts.

In the *Apocryphon of John*, the luminaries emerge from the light that is Christ/Autogenes and Incorruptibility through the Spirit’s grace. In the *Gospel of the Egyptians*, the relationships are expanded into ogdoads, consorts, ministers, and liturgical choirs. *Trimorphic Protennoia* contains wording in which Christ establishes the aeons but they also appear to engender him; this may reflect corruption, reciprocal aeonic generation, or redactional seams rather than a consistent alternate genealogy.

### 4.2 Every documented interaction with another named divine being

The following are discrete textual contacts, not inferred associations.

- **Invisible Spirit — all four:** They stand before, praise, and glorify the Invisible Spirit; the Spirit’s will establishes their order. *Apocryphon of John*, second–third century. **[EA/CE]**
- **Barbelo — all four:** They belong to the Barbeloite divine economy, though direct dialogue is not recorded. *Apocryphon of John* and related works. **[CE]**
- **Autogenes/Christ — all four:** They emanate from, attend, stand before, and praise Autogenes; in some formulations they inhabit the Autogenes aeon. Irenaeus 1.29 and *Apocryphon of John*. **[EA]**
- **Incorruptibility — all four:** With Christ/light, Incorruptibility participates in their procession. *Apocryphon of John*. **[CE]**
- **Adamas/Geradamas — Harmozel:** Adamas is stationed in the first aeon with Harmozel and its powers. *Apocryphon of John*; *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[CE]**
- **Seth — Oroiael:** Seth is placed in the second realm with Oroiael. Same sources. **[CE]**
- **Seth’s incorruptible seed — Daveithai:** The seed or offspring of Seth and souls of saints dwell or repose in Daveithai. Same sources. **[CE]**
- **Later repentant souls — Eleleth:** Those ignorant of the Fullness who repent after delay are gathered in Eleleth. *Apocryphon of John*. **[CE]**
- **Souls of Seth’s offspring — Eleleth:** They repose in Eleleth. *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[RV]**
- **Sophia — Eleleth:** Sophia is the third associated aeon in Eleleth’s triad. *Apocryphon of John*. **[CE]**
- **Sophia/Epinoia — Eleleth:** Eleleth’s Epinoia recognizes the lower order sought by Yaldabaoth and petitions for another order; the higher realm assents. *Trimorphic Protennoia*. **[RV]**
- **Yaldabaoth/Saklas/Samael — Eleleth:** Eleleth’s royal word immediately precedes and occasions the demon’s appearance; Sophia/Epinoia nevertheless remains his prior source. *Trimorphic Protennoia*. **[RV]**
- **Sakla — Eleleth:** Eleleth commands that one rule Chaos and Hades; Sakla subsequently appears. *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[RV]**
- **Nebruel — Eleleth:** Nebruel, Sakla’s partner in creation, emerges in the damaged sequel to Eleleth’s command. *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[RV]**
- **Norea — Eleleth:** Eleleth is sent in response to Norea’s cry, rescues her from the rulers, and gives her a revelation concerning her origin and salvation. *Hypostasis of the Archons*. **[RV]**
- **Yaldabaoth — Eleleth:** Eleleth recounts the chief ruler’s origin, blasphemy, punishment, and displacement. *Hypostasis*. **[RV]**
- **Sabaoth — Eleleth:** Eleleth narrates Sabaoth’s repentance, praise of Sophia, and enthronement above the lower powers. *Hypostasis*. **[RV]**
- **Gamaliel — Harmozel:** Gamaliel ministers to the first great light. *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[CE]**
- **Gabriel — Oroiael:** Gabriel ministers to the second light. *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[CE/SI]**
- **Samblo/Samlo — Daveithai:** Samblo ministers to the third light. *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[CE]**
- **Abrasax — Eleleth:** Abrasax ministers to the fourth light. *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[CE/SI]**
- **Yoel/Youel — collective:** A fifth-light or baptismal presidency occurs in the expanded *Gospel of the Egyptians* system, but Yoel does not replace the tetrad. **[CE]**
- **Zostrianos — all four:** The visionary encounters their aeonic regions in his ascent. *Zostrianos*. **[LS]**
- **Four attendant angels — Autogenes:** Four angels come from another cloud to serve the angelic Self-Generated One. *Gospel of Judas* 47. **[RV]**
- **Eleleth — twelve lower angels:** In the proposed restoration of *Gospel of Judas* 51, Eleleth calls twelve angels into being to rule Chaos and Hades. **[RV, restoration uncertain]**
- **Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Souriel and other archangels — Daveithe/Eleleth:** They are jointly invoked in late Coptic spells. **[LA/SI]**
- **Jesus — Eleleth, Daveithe, Harmosiel:** In the *Investiture of Gabriel*, Jesus summons angels who introduce themselves; the luminary names are absorbed into Christian angelology. **[LA]**

**Absence finding:** There is no recorded combat, marriage, sexual union, or rivalry among Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, and Eleleth themselves. Their relationship is ordered collegiality, not narrative drama.

## 5. Major myths and narrative cycles

### 5.1 Procession of the four lights

**Earliest form:** Irenaeus 1.29. Christ/light and Incorruptibility produce four luminaries to attend Autogenes. Four attendant powers accompany them. **[EA]**

**Developed form:** In *Apocryphon of John*, Autogenes has emerged from Barbelo and the Invisible Spirit. Four lights then stand before him, each governing a triadic set of aeons. Together they provide the formal structure in which divine humanity appears.

**Development:** Irenaeus shows that the tetrad existed by the late second century. The Coptic works elaborate names, triads, ministers, inhabitants, and ritual functions. It is not demonstrable that a single founder designed the final system.

### 5.2 The four dwellings of divine humanity

Adamas appears from the foreknowledge of perfect Mind and is placed in Harmozel. Adamas places Seth in Oroiael. Seth’s seed and saintly souls are stationed in Daveithai. Those who initially remain ignorant but later repent are received in Eleleth.

The scheme converts Genesis genealogy into transcendent geography. Earthly humanity is interpreted as a deficient copy of heavenly archetypes, while salvation becomes a return to the proper aeonic dwelling.

The *Gospel of the Egyptians* modifies the last two classes: Seth’s offspring repose in Daveithe, while “souls of the offspring” repose in Eleleth. This should not be harmonized automatically with the repentant class in *Apocryphon of John*.

### 5.3 Eleleth and Norea

In *Hypostasis of the Archons*, the rulers who had violated Eve attempt to assault Norea. She rejects their claim over her, invokes the God of the entirety, and Eleleth descends. He drives the rulers away and tells her that she belongs to the imperishable realm. He then delivers a cosmogonic revelation:

1. Sophia’s activity produces the arrogant ruler;
2. the ruler declares himself sole god;
3. his declaration exposes his ignorance;
4. Sophia’s daughter Zoe rebukes or punishes him;
5. Sabaoth repents, condemns his father, and praises Sophia;
6. Sabaoth is enthroned above the powers of Chaos;
7. Norea and her spiritual offspring will be delivered when the True Human appears.

This is the only surviving extended dialogue in which one luminary acts as a named rescuing revealer. Whether “one of the Four Luminaries” was original to the Norea source or attached by a redactor remains disputed.

### 5.4 Eleleth and the manifestation of the demiurge

Three related but nonidentical texts associate Eleleth with the rise of lower rulers:

- *Trimorphic Protennoia*: a word from Eleleth asks who belongs to Chaos and Hades; Saklas/Samael/Yaldabaoth immediately appears. Sophia/Epinoia remains his earlier source.
- *Gospel of the Egyptians*: after five thousand years Eleleth orders that one reign over Chaos and Hades; Sakla and Nebruel emerge.
- *Gospel of Judas*: if the damaged name is correctly restored, Eleleth commands twelve angels to rule Chaos and Hades, followed by Nebro and Saklas.

This may be a deliberate theological revision that makes cosmic deficiency part of providential order instead of merely Sophia’s uncontrolled mistake. John Turner connects it with a late-second-century “Christianization” of the descent myth and exculpation of Sophia; Dylan Burns accepts the family resemblance but warns that the precise literary history cannot be recovered. **[MSR, disputed]**

### 5.5 Ascent through the luminaries

*Zostrianos* transforms the four stations into stages in a visionary ascent. The soul receives baptisms, seals, names, knowledge, and transformations while rising through Autogenes toward Protophanes and Kalyptos. The luminaries serve as intelligible structures and populations of the heavenly world rather than protagonists in a continuous adventure. This is a learned Platonizing development, probably later than the simpler cosmogonic map. **[LS]**

## 6. Cult, temples, and ritual practice

### 6.1 Temples and priesthood

**[NE]** No temple, altar, priesthood, civic festival, sacrificial tariff, dedicatory inscription, or theophoric personal name can be securely assigned to the Four Luminaries as a collective.

No evidence supports human sacrifice, animal sacrifice, sacred prostitution, cult violence, or state-sponsored worship in their name. Their historical setting is manuscript religion, baptismal speculation, visionary ascent, prayer, and ritual power—not a territorially organized temple cult.

### 6.2 Sethian ritual

Scholars reconstruct a ritual matrix involving:

- baptism and repeated heavenly baptisms;
- the Five Seals;
- invocation of divine names;
- ascent through aeons;
- investiture, robing, enthronement, and transformation;
- liturgical praise by aeonic beings.

Whether every literary baptism describes an enacted community ritual is disputed. Some may be celestial projections, contemplative exercises, or literary metaphors. Turner’s reconstruction of a coherent Sethian baptismal tradition is influential, but it cannot be treated as a recovered service book for a single church. [Turner and Majercik on Sethian baptism](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/TurnerMajercik.pdf)

### 6.3 Coptic ritual-power texts

The strongest evidence for actual invocation comes after the classic Sethian literary period.

- **P.Lond.Or. 6794:** A spell for acquiring a good voice invokes Daveithe and Eleleth. Daveithe lies upon the Tree of Life, bears a golden ring and spiritual lyre, and gathers angels to greet the Father. **[LA]**
- **P.Lond.Or. 5987**, seventh–eighth century: An exorcistic/general-power text invokes “Arimiel Davithe Eleleth…” Daveithe has golden hair, lightning eyes, divine keys, a golden cup, and a trumpet gathering angels and archangels. **[LA]**
- **P.Copt.Mus. 4958:** A Prayer of Mary recension invokes great luminaries including Taveithe, Oriel, and Eleleth at the four corners of heaven. **[LA/SI]**
- **P.Macq. I 1**, later first millennium: Uses Barbeloite and Sethian names but adapts them to practical Christian ritual; Daveithe becomes a celestial gatekeeper with keys and trumpet. **[LA]**
- **P.Med. I 20**, fifth–sixth century: A Greek papyrus amulet places a form of Eleleth among Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, and Uriel. **[LA/SI]**
- **Getty gold amulet**, third or more probably fourth century: Invokes Eleleth with the God of Abraham, Iao Sabaoth, Raphael, Gabriel, and Abrasax to cure Aurelia’s epilepsy. **[EA/LA]**
- **J. Pierpont Morgan M593**, copied 892/893: The *Investiture of Gabriel* presents Eleleth, Daueithel, and Harmosiel as Christian angels; Harmosiel is trumpeter of the aeons of light. **[LA]**

These manuscripts demonstrate living ritual reception but not an unchanged underground Sethian church. Their highly variable lists and Christian framing show adaptation.

## 7. Historical evolution

### c. 150–190: formation and first external witness

A Barbelo–Autogenes myth containing four attendant luminaries was circulating by Irenaeus’s time. Its Jewish scriptural reinterpretation, Christian terminology, Greek metaphysics, and magical/angelic names point to a mixed late-antique intellectual environment. Exact place and founding community are unknown.

### Second–third centuries: literary expansion

The *Apocryphon of John*, *Gospel of the Egyptians*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*, *Hypostasis*, *Zostrianos*, *Melchizedek*, and related writings develop different parts of the system. The four lights become:

- the architecture of the Autogenes aeon;
- dwellings of divine humanity;
- stations of ascent;
- liturgical beings;
- in Eleleth’s case, revealer and mediator at the boundary with Chaos.

This is the peak of their documented mythological elaboration, not evidence for maximum population or institutional power.

### Third–fourth centuries: Platonizing and ritual transformations

*Zostrianos* and related texts reframe the system through Middle- and Neoplatonic metaphysics. At the same time, Eleleth and Daveithe appear in ritual materials outside a complete Sethian theology. This complicates any simple line from “Gnostic doctrine” to “degenerate magic”: the traditions may have overlapped from an early period.

### Fourth–fifth centuries: manuscript preservation

Egyptian scribes copied the Nag Hammadi and Berlin codices. The owners’ identity is disputed: monks, ascetics, educated Christians, ritual specialists, or overlapping networks have all been proposed. The bindings and scribal groupings demonstrate collection and reading, not the precise theology of every owner.

### Fifth–tenth centuries: Christian angelological afterlife

The collective cosmology fades from visible institutional history, while individual names survive in amulets, exorcisms, prayers, homilies, and angelic investiture literature. Daveithe and Eleleth become especially durable. Piwowarczyk’s study shows that the highly standardized Sethian order becomes less stable in ritual texts, while the motif of a celestial concert expands under Christian angelological influence. [Piwowarczyk, 2023](https://brill.com/view/journals/gnos/8/1/article-p102_6.xml)

### Suppression and disappearance

Heresiologists condemned the systems in which the luminaries appeared, but no decree, prosecution, temple destruction, forced conversion, or execution can be tied specifically to their worship. Broader Christian anti-heretical pressure and changing institutions likely contributed to textual marginalization, but claims of a documented campaign against a “cult of the Four Luminaries” would exceed the evidence.

### Medieval to early modern period

The names persisted in Coptic Christian ritual manuscripts into the end of the first millennium. There is no demonstrated continuous European Hermetic, Kabbalistic, Islamic, or Renaissance magical lineage preserving the tetrad intact. **[NE]**

### Modern period

Modern access begins with acquisition and publication of manuscripts, especially Berlin Papyrus 8502 and the Nag Hammadi codices. Translation turned an obscure heresiological list into a reconstructable divine hierarchy. Modern devotional use is a revival based on published texts, not documented unbroken succession.

## 8. Mandaean and Manichaean “cousins”

### 8.1 Mandaean comparison

Mandaean literature knows ordered Worlds of Light populated by *uthras*, and texts refer to “four men, sons of peace” in Qulasta prayers and the *Right Ginza*. Some scholars have proposed comparison with the Sethian luminaries. Mandaean cosmology also includes successive Lives and a lower demiurgic Ptahil. [Study of *Ginza Rabba* creation](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1444088/)

The limits are decisive:

- Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, and Eleleth are not the Mandaean names of these four.
- No surviving Mandaean text identifies the “sons of peace” with the Sethian tetrad.
- The date and transmission of Mandaean compositions make one-way borrowing difficult to prove.
- Structural resemblance—four light beings, heavenly peace, demiurgic descent—is evidence for comparison, not identity.

Thus “Mandaean cousins” is a useful comparative description, **[MSR/SI]**, not an ancient equation.

### 8.2 Manichaean comparison

Manichaeism, founded by Mani in third-century Mesopotamia, possesses a complex World of Light, Father of Greatness, aeons, evoked divine powers, Primal Man, Living Spirit, and cosmic mechanisms for recovering imprisoned light. The Coptic *Kephalaia*, Cologne Mani Codex, Iranian Turfan fragments, Augustine, and al-Nadim’s *Fihrist* document this system.

No Manichaean primary text securely identifies the four Sethian luminaries by name. Similarities include:

- transcendent light opposed to chaotic darkness;
- ordered divine emanations;
- heavenly anthropological archetypes;
- descent and recovery of light;
- prominent numerical groupings.

Differences include Manichaeism’s radical two-principle cosmogony, its own named emanations, missionary church, ethical disciplines, and detailed world-machine. Generic tetrads or light beings must not be converted into a lost Harmozel–Eleleth tradition. [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Manichaean cosmology](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-iii/)

Claims of direct Manichaean worship of the Four Luminaries are **[NE]**.

## 9. Modern reception and reinvention

### 9.1 Scholarship-driven revival

Translations by Carl Schmidt, Walter Till, James M. Robinson’s Nag Hammadi project, Bentley Layton, Michael Waldstein and Frederik Wisse, Marvin Meyer, and others made the names widely available. Modern churches and independent practitioners can therefore reconstruct prayers from published sources.

### 9.2 Occultism

The tetrad is not a standard component of the Renaissance magical corpus, Éliphas Lévi, the original Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, or classical Theosophy. Searches for such links primarily return recent adaptations. **[NE for a pre-Nag-Hammadi occult lineage]**

A **“Gnostic Banishing Ritual of the Four Luminaries,”** published online in 2012, deliberately models a modern rite on ceremonial banishing practice and explicitly acknowledges its improvisation. It assigns the luminaries a protective archangelic function and uses a cross or pentagram framework. This is **[MI]**, not a recovered ancient rite. [Modern ritual](https://jeremypuma.wordpress.com/2012/07/24/gnostic-banishing-ritual-of-the-four-luminaries/)

Modern occult works such as the *Book of Abrasax* and later “Sethian magic” manuals invoke or evoke the four lights. These constitute post-publication religious creativity. Their elemental, directional, planetary, sigil, color, and psychological correspondences require individual modern attribution; they are not supplied by the Nag Hammadi texts.

### 9.3 Modern Gnostic religion

Independent Gnostic churches and solitary practitioners employ the luminaries in prayers, Eucharistic rites, meditation, and ritual ascent. Scale cannot be established reliably, and institutional practice is not uniform. Any claim to direct apostolic or secret organizational succession from a second-century Sethian community is historically unverified.

### 9.4 Literature, games, and popular culture

The names appear sporadically in online fiction, worldbuilding, games, and fandom speculation. Such appearances have not produced a stable mass-media iconography comparable to Sophia or the Demiurge. Modern illustrated forms—four winged archangels, elemental guardians, alien administrators, or Jungian stages—are generally **[MI]**.

### 9.5 Political and extremist appropriation

**[NE]** No significant nationalist, racist, or extremist movement centered specifically on these four was located. Broader “Gnostic” rhetoric sometimes appears in conspiracist or anti-cosmic subcultures, but assigning that material to the historical luminaries without direct names and sources would be misleading.

## 10. Historiography and scholarly debates

### 10.1 Discovery and editions

Berlin Papyrus 8502, purchased in Cairo in 1896, supplied an early Coptic *Apocryphon of John* witness but was not fully published until the twentieth century. The 1945 Nag Hammadi discovery added three more witnesses and the larger comparative corpus. The Coptic texts are edited in the Brill *Coptic Gnostic Library*; Waldstein and Wisse’s synoptic edition is indispensable for the four *Apocryphon* witnesses.

### 10.2 The “Sethian” category

Hans-Martin Schenke defined a cluster through recurring features: Barbelo, Autogenes, Adamas, Seth and his seed, the Four Luminaries, hostile rulers, baptismal ascent, and characteristic prayers. John Turner developed a historical model moving through pre-Christian, Christianized, and Platonizing phases.

Michael Williams and Karen King cautioned that “Gnosticism” can reify polemical and modern taxonomies. The proper conclusion is not that the texts are unrelated, but that “Sethianism” is a scholarly model inferred from literary recurrence, not the securely documented name of one continuous church.

### 10.3 Origin of the names

Howard Jackson argued that some Sethian divine names arose from incantatory *voces magicae*. Dylan Burns emphasizes that Eleleth and Daveithe circulated independently in Egyptian Christian magic. Against a simple magical origin, Irenaeus already presents the four as an organized Barbeloite tetrad around 180. The direction of borrowing cannot be recovered securely.

### 10.4 Eleleth and the Demiurge

Turner reads *Trimorphic Protennoia*, *Gospel of the Egyptians*, and related material as a revision that protects Sophia from culpability and places cosmic descent under providential control. Burns agrees that the pattern is real but questions whether *Hypostasis* preserves the necessary historical bridge. The *Gospel of Judas* evidence depends partly on restoration of damaged text.

### 10.5 Eleleth in *Hypostasis*

Two positions remain viable:

- **Sethian integration:** Eleleth’s identity as one of the Four Luminaries makes the Norea revelation part of a Sethian mythic complex.
- **Composite/gloss theory:** An independent Eleleth–Norea revelation was supplemented with “Four Luminaries” and “Invisible Spirit” language; the text otherwise lacks many defining Sethian features.

The manuscript preserves the final composite, whichever layer came first.

### 10.6 Iranian, Mandaean, and Manichaean theories

Older comparativist scholarship sometimes moved too quickly from shared dualism, tetrads, or light imagery to historical dependence. Later work accepts possible contact in the religious environment of the eastern Roman and Sasanian worlds but demands specific vocabulary, narrative sequence, and transmission mechanisms. No Iranian etymology or Mandaean/Manichaean identity for the four names is established.

### 10.7 Material record

What survives is unusually rich textually but poor archaeologically:

- four *Apocryphon of John* manuscripts;
- two *Gospel of the Egyptians* manuscripts;
- single, often damaged witnesses of several other works;
- heresiological reports;
- ritual papyri, parchment codices, an ostracon tradition, and amulets;
- no securely identified temple or Sethian image cycle.

The evidence therefore supports a history of texts, readers, ritual specialists, and changing angelology—not a conventional cult history.

## 11. Dedicated contested-material findings

### Etymology

Semitic resonance is likely; full translations of the four names are not securely recoverable. Popular Hebrew expansions are **modern conjectures**.

### Origin

The collective is securely attested in a second-century Barbeloite system. Whether individual names originated in Jewish angelology, Egyptian ritual language, invented divine nomenclature, or some combination remains unresolved.

### Syncretism

- Oroiael’s resemblance to Uriel and Irenaeus’s Raguel: probable angelological assimilation, not proven identity.
- Daveithai/David: strong linguistic and later musical association, but the precise development is uncertain.
- Eleleth with ordinary Christian archangels: securely attested in late-antique and medieval ritual texts.
- Four luminaries with the four throne angels or four directions: late antique comparison/adaptation, not the earliest system.
- Mandaean “sons of peace” and Manichaean light powers: comparative parallels only.

### Sacrifice and abusive cult practice

No evidence connects the tetrad with human sacrifice, animal sacrifice, temple prostitution, ritual sexual abuse, or cult warfare. *Hypostasis* depicts attempted sexual violence by hostile archons, which Eleleth prevents; that is mythic narrative, not evidence for enacted ritual.

### Suppression

Christian heresiologists condemned the theology, but no specific suppression event directed at a temple, priesthood, or congregation of the Four Luminaries is documented.

### Occult and neopagan claims

Continuous transmission through Hermeticism, Kabbalah, the Golden Dawn, or pagan witchcraft is unestablished. Modern banishing rites, elemental attributions, and sigils are demonstrably recent constructions.

## 14. Conclusions

The Four Luminaries are first securely visible around 180 CE as an already organized tetrad attending Autogenes. In the surviving Coptic Sethian corpus they become the principal fourfold architecture of the lowest Pleromatic world: Harmozel houses Adamas, Oroiael Seth, Daveithai Seth’s seed, and Eleleth the souls closest to deficiency, delayed repentance, or later restoration.

They are not merely four interchangeable angels. Harmozel, Oroiael, and Daveithai principally organize celestial space; Eleleth crosses the boundary into narrative, rescuing Norea and participating—ambiguously and differently across texts—in the appearance of the demiurgic rulers. Later Egyptian Christianity preserved Eleleth and Daveithe especially as powerful angels of healing, exorcism, music, keys, trumpets, and celestial command.

No temple cult, priesthood, sacrifice, or unbroken institutional succession can be reconstructed. The durable historical record consists of polemic, codices, ascent literature, ritual manuscripts, amulets, and modern revivals. Mandaean and Manichaean light-world systems are illuminating comparanda, but neither tradition identifies its beings with Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, and Eleleth. Modern elemental correspondences, Golden Dawn affiliations, Hebrew “translations,” and banishing rites should be labeled as conjecture or invention rather than ancient teaching.

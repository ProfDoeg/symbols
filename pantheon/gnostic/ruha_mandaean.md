# Ruha (Mandaean): Research Dossier

> **Class:** In Mandaean texts, Ruha is a female personified spirit and cosmic ruler associated primarily with the World of Darkness, the material world, earth, embodied vitality, the planets, and the zodiac. She is normally treated as a demonic or hostile power, not as a goddess worshipped by Mandaeans; some passages, however, make her an ambiguous intermediary capable of revelation, repentance, and eventual redemption.
>
> **Earliest secure attestation:** Mandaic magical texts and incantation bowls attest the religious milieu and some supernatural vocabulary of Mandaeism from approximately the third–fourth centuries CE, but presently available catalogues do not establish that every early bowl naming a *ruha* refers to the individualized cosmic Ruha. The earliest securely dated physical manuscript that unquestionably preserves her developed mythology is therefore Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, Codex Sabéen 1, a *Ginza Rabba* copied in 1560 CE. Colophons and linguistic evidence indicate that substantial portions of the *Ginza*, its liturgies, and their Ruha traditions are many centuries older, with compilation or final redaction of much of the corpus commonly placed in late antiquity and the early Islamic period. The distinction between early composition and late surviving witnesses is essential.

## Evidentiary key

- **[EA] Securely attested early:** late-antique physical or externally datable evidence.
- **[LC] Later canonical:** material in the transmitted Mandaean scriptural or liturgical corpus, whose surviving manuscripts are predominantly sixteenth century or later.
- **[RV] Regional or textual variant:** one recension, tractate, manuscript family, or local tradition against another.
- **[LS] Learned systematization:** priestly, scholastic, astrological, or modern theological ordering of diverse traditions.
- **[SI] Syncretistic identification:** a historically proposed equation or reuse of another tradition’s figure or terminology.
- **[MA] Medieval or early-modern accretion:** material demonstrably belonging to later transmission.
- **[F] Folklore or oral testimony:** reported living or recent tradition.
- **[MSR] Modern scholarly reconstruction:** an analytical proposal rather than an explicit equation in a primary text.
- **[MI] Modern invention:** an equation or narrative absent from the primary record and first demonstrable in modern reception.

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### Basic identity

**[LC]** Ruha is one of the principal powers through whom Mandaean literature explains the mixed and unstable character of embodied existence. In the dominant hostile presentation she rules or leads the forces of darkness, generates the seven planets and twelve zodiacal powers with the dragon Ur, helps confine souls within the world, and embodies the earthly or affective “spirit” that vacillates between the light-derived soul and the body. She is accordingly a cosmic antagonist, mother, seductress, magician, city-builder, and ruler of planetary fate.

**[LC/MSR]** She is not uniformly evil. In *Ginza Rabba* 6 she speaks as the union of opposites—life and death, light and darkness, truth and error, wound and healing. Elsewhere she blesses Hibil Ziwa, reveals cosmological knowledge, recognizes the superiority of the Lightworld, laments her exile, receives the seal of Life, or is seemingly identified with a female being welcomed upward. Jorunn Jacobsen Buckley therefore interprets her as an intrinsically ambiguous “middle” figure rather than an uncomplicated Mandaean Satan. Buckley’s position is a modern synthesis, but it rests on genuine textual anomalies that older summaries often minimized. [Buckley, *The Mandaeans*, pp. 40–48](https://lib.wildla.in/book/The%20Mandeans%20-%20Ancient%20Texts%20and%20Modern%20People%20by%20Jorunn%20Jacobsen%20Buckley.pdf)

### Names and forms

- **ࡓࡅࡄࡀ, *Ruha/Rūhā*** — “spirit,” “breath,” or “wind,” from the common Northwest Semitic root r-w-ḥ. Cognates include Jewish Aramaic and Syriac *rūḥā*, Hebrew *rûaḥ* (רוּחַ), and Arabic *rūḥ* (روح). The etymology is transparent; what is not transparent is when an ordinary noun became this particular personified cosmic being. **[LC/MSR]**

- **Ruha ḏ-Qudša / Rūhā d-Qudšā** — literally “Spirit of Holiness” or “Holy Spirit.” Mandaean contexts usually make this a hostile or polemically devalued title. The phrase is formally cognate with Jewish and Christian Aramaic designations of the Holy Spirit. Buckley calls it “a devalued Christian Holy Spirit,” but “inversion” should not be treated as a proven single act by a known author: the title may preserve several stages of Jewish, Syriac-Christian, and intra-Mandaean polemic. **[LC/SI/MSR]** The Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon recognizes both the Syriac “Holy Spirit” and the specifically Mandaean Ruha as the leading spirit of the dark side. [Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon](https://cal.huc.edu/getlex.php?coord=620461213&word=5)

- **Ruha Qadišta / Qadašta** — a spelling or morphological variant of the “Holy Spirit” title. Proposals connecting *qadišta* to Akkadian *qadištu*, a category of sacred woman, are comparative hypotheses; resemblance does not prove that the Mandaean epithet historically derives from that institution. **[MSR]**

- **Ewat / Hiwat / Ḥiwat** — a name applied to Ruha in the Dinanukht narrative of *Ginza Rabba* 6. There she delivers a first-person revelation embracing opposed cosmic categories. **[LC/RV]**

- **Qin** — normally Ruha’s mother, but certain esoteric texts identify Qin and Ruha with one another. The corpus therefore preserves both a genealogical distinction and a mystical or functional equation. **[LC/RV/LS]**

- **Nitufta, “Drop” or “Cloud” compounds** — titles of a female figure in the *Mandaean Book of John*. Buckley argues that her origin in the world of Gaf and her subsequent invitation into the Lightworld make her a redeemed form or counterpart of Ruha. The text does not explicitly say “this is Ruha”; this remains a strong but contestable reconstruction. **[LC/MSR]**

- **Simat Hiia, “Treasure of Life”** — normally the consort of Yawar Ziwa, but some liturgical passages identify her with Ruha or describe her ascent from darkness in terms resembling Ruha’s rescue. This is a fluctuating textual equation, not a universally stable identity. **[LC/RV/SI]**

- **Libat-Ištar-Amamit** — a compound identification reported in *Ginza* materials: *Libat* is Venus, *Ištar* the Mesopotamian goddess, and *Amamit* a destructive or infernal name. This is the strongest textual ground for associating Ruha with the Venus–Ishtar complex. It does not justify equating every appearance of Libat or Ishtar with Ruha. **[LC/SI]**

- **Earth, “the Left,” and the earthly spirit** — functional rather than personal names. Esoteric expositions identify Ruha externally with earth and internally with the unstable vital or emotional spirit between body and soul. **[LC/LS]**

### Classification problems

Calling Ruha a “goddess” is potentially misleading. She has cosmic sovereignty, offspring, sexuality, and relationships comparable to those of ancient goddesses, but Mandaeans do not offer her cult. “Female demon” catches the dominant polemical valuation but misses her role as the breath enabling earthly life. “Fallen Sophia” is Buckley’s comparative category, not an attested Mandaean title. “Holy Spirit” is linguistically exact but theologically liable to erase the Mandaean inversion.

The most evidence-sensitive description is: **a female personified cosmic Spirit, normally classified among the rulers or powers of darkness, but also functioning as the indispensable intermediary between darkness, material creation, human vitality, and possible redemption.**

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### Physical evidence and dating

**[EA]** The oldest datable Mandaic materials are late-antique magical inscriptions, especially incantation bowls from Sasanian Mesopotamia, broadly third–seventh centuries CE. They demonstrate an early Mandaic-speaking ritual environment and share formulas and supernatural vocabulary with later Classical Mandaic writings. They do not supply a continuous, securely dated copy of the developed *Ginza* Ruha cycle. Encyclopaedia Iranica dates the oldest clearly datable Mandaean sources to approximately the third–fourth centuries and emphasizes that no consensus exists on source division or redaction. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “The Mandaean Religion”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mandaeans/mandaeans-ii-the-mandaean-religion/)

The manuscript gap is large:

- Bodleian Marsh 691, prayers copied at Ḥuweiza in 1529–30, is the earliest known substantial Mandaic codex.
- BnF Codex Sabéen 1, copied in 1560, is the oldest dated surviving *Ginza Rabba*.
- BnF Codex Sabéen 12, copied in 1570, is the earliest surviving *Sidra ḏ-Nišmata*.
- BnF Codex Sabéen 8, copied in 1631, is the earliest surviving *Mandaean Book of John*.
- BnF Codex Sabéen 26, copied in 1797–98, is the earliest presently known copy of the *Book of the Zodiac*.

These dates concern surviving artifacts, not necessarily composition. [Morgenstern, “A New Mandaic Dictionary,” manuscript survey](https://www.gorgiaspress.com/images/uploaded/Gorgias%20Open%20Repository/978-1-4632-0608-6_QAOK.pdf)

### The *Ginza Rabba*

**Language:** Classical Mandaic, an Eastern Aramaic language.  
**Form:** composite scripture in two opposed parts, Right Ginza (*Ginza Yamina*) and Left Ginza (*Ginza Smala*).  
**Composition:** heterogeneous late-antique materials; collection or major redaction commonly placed in the early Islamic period, perhaps the mid-seventh century.  
**Earliest complete physical witnesses:** sixteenth century.

Ruha is especially prominent in:

- **Right Ginza 3:** creation of the material world; Ruha’s relations with Ur and Ptahil; birth of the Seven; formation of humanity; the planets’ attempt to retain Adam.
- **Right Ginza 5.1:** Hibil Ziwa’s descent through the worlds of darkness; deception, removal, confinement, and partial reassurance of Ruha; Ur’s birth and defeat.
- **Right Ginza 6:** Dinanukht’s vision; Ruha/Ewat’s proclamation that she embodies cosmic opposites.
- **Right Ginza 8:** warning against Ruha and the lower powers.
- **Right Ginza 15 and related Jerusalem material:** Ruha builds Jerusalem and confronts Anuš Uthra.
- **Other Ginza passages:** Ruha as pattern of Eve, seducer of the younger Adam, mother of planets and zodiac, earth, and antagonist or reluctant recipient of gnosis.

**[LC]** The *Ginza* is not the work of one author. Contradictions concerning Ruha are therefore evidence of textual plurality, not mistakes that a dossier should repair. Buckley describes GR 3 as a large poetic creation account and places Ruha and Ptahil at its creative center. [Buckley, *The Mandaeans*, introduction and Ruha chapter](https://lib.wildla.in/book/The%20Mandeans%20-%20Ancient%20Texts%20and%20Modern%20People%20by%20Jorunn%20Jacobsen%20Buckley.pdf)

### The *Mandaean Book of John*

**Language:** Classical Mandaic with sections exhibiting later linguistic features.  
**Surviving witness:** earliest known manuscript BnF CS 8, 1631.  
**Modern critical edition:** Charles G. Häberl and James F. McGrath, *The Mandaean Book of John: Critical Edition, Translation, and Commentary* (2019/2020).

The *Book of John* does not merely duplicate the *Ginza*. Its Ruha-relevant material includes:

- Manda d-Hiia’s descent to the world of Gaf.
- The female Nitufta figure who waits beyond the walls of darkness and is invited upward by the Life.
- polemics involving the Holy Spirit, Jesus, Jerusalem, and rival religious communities.
- traditions in which Jesus is linked negatively to the lower cosmic or planetary establishment.

Buckley regards the Nitufta story as a possible conclusion to Ruha’s abandoned state in *Ginza* 5.1. That identification must remain marked **[MSR]**, because the surviving text does not make it explicit. [Häberl and McGrath edition record and downloadable text](https://digitalcommons.butler.edu/facsch_papers/1065/)

### *Qulasta* or Canonical Prayerbook

**[LC]** Drower’s *Canonical Prayerbook of the Mandaeans* (1959) contains more than 400 numbered prayers drawn from several older collections. Colophons associate some liturgical units with the third-century copyist Zazai of Gawazta, but the surviving copies are much later.

Ruha appears particularly in:

- **Prayer 75:** her lament from darkness and address to the divine father, located within a sequence in which subdued powers praise the Light.
- **Prayer 376:** Simat Hiia rises from darkness; Buckley reads the passage in relation to traditions equating her with Ruha.
- baptismal and mortuary texts presupposing the opposition and interdependence of body, *ruha*, and *nišimta*.

Ruha is not the recipient of Mandaean worship in these prayers. Liturgical references subordinate, repel, transform, or explain her.

### *Alf Trisar Šuialia* (“1,012 Questions”)

**[LC/LS]** This priestly-esoteric compilation gives some of the most systematic accounts of Ruha:

- Ruha or Qin represents darkness against Hibil’s light, yet both voices can degrade or elevate.
- light and darkness emerge from one Mystery.
- the body requires the sign of darkness before baptism can impress the sign of Life.
- Ruha is earth and the human vital spirit.
- the soul’s superiority is deliberately limited by the presence of the earthly spirit.
- Ruha and her creatures can receive the seal of Life.

This is priestly systematization, not necessarily the earliest layer of the mythology.

### *Haran Gawaita*

**[LC]** The migration-history known as the *Haran Gawaita* mentions Hibil removing powers from darkness and preserves polemical accounts of Jerusalem, Judaism, Christianity, and Mandaean origins. Its historical narrative cannot be read as a straightforward chronicle. Where it intersects with Ruha mythology, it supports the removal of a darkness-power necessary for later creation and supplies a setting for anti-Jerusalem polemic.

### The *Book of the Zodiac*

**[LC/MA/LS]** The *Asfar Malwašia* arranges planets, zodiacal signs, days, elements, destinies, and secret names for priestly astrological use. Its earliest known manuscript is late eighteenth century, although portions are linguistically or conceptually older. Ruha’s offspring supply the hostile or ambivalent astral structure within which horoscopy operates. Mandaeans’ ritual use of astrology does not mean that they worship Ruha or the planets.

### Mandaic magical texts

**[EA]** Bowls and lead amulets invoke, bind, repel, or manipulate spirits, liliths, planetary forces, and named beings. They are indispensable for reconstructing the late-antique environment of Mandaean demonology. Yet magical texts vary in sectarian specificity; a bowl written in Mandaic script is not automatically a doctrinally Mandaean scripture.

### Nag Hammadi and other “Gnostic” comparanda

None of the following directly attests Mandaean Ruha by that name:

- *Apocryphon of John*, long and short recensions
- *Hypostasis of the Archons*
- *On the Origin of the World*
- *Gospel of Truth*
- *Tripartite Tractate*
- *Gospel of the Egyptians*
- *Zostrianos*
- *Allogenes*
- *Trimorphic Protennoia*
- *Eugnostos*
- *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth*
- Berlin Codex
- *Pistis Sophia*
- Books of Jeu
- *Gospel of Judas*

**[EA/comparative]** These Coptic works preserve independently developed Sethian, Valentinian, Hermetic, and other late-antique cosmologies. Their Sophia, Barbelo, Protennoia, Eve, archons, rulers, and fallen or divided female principles offer comparative structures. They are not episodes from Ruha’s biography. The Nag Hammadi manuscripts were found in 1945 and contain more than fifty texts in fourth-century Coptic codices, usually translated from earlier Greek compositions. [Claremont Nag Hammadi Archive](https://ccdl.claremont.edu/digital/collection/nha)

The strongest comparison is between Ruha and a **fallen or lower Sophia**:

- both are feminine intermediaries;
- both become entangled in lower creation;
- both combine error with residual knowledge of the upper world;
- both may be restored.

But the Mandaean corpus lacks a direct equivalent to the familiar Sophia “fall” narrative explaining how Ruha first entered darkness. Buckley explicitly treats that absence as significant.

### Heresiologists

Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Epiphanius, Tertullian, and Clement preserve hostile but valuable reports about Valentinian, Sethian, Ophite, and related systems. They do not securely name Mandaean Ruha. Their accounts may illuminate the wider late-antique repertoire from which female Wisdom, Spirit, archons, planets, and hostile creators were constructed. Using them to fill gaps in Ruha’s story would be **[MSR]**, not primary Mandaean evidence.

### Hermetic material

The *Poimandres* of the *Corpus Hermeticum* presents Mind, cosmic governors, planetary spheres, Nature, and an androgynous human’s entanglement in material generation. It is structurally relevant to Ruha’s planetary and anthropological role but does not mention or identify her. Any equation between Ruha and Hermetic Nature is comparative, not attested.

### Manichaean material

The *Kephalaia*, Cologne Mani Codex, Turfan fragments, Coptic Manichaean codices, Augustine’s anti-Manichaean works, and al-Nadim’s *Fihrist* preserve a related but distinct religion. The Coptic *Kephalaia* survive mainly in early fifth-century papyrus codices; the genre probably originated in Mani’s community in the late third or early fourth century. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Kephalaia”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kephalaia/)

Manichaean Matter, Darkness, Desire, the female powers involved in mixture, and planetary machinery are legitimate comparanda. No examined primary Manichaean witness establishes that “Ruha” was a Manichaean goddess or that Manichaeans adopted the full Mandaean Ruha narrative. “Manichaean cousin” is therefore a comparative description, not an identity.

## 3. Domain, Attributes, and Iconography

### Domains

**[LC]** Ruha’s domains include:

- the World of Darkness or an intermediate lower realm;
- earth and material fertility;
- embodied breath and vital-affective spirit;
- sexuality, desire, generation, mourning, and emotional instability;
- magic, mirrors, secret knowledge, deception, and revelation;
- the seven planetary powers;
- the twelve zodiacal powers;
- Jerusalem and hostile religious-political order;
- the *matarta*, or astral toll-stations obstructing the soul;
- the mixture of light and darkness necessary for temporary embodied life.

Ruha is not simply “matter.” Ptahil is the more usual demiurgic craftsman, while Ruha supplies forces, substance, fertility, opposition, and astral government. She can assist, rival, or undermine him.

### Human anthropology

**[LC/LS]** Mandaean anthropology often distinguishes:

- *pagra* — body;
- *ruha* — vital, emotional, or earthly spirit;
- *nišimta* — light-derived soul.

The individualized cosmic Ruha and the human *ruha* overlap conceptually but are not always grammatically identical. Esoteric texts exploit the overlap: cosmic Ruha becomes the macrocosmic form of the unstable spirit in each embodied person.

Buckley’s formulation is concise: externally Ruha is earth; internally she is the middle element wavering between body and soul. Without that middle element the body cannot live long enough to receive baptism and liberation.

### Form and gender

Ruha is predominantly female: mother, daughter, sister, bride, sexual partner, seductress, and female pattern for Eve. Yet in the Dinanukht revelation she calls herself an exalted preexistent “man.” **[LC/RV]** This may express transcendence of ordinary gender, textual borrowing from a revelatory discourse, or her claim to encompass opposites. It prevents reduction of her identity to grammatical femininity alone.

### Emblems and substances

Attested associations include:

- a magic mirror showing upper and lower worlds;
- bitterness and jewel (*mrara u-gimra*), powers stolen from darkness;
- the cosmic egg or *hilbuna*;
- black or turbid water;
- earth;
- Venus/Libat in some identifications;
- the left side or left-hand sign;
- seven and twelve as the numbers of her astral offspring;
- gates, walls, and pillars of Jerusalem;
- darkness, cloud, fog, and subterranean depth.

### Iconography

No securely identified ancient statue, relief, temple image, or manuscript portrait of Ruha is presently established. **Absence of evidence is important:** Mandaeism is textually and ritually rich but not organized around anthropomorphic cult statues. Modern illustrations presenting Ruha as a horned demon, winged goddess, Lilith, or dark Sophia are artistic interpretations unless tied to a documented manuscript image.

The Mandaean *drabša*, baptismal banner, is a sign of the Lightworld, not Ruha’s emblem. Likewise, planetary diagrams in astrological manuscripts represent cosmological structures and do not necessarily constitute devotional images of their mother.

## 4. Genealogy and Relations

### Genealogical variants

#### Darkness-family version

**[LC]**

- **Mother:** Qin.
- **Brother and intended husband:** Gaf.
- **Child by Gaf:** Ur, the dragon or Leviathan-like monster.
- **Partner/son in subsequent incestuous generation:** Ur.
- **Children by Ur:** the Seven planets and Twelve zodiacal signs.

This is the most coherent reconstruction of *Ginza* 5.1 and 3, but other summaries call Ur Ruha’s consort without foregrounding that he is also her son.

#### Primordial or Lightworld-origin version

**[LC/RV/MSR]** Hibil’s claims that Ruha’s true parents live above, her yearning for them, her possession of light-like knowledge, and traditions of her eventual return suggest an origin beyond the infernal family she knows. The text leaves open whether Hibil speaks truth or deceives her. Buckley reconstructs a suppressed or incomplete Lightworld origin; the corpus supplies no surviving birth narrative that resolves it.

#### Identity-with-Qin version

**[LC/LS]** Some esoteric texts collapse mother and daughter: Qin is Ruha and Ruha is Qin. This is incompatible with a literal family tree but intelligible as a functional equation of Darkness and Spirit.

#### Simat Hiia/Nitufta versions

**[LC/RV/MSR]** Liturgical identification with Simat Hiia and Buckley’s proposed identification with Nitufta imply a redeemed or upper double. These should be placed alongside, not substituted for, the darkness genealogy.

### Every documented divine interaction

The following list separates explicit episodes from scholarly equations:

- **Qin — mother/daughter and occasional identity.** Qin prepares Ruha’s marriage to Gaf; in esoteric passages Qin and Ruha are identified. *Ginza Rabba* 5.1; *1,012 Questions*. **[LC/RV/LS]**

- **Gaf — sibling, intended spouse, and father of Ur.** Ruha is pregnant by Gaf when Hibil removes and confines her. *Ginza Rabba* 5.1. **[LC]**

- **Hibil Ziwa — captor, deceiver, teacher, conqueror, and promised liberator.** Hibil disguises himself, removes Ruha from darkness, imprisons her, steals her mirror, defeats Ur, gives Ruha knowledge, strikes her when she preaches, receives her blessing, and promises that Ur rather than Ruha is the chief enemy. *Ginza Rabba* 5.1 and related tractates; *1,012 Questions*. **[LC]**

- **Ur — son, sexual partner, ally, and eventual enemy in counsel.** Ruha births Ur after Gaf impregnates her; she later joins sexually with Ur and produces the planetary powers. After seeing the Light in Ruha’s mirror, Ur wants to attack it, while Ruha advises attacking darkness instead. *Ginza Rabba* 3 and 5.1. **[LC]**

- **The Seven planets — mother and commander.** Ruha and Ur generate them; Ruha commands them in building Jerusalem and in attempts to hold Adam within the world. *Ginza Rabba* 3, 15, and parallel cosmological passages. **[LC]**

- **The Twelve zodiacal signs — mother and astral sovereign.** Ruha and Ur are their parents; they participate in astral fate and obstruction of souls. *Ginza Rabba*; later *Book of the Zodiac*. **[LC/LS]**

- **Ptahil — uneasy collaborator and rival in creation.** When Ptahil’s creative power ebbs, Ruha’s rises; she and her astral beings supply conditions or material for the lower creation. *Ginza Rabba* 3. **[LC]**

- **Adam — adversary and claimant over the soul-bearer.** Ruha and the planets seek to keep Adam in the world; she laments his death because the soul-treasure escapes her control. *Ginza Rabba* 3 and related death traditions. **[LC]**

- **Hawa/Eve — prototype and tempter.** Ptahil proposes Adam as his image and Hawa as Ruha’s; Ruha later incites Hawa to mourn noisily and laments when Hawa departs with Hibil. *Ginza Rabba*. **[LC/RV]**

- **The younger Adam — seductress and revealer.** Disguised as his wife, Ruha seduces him but also teaches him that sexual differentiation results from cosmic imbalance. *Ginza Rabba* 3. **[LC]**

- **Anuš Uthra — opponent over Jerusalem.** Anuš repeatedly frustrates Ruha’s building; after the killing of his Mandaean followers he destroys Jerusalem. Ruha begs for the city, then offers to kill its Jewish inhabitants herself; Anuš refuses. *Ginza Rabba* 15 or associated Jerusalem cycle. **[LC]**

- **Manda d-Hiia — possible rescuer of Ruha’s redeemed double.** In the *Book of John*, Manda d-Hiia encounters a Nitufta figure emerging from Gaf’s world; a messenger invites her upward. Identification with Ruha is inferred, not explicit. **[LC/MSR]**

- **Adonai — lover or political-religious ally.** Ruha is described as mistress of Adonai, the hostile Mandaean representation of the Jewish Lord, and is associated with Jerusalem’s rule. Exact details vary across polemical passages. **[LC/SI]**

- **Jesus/Yišu Mšiha — mother or source through the devalued Holy Spirit.** Some Mandaean polemics call Jesus the son of Ruha ḏ-Qudša, exploiting Christian language of conception by the Holy Spirit. This is an anti-Christian genealogical claim, not a neutral Mandaean adoption of Christian pneumatology. **[LC/SI]**

- **Simat Hiia — identity or redeemed counterpart.** Prayer 376 and related traditions associate Ruha with Simat Hiia’s ascent from darkness. In other texts they remain separate. **[LC/RV/SI]**

- **Yawar Ziwa — indirect consort relation through Simat Hiia.** Where Ruha and Simat Hiia are identified, Ruha acquires the position of the bride whose ascent enables her liberating *uthra* to be purified. This is not a universal direct marriage of Ruha and Yawar. **[LC/RV]**

- **The Great Life/First Life — adversarial sovereign, possible true parent, and final source of mercy.** Ruha’s powers are subdued under the Life, yet she addresses a divine Father and may ultimately be sealed and restored. *Qulasta* 75; *1,012 Questions*; *Ginza* traditions. **[LC/LS]**

- **Krun — no direct interaction securely documented.** Hibil defeats Krun during the same descent in which he encounters Ruha, but shared narrative proximity does not establish a Ruha–Krun episode.

- **Sophia, Barbelo, Achamoth, Protennoia, Hermetic Nature, or Manichaean Matter — no primary-text meeting or equation.** All such relations are modern comparison. **[MSR/MI if presented as ancient identity]**

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### A. Hibil Ziwa’s descent and the removal of Ruha

**Source:** *Ginza Rabba* 5.1.  
**Tier:** **[LC]**, probably preserving late-antique mythic material.

Before the formation of the earthly world, forces below plan war against the Light. Hibil Ziwa descends through seven infernal regions. In the first he finds Ruha, though the text does not explain why she is there. He remains unseen for an immense period, continues downward, defeats the flesh-mountain Krun, and acquires powers or passports of darkness.

On returning, Hibil infiltrates Ruha’s family. Qin is preparing Ruha’s marriage to her brother Gaf. Disguised as a demon, Hibil impresses Qin, who gives him Ruha’s sister Zahriel; he neither consummates the marriage nor eats demonic food. He tricks Qin into revealing secrets in a mirror, steals it, assumes Gaf’s appearance, and tells Ruha that he will take her to her “parents” above.

Ruha follows, only to be sealed in an intermediate world. Pregnant by Gaf, she gives birth to Ur. Her confinement is not merely punitive: Hibil has removed from the underworld a power necessary for subsequent creation. Ruha repeatedly asks about her parents and possible ascent. Hibil’s answers oscillate between deception and disclosure.

When Ruha shows Ur the mirror, he sees the Light and desires war against it. Ruha instead advises him to attack darkness. Hibil takes the mirror, chains Ur, and reassures Ruha that Ur—not she—is the Great Life’s enemy. She remains confined pending a later liberation.

**Development:** Older dualistic summaries treat Hibil as pure hero and Ruha as captured evil. Buckley emphasizes that Hibil deceives her, that she counsels Ur against attacking the Light, and that Hibil preserves rather than destroys her. Both readings describe real features of the episode.

### B. Ruha, Ur, and the birth of the astral rulers

**Source:** *Ginza Rabba* 3 and parallel cosmologies.  
**Tier:** **[LC]**.

Ruha sleeps with Ur to obtain release or creative power. After seven days she bears the Seven planets. They disappoint her because they are mutually dissimilar. The Twelve zodiacal beings are likewise attributed to Ruha and Ur. Together these powers structure time, fate, illness, temperament, political order, and the astral barriers confronting souls.

The myth encodes incest, hostile generation, and astrological determinism. The Seven and Twelve are not merely astronomical objects; they are personal rulers. Yet later priestly astrology also employs their patterns pragmatically in naming and horoscopy. Hostile cosmology and ritual expertise therefore coexist.

**Variant:** Some summaries call Ur Ruha’s spouse and omit his birth from her; others preserve the full sequence of brother–sister and mother–son unions. The latter is textually important and should not be sanitized.

### C. Ruha and Ptahil create the lower world

**Source:** *Ginza Rabba* 3.  
**Tier:** **[LC]**.

Ptahil descends from the Lightworld but feels his living fire weakening. He attempts to stabilize the earth and cannot do so independently. Ruha’s strength rises as his declines. Their relationship is competitive cooperation: neither is an omnipotent creator, and the world emerges from unequal participation by light-derived craftsmanship and lower forces.

The planets learn that higher beings intend to place Adam and the luminous Adam Kasia/Adakas in a dwelling of light. They plot to seize Adam and confine him. The lower makers form the body, but it cannot stand until the *nišimta* arrives from the Lightworld. Ruha supplies or personifies the vital-affective component that allows embodied life but also makes deception, passion, and bondage possible.

**Interpretation:** Ruha is not simply the creator of evil matter. She is necessary to animation, fertility, earthly life, and the soul’s opportunity to undergo baptism. The same necessity makes her dangerous.

### D. Ruha, Adam, and Eve

**Sources:** *Ginza* creation and death traditions.  
**Tier:** **[LC/RV]**.

One account has Ptahil declare that Adam will be made in his image and Hawa in Ruha’s. When Adam dies, Ruha induces Hawa to perform loud lamentation. Mandaean mortuary ideology rejects such lament because death can release the soul; Ruha mourns because she loses a soul-bearing body under her dominion.

When Hawa’s death comes, Ruha addresses her with attachment and fear. Hibil removes Hawa, and Ruha complains that the Light’s agents take everything precious and leave the worthless behind. Her speech acknowledges the soul’s value and her own deprivation.

In another episode Ruha disguises herself as the younger Adam’s wife and seduces him. Yet she also teaches him that the division into male and female results from cosmic disequilibrium. The supposed deceiver becomes the revealer, while Adam receives a negative prophetic epithet.

### E. Ruha builds Jerusalem

**Source:** *Ginza* Jerusalem/Anuš cycle.  
**Tier:** **[LC]**, with overt anti-Jewish polemic.

Ruha commands her seven planetary sons to establish Jerusalem. Anuš warns that 360 Mandaean disciples or priests will arise there and repeatedly curses her building attempts. Ruha eventually erects seven pillars, and a Jewish population prospers.

Anuš descends and converts inhabitants. When hostile Jews kill the converts, he resolves to destroy the city. Ruha first begs him to spare her work, then changes sides and offers to collapse the walls on the Jews responsible. Anuš rejects her offer and destroys Jerusalem himself.

This is sectarian myth, not recoverable political history. It contains violent anti-Jewish imagery and attributes mass killing to both Ruha’s proposal and Anuš’s retaliation. It should neither be suppressed nor treated as evidence that such a destruction historically occurred.

### F. Dinanukht and Ruha’s self-revelation

**Source:** *Ginza Rabba* 6.  
**Tier:** **[LC/RV]**.

Dinanukht, imagined as half man and half book, encounters the smaller book Dišai and receives disturbing prophecy. After attempting unsuccessfully to destroy it, Dinanukht sleeps. Ruha/Ewat appears and proclaims herself primordial Life and Truth, radiance and darkness, death and life, destruction and construction, error and healing. She also speaks as a preexistent exalted man.

Dinanukht then ascends through astral stations. In the third *matarta* he meets Ruha again, now as its female ruler and seductress. At Abatur’s station he sees the stored opposites proclaimed in her revelation.

The narrative refuses a single moral classification: Ruha is both cosmic revealer and obstructing planetary ruler.

### G. Ruha’s lament, repentance, and possible redemption

**Sources:** *Qulasta* 75 and 376; *1,012 Questions*; *Ginza*; possibly the *Book of John*.  
**Tier:** **[LC/LS/MSR]**.

In Prayer 75 Ruha asks why her Father created her, cast her far away, and left her powerless in the depths. The lament belongs to a liturgical passage in which conquered darkness-powers praise the Light. It demonstrates regret and longing but not necessarily completed repentance.

In *1,012 Questions*, Hibil reports that Ruha and her creatures have been subdued and sealed by Life. Another teaching makes the deficient mark of darkness a prerequisite for bodily existence and baptism. Prayer 376 describes Simat Hiia rising from darkness and enabling her liberator’s purification; in traditions that equate Simat Hiia with Ruha, this becomes an image of Ruha’s restoration.

The *Book of John* tells of a female Nitufta power emerging from Gaf’s darkness, waiting outside the seven walls, and being invited upward by the Life that loves her. Buckley identifies her as Ruha and treats this as the completion of the earlier abandonment. Because the text uses another name, the conclusion remains **[MSR]**.

Buckley’s strongest conclusion is that Ruha rules and personifies the earthly world until its end, but is then due for redemption under Hibil’s promise. That reading is plausible and textually cumulative; no single universally authoritative Mandaean verse supplies the entire sequence.

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

### No cult of Ruha

There is no securely documented temple dedicated to Ruha, priesthood serving her, festival honoring her, sacrificial economy, pilgrimage center, or votive corpus. She is not a cult goddess in the ordinary ancient Mediterranean sense.

Mandaean ritual is directed toward the Great Life and Lightworld beings. Ruha appears as:

- the power from whose mixture believers seek purification;
- the earthly condition that makes baptism necessary;
- the astral ruler whose stations the soul must cross;
- a subordinated being whose lament may be recited;
- an explanatory figure in priestly teaching;
- a hostile or ambivalent power invoked in magical and protective settings.

### Baptism

**[LC/living practice]** Repeated immersion in flowing “living water” (*yardna*) is central to Mandaean life. Water from the Lightworld fertilizes or overcomes earth/Ruha in cosmological exposition. The rite marks an embodied being already bearing the left-hand or darkness sign with the sign of Life. Ruha is therefore structurally present, not worshipped.

### Death rites

The *masiqta* assists the soul’s ascent through the planetary toll-stations. Because Ruha and her offspring govern the lower cosmic machinery, mortuary ritual counters their capacity to retain the soul. Ruha’s mourning for Adam and Hawa also supplies a mythic negative model for excessive lamentation.

### Astrology and naming

Priests use the *Book of the Zodiac* to calculate *malwaša* names and horoscopes. This is not evidence of devotional astral worship. It is ritual management of a cosmos governed partly by Ruha’s children.

### Magic

Mandaic incantation bowls and amulets address spirits, liliths, planets, illnesses, and hostile agents. Some belong clearly to Mandaean settings; others were products of a broader Mesopotamian magical market. Ruha’s association with sorcery and the mirror is literary, while the bowls document actual ritual technologies. A direct attribution of every Mandaic magical object to Ruha would exceed the evidence.

### Sacrifice and sexual rites

The contested-material search produced no credible evidence of human sacrifice to Ruha, temple prostitution in her service, or a Ruha cult practicing sexual rites. Her incestuous and seductive episodes are myths, not records of ritual reenactment. Comparisons between *qadišta* and Mesopotamian “sacred women” do not establish Mandaean temple prostitution.

## 7. Historical Evolution

### Third–seventh centuries: formation in late-antique Mesopotamia

**[EA/MSR]** Mandaic magical texts show a religious-linguistic environment in Sasanian Mesopotamia by approximately the third–fourth centuries. The developed Ruha figure probably emerged through interaction among:

- Aramaic personifications of spirit;
- Jewish and Christian Holy Spirit language;
- Mesopotamian planetary religion and the Ishtar–Venus complex;
- myths of hostile cosmic rulers;
- Iranian dualistic and astrological traditions;
- internally Mandaean reflection on body, soul, and baptism.

No source permits these ingredients to be assigned to one moment or founder. Encyclopaedia Iranica cautions that no scholarly consensus exists on the redactional growth of Mandaean traditions. [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mandaeans/mandaeans-ii-the-mandaean-religion/)

### Third-century colophon horizon

Mandaean colophons remember Zazai of Gawazta and other transmitters conventionally placed around the third century. Buckley has argued that their chains preserve real scribal memory. Critics note that the surviving colophons are much later and that some chronological calculations are ambiguous. The colophons support early transmission but do not make the extant sixteenth-century manuscripts third-century artifacts. [Buckley, “The Colophons in H. Petermann’s Sidra Rabba”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/abs/colophons-in-h-petermanns-sidrarabba/16B8984FF6EC3AF1C35466B256C380F2)

### Sixth–seventh centuries: canonical collection and polemical sharpening

**[LC/MSR]** Much of the *Ginza* may have reached collected form around the mid-seventh century. Anti-Christian, anti-Jewish, and apocalyptic materials reflect a community defining itself among competing scriptural groups. The presentation of Ruha ḏ-Qudša as a hostile Holy Spirit and mother of the false messiah belongs naturally to this environment, though individual passages may be older or younger.

A traditional account says that Mandaean authorities presented the *Ginza* to Muslim rulers as proof of scriptural status and protection from forced conversion. This is preserved through later Mandaean historical memory and should not be treated as independently documented for every detail.

### Islamic centuries

Mandaeans survived chiefly in southern Iraq and Khuzestan as an endogamous baptist community. Ruha remained part of scripture, priestly teaching, astrology, magic, and ritual anthropology. Islamic vocabulary entered some texts: Prayer 75’s address to “Allah,” for example, belongs to the transmitted form of the lament and may indicate adaptation during Islamic rule rather than the myth’s original wording.

### Sixteenth–nineteenth centuries: surviving manuscripts

The oldest surviving codices establish the physical history:

- 1529–30: Marsh 691 prayers.
- 1560: earliest surviving *Ginza* manuscript.
- 1570: earliest surviving *Sidra ḏ-Nišmata*.
- 1631: earliest surviving *Book of John*.
- seventeenth–nineteenth centuries: continued copying, commentary, magical texts, and vernacular influence.
- 1797–98: earliest known *Book of the Zodiac* witness.

European collectors and missionaries acquired manuscripts, often separating them from community use. Petermann published the Mandaic *Sidra Rabba* in 1867; Lidzbarski’s German *Ginza* appeared in 1925; Drower’s fieldwork and editions greatly enlarged the accessible corpus.

### Twentieth century: comparative “Gnosticism”

Ruha was commonly placed in a generalized Gnostic scheme as evil Spirit, fallen Sophia, world-soul, or female demon. Jonas and Rudolph made Mandaeism central to reconstructions of “Gnostic religion,” while later scholars such as Williams and King criticized treating “Gnosticism” as a single transhistorical religion.

Buckley’s 1980s–2000s work shifted focus from an assumed static dualism to Ruha’s contradictory textual performances. She treated the inconsistencies as constitutive of Ruha’s mediating role.

### Twenty-first century

Ruha remains scripturally meaningful within living Mandaean communities, but no evidence shows a revivalist cult specifically dedicated to her. Displacement from Iraq and Iran has moved Mandaean ritual and manuscript culture into Australia, Europe, and North America. Digital editions, community translations, and the 2019/2020 Häberl–McGrath *Book of John* have made primary materials more widely available.

Modern persecution and forced displacement affect the community that transmits Ruha’s stories, but no documented campaign has targeted Ruha’s figure specifically.

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Scholarship and popular Gnosticism

**[MSR]** Ruha now appears in comparative discussions of Sophia, Lilith, the Holy Spirit, Ishtar, the Shekinah, and Manichaean Matter. Some comparisons are productive; none should be converted into an ancient identity without a primary equation.

### Occult and esoteric reception

Searches found modern websites and social-media discussions presenting Ruha as:

- “the dark Holy Spirit”;
- a form of Lilith;
- Sophia’s demonic aspect;
- an astral goddess;
- the feminine divine suppressed by institutional religion;
- an occult power suitable for invocation.

These are generally **[MI]** unless explicitly presented as modern creative theology. No evidence was found that Renaissance Hermetists, the Theosophical Society, the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, or early modern grimoires maintained a continuous Ruha tradition. Ruha’s entry into Anglophone occult circulation appears to depend chiefly on nineteenth- and twentieth-century translations of Mandaean literature.

### Neopagan or reconstructionist worship

No well-documented, historically continuous Ruha cult exists, and no securely measurable organized neopagan denomination devoted to her was identified. Individual online devotion may exist, but it should be classified as modern personal or occult innovation, not revived ancient Mandaean religion.

### Literature, film, comics, and games

Ruha has not acquired the large commercial afterlife of Lilith, Sophia, or Ishtar. Where the name appears in modern fantasy or online demonology, the representation usually blends those better-known figures. Such blending is modern reception, not evidence about Mandaean antiquity.

### Political appropriation

The Jerusalem cycle contains severe anti-Jewish polemic and imagined collective punishment. Repetition of it in modern sectarian or antisemitic discourse would be appropriation of a late-antique/medieval religious polemic, not neutral historical reportage. No large extremist movement centered on Ruha was identified in the searched evidence.

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Is Ruha simply evil?

- **Older dominant view:** Ruha is queen or leader of darkness, mother of hostile astral beings, seducer, and opponent of the Light.
- **Buckley’s revision:** those traits are real but incomplete. Her knowledge, lament, restraint of Ur, blessing of Hibil, identification with cosmic opposites, and prospective sealing show radical ambiguity.
- **Evaluation:** the “pure evil” reading better describes many isolated polemical passages; Buckley’s reading better explains why the corpus repeatedly gives Ruha revelatory and redemptive functions.

### Is Ruha the Christian Holy Spirit inverted?

- **For:** her title precisely reproduces Aramaic “Holy Spirit”; she is connected polemically with Jesus and rival Christianity; Christian Syriac also treats grammatical Spirit as feminine.
- **Against a simple derivation:** *ruha* is ordinary Semitic “spirit”; Jewish, Christian, and wider Aramaic usages overlap; Mandaean Ruha has planetary, Mesopotamian, and anthropological features not reducible to Christian pneumatology.
- **Conclusion:** Christian polemical inversion is likely one layer, not a complete origin theory.

### Is Ruha Sophia?

- **For:** female intermediary, compromised lower creation, residual upper knowledge, suffering, revelatory speech, and possible restoration.
- **Against:** no Mandaean text calls her Sophia; her explicit origin/fall narrative is missing; her genealogy with Qin, Gaf, and Ur is distinct.
- **Conclusion:** “fallen-wisdom figure” is a useful typological description. “Ruha is Achamoth/Sophia” as a literal ancient equation is unsupported.

### Is Ruha Ishtar or Venus?

- **For:** textual compound names such as Libat-Ištar-Amamit, motherhood of astral powers, sexuality, fertility, and planetary associations.
- **Against:** Ruha’s principal role is not simply that of Venus; Libat can appear independently; the transmitted figure incorporates multiple traditions.
- **Conclusion:** an Ishtar–Venus stratum or identification is attested, but Ruha cannot be collapsed into Ishtar.

### Is Ruha Lilith?

Mandaean literature knows liliths as a class of female demons. Ruha shares sexuality, danger, darkness, and maternity with them, but she has a far broader cosmic role. No secure primary equation makes her the Jewish Lilith. Modern occult conflation is **[MI]**.

### Is Ruha Manichaean?

No. Mandaeism and Manichaeism arose in overlapping Mesopotamian environments and share dualistic, planetary, and mixture cosmologies. Manichaean Matter and female darkness-powers illuminate the comparative setting, but the *Kephalaia* and Cologne Mani Codex do not make Ruha a member of Mani’s pantheon. The Cologne codex is a small fifth-century Greek manuscript discovered near Asyut in 1969. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Cologne Mani Codex”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cologne-mani-codex-parchment/)

### Dating disputes

The chief problem is that late-antique composition is reconstructed through:

- language;
- incantation parallels;
- colophon genealogies;
- internal historical allusions;
- relationships with Jewish, Christian, Iranian, and Islamic terminology.

But the securely dated full manuscripts begin only in 1529–60. Claims that the extant *Ginza* “is a third-century manuscript” are false. Claims that all its contents were composed in the sixteenth century are equally untenable.

### Primary evidence and editions

Major landmarks include:

- Heinrich Petermann, *Thesaurus sive Liber Magnus vulgo “Liber Adami” appellatus* / *Sidra Rabba* (1867), Mandaic text and Latin translation.
- Mark Lidzbarski, *Ginzā: Der Schatz oder das große Buch der Mandäer* (1925), German translation and commentary.
- E. S. Drower, *The Mandaeans of Iraq and Iran* (1937).
- Drower, *The Canonical Prayerbook of the Mandaeans* (1959).
- Drower and Rudolf Macuch, *A Mandaic Dictionary* (1963).
- Kurt Rudolph, *Die Mandäer* and later synthetic works on Mandaean religion.
- Jorunn Jacobsen Buckley, “A Rehabilitation of Spirit Ruha in Mandaean Religion” and *The Mandaeans: Ancient Texts and Modern People* (2002).
- Buckley, *The Great Stem of Souls* (2005/2006), on scribal lineages.
- Charles G. Häberl and James F. McGrath, *The Mandaean Book of John* (2019/2020).

### Discredited or unsupported claims still circulating

- Ruha was universally worshipped as a goddess: **unsupported**.
- Ruha is simply Lilith: **unsupported**.
- Ruha is explicitly named in Nag Hammadi scriptures: **false**.
- Ruha is a Manichaean deity attested in the *Kephalaia*: **unsupported**.
- Mandaeans practiced human sacrifice or sacred prostitution for Ruha: **no credible evidence found**.
- Every Mandaic incantation bowl is doctrinally Mandaean: **methodologically unsound**.
- The surviving *Ginza* manuscripts date from the third century: **false**.
- Buckley proved a single canonical “repentance of Ruha”: **overstatement**; she reconstructed a redemptive trajectory from several passages and identifications.
- Ruha’s equation with Sophia is an ancient Mandaean statement: **false**; it is modern comparative scholarship.
- Modern AI or occult portraits reproduce ancient iconography: **false unless tied to a documented manuscript image**.

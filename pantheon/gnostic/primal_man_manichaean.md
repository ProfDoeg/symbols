# The Primal Man (Manichaean): Research Dossier

> **Class:** A male divine hypostasis, warrior-savior, and son/emanation of the Father of Greatness; not the earthly Adam and not a historical human. In Manichaean enumerations he belongs to the **First Creation/Evocation**, together with the Mother of Life and the five light-elements who are his “sons,” armor, limbs, and living soul.
>
> **Earliest secure attestation:** The figure belongs to Mani’s third-century cosmological teaching. The earliest composition-level evidence is therefore **c. 240–276 CE**, especially Mani’s *Šābuhragān* and the Syriac cosmogony later quoted by Theodore bar Kōnay. No third-century physical manuscript naming him survives. The earliest extant witnesses are late-third- to fourth-century anti-Manichaean accounts, followed by fourth-/fifth-century Manichaean manuscripts. Claims that a pre-Manichaean “Primal Man” identical to Mani’s figure is securely documented are not established.
>
> **Principal names:** Syriac **ܢܫܐ ܩܕܡܝܐ** *nāšā qadmāyā*, “First/Primal Man”; Greek **πρῶτος ἄνθρωπος** *prōtos anthrōpos*; Latin *Primus Homo*; Coptic **ϣⲁⲣⲡ̄ ⲛ̄ⲣⲱⲙⲉ** *šarp n-rōme*, “First Man”; Middle Persian **Ohrmizd-bay / Ohrmazd-bag** (“the god Ohrmazd”); Parthian **mard hasēnag**, “First Man”; Arabic **الإنسان القديم** *al-insān al-qadīm*, “the ancient/primal man.”

## Evidentiary labels

- **[EA] Securely attested early:** third- or fourth-century material, or a later witness demonstrably quoting early Manichaean teaching.
- **[CE] Community elaboration:** doctrine in Manichaean liturgical, exegetical, or narrative literature after Mani.
- **[RV] Regional variant:** Iranian, Coptic, Central Asian, Chinese, or Islamicate adaptation.
- **[PS] Priestly/scholastic systematization:** enumerations and harmonizations created within learned Manichaean transmission.
- **[SI] Syncretistic identification:** a translation or equation with another tradition’s figure.
- **[LA] Late accretion:** substantially later medieval development.
- **[MSR] Modern scholarly reconstruction:** an inference, comparison, or disputed historical model.
- **[MI] Modern invention or modern religious reuse.**

## 1. Identification, names, and epithets

### Identity

The Primal Man is the first divine combatant sent against invading Darkness. In the myth’s most stable form, the Father of Greatness evokes the Mother of Life, and she evokes or gives rise to the Primal Man. He in turn summons five sons—the living light-elements—and “puts them on” as garments, armor, or weapons. He descends into Darkness, is overwhelmed, and allows the five elements to be swallowed by the demonic powers. Their presence within Darkness produces the primordial Mixture of Light and matter. The Primal Man himself is awakened and rescued after the Living Spirit’s Call; his elemental sons remain dispersed as the suffering Living Soul or World Soul. **[EA]**

This defeat is not treated simply as divine incompetence. Manichaean sources variously describe it as combat, sacrifice, bait, snare, and a temporarily losing move whose hidden consequence is the poisoning and eventual defeat of Darkness. These interpretations coexist rather than constituting successive, mutually exclusive versions. [Encyclopaedia Iranica’s general survey](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-1-general-survey/) calls the episode a divine self-sacrifice; its [cosmogony article](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-iii/) documents the devouring of the five sons and subsequent rescue.

### Names and language history

| Form | Language and period | Status and meaning |
|---|---|---|

| *prōtos anthrōpos* | Greek | “First Man.” Attested in Greek and Greek-derived Christian descriptions of Manichaeism. **[EA/CE]** |
| *Primus Homo* | Latin | Standard Latin rendering in western anti-Manichaean literature. **[EA/CE]** |

| *Ohrmizd-bay*, *Ohrmazd-bag*, written forms such as ʾwhrmyzd-bg | Middle Persian Manichaean | “God Ohrmazd.” A deliberate Iranian localization of the Primal Man, not proof that Mani merely copied the Zoroastrian Ahura Mazdā. **[SI/RV]** |
| *mard hasēnag* | Parthian | “First/primordial man.” **[RV]** |
| *al-insān al-qadīm* | Arabic | “The ancient/primal man,” especially in Ibn al-Nadīm and later Islamicate accounts. **[LA/RV]** |
| “Original Man,” “Primordial Man,” “First Man,” “Primal Man” | Modern European scholarship | Translation alternatives, not separate beings. **[MSR]** |

The cross-language forms are summarized in specialist treatments of the [Manichaean pantheon](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-ii-the-manichean-pantheon/) and [Manichaean cosmogony](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-iii/).

### “Ohrmazd” and Iranian identification

In Iranian Manichaean texts, the First Man is **Ohrmizd/Ohrmazd Bay**. His five sons can correspond terminologically to the *Amahraspands*, the Zoroastrian beneficent immortals. This is a documented missionary translation strategy. It does not make the two mythologies identical:

- Zoroastrian Ohrmazd is the supreme creator opposed to Ahriman.
- Manichaean Ohrmizd Bay is a subordinate emanated warrior, son of the supreme Father/Zurwān.
- Zoroastrian creation is fundamentally Ohrmazd’s good creation subsequently attacked; the Manichaean material cosmos is a salvific machine constructed from captured demons and mixed light.
- The Manichaean First Man is defeated and rescued; this is not the standard Zoroastrian biography of Ahura Mazdā.

Older Iranian-origin theories often treated the correspondence as proof that the whole myth was essentially Zurvanite or Zoroastrian. Current source criticism is more cautious: Iranian nomenclature is undeniable, but some similarities may be superficial, and the principal surviving Zoroastrian systematizations are themselves later than Mani. The issue is reviewed in [“Cosmogony and Cosmology iii. In Manicheism”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-iii/). **[SI; disputed MSR]**

### Titles and functional descriptions

The sources call or portray him as:

- **Son of the Father/King:** a relational title in the first divine triad. **[EA]**
- **Warrior or champion of Light:** inferred directly from his mission against Darkness. **[EA]**
- **Soul or self-manifestation of the Father:** some systematizations make him the Father’s own soul or person in sacrificial descent. This does not erase the narrative distinction between Father and Son. **[CE/PS]**
- **Bait, snare, or poison for Darkness:** exegetical metaphors explaining why his defeat ultimately benefits Light. **[CE/PS]**
- **Father of the five light-elements:** the elements are simultaneously sons, garments, armor, weapons, limbs, and the Living Soul. **[EA/CE]**
- **Mild or gentle exemplar:** an eastern Manichaean characterological saying contrasts the First Man’s mildness with the Living Spirit’s severity. **[RV/CE]**

No securely attested epithet makes him a solar deity, creator of the material universe, earthly first human, or Kabbalistic Adam Kadmon.

## 2. Earliest attestation and textual corpus

### The problem of “earliest”

Three different dates must not be collapsed:

1. **Composition:** Mani taught and wrote in the third century.
2. **Earliest report:** hostile late-third-/fourth-century authors preserve portions of his system.
3. **Physical manuscript:** most surviving Manichaean manuscripts are several centuries later than Mani.

There is no surviving inscription or securely identified third-century image of the Primal Man. The earliest evidence is textual.

### Principal witnesses

#### Mani’s *Šābuhragān*

Mani composed the *Šābuhragān* in Middle Persian for Šābuhr I, conventionally in the 240s. Its surviving Turfan fragments are medieval manuscript copies, not Mani’s autograph. The work supplies early Iranian nomenclature and demonstrates that Mani adapted his universal message to the Sasanian court. The [Iranica article on the *Šābuhragān*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/sabuhragan/) records fragments containing Ohrmazd/Ohrmizd and Ahriman and discusses identifications with passages quoted by Ibn al-Nadīm. **[EA at composition level; RV in terminology]**

#### Mani’s Syriac cosmogony through Theodore bar Kōnay

Theodore bar Kōnay’s *Book of Scholia*, generally dated to the late eighth century, is hostile Christian testimony, but its Manichaean cosmogony appears to quote or closely paraphrase a Syriac Manichaean source. Because Syriac was close to Mani’s own Aramaic, it provides unusually direct access to his terminology. It narrates the Father’s response to Darkness, the evocation of the Mother and First Man, the five sons, defeat, Call, Answer, rescue, and world construction. [John C. Reeves supplies a translation and textual orientation](https://pages.charlotte.edu/john-reeves/publications/unpublished-lectures-and-fragmenta/theodore-bar-konai-on-mani-and-manichaeism/). **[EA tradition through LA manuscript/report]**

#### Alexander of Lycopolis

Alexander’s Greek *Against the Doctrines of Mani*, probably composed around 300 CE, is among the earliest substantial external accounts. It confirms major features of the two-principle system and the mixing of divine power with matter. Its vocabulary does not preserve every detail of the mature fivefold myth. **[EA hostile witness]**

#### *Acta Archelai*

The *Acta Archelai*, surviving principally in Latin from a lost Greek work and conventionally dated c. 330–350, stages a fictionalized debate with Mani. It is polemical and historically unreliable as narrative, but valuable for fourth-century knowledge of Manichaean doctrine. **[EA/hostile, with legendary frame]**

#### Ephrem the Syrian

Ephrem’s fourth-century prose refutations attack Manichaeans in Syriac and knew live Manichaean teaching. He confirms that the Primal Man is a heavenly being sent against Darkness, not Adam. The surviving text is transmitted through a palimpsest; an older English edition is available in [Ephrem’s prose refutations](https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/ephraim2_8_intro3.htm). **[EA hostile witness]**

#### Augustine

Augustine had been a Manichaean Hearer for roughly nine years before writing against the religion. Relevant works include *De moribus Manichaeorum*, *De duabus animabus*, *Contra Fortunatum*, *Contra Faustum*, *Contra Felicem*, *De natura boni*, and *Contra Secundinum*. He describes divine substance trapped through the primordial conflict, but often selects formulations vulnerable to Christian criticism. He is an informed yet adversarial witness, not a neutral ethnographer. **[EA/CE hostile witness]**

#### Coptic *Kephalaia*

The *Kephalaia of the Teacher* and the Chester Beatty “Dublin Kephalaia” preserve teachings placed in Mani’s mouth. They developed from early community traditions but underwent collection, translation, and editorial expansion. The extant papyrus codices are now generally assigned to the early fifth century. They portray:

- the First Man putting on his five sons;
- the five sons as weapons, robes, powers, and soul;
- his descent, combat, and rescue;
- the pentadic organization of divine history;
- moral and ecclesiological applications of the myth.

The genre and manuscripts are surveyed in [Iranica’s “Kephalaia”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kephalaia/). **[CE/PS, preserving EA traditions]**

The Medinet Madi codices were acquired and brought to scholarly attention in 1929–31. The library included the two *Kephalaia* collections, Psalm Book, Homilies, Acts/church history, Synaxeis fragments, and Mani’s Epistles. Their discovery ended scholars’ near-total dependence on polemical sources; the collection is described under [Coptic Manichaean texts](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/coptic-manichean-texts/) and by the [University of Münster’s Manichaeism project](https://www.uni-muenster.de/IAEK/forschung/koptologie/manichaeismusforschung/index.html).

#### Coptic Manichaean Psalm Book

The fourth-century compositions preserved in a fifth-century codex include psalms “Concerning the First Man” and related hymns to the Living Soul and saving gods. These turn cosmology into lament, praise, and soteriological devotion. The five elements’ captivity is not abstract physics: it is divine suffering repeated in plants, bodies, and the world. **[CE/liturgical]**

#### Cologne Mani Codex

The miniature Greek parchment codex, probably fifth century and discovered near Asyūṭ in 1969, contains *On the Origin of His Body*, a biography of Mani drawing on earlier disciple traditions. It is not the principal source for the Primal Man battle, but is crucial for Mani’s Elchasaite/Judaeo-Christian environment and therefore for evaluating the myth’s origins. See [“Cologne Mani Codex”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cologne-mani-codex-parchment/). **[CE, contextual]**

#### Iranian and Central Asian fragments

Middle Persian, Parthian, Sogdian, and Old Uyghur fragments from Turfan preserve hymns, sermons, canonical works, and cosmogonies. Among their characteristic contributions are:

- *Ohrmizd Bay* as the First Man;
- the elements as his *Amahraspands*;
- Iranianized names for the Father, Living Spirit, and other gods;
- the First Man praying to his Mother;
- praise of the suffering Living Soul;
- Central Asian transmission of the stable combat-rescue pattern.

The texts are later manuscript witnesses, mostly from the first millennium’s later centuries, but some translate works by Mani or early disciples. A broad translated collection is available in [Skjærvø’s *Manichaean Texts*](https://www.manichaeism.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Skjaervo-Manichaean-Texts-trans..pdf). **[RV/CE, sometimes preserving EA composition]**

#### Chinese *Compendium*

The *Compendium of the Doctrines and Styles of the Teaching of Mani, Buddha of Light*, compiled in 731 CE for the Tang court, gives a concise sinicized account of Manichaean doctrine and practice. It preserves the rescue of the Primal Man but dresses the religion in Buddhist and Chinese administrative vocabulary. It is an exposition composed in Chinese rather than necessarily a literal translation of one western scripture. **[RV/PS]**

#### Ibn al-Nadīm’s *Fihrist*

Written in Baghdad in 987–988, the *Fihrist* reports that the Primal Man clothed himself in five principles or gods—ether/air, wind, light, water, and fire—and fought the Primal Iblīs. Ibn al-Nadīm had access to Manichaean informants and books, but his account is a tenth-century Islamicate dossier, not a third-century manuscript. Its Arabic names—*al-Insān al-Qadīm*, *Iblīs al-Qadīm*, *Umm al-Ḥayāt*, and *Rūḥ al-Ḥayāt*—represent translation into Islamic-era vocabulary. The relevant section can be read in an online version of the [Dodge translation](https://dokumen.pub/the-fihrist-of-al-nadim-a-tenth-century-survey-of-muslim-culture-vol-2-9780231029254-m-2954385.html), with its historical value assessed by [Iranica’s *Fehrest* article](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fehrest-iii-representation-of-manicheism/). **[LA/RV, preserving older doctrine]**

### Texts named in the brief that do **not** attest this being

The following are essential for comparison but are not primary witnesses to the Manichaean Primal Man:

- The Nag Hammadi *Apocryphon of John*, *Hypostasis of the Archons*, *On the Origin of the World*, *Gospel of Truth*, *Tripartite Tractate*, *Gospel of the Egyptians*, *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*, *Eugnostos*, and *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* contain several distinct heavenly-Man, Adamas, Geradamas, Autogenes, or Anthropos traditions. None names or narrates Mani’s First Man with his five elemental sons and rescue by the Living Spirit. **Absence is the finding.**
- The Berlin Codex’s *Apocryphon of John* and related works likewise provide Sethian or other Christian-Gnostic comparanda, not Manichaean testimony.
- *Pistis Sophia* and the *Books of Jeu* possess elaborate light hierarchies but do not attest Mani’s figure.
- The *Gospel of Judas* does not attest the Manichaean Primal Man.
- Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Epiphanius, Tertullian, and Clement preserve earlier “heavenly Man” systems—Valentinian Anthropos, Adamas, and related figures—but these are genealogically and narratively distinct.
- *Corpus Hermeticum* I, the *Poimandres*, makes a divine Anthropos descend after loving his reflected form in nature. It lacks the Father’s warrior, five elemental sons, demonic swallowing, Call, and rescue.
- The Mandaean *Ginza Rabba* contains Adam, Adam Kasia/Rba, Manda d-Hayye, and other anthropic light figures, but none is simply Mani’s Primal Man under another name.

These materials establish a late-antique **field of anthropic speculation**, not a single trans-religious deity.

## 3. Domain, attributes, and iconography

### Domain and powers

The Primal Man’s domain is the **first defensive action of Light against Darkness**. His functions are:

- champion and frontier-defender of the Realm of Light;
- voluntary bearer of the five light-elements into the abyss;
- sacrificial bait whose light intoxicates or disables the demons;
- prototype of descent, awakening, answering revelation, and ascent;
- personal concentration of the divine substance later dispersed as the Living Soul;
- cause, through his sons’ captivity, of the subsequent cosmos and salvation history.

His limitation is fundamental: he can be overwhelmed. Manichaean divinity is not uniformly omnipotent in the later classical-monotheist sense. The first divine force loses the opening engagement, although the loss becomes strategically salvific. **[EA]**

### The five sons, armor, and living substance

Lists differ in order and translation, but the standard five are:

1. **Living Air / Ether / Gentle Breeze**
2. **Wind**
3. **Light**
4. **Water**
5. **Fire**

Each counters a corresponding dark element. The five may be called sons, gods, limbs, garments, armor, weapons, or soul. These are not merely poetic synonyms: they express how Manichaean myth converts divine psychology, matter theory, and kinship into one pentadic structure. The *Kephalaia*’s pentadic arrangements are analyzed in Timothy Pettipiece’s [*Pentadic Redaction in the Manichaean Kephalaia*](https://libraryofagartha.com/Philosophy/Gnosticism/Gnostic%20sects/Manichaen/Pettipiece%20T.%20-%20Pentadic%20Redaction%20in%20the%20Manichaean%20Kephalaia.pdf). **[EA/PS]**

### Form

The sources portray him anthropomorphically enough to arm, descend, fall, hear, answer, receive a hand, and ascend. They do not provide a stable physiognomy, skin color, age, hairstyle, or dress independent of his elemental armor. “Man” denotes a divine person/form, not membership in the biological human species.

### Iconography

No surviving image has been securely and generally identified as an individual portrait of the Primal Man. Manichaean art from Turfan and China includes divine assemblies, cosmic diagrams, judgment scenes, the Living Soul’s liberation, Mani, Jesus, clerics, and the Bema festival. Some lost or fragmentary cosmological paintings may once have represented his episode, but identification of a particular surviving figure as Ohrmizd Bay is not secure. The comprehensive overview of [Manichaean art](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manichean-art/) does not establish a standard Primal-Man iconographic type.

Accordingly:

- **Standard emblem:** none uniquely attested.
- **Weapon:** the five sons collectively; light can function as lance and shield in later summaries.
- **Animal, plant, color, number, weekday, or planet:** no exclusive cultic correspondences are securely documented.
- **Defining number:** five, because of his elemental sons; this is theological rather than evidence for a separate numerological cult.

## 4. Genealogy and relations

### Genealogical structure

A common western formulation is:

```text
Father of Greatness
        │
 evokes Mother of Life
        │
 evokes/bears Primal Man
        │
 summons five sons:
 Living Air – Wind – Light – Water – Fire
```

The language alternates between begetting, evoking, calling forth, and manifestation. Syriac Manichaeans could avoid ordinary sexual-generation language; later narrators nevertheless describe a Mother-Son relation. The Primal Man is therefore both distinct from and an expression of the Father. **[EA/CE]**

He has no wife or sexual consort. His “offspring” are the five elements. The Living Spirit is not normally his biological brother, but belongs to the Second Creation summoned for his rescue. The earthly Adam is a later demonic imitation made from swallowed light and is emphatically not the Primal Man.

### Every documented divine interaction

- **Father of Greatness — commission/emanation:** The Father responds to Darkness by calling forth the First Creation and sending the Primal Man into battle. Theodore bar Kōnay’s Syriac cosmogony, ultimately third-century teaching; Coptic *Kephalaia*, fourth-century traditions in fifth-century copies. **[EA]**

- **Mother of Life — mother/emanator and intercessor:** She evokes or bears the Primal Man; in Iranian versions he later prays to her and she appeals upward for rescue. Theodore bar Kōnay; Iranian cosmogonic fragments; *Kephalaia*. **[EA/RV]**

- **Living Air — son, armor, and captive light:** The Primal Man summons and puts on Living Air; it is swallowed or imprisoned in its dark counterpart. Theodore bar Kōnay; *Kephalaia*; Ibn al-Nadīm. **[EA/CE]**

- **Wind — son, armor, and captive light:** The Primal Man puts on Wind, which enters the primordial Mixture. Same sources. **[EA/CE]**

- **Light — son, armor/weapon, and captive light:** The Primal Man arms himself with Light; it is dispersed in Darkness. Same sources. **[EA/CE]**

- **Living Water — son, armor, and captive light:** The Primal Man puts on Water; it is swallowed and becomes part of the suffering Living Soul. Same sources. **[EA/CE]**

- **Living Fire — son and first or principal weapon:** Some *Kephalaia* passages make Fire the first robe expended against Darkness before the remaining four are surrendered. **[CE/PS]**

- **King of Darkness / Primal Devil / Primal Iblīs — combat and strategic sacrifice:** The Primal Man descends against the ruler and five realms of Darkness, is overwhelmed, and yields his sons as food, bait, poison, or snare. Theodore bar Kōnay; Ephrem; *Kephalaia*; Ibn al-Nadīm. **[EA; Arabic nomenclature RV]**

- **Living Spirit — awakening, handshake, and rescue:** The Living Spirit descends, utters the Call, receives the Primal Man’s Answer, extends his right hand, and raises him from Darkness. Theodore bar Kōnay; Coptic and Iranian sources; Chinese *Compendium*. **[EA/CE/RV]**

- **The Call — awakener:** The Living Spirit’s uttered Call becomes sufficiently personified in Manichaean theology to function as a divine hypostasis. It reaches the unconscious Primal Man. Theodore bar Kōnay and later systematizations. **[EA/PS]**

- **The Answer — responsive hypostasis:** The Primal Man answers the Call; his Answer is likewise personified. Call and Answer become the archetype of revelation entering the soul and the soul’s saving response. Theodore bar Kōnay; Coptic texts. **[EA/PS]**

- **Five sons of the Living Spirit — rescuing host:** With the Living Spirit they descend into Darkness, assist in locating the Primal Man, subdue demons, and construct the cosmos from demonic bodies. Their joint scene is attested, although individual one-to-one conversations with the Primal Man are not. Theodore bar Kōnay; later cosmogonies. **[EA/CE]**

- **Beloved of Lights and Great Builder — membership in the Second Creation:** They belong to the divine response initiated after his defeat, but no surviving source securely records a direct conversation or combat involving either one individually and the Primal Man. **Absence of direct interaction.**

- **Third Messenger — indirect continuation of rescue:** The Third Messenger’s later seduction of the archons liberates light belonging ultimately to the Primal Man’s captured sons. No direct meeting between the two is securely narrated. **[EA/CE; indirect only]**

- **Jesus the Splendour — indirect awakening analogue:** Jesus awakens earthly Adam as the Living Spirit’s Call awakened the Primal Man. This is a structural repetition, not a documented meeting of Primal Man and Jesus. **[CE/MSR]**

- **Earthly Adam — archetypal relation without meeting:** Demons fashion Adam from light derived ultimately from the Primal Man’s sons and in imitation of a heavenly divine image. No direct encounter between the two is securely attested. **[EA/CE]**

- **Nahashbat — herald in some eastern summaries:** A victory-crown-bearing angel precedes the Primal Man into combat in later reconstructions based on eastern material. The evidence is limited and the figure is not part of every recension. **[RV/CE]**

## 5. Major myths and narrative cycles

### The first assault

Before the cosmos, Light and Darkness exist as separate, coeternal realms. Darkness is internally turbulent. Its beings reach the boundary, glimpse the luminous realm, and desire it. The Father does not fight personally in his transcendent condition; he brings forth the First Creation. **[EA]**

This absolute two-principle scheme distinguishes Mani’s system from myths in which a single creator deliberately makes both good and evil. Manichaean accounts nevertheless call later divine forces into existence in historical sequence, producing tension between eternal divine unity and narrated emanation.

### Arming of the Primal Man

The Mother of Life calls forth the Primal Man. He summons his five sons and puts them on as armor. Lists and order vary; *Kephalaia* passages sometimes describe successive robes, while Ibn al-Nadīm presents five “principles” or gods. The armor is alive and consubstantial with Light. **[EA, with CE variants]**

### Descent, defeat, and swallowing

The Primal Man goes down to meet Darkness. The five dark elements and their rulers overcome or engulf the five light-elements. The First Man lies unconscious or insensible in the abyss. His sons are devoured.

Several source-based interpretations coexist:

- **Military defeat:** the first champion loses. **[EA]**
- **Voluntary sacrifice:** he gives himself and his sons to protect the Realm of Light. **[EA/CE]**
- **Poisoned bait:** as poison hidden in food, Light enters the demons and begins their destruction. **[CE]**
- **Snare or net:** the demons’ appetite entangles them in the divine substance. **[CE/PS]**

The result is the **Mixture**. The five sons become the Living Soul suffering in material existence. This is why salvation concerns literal divine substance, not only human moral improvement.

### The Call and the Answer

The Father initiates a Second Creation. The Mother of Life and Living Spirit descend with the Living Spirit’s five sons. The Living Spirit utters a Call; the Primal Man hears and answers. Call and Answer are hypostatized as divine powers and as the model of religious awakening:

1. saving knowledge comes from above;
2. the soul recognizes its origin;
3. recognition produces the answering movement upward.

The Living Spirit gives the Primal Man his right hand and raises him. The “right hand” became an important symbol of salvation, ecclesial fellowship, and ritual greeting in Manichaean tradition. **[EA/CE]**

### Rescue—but not of the five sons

The Primal Man returns to Light, but his five sons remain mixed with Darkness. This distinction is essential. Statements that “the Primal Man was swallowed” can refer loosely to the whole divine force; strictly, many sources distinguish the rescued person from the still-captive elemental armor.

The rest of cosmic history exists to recover those elements. The Living Spirit defeats the archons and makes the heavens and earth from their bodies. The Third Messenger provokes further release of light. Demons create Adam and Eve partly to imprison concentrated light. Prophets and the Manichaean church teach the elect how to separate it through knowledge, discipline, and the ritual meal. **[EA/CE]**

### Eschatological completion

At the end, the liberated light is gathered into a New Paradise or final divine structure. A last fire separates remaining light from matter; irrecoverable mixture is confined with Darkness. Some Manichaean sources admit that a residue of light will remain trapped, a point Christian critics used to argue that the Manichaean God could never be restored perfectly. The First Man’s initial sacrifice is thus both the origin of the world-process and the standard for its completion. [Iranica’s treatment of Manichaean eschatology](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/eschatology-ii/) documents the unresolved residue. **[CE/PS; variant outcomes]**

## 6. Cult, temples, and ritual practice

### Was there a separate cult of the Primal Man?

No independent temple, priesthood, festival, oracle, pilgrimage center, sacrifice, or theophoric naming tradition devoted exclusively to the Primal Man is securely attested. He was worshipped or praised within the total Manichaean divine economy. Statements that he had a distinct “cult” overstate the evidence.

### Liturgical presence

His myth entered worship through:

- psalms and hymns about the First Man and Living Soul;
- daily prayers addressed to or naming divine emanations;
- sermons interpreting the five elements;
- the ritual right hand;
- confession over damage done to light in plants and food;
- the elect’s sacred meal, understood to liberate light from edible matter.

The point of consuming selected plant food was not sacrifice to the Primal Man. Manichaean physiology held that the disciplined elect could separate the captive light—the substance of his sons—and send it upward through prayer and digestion. **[CE/ritually attested]**

### Institutions

The church was divided into the Leader/Archegos, teachers, bishops, presbyters, elect, and hearers. The Primal Man had no separate priesthood. Elect observed stringent commandments; hearers supported them with food and labor. The [general survey of Manichaeism](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-1-general-survey/) describes scripture-reading, hymns, prayer, communal meals, teaching, and mission.

### Temples and material sites

Manichaean churches and monasteries existed from the Roman world to Central Asia and China, but none can be securely called a “Temple of the Primal Man.” Important sites include:

- **Medinet Madi, Egypt:** findspot of the great Coptic library, not securely identified as his sanctuary.
- **Kellis, Dakhla Oasis:** documentary evidence for a fourth-century Manichaean community.
- **Qocho/Turfan:** monasteries, manuscript remains, and wall paintings from the eastern church.
- **Tang and Uyghur cities:** monasteries supported by Uyghur patronage.
- **Cao’an, Fujian:** medieval Manichaean sanctuary later preserved in Buddhist guise, centered on Mani/Buddha of Light rather than a distinct Primal-Man cult.

### Sacrifice, sexuality, and violence

The dedicated contested-material search yielded:

- **Human sacrifice:** no credible textual, archaeological, or legal evidence that Manichaeans sacrificed humans to the Primal Man.
- **Animal sacrifice:** contrary to Manichaean ethics, which discouraged killing living beings.
- **Temple prostitution:** no evidence.
- **Ritual sexual intercourse:** polemical accusations of sexual rites occur in heresiological literature, but are not confirmed by internal Manichaean evidence.
- **Cult violence:** no evidence of violence performed for this deity. Manichaean communities more often appear as targets of state and ecclesiastical repression.
- **Cosmic violence:** abundant, but it belongs to mythic combat rather than documented human ritual.

## 7. Historical evolution

### c. 240–276: Mani’s formulation

Mani constructed the Primal Man as a principal actor in a universal cosmogony combining Syro-Mesopotamian Christian, Jewish, Iranian, and wider late-antique materials. The figure’s precise derivation remains disputed. The myth is already coherent enough in early witnesses that its main architecture should be attributed to Mani, not to late medieval systematizers. **[EA]**

### Late third–fifth centuries: western transmission

Greek, Syriac, Coptic, and Latin communities transmitted the myth. Polemicists Alexander, the *Acta Archelai*, Ephrem, Augustine, and others attacked the divine defeat and the doctrine that God’s substance was contaminated. Coptic Manichaeans expanded the myth in *Kephalaia* and psalmody. **[EA/CE]**

The Roman state acted against Manichaeans from Diocletian onward. Later Christian imperial and episcopal suppression included confiscation, exile, coercive interrogation, and sometimes capital penalties. The Primal Man’s myth was condemned as evidence that Manichaeans divided divine sovereignty and treated material nature as imprisoned deity.

### Third–seventh centuries: Iranian adaptation

Iranian missionaries identified the First Man as Ohrmizd Bay and his sons with Iranian divine terminology. This did not replace the story: it translated its dramatis personae. Under Sasanian authorities, Mani was imprisoned and died under Bahrām I’s regime; later communities experienced intermittent persecution by Zoroastrian and political authorities. **[RV/SI]**

### Sixth–tenth centuries: Central Asia and Uyghur ascendancy

Sogdian merchants and missionaries carried Manichaeism eastward. Under the Uyghur Khaganate, particularly after Bögü Qağan’s conversion in 762/763, Manichaeism became a court-supported religion. The First Man survived as part of the standard cosmology, increasingly expressed through Iranian, Turkic, Buddhist, and Chinese vocabulary.

After the Uyghur state’s collapse in 840, protection vanished. Tang authorities proscribed Manichaeism; temples and property were confiscated, scriptures burned, and clergy laicized, exiled, or executed. The broader chronology is documented in [Iranica’s account of Manichaeism in China](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chinese-turkestan-vii/) and [Chinese-Iranian relations](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chinese-iranian-i/).

### Eighth–tenth centuries: Islamicate preservation

Manichaean communities persisted under Islamic rule, usually categorized polemically as *zanādiqa*. Ibn al-Nadīm still encountered Manichaean books and informants in Abbasid Baghdad. Arabic recensions renamed the adversary Primal Iblīs and the hero *al-Insān al-Qadīm*. This is translation and contextualization, not evidence that the myth originated in Islam. **[RV/LA]**

### Tenth–seventeenth centuries: southern Chinese survival

After northern suppression, communities persisted in southeastern China under the names Religion of Light and related designations, often blending with Buddhist, Daoist, and local ritual forms. Mani, rather than the Primal Man, became the most visible cult image. Cosmological elements survived in texts, but evidence for continued, separately articulated devotion to the First Man becomes thin.

### Modern period

There is no demonstrable uninterrupted modern church preserving a specific cult of the Primal Man. Fujian ritual communities retain genuine Manichaean-derived materials, but their exact continuity, self-identification, and doctrinal knowledge differ by locality. Modern internet “Manichaean churches” are revivals, not proof of institutional continuity from antiquity.

## 8. Modern reception and reinvention

### Scholarship and existential interpretation

Hans Jonas made the myth a centerpiece of his account of “Gnostic” alienation: divine life falls into an alien cosmos and must remember itself. His presentation powerfully shaped twentieth-century readers but often treated diverse movements as manifestations of a single Gnostic existential structure. **[MSR]**

Kurt Rudolph likewise situated the Primal Man within a broad history of Gnosis. Bentley Layton, Michael Williams, Karen King, Birger Pearson, John Turner, and Einar Thomassen subsequently emphasized the dangers of turning “Gnosticism” into one religion with one mythology. Their critiques require treating Sethian Adamas, Valentinian Anthropos, Hermetic Man, Mandaean figures, and Mani’s warrior as related comparanda rather than synonyms.

### Adam Kadmon

The equation “Manichaean Primal Man = Adam Kadmon” is a modern comparative shorthand, not an ancient Manichaean identification.

- The Manichaean figure is third century.
- The exact Kabbalistic term **Adam Kadmon** is first securely attested in an early thirteenth-century treatise; it later becomes central in Lurianic Kabbalah.
- Adam Kadmon is a cosmic configuration of divine emanation, especially the sefirotic worlds.
- Mani’s First Man is an armed son sent into Darkness, defeated, called, and rescued.

Structural analogy is legitimate; identity or documented transmission is unproved. The dating problem is summarized in the [Jewish Virtual Library entry on Adam Kadmon](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0001_0_00386.html). **[MSR; identity claim MI]**

### Occultism, Theosophy, and New Age use

The contested-material search found no evidence that Renaissance Hermetists, the Golden Dawn, or early Theosophical institutions maintained a historically transmitted cult of the Manichaean First Man. Modern esoteric literature sometimes merges him with:

- Adam Kadmon;
- the Hermetic Anthropos;
- the Jungian Self;
- the “cosmic Christ”;
- a universal androgyne;
- Iranian Gayōmard;
- Mandaean Adam Kasia;
- Hindu Puruṣa.

These combinations are modern comparative or religious constructions unless a specific historical transmission is demonstrated. Claims that all are names of one primordial deity are **[MI]**.

### Modern revival

Small online communities now translate prayers, reconstruct calendars, and venerate the Manichaean pantheon. A contemporary revival site publishes seven daily prayer times, including a “Prayer of the Emanations.” This is evidence for twenty-first-century religious reconstruction, not for an unbroken Primal-Man cult: [Manichaean Faith prayer page](https://manichaean.org/en/prayers/standard-times-of-prayer/). **[MI]**

No reliable demographic data establish the number of people specifically worshipping the Primal Man today.

### Popular culture

Manichaean dualism has strongly influenced general literary and political vocabulary, but the named Primal Man has not acquired a stable mass-cultural image comparable to Lucifer, Odin, or Anubis. Occasional fictional, gaming, and internet-esoteric uses generally conflate him with Adam Kadmon or a generic “primordial human.” No major modern commercial depiction can be shown to preserve ancient iconography, because no secure ancient iconographic type exists.

## 9. Historiography and scholarly debate

### Iranian-origin theory

Earlier scholars, including representatives of the *Religionsgeschichtliche Schule* and Geo Widengren, emphasized Iranian or Zurvanite origins. The Iranian name Ohrmizd and the fivefold divine armor supported this reading.

Objections include:

- the myth is preserved most directly in Syriac;
- Mani’s upbringing was Mesopotamian and Judaeo-Christian;
- Iranian names can be missionary substitutions;
- Zoroastrian parallels do not reproduce the Call-and-Answer rescue;
- much detailed Zoroastrian cosmology survives only in later texts.

Present consensus favors a composite formation rather than a purely Iranian myth. **[disputed MSR]**

### Gnostic and Christian origin

The heavenly Anthropos, divine descent, captive soul, and revelatory awakening have analogues in Christian-Gnostic systems. Fernando Bermejo argues that the Primal Man’s status as sent Son also carries substantial Christian structural content in [“Primal Man, Son of God”](https://brill.com/previewpdf/book/edcoll/9789004289123/B9789004289123_005.xml). Yet no surviving pre-Manichaean text gives Mani’s complete sequence. **[MSR]**

### Bardaisan

Bardaisan’s Father and Mother of Life, elements, and divine “Word of Intention” offer close regional analogies. These may reflect a shared Syriac intellectual environment, direct borrowing, or polemical transformation. They do not establish that Bardaisan already possessed Mani’s First Man. The comparison is surveyed in [Iranica’s article on Bardaisan](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bardesanes-syr/). **[MSR]**

### Jewish apocalyptic contribution

The cosmic use of imprisoned demons and the generation of humanity through fallen powers have strong analogies to Enochic Watcher traditions. John C. Reeves and others have demonstrated substantial Jewish lore within Manichaean cosmology, especially the *Book of Giants*. That evidence concerns ingredients in Mani’s synthesis, not a Jewish text already containing the complete Primal-Man myth. **[MSR]**

### Mandaean priority

Older scholarship sometimes made Mandaeism the bridge from an ancient Iranian Anthropos to Mani. Problems include the difficult dating and redaction of Mandaean texts and the absence of Mani’s precise warrior-rescue narrative. Contact between Mandaeans and Manichaeans is historically plausible, but direction and date of influence must be argued passage by passage. Recent discussion notes the long history of speculative reconstructions in [research on Mandaean-Manichaean relations](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/1/14). **[disputed MSR]**

### Hermetic *Poimandres*

The Hermetic Anthropos descends through the spheres because of love for his image in nature. Mani’s figure descends under orders to stop an invasion. Both myths connect a heavenly Man to the soul’s presence below, but narrative mechanism, ethical valence, and outcome differ. The Hermetic figure is therefore a parallel, not a secure source or identity.

### “Gnosticism” as category

Jonas and Rudolph treated the Primal Man as evidence for a general Gnostic myth. Williams and King challenged the category’s tendency to homogenize unrelated communities and inherit heresiological assumptions. For this dossier, “Gnostic” is useful only as a modern comparative classification. Manichaeans themselves claimed a universal apostolic church and named Mani its final apostle; they did not identify themselves as one branch of a modern scholarly entity called “Gnosticism.” **[MSR]**

### State of the evidence

Major holdings and discoveries include:

- **Berlin State Museums/Papyrus Collection:** portions of the Medinet Madi codices.
- **Chester Beatty Library, Dublin:** Coptic Manichaean codices, including Kephalaia material.
- **University of Cologne:** Cologne Mani Codex.
- **Berlin Turfan Collection:** Iranian, Sogdian, and Uyghur fragments.
- **British Library and Bibliothèque nationale de France:** Chinese and Central Asian materials from Dunhuang.
- **Kellis collections:** papyri and wooden tablets documenting a Manichaean community.
- **Chinese and Japanese temple collections:** medieval Chinese Manichaean paintings and ritual texts.

War, dispersal, antiquities dealing, manuscript fragmentation, and deterioration destroyed context and portions of the record. Multispectral imaging is now recovering text from damaged papyri; the University of Hamburg reports substantial new readings from the Manichaean Epistles in its [Manichaean papyri case study](https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/profiling-guide/case-studies/manichaean-papyri.html).

## 11. Findings on the specifically contested claims

- **“The Primal Man is Adam.”** False as a description of Manichaean doctrine. Earthly Adam is created later by demonic rulers using stolen light. **[contradicted by EA sources]**
- **“The Primal Man is Adam Kadmon.”** A modern comparison, not an ancient identification. **[MSR/MI]**
- **“He is simply Ahura Mazdā.”** The Iranian name is securely attested, but the functions and hierarchy differ. **[SI, not timeless identity]**
- **“He originated in the Nag Hammadi religion.”** No single “Nag Hammadi religion” existed, and none of the named codices attests his complete myth. **[MI]**
- **“He is Mandaean Manda d-Hayye or Adam Kasia.”** Comparability does not establish identity or direction of borrowing. **[disputed MSR]**
- **“His defeat proves a late corruption of an originally victorious Iranian god.”** No documentary sequence demonstrates such a corruption. The defeat is integral to the earliest reconstructable Manichaean system. **[unsupported]**
- **Human sacrifice, temple prostitution, or a violent historical cult:** no supporting evidence.
- **A unique ancient portrait or emblem:** none securely identifiable.
- **A continuous modern priesthood:** none established.
- **Occult correspondences with planets, chakras, tarot, or Kabbalistic sefirot:** modern constructions unless explicitly presented as comparison. **[MI]**

# Autogenes (the Self-begotten): Research Dossier

> **Class:** A transcendent male or androgynous divine aeon, hypostasis, savior, and—in Christianized Sethian texts—the pre-existent Christ. “God” is defensible as a functional description, but the surviving sources more characteristically call Autogenes an aeon, Son, Light, Logos, Mind, or divine self-generated one.  
> **Earliest secure attestation:** Irenaeus of Lyons, *Against Heresies* 1.29.2, written approximately 175–185 CE, reporting a Greek-speaking “Barbeloite” system in which Autogenes is emanated from Ennoia and Logos, paired with Aletheia, honored above all subordinate beings, and surrounded by four Luminaries. This is the earliest securely datable surviving witness, although Irenaeus’s Greek original survives here chiefly through an ancient Latin translation.  
> **Earliest surviving manuscripts:** Fourth-century Coptic copies from the Nag Hammadi codices; the fifth-century Coptic Berlin Codex 8502 preserves an additional recension of the *Apocryphon of John*.  
> **Central finding:** Autogenes is not one continuously worshipped god with a stable biography. The name designates a developing divine function. In Irenaeus’s report Autogenes remains distinguishable from Christ; the *Apocryphon of John* identifies “the divine Autogenes” with Christ, Barbelo’s Son; the *Gospel of the Egyptians* elaborates him as the great Logos presiding over a complex luminous pleroma; and *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, and *Marsanes* recast Autogenes as the lowest, life-generating or noetic mode of Barbelo’s transcendental triad. No ancient source establishes that the Sethian Autogenes was adopted under that proper name into Mandaeism or Manichaeism.

## Evidentiary labels

- **[EA] Securely attested early material:** A source datable to approximately the second or third century, or an early external witness to it.
- **[LE] Later ancient elaboration:** A development in later Sethian or related texts, normally second–fourth century.
- **[RV] Regional or textual variant:** A competing recension, linguistic form, or local system.
- **[LS] Learned systematization:** A secondary philosophical, priestly, or modern scholarly construction organizing disparate evidence.
- **[SI] Syncretistic identification:** A historically made equation with another divine being or philosophical principle.
- **[MA] Medieval or early-modern accretion.**
- **[F] Folklore.**
- **[MSR] Modern scholarly reconstruction:** A conclusion inferred by scholars rather than stated in an ancient source.
- **[MI] Modern invention or modern religious reception.**
- **[NE] Not established:** A proposition for which the presently identifiable evidence is absent or inadequate.

Dates assigned to compositions remain approximate. Dates of surviving manuscripts are materially later and must not be confused with composition dates.

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### Orientation

**[EA]** Autogenes belongs to the divine world above the material cosmos. He is not the demiurge and does not undergo an earthly birth, reign, death, or resurrection. In the myth reported by Irenaeus, he is an emanated image of the Great Light and subsequently emits the perfect Human, Adamas. In the four recensions of the *Apocryphon of John*, he is Barbelo’s divinely generated Son, anointed by the Invisible Spirit and therefore called Christ. Through him appear the four Luminaries and their twelve aeons. In the Platonizing Sethian texts, especially *Zostrianos* and *Allogenes*, “Autogenes” can name both a divine being and an ontological level within the aeon of Barbelo.

The familiar formula “Invisible Spirit–Barbelo–Autogenes” is a useful synopsis of Sethian Father–Mother–Child theology. **[MSR]** It is nevertheless a modern analytical formula, not a single creed preserved verbatim in every text. The internal structure, gendering, genealogy, and relation to Christ change from work to work.

### Greek name

**[EA]** Greek: **Αὐτογενής**, *Autogenēs*; also related forms **αὐτογενήτωρ**, *autogenētōr*, and **αὐτογέννητος**, *autogennētos*. The components are **αὐτός**, “self,” and the stem **γεν-**, “beget, generate, come into being.” Context permits “self-generated,” “self-begotten,” “self-produced,” or “self-born.”

“Self-created” is less precise because the sources describe emanation and generation, not manufacture. “Only-begotten” translates **μονογενής**, *monogenēs*, a different word. Confusion between the two obscures the paradox that Autogenes is called self-generated while some texts also say that Barbelo, or a syzygy, generated him.

### Coptic forms

**[EA/RV]** The Coptic manuscripts normally transliterate the Greek title, with orthographic variation caused by dialect, scribal practice, or the state of the papyrus. It functions variously as:

- a substantivized title, “the Self-generated One”;
- a proper divine name, “Autogenes”;
- an adjectival designation of an aeon or class, “self-generated”;
- an epithet of Christ;
- an ontological region, “the Autogenes aeon” or the realm of the self-generated ones.

The title’s exact Greek ending cannot always be recovered from a Coptic transliteration.

### Principal documented titles

- **Divine Autogenes** — *Apocryphon of John*, *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*. **[EA/LE]**
- **Christ, the divine Autogenes** — all four recensions of the *Apocryphon of John*. **[EA/RV]**
- **Son** or **Child** of the Invisible Spirit and Barbelo — *Apocryphon of John*. **[EA]**
- **Great Light** or image of the Great Light — Irenaeus 1.29; related language in the *Apocryphon*. **[EA]**
- **Great Logos** and “word of the pleroma of the four Lights” — *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[LE]**
- **Good divine Autogenes** — *Allogenes* 58–59. **[LE]**
- **Rational expression/Logos and knowledge of the perfect Mind of Truth** — *Zostrianos* 29–30. **[LE]**
- **Aeon of Autogenes** — *Zostrianos* and later Sethian metaphysical discourse. **[LE]**
- **Primordial or archetypal Human:** not a regular ancient title, but a scholarly description of Autogenes’s relation to Adamas in Irenaeus 1.29. **[MSR]**

### Classification and gender

**[EA]** Masculine grammar and the title “Son” predominate in the Christianized texts. **[LE]** Sethian divine gender is nevertheless symbolic and frequently androgynous: Barbelo is Mother-Father and “virginal male,” while the Child can be embedded in a “Triple-Male” structure. Thus “male god” is too narrow; “divine aeon or hypostasis, conventionally masculine” is more exact.

### Identifications and comparisons

- **Christ:** Explicit in the *Apocryphon of John*. **[SI/EA]**
- **Logos:** Explicit or nearly explicit in the *Gospel of the Egyptians* and *Zostrianos*. **[SI/LE]**
- **Perfect Mind or an expression of it:** Developed in *Zostrianos*. **[LE]**
- **Lower or life-generating mode of Barbelo:** A reconstruction from *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, and *Marsanes*. **[MSR]**
- **Platonic Soul or World Soul:** A modern philosophical comparison, not an ancient proper-name equation. **[MSR]**
- **Hermetic Self-begotten/Heavenly Human:** A significant ancient comparison, especially with *Poimandres* and the *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth*, but no text says that the Sethian Autogenes simply “is” the Hermetic Anthropos. **[MSR/NE]**
- **Egyptian self-generating creator gods:** Possible conceptual background for Hermetic autogenetic language; a direct genealogical derivation of the Sethian figure from Atum, Amun, or another Egyptian god is not demonstrated. **[MSR/NE]**
- **Mandaean Great Life, Yoshamin, Abatur, Ptahil, or Manda d-Hiia:** Comparanda only. No Mandaean text securely identifies any of these beings as the Sethian Autogenes. **[NE]**
- **Manichaean Father of Greatness, First Man, Living Spirit, Third Messenger, or Jesus the Splendor:** Comparanda only. No secure Manichaean occurrence of Autogenes as this Sethian proper name has been established. **[NE]**

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### Irenaeus: the earliest secure witness

**[EA]** In *Against Heresies* 1.29, approximately 175–185 CE, Irenaeus reports a system he associates with “a multitude of Gnostics,” subsequently labeled Barbeloites by scholars and later heresiologists. After Barbelo, Foreknowledge, Incorruptibility, Eternal Life, Christ, Mind, and Logos emerge, “Autogenes” is emitted from Ennoia and Logos as an image of the Great Light. Aletheia, Truth, accompanies him as a syzygy. Four Luminaries from Christ and Incorruptibility surround him; four ministering powers attend the Luminaries. Autogenes then emits the true and perfect Human, Adamas.

This account makes Autogenes and Christ distinguishable. That is the most consequential difference from the *Apocryphon of John*, where “Christ, the divine Autogenes” is a single figure. Irenaeus may be abridging, misunderstanding, or transmitting a system different from the extant Coptic works; none of those explanations can be proved exclusively. The text is available in translation at [Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.29](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103129.htm).

### The *Apocryphon of John*

**[EA/RV]** The work survives in four Coptic witnesses translated from Greek:

- NHC II,1: long recension, Sahidic Coptic, fourth-century manuscript.
- NHC IV,1: long recension, closely related to NHC II, fourth-century manuscript.
- NHC III,1: short recension, fourth-century manuscript.
- BG 8502,2: short recension, fifth-century manuscript.

The Berlin museum catalog dates Papyrus Berolinensis 8502 to the fifth century and identifies its four works: the *Gospel of Mary*, *Apocryphon of John*, *Sophia of Jesus Christ*, and *Act of Peter* ([Berliner Papyrusdatenbank](https://berlpap.smb.museum/17878-2/)).

Composition is normally placed in the second century, with redaction continuing thereafter. Irenaeus 1.29 provides a secure terminus for closely related mythology, but whether he knew the present *Apocryphon* itself remains disputed.

In all four recensions:

1. Barbelo asks the Invisible Spirit for Foreknowledge, Incorruptibility, Eternal Life, and Truth.
2. A spark or Light appears through the Spirit’s and Barbelo’s mutual contemplation.
3. The Spirit anoints the Light, making him Christ.
4. Mind is granted to him.
5. The four Luminaries—Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, and Eleleth—stand before him.
6. Twelve aeons or powers are distributed among the Luminaries.
7. The perfect Human Adamas appears through the will of the Spirit and Autogenes.
8. Adamas, Seth, Seth’s offspring, and repentant souls receive places within the four luminous realms.

The relevant text calls the four beings “the four lights which attend the divine Autogenes” and the twelve aeons those attending “the Autogenes, the Christ” ([Wisse translation](https://gnosis.org/naghamm/apocjn.html?source=post_page---------------------------)). Karen King’s synopsis likewise identifies the Son as “the divine self-generated Christ” ([King, introduction](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/SBJ/King-Iintroduction-SRJ.htm)).

### *Gospel of the Egyptians* or *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit*

**[LE]** Survives in NHC III,2 and IV,2, fourth-century Coptic manuscripts of a Greek work usually assigned to the second or third century. It presents a more elaborate Sethian liturgical cosmogony. Autogenes appears as the great Logos, connected with the four Lights and the completion of luminous ogdoads. Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, and Eleleth receive consorts and ministers. Autogenes joins the pleroma in praise of the Invisible Spirit. The work is directly accessible in [MacRae’s translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/goseqypt.html).

### *Zostrianos*

**[LE]** NHC VIII,1, a highly fragmentary fourth-century Coptic manuscript, translated from a Greek apocalypse probably composed in the late second or third century. Porphyry’s *Life of Plotinus* 16 reports that an apocalypse of “Zostrianos” circulated among Plotinus’s associates at Rome in the 260s. Whether the Nag Hammadi text is exactly the same edition cannot be proved, but substantial identity or close kinship is widely accepted.

Autogenes has several connected meanings in the work:

- the deity in whose name Zostrianos receives repeated baptisms;
- a noetic Logos and knowledge;
- the region of “perfect individuals”;
- a level of Barbelo below Protophanes and Kalyptos;
- the aeon containing the four luminous sub-aeons.

Zostrianos is baptized repeatedly “in the name of the divine Autogenes,” becoming successively contemplative, masculine, holy, and perfect angel. The text associates these transformations with living water, purification, sealing, and ascent ([Turner translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/zostr-jdt.html)).

### *Allogenes*

**[LE]** NHC XI,3, a damaged fourth-century Coptic copy of a Greek apocalypse generally assigned to the third century. An *Allogenes* circulated in Plotinus’s milieu according to Porphyry. The visionary sees “the good divine Autogenes,” the youthful Triple-Male Child/Savior, Protophanes-Harmedon, Kalyptos, Barbelo, and the transcendent Triple-Powered Invisible One. Autogenes here is not straightforwardly the Christian Christ: the surviving work lacks an overt Jesus narrative. Its hierarchy is philosophical and apophatic. See the [surviving translated text](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/allogene.html).

### *Three Steles of Seth*

**[LE]** NHC VII,5, fourth-century Coptic manuscript; composition usually placed in the late second or third century. Three hymnic “steles” praise Geradamas/Adamas, Barbelo, and the Unbegotten One. Autogenetic terminology occurs in the first hymn and in the text’s ascent structure. The work seems better suited to recitation and contemplative ascent than to narrative instruction. Autogenes is less a dramatic character than a divine identity or power disclosed through praise.

### *Marsanes*

**[LE]** NHC X, an exceptionally damaged fourth-century Coptic manuscript, probably translating a third-century Greek revelation. Autogenes forms part of its complex noetic taxonomy; the speaker insists on teaching the “entire place” of Autogenes. Its surviving fragments include technical discussions of seals, souls, being, number, and cosmic configurations. See [Turner’s translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/marsanes-jdt.html).

### *Thought of Norea*

**[LE]** NHC IX,2, fourth-century Coptic manuscript; probable second- or third-century composition. Its short prayer says that Norea cries to the divine Autogenes and the four holy helpers. The text therefore preserves invocation, not merely cosmological exposition. Because it is very short and damaged, the ritual setting cannot be securely reconstructed.

### *Trimorphic Protennoia*

**[LE]** NHC XIII,1, fourth-century Coptic manuscript, normally dated to the second or third century. Autogenes is important more by structural implication than by sustained personal action. Protennoia descends as Father/Voice, Mother/Speech, and Son/Word; the final descent includes Five Seals and clothing the initiate in light. Scholars relate this triadic revelation to the Invisible Spirit–Barbelo–Autogenes pattern, but the equation must not erase the work’s own first-person female revealer. **[MSR]**

### Hermetic witnesses

**[SI/EA]** The Greek *Corpus Hermeticum* I, *Poimandres*, probably second or third century, presents a divine Mind and archetypal Human rather than the named Sethian Autogenes. The Coptic *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* (NHC VI,6) distinguishes the Unbegotten, Self-begotten, and Begotten. This shows that “self-begotten” was part of a broader Greco-Egyptian theological vocabulary. It does not establish a single Sethian-Hermetic cult.

### Texts named in the wider brief that do not securely document this being

**[NE]** The *Hypostasis of the Archons*, *On the Origin of the World*, *Gospel of Truth*, *Tripartite Tractate*, *Eugnostos*, *Pistis Sophia*, *Books of Jeu*, and *Gospel of Judas* are important for Gnostic cosmology but are not primary Autogenes dossiers in the strict sense. Some have analogous Sons, Christs, Lights, self-generating principles, or celestial humans. Those analogies should not be converted into occurrences of this named being.

Clement’s *Excerpta ex Theodoto*, Tertullian’s *Against the Valentinians*, Hippolytus’s *Refutation*, and Epiphanius’s *Panarion* preserve related Valentinian or “Gnostic” systems. Their value lies chiefly in comparison and reception of earlier heresiological taxonomies, not in supplying an independent biography of the Sethian Autogenes.

## 3. Domain, Attributes, and Iconography

### Domain and functions

Autogenes has no terrestrial portfolio comparable to a city god, storm god, or patron of a craft. His documented domains are cosmological and initiatory:

- **Divine self-manifestation:** image or manifestation of the higher Light. **[EA]**
- **Sonship:** the Child in a Father–Mother–Son structure. **[EA/LS]**
- **Christ and anointing:** in the *Apocryphon*, the Light becomes Christ when the Invisible Spirit anoints him. **[EA/SI]**
- **Generation and ordering of the pleroma:** production or establishment of the four Luminaries and twelve subordinate aeons. **[EA]**
- **Archetype of perfect humanity:** direct emitter of Adamas in Irenaeus; participant in Adamas’s appearance in the *Apocryphon*. **[EA/RV]**
- **Knowledge and Logos:** rational expression of the perfect Mind in *Zostrianos*. **[LE]**
- **Baptismal transformation:** name and realm through which the visionary is purified, sealed, and angelified. **[LE]**
- **Contemplative ascent:** a station perceived before the higher levels Protophanes, Kalyptos, Barbelo, and the Invisible Spirit. **[LE]**

### Powers and limitations

**[EA]** Autogenes can generate or mediate other aeons, cooperate in producing Adamas, receive praise, and become the object of blessing or invocation. He is subordinate to the Invisible Spirit and, in most systems, to Barbelo. He is not omnipotent in isolation, is not the originless first principle, and is not the material creator.

**[LE]** In the Platonizing texts the visionary must pass beyond Autogenes toward increasingly transcendent levels. That placement is not a demotion to evil or ignorance; it locates Autogenes at the boundary where plurality, individuality, life, and intelligibility become articulated.

### Iconography

**[NE]** No securely identified ancient statue, relief, painted panel, gem, temple image, or coin can be assigned to Autogenes. No surviving source provides a fixed bodily type, color, animal, weapon, garment, astronomical body, weekday, number, or emblem exclusive to him.

Relevant imagery is verbal and cosmological:

- Light, spark, radiance, and anointing. **[EA]**
- The four Luminaries surrounding or standing before him. **[EA]**
- Living water, baptism, sealing, and garments of glory. **[LE]**
- Triple-male or androgynous divine fullness. **[LE]**
- A tiered luminous pleroma. **[LE]**

Modern diagrams depicting Autogenes as a young man, winged angel, solar disk, cross-bearing Christ, or occult sigil are interpretive art unless tied to a separately identified source. **[MI]** None is an authenticated ancient iconographic type.

### Sacred associations

- **Number four:** four Luminaries and four luminous realms. **[EA]**
- **Number twelve:** three aeonic attributes associated with each Luminary in the *Apocryphon*. **[EA]**
- **Five:** Five Seals and repeated baptisms in Sethian ritual texts; the exact relation between fivefold ritual and Autogenes varies. **[LE]**
- **Water and light:** strongly attested ritual-metaphysical media. **[LE]**
- **Truth/Aletheia:** syzygy of Autogenes in Irenaeus. **[EA]**

No exclusive sacred animal, plant, metal, incense, festival date, or planetary attribution is securely attested. **[NE]**

## 4. Genealogy and Relations to Other Divine Beings

### Competing genealogies

#### Irenaeus 1.29

**[EA]**

1. An unaging aeon exists in a virginal Spirit.
2. Barbelo and the unnamed Father participate in a sequence producing Foreknowledge, Incorruptibility, Eternal Life, Light/Christ, Mind, and Logos.
3. **Ennoia and Logos emit Autogenes.**
4. Aletheia is emitted with him as consort.
5. Christ and Incorruptibility emit four Luminaries to surround him.
6. Autogenes emits perfect Anthropos/Adamas, paired with Perfect Knowledge.

Here Autogenes is not identical to Christ.

#### *Apocryphon of John*

**[EA/RV]**

1. Invisible Spirit is the ineffable first principle.
2. Barbelo, First Thought and Mother-Father, emerges through self-contemplation.
3. A luminous spark/only Child appears through the Spirit and Barbelo.
4. The Spirit anoints this Child; **he is Christ, the divine Autogenes**.
5. Mind and other powers complete him.
6. Through him the four Luminaries and twelve aeons appear.
7. Adamas is produced through the will of the Spirit and Autogenes.

This recension suppresses Autogenes’s separate consort Aletheia and fuses Autogenes with Christ.

#### *Gospel of the Egyptians*

**[LE/RV]** Autogenes/Logos occupies a dense hierarchy involving the Invisible Spirit, Barbelo-like maternal powers, the Triple-Male Child, Youel, Esephech, Doxomedon, the four Lights, their consorts, and ministers. The system does not reduce cleanly to either Irenaeus or the *Apocryphon*.

#### Platonizing Sethian texts

**[LE]** In *Zostrianos* and *Allogenes*, the Barbelo aeon is differentiated into Kalyptos (“Hidden”), Protophanes (“First-appearing”), and Autogenes (“Self-generated”). Their relation is logical and ontological rather than a simple family tree. Autogenes is the level closest to articulated individuals and souls.

### Every securely documented named divine interaction

The following entries concern explicit meetings, generation, assistance, praise, vision, invocation, or joint action. Mere membership in the same pantheon is excluded.

- **Invisible Spirit — generates, perfects, or anoints Autogenes; Autogenes acts according to the Spirit’s will.** *Apocryphon of John*, second-century composition in four Coptic witnesses. **[EA/SI]**
- **Barbelo — bears or manifests Autogenes as divine Child; in later metaphysics Autogenes is a mode within her aeon.** *Apocryphon of John*; *Zostrianos*; *Allogenes*, second–third centuries. **[EA/LE]**
- **Ennoia — with Logos emits Autogenes.** Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.29.2, c. 175–185. **[EA/RV]**
- **Logos — with Ennoia emits Autogenes; elsewhere Autogenes is himself called Logos.** Irenaeus 1.29; *Gospel of the Egyptians*; *Zostrianos*. **[EA/LE/RV]**
- **Aletheia/Truth — emitted as Autogenes’s syzygy.** Irenaeus 1.29.2. **[EA]**
- **Christ — distinct superior Light in Irenaeus; identical with Autogenes in the *Apocryphon of John*.** Irenaeus 1.29; *Apocryphon of John*. **[EA/SI/RV]**
- **Aphtharsia/Incorruptibility — with Christ emits four Luminaries around Autogenes.** Irenaeus 1.29.2. **[EA]**
- **Nous/Mind — granted as helper to the divine Child; Autogenes becomes or expresses perfect Mind in later texts.** Irenaeus 1.29; *Apocryphon of John*; *Zostrianos*. **[EA/LE]**
- **Harmozel/Armozel — first Luminary attending Autogenes; governs the realm assigned to Adamas.** *Apocryphon of John*; *Gospel of the Egyptians*; *Zostrianos*. **[EA]**
- **Oroiael/Oriel — second Luminary attending Autogenes; governs Seth’s realm.** Same sources. **[EA]**
- **Daveithai/Davithe — third Luminary attending Autogenes; governs the seed of Seth.** Same sources. **[EA]**
- **Eleleth — fourth Luminary attending Autogenes; governs a fourth class of souls and, in the *Hypostasis of the Archons*, separately assists Norea.** *Apocryphon of John* and related texts. Autogenes and Eleleth have no independent dramatic encounter beyond the hierarchical relation. **[EA]**
- **Adamas/Geradamas/Pigeradamas — emitted by Autogenes in Irenaeus; produced through his will in the *Apocryphon*; called Autogenes’s “eye” in *Zostrianos*.** Second–third centuries. **[EA/LE/RV]**
- **Perfect Knowledge — paired with Adamas, who issues from Autogenes.** Irenaeus 1.29.3. The text does not narrate a direct exchange with Autogenes. **[EA]**
- **Seth — placed in the second Luminary’s realm under the higher order of Autogenes; blessed together with Autogenes in *Zostrianos*.** *Apocryphon of John*; *Zostrianos*. **[EA/LE]**
- **Sophia — one of the twelve aeons standing before Autogenes and associated with Eleleth; her independent production of Yaldabaoth initiates the lower cosmogony.** *Apocryphon of John*. No direct conversation or combat between Sophia and Autogenes is narrated. **[EA]**
- **Yaldabaoth — indirectly opposed in cosmological rank: Autogenes belongs to the superior pleroma whose image Yaldabaoth ignorantly imitates.** *Apocryphon of John*. No face-to-face episode is preserved. **[EA/NE as direct interaction]**
- **Kalyptos — higher “hidden” mode within Barbelo, seen above Autogenes by the visionary.** *Zostrianos*; *Allogenes*. **[LE]**
- **Protophanes-Harmedon — noetic “first-appearing” mode above Autogenes; jointly seen by Allogenes.** *Zostrianos*; *Allogenes*. **[LE]**
- **Triple-Male Child/Savior — closely coordinated or partially identified with Autogenes in the later Sethian hierarchy.** *Zostrianos*; *Allogenes*. The damaged texts do not permit a universally agreed identity statement. **[LE/MSR]**
- **Youel — reveals the hierarchy containing Autogenes to Allogenes and anoints or empowers the visionary.** *Allogenes*, third century. **[LE]**
- **Mirothea — associated with Adamas and with Autogenes’s manifestation in *Zostrianos*.** Third century. **[LE]**
- **Prophania — mother or manifestation associated with the Lights beneath Autogenes.** *Zostrianos*. **[LE]**
- **Norea — invokes or cries to the divine Autogenes and four holy helpers.** *Thought of Norea*, second or third century. **[LE]**
- **Zostrianos — human visionary baptized in Autogenes’s name, blesses him, and enters his aeonic region.** *Zostrianos*, late second or third century. **[LE]**
- **Allogenes — visionary beholds the good divine Autogenes during ascent.** *Allogenes*, third century. **[LE]**

**[NE]** No ancient text records Autogenes fighting, marrying, seducing, deceiving, or being killed by another god. No secure interaction with Manda d-Hiia, Ptahil, the Mandaean Great Life, the Manichaean Father of Greatness, First Man, Living Spirit, or Mani is documented.

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### The emanation of the divine Child

**[EA]** In the *Apocryphon of John*, the Invisible Spirit’s first Thought, Barbelo, requests and receives divine attributes. A spark of light appears through their mutual contemplation. The Spirit anoints the light with goodness, producing the Anointed—Christ. The Child receives Mind and participates in the unfolding of the divine fullness.

This is not ordinary birth. The language alternates among thought, reflection, request, generation, appearance, and anointing. “Self-begotten” therefore describes divine self-manifestation within the first principle’s own fullness, not independence from every prior reality.

**Variant:** In Irenaeus, Christ already exists before Ennoia and Logos emit Autogenes. **[EA/RV]** A recent reconstruction argues that this may preserve an earlier system in which Autogenes was a distinct Primordial Human later absorbed into Christ; the *Apocryphon* then reassigned the perfect Human role to Barbelo and Adamas. This is plausible but remains reconstruction rather than manuscript fact ([study of Irenaeus’s system](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110705829-007/pdf?licenseType=free)).

### The establishment of the Four Luminaries

**[EA]** Autogenes brings forth or stands above four luminous powers:

1. Harmozel/Armozel;
2. Oroiael/Oriel;
3. Daveithai/Davithe;
4. Eleleth.

Each contains three aeonic qualities, giving twelve aeons. In a common form:

- Harmozel: Grace, Truth, Form;
- Oroiael: Conception/Insight, Perception, Memory;
- Daveithai: Understanding, Love, Idea;
- Eleleth: Perfection, Peace, Wisdom.

The allocation varies by recension and translation. The Luminaries are at once beings, places, and modes of light. Later texts attach consorts, ministers, baptismal powers, and extensive secondary hierarchies to them.

### The perfect Human and Sethian race

**[EA]** In Irenaeus, Autogenes emits the perfect Human, Adamas. In the *Apocryphon*, Adamas appears through the Invisible Spirit’s foreknowledge, Autogenes’s will, and divine power. The Spirit places Adamas in Harmozel’s realm; Seth in Oroiael; Seth’s seed in Daveithai; and other souls in Eleleth.

This celestial genealogy legitimates the “immovable race”: the saving community understands itself as descended from or spiritually identified with Seth rather than with the ignorant cosmic rulers. Autogenes is not Seth but stands above the celestial structure in which Seth and his descendants dwell.

### Sophia’s error and the lower creation

**[EA]** Sophia, the last of the twelve aeons associated with Autogenes’s luminous order, produces without the proper consent or syzygetic cooperation. Her malformed offspring, Yaldabaoth, generates the archons and material cosmos. Autogenes does not personally fight Yaldabaoth in the surviving *Apocryphon*. His significance is structural: the perfect luminous generation above provides the archetype that the lower creator imitates badly.

Claims that “Autogenes defeated the Demiurge” are therefore modern compression or invention unless “defeat” refers generally to revelatory salvation. **[MI/NE]**

### The baptisms of Zostrianos

**[LE]** Zostrianos ascends through celestial regions and receives multiple baptisms in living water. In the Autogenes stage he is baptized repeatedly by powers including Michar and Micheus, purified by Barpharanges, and sealed by named celestial beings. With each baptism his status changes: contemplative angel, masculine angel, holy angel, perfect angel.

These scenes are the fullest evidence for an Autogenes-centered rite. Whether they reproduce physical community baptism, visionary rehearsal, post-baptismal mystagogy, or literary theurgy is disputed. The safest conclusion is that the text encodes ritual language and progressive transformation; it does not provide an excavated baptismal manual.

### The ascent of Allogenes

**[LE]** Allogenes receives revelations from Youel, practices prolonged contemplative withdrawal, and ascends through divine levels. He sees Autogenes, the youthful Savior/Triple-Male Child, Protophanes, Kalyptos, Barbelo, and finally approaches the unknowable Triple-Powered One through negation.

Autogenes is a station of genuine divinity but not the final object of apophatic contemplation. The myth has become metaphysical pedagogy: ascent requires abandoning positive concepts as the visionary approaches what is beyond being and knowledge. Scholarship connects this setting with the “Gnostics” known to Plotinus’s school. Porphyry explicitly reports revelations of Zostrianos and Allogenes circulating there ([discussion of Porphyry’s testimony](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/abs/existencelifeintellect-triad-in-gnosticism-and-neoplatonism/7170A9B11896EDD21623AFD61B75DE15)).

### The Sethian hymn and contemplative cycle

**[LE]** *Three Steles of Seth* moves upward through hymns addressed to Geradamas, Barbelo, and the Unbegotten One and then implies return. Autogenetic identity operates within this praise. John Turner argues that its prayers suit communal contemplative ascent and “spiritual sacrifice,” not animal sacrifice ([Turner and Majercik on ritual](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/TurnerMajercik.pdf)).

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

### Material record

**[NE]** No temple, sanctuary, altar, priestly archive, dedicatory inscription, votive statue, coin type, procession, civic festival, sacred estate, or theophoric personal name is securely attributable to a cult of Autogenes. “Pantheon” is useful in an atlas but may falsely suggest a Greek or Egyptian temple cult.

The archaeological objects are principally books: codices that preserve theology, hymns, revelations, and ritual language.

### Manuscript communities

**[EA/LE]** The Nag Hammadi collection consists of thirteen papyrus codices discovered near Nag Hammadi in Egypt in 1945. The bindings and papyri are generally assigned to the fourth century. Their assemblage may reflect monastic, ascetic, private scholarly, ritual, or bibliophile interests; ownership by a single self-identified “Sethian church” has not been demonstrated. The Coptic Museum describes the Nag Hammadi codices as a major fourth-century papyrus group ([Coptic Museum](https://egymonuments.gov.eg/en/subportals-group/coptic-museum/coptic-museum/manuscripts/)).

### Baptism and the Five Seals

**[EA/LE]** Sethian texts repeatedly employ baptism, washing, sealing, robing, naming, enthronement, and luminous water:

- *Zostrianos* narrates repeated baptisms in Autogenes’s name.
- *Trimorphic Protennoia* associates the revealer’s final descent with the Five Seals and a garment of light.
- *Gospel of the Egyptians* concludes with baptismal and doxological material.
- *Apocryphon of John* provides the cosmology presupposed by such rites.

Scholars dispute whether the Five Seals were five immersions, five invocations, five ritual stages, a visionary seal, chrism, or combinations of these. **[MSR]** The sources support an initiatory complex but not a single recoverable standardized liturgy.

### Prayer and hymnody

**[LE]** Autogenes is praised in *Three Steles of Seth*, blessed by Zostrianos, invoked by Norea, and included in the great pleromatic praise of the *Gospel of the Egyptians*. These are stronger indications of devotional practice than abstract cosmological mention.

### Sacrifice, sexuality, and misconduct allegations

**[NE]** No reliable source documents human sacrifice, animal sacrifice, temple prostitution, cult prostitution, or ritual murder offered to Autogenes.

**[EA but hostile testimony]** Heresiologists accused some groups classified as “Gnostic,” “Barbeloite,” “Borborite,” or “Phibionite” of sexual rites, consumption of sexual fluids, abortion, and other transgressive practices. Epiphanius’s fourth-century *Panarion* is the principal lurid witness. Those allegations postdate Irenaeus, are polemical, and cannot be transferred automatically to the readers of the *Apocryphon*, *Zostrianos*, or *Allogenes*. Neither Autogenes nor an Autogenes cult is securely tied to the alleged acts.

**[LE]** The clearest sacrifice associated with the Sethian ascent texts is metaphorical or “spiritual sacrifice”: praise, knowledge, and contemplative ascent.

### Institutions and clergy

**[NE]** No office titled “priest of Autogenes” is attested. No rules of recruitment, property privileges, temple economy, or public cult calendar survive. The existence of teachers, initiators, scribes, readers, and ritual specialists is reasonable but usually inferred rather than named.

## 7. Historical Evolution of the Figure

### Before approximately 175 CE: formative background

**[MSR]** The figure likely emerged in a Greek-speaking Jewish, Christian, or mixed intellectual environment that combined Genesis exegesis, personified Wisdom and Thought, heavenly-human traditions, baptismal language, and Middle Platonic metaphysics. Turner has proposed developmental stages involving a fusion of “Barbeloite” and “Sethite” materials; other scholars question whether those hypothetical communities can be recovered from literary strata.

No first-century inscription or manuscript securely names the Sethian Autogenes. **[NE]**

### Approximately 175–185: external attestation

**[EA]** Irenaeus records Autogenes in a Barbelo-centered genealogy. His testimony proves that the name and core luminous hierarchy existed by the late second century. It does not prove that adherents called themselves “Sethians” or “Barbeloites.”

### Late second to early third century: Christian Sethian synthesis

**[EA/MSR]** The *Apocryphon of John* identifies Autogenes with Christ and embeds him in a revelation by the risen Savior to John. The figure becomes the divine Christ above the creator of Genesis. The Four Luminaries and celestial Adamas–Seth genealogy integrate cosmology, anthropology, and salvation.

Dating the short recension earlier than the long one is common but not uncontested. The precise direction of redaction among the four witnesses remains debated.

### Second–third century: liturgical elaboration

**[LE]** The *Gospel of the Egyptians*, *Thought of Norea*, *Three Steles of Seth*, and *Trimorphic Protennoia* expand doxology, invocation, descent, sealing, and baptism. Autogenes may be a named recipient of praise, a cosmic Logos, or an implicit position in a triadic structure.

### Third century: Platonizing transformation

**[LE]** *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, and *Marsanes* place Autogenes within an increasingly technical metaphysics. Christian narrative recedes; apophatic ascent, ontological triads, and contemplation dominate. Autogenes becomes a level of divine Life, Mind, individuality, or self-generation within Barbelo.

John Turner reconstructs this as a post-Christian or de-Christianized Sethian phase increasingly engaged with Platonism. Critics caution that literary differences need not map neatly onto one sect’s chronological evolution. The broad engagement with contemporary Platonism is secure; the exact institutional history is not.

### 260s CE: encounter with Plotinus’s school

**[EA]** Porphyry reports that revelations attributed to Zostrianos, Allogenes, and others circulated among Plotinus’s associates. Plotinus composed the treatise now numbered *Ennead* II.9 against positions associated with these “Gnostics”; Amelius and Porphyry also wrote refutations. This is the only firm placement of texts from the Autogenes complex in a named philosophical circle.

The encounter was controversy, not simple influence in one direction. Similar triadic metaphysics may indicate borrowing, a shared Middle Platonic environment, debate, or reciprocal development.

### Fourth–fifth centuries: manuscript preservation and heresiology

**[LE]** Coptic translations were copied in Egypt. Meanwhile Epiphanius and other church writers repeated and expanded classifications of Gnostic groups. Imperial Christianization marginalized many non-Nicene and non-episcopal movements, but there is no documented decree or raid specifically suppressing “the cult of Autogenes.”

The Nag Hammadi books were deposited or discarded in the fourth or later fourth century. Claims that monks hid them from Athanasius’s 367 Festal Letter remain possible but unproved. **[MSR/NE]**

### Medieval period

**[NE]** No continuous medieval community worshipping Autogenes under that name is documented. Bogomil and Cathar dualisms are sometimes called “Gnostic,” but a direct institutional transmission of the Sethian Autogenes is unproved. There is no secure Mandaean or Manichaean preservation of this proper name.

### Mandaean comparison

**[MSR/NE]** Mandaean texts preserve a World of Light, emanated Lives and uthras, baptism, ascent, and a demiurgic Ptahil. The *Ginza Rabba*’s creation material speaks of First Great Life, Second Life/Yoshamin, Third Life/Abatur, and Fourth Life/Ptahil ([study of the *Ginza* creation narrative](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1444088/?mobile-app=true&theme=dark)). These features make comparison worthwhile.

They do not establish that Autogenes became Yoshamin, Abatur, Ptahil, Manda d-Hiia, or any other Mandaean being. Mandaeism has its own language, ritual continuity, priesthood, scriptures, and evolving historical setting. Calling its beings “cousins” is comparative shorthand, not genealogy.

### Manichaean comparison

**[MSR/NE]** Mani’s third-century religion has a Father of Greatness, successive divine evocations, First Man, Living Spirit, Third Messenger, Jesus the Splendor, and a complex rescue of Light. Manichaean theology often deliberately avoids sexual “generation” language in favor of the Father “calling forth” divine powers. Its Coptic *Kephalaia* survive principally in early fifth-century codices ([Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Kephalaia”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kephalaia/)); its pantheon is surveyed in [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-ii-the-manichean-pantheon/).

No source makes Autogenes a Manichaean god. Similarities concern emanation, light, savior figures, and layered heavens. Direct identity is unestablished.

### Nineteenth century to present

**[MSR/MI]** Before the Coptic discoveries, Autogenes was known chiefly through Irenaeus and later heresiologists. Publication of the Berlin Codex and then Nag Hammadi transformed him from an isolated heresiological name into a recoverable element of multiple primary texts.

Modern Gnostic churches, esoteric readers, and online reconstructionists sometimes invoke “Christos Autogenes,” create diagrams, prayers, seals, or guided ascents, and treat the Sethian corpus as a practical canon. Such activity is a modern reception of ancient manuscripts, not evidence of uninterrupted ancient worship.

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Scholarship-driven recovery

**[MSR]** Autogenes’s modern visibility depends almost entirely on manuscript publication:

- Carl Schmidt acquired and began studying Berlin Codex 8502.
- Walter Till’s 1955 edition made its Coptic Gnostic texts broadly available.
- The Nag Hammadi codices yielded three more witnesses to the *Apocryphon* and the first surviving copies of *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, *Marsanes*, and related texts.
- The *Coptic Gnostic Library* supplied critical Coptic editions.
- James M. Robinson’s *Nag Hammadi Library in English*, Bentley Layton’s *Gnostic Scriptures*, Marvin Meyer’s collections, and later international editions expanded English access.

### Modern Gnostic religion

**[MI]** Nineteenth-century French Gnostic churches predate discovery of Nag Hammadi and therefore could not have inherited detailed Autogenes theology from those codices. Twentieth- and twenty-first-century churches can incorporate it through published translations.

The Ecclesia Gnostica, established in its modern form through Richard Duc de Palatine and developed in the United States by Stephan Hoeller, uses material from Nag Hammadi alongside Christian, Hermetic, Manichaean, and Mandaean sources. Its own account distinguishes the church from secret initiatory orders ([Ecclesia Gnostica position paper](https://gnosis.org/ecclesia/PositionSIOrders.htm)). That synthesis is modern and selective.

No reliable census isolates worshippers of Autogenes, and no large independent “Church of Autogenes” is documented. **[NE]**

### Occultism, Thelema, Golden Dawn, and chaos magic

**[NE]** Autogenes does not have a securely documented major role in the foundational rituals of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, Crowley’s *Liber XV* Gnostic Mass, or the classical grimoires. Modern occult writers sometimes import him into aeonic maps or personal rituals after reading Nag Hammadi. Those uses are modern constructions.

Aleister Crowley’s “Gnostic Mass” is Thelemic and was composed before the Nag Hammadi corpus became available; its title does not establish a historical Autogenes cult. **[MI]**

### Jungian and New Age reception

**[MI]** Carl Jung treated “Gnostic” myth as psychologically significant but did not leave a standard cult of Autogenes. Later Jungian and New Age writers may interpret Autogenes as the emergent Self, autonomous consciousness, divine child, or self-realizing mind. These are psychological analogies, not translations of an ancient doctrine.

April DeConick documents the broad modern reinvention of “Gnosticism” as countercultural and New Age spirituality ([*The Gnostic New Age*](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/the-gnostic-new-age/9780231542043/)). That history explains the environment in which Autogenes is repersonalized, but it does not show continuous transmission.

### Digital and popular reinvention

**[MI]** Online deity catalogs frequently assign Autogenes images, symbols, correspondences, or simplified genealogies. Typical errors include:

- equating him unqualifiedly with the Monad;
- calling him the material creator;
- treating Kalyptos and Protophanes as ordinary siblings in every source;
- assigning planetary, zodiacal, elemental, or color correspondences without ancient evidence;
- equating him with Abraxas, Chnoubis, Lucifer, Atum, or the Manichaean Father of Greatness;
- claiming a single ancient “Sethian church” with a uniform creed;
- supplying sigils that are modern graphic designs.

A modern video game or artwork using *autogeny* or *Autogenes* cannot be assumed to depict this being merely because the words share a Greek root.

### Political and extremist appropriation

**[NE]** No substantial, well-documented nationalist or extremist movement centered on Autogenes was located. General modern rhetoric about “Gnosticism” as nihilism, revolution, elitism, or secret global control usually concerns a polemical category rather than this divine figure. Applying those theories to Autogenes without textual evidence is modern ideological projection.

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### The category “Gnosticism”

Hans Jonas and Kurt Rudolph presented Gnosticism as a recognizable late-antique religion of alienation, knowledge, and cosmic dualism. Their syntheses remain influential but sometimes flatten major textual differences.

Michael Williams, in *Rethinking “Gnosticism”*, and Karen King, in *What Is Gnosticism?*, argued that the category derives partly from ancient heresiological polemic and modern typology. **[MSR]** Their critique does not make Autogenes fictitious; it warns against imagining that every work using similar mythology came from one church.

### “Sethianism”

Hans-Martin Schenke identified a Sethian corpus through recurring traits: the Invisible Spirit–Barbelo–Autogenes triad, heavenly Seth, the immovable race, four Luminaries, and related ritual patterns. John D. Turner developed a literary history progressing through pre-Christian, Christian, and Platonizing phases.

The strength of this model is its explanatory power across a real constellation of repeated names and structures. Its weakness is that “Sethian” is primarily a modern taxonomic category; the manuscripts do not supply a single membership list, founder, creed, or institutional history.

### Was Autogenes originally Christ?

There are at least three positions:

1. **Identity is fundamental:** all four *Apocryphon* recensions call him Christ, so Christian identification belongs to the recoverable text’s core. **[EA]**
2. **Christological identification is a Christianizing redaction:** an earlier non-Christian Autogenes myth was adapted to a revelation of Christ. **[MSR]**
3. **Multiple related systems coexisted:** Irenaeus and the *Apocryphon* preserve different configurations rather than a simple linear evolution. **[MSR]**

The four-recension agreement makes the equation early within the *Apocryphon*’s recoverable transmission. It does not establish that the figure’s ultimate origin was Christian.

### Irenaeus versus the *Apocryphon*

In Irenaeus:

- Christ precedes Autogenes.
- Ennoia and Logos emit Autogenes.
- Aletheia is his partner.
- Autogenes emits Adamas.

In the *Apocryphon*:

- Christ and Autogenes are one.
- Barbelo and the Invisible Spirit generate or manifest the Child.
- The separate syzygy with Aletheia disappears.
- Adamas appears through the coordinated will of higher powers.

Roelof van den Broek and later scholars infer that Autogenes may once have occupied a more exalted Primordial-Human position before assimilation to Christ. **[MSR]** The hypothesis explains the differences but depends on reconstruction of lost sources.

### Sethianism and Platonism

Turner argues for sustained exchange between Sethian thinkers and Platonists, culminating in *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, *Three Steles*, and *Marsanes*. The Existence–Life–Mind structures and apophatic ascent are central evidence. Plotinus’s polemic and Porphyry’s testimony establish contact by the 260s.

Debate concerns direction and chronology:

- Sethians borrowed from Middle Platonism or early Neoplatonism;
- Plotinus reacted to and perhaps learned from Gnostic formulations;
- both used a shared philosophical vocabulary;
- the surviving Coptic works may contain revisions later than the Greek texts known in Plotinus’s circle.

The safest result is reciprocal intellectual proximity, not a one-way derivation.

### Ritual or metaphor?

The Five Seals, multiple baptisms, living waters, seals, names, and garments may reflect:

- enacted water baptism;
- visionary ascent;
- liturgical recitation;
- post-baptismal instruction;
- theurgical contemplation;
- literary dramatization.

No surviving Sethian church order settles the question. Ritual interpretation is well supported; exact reconstruction is not.

### Primary evidence and its repositories

- Nag Hammadi codices: principally Coptic Museum, Cairo; some leaves historically passed through other collections.
- Berlin Codex 8502: Ägyptisches Museum und Papyrussammlung, Berlin.
- Askew Codex: British Library, preserving *Pistis Sophia* but not a primary Autogenes text.
- Bruce Codex: Bodleian Library, Oxford, preserving the *Books of Jeu* and an untitled Gnostic work.
- Codex Tchacos: best known for the *Gospel of Judas*; not a primary witness to the classical Sethian Autogenes.

The main documentary limitation is that nearly all direct witnesses are Coptic translations made centuries after the proposed Greek compositions. The Greek originals are lost.

### Discredited or unsupported claims still circulating

- **“Autogenes was an Egyptian solar god with temples.”** **[MI/NE]**
- **“Autogenes is another name for Abraxas.”** **[MI/NE]**
- **“The Mandaeans preserved Autogenes unchanged.”** **[MI/NE]**
- **“Mani worshipped Autogenes.”** **[MI/NE]**
- **“Sethian priests sacrificed humans to Autogenes.”** **[MI/NE]**
- **“A known ancient sigil represents Autogenes.”** **[MI/NE]**
- **“All ancient Gnostics shared the Invisible Spirit–Barbelo–Autogenes trinity.”** **[LS/NE]**
- **“Nag Hammadi proves a continuous secret church survived unchanged to modern occult orders.”** **[MI/NE]**

### Contested-material conclusion

The dedicated searches for Autogenes in connection with etymology, origin, syncretism, sacrifice, suppression, occultism, neopaganism, and modern reception produced a sharply asymmetrical record. Etymology, literary origin, Christological identity, Sethian classification, Platonic interaction, and baptismal ritual are genuine scholarly disputes. Human sacrifice, temple prostitution, temples, exclusive iconography, ancient sigils, Mandaean or Manichaean identity, continuous medieval worship, and specific extremist appropriation are not established. Modern ritual and occult uses exist, but are reconstructions after publication of the manuscripts.

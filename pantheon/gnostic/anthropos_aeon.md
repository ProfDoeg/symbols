# Anthropos (the aeon Man): Research Dossier

**Class:** In Valentinian Christian cosmology, a male **aeon**—an eternal, personal hypostasis within the divine Fullness (*Plerōma*)—paired with the female aeon Ecclesia (“Church”). In related but non-identical Sethian systems, the corresponding figure is the heavenly, perfect Human **Adamas** or **Pigeradamas**; in *Poimandres*, a divine archetypal **Man** generated by the supreme Mind; among the Naassenes, the transcendent “Man” and “Son of Man”; in Mandaeism, the Hidden or Secret Adam; and in Manichaeism, the militant Primal Man. These figures must not be collapsed into one deity.

**Earliest secure attestation:** The earliest datable, explicit attestation of **Anthropos as a named aeon** is Irenaeus of Lyon’s Greek *Detection and Overthrow of Knowledge Falsely So Called*, conventionally *Against Heresies* 1.1, composed about **180 CE**. Irenaeus says that Valentinian teachers placed Anthropos and Ecclesia in the primordial Ogdoad and derived them from Logos and Zoe. His is a hostile report, but it is early, unusually detailed, and partly corroborated by independent Valentinian writings. The earliest surviving physical manuscripts that transmit cognate heavenly-Human myths are mostly fourth-century Coptic codices, not second-century originals.

## Evidentiary labels

- **[Early attested]** Securely attested in a second- or early-third-century source, although sometimes preserved in a later manuscript.
- **[Later ancient elaboration]** A development in later antique scripture, commentary, or heresiology.
- **[Regional/systemic variant]** A parallel construction belonging to a distinct school or religion.
- **[Learned systematization]** A theologian’s or heresiologist’s ordered synthesis.
- **[Syncretistic identification]** An ancient or modern equation between figures not originally identical.
- **[Medieval/early-modern accretion]** A later Jewish, Christian, Hermetic, or esoteric development.
- **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]** A proposed historical relationship rather than an explicit ancient identification.
- **[Modern invention/reception]** A recent ritual, occult, psychological, literary, or internet construction.

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### The Valentinian Anthropos

The Greek common noun **ἄνθρωπος**, *ánthrōpos*, means “human being,” “person,” or generically “man.” In Valentinian cosmology it becomes a proper divine name, **ὁ Ἄνθρωπος**, “the Man” or “Human.” Latin heresiologists translate it as **Homo**. This is a hypostatization, not an assertion that a deceased terrestrial man was deified.

In the Ptolemaean Valentinian system reported by Irenaeus:

1. Bythos/Depth and Sige/Silence generate Nous/Mind and Aletheia/Truth.
2. Nous and Aletheia generate Logos/Word and Zoe/Life.
3. Logos and Zoe generate **Anthropos/Man and Ecclesia/Church**.
4. These four male-female pairs constitute the primordial Ogdoad.
5. Anthropos and Ecclesia then generate twelve further aeons, completing the Thirty.

Irenaeus preserves the formula explicitly: “from the conjunction of Logos and Zoe were brought forth Anthropos and Ecclesia.” He also says every pair is jointly masculine-feminine in the structural sense that each male aeon has a female *syzygos*, not that every named aeon is necessarily an androgynous body. [Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.1, c. 180 CE](https://logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/101.html).

**Documented titles and descriptions:**

- **Anthropos** — “Human/Man”: Irenaeus 1.1; Tertullian, *Against the Valentinians*.
- **Aeon** — a constituent of the Pleroma: Irenaeus 1.1.
- **Fourth male principle of the Ogdoad** — when the male members alone are enumerated as Bythos, Nous, Logos, Anthropos.
- **Syzygos of Ecclesia** — Irenaeus 1.1.
- **Parent of the Dodecad** — together with Ecclesia, in the Ptolemaean scheme.
- **Pre-existent Man**, Greek *proōn anthrōpos* or *anthrōpos proōn* — the heavenly model in whose image earthly Adam is produced; attested in Valentinian reports and Tertullian’s Latin paraphrase.
- **Power of the Highest** — in a Marcosian exegesis of Luke 1:35 reported by Irenaeus, the “Power of the Highest” occupies the place of Anthropos, while Gabriel corresponds to Logos, the Holy Spirit to Zoe, and Mary to Ecclesia. This is an exegetical correspondence, not a universal name for Anthropos. [Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.14](https://ccel.org/ccel/irenaeus/against_heresies_i/anf01.ix.ii.xvi.html).

### Variant Valentinian genealogies

Anthropos’ place was disputed inside Valentinian Christianity.

- **Ptolemaean scheme [early attested]:** Logos + Zoe → Anthropos + Ecclesia.
- **Colorbasian variant [early attested regional/systemic variant]:** Anthropos + Ecclesia precede and generate Logos + Zoe. Irenaeus explicitly contrasts this with “others.” [Irenaeus 1.12](https://logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/112.html).
- **Other Valentinian variants [early attested]:** Jesus is variously generated from Theletos, Christ, or Anthropos and Ecclesia. [Irenaeus 1.11](https://logoslibrary.org/irenaeus/heresies/111.html).
- **Marcosian numerical and scriptural systematization:** Anthropos is incorporated into an alphabetic and numerical exegesis of the Ogdoad; this is not evidence that every Valentinian school accepted Marcus’ constructions.

Thus “son of Logos and Zoe” is the best-attested Ptolemaean arrangement, not an uncontested universal dogma.

### Sethian names

The heavenly Human of the Sethian texts is normally not called simply the Valentinian aeon Anthropos. Its names include:

- **Adamas**, Ἀδάμας in the presumed Greek underlying text; represented in Coptic translations. The name plays upon Adam while also suggesting Greek *adámas*, “unconquerable,” “adamantine.”
- **The perfect Human**, **holy Human**, **first Human**, **true Human**.
- **Adam**, as the heavenly archetype, distinct from the psychophysical Adam manufactured by the rulers.
- **Pigeradamas**, an expanded form used especially in *Zostrianos* and associated texts; its precise morphology and transmission remain disputed.
- **Adam of Light** or **luminous Adam**, especially in *Eugnostos* and *On the Origin of the World*.
- **Geradamas** and related forms in portions of the wider Sethian corpus.

The short recension of the *Apocryphon of John* calls the first heavenly Human “Adamas,” places him in the first aeon of Harmozel, and makes the lower rulers copy his revealed image. [*Apocryphon of John*, short recension](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/apocjn-short.html).

### Hermetic Man

In *Corpus Hermeticum* I, *Poimandres*, the name is simply **ὁ ἄνθρωπος**, “the Man.” He is not a member of a Valentinian Thirty. The supreme Nous, described as Father of all, generates him in its own image. The Man is beautiful, bears the Father’s image, receives authority over the cosmos, passes through the seven planetary spheres, reveals the divine form to Nature, and unites with her.

“Poimandres” is the revealer, not the Man. The etymology of **Ποιμάνδρης** has been disputed: the traditional Greek hearing connects it with “shepherd of men,” while Egyptian derivations have also been proposed. Neither etymology turns the Hermetic Man into the Valentinian Anthropos automatically.

### Naassene names

Pseudo-Hippolytus’ *Refutation* 5 reports a group he calls the **Naassenes**, probably connecting their name with Hebrew נָחָשׁ, *nāḥāš*, “serpent.” They allegedly exalt:

- **Man**
- **Son of Man**
- **Adamas**
- **the Man above**
- **the blessed Man**
- **the unportrayed or uncharacterized Man**
- **the perfect Man**
- **Papa**, in a Phrygian interpretive equation

The report says they distinguish the blessed nature of the upper Adam from mortal nature below and interpret diverse Greek, Phrygian, biblical, and mystery materials through that distinction. [Pseudo-Hippolytus, *Refutation* 5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050105.htm).

### Mandaean names

The closest Mandaean construction is **Adam Kasia**, Classical Mandaic ࡀࡃࡀࡌ ࡊࡀࡎࡉࡀ, “Hidden Adam” or “Secret Adam,” also contracted as **Adakas**, and sometimes **Adakas Ziwa**, “Radiant Adakas.” He is distinguished from **Adam Pagria/Pagra**, the bodily Adam. Drower’s phrase “Secret Adam” became standard in English-language discussion.

Adam Kasia may be identified or closely coordinated with **Šišlam Rabba**, the primordial priest and heavenly prototype of the Mandaean priesthood. This is a Mandaean system, preserved in texts with complicated and often late manuscript transmission; it is not direct evidence about second-century Valentinians.

### Manichaean names

The Manichaean **Primal Man/First Man** is:

- Syriac **Nāšā Qadmāyā**, “First Man”
- Middle Persian **Ohrmazd Bay**, “the god Ohrmazd”
- Parthian and Sogdian equivalents adapted to local Iranian divine vocabulary
- Greek **Prōtos Anthrōpos**
- Latin **Primus Homo**
- Coptic forms translating “First Man”

He is a warrior emanation sent against the Kingdom of Darkness, not the passive eighth aeon of the Valentinian Ogdoad. The equation “Anthropos = Manichaean Primal Man” is therefore comparative, not an ancient identity. The Manichaean pantheon deliberately used Iranian divine names in Iranian-language settings. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Manicheism: The Manichean Pantheon”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-ii-the-manichean-pantheon/).

### Adam Kadmon

Hebrew **אָדָם קַדְמוֹן**, *ʾĀdām Qadmōn*, means “Primordial Man.” The fully articulated Adam Kadmon belongs to **medieval Kabbalah and especially sixteenth-century Lurianic Kabbalah**, not to the second-century Valentinian archive.

Earlier Jewish materials—Genesis 1:26–27, Ezekiel’s humanlike throne-figure, Philo’s intelligible/heavenly man, rabbinic speculation on primordial Adam, and *Shiʿur Qomah*—provide precedents for heavenly anthropomorphism. Scholem judged that the *Bahir* contains elements of the idea but not yet a clear, complete Adam Kadmon containing the whole Kabbalistic pleroma. [Scholem, *Origins of the Kabbalah*](https://kronadaran.am/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/G.-Scholem.-Origins-of-the-Kabbalah.pdf).

Consequently:

- **Similarity:** both are cosmic or intelligible Human figures and may function as paradigms of lower humanity.
- **Difference:** Valentinian Anthropos is one paired aeon in an Ogdoad; Lurianic Adam Kadmon is the first configuration or “world” after divine contraction and the channel through which the sefirotic order emerges.
- **Historical verdict:** direct descent is possible only as a hypothesis about long-term traditions of Jewish and late-antique anthropomorphic speculation. It is not demonstrated by an ancient text equating the two.

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### Earliest textual witness

Irenaeus composed *Against Heresies* principally during the later second century, commonly around 180 CE. The work was written in Greek; much survives completely only in an early Latin translation, with Greek fragments and ancient Armenian and Syriac witnesses. He wrote as a polemicist, but his orderly report of Valentinian aeons preserves technical distinctions and variant genealogies unlikely to have been invented wholesale.

What this evidence establishes:

- By c. 180, at least some Valentinian Christians named Anthropos as an aeon.
- They paired him with Ecclesia.
- Ptolemaean teachers derived the pair from Logos and Zoe.
- Rival Valentinian sequences existed.
- Anthropos was invoked in exegesis of Genesis, John, Luke, and “Son of Man” language.

What it does **not** establish:

- That Valentinus himself devised every reported detail.
- That there was a single international “cult of Anthropos.”
- That Hermetic, Sethian, Naassene, Mandaean, and Manichaean heavenly Men were one worshipped god.
- That Irenaeus’ sarcastic presentation reproduces ritual practice without distortion.

### Principal corpus

#### Valentinian and heresiological evidence

- **Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1** — Greek, c. 180; principal witness for Anthropos and Ecclesia in the Ogdoad, their descendants, competing genealogies, and scriptural exegesis.
- **Clement of Alexandria, *Excerpta ex Theodoto*** — Greek excerpts and notes, late second/early third century; evidence for eastern Valentinian teaching about aeons, the Pleroma, Sophia, Christ, Jesus, and the Church. The anthology’s boundaries between Theodotus, other Valentinians, and Clement’s comments are not always certain. [English text based on Casey’s edition](https://www.gnosis.org/library/excr.htm).
- **Tertullian, *Adversus Valentinianos*** — Latin, c. 206–212; a rhetorically hostile retelling, dependent substantially on earlier heresiology but valuable for Latin terminology and western reception.
- **Pseudo-Hippolytus, *Refutatio omnium haeresium*** — Greek, early third century; Naassene, Peratic, Sethian, Justinian, and Valentinian materials. Authorship by Hippolytus remains disputed.
- **Epiphanius, *Panarion*** — Greek, c. 374–377; much later compilation preserving documents and accusations alongside derivation from earlier heresiologists. It must not automatically be treated as independent second-century evidence.

#### Direct Valentinian texts from Nag Hammadi

- **Gospel of Truth**, NHC I,3 and fragments in XII — Greek composition commonly placed in the second century; fourth-century Coptic manuscripts. It presents the Father, Name, aeons, error, revelation, and the perfect Human, but does not simply reproduce Irenaeus’ thirty-name genealogy. [Text](https://gnosis.org/naghamm/got.html).
- **Tripartite Tractate**, NHC I,5 — probably third-century Greek composition, surviving in fourth-century Coptic; an extensive Valentinian protology and soteriology. Its ordered classes and emanations constitute an independent Valentinian system, not a transparent copy of Irenaeus.
- **Valentinian Exposition**, NHC XI,2, with liturgical appendices — fragmentary Coptic witness to Valentinian doctrine and ritual.
- **Interpretation of Knowledge**, NHC XI,1 — Christian text with Valentinian affinities.
- **Gospel of Philip**, NHC II,3 — third-century Valentinian sacramental anthology; important for images, names, bridal-chamber symbolism, and the distinction between archetype and image.
- **First Apocalypse of James** and related writings — relevant to Valentinian or adjacent Christian networks but not direct biographies of Anthropos.

#### Sethian heavenly-Human texts

- **Apocryphon of John** — four Coptic witnesses: the long recension in NHC II,1 and IV,1; short recension in NHC III,1 and Berlin Codex 8502. A Greek form existed by the second century because Irenaeus 1.29 knows closely related material, although the extant recension history is debated. Its Adamas is the perfect heavenly Human whose image the rulers imitate.
- **Hypostasis of the Archons**, NHC II,4 — probably third century; retells Genesis with a heavenly incorruptible model, the rulers’ failed Adam, the spiritual Woman, and Norea.
- **On the Origin of the World**, NHC II,5 and fragment NHC XIII — probably later second or third century; “Adam of Light,” cosmic rulers, Sophia, Eve, and eschatology. [Text](https://gnosis.org/naghamm/origin.html).
- **Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit/Gospel of the Egyptians**, NHC III,2 and IV,2 — Sethian baptismal and doxological material; Adamas and the incorruptible race.
- **Eugnostos the Blessed**, NHC III,3 and V,1 — non-narrative revelatory wisdom text; describes Immortal Androgynous Man and “Adam of Light.” [Text](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/eugn.html).
- **Sophia of Jesus Christ**, NHC III,4 and BG 8502 — Christianized dialogue extensively dependent on *Eugnostos*.
- **Zostrianos**, NHC VIII,1 — Platonizing Sethian ascent apocalypse, likely third century; versions circulated among readers criticized by Plotinus’ circle.
- **Allogenes**, NHC XI,3 — Platonizing Sethian revelation; likely third century.
- **Trimorphic Protennoia**, NHC XIII,1 — Sethian revelatory monologue, probably second or third century, with signs of redaction and Christianization. [Turner translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/trimorph-JDT.html).
- **Three Steles of Seth**, NHC VII,5 — contemplative hymns invoking Geradamas, Seth, Barbelo, and the transcendent triad.
- **Apocalypse of Adam**, NHC V,5 — Adam’s revelation to Seth; no simple named aeon Anthropos, but an exalted anthropology and conflict between the seed of Seth and the creator’s powers. [Text](https://gnosis.org/naghamm/adam.html).

Brill’s **Coptic Gnostic Library Online** supplies the authoritative Coptic texts and translations for much of this corpus and enumerates 46 works in 55 Nag Hammadi and Berlin versions. [Brill CGL](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/cglo/).

#### Hermetic evidence

- **Corpus Hermeticum I, *Poimandres*** — Greek, generally assigned to the first centuries CE, often the second or third century; surviving through medieval Byzantine manuscripts. The divine Man is generated by Nous, receives the cosmos, descends through the spheres, loves the reflected divine form in Nature, and becomes the cause of terrestrial humanity’s double nature.
- **Corpus Hermeticum X, *The Key*** — offers related accounts of Mind, cosmos, soul, and humanity but should not be made to agree mechanically with CH I.
- **Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth**, NHC VI,6 — Coptic Hermetic initiatory dialogue; ascension, rebirth, praise, and visionary union, but not a full retelling of CH I’s Man-and-Nature episode.
- Standard critical edition: A.-J. Festugière and A. D. Nock, *Corpus Hermeticum*, with Brian Copenhaver’s English translation a standard modern point of access. Scholarship has specifically challenged Hans Jonas’ treatment of *Poimandres* as the exemplary “Gnostic” Anthropos myth. [Critical discussion](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jclst/28/0/28_KJ00005744622/_article/-char/en).

#### Pistis Sophia and Books of Jeu

- **Pistis Sophia/Books of the Savior** — heterogeneous Coptic dialogue, conventionally dated to the third or fourth century; preserved only in the Askew Codex, British Library Add. 5114. Proposed manuscript dates range from the fourth century later, while composition may be third–fourth century. It contains elaborate light-world hierarchies but is not direct evidence for the Valentinian Ogdoad. [NASSCAL description](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/pistis-sophia/).
- **Books of Jeu** — Coptic ritual-cosmological works in Bodleian Bruce 96, the Bruce Codex; sole surviving witness, acquired in Upper Egypt by James Bruce about 1769. Their diagrams, seals, passwords, baptisms, and treasury-worlds document a late antique initiatory system. Their supreme hierarchies should not be retrojected into Irenaeus’ second-century report. [Online text and manuscript history](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/booksjeu.html).

#### Mandaean corpus

- **Ginza Rba/Ginza Rabba**, “Great Treasure” — Classical Mandaic compilation divided into Right and Left Ginza. Its units have different histories; surviving manuscripts are far later than the proposed origins of many traditions. The work narrates creation, Adam and Hawa, messengers of Life, the soul’s descent and ascent, and conflict with Ruha and the planets.
- **Qolasta**, canonical prayer collection — baptismal, masiqta, priestly, and commemorative liturgies.
- **Book of John** — teachings and narratives involving John, messengers, Adam, and hostile powers.
- **Drower Collection manuscripts** — crucial twentieth-century manuscript record, supplemented by fieldwork among living Mandaeans.
- Editions and translations include Mark Lidzbarski’s German *Ginzā*, E. S. Drower’s works, and later Mandaean-sponsored translations. The printed *Ginza* tradition began with nineteenth-century editions; that publication date is not the composition date of each tractate. [Ginza publication history](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.31826/9781463212247/html).

#### Manichaean corpus

- **Cologne Mani Codex** — tiny Greek parchment codex, probably fifth century, containing traditions about Mani’s youth and revelations.
- **Coptic Kephalaia** — fourth/fifth-century codices from Medinet Madi preserving discourses attributed to Mani and later school material.
- **Coptic Manichaean Psalm-Book**, Homilies, Synaxeis, Acts, and letters.
- **Middle Persian, Parthian, Sogdian, Old Uyghur, Chinese, and other Turfan fragments**, discovered principally through early-twentieth-century expeditions.
- **Šābuhragān fragments**, from Mani’s Middle Persian royal presentation.
- **Augustine’s anti-Manichaean works**, late fourth/early fifth century: hostile but indispensable Latin witnesses.
- **Titus of Bostra, Alexander of Lycopolis, Ephrem, Theodore bar Konai**, and later Christian polemicists.
- **Ibn al-Nadīm, *Fihrist***, Arabic, 987–988 CE — late but exceptionally important account drawing on earlier materials.
- The Medinet Madi finds transformed the field by supplying Manichaean community texts rather than only opponents’ reports. [Iranica, “Coptic Manichean Texts”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/coptic-manichean-texts/).

### Physical manuscript record

The Nag Hammadi collection comprises twelve substantial codices plus leaves conventionally numbered Codex XIII, found near Nag Hammadi in Egypt in 1945. The books were copied in Coptic in the fourth century; the compositions they contain are generally earlier and often translations from Greek. The collection’s exact ancient owners, reason for burial, and relationship to nearby Pachomian monasteries remain debated.

Berlin Papyrus 8502 is a papyrus codex containing the *Gospel of Mary*, *Apocryphon of John*, *Sophia of Jesus Christ*, and an act of Peter. The Berlin papyrus database dates it to the fifth century, while the museum catalogue gives the fourth century—an instructive example of continuing palaeographical disagreement. [Berlin Papyrus Database](https://berlpap.smb.museum/17878/) and [Staatliche Museen object record](https://search.smb.museum/object/obj-1388769).

## 3. Domain, Attributes, and Iconography

### Valentinian domain

Anthropos does not govern a terrestrial department such as storms, war, fertility, or a city. His “domain” is structural:

- He embodies transcendent, archetypal Humanity within the Pleroma.
- With Ecclesia, he mediates between the upper Ogdoad and the later twelve aeons.
- He provides the intelligible pattern behind earthly humanity.
- In some exegesis he helps explain the title “Son of Man.”
- In Marcosian exegesis he corresponds to the “Power of the Highest.”
- His pairing with Ecclesia binds ideal Humanity to the ideal Church or assembly.

There is no securely documented sacred animal, plant, metal, color, weekday, planet, weapon, temple emblem, or festival belonging to the Valentinian Anthropos.

### Form

No ancient source gives the Valentinian Anthropos a stable bodily description. “Man” is an intelligible designation. Treating him as a gigantic bearded humanoid is a modern visualization unless attached to a particular modern artist.

The Sethian Adamas is likewise primarily a luminous heavenly archetype. The rulers see his image or likeness reflected below, but the surviving texts do not provide a canonical costume or set of attributes.

The Hermetic Man is “most beautiful,” bears the Father’s image, and possesses creative authority. His manifestation in Nature’s watery or shadowy form triggers their mutual love. This is narrative imagery, not evidence for statues.

The Manichaean First Man is much more iconographically narratable: he arms himself with the five luminous elements—often treated as his “sons” or armor—and descends to battle Darkness. Manichaean illuminated books may have depicted cosmogony, and Mani’s lost *Book of Pictures* was central to tradition, but no surviving image can be securely labeled “the Valentinian Anthropos.”

### Iconographic absence

There is no securely identified ancient cult statue, temple relief, coin, painted icon, amulet, or mosaic labeled **Anthropos and Ecclesia**. The absence is significant. Evidence for the being is overwhelmingly textual and conceptual.

Diagrams in Ophite or related heresiological reports, the seals and figures of the Books of Jeu, Manichaean cosmological art, and later Kabbalistic “Adam Kadmon” diagrams belong to separate systems. They may illustrate the broader visual history of cosmic-human concepts, but they are not portraits of the same being.

## 4. Genealogy and Relations to Other Divine Beings

### Ptolemaean Valentinian genealogy

```text
Bythos (Depth) — Sige (Silence)
            |
Nous/Monogenes (Mind/Only-Begotten) — Aletheia (Truth)
            |
        Logos (Word) — Zoe (Life)
                    |
          Anthropos (Man) — Ecclesia (Church)
                    |
             the twelve-aeon Dodecad
```

The thirty aeons collectively produce or contribute to the Savior in several accounts. Sophia’s passion, exclusion, and restoration initiate the lower cosmological drama, but Anthropos is rarely an individual protagonist in that drama.

### Competing genealogy

Colorbasus reverses the middle order:

```text
... → Anthropos — Ecclesia
              |
          Logos — Zoe
```

Other reported Valentinians derive Jesus variously from Anthropos and Ecclesia, Christ, or Theletos. These are genuine rival constructions, not scribal noise to be harmonized.

### Sethian positioning

In the *Apocryphon of John*, the great Invisible Spirit and Barbelo produce Autogenes/Christ. The four Luminaries—Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithai, and Eleleth—organize the divine realm. Adamas is placed in Harmozel’s first aeon; Seth and the incorruptible race occupy subsequent levels. The lower rulers later imitate the heavenly Human’s image.

### Every documented divine interaction

The following list includes specific interactions in the principal Anthropos/heavenly-Human sources. Co-membership in a list without action is labeled accordingly.

- **Logos and Zoe — generation:** They emit Anthropos and Ecclesia in the Ptolemaean system. Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.1, c. 180. **[Early attested; hostile report]**
- **Ecclesia — syzygy and co-generator:** Anthropos is paired with Ecclesia; together they emit the Dodecad. Irenaeus 1.1. **[Early attested]**
- **Bythos, Sige, Nous, Aletheia — hierarchical co-presence:** Anthropos belongs with them to the Ogdoad but has no separately narrated meeting with them. Irenaeus 1.1. **[Early attested; absence of episode]**
- **The twelve aeons — generation:** Anthropos and Ecclesia jointly produce the Dodecad. Irenaeus 1.1. **[Early attested]**
- **Jesus/Savior — disputed generation:** Some Valentinians derive Jesus from Anthropos and Ecclesia; other reported schools do not. Irenaeus 1.11. **[Early attested school variant]**
- **Gabriel, Holy Spirit, and Mary — typological coordination:** Marcosian exegesis maps Gabriel to Logos, Spirit to Zoe, “Power of the Highest” to Anthropos, and Mary to Ecclesia in Jesus’ conception. Irenaeus 1.14. **[Early attested learned systematization]**
- **The Demiurge — indirect archetypal relation:** The lower creator makes earthly humanity according to an image traceable to the pre-existent Human. Tertullian, *Against the Valentinians* 19, early third century. **[Later ancient polemical report]**
- **The great Invisible Spirit — authorization/naming:** In the *Apocryphon of John*, the perfect heavenly Human Adamas appears or is named with the approval of the Invisible Spirit. **[Sethian systemic variant]**
- **Barbelo/Pronoia — revelatory mediation:** The upper divine order associated with Barbelo enables revelation of the heavenly Human’s form to the lower rulers. *Apocryphon of John*. **[Sethian variant]**
- **Autogenes/Christ — placement beside:** Adamas is established beside the divine Autogenes in the first aeon of Harmozel. *Apocryphon of John*. **[Sethian variant]**
- **Harmozel — aeonic residence:** The heavenly Adamas is assigned to Harmozel’s luminary realm. *Apocryphon of John*; *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **[Sethian variant]**
- **Seth — archetypal succession:** Seth is placed in the second luminary’s realm after Adamas and becomes progenitor of the incorruptible race. *Apocryphon of John*. **[Sethian variant]**
- **Yaldabaoth — imitation and opposition:** Yaldabaoth hears that “the Human exists,” sees the upper image, and participates in creating the lower Adam as a copy; he does not generate the heavenly Adamas. *Apocryphon of John*. **[Sethian variant]**
- **The archons/authorities — collective imitation:** They see the revealed Human’s pattern in the waters and say, “Let us create a human,” distributing psychic and bodily powers into Adam. *Apocryphon of John*. **[Sethian variant]**
- **Sophia — indirect causal relation:** Sophia’s deficient production of Yaldabaoth initiates the lower creation in which the image of the heavenly Human is copied. There is no clearly narrated personal meeting between Sophia and Adamas. *Apocryphon of John*. **[Sethian variant; absence of direct encounter]**
- **Nature/Physis — mutual love and union:** The Hermetic Man displays his form to Nature; she reflects it, they love one another, and their union produces earthly humanity’s double nature. CH I.12–15. **[Hermetic systemic variant]**
- **Nous/Father — generation and likeness:** Nous generates the Hermetic Man in his own image and loves him as his child. CH I.12. **[Hermetic variant]**
- **The seven Governors — passage and participation:** The Man breaks through or traverses their spheres and shares in their nature and rule. CH I.13–14. **[Hermetic variant]**
- **Demiurgic Mind/Logos — cosmic coordination:** The Hermetic account associates the rational cosmic order and seven Governors with the creative activity through which Man encounters the cosmos; recensions and translations differ in terminology. CH I. **[Hermetic variant]**
- **Attis — interpretive identification:** The Naassene report interprets Phrygian Attis through the primal-Man doctrine and quotes an Attis hymn. Pseudo-Hippolytus, *Refutation* 5. **[Early-third-century syncretistic exegesis]**
- **Hermes — interpretive witness:** Naassene exegesis invokes Hermes among pagan testimonies to its Man doctrine. *Refutation* 5. **[Syncretistic learned systematization]**
- **Osiris — interpretive equation or parallel:** Egyptian mysteries are interpreted anthropologically in the Naassene dossier. *Refutation* 5. **[Syncretistic learned systematization]**
- **Mariamne — transmitter:** The Naassenes allegedly claimed teaching from James through Mariamne; this concerns transmission rather than a divine interaction. *Refutation* 5. **[Hostile report]**
- **Manda d-Hiia — revelatory/redemptive relation:** In Mandaean cosmogony, the Gnosis of Life participates in the mission from the World of Light to the lower world and in Adam’s instruction. *Ginza Rba*, multiple tractates. **[Mandaean systemic variant]**
- **Hibil Ziwa — rescue and instruction:** The radiant messenger descends, overcomes powers of darkness, and aids souls and primordial humanity. *Ginza Rba*. **[Mandaean variant]**
- **Ruha — temptation and opposition:** Ruha and her planetary children attempt to entrap or seduce Adam in the material cosmos. *Ginza Rba*, especially creation materials. **[Mandaean variant]**
- **Šišlam Rabba — identity/coordination:** Adam Kasia is identified or closely coordinated with the primordial priest Šišlam in priestly cosmology. **[Mandaean learned systematization]**
- **Father of Greatness — generation and commission:** In Manichaean myth the Father calls forth the Mother of Life, who calls forth the First Man, or the emanational sequence is compressed so that the First Man is the Father’s champion. *Kephalaia* and eastern fragments. **[Manichaean systemic variant]**
- **Mother of Life — maternal generation:** She produces or calls forth the First Man for battle. Manichaean cosmogonic sources. **[Manichaean variant]**
- **Five Sons/elements — armor and companions:** The First Man clothes himself in Light, Air/Wind, Fire, Water, and the luminous element conventionally translated Ether; he offers them in battle, and they become mixed with Darkness. **[Manichaean variant]**
- **Kingdom/Prince of Darkness — combat and strategic defeat:** The First Man descends against Darkness and is temporarily overwhelmed; his apparent defeat entraps Darkness through its consumption of Light. **[Manichaean variant]**
- **Living Spirit — rescue:** The Living Spirit calls; First Man answers, establishing the “Call and Answer,” and is drawn from the abyss. **[Manichaean variant]**
- **Light Adamas — later combat:** This son of the Living Spirit attacks and subdues a major demonic power during subsequent cosmic construction. He is not the First Man, despite the name Adamas. [Iranica cosmogony](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-iii/). **[Manichaean variant; distinct being]**
- **Adam Kadmon — no ancient interaction:** No Valentinian, Sethian, Hermetic, Naassene, Mandaean, or Manichaean text records Anthropos meeting Adam Kadmon. Their equation is comparative and post-antique. **[Absence of evidence]**

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### The Valentinian emanation

**Earliest attestation:** Irenaeus 1.1, c. 180.

Depth exists with Silence. From them arise Mind and Truth; from Mind and Truth, Word and Life; from Word and Life, Man and Church. These four pairs form the primordial Ogdoad. Word and Life produce ten more aeons; Man and Church twelve. The resulting Thirty constitute the Pleroma.

Anthropos’ principal act is generative rather than heroic. The youngest aeon Sophia attempts to know or approach the unknowable Father without her partner, suffers passion, and is separated or purified. Christ, the Holy Spirit, Jesus/Savior, and Horos restore order in different versions. The lower world eventually emerges from the passions of Sophia or Achamoth. Anthropos participates structurally in the Fullness and sometimes in the collective production of the Savior, but he does not normally descend and fight the Demiurge.

**Variants:**

- Colorbasus reverses Anthropos/Ecclesia and Logos/Zoe.
- Some systems generate Jesus from Anthropos and Ecclesia.
- Marcosian teaching converts the aeonic scheme into letters, numbers, liturgical sounds, and biblical correspondences.
- Direct Valentinian texts often offer less tidy or differently ordered cosmologies than Irenaeus’ catalog.

### The heavenly model and earthly Adam

In Valentinian and Sethian traditions, Genesis’ “image and likeness” is reread vertically. Earthly Adam is not necessarily a direct copy of the unknowable highest God; he is formed according to a revealed heavenly Human.

In the *Apocryphon of John*, a voice announces the Human and the Son of Human. The rulers see an upper likeness in the waters and manufacture Adam from their powers. Their body initially cannot stand. The divine power stolen from Sophia enters him, and higher Pronoia ultimately awakens or instructs him. This myth makes the lower human a contested composite:

- body and psychic mechanisms from the rulers;
- a life-giving power ultimately derived from above;
- the form or pattern of the heavenly Human;
- potential liberation through knowledge and the descent of Pronoia.

This is not the same genealogy as Valentinian Anthropos and Ecclesia, even though both use an intelligible Human to interpret Genesis 1:26.

### The Hermetic Man’s descent and love of Nature

In CH I, supreme Mind generates a Man like itself and gives him authority. The Man desires to inspect or participate in the cosmic craftsmanship. Passing through the seven spheres, he receives their powers. Nature sees his magnificent divine form reflected in her water and shadow; he sees the corresponding form in her and loves it. They unite.

From this union arise terrestrial humans, mortal in body but immortal in essential origin, subjected to fate through their cosmic composition yet capable of ascent through knowledge. The episode explains humanity’s divided condition without using the Valentinian sequence Anthropos–Ecclesia–Dodecad.

Hans Jonas treated this as a central instance of a broad “Gnostic” myth of the heavenly Man’s descent and entrapment. Later scholarship has objected that this can subordinate the individual Hermetic tract’s positive valuation of cosmos and generation to a preselected anti-cosmic model. The debate is methodological, not merely terminological. [Critique of Jonas on *Poimandres*](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jclst/28/0/28_KJ00005744622/_article/-char/en).

### The Naassene primal Man

Pseudo-Hippolytus says the Naassenes begin from Man and Son of Man. The upper Man is spiritual and blessed; the lower is mortal. They read the same mystery through:

- Adam in Genesis;
- Jesus’ “Son of Man” language;
- the Jordan and Eden’s rivers;
- Greek poetry;
- Phrygian Attis;
- Samothracian and Eleusinian mysteries;
- anatomy and sexual generation.

The primal Man becomes a universal exegetical key. The report includes a hymn in which soul moves through cosmic suffering and calls for deliverance. Because the dossier is preserved only by an opponent, scholars dispute whether it represents one coherent Naassene book, several sources excerpted together, or Pseudo-Hippolytus’ editorial construction. A study of the report has even argued that an underlying document began as an exposition of Eleusinian mysteries. [M. J. Edwards, “The Naming of the Naassenes”](https://www.uni-koeln.de/phil-fak/ifa/zpe/downloads/1996/112pdf/112074.pdf).

### Mandaean Hidden Adam

Mandaean sources distinguish the secret, inner, heavenly Adam from bodily Adam. The earthly Adam and Hawa inhabit a world shaped by lower powers, yet emissaries from the World of Light reveal their origin and the saving rites. Adam’s condition becomes the paradigm for every embodied soul.

The myth is embedded in ongoing ritual religion:

- baptism in flowing “living water”;
- priestly initiation;
- death rituals and the soul’s ascent;
- recitation of prayers attributed to or patterned upon primordial figures;
- Šišlam as heavenly priest.

It is unsafe to infer a straight line from second-century “Gnostics” to the present Mandaean community. Mandaean literature has its own Aramaic language, priestly transmission, Mesopotamian setting, and extensive redactional history.

### Manichaean First Man

The Manichaean cycle is the most fully narrative primal-Man myth.

1. The Kingdom of Darkness threatens the Realm of Light.
2. The Father of Greatness calls forth the Mother of Life.
3. She calls forth the First Man.
4. He clothes himself with five luminous sons/elements and descends.
5. He confronts the powers of Darkness.
6. He is overcome, and the demons consume or seize his luminous armor.
7. This “defeat” also binds the chaotic powers to Light and prepares their containment.
8. The Mother of Life and Father call forth the Living Spirit.
9. The Living Spirit issues the Call; the First Man answers.
10. The rescue of First Man establishes the divine Call-and-Answer, while his Five Sons remain mixed in matter.
11. The cosmos becomes a mechanism for extracting those light particles.
12. Later creation of Adam and Eve by demonic powers concentrates imprisoned Light in humanity.
13. Revelation, ascetic discipline, and cosmic processes liberate the Light.

The First Man’s sacrifice has sometimes been compared with Christ. Manichaean sources themselves construct typological relations between successive apostles and saving events, but “crucified cosmic Christ = First Man” varies by text and scholarly reconstruction.

### Adam Kadmon

In Lurianic Kabbalah, after *ṣimṣum*—the contraction or concealment of the Infinite—a ray of divine light enters the primordial space and assumes the configuration called Adam Kadmon. From its eyes, ears, nose, and mouth emerge lights associated with later worlds and configurations. Subsequent vessel-breaking and restoration belong to this specifically Kabbalistic system.

This is a **medieval and early-modern development**, not a lost chapter of Valentinian mythology. Similarity of “primordial Man” language permits comparison; it does not establish identity.

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

### No independent cult of Anthropos

No inscription records a priest “of Anthropos,” no excavated sanctuary can be assigned to him, and no ancient civic calendar lists his festival. No altar, votive, sacrifice tariff, sacred treasury, pilgrimage center, or theophoric personal name securely documents a public cult.

“Anthropos cult” is therefore misleading if it suggests a Greco-Roman temple institution. The evidence concerns Christian teaching, scriptural interpretation, initiation, prayer, and sacramental communities.

### Valentinian practice

Sources associate Valentinian Christians with:

- baptism and post-baptismal anointing;
- Eucharistic meals;
- anointing with oil;
- invocations of divine names;
- redemption rites for the dying or dead;
- bridal-chamber symbolism;
- catechesis in cosmic genealogy;
- exegesis of canonical Christian scripture;
- ecclesial offices and teachers.

The *Gospel of Philip* treats baptism, chrism, Eucharist, redemption, and bridal chamber as interlocking mysteries. Heresiologists report elaborate formulas, but polemical framing and school variation require caution.

Anthropos has no securely attested individual sacrifice or offering. Nor is there evidence of human sacrifice in his honor.

### Sethian practice

The Sethian texts attest or imply:

- repeated baptismal language;
- seals and renunciations;
- invocation of Barbelo, Autogenes, the Four Luminaries, Adamas, and Seth;
- visionary ascent;
- contemplative praise;
- ritual proclamation of secret names;
- perhaps group recitation of hymnic texts such as the *Three Steles of Seth*.

Turner reconstructed a history moving from baptismal Sethianism toward contemplative and Platonizing ascent practice. This is influential but remains a **modern scholarly reconstruction**, not an ancient institutional charter. His larger study emphasizes the differing Christian, non-Christian, and Platonizing phases of the texts. [Turner, *Sethian Gnosticism and the Platonic Tradition* overview](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.37972947).

### Hermetic practice

*Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* supplies stronger ritual evidence than CH I alone: teacher and disciple prepare, pray, embrace, experience ascent, sing vowel-like praise, and order the revelation inscribed for future transmission. Yet there is still no documented temple of the Hermetic Man.

### Mandaean practice

Mandaeism supplies the clearest continuous ritual setting among the compared traditions:

- frequent immersion baptism (*maṣbuta*) in living water;
- priestly initiation and lineage;
- *masiqta* rites for the dead;
- ritual meals;
- white garments and crowns;
- construction of temporary or permanent ritual enclosures near water;
- recitation from the *Qolasta* and other priestly scrolls.

These rites address the World of Light and its messengers; Adam Kasia functions as heavenly prototype rather than a god receiving animal or human sacrifice.

### Manichaean practice

Manichaean communities distinguished Elect and Hearers. Documented practices include:

- fasting and confession;
- daily prayers oriented by sun and moon;
- almsgiving and support of the Elect;
- ritual meals intended to liberate Light from food;
- annual commemoration of Mani’s death at the Bema;
- copying, translating, and illuminating scripture;
- missionary organization.

The First Man’s descent supplied the cosmic rationale for releasing Light, but no temple sacrifice to him is attested.

### Allegations of sexual libertinism and sacrifice

Heresiologists accuse certain “Gnostics” of sexual rites, semen or menstrual ingestion, abortion, infanticide, and other crimes. Such accusations, especially in Epiphanius’ account of the so-called Phibionites/Borborites, belong to a standard ancient repertoire of charges against secretive minorities. They are not securely corroborated for Valentinian devotees of Anthropos.

No reliable evidence connects Anthropos with:

- human sacrifice;
- temple prostitution;
- ritual murder;
- mandatory sexual libertinism;
- cult violence.

This is not sanitization: the allegations exist and must be recorded, but their evidentiary status is **late hostile accusation**, not demonstrated practice.

## 7. Historical Evolution

### First and second centuries: antecedents and formation

Jewish interpretation of Genesis 1:26–27, Ezekiel 1, Daniel 7’s “one like a son of man,” Pauline Adam-Christ typology, and Philo’s intelligible human supplied conceptual resources. Middle Platonism furnished distinctions among intelligible archetype, cosmic craftsman, soul, and sensible copy. Egyptian and Iranian comparisons have also been proposed.

No surviving document, however, identifies a single source from which every primal-Man doctrine descended.

Valentinus taught in the mid-second century, reportedly in Alexandria and Rome. His followers developed multiple schools. By c. 180 Irenaeus knew the Ptolemaean Anthropos-Ecclesia pair and rival arrangements.

### Late second to third centuries: diversification

- Sethian texts developed the Adamas/Seth/incorruptible-race complex.
- Hermetic authors narrated the descent of the divine Man into Nature.
- Naassene exegetes, as reported by Pseudo-Hippolytus, universalized “Man and Son of Man.”
- Valentinian schools generated competing genealogies and sacramental interpretations.
- Plotinus and his circle encountered versions of *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, and related revelations and attacked teachings they regarded as denigrating the cosmos.
- Mani, active from the 240s to 270s, placed the First Man at the beginning of a world-historical struggle between Light and Darkness.

This is a period of parallel development and cross-fertilization, not evidence for one organized Anthropos church.

### Fourth to sixth centuries: manuscript preservation and suppression

The Nag Hammadi codices and many Manichaean Coptic books were copied in fourth-century Egypt. Berlin 8502 was copied approximately in the fourth or fifth century. The Askew and Bruce codices are harder to date securely.

Imperial Christian law increasingly restricted groups classified as heretical. Manichaeans suffered particularly severe legal sanctions under Roman, Sasanian, and later Islamic regimes. Books could be confiscated or destroyed; clergy and adherents could be exiled, imprisoned, or executed. Valentinian institutions disappear from the documentary record rather than undergoing an attested mass conversion centered on Anthropos.

The burial of the Nag Hammadi books is often connected with ecclesiastical suppression or Athanasius’ 367 Festal Letter, but this remains a hypothesis. No note in the codices states why they were concealed.

### Seventh to fifteenth centuries

Mandaeans continued in southern Mesopotamia and southwestern Iran, periodically protected and periodically endangered under changing regimes. Their manuscripts were recopied by priestly families.

Manichaeism spread east through Central Asia into China, obtained Uyghur royal patronage in the eighth and ninth centuries, lost that political protection after the Uyghur collapse, and survived in altered Chinese religious environments for centuries.

The Valentinian Anthropos did not retain an identifiable continuous cult. Cosmic-Man speculation instead continued independently in Jewish mysticism, Christian theology, Islamic esotericism, and Hermetic traditions.

### Fifteenth to eighteenth centuries: Hermetic and Kabbalistic recovery

Marsilio Ficino translated the Greek Hermetic corpus into Latin in the fifteenth century, giving *Poimandres* a major European afterlife. Renaissance thinkers integrated Hermetic Man, Christian Logos theology, Kabbalah, and microcosm-macrocosm correspondences.

Christian Kabbalists compared Adam Kadmon with Christ, the Logos, and universal humanity. These were learned syncretisms, not recovered Valentinian ritual traditions.

### Eighteenth and nineteenth centuries: manuscript discoveries

- The Bruce Codex was acquired in Upper Egypt by James Bruce around 1769.
- The Askew Codex entered the British Museum in the eighteenth century.
- Berlin Codex 8502 was acquired in Cairo in 1896.
- Editions and translations made previously inaccessible Coptic “Gnostic” traditions available.
- Theosophists and occultists appropriated “Gnostic,” Hermetic, and Kabbalistic cosmic-Man language, often treating historically distinct figures as expressions of one perennial archetype.

### Twentieth century

- Turfan expeditions recovered Manichaean materials in multiple Iranian, Turkic, and Chinese languages.
- Medinet Madi yielded major Coptic Manichaean codices.
- Nag Hammadi’s discovery in 1945 transformed the field from dependence on hostile heresiologists to direct texts.
- Drower documented Mandaean ritual and manuscript culture.
- Jonas interpreted Gnosticism phenomenologically through alienation and the fallen divine self.
- Jung adopted the Anthropos as an archetype of psychic totality and associated it with Adam Kadmon, Christ, and the Self.
- Robinson’s Nag Hammadi project, Layton’s translations, and the Brill *Coptic Gnostic Library* established critical access.
- Williams, King, and others challenged the reification of “Gnosticism.”

### Twenty-first century

There are modern Gnostic churches, independent sacramental groups, internet communities, occult lodges, and solitary practitioners. Some invoke “the Anthropos” as divine humanity or the restored Self. No reliable census isolates worship of Anthropos, and there is no demonstrable institutional continuity from a second-century Valentinian congregation.

Mandaeans remain a living ethnoreligious community, but displacement from Iraq and Iran has disrupted language, priestly succession, and access to running-water ritual sites. This is continuous Mandaean religion, not a modern revival of Valentinianism.

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Renaissance Hermetism

Ficino’s Latin translation of the Hermetic corpus made the divine Man of *Poimandres* a major witness to humanity’s dignity and cosmic status. Renaissance readers often believed Hermes Trismegistus vastly predated Moses; Isaac Casaubon’s seventeenth-century philological dating of the Hermetic writings to the post-classical era undermined that chronology.

The “dignity of man” current is related to *Poimandres* but should not be reduced to a cult of Anthropos.

### Theosophy and ceremonial magic

Helena Blavatsky and later Theosophists combined:

- Gnostic aeons;
- Kabbalistic Adam Kadmon;
- Hermetic macrocosm and microcosm;
- Hindu *puruṣa*;
- evolutionary “root races”;
- a perennial “divine Man.”

This synthetic Anthropos is **modern occult systematization**. It is not the genealogy in Irenaeus.

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, founded in London in 1888, integrated Hermetic, Rosicrucian, Christian, Egyptian, and Kabbalistic materials. Its rituals more commonly use Adam Kadmon, the Tree of Life, Christ, and generalized macrocosmic Man than the Valentinian aeon Anthropos by name. Claims that the Golden Dawn preserved an unbroken Valentinian initiation are unsupported. The order itself is a nineteenth-century fraternity. [Historical description](https://hermeticgoldendawn.org/what-is-the-golden-dawn/).

### Jung

C. G. Jung interpreted the **Anthropos** as an archetype of wholeness and a symbol of the Self, drawing together Christ, Adam Kadmon, the Gnostic primordial Man, and alchemical *homo maximus*. Jungian interpretation materially shaped modern popular understanding.

This is a twentieth-century psychological hermeneutic. The second-century evidence does not say Anthropos is a structure of the collective unconscious.

### Modern Gnostic churches and reconstruction

Modern ecclesial Gnostic movements frequently use Valentinian, Sethian, Hermetic, and Cathar language together. Some offer Eucharists, initiations, or meditations focused on awakening the divine Human within. Such practice may be religiously meaningful to participants but is historically a reconstruction using published texts.

There is no attested ancient liturgy titled “Invocation of the Aeon Anthropos.” Any such ritual currently circulating should be labeled modern unless a manuscript source is supplied.

### Literature, art, games, and internet mythology

“Anthropos” appears in modern fantasy, role-playing cosmologies, esoteric websites, comics, and syncretic charts. Frequent distortions include:

- making Anthropos the sole supreme Gnostic god;
- describing him as a literal giant;
- assigning him a planet, color, seal, or demon without ancient citation;
- identifying him unconditionally with Adam Kadmon, Gayōmard, Purusha, Christ, Atum, or the Manichaean First Man;
- presenting one standardized “Gnostic pantheon” compiled from mutually incompatible texts.

These are **modern inventions or creative syncretisms**, not ancient consensus.

### Political and extremist appropriation

No significant ancient political cult of Anthropos is documented. Modern racial or nationalist readings of “primordial man,” “Aryan man,” or “root races” derive largely from nineteenth- and twentieth-century occult racial theories, not from the Valentinian Anthropos. Assertions that Anthropos represents a biologically superior race are unsupported by the ancient sources.

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Was there a single “Anthropos myth”?

The classic history-of-religions approach reconstructed a widespread *Urmensch* or “Primal Man” myth behind Gnostic, Hermetic, Mandaean, Manichaean, Iranian, and Kabbalistic materials. Proposed origins included:

- Iranian Gayōmard;
- Jewish heavenly Adam speculation;
- Philo’s intelligible man;
- Platonic archetype-copy metaphysics;
- ancient Near Eastern cosmic anthropology;
- Egyptian divine-kingship or creator anthropology.

The weakest form of the theory assumes that similarity proves borrowing. The stronger form compares documented structures while allowing independent development and shared scriptural or philosophical environments.

An older Catholic Encyclopedia entry confidently derived Talmudic, Philonic, Gnostic, and Hermetic ideas from late Mazdean Gayōmard traditions. That sweeping single-origin account is characteristic of older comparativism and is not a present consensus. [Older formulation](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06592a.htm).

### Jewish origin and Philo

Modern work has strengthened the case that Valentinian protology cannot be understood without Jewish scriptural interpretation and Philo’s distinction between intelligible and earthly humanity. A Helsinki research project explicitly argues that Valentinians reworked earlier “Gnostic” myth through Philonic exegesis and ancient theories of elemental differentiation. [“Philo and the Valentinians”](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/philo-and-the-valentinians-protology-cosmogony-and-anthropology/).

This does not mean Philo invented Anthropos as a Valentinian aeon. It establishes a plausible intellectual environment and specific exegetical resources.

### “Gnosticism” as a disputed category

Hans Jonas’ *Gnosis und spätantiker Geist* and *The Gnostic Religion* presented Gnosticism as a religion of alienation, acosmism, and the divine self trapped in an alien world. His synthesis powerfully joined Valentinian, Sethian, Mandaean, Hermetic, and Manichaean materials. It also encouraged readers to treat them as manifestations of one essence. Jonas’ *The Gnostic Religion* appeared in English in 1958. [Jonas overview](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jonas-hans/).

Michael Williams’ *Rethinking “Gnosticism”* (1996) argued that “Gnosticism” is too unstable and value-laden to function as a single religious entity and proposed “biblical demiurgical traditions” for a narrower family. [Publisher description](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9781400822218/html).

Karen King’s *What Is Gnosticism?* (2003) traced how heresiological and modern scholarly boundary-making constructed the category and warned that origin-hunting can reproduce ancient orthodox polemic. [Book record](https://books.google.ca/books?id=df1Tz5Cn8BQC).

These critiques do not erase the texts or prohibit comparison. They require that “Anthropos” be attributed to particular sources and schools rather than to an imagined uniform Gnostic religion.

### Sethian reconstruction

John D. Turner reconstructed phases of Sethian history:

1. formative myth and baptismal practice;
2. Christian interaction and redaction;
3. separation from Christian communities;
4. intensive engagement with Platonism in *Allogenes*, *Zostrianos*, *Marsanes*, and the *Three Steles*.

The model explains significant textual differences, but its chronology remains reconstructive. Recent scholarship continues to debate which treatises are earliest, whether some were initially non-Christian, and how directly Plotinus’ opponents can be identified with the texts now preserved.

### Heresiologists versus direct sources

The Nag Hammadi discoveries both confirmed and destabilized Irenaeus:

- He genuinely knew systems resembling those in direct texts.
- His catalog preserves authentic technical names.
- Yet direct writings show more diversity and less rigid schematization than his ordered succession of “heresies.”
- Polemical reports often combine quotation, paraphrase, ridicule, and genealogical classification.

Consequently, heresiologists remain primary witnesses but not neutral ethnographers.

### Textual and material uncertainties

- The Greek originals of most Nag Hammadi works are lost.
- Coptic translations can obscure technical gender, syntax, and wordplay.
- Composition dates are often ranges inferred from theology, literary relationships, and external citation.
- Codex date and text date must be separated.
- “Sethian” and “Valentinian” are scholarly classifications whose borders are porous.
- The find circumstances of Nag Hammadi were retold in changing and sometimes romanticized forms.
- The owners and intended use of the codices remain uncertain.
- The Bruce Codex lost its original folio order, complicating reconstruction.
- Mandaean manuscripts are often recent copies of substantially older but difficult-to-date compositions.
- Manichaean texts survive fragmentarily across many languages.

### Discredited or unsupported claims still circulating

- **“Anthropos was worshipped in a great ancient temple.”** No evidence.
- **“All Gnostics believed in the same thirty aeons.”** False.
- **“Adamas, Anthropos, Adam Kadmon, Gayōmard, Purusha, and First Man are merely different spellings of one god.”** Unsupported.
- **“The Nag Hammadi codices were certainly buried because Athanasius ordered them destroyed.”** Possible but unproved.
- **“The Valentinian Anthropos received human sacrifice or ritual sexual offerings.”** No corroborating evidence.
- **“Modern Gnostic churches descend institutionally from Valentinus.”** No demonstrable continuous succession.
- **“Adam Kadmon proves that medieval Kabbalah copied Gnosticism directly.”** Similarity exists; the route and extent of transmission are disputed.
- **“Poimandres is the name of the heavenly Man.”** Poimandres is the revealer/Mind; the generated figure is Man.
- **“Light Adamas is another name for the Manichaean First Man.”** They are distinct actors in the Manichaean sequence.
- **“Anthroposophy derives its name from worship of the Valentinian Anthropos.”** Rudolf Steiner’s term means “wisdom/knowledge of the human”; historical influence from Western esotericism does not establish this identity.

### Overall finding

The securely attested **Anthropos proper** is the Valentinian aeon paired with Ecclesia and located in the divine Ogdoad by about 180 CE. The Sethian Adamas, Hermetic Man, Naassene primal Man, Mandaean Hidden Adam, Manichaean First Man, and Kabbalistic Adam Kadmon form a historically important comparative family, but no ancient source makes all of them one being. Their differences—passive aeon, revealed archetype, cosmic lover, universal exegetical principle, inner priestly Adam, sacrificial warrior, and sefirotic macrocosm—are not noise around a hidden canonical biography. They are the documented history of how late-antique and later communities repeatedly used the human form to think about divinity, cosmos, embodiment, revelation, and salvation.

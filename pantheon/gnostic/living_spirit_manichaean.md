# The Living Spirit (Manichaean): Research Dossier

**Class:** A male divine emanation, savior, warrior, and beneficent demiurge in Manichaean tradition; member of the “Second Evocation” or second divine creation. He is not a demon, fallen archon, or creator opposed to the supreme God.  
**Earliest secure attestation:** Mani’s third-century Middle Persian *Šābuhragān*, composed for Šābuhr I after his accession in 240/241—probably in or after Mani’s audience with him in 242—surviving only in later Turfan fragments and related Parthian paraphrases. These fragments name or presuppose **Mihryazd**, the Iranian form of the Living Spirit, as maker of the cosmic structure and of the sun and moon. The oldest surviving physical witnesses are much later than Mani: chiefly Coptic Manichaean codices copied around 400 CE and Central Asian fragments from approximately the eighth–eleventh centuries. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Šābuhragān”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/sabuhragan/); [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “The Manichean Pantheon”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-ii-the-manichean-pantheon/)

## Evidentiary labels

- **Secure early attestation:** recoverable from Mani’s own third-century works or early Manichaean tradition, although often preserved in later manuscripts or hostile quotation.
- **Later canonical elaboration:** preserved in Manichaean scripture, hymns, homilies, or teaching collections later than Mani but accepted within historical Manichaean communities.
- **Regional variant:** a translation, adaptation, or reassignment peculiar to Iranian, Coptic, Central Asian, Uyghur, or Chinese Manichaeism.
- **External testimony:** evidence supplied by Christian, Zoroastrian, Muslim, or other non-Manichaean witnesses; potentially valuable but polemically conditioned.
- **Syncretistic identification:** an explicit equation made during transmission into another religious language.
- **Modern scholarly reconstruction:** a conclusion inferred by comparison of fragmentary witnesses.
- **Modern invention or unverified revival claim:** unattested in premodern evidence.

## 1. Identification, names, and epithets

### Core identity

**Secure early attestation.** The Living Spirit belongs to the second stage of Manichaean cosmogony. After the First Man descends with his five light-elements and is overwhelmed by Darkness, the Father of Greatness initiates another emanational sequence. The Friend of the Lights evokes the Great Builder; the Great Builder evokes the Living Spirit. The Living Spirit in turn evokes five sons. He descends, defeats or immobilizes the demonic rulers, calls to the First Man, raises him by the right hand, and—with the Mother of Life and his sons—constructs the cosmos from the bodies and skins of the defeated archons. The material cosmos is therefore both a demonic corpse and a divinely engineered prison and purification apparatus.

Calling him “the Manichaean demiurge” is accurate only with this qualification: unlike the ignorant or hostile demiurges in several Sethian texts, he is a legitimate emanation of the Realm of Light. His fabrication of the cosmos is a rescue operation authorized by the Father of Greatness. This positive demiurge is one important reason Michel Tardieu and other scholars have resisted treating Manichaeism as merely another form of “Gnosticism.” [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Manicheism: General Survey”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-1-general-survey/)

### Documented names

- **Syriac:** ܪܘܚܐ ܚܝܐ, *rūḥā ḥayyā*, “Living Spirit.” **Secure early terminology**, preserved most clearly in Syriac-derived testimony, especially Theodore bar Kōnay’s eighth-century *Book of Scholia*. Syriac *rūḥā* is grammatically feminine, a fact relevant to disputes about the figure’s original gender imagery.
- **Greek:** τὸ ζῶν πνεῦμα, *to zōn pneuma*, “the Living Spirit,” and Latin **spiritus vivens** in Western reports. **External translation tradition.**
- **Coptic:** “Living Spirit,” also “Father of Life.” The figure is grammatically masculine in the surviving Coptic Manichaean texts. **Later canonical witness**, principally the Medinet Madi *Kephalaia* and Psalm-Book.
- **Middle Persian:** *Mihryazd*, conventionally segmented *Mihr-yazd*, “the god Mihr/Mithra.” **Secure early Iranian adaptation**, found in the tradition of the *Šābuhragān* and other Middle Persian material.
- **Parthian:** *wād žīwandag*, “Living Wind/Spirit.” **Regional variant.**
- **Sogdian:** forms such as *wāδ žīwandī* or *wād žīwandag*, likewise “Living Wind/Spirit.” **Regional variant.**
- **Chinese:** forms translated as “Pure Wind,” notably 淨風, *Jìngfēng*; a later southeastern Chinese formulation gives 日光淨風, “Sunlight Pure Wind.” **Regional translation and systematization.** In some Chinese material “Pure Wind” became confused with the Light-Nous or Holy Spirit of the third creation rather than preserving the Living Spirit’s earlier demiurgic position. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Cosmogony and Cosmology in Manicheism”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-iii/)

### Mihr and the Mithra problem

**Syncretistic identification, with disputed interpretation.** Middle Persian Manichaeans identified the Living Spirit as *Mihryazd*. This did not make every feature of the Iranian Mithra cult part of his identity. It was a translation of a Manichaean office into Iranian divine vocabulary.

Parthian and Sogdian Manichaeans instead normally applied Mihr/Miši to the **Third Messenger**, a solar and sexually revelatory deity of the third creation. Mary Boyce explained this through the increasing solar character of Mithra in Parthian religion. Ilya Gershevitch objected that Sogdian calls the sun “Mithra” specifically in Manichaean texts, making simple borrowing from an established Sogdian solar nomenclature doubtful. Werner Sundermann proposed historical divergence: Mani devised the Middle Persian terminology, while the apostle Mār Ammō carried an earlier or incomplete scheme into Parthian territory and independently assigned Mihr to the Third Messenger. Consequently, “Mihryazd” can mean different Manichaean gods depending on language and region. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Mithra in Manicheism”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mithra-in-manicheism-1/)

A recent focused study concludes that the Manichaean Mihryazd is not simply a pre-existing Zoroastrian deity imported unchanged, but a Manichaean construction influenced by Mithraic language and attributes. That is a **modern scholarly reconstruction**, not an ancient self-description. [Usuteaduslik Ajakiri, “The God Mihr(yazd) in Manichaeism”](https://ojs.utlib.ee/index.php/UA/article/view/23785?articlesBySameAuthorPage=2)

### Gender

**Attested ambiguity and scholarly dispute.** Coptic grammar treats the Living Spirit as masculine, and his titles “Father of Life” and “son” place him in masculine kinship language. Syriac *rūḥā*, however, is grammatically feminine. Some reconstructions argue that Mani exploited the feminine Syriac Spirit as a maternal or androgynous creative principle; others caution that grammatical gender does not establish mythological sex. The earliest recoverable system pairs and overlaps paternal, maternal, and androgynous imagery, but no surviving text licenses a simple statement that the Living Spirit “was originally a goddess.” The explicitly male presentation is dominant in the Coptic, Iranian, and Western evidence.

## 2. Earliest attestation and textual corpus

### Mani’s writings

**The *Šābuhragān*.** Mani composed this Middle Persian exposition for Šābuhr I in the mid-third century. Turfan fragments and Parthian paraphrases describe the two realms, the cosmic conflict, and the Living Spirit’s construction of the sun and moon. The work supplies the earliest datable literary stratum, although its physical manuscripts are medieval Central Asian fragments rather than Mani’s autograph. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Šābuhragān”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/sabuhragan/)

**The *Book of Mysteries*, *Living Gospel*, *Treasure of Life*, *Pragmateia*, *Epistles*, *Psalms and Prayers*, and *Image/Book of Pictures*.** These formed Mani’s Syriac canon, but most are lost or fragmentary. Quotations and summaries preserve elements of cosmology, but not every later detail can be assigned confidently to a particular authored book. Augustine quotes the *Fundamental Epistle*, while al-Bīrūnī quotes the *Treasure of Life*. The *Book of Pictures* gave visual form to doctrine but is not directly preserved.

### Theodore bar Kōnay

**External testimony, late eighth century, preserving early Syriac material.** Theodore’s *Ketbā d-Eskōlyōn* (*Book of Scholia*) gives the most continuous Syriac account of Mani’s cosmogony. It names the emanational sequence, the Living Spirit and his five sons, the Call and Answer, the archons, and the construction of ten heavens and eight earths. Because Theodore wrote as a Christian opponent roughly five centuries after Mani, his framing is hostile; nevertheless, his terminology and systematic correspondences with independent Coptic and Iranian evidence make the account indispensable. An English translation is available in John C. Reeves’s presentation. [John C. Reeves, “Theodore bar Konai on Mani and Manichaeism”](https://pages.charlotte.edu/john-reeves/publications/unpublished-lectures-and-fragmenta/theodore-bar-konai-on-mani-and-manichaeism/)

### Coptic Manichaean codices

**Later canonical elaboration.** The Medinet Madi library, discovered in the Fayyum in 1929, includes the *Kephalaia of the Teacher*, *Kephalaia of the Wisdom of My Lord Mani*, Manichaean Homilies, Psalm-Book, and Synaxeis. The codices circulated in Egypt around 400 CE, probably as Coptic translations of earlier Greek or Aramaic materials. The *Kephalaia* are not books authored directly by Mani; they collect teachings placed in his mouth and community interpretations of them. They repeatedly situate the Living Spirit within the second divine creation and explain the functions of his sons. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Kephalaia”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kephalaia/)

### Cologne Mani Codex

**Early biographical witness, manuscript ca. 400 CE.** This minute Greek parchment codex, *On the Origin of His Body*, preserves accounts of Mani’s youth, revelations, and departure from his baptist community. It is crucial for Mani’s setting and self-understanding, but it does not provide the fullest extant narrative of the Living Spirit. Its relevance is contextual rather than a reason to attribute every later cosmological detail directly to Mani.

### Turfan and Central Asian material

**Regional canonical witnesses, approximately eighth–eleventh centuries.** Thousands of fragments in Middle Persian, Parthian, Sogdian, Bactrian, and Old Uyghur preserve hymns, catechesis, cosmology, and church literature. These identify the Living Spirit as Mihryazd in Middle Persian but as “Living Wind/Spirit” in Parthian and Sogdian; they document the Call and Answer and the duties of his sons. The heterogeneous Turfan corpus numbers about 4,700 fragments and cannot be treated as a single book or date. [Bibliographia Iranica, “Personal Names in Iranian Manichaica”](https://www.biblioiranica.info/personal-names-in-iranian-sources-of-manichaeism/)

### Augustine and other Western witnesses

**External testimony, fourth–fifth centuries.** Augustine had been a Manichaean Hearer before becoming a Nicene Christian. His *Contra Faustum*, *Contra Epistulam Fundamenti*, *De natura boni*, *De duabus animabus*, and *Contra Felicem* preserve genuine Manichaean propositions embedded in sustained polemic. He confirms the First Man, the surrender of divine members to Darkness, and the mixed world, but his purpose is refutation rather than neutral documentation. [Augustine, *Contra Faustum* V](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/140605.htm); [Latin *Epistola Fundamenti* fragments](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Epistola_Fundamenti)

Alexander of Lycopolis, Titus of Bostra, Ephrem, Epiphanius, the *Acta Archelai*, Severus of Antioch, and Theodoret supply additional hostile testimony. Concordance with internal Manichaean sources must be assessed claim by claim.

### Arabic and Persian witnesses

Ibn al-Nadīm’s tenth-century *Fihrist*, al-Bīrūnī’s works, al-Yaʿqūbī, al-Shahrastānī, and other Muslim authors preserve book lists, doctrine, chronology, and quotations. They are **medieval external witnesses**, sometimes using Manichaean informants or writings now lost. They should not automatically be promoted to third-century evidence.

### What the named “Gnostic corpus” does—and does not—attest

The *Apocryphon of John*, *Hypostasis of the Archons*, *On the Origin of the World*, *Gospel of Truth*, *Tripartite Tractate*, *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit*, *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*, *Eugnostos*, *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth*, *Pistis Sophia*, *Books of Jeu*, *Gospel of Judas*, and *Poimandres* do **not** name the Manichaean Living Spirit as such. Nor do Irenaeus’s Valentinian reports, Hippolytus, Tertullian’s *Against the Valentinians*, or Clement’s *Excerpta ex Theodoto*.

These works document neighboring demiurgical, emanational, archontic, salvific, and revelatory vocabularies. Similarities—an upper divine world, emanations, hostile archons, captured light, salvific descent—are appropriate comparative evidence. Direct identity, literary dependence, or membership in one “Gnostic pantheon” is not established. The Nag Hammadi codices may even postdate a century of Manichaean activity in Egypt, so influence cannot always be presumed to run toward Mani. [Aarhus University, “Manichaeism and the Nag Hammadi Library”](https://cas.au.dk/en/up-to-date/arrangementer/show/artikel/manichaeism-and-the-nag-hammadi-library)

### Mandaean corpus

The *Ginza Rba*, *Qolasta*, *Mandaean Book of John*, and *Baptism of Hibil Ziwa* likewise do not straightforwardly attest the Manichaean Living Spirit. Hibil Ziwa and Manda d-Hayyi are rescuing *uthras* descending from the World of Light, and comparison is historically appropriate. Direct equation with the Living Spirit remains **modern comparative reconstruction**, not Mandaean or Manichaean scripture. Mandaean Hibil is also identified in the *Ginza* with Iranian Muhtur/Mihr, but this is an internal Mandaean syncretism, not proof that Hibil and Mihryazd were one cult figure. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “The Mandaean Religion”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mandaeans/mandaeans-ii-the-mandaean-religion/); [“Mandaeans and Iranian Religion”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mandaeans/mandaeans-iii-interaction-with-iranian-religion/)

## 3. Domain, attributes, and iconography

### Domains and powers

**Secure early attestation:**

- Rescue of the First Man.
- Victory over and confinement of archons.
- Beneficent demiurgy: constructing the mixed cosmos to prevent another invasion of Light.
- Organization of ten heavens and eight earths.
- Installation of cosmic guardians.
- Extraction or management of swallowed Light.
- Creation or arrangement of the sun and moon in some Iranian accounts.
- Initiation of the Call that elicits the First Man’s Answer.
- Enforcement of the cosmic boundary through his sons.

He is powerful but not supreme. He acts because the Father of Greatness initiates the second creation, through an emanational chain involving the Friend of the Lights and Great Builder. He cannot simply annihilate Matter or recover all Light immediately. The cosmos must separate the mixture over time.

### Characteristic form

No securely identified late-antique portrait survives with an inscription naming the Living Spirit. Texts present him anthropomorphically: he descends, displays himself, speaks, extends his right hand, fights, flays archons, and constructs the world. These are narrative forms rather than a fixed statue type.

The **right hand** is his most distinctive gesture. A late report explains that Manichaeans greeted one another by clasping right hands because the Living Spirit rescued the First Man in this fashion. This gives a plausible ritual afterlife to the myth, although the report’s historical precision remains debated. [Gardner and Lieu, *Manichaean Texts from the Roman Empire*](https://gnosis.study/library/%D0%94%D1%80%D1%83%D0%B3%D0%B8%D0%B5%20%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F/%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%85%D0%B5%D0%B9%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%BE/%D0%9A%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%B8/ENG/Gardner%20I.%2C%20Lieu%20S.N.C.%20-%20Manichaean%20Texts%20from%20the%20Roman%20Empire.pdf)

### Symbols and associated structures

The ten heavens, eight earths, sun and moon, five sons, captured archons, and salvific handclasp are his securely associated motifs. Five is structurally important because Manichaean cosmology repeatedly organizes divine powers and elements pentadically, but there is no evidence for a personal “sacred number cult” devoted to him.

No securely attested personal sacred animal, plant, weekday, festival, color, or astronomical body belongs exclusively to the Living Spirit. Modern lists assigning him such correspondences are **modern invention unless independently sourced**.

### Surviving art

The thirteenth–fourteenth-century southern Chinese **Manichaean Diagram of the Universe**, reconstructed from three silk sections, depicts ten heavens, the terrestrial layers, judgment, and the Realm of Light. It is direct evidence for the late Chinese visualization of the cosmos he was believed to have constructed, but no individual figure within it has been securely demonstrated to be a portrait of the Living Spirit. [“Manichaean Diagram of the Universe”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaean_Diagram_of_the_Universe); [Gulácsi, “Searching for Mani’s Picture Book”](https://heiup.uni-heidelberg.de/journals/index.php/transcultural/article/download/6173/2966?inline=1)

Turfan manuscript paintings demonstrate a developed tradition of “visual scripture.” The best-understood scenes show the liberation of Light through the ritual meal and its conveyance through the sun and moon. These illustrate the cosmic machine’s continuing work, but should not be mislabeled as images of its demiurge. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Manichean Art”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manichean-art/)

## 4. Genealogy and relations

### Emanational genealogy

The most widely reconstructed sequence is:

Father of Greatness → Mother of Life → First Man  
Father of Greatness → Friend of the Lights → Great Builder → Living Spirit → five sons

This is an emanational and functional genealogy, not sexual reproduction. Some texts compress or rearrange the process. “Second emanation” is therefore shorthand for the second great divine mobilization, not necessarily “the second being ever generated.”

### Five sons

1. **Keeper of Splendor** (*Splenditenens*): suspends or holds the ten heavens.
2. **King of Honor:** associated with the upper cosmic structure and the gathering or governance of Light.
3. **Adamas of Light:** armed guardian who restrains the powers of Darkness.
4. **King of Glory:** turns or governs the heavenly wheels.
5. **Atlas/Supporter:** bears the eight earths.

The exact titles and division of labor vary by language and source. The **Call** is sometimes counted as a sixth son, while the **Answer** belongs structurally with the rescued First Man. The five sons should not be confused with the First Man’s five light-elements.

### Every documented interaction with another named divine being

- **Father of Greatness — emanational commission:** the Father hears the First Man and causes the second divine rescue to come forth, ultimately producing or commissioning the Living Spirit. Theodore bar Kōnay, preserving earlier Syriac tradition; *Kephalaia*, ca. 400 manuscript. **Secure early core through later witnesses.**
- **Friend of the Lights — genealogical predecessor:** the Friend evokes the Great Builder, beginning the chain that produces the Living Spirit. Theodore bar Kōnay; Coptic systematizations. **Later canonical/systematic formulation.**
- **Great Builder — immediate emanational predecessor:** the Great Builder calls forth the Living Spirit. Same witnesses. **Later canonical/systematic formulation.**
- **Mother of Life — joint descent and cosmic labor:** she accompanies or assists the Living Spirit in rescuing the First Man and constructing the universe from defeated demons. Theodore bar Kōnay; *Kephalaia*; Iranian fragments. **Secure early core.**
- **First Man — rescue:** the Living Spirit calls; the First Man answers; the Living Spirit extends his right hand and raises him from Darkness. Theodore bar Kōnay and multiple Manichaean traditions. **Secure early core.**
- **First Man — joint petition after world-making:** Middle Persian tradition has the Living Spirit, First Man, and Mother of Life ascend and petition the Father for the Third Messenger. **Regional Iranian witness.**
- **Call (*Xrōštag*) — personified utterance:** the Living Spirit “clothes himself” in or emanates the Call directed downward. Iranian cosmogonic texts. **Later canonical personification rooted in an early mythic act.**
- **Answer (*Padwāxtag*) — responsive partner:** the Answer rises from the First Man toward the Living Spirit and Mother of Life. Iranian texts. **Later canonical personification.**
- **Keeper of Splendor — son and subordinate:** generated for the second creation and assigned to sustain the heavens. Theodore bar Kōnay and Coptic/Iranian sources. **Canonical.**
- **King of Honor — son and subordinate:** generated for cosmic governance and recovery of Light. Same source complex. **Canonical, with regional variation in function.**
- **Adamas of Light — son and warrior:** generated to restrain Darkness, characteristically armed with sword and shield. Same source complex. **Canonical.**
- **King of Glory — son and cosmic governor:** generated to manage celestial rotation or heavenly structures. Same source complex. **Canonical.**
- **Atlas/Supporter — son and bearer:** placed beneath the eight earths as their support. Same source complex. **Canonical.**
- **Archons/demonic rulers — combat and imprisonment:** the Living Spirit defeats, kills, flays, suspends, or confines them; their bodies and skins become the heavens and earths. Theodore bar Kōnay, Iranian texts, Coptic teaching literature. **Secure early core with variable detail.**
- **King of Darkness — indirect adversary:** the Living Spirit undoes the consequence of the King’s attack by rescuing the First Man and imprisoning the King’s forces. No surviving source clearly describes a personal duel between the two. **Attested opposition; direct combat unestablished.**
- **Third Messenger — preparation and petition:** after the cosmos is built, the Living Spirit joins the other gods in requesting the next salvific mechanism. Middle Persian text. **Regional canonical witness.**
- **Third Messenger — duplicated seduction motif:** Theodore’s account gives the Living Spirit a display before the archons that causes them to release swallowed Light; other witnesses assign the great male/female sexual display to the Third Messenger. **Conflicting versions; probable motif transfer.**
- **Light-Nous/Holy Spirit — later Chinese conflation:** Chinese “Pure Wind” terminology sometimes shifts the Living Spirit into the role of the third-creation Light-Nous. **Regional syncretistic confusion, not original identity.**
- **Mithra/Mihr — Iranian equation:** Middle Persian tradition names the Living Spirit Mihryazd; Parthian and Sogdian traditions generally give the Mihr-name to the Third Messenger. **Historically datable regional identifications.**
- **Hibil Ziwa — no attested meeting:** their resemblance as descending rescuers is comparative; neither Manichaean nor Mandaean scripture records an encounter. **Absence of evidence.**
- **Manda d-Hayyi — no attested meeting:** analogous messenger language does not constitute an interaction. **Absence of evidence.**
- **Yaldabaoth, Sabaoth, Sophia, Barbelo, Poimandres — no attested interaction:** these belong to distinct Sethian, Valentinian, or Hermetic narratives. **Absence of evidence.**

## 5. Major myths and narrative cycles

### The rescue of the First Man

**Secure early core.** The First Man descends with his five sons or elemental armaments to meet the assault of Darkness. Overwhelmed, he relinquishes the five light-elements, which are swallowed by the demons. From the abyss he prays. The second divine creation unfolds, culminating in the Living Spirit.

The Living Spirit reaches the frontier and issues the **Call**. The First Man produces the **Answer**. These spoken acts become enduring divine hypostases: the descending summons and the upward assent. The Living Spirit extends his right hand; the First Man grasps it and is raised to the Mother of Life and the Realm of Light.

The five elements remain mixed with Darkness. The rescue is therefore personal but not yet cosmic. It establishes the archetype of Manichaean salvation: revelation descends as a call; the trapped soul recognizes it and answers; divine power raises the respondent.

### The defeat of the archons and formation of the cosmos

**Secure early core with substantial variants.** The Living Spirit and his forces attack the archons. In Theodore’s account, he orders three sons to perform the principal architectural work. Demon skins are stretched into ten heavens; bodies are cast down to form eight earths. Other archons are suspended in the heavens as prisoners or placed at cosmic stations.

The Keeper of Splendor supports the heavens; Atlas bears the earths; Adamas restrains Darkness; the other sons operate the celestial machinery. The world is consequently the “Great Corpse”—made from dead or immobilized demonic substance—but also a machine designed by a good god.

This produces a deliberate paradox. Matter is not the Living Spirit’s creation ex nihilo; its substance belongs to Darkness. The ordered cosmos, however, is his good and strategically necessary fabrication. Calling him “creator of matter” is therefore misleading; calling him “architect of the present mixed cosmos” is accurate.

### The Call and Answer

**Early core; later hypostatic elaboration.** In the simplest narrative the Call and Answer are utterances exchanged during rescue. Iranian texts make them divine persons, *Xrōštag* and *Padwāxtag*. The Call is commonly associated with the Living Spirit and may be counted as his sixth son; the Answer rises from the First Man and is sometimes described as the Mother of Life’s beloved son.

The pair embodies the structure of salvation throughout Manichaean preaching. The historical missionary proclamation repeats the heavenly Call, while recognition and confession repeat the Answer. Their personification is well attested, but an elaborate independent biography for either is not.

### Extraction of Light and the seduction problem

**Conflicting attested versions.** Theodore bar Kōnay says that the Living Spirit revealed his forms to the captured archons, causing them to discharge swallowed Light. Better-known Iranian accounts assign a dramatic male-and-female display to the **Third Messenger**, whose beauty causes male demons to ejaculate and female demons to abort. The released mixture contributes to the later formation of plants, animals, Adam, and Eve.

Scholars generally treat the duplication as motif transfer between the second and third creations, whose agents perform analogous salvific work. It must not be harmonized into a single episode without qualification. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Cosmogony and Cosmology in Manicheism”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-iii/)

### Petition for the Third Creation

**Iranian canonical variant.** Having built the cosmos, the Living Spirit, the Mother of Life, and First Man ascend and ask the Father of Greatness for a further power capable of extracting the remaining Light. The Third Messenger and associated deities are then evoked. The Living Spirit’s role changes from active rescuer and builder to established cosmic governor whose structure the later gods operate.

### Eschatological role

**Later canonical elaboration.** The Living Spirit’s sons guard the cosmic system; rebellious demons may be cast down. At the end, however, the cosmos itself will cease after its light has been extracted. The Living Spirit is not consistently presented as the final judge or the deity who personally ignites the terminal conflagration. Those functions belong to other figures in different texts. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Manichean Eschatology”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/eschatology-ii/)

## 6. Cult, temples, and ritual practice

There is no evidence for an independent cult of the Living Spirit with its own temples, hereditary priesthood, sacrificial calendar, oracle, or pilgrimage center. He was worshipped within the Manichaean pantheon and invoked in hymnic and doctrinal settings, not served by a separately documented institution.

### Communal practice

The Manichaean church consisted principally of **Elect** and **Hearers**. The Elect followed severe dietary and sexual discipline; Hearers supported them, supplied food, confessed, prayed, and hoped for eventual advancement. The daily meal performed the liberation of Light from plants and fruit. Sun and moon then conveyed purified Light upward through the cosmic mechanism whose construction began with the Living Spirit.

The right-hand greeting is explicitly connected in external testimony with his rescue of the First Man. If historically accurate, this is the clearest ritual enactment specific to his myth.

### Prayer and hymns

Coptic and Iranian Manichaean hymns praise the gods of the Light collectively and recount the second creation. Surviving texts do not support reconstruction of a standardized sacrificial liturgy offered uniquely to the Living Spirit.

### Sacrifice and contested allegations

The requested contested-material search produced **no credible evidence of human sacrifice to the Living Spirit**, no temple prostitution, and no cult violence performed in his name. Manichaean ethics normally rejected killing animals and discouraged injury to living things because particles of Light were dispersed through living nature.

Christian opponents accused Manichaeans of obscene Eucharistic rites, sexual immorality, and ritual use of semen or menstrual material. These accusations belong to the broader ancient heresiological repertoire and are not corroborated by internal Manichaean texts or archaeology. They cannot be treated as documented ritual.

## 7. Historical evolution

### Third century: formation

Mani preached publicly from the reign of Šābuhr I and supplied his universal religion with scriptures and pictures. The Living Spirit was probably present from the system’s earliest recoverable formulation: the *Šābuhragān* tradition already knows Mihryazd’s cosmological labor. His mythology combined Syriac-Christian language of the Spirit, biblical and Enochic archons, Iranian divine nomenclature, and a distinctive Manichaean theory of salvific world-construction.

### Fourth–fifth centuries: western expansion and Coptic systematization

Manichaeism spread through Mesopotamia, Syria, Egypt, Roman North Africa, and the Mediterranean. The Coptic codices copied around 400 preserve the figure as a standard component of the pantheon. Augustine and other opponents confirm that the First Man’s defeat and rescue remained central teachings.

Roman suppression increasingly criminalized Manichaeans. Diocletian ordered severe measures around 302; subsequent Christian emperors confiscated property, exiled adherents, and sometimes imposed death. These measures targeted Manichaean communities as a whole, not devotion to the Living Spirit separately.

### Sasanian and early Islamic Iran

Mani died under Sasanian royal authority, traditionally dated 276/277. Later Zoroastrian persecution affected his church intermittently. Under Islamic rule, Manichaeans sometimes obtained limited tolerance but were also prosecuted as *zanādiqa*. Their texts and terminology survived eastward even as organized western communities declined.

### Central Asia and the Uyghur period

The Uyghur Khaganate adopted Manichaeism as a court-supported religion in 762. Iranian terminology was translated into Sogdian and Uyghur cultural settings. The Living Spirit remained the demiurge, but Mihr terminology varied geographically. Turfan manuscripts and paintings are the principal surviving evidence from this phase.

### China

Manichaeism entered Tang China through Central Asian networks. Chinese translators rendered divine names through Buddhist and Daoist vocabulary. “Living Spirit” became “Pure Wind”; in some texts this figure was displaced or merged with the Light-Nous. This is a historical transformation of classification, not evidence that Mani originally identified the two.

The fall of the Uyghur Khaganate in 840 removed major political protection. Tang persecution in 843–845 closed temples, confiscated property, and killed or dispersed foreign clergy and adherents. Manichaeism survived clandestinely or in syncretic forms in southeastern China. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Iranian Religions: Manicheism”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iran-ix1-religions-in-iran/iran-ix12-manicheism/)

### Song–Yuan–Ming transformations

Chinese Manichaeans adapted to Buddhist, Daoist, and popular religious settings. Yuan-era paintings preserve a highly developed visual cosmology; later communities associated Mani with a Buddha of Light. The Living Spirit’s distinct personality became harder to isolate as offices were translated and conflated.

Cao’an in Fujian preserves a famous image of Mani and material evidence for Chinese Manichaean history, but it is not a surviving temple of the Living Spirit specifically.

### Present

There is no securely documented continuous modern institution worshipping the Living Spirit under his ancient name. Reports of secret Manichaean survival in Fujian require ethnographic verification; local traditions and buildings may preserve Manichaean elements without maintaining the complete medieval pantheon.

Online “Manichaean churches,” catechisms, icons, prayer beads, and reconstructions are **modern revivals**. Some responsibly use translated sources; others invent scriptures, uninterrupted lineages, prophecies, or elaborate correspondences. No claim of contemporary continuity should be accepted solely from self-published material.

## 8. Modern reception and reinvention

The Living Spirit has had a modest independent afterlife. Modern scholarship, rather than literature or film, created his principal contemporary profile.

Hans Jonas made Manichaean myth central to an existential interpretation of “Gnosticism,” emphasizing alienation and rescue. Kurt Rudolph, Bentley Layton, Birger Pearson, Michael Allen Williams, Karen King, John D. Turner, and Einar Thomassen refined or criticized the category “Gnosticism.” Their works illuminate the comparative setting but do not make the Living Spirit a figure in Sethian or Valentinian scripture.

Modern popular summaries frequently commit four errors:

1. They equate him directly with the Christian Holy Spirit.
2. They equate him directly with Mandaean Hibil Ziwa.
3. They identify him with Mithra across every Iranian language.
4. They make him an evil Gnostic demiurge.

All four erase documented distinctions.

No substantial Renaissance Hermetic, Golden Dawn, Theosophical, or pre-twentieth-century occult cult of the Living Spirit was located. Modern occult or neopagan invocations appear derivative of twentieth- and twenty-first-century translations. Claims that such practice preserves an ancient initiatory lineage are **unverified modern invention**.

Modern artwork depicting him as a Persian winged god, Buddhist bodhisattva, Zoroastrian yazata, or angel can be meaningful creative reception, but it is not a reproduction of securely identified ancient iconography.

No documented nationalist or extremist movement centered specifically on the Living Spirit was located. “Manichaean” is frequently used politically to mean rigid dualism, but this metaphor rarely refers to the deity or accurately reflects his role as organizer of a mixed cosmos.

## 9. Historiography and scholarly debate

### Discovery history

Before the twentieth century, Manichaeism was reconstructed mainly from opponents: Augustine, Ephrem, the *Acta Archelai*, Greek and Syriac heresiologists, and Islamic authors. This encouraged the picture of a derivative and bizarre heresy.

The Turfan expeditions beginning around 1900 recovered Iranian, Uyghur, and Chinese fragments. The Medinet Madi discovery in 1929 produced a Manichaean library in Coptic. The Cologne Mani Codex, recognized in the twentieth century, transformed understanding of Mani’s youth. Southern Chinese paintings identified in the twenty-first century transformed knowledge of Manichaean visual culture.

### Principal debates

**Good demiurge versus “Gnostic” creator.** The evidence firmly makes the Living Spirit good. The debate concerns classification: whether Manichaeism belongs within “Gnosticism” despite its positive demiurge and cosmic apparatus, or whether the category obscures important differences.

**Original gender.** Syriac grammatical femininity and maternal imagery support investigation of feminine or androgynous aspects. Coptic masculine grammar and paternal titles oppose a confident reconstruction of an originally female deity.

**Mithraic origin.** The Living Spirit is not simply Iranian Mithra transplanted. Middle Persian identification is early, but Parthian and Sogdian assignments diverge. Scholars dispute whether this represents varying local solar theology, independent missionary translation, or stages in Mani’s evolving terminology.

**Seduction of the archons.** The assignment of the revelatory display to both Living Spirit and Third Messenger is best treated as competing transmission, not as two automatically consecutive seductions.

**Five or six sons.** Most systematic accounts give five sons; some count the Call as a sixth. The discrepancy reflects the fluid boundary between divine utterance, hypostasis, and genealogy.

**Nag Hammadi dependence.** Shared motifs do not prove that Mani copied the surviving Nag Hammadi treatises. Some codices are later than early Manichaean activity, and influence may have been indirect or reciprocal.

**Mandaean relationship.** Comparable Aramaic vocabulary and savior descents indicate a common late-antique religious environment. A direct Living Spirit–Hibil identity, a common canonical pantheon, or Mani’s membership in the historical Mandaean religion is not established.

### State of the evidence

The record is fragmentary and multilingual. Mani’s Syriac originals are mostly lost; surviving Iranian texts are often scraps; Coptic codices suffered deterioration and wartime disruption; Chinese texts translate technical language into Buddhist and Daoist categories. Every reconstruction is therefore synoptic. Theodore bar Kōnay’s tidy Syriac sequence cannot be treated as the sole canonical version, but neither can it be dismissed when independent sources corroborate it.

## 10. Chronology

- **216 CE:** Traditional date of Mani’s birth in southern Mesopotamia. The Living Spirit is not yet attested.
- **228 and 240:** Mani’s reported revelations at ages twelve and twenty-four; later biographical tradition, not independent attestation of the deity.
- **240/241:** Accession of Šābuhr I.
- **242:** Probable date of Mani’s royal audience and public mission.
- **Mid-third century:** Mani composes the *Šābuhragān*; its cosmology supplies the earliest datable stratum for Mihryazd/Living Spirit.
- **276/277:** Death of Mani under Sasanian authority; later commemorated at the Bema.
- **Ca. 302:** Diocletianic repression of Manichaeans in the Roman Empire.
- **Fourth century:** Christian anti-Manichaean testimony expands; Manichaean communities flourish in Egypt and North Africa.
- **Ca. 372–382:** Augustine participates as a Manichaean Hearer.
- **Late fourth–early fifth century:** Coptic Medinet Madi texts circulate; *Kephalaia* systematize the second creation.
- **397–398:** Augustine writes *Contra Faustum*, preserving and attacking Western Manichaean claims.
- **Fifth century:** Continued Roman/Byzantine legal suppression.
- **Sixth–seventh centuries:** Organized western Manichaeism contracts; Central Asian transmission continues.
- **Late eighth century:** Theodore bar Kōnay records the most continuous surviving Syriac narrative of the Living Spirit.
- **762:** Uyghur ruler Bügü Qaghan adopts Manichaeism; Central Asian institutional expansion follows.
- **Eighth–eleventh centuries:** Major period represented by the Turfan fragments.
- **840:** Collapse of the Uyghur Khaganate removes Manichaeism’s chief eastern imperial patron.
- **843–845:** Tang suppression closes temples and disperses adherents.
- **Tenth century:** Ibn al-Nadīm records Manichaean books and doctrines in the *Fihrist*.
- **Ca. 1030:** Al-Bīrūnī preserves quotations and comparative information.
- **Thirteenth–fourteenth centuries:** Southern Chinese *Diagram of the Universe* visualizes the layered Manichaean cosmos.
- **Ming period and after:** Chinese Manichaean communities become increasingly syncretic and clandestine.
- **Ca. 1900 onward:** Turfan discoveries restore internal Iranian and Central Asian sources.
- **1929:** Medinet Madi Manichaean library discovered.
- **1969:** Cologne Mani Codex becomes known to scholarship.
- **Late twentieth century:** Critical editions and comparative studies clarify the Living Spirit’s multilingual names and distinguish Middle Persian Mihryazd from the Parthian/Sogdian Mihr–Third Messenger equation.
- **2010:** A southern Chinese silk painting is identified as a Manichaean cosmological diagram.
- **Present:** The Living Spirit survives as an object of scholarship and small-scale religious reconstruction; no continuous independent cult is securely documented.

# Saklas: Research Dossier

**Class and earliest secure attestation.** In the surviving Sethian and closely related Christian-Gnostic texts, Saklas is an **archon**, **demiurgic ruler**, or **demon**: an ignorant and usually hostile maker or governor of the lower cosmos, not the transcendent God. In the *Apocryphon of John*, “Saklas” is one of three names—Yaldabaoth, Saklas, Samael—of a single chief ruler. In the *Gospel of Judas*, Saklas and Nebro are separate subordinate rulers. In Western Manichaean tradition, Saklas is a male demon or archon who, with a female partner, fashions Adam and Eve. No securely identified cult, temple, priesthood, or ancient image of Saklas is known. The earliest recoverable literary stratum bearing the name is conventionally placed in the **late second or early third century CE**, especially the Greek antecedents of the *Apocryphon of John* and *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit*; the earliest surviving physical witnesses are fourth-century Coptic codices. Epiphanius independently records a Saklas as an “archon of fornication” around 375 CE. Claims for a first-century Saklas, a pre-Christian cult of Saklas, or an unbroken Saklas-worshipping community are unestablished.

Labels used below:

- **[EA]** securely attested early material, approximately second–third century
- **[LC]** later canonical or school elaboration within an ancient tradition
- **[RV]** regional or textual variant
- **[LS]** learned systematization
- **[SI]** syncretistic identification
- **[MA]** medieval or early-modern accretion
- **[F]** folklore
- **[MSR]** modern scholarly reconstruction
- **[MI]** modern invention or reception

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### Orientation

Saklas is not one stable personality transmitted by a single church. The name occurs in several mythological arrangements created by late-antique religious authors who reworked Genesis, Jewish angelology, Christian revelation discourse, Platonic cosmology, and Near Eastern dualisms.

Three configurations must remain separate:

1. **The three-name demiurge**: in the *Apocryphon of John*, *Hypostasis of the Archons*, and *Trimorphic Protennoia*, Saklas is substantially the same ruler as Yaldabaoth and Samael. **[EA/RV]**
2. **A distinct lower ruler**: in the *Gospel of Judas*, Nebro creates six angels “as well as Saklas”; Saklas subsequently commands the creation of Adam and Eve. He is not simply identified with Nebro or Yaldabaoth. **[EA/RV]**
3. **The Manichaean procreator**: in Western Manichaean sources Saklas, Ašqalūn, Šaqlūn, or a related form names the male archon who, with Nebroel or her Iranian equivalents, concentrates imprisoned light in the human body. **[LC/RV]**

The Mandaean Ptahil is a **structural cousin**, not a documented alias. Ptahil constructs the material world with Ruha, the Seven, and the Twelve, and the material body of Adam must receive life from the World of Light. No reliable evidence examined for this dossier calls Ptahil “Saklas.” **[MSR: comparison, not identity]**

### Forms of the name

- **Coptic:** normally transcribed **Saklas**, **Sakla**, or **Saklas/Sakla**. Coptic manuscripts frequently preserve foreign names through spellings dependent on a Greek intermediary.
- **Greek:** reconstructed or reported as **Σακλᾶς**, *Saklas*. The final *-s* is the Greek nominative ending.
- **Syriac/Aramaic:** **ܣܟܠܐ**, *saklā*, “fool,” “foolish one,” “ignoramus.” This is the conventional and philologically persuasive explanation of the Gnostic name. **[EA/LS]**
- **Latin:** **Saclas**, in Western reports or translations connected with Manichaeism. **[RV]**
- **Middle Iranian Manichaean forms:** **Šaklūn/Šaqlūn**, with related spellings; these reflect a Syriac form conventionally vocalized **Ašqalūn/Ašaqlūn**, but their exact relationship to Greek *Saklas* is disputed. **[RV/contested]**
- **Syriac Manichaean:** Theodore bar Konai writes the male procreator’s name consonantally as **ʾšqlwn**. Franz Cumont read *Ašaqlūn* partly by comparison with Saklas; Martin Schwartz instead proposed an adaptation involving **Ashkelon**, the Palestinian city. Encyclopaedia Iranica cautions that Greek *Saklas*, transparently explained as Syriac *saklā*, should be kept distinct from the problematic Manichaean Ašqalūn forms. **[MSR/disputed]**
- **Arabic Manichaean:** al-Nadīm’s *Fihrist* has **Ṣendid/al-Ṣendīd** for a related demonic figure. This is not etymologically “Saklas,” although scholars have proposed narrative identity with Šaqlūn in parts of the transmitted myth. **[RV/MSR]**
- **Iranian female-partner forms:** Saklas’s partner is **Nebroel/Nebrōʾēl**, but Parthian and Sogdian sources substitute **Pēsūs**, and a Middle Persian equivalent may be **Afsōsag**. **[RV]**

### Epithets and classifications

- **“The Fool” / “Foolish One.”** The name’s most secure gloss, from Syriac or Aramaic *saklā*. **[EA]**
- **“Chief ruler” or “ruler.”** In texts where Saklas equals Yaldabaoth, he heads the lower archons. **[EA]**
- **“Demiurge.”** A modern analytical term, though based on the ancient function of cosmic craftsman or maker. The Coptic texts do not everywhere use “Saklas” as a fixed proper name for a Platonic *dēmiourgos*. **[MSR]**
- **“Great demon.”** *Trimorphic Protennoia* calls the lower ruler a great demon governing the lowest underworld and Chaos before naming him Saklas/Samael/Yaldabaoth. **[EA]**
- **“God of this world.”** The *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit* presents Saklas as the hostile worldly god who seeks to enslave Seth’s incorruptible race. **[EA]**
- **“Archon of fornication.”** Epiphanius, *Panarion* 26.10, places Saklas in the second heaven and gives him this title in his hostile description of the Phibionites. **[LC; hostile testimony]**
- **Male procreator of humankind.** In Manichaean cosmogony, Saklas is a demonic embodiment or instrument of Lust/Greed rather than an independent supreme creator. **[LC/RV]**

### The three names

The long and short recensions of the *Apocryphon of John* identify the chief ruler with three names:

1. **Yaldabaoth/Ialdabaoth** — first name;
2. **Saklas/Sakla** — second name, “Fool”;
3. **Samael** — third name, glossed in related texts as “blind god” or “god of the blind.”

This is a triple naming of one entity in that textual family, not evidence that all ancient Gnostic authors considered the three names universally interchangeable. *Trimorphic Protennoia* similarly strings the names together. *Hypostasis of the Archons* alternates them within its narrative. The *Gospel of Judas*, however, makes Saklas a distinct being subordinate to or associated with Nebro and also mentions Yaldabaoth separately. **[EA/RV]**

The widespread modern formula “Yaldabaoth = Saklas = Samael = Yahweh = Satan” is therefore an over-harmonization. The first three are co-referential in some texts; identification with the biblical creator is an exegetical implication or polemical function; equation with the Jewish Samael tradition, Satan, Lucifer, or a modern “prison-planet” ruler varies by source and is often modern. **[MSR/MI]**

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### Earliest evidence

No inscription, coin, theophoric personal name, statue, or temple dedication to Saklas is known. The evidence is literary.

The earliest **surviving artifacts** containing the Sethian Saklas traditions are Coptic papyrus codices produced in Egypt in approximately the fourth century:

- Nag Hammadi Codices II, III, IV, and XIII;
- Berlin Papyrus Codex 8502;
- later, Codex Tchacos for the *Gospel of Judas*.

The Nag Hammadi collection consists of thirteen papyrus codices, generally assigned to the later fourth century, found near Jabal al-Tarif in Upper Egypt in 1945. Their tractates are mostly Coptic translations of earlier Greek compositions. The codices are now principally in the Coptic Museum, Cairo. The reason for their burial and the identity of their owners remain disputed. **[physical evidence]**

### Apocryphon of John

**Witnesses.**

- NHC II,1 and IV,1: long recension.
- NHC III,1 and Berlin Codex 8502,2: short recension.
- Language of surviving witnesses: Coptic, in more than one dialectal form.
- Archetype: Greek.
- Manuscripts: fourth century.
- Composition: normally placed in the second century, with redactional development continuing into the third. Irenaeus’s *Against Heresies* 1.29–30, around 180 CE, describes closely related Barbeloite and Ophite mythology but does not securely prove that he possessed the extant recension or that the word “Saklas” stood in his source. **[EA/MSR]**

**Role.** Sophia produces a malformed ruler without the consent of her consort. She hides him outside the immortal realm. He creates subordinate authorities, takes power from his mother, and boasts that he alone is God because he is ignorant of what lies above him. The text names him Yaldabaoth, Saklas, and Samael. He and his rulers fashion Adam after a revealed heavenly image; through a stratagem originating above them, the power stolen from Sophia enters Adam. Saklas tries to restrain humanity through bodily imprisonment, counterfeit spirit, sexual reproduction, death, and flood. **[EA]**

The recensions differ in ordering, wording, lists of authorities, and a long recension’s additional material often associated with a “Book of Zoroaster.” Consequently, no single English translation should be treated as the pristine text. Waldstein and Wisse’s synoptic critical edition is indispensable; the Brill *Coptic Gnostic Library Online* supplies Coptic texts and translations. **[LS]**

### Hypostasis of the Archons

- NHC II,4; Coptic translation of a Greek work.
- Surviving manuscript: fourth century.
- Composition: usually third century, possibly drawing on earlier Jewish exegetical materials. **[EA/MSR]**

The arrogant chief ruler is first corrected by a heavenly voice—“You are mistaken, Samael”—and later by Zoe, daughter of Pistis Sophia: “You are mistaken, Sakla,” after which Zoe’s breath becomes a fiery angel that binds Yaldabaoth and casts him into Tartarus. Saklas, Samael, and Yaldabaoth operate as names of the same ruler in this narrative. His son Sabaoth rebels against him and is elevated by Sophia/Zoe. **[EA]**

### On the Origin of the World

- NHC II,5 and NHC XIII,2; Coptic witnesses.
- Composition commonly placed in the later third or early fourth century, though it incorporates earlier materials. **[LC/MSR]**

This work extensively develops the *Hypostasis*-type myth: Pistis, Sophia, Yaldabaoth/Samael, Sabaoth, Zoe, the planetary rulers, Paradise, Eve, and the destruction of the rulers. Translations and summaries often supply “Saklas” as an equivalent for Yaldabaoth, but the dossier should not count every occurrence of the chief ruler as a manuscript occurrence of the word. **[LC; textual caution]**

### Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit / Gospel of the Egyptians

- NHC III,2 and IV,2.
- Coptic translations from Greek.
- Surviving manuscripts: fourth century.
- Composition: commonly later second to third century. **[EA/MSR]**

Saklas is the “god of the world” who seeks to destroy or enslave the incorruptible seed of Seth. The heavenly powers arrange Seth’s descent and preservation of his race. The tractate’s final form incorporates baptismal and hymnic material connected with the Five Seals. The title “Gospel of the Egyptians” is given in its colophon and should not be confused with the unrelated Greek gospel known from Clement of Alexandria. **[EA]**

### Trimorphic Protennoia

- NHC XIII,1.
- Coptic translation of a Greek work; fourth-century manuscript.
- Composition often placed in the later second or third century. **[EA/MSR]**

The descending divine First Thought describes a “great demon” governing the lowest underworld and Chaos: “he is called Saklas, that is, Samael, Yaltabaoth.” He stole power from Sophia or the descended Epinoia and produced lower aeons as counterfeits of the true ones. **[EA]**

### Gospel of Judas

- Codex Tchacos, tractate 3.
- Coptic; underlying Greek composition.
- Codex radiocarbon dating: approximately 280 CE ±60 years, broadly consistent with a third- or fourth-century manuscript.
- Greek composition: usually placed in the middle or later second century, partly because Irenaeus mentions a “Gospel of Judas” around 180 CE; whether his gospel was precisely the surviving text remains debated. **[EA/MSR]**

Here Nebro, whose name is glossed “rebel” and whose appearance is blood-stained and fiery, creates six angels “as well as Saklas” as assistants. Saklas and the angels produce twelve heavenly rulers. Saklas orders: “Let us create a human being after the likeness and after the image,” and Adam and Eve are made.

Jesus accuses the disciples’ sacrificial cult of being directed to Saklas. Judas will “exceed” the others by sacrificing the human being who clothes Jesus. Initial publicity construed Judas as Jesus’s enlightened helper; April DeConick and other scholars argued that the grammar and narrative make him a tragic or demonic figure who exceeds the others in wickedness. Later studies have proposed intermediate or more complex readings. The damaged manuscript prevents complete certainty. **[EA; contested interpretation]**

### Epiphanius, Panarion 26

- Greek, composed approximately 374–377 CE.
- Hostile Christian heresiology. **[LC/hostile testimony]**

Epiphanius claims that people he calls Gnostics, Borborites, or Phibionites assigned archons to heavens: Iao in the first, Saklas “the archon of fornication” in the second, Seth in the third, David in the fourth, and other figures above. His accompanying accusations of ritual sexual activity and consumption of sexual fluids are polemical and cannot be accepted without independent evidence. The name and heavenly list may preserve genuine classified mythology; the sensational ethnography is uncorroborated. **[LC; contested]**

### Manichaean corpus

#### Kephalaia

The Coptic *Kephalaia* manuscripts from Medinet Madi date approximately 380–430 CE. They are evolving community compilations presented as teachings of Mani, not books authored directly by him. The genre may have begun within Mani’s circle in the late third or early fourth century, but its layers cannot be treated as uniformly early.

The *Kephalaia* contain **Saklas** and preserve Manichaean anthropogony in which demonic rulers concentrate imprisoned light in the first humans. Gardner’s translation of the edited *Kephalaia of the Teacher* remains standard in English; later work by Wolf-Peter Funk completed additional portions of the Berlin codex. **[LC]**

#### Iranian fragments

Middle Persian, Parthian, and Sogdian fragments from Central Asia use forms such as Šaklūn or Šaqlūn. The male and female procreators devour demonic offspring laden with light, then form Adam and Eve in the image of the Third Messenger. The fragments are direct Manichaean evidence but frequently later manuscript witnesses to earlier compositions. **[LC/RV]**

#### Theodore bar Konai

Theodore’s Syriac *Book of Scholia*, late eighth century, preserves an important description of Manichaean cosmology, possibly dependent on older Syriac sources. It calls the male human-maker ʾšqlwn and his female companion Nebroel under variant spellings. Whether ʾšqlwn should be vocalized to connect directly with Saklas is disputed. **[MA source preserving LC tradition]**

#### Fihrist of al-Nadīm

Al-Nadīm’s Arabic *Fihrist*, completed in 987–988 CE, gives a detailed report of Manichaeism. Its two unnamed male and female archons create Adam and Eve; later a demon called al-Ṣendīd seduces Eve and attacks Seth. Scholars compare al-Ṣendīd with Saklas/Šaqlūn, but this identity is reconstructed rather than explicit in every passage. **[MA/MSR]**

#### Augustine and other anti-Manichaean witnesses

Augustine’s late-fourth- and early-fifth-century works preserve Latin Manichaean doctrines and quotations, particularly from Faustus and Mani’s *Fundamental Epistle*. They document anthropological dualism, imprisoned divine substance, and polemics over Genesis, but they are not straightforward witnesses to every detail of the Saklas narrative. The *Acta Archelai* and later abjuration formulae provide further hostile testimony. **[LC/hostile testimony]**

### Named works in the brief that do not securely attest Saklas

Absence must not be converted into an appearance:

- *Gospel of Truth* and *Tripartite Tractate*: Valentinian cosmologies; no secure named Saklas.
- *Zostrianos* and *Allogenes*: later Platonizing Sethian revelation texts; no substantial Saklas narrative.
- *Eugnostos the Blessed* and *Sophia of Jesus Christ*: cosmological discourse, but no secure Saklas episode.
- *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth*: Hermetic ascent text, not a Saklas source.
- *Corpus Hermeticum* I, *Poimandres*: contains cosmic rulers and an anthropogony important for comparison, but not Saklas.
- *Pistis Sophia* and *Books of Jeu*: contain elaborate archon, seal, and ascent systems; the familiar Yaldabaoth figure occurs in *Pistis Sophia*, but these works should not automatically be cited for the specific name Saklas.
- *Excerpta ex Theodoto*, Tertullian’s *Against the Valentinians*, and Valentinian reports in Irenaeus: crucial for neighboring demiurgic systems, but not evidence that all Valentinians called their creator Saklas.
- Cologne Mani Codex: essential for Mani’s youth and prophetic formation, not a primary Saklas anthropogony.
- Mandaean *Ginza Rabba*: primary evidence for Ptahil, Ruha, Ur, the Seven, and Adam’s light-soul, not for a being named Saklas.

## 3. Domain, Attributes, and Iconography

### Domains

**[EA] Sethian-related texts:**

- rule of the lower cosmos, Chaos, Hades, or the planetary heavens;
- organization or imitation of the visible cosmos;
- production of archons, angels, demons, and counterfeit aeons;
- formation or attempted enslavement of Adam and humanity;
- maintenance of ignorance through counterfeit spirit, fate, bodily desire, and death;
- opposition to Sophia, Epinoia, Zoe, Seth, and the saving revealer.

**[EA/RV] Gospel of Judas:**

- subordinate cosmic administration under or beside Nebro;
- creation of angels and the first human couple;
- recipient or symbolic object of the disciples’ corrupt sacrifices;
- association with the stars and rulers governing mortal generations.

**[LC/RV] Manichaeism:**

- embodiment or servant of Lust/Greed and matter;
- concentration of light particles inside human bodies;
- procreation or formation of Adam and Eve;
- sexual predation, seduction, and attempts to destroy Seth in later reports.

### Form

The *Apocryphon of John* describes Yaldabaoth, Saklas’s co-referent, as having a lion-like face and serpentine body or appearance. He is fiery because he is made from luminous power mixed with deficient matter. He is polymorphic in some recensions and can assume different faces. **[EA]**

The *Gospel of Judas* assigns the blood-stained fiery face explicitly to **Nebro**, not necessarily Saklas. Modern images that give Saklas Nebro’s bloody countenance conflate the two. **[EA/textual distinction]**

The Manichaean human-makers may be described as lion-shaped *āsrēštārs*, or Lust may “put on” male and female lion forms. Exactly how this corresponds to Saklas and Nebroel differs among Iranian, Syriac, Coptic, and Arabic sources. **[LC/RV]**

### Symbols and correspondences

No securely attested individualized emblem, sigil, sacred plant, liturgical color, festival day, or numerical cult correspondence belongs to Saklas.

Documented associations include:

- **lion and serpent:** from the Yaldabaoth form; **[EA]**
- **fire:** the lower ruler’s substance and power; **[EA]**
- **seven or twelve archons/heavens:** varying cosmological administrations; **[EA/RV]**
- **365 subordinate demons:** *Apocryphon of John*’s system; **[EA]**
- **planetary fate:** inferable from the archontic heavens and explicit in related systems; **[EA/LS]**
- **second heaven and fornication:** Epiphanius’s Phibionite list; **[LC/hostile]**
- **sexuality, cannibalism, and matter:** Manichaean cosmogony; **[LC]**

Modern internet sigils attributed to Saklas are not ancient. Saklas is absent from the standard Solomonic spirit catalogues and does not possess an authenticated medieval seal. **[MI]**

### Visual record

No ancient image bears a secure inscription identifying it as Saklas. Lion-headed serpentine figures on gems, magical objects, or modern diagrams are sometimes labeled “Yaldabaoth” or “Saklas,” but resemblance to the literary description does not establish identity. The Ophite diagram known through Origen and Celsus provides comparative archontic imagery but does not furnish a securely surviving portrait of Saklas. **[MSR; identification unproven]**

## 4. Genealogy and Relations to Other Divine Beings

### Genealogical systems

#### Apocryphon of John system

- **Mother:** Sophia, specifically a lower Wisdom who acts without her consort.
- **Higher source of his power:** the luminous power stolen from or inherited through Sophia.
- **Children/subordinates:** multiple authorities, often twelve chief authorities and a sevenfold planetary government.
- **Notable son:** Sabaoth, who repents or rebels against his father in related texts.
- **Opponents:** Sophia, Pronoia/Epinoia, Zoe, the revealer Christ, and the incorruptible race.
- **Human products:** Adam and Eve are fashioned by his authorities, although their living spiritual power comes from above.

#### Gospel of Judas system

- **Higher originator:** the luminary Eleleth produces lower cosmic powers.
- **Nebro:** primary rebellious ruler; creates six angels as well as Saklas.
- **Saklas:** produces or organizes twelve angels and commands Adam’s creation.
- **Adam and Eve:** created under Saklas’s command.
- **Jesus and Judas:** Jesus exposes Saklas’s cult; Judas’s sacrifice is placed within the overthrow or fulfillment of the lower rulers.

#### Manichaean system

- **Ontological parentage:** forces of Darkness and Lust/Greed rather than Sophia.
- **Partner:** Nebroel, Namrael, Pēsūs, Afsōsag, or an unnamed female archon.
- **Model copied:** the androgynous or sexually differentiated manifestation of the Third Messenger.
- **Products:** Adam and Eve.
- **Later relations:** Eve, Cain, Abel, Seth, and Wise of the Ages in the Arabic report.

### Every documented named divine interaction

The following list separates texts rather than harmonizing them.

- **Sophia — produces and then hides Saklas/Yaldabaoth; he steals or retains part of her light-power.** *Apocryphon of John*, Greek composition second–third century; Coptic witnesses fourth century. **[EA]**
- **Light Epinoia — Saklas overpowers or seizes power from her after her descent.** *Trimorphic Protennoia*, second–third century composition. **[EA/RV]**
- **Pronoia/Barbelo’s providential power — secretly causes Saklas to transfer his stolen power into Adam.** *Apocryphon of John*. **[EA]**
- **Christ/the revealer — exposes Saklas’s ignorance and descends into his creation to awaken humanity.** *Apocryphon of John* and *Trimorphic Protennoia*. **[EA]**
- **Zoe, daughter of Pistis Sophia — rebukes him as Sakla, breathes out a fiery angel, binds him, and casts him into Tartarus.** *Hypostasis of the Archons*, third-century composition. **[EA]**
- **Pistis Sophia — indirectly opposes him and supports the higher reordering of his rebellious son Sabaoth.** *Hypostasis of the Archons* and *On the Origin of the World*. **[EA/LC]**
- **Sabaoth — rebels against his father, condemns him, and is elevated above the lower powers by Sophia/Zoe.** *Hypostasis of the Archons* and *On the Origin of the World*. **[EA/LC]**
- **Eleleth — in the *Gospel of Judas* cosmology, produces or authorizes Nebro and Saklas within the lower order.** *Gospel of Judas*, second-century composition; Codex Tchacos third–fourth century. **[EA/RV]**
- **Nebro — creates six angels “and Saklas” as assistants; the two organize the lower heavens.** *Gospel of Judas*. **[EA/RV]**
- **Yaldabaoth — same entity as Saklas in the *Apocryphon of John*, *Hypostasis*, and *Trimorphic Protennoia*; a distinguishable named ruler in the *Gospel of Judas*.** **[EA/RV]**
- **Samael — same entity as Saklas in the three-name traditions; not securely the Jewish angel Samael in all respects.** *Apocryphon of John*, *Hypostasis*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*. **[EA]**
- **Jesus — names the disciples’ worship or sacrifice as service to Saklas and reveals the cosmic order to Judas.** *Gospel of Judas*. **[EA]**
- **Judas — sacrifices the bodily human who bears Jesus; the act is described in relation to those sacrificing to Saklas. Whether Judas thereby defeats, serves, or surpasses Saklas’s servants is disputed.** *Gospel of Judas*. **[EA; contested]**
- **Third Messenger — Saklas/Ašqalūn and his partner copy the Messenger’s displayed male-female beauty when fashioning humanity.** Manichaean cosmogony in Syriac and Iranian witnesses. **[LC/RV]**
- **Nebroel/Namrael/Pēsūs — female partner; together they consume light-bearing demonic offspring and fashion Adam and Eve.** Manichaean sources, with late-antique traditions preserved in Coptic, Iranian, Syriac, and Arabic witnesses. **[LC/RV]**
- **Āz (Lust/Greed) — Saklas and his partner are embodiments, garments, or instruments of Āz in Iranian versions.** Middle Persian Manichaean texts. **[LC/RV]**
- **King of Darkness/Ahriman — remote head of the demonic order to which the human-makers belong.** Manichaean cosmological sources. **[LC]**
- **Seth — Saklas or the related al-Ṣendīd attempts to destroy Seth; Adam saves him.** *Fihrist* and fragmentary Iranian parallels. **[MA witness to LC tradition]**
- **No documented interaction with Ptahil.** Their equation is comparative scholarship or modern harmonization. **[absence finding]**
- **No documented interaction with the Mandaean Hibil-Ziwa, Manda d-Hayyi, Ruha, or Ur.** **[absence finding]**
- **No documented ancient interaction with Lucifer.** The equation belongs to modern syncretism. **[MI]**

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### Sophia and the ignorant ruler

**[EA]** Sophia attempts to produce independently of her celestial partner. Her product is deficient, misshapen, and ignorant. Ashamed or alarmed, she hides him outside the immortal realms. Because he carries a portion of her power, he can create and command, but because he cannot see above himself, he declares that he alone is God.

He generates authorities and a lower cosmic imitation. The boast reworks biblical declarations such as Isaiah 45:5—“I am the Lord, and there is no other”—as evidence of ignorance: only a being unaware of higher reality would insist that nothing exists above him.

**Variants:**

- *Apocryphon of John*: extended Sophia-Yaldabaoth genealogy; Saklas is the second of his three names.
- *Hypostasis*: begins closer to Genesis exegesis and emphasizes heavenly rebukes, Norea, Zoe, and Sabaoth.
- *On the Origin of the World*: expands the rulers, Paradise, Sabaoth’s conversion, and eschatological destruction.
- *Trimorphic Protennoia*: frames the story from the descending First Thought’s voice and calls Saklas a great demon.
- *Gospel of Judas*: does not reproduce this genealogy; Nebro and Saklas arise in a different lower-cosmic scheme.

### Formation and animation of Adam

**[EA]** Saklas and his authorities see a luminous human image reflected or revealed from above. They decide to reproduce it. The body they form is inert. Higher powers trick Saklas into blowing or transferring his mother’s power into Adam. Adam then becomes luminous, intelligent, and superior to his makers. The rulers become jealous and imprison him in matter or Paradise.

The myth interprets Genesis 1:26’s plural “Let us make humanity in our image” as speech among lower rulers. It also separates bodily manufacture from spiritual animation.

In the *Gospel of Judas*, Saklas simply commands his angels to make a human “after the likeness and image,” and Adam and Eve are named. The surviving text does not reproduce the full stolen-light stratagem. **[RV]**

### Paradise, Eve, and the rulers

**[EA/LC]** In the *Apocryphon of John* family, the rulers place Adam in a deceptive paradise and attempt to control the spiritual power through sexuality and reproduction. The revealer reinterprets the serpent, tree, sleep, rib, and expulsion episodes in opposition to the chief ruler. The recensions differ over the roles of Epinoia, the serpent, and counterfeit spirit.

Care is necessary here: not every action of Yaldabaoth in these texts is accompanied by the name “Saklas,” though the *Apocryphon* has established the identity.

### Sabaoth’s rebellion

**[EA/LC]** Sabaoth, one of the chief ruler’s offspring, hears the higher voice, recognizes his father’s error, and condemns him. Sophia and Zoe elevate Sabaoth, give him a throne and celestial attendants, and establish him above the forces of Chaos. The father envies him. This is a striking exception to a simple “all archons are evil” scheme: a child of the demiurge can repent and be promoted.

### The binding of Saklas

**[EA]** In *Hypostasis of the Archons*, Zoe confronts the boastful ruler, breathes into his face, and her breath becomes a fiery angel. The angel binds Yaldabaoth and casts him into Tartarus. The passage names him Sakla/Yaldabaoth. This is one of the few narratives giving the ruler a concrete defeat rather than merely predicting his eventual dissolution.

### Seth and the incorruptible race

**[EA]** The *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit* presents the heavenly Seth and his seed as the object of Saklas’s aggression. Higher beings protect this race and arrange salvific descent, baptismal sealing, and the transmission or concealment of the book. The myth makes Saklas the persecuting god of the visible order, opposed to an elect race whose true identity is heavenly.

This language is mythic and ritual, not evidence for a biological ethnic group. Modern racial readings of the “seed of Seth” are unwarranted. **[MI when literalized racially]**

### Saklas, Nebro, and Judas

**[EA/RV]** In the *Gospel of Judas*, the immortal realms produce lower rulers. Nebro—the rebel—creates assistants including Saklas. Saklas creates further angels and commands the formation of Adam and Eve. The stars govern mortal people.

Jesus laughs at the disciples’ inability to recognize his origin. Their vision of a temple, altar, priests, sexual wrongdoing, killing, and child sacrifice is interpreted as their own corrupted ministry. They offer sacrifice to Saklas rather than the supreme God.

Judas is told that he will sacrifice the human who clothes Jesus. Three principal readings exist:

- **Positive Judas:** Judas knowingly releases Jesus from his body and surpasses the other disciples. This was prominent in the 2006 National Geographic presentation. **[modern interpretation]**
- **Negative Judas:** “You will exceed them” means that Judas will exceed the evil sacrificers in wickedness; he is called a *daimōn* and remains subject to the rulers. April DeConick and others advanced this reading. **[modern interpretation]**
- **Ambiguous/intermediate Judas:** Judas possesses superior knowledge yet remains implicated in sacrifice and astral fate; the text’s point is anti-sacrificial polemic more than simple rehabilitation or condemnation. **[modern interpretation]**

The damaged state of Codex Tchacos prevents a final harmonized reading.

### Manichaean creation of humanity

**[LC/RV]** The Manichaean story begins with two opposed realms, Light and Darkness. The forces of Darkness swallow the luminous sons of First Man. The cosmic machinery created by the powers of Light draws out much of the imprisoned substance. The Third Messenger appears in beautiful male and female forms, arousing the demons; their emissions and abortions contain light.

Lust/Greed devises a countermeasure. The male and female procreators—Saklas/Ašqalūn and Nebroel or their equivalents—devour light-bearing demonic offspring. They copulate and fashion Adam and Eve as images of the Third Messenger, concentrating the remaining light in human bodies. Humans are therefore both prisons constructed by Darkness and vessels of divine light capable of liberation.

Some sources make Saklas a seducer of Eve and aggressor against Seth. Al-Nadīm’s report adds incest, rape, Cain, Abel, and Wise of the Ages. These are genuine parts of the transmitted Manichaean dossier but survive in comparatively late hostile sources and cannot automatically be projected into Mani’s own wording. **[MA witness/LC tradition]**

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

### No cult of Saklas

There is no archaeological or epigraphic evidence for:

- a temple dedicated to Saklas;
- a priesthood of Saklas;
- a Saklas festival;
- sacrifices intentionally offered to Saklas by a self-identified community;
- votive statues or inscriptions;
- theophoric personal names;
- pilgrimage to a sanctuary;
- an oracle;
- an ancient magical invocation or sigil securely addressed to Saklas.

The texts portray him overwhelmingly as a being to be recognized, escaped, exposed, or defeated—not worshipped.

### Ritual contexts of the texts

The *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit* contains Five Seals and baptismal language. Saklas functions as the hostile ruler from whose world the sealed race is protected. The ritual is directed toward higher powers, not toward him. **[EA]**

The *Gospel of Judas* polemicizes against what it characterizes as sacrifice to Saklas. Most scholars read this as an attack on rival Christian ritual and theology—especially Eucharistic sacrifice, martyrdom, baptism, clerical misconduct, or sacrificial interpretations of Jesus’s death—not as documentation of literal people knowingly sacrificing humans to a deity named Saklas. **[EA/MSR]**

Epiphanius alleges that Phibionites offered sexual substances to archons, including Saklas’s surrounding hierarchy. His report is late, hostile, rhetorically pornographic, and uncorroborated. It cannot securely establish temple prostitution, ritual rape, infant sacrifice, or a Saklas priesthood. **[LC/contested]**

### Human sacrifice

The dossier finds **no secure evidence of a historical Saklas cult practicing human sacrifice**.

Human sacrifice appears in two documentary senses:

1. the *Gospel of Judas*’s visionary accusation that rival Christian priests kill children, spouses, or followers and offer sacrifice to Saklas;
2. its interpretation of Judas’s handing over Jesus’s mortal body as a sacrifice.

These are theological polemic and mythic narration. They are not archaeological testimony to an organized sacrificial cult. **[EA, literary claim only]**

### Manichaean practice

Manichaeans did not worship Saklas. Their ethical discipline sought to release light trapped through the processes Saklas and his partner had advanced. The Elect’s food practices, celibacy, confession, prayer, and avoidance of procreation were tied to the liberation of light and refusal to assist the demonic reproductive machine. Saklas belongs to explanatory myth, not the recipient side of Manichaean liturgy. **[LC]**

## 7. Historical Evolution

### Second century: formation of the demiurgic name

Jewish and Christian authors working with Genesis, Isaiah, angelology, and Platonism developed myths in which the biblical creator’s arrogance became evidence of ignorance. “Saklas,” the Fool, functioned as an insulting revelatory name. Irenaeus around 180 documents closely related systems centered on Barbelo, Sophia, Ialdabaoth, archons, and Adam, although his surviving text does not securely establish his use of “Saklas.” **[EA/MSR]**

### Late second to third century: diversified Sethian literature

The *Apocryphon of John*, *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit*, *Hypostasis*, and *Trimorphic Protennoia* preserve overlapping but non-identical Saklas traditions. The *Gospel of Judas* shows that Saklas could be separated from Yaldabaoth and subordinated to Nebro. There was therefore no single fixed “Saklas theology.” **[EA/RV]**

### Third century: Manichaean adoption and transformation

Mani’s religion synthesized Christian, Iranian, Mesopotamian, and Jewish-apocalyptic materials. Western Manichaean sources used Saklas for the male procreator of Adam. Iranian traditions transmitted a related but linguistically disputed Ašqalūn/Šaqlūn. In this system the figure is no longer Sophia’s solitary offspring: he belongs to primordial Darkness and acts with a female partner to imprison light in humanity. **[LC/SI]**

### Fourth–fifth centuries: manuscripts and polemics

The Nag Hammadi codices preserve the principal Sethian accounts in Coptic. Epiphanius classifies Saklas as an archon of fornication. The Coptic Manichaean codices from Medinet Madi preserve *Kephalaia* traditions. Imperial Christianization marginalized communities later classified as Gnostic, while Manichaeans faced legal persecution under both Roman and Sasanian regimes. No surviving decree singles out Saklas; suppression targeted communities and books, not this being’s cult. **[LC]**

### Sixth–tenth centuries: indirect survival

Named Sethian Saklas traditions disappear from securely documented institutional continuity. Manichaean versions survive in Syriac, Iranian, Central Asian, Chinese, and Arabic transmission. Theodore bar Konai and al-Nadīm preserve anthropogonies resembling the Saklas myth. **[MA]**

Claims that medieval Bogomils or Cathars directly worshipped or transmitted “Saklas” lack textual evidence. Their hostile-creator myths may have typological similarities or mediated inheritances, but the name is not a demonstrated continuous link. **[MSR; contested]**

### Nineteenth–twentieth centuries: recovery

European publication of Mandaean, Manichaean, and Coptic manuscripts enabled comparison of previously separate demiurge traditions. Berlin Codex 8502 was acquired in 1896 and eventually published in 1955. Manichaean codices appeared on the Cairo antiquities market in 1929. Nag Hammadi’s discovery in 1945 transformed direct access to Sethian literature.

The full Nag Hammadi English translation associated with James M. Robinson and the critical *Coptic Gnostic Library* made Saklas widely accessible. **[modern rediscovery]**

### Twenty-first century

Publication of the *Gospel of Judas* in 2006 brought Saklas into mass media. Publicity commonly portrayed him as the evil god worshipped unknowingly by ordinary Christians and Judas as Jesus’s heroic confidant; subsequent philological debate sharply qualified that reading.

Modern Gnostic churches and individual practitioners read the ancient texts, but no reliable demographic or institutional evidence establishes a large contemporary cult specifically devoted to Saklas. Usually he remains an adversarial symbol of ignorance, oppressive cosmic authority, or religious literalism. **[MI]**

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Scholarship and Jungian reception

Hans Jonas’s *The Gnostic Religion* interpreted Gnostic myths existentially as expressions of cosmic alienation. Kurt Rudolph synthesized the late-antique evidence into a history of “Gnosis.” These works strongly influenced twentieth-century presentations of the demiurge.

C. G. Jung’s *Seven Sermons to the Dead* and later psychological readings used motifs of the demiurge, Abraxas, and Sophia. Jung did not simply revive a historical Saklas cult, and **Abraxas is not an ancient synonym for Saklas**. Their equation in popular Jungian writing is a modern synthesis. **[MI]**

### Modern occultism

No evidence places Saklas in the classical Renaissance planetary magic, the Solomonic grimoires, the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn’s standard ritual corpus, or Crowley’s canonical Thelemic hierarchy. Modern invocations, seals, and demonolatry profiles of Saklas are contemporary constructions based on published Gnostic translations. **[MI]**

A contemporary “Saklas Publishing” uses the name commercially for occult books, ceremonial magic, Tarot, Thelema, and alchemy. This is reception and branding, not continuity with an ancient cult. **[MI]**

### Neo-Gnosticism

Modern Gnostic organizations, including the Ecclesia Gnostica founded under Stephan Hoeller’s leadership in Los Angeles in the 1970s, use Nag Hammadi scriptures in sacramental and educational settings. Their institutional and liturgical forms are modern reconstructions combining Independent Catholic, esoteric, Jungian, and ancient textual elements. Saklas is normally treated as the false creator or psychic image of ignorance, not adored.

Claims of uninterrupted apostolic transmission from the ancient Sethians are not historically demonstrated. **[MI]**

### Literature and games

- **Michael D. O’Brien, *The Father’s Tale* and other apocalyptic fiction:** comparable demiurgic ideas occur, though not always under Saklas’s name.
- **Philip K. Dick, *The Divine Invasion*, *VALIS*, and *The Transmigration of Timothy Archer*:** modern fiction deeply shaped by Gnostic false-creator motifs, but it does not provide ancient Saklas tradition.
- **Richard K. Morgan, *The Flight to Lucifer*:** features Saklas as a controlling false creator. **[MI]**
- **Tiny Tina’s Wonderlands:** “Saklas” is an NPC and quest giver who tries to forestall a calamity. This is a name-borrowing rather than an adaptation faithful to the ancient myths. **[MI]**

Modern internet narratives identify Saklas with extraterrestrials, simulation operators, “prison planet” wardens, reptilians, Satan, Lucifer, Yahweh, or artificial intelligence. These are modern inventions unless explicitly presented as new religious interpretation. No Nag Hammadi manuscript says that Saklas is an alien computer, reptilian species, or simulation architect. **[MI]**

### Political and extremist appropriation

No major historically documented ancient or modern nationalist cult of Saklas was found. Anti-Jewish writers can exploit the Gnostic identification of the ignorant ruler with the creator of Jewish scripture to portray Judaism as worship of an evil god. Ancient anti-creator exegesis should therefore be distinguished from modern racial antisemitism: the ancient texts conduct intra-Jewish and intra-Christian scriptural polemic, whereas modern racialized claims add categories not present in the manuscripts. **[MI]**

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Is “Gnosticism” one religion?

Earlier scholarship—from Hans Jonas through many textbook syntheses—often described a unified “Gnostic religion” with a standard myth: unknowable Father, Sophia’s fall, evil demiurge, divine spark, and saving knowledge.

Michael Allen Williams’s *Rethinking “Gnosticism”* argued that “Gnosticism” misleadingly groups unlike communities. Karen King likewise examined how the category was produced by ancient heresiology and modern historiography. Bentley Layton preferred more restricted groupings based on myth and texts. John D. Turner reconstructed a historical “Sethian” trajectory from pre-Christian or non-Christian myth through Christianization, ritual elaboration, and Platonizing contemplative literature. Birger Pearson, David Brakke, Einar Thomassen, and others differ over boundaries, chronology, and social institutions.

For Saklas, the practical conclusion is firm: the name cannot be assigned one universal definition across “Gnosticism.” **[MSR]**

### Date of the Apocryphon of John

Arguments for composition before 180 rely on Irenaeus’s close parallels. Critics note that parallel mythology does not prove identity with the surviving work. The four Coptic witnesses establish a developed textual tradition by the fourth century; their long and short recensions demonstrate substantial redaction. A second-century core with third-century development remains a common reconstruction, not a dated autograph. **[MSR]**

### Etymology

The derivation of Greek/Coptic Saklas from Syriac-Aramaic *saklā*, “fool,” is strong because it fits both form and narrative function.

The equation of Manichaean ʾšqlwn with the same name is less secure. Cumont’s *Ašaqlūn*, Nöldeke’s proposed derivation from a term meaning “porter,” and Schwartz’s Ashkelon-related reading illustrate the debate. The Greek Saklas form should not be used to settle the Iranian/Syriac vocalization automatically. **[MSR/contested]**

### Saklas and Yaldabaoth

They are explicitly one being in the *Apocryphon of John* family. They are not universally identical: the *Gospel of Judas* distinguishes the named lower rulers. Scholarship that speaks of “the Gnostic demiurge” is useful shorthand only if the textual configuration is specified. **[EA/MSR]**

### Saklas and the biblical God

The texts clearly parody or invert biblical creator-language. The boast echoes Isaiah and Deuteronomy; “Let us make” reinterprets Genesis. It is historically reasonable to say that Saklas represents a hostile rereading of the biblical creator. It is less exact to assert that every text explicitly says “Saklas is YHWH,” since the tetragrammaton is not consistently used as an equation and subordinate archons bear names derived from Iao, Sabaoth, Adonai, and Elohim. **[MSR]**

### Gospel of Judas controversy

The first published translation and media framing in 2006 depicted Judas positively. DeConick, Louis Painchaud, John Turner, Simon Gathercole, Lance Jenott, and others disputed particular restorations and the meaning of *daimōn*, “thirteenth,” “exceed,” and “sacrifice.” The dispute is inseparable from Codex Tchacos’s fragmentation and the pressure of rapid publication.

Consensus has shifted away from the simplest “Judas was the good disciple obeying Jesus” headline. No single negative interpretation has nevertheless eliminated every ambiguity. **[MSR]**

### Epiphanius and sexual rites

Epiphanius claims personal knowledge of a sexually libertine group and reports ritual collection of sexual fluids and aborted fetuses. Scholars debate whether he preserves observation, recycled heresiological stereotypes, literary fabrication, or some mixture. Without independent artifacts or internal group texts, the allegations cannot prove actual Phibionite practice. The name Saklas in his archon list is more readily separable from the lurid accusations than the accusations are verifiable. **[contested]**

### Mandaean comparison

Jonas, Rudolph, Drower, Buckley, and later specialists compare Mandaean cosmology with Sethian and Manichaean materials. The analogies are real: lower creators, hostile planets, a light-soul in Adam, redeemers from the World of Light. Yet Ptahil is not simply evil in every passage and is not named Saklas. Mandaeism is a living religion with its own textual and priestly history, not a fossilized Sethian sect. **[MSR]**

### State of the evidence

- Nag Hammadi codices: principally Coptic Museum, Cairo; one Codex III leaf at Yale’s Beinecke Library.
- Berlin Codex 8502: Egyptian Museum and Papyrus Collection, Berlin.
- Medinet Madi Manichaean *Kephalaia*: Berlin Papyrus Collection and Chester Beatty Library, Dublin.
- Codex Tchacos: heavily damaged, dispersed, partly recovered; modern conservation and ownership history affected the text.
- Central Asian Manichaean fragments: chiefly Berlin and other collections formed from Turfan expeditions.
- Mandaean manuscripts: Bodleian, Bibliothèque nationale de France, British Library, Drower Collection, and living Mandaean collections.

The physical manuscripts postdate the presumed compositions, sometimes by centuries. Every confident reconstruction must therefore distinguish composition, recension, translation, and surviving copy.

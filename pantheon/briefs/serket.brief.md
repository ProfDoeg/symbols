BEING. Serket (Selket) (Egyptian pantheon). State at the top the being's class (god, goddess, spirit, demon,
monster, collective) as the tradition classifies it, and its earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the scorpion goddess of the canopic shrine of Tutankhamun, the Scorpion Macehead question, the healer of stings, the Metternich Stela's Isis and the seven scorpions, the Serket priests as physicians.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Pyramid Texts (Allen, Faulkner), Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead (Faulkner, Allen), the Memphite Theology (Shabaka Stone), the Contendings of Horus and Seth, Plutarch's De Iside et Osiride, the Greek Magical Papyri, temple inscriptions (Edfu, Dendera, Karnak, Philae), the Turin king list; Wilkinson's Complete Gods and Goddesses, Hornung's Conceptions of God, Assmann, Pinch's Handbook of Egyptian Mythology, the Lexikon der Ägyptologie, the Digital Giza and TLA (Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae), the British Museum and Louvre records.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named divine being gets its own line (the other being, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for most of this pantheon and the
files cross-reference by name. Absence of evidence is a finding.

BEING. Tuisto and Mannus (Norse and Germanic pantheon). State at the top the being's class (god, goddess, spirit, demon,
monster, collective) as the tradition classifies it, and its earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: Tacitus's earth-born god and his son Mannus, the three sons and the tribes (Ingaevones, Herminones, Istaevones), the Ymir comparison, the sixteenth-century humanist reception.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Poetic Edda (Larrington, Bellows, the Codex Regius), Snorri's Prose Edda (Faulkes) and Heimskringla, the skaldic corpus (Skaldic Project), Saxo Grammaticus, Tacitus's Germania, Adam of Bremen, the Merseburg charms, the runic inscriptions (Samnordisk runtextdatabas), the Nibelungenlied and Völsunga saga, Wagner's Ring texts where the brief names them; Simek's Dictionary of Northern Mythology, Lindow, Turville-Petre, de Vries, Dumézil, Orchard, the Kulturhistorisk leksikon.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named divine being gets its own line (the other being, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for most of this pantheon and the
files cross-reference by name. Absence of evidence is a finding.

BEING. Osumare (Oxumaré) (Yoruba (the orisha, with their Cuban and Brazilian forms) pantheon). State at the top the being's class (god, goddess, spirit, demon,
monster, collective) as the tradition classifies it, and its earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the rainbow serpent, male and female by turns, the Dahomean Dan/Aido-Hwedo, the Brazilian Oxumaré with the snake, the Cuban near-absence.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Ifá corpus (the Odu Ifá as recorded by Bascom, Abimbola, Epega and Neimark), the oriki praise poems, Samuel Johnson's History of the Yorubas (1921), Frobenius, the Ile-Ife and Oyo archaeology and the Ife heads, Idowu's Olodumare, Bascom's Ifa Divination and Sixteen Cowries, Pemberton, Drewal, Apter, Peel's Religious Encounter and the Making of the Yoruba (the CMS missionary journals), Matory, Thompson's Flash of the Spirit; for the diaspora: Lydia Cabrera's El Monte, Ortiz, Bastide's Candomblé da Bahia, Verger's Orixás and Notes sur le culte des orisa, Prandi's Mitologia dos Orixás, the Umbanda literature (Ortiz's A morte branca do feiticeiro negro), and the Argentine Umbanda and Batuque scholarship (Frigerio, Carozzi).

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named divine being gets its own line (the other being, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for most of this pantheon and the
files cross-reference by name. Absence of evidence is a finding.

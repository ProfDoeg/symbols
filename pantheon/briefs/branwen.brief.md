BEING. Branwen (Celtic (Irish, Welsh, Gaulish and British) pantheon). State at the top the being's class (god, goddess, spirit, demon,
monster, collective) as the tradition classifies it, and its earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the sister of Brân married to Matholwch of Ireland, the insult and the horse-mutilation, the starling messenger, the death of heart-break at Aber Alaw, the Second Branch, the Bedd Branwen cairn on Anglesey.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Irish mythological cycle (Lebor Gabála Érenn, Cath Maige Tuired, the Dindshenchas, the Táin, Tochmarc Étaíne, the Acallam), the Welsh Mabinogion (the Four Branches, Culhwch ac Olwen, the Book of Taliesin), Caesar's Gallic War VI, Lucan's Pharsalia I and its scholia, the Gaulish and Romano-British inscriptions (RIG, RIB), the Gundestrup cauldron, the Pillar of the Boatmen, the Coligny calendar; MacKillop's Dictionary of Celtic Mythology, Ó hÓgáin's The Lore of Ireland, Rees and Rees's Celtic Heritage, Green's Dictionary of Celtic Myth and Legend, Maier's Dictionary of Celtic Religion, Carey, Koch's Celtic Culture, the CELT corpus (UCC) for the Irish texts, the modern Celtic revival and neopagan reception (Yeats, Gregory, the Druid orders) as documented reception.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named divine being gets its own line (the other being, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for most of this pantheon and the
files cross-reference by name. Absence of evidence is a finding.

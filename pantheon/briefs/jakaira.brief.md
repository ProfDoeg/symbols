BEING. Jakaira (Guaraní, with the Paraguayan and Argentine (Corrientes, Misiones) folklore pantheon). State at the top the being's class (god, goddess, spirit, demon,
monster, collective) as the tradition classifies it, and its earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the lord of the mist and the tobacco smoke that protects, the fourth of the four (with Ñamandu, Karai, Tupã), the Ayvu Rapyta.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: Montoya's Tesoro de la lengua guaraní and Conquista espiritual (1639), Cadogan's Ayvu Rapyta (the Mbyá creation texts, 1959) and Ywyra Ñe'ery, Nimuendajú's Die Sagen von der Erschaffung und Vernichtung der Welt (the Apapokuva and the Land Without Evil, 1914), Métraux's La religion des Tupinamba, Hélène Clastres's La Terre sans Mal, Pierre Clastres's Le grand parler, Bartomeu Melià, Schaden, Susnik, Narciso R. Colmán's Ñande Ypy Kuéra (1929, the seven monsters as a literary systematization: say so), Ambrosetti and Gonzalez on Misiones and Corrientes, Villafañe on the Pombero, Susana Chertudi and Berta Vidal de Battini's Cuentos y leyendas populares de la Argentina, the Jesuit reductions' records.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named divine being gets its own line (the other being, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for most of this pantheon and the
files cross-reference by name. Absence of evidence is a finding.

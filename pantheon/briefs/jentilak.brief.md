BEING. The Jentilak (Iberian (Basque, Asturian-Cantabrian, Galician, pre-Roman) pantheon). State at the top the being's class (god, goddess, spirit, demon,
monster, collective) as the tradition classifies it, and its earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the pagan giants who built the dolmens and threw rocks between mountains, the Jentilarri, the last of them who announced the birth of Christ (Kixmi) and buried themselves, Olentzero as the one who stayed, the Basque prehistory as myth.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: Barandiarán's Mitología vasca and the Anuario de Eusko-Folklore, Caro Baroja, Azkue's Euskalerriaren Yakintza, Satrústegi, Hartsuaga; for Asturias and Cantabria: Cabal's La mitología asturiana, Llano Roza de Ampudia, Aurelio de Llano, Manuel Llano, Cotera, García Lomas's Mitología y supersticiones de Cantabria; for Galicia: Risco's Tradicións populares, Cuba and Reigosa's Diccionario dos seres míticos galegos, Lisón Tolosana; for the pre-Roman gods: the Latin inscriptions (the HEp and Hispania Antiqua corpora, the Endovelicus sanctuary at São Miguel da Mota, Ataecina at Turóbriga), Blázquez, Olivares Pedreño's Los dioses de la Hispania céltica, Prósper; Strabo III and the Roman authors.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named divine being gets its own line (the other being, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for most of this pantheon and the
files cross-reference by name. Absence of evidence is a finding.

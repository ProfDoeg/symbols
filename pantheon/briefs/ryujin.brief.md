BEING. Ryūjin (Watatsumi) (Japanese (Shinto, with the Buddhist and folk figures) pantheon). State at the top the being's class (god, goddess, spirit, demon,
monster, collective) as the tradition classifies it, and its earliest secure attestation.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the dragon king of the sea with the tide jewels, the palace under the sea (Ryūgū-jō), Hoori and Toyotama-hime, Urashima Tarō as reception, the Empress Jingū's jewels, the dragon kings of Buddhism as source.

WHERE THE SOURCES ARE for this tradition: the Kojiki (712, Philippi's translation) and Nihon Shoki (720, Aston), the Engishiki and the norito, the Fudoki, the Man'yōshū, the Kogo Shūi, the shrine records (Ise, Izumo, Usa), the Konjaku monogatari, Toriyama Sekien's yōkai catalogues; Motoori Norinaga's Kojiki-den, Hirata Atsutane, Yanagita Kunio's Tōno monogatari and folklore studies, Hori Ichirō, Breen and Teeuwen's A New History of Shinto, Kuroda's critique of 'Shinto' as a category, Foster's Book of Yōkai, Kokugakuin's Encyclopedia of Shinto online, the State Shinto period and the postwar shrine system as documented history.

STANCE. Legend as fully as fact; nothing discarded for being unprovable, everything labeled.
Where a thread above names a dispute, present both sides with their evidence. Every documented
interaction with another named divine being gets its own line (the other being, the nature of the
interaction, the source and date), since sibling dossiers exist for most of this pantheon and the
files cross-reference by name. Absence of evidence is a finding.

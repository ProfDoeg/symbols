# Adam Kadmon: Research Dossier

> **Class:** In Jewish Kabbalah, Adam Kadmon is a primordial divine configuration, supernal “world,” or anthropomorphic ordering of emanated divine light—not an angel, demon, independently worshipped god, or the biological Adam. In Christian Cabala, Hermetic Qabalah, Theosophy, and modern psychology, the figure was variously reclassified as Logos, cosmic humanity, occult archetype, or symbol of the Self.
>
> **Earliest secure attestation of the exact name:** **אָדָם קַדְמוֹן, ʾĀdām Qadmōn**, in *Sod Yediʿat ha-Meẓiʾut* (“Secret of the Knowledge of Reality”), an anonymous early-thirteenth-century Kabbalistic treatise. The concept’s scriptural, Philonic, rabbinic, and Gnostic analogues are older, but none securely uses this Hebrew name. [Encyclopaedia Judaica’s “Adam Kadmon” entry](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0001_0_00386.html)

## Evidentiary key

- **[Early antecedent]** A source earlier than the exact medieval term, relevant to its intellectual genealogy but not itself an attestation of Adam Kadmon.
- **[Secure medieval attestation]** A medieval Jewish source that names or clearly formulates the Kabbalistic figure.
- **[Later canonical elaboration]** A development that became authoritative within a later Kabbalistic school.
- **[Learned systematization]** A philosophical, diagrammatic, or scholastic ordering of earlier material.
- **[Syncretistic identification]** A historically datable equation with a figure from another tradition.
- **[Early-modern/modern reception]** A Christian, occult, literary, psychological, or popular-culture transformation.
- **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]** A historical comparison or genealogy proposed by scholars rather than stated by the primary sources.
- **[Modern invention]** A recent claim presented without support in the premodern record.

## 1. Identification, names, and epithets

### Core identity

**[Secure medieval attestation / later canonical elaboration]** Adam Kadmon is the human-shaped totality or first configuration of divine emanation. Early Kabbalistic texts can use “upper man” for the ten *sefirot* collectively, or for a particular supernal potency. In the mature Lurianic system transmitted by Ḥayyim Vital, Adam Kadmon is more precisely the first world formed within the primordial space produced by *ẓimẓum*, above the four familiar worlds of Emanation, Creation, Formation, and Making. It mediates between the unknowable *Ein Sof* and everything below, though “mediation” must not be mistaken for an independent person standing between two other persons. The figure is divine light articulated in human form.

It is therefore misleading to list Adam Kadmon as an angel alongside Michael, Gabriel, Metatron, or the fallen angels. No early or authoritative Kabbalistic source gives Adam Kadmon an angelic office, cult, messenger role, personal biography, individual will, or membership in an angelic choir. Nor is it a demon. Its placement in an “angelology and demonology” atlas is taxonomic convenience, not the source tradition’s classification.

### Names and forms

- **אָדָם קַדְמוֹן — ʾĀdām Qadmōn**, “Primordial Man/Human,” “Primal Humanity.” *ʾĀdām* can mean Adam, a human, or humanity collectively; *qadmōn* means primordial, anterior, or belonging to earliest time.
- **א״ק — A”K / ʾA.Q.**, the standard Hebrew abbreviation in later Kabbalistic writing.
- **אָדָם עֶלְיוֹן — ʾĀdām ʿElyon**, “Upper” or “Supreme Man.”
- **אָדָם עִילָּאָה / אָדָם דִּלְעֵילָא — ʾĀdām ʿIllaʾah / ʾĀdām di-leʿeila**, Zoharic Aramaic, “the supernal man” or “man above.”
- **אָדָם הַגָּדוֹל — ʾĀdām ha-Gadol**, “the Great Man,” found in later Zoharic/Tiqqunic discourse.
- **Adam Qadmon, Adam Kadmon, Adam Ḳadmon**, scholarly and occult transliterations.
- **Homo primordialis, homo archetypus, der Urmensch, l’Homme primordial**, early-modern and modern learned translations.
- **Macroanthropos / Anthropos**, comparative labels. These are not translations appearing in the earliest Hebrew witness.
- **Macroprosopus**, “Great/Long Countenance,” is sometimes loosely equated with Adam Kadmon in occult literature. In stricter Kabbalistic usage *Arikh Anpin* is a *parẓuf* within the world of *Aẓilut*, not simply identical to the prior world Adam Kadmon.

The early-twentieth-century *Jewish Encyclopedia* preferred *Adam ha-Qadmoni*, citing Numbers Rabbah’s epithet for the biblical Adam. That does not displace the securely attested Kabbalistic form *Adam Qadmon*. It instead illustrates how biblical Adam traditions and the later technical term were historically conflated. [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/761-adam-kadmon)

### Etymological cautions

**[Secure philology]** The name is ordinary Hebrew, not a cipher derived from “Ad-am-ak-ad-mon,” “red earth,” or an alleged prehistoric universal language. Occult etymologies of that kind are nineteenth-century inventions.

**[Later learned association]** The numerical value of **אדם**, *Adam*, is 45. Lurianic teaching associates this with the divine-name expansion called **מ״ה, MaH**, also 45. This is an exegetical-gematric association, not the historical etymology of the word.

**[Modern invention]** Online claims that *Adam Kadmon* was an ancient Semitic god called El, a Hebrew form of Egyptian Atum, a disguised extraterrestrial, or the historical object of a suppressed cult have no textual, epigraphic, or archaeological support.

## 2. Earliest attestation and textual corpus

### A. Sources that do not contain Adam Kadmon

The Hebrew Bible, Septuagint, 1 Enoch, Jubilees, Tobit, Dead Sea Scrolls, New Testament, Qurʾan, canonical hadith, and the classical Hekhalot corpus do **not** name Adam Kadmon. They matter only as sources for earlier images later reused in constructing primordial-human theology.

Likewise, *Sefer Raziel ha-Malʾakh*, *The Book of Abramelin*, and the *Lemegeton* do not supply an ancient narrative, invocation, or spirit-list entry for Adam Kadmon. Milton and Dante treat Adam, angels, creation, and cosmic order but do not document a cult or biography of Adam Kadmon.

### B. Biblical and ancient Jewish antecedents

#### Genesis 1:26–27 and 2:7

**[Early antecedent]** Genesis contains two differently framed presentations of humanity:

- Genesis 1:26–27: humanity, **אָדָם**, is created in the divine “image” and “likeness,” “male and female.”
- Genesis 2:7: YHWH Elohim forms **הָאָדָם**, “the human,” from soil and breathes life into it.

The text itself does not posit two Adams or name a heavenly archetype. The later distinction between supernal and terrestrial humanity is exegesis of these passages, especially their differences.

#### Ezekiel 1:26

**[Early antecedent]** Above the chariot-throne Ezekiel sees **דְּמוּת כְּמַרְאֵה אָדָם**, “a likeness as the appearance of a human.” Later Kabbalists could take this human form as warrant for anthropomorphic descriptions of the divine structure. Ezekiel does not call it Adam Kadmon.

#### Daniel 7:13–14

**[Early antecedent]** Daniel’s “one like a son of man” became central to Jewish and Christian heavenly-man speculation. It is not Adam Kadmon in the book of Daniel.

#### Philo of Alexandria

**[Early antecedent; first explicit Jewish two-human metaphysics]** In *De opificio mundi* §§69–71 and 134–35 and *Legum allegoriae* I, Philo distinguished the intelligible or heavenly human of Genesis 1 from the molded, sense-perceptible human of Genesis 2. The former is incorporeal, neither male nor female, and associated with divine image and paradigmatic reason. Philo wrote in Greek in the first century BCE/CE. This is the closest securely dated ancient Jewish antecedent, but calling Philo’s figure “Adam Kadmon” is retrospective. [Discussion and text summary](https://thetelos.org/predications-synagogales-philo-of-alexandria/)

#### Pauline heavenly Adam

**[Early Christian antecedent]** In 1 Corinthians 15:45–49, Paul opposes the first Adam, “from earth,” to the “last Adam” and “second man from heaven,” Christ. Romans 5 develops the Adam–Christ typology. Paul’s saving, eschatological Christ differs categorically from the Kabbalistic pre-cosmic configuration, although Christian Cabalists later identified them.

#### Rabbinic cosmic Adam

**[Early antecedent]** Several rabbinic traditions magnify *Adam ha-Rishon*, the first earthly Adam:

- Genesis Rabbah 8 describes the first human as androgynous or double-faced before division into male and female. [Bereshit Rabbah 8](https://www.sefaria.org/Bereshit_Rabbah.8?lang=bi&ven=english%7CThe_Sefaria_Midrash_Rabbah%2C_2022&vhe=hebrew%7CMidrash_Rabbah_--_TE&with=Translations)
- Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 38b and Ḥagigah 12a, says Adam originally extended from one end of the world to the other, or from earth to heaven, before God diminished him after his sin. [Sanhedrin 38b](https://www.sefaria.org/Sanhedrin.38b); [Ḥagigah 12a](https://www.sefaria.org/Chagigah.12a?lang=en)
- Related midrash presents Adam as a cosmic *golem* whose unformed body stretched across creation.

These passages contribute cosmic size, total humanity, and sexual undifferentiation. They still speak of the created Adam of Genesis, not a pre-cosmic world between *Ein Sof* and the *sefirot*.

#### Shiʿur Qomah and Hekhalot

**[Early-medieval antecedent]** The *Shiʿur Qomah* traditions assign immense measurements and divine names to the limbs of the enthroned divine body. Their surviving textual formation is usually placed in late antique or early-medieval Jewish mysticism, with difficult redactional histories. Kabbalistic anthropomorphism shares this symbolic environment, but the texts do not securely name Adam Kadmon. The relationship is conceptual and genealogical, not identity.

### C. Earliest exact Kabbalistic witnesses

#### *Sod Yediʿat ha-Meẓiʾut*

**[Secure medieval attestation, early thirteenth century]** The exact term *Adam Kadmon* first appears in this anonymous Kabbalistic treatise. Its physical manuscript history and precise authorship remain insufficiently settled for a more exact date or named author. This is consequently the correct earliest attestation—not Genesis, Philo, the Talmud, Isaac the Blind, or the Zohar. [Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0001_0_00386.html)

#### Isaac the Blind and early Provence

**[Related early Kabbalah; attribution disputed]** Isaac the Blind, son of Abraham ben David of Posquières, flourished around the turn of the thirteenth century and was foundational for Provençal Kabbalah. His surviving commentary on *Sefer Yeẓirah* and teachings transmitted by disciples articulate emanation, hidden causes, and the *sefirot*. The item brief’s phrase “the Adam Qadmon of Isaac the Blind” is too strong: the exact technical term cannot securely be assigned to Isaac on the present evidence. His school belongs to the milieu from which such speculation emerged; it is not the earliest unambiguous named witness.

### D. The Zoharic corpus

**[Secure medieval attestation / later canonical Kabbalah]** The main Zoharic corpus emerged in late-thirteenth-century Castile, substantially associated in modern scholarship with Moses de León and a wider circle, although it speaks pseudepigraphically through second-century Rabbi Simeon bar Yoḥai and companions. The first standard printed editions appeared at Mantua and Cremona in 1558–60. [History of the Mantua edition](https://beithazohar.com/en/the-printing-of-the-zohar-in-mantua-by-daniel-abrams/)

The Zohar more commonly speaks of:

- **Adam di-leʿeila / Adam ʿillaʾah**, the upper or supernal man;
- the divine human form as the pattern joining above and below;
- the ten *sefirot* configured as a body;
- corresponding male and female structures;
- the earthly human as an image of the supernal form.

In *Idra Rabbah* and *Idra Zuta*, the divine physiognomy is elaborated through heads, faces, hair, eyes, nose, beard, arms, torso, genital symbolism, and other bodily correspondences. These are symbolic articulations of divine powers. They are not portraits of a discrete giant walking through a narrative world.

The *Tiqqunei Zohar*, probably composed in the late thirteenth or early fourteenth century and distinct in authorship and vocabulary from the main Zohar, speaks of “the Great Man” and elaborates correspondence among divine names, *sefirot*, scripture, and body. Later readers often projected mature Lurianic Adam Kadmon back into these passages.

### E. Cordoverian synthesis

**[Learned systematization, sixteenth century]** Moses Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548; printed 1592) systematized pre-Lurianic Kabbalah. In this environment the human form organizes the *sefirot*, but Adam Kadmon does not yet occupy precisely the supra-*Aẓilut* position assigned by Luria. It may mean the total configuration of emanation or a high divine level. Treating Cordovero and Luria as teaching the same five-world scheme erases a historical change.

### F. Isaac Luria and Ḥayyim Vital

**[Later canonical elaboration, Safed, 1569–72 and after]** Isaac Luria taught orally in Safed for a brief period before his death in 1572. His disciples produced differing redactions. Ḥayyim Vital became the most influential transmitter; Joseph ibn Ṭabūl, Moses Jonah, Israel Sarug, Jacob Zemah, and Meir Poppers preserve or reorganize alternative versions. Contradictions among them should not be harmonized away.

Vital’s *ʿEẓ Ḥayyim*, *Oẓrot Ḥayyim*, *Mevo Sheʿarim*, and *Shaʿar ha-Haqdamot* supply the mature Adam Kadmon cosmology. *ʿEẓ Ḥayyim* circulated in manuscripts and was first printed at Korets in 1782; its familiar form was edited from several layers of Lurianic material. [Sefaria text](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.1.1-5.4); [first-edition record](https://winners-auctions.com/en/items/etz-hayim-korets-5542-1782-first-edition/)

The broad Lurianic sequence is:

1. *Ein Sof* fills all reality.
2. Through *ẓimẓum* it withdraws or conceals its light, leaving the *ḥalal*, primordial “vacated space,” with a residual impression, *reshimu*.
3. A ray, *qav*, enters.
4. Within the space, circular and linear configurations develop.
5. The linear, harmonically ordered configuration bears the form and name Adam Kadmon.
6. Lights issue metaphorically from its ears, nose, mouth, eyes, and forehead.
7. These produce the stages *AḥP* (ear–nose–mouth), *ʿAqqudim* (“bound”), *Neqqudim* (“points”), and *Berudim* (“mottled/interconnected”), though details vary by redaction.
8. In the world of points, vessels fail to contain the light: *shevirat ha-kelim*, the breaking of the vessels.
9. The subsequent ordering into *parẓufim*, divine configurations or “faces,” begins *tiqqun*, rectification.
10. Below emerge *Aẓilut*, *Beriʾah*, *Yeẓirah*, and *ʿAsiyyah*.

Adam Kadmon is thus prior to the conventional four worlds; sources that call it “the first of the four worlds” have usually collapsed a five-level scheme.

## 3. Domain, attributes, powers, and iconography

### Domain and function

**[Later canonical elaboration]** Adam Kadmon governs no bounded domain such as war, weather, healing, death, or fertility. It is the first comprehensive ordering of post-*ẓimẓum* divine light and the template through which lower worlds become possible.

Its principal attributes are:

- **Totality:** the ten *sefirot* held in primordial unity.
- **Mediation:** it makes the unbounded light communicable to subsequent levels.
- **Human form:** not a biological body but the relational organization of powers.
- **Macrocosm:** earthly humanity is a lower analogue of its supernal structure.
- **Androgynous or sexually inclusive totality:** later interpreters describe it as containing masculine and feminine principles before their differentiation. This should not be reduced to a modern claim about literal anatomy or gender identity.
- **Light rather than flesh:** mature Lurianic discourse describes radiances, vessels, line, circles, and emanating apertures.
- **Transcendence of the ordinary four worlds:** Adam Kadmon stands above *Aẓilut*, *Beriʾah*, *Yeẓirah*, and *ʿAsiyyah*.

### Body correspondences

**[Medieval and early-modern learned systematization]** In the familiar anthropomorphic mapping:

- *Keter* — crown/head;
- *Ḥokhmah* and *Binah* — right and left cerebral or facial poles;
- *Ḥesed* and *Gevurah* — right and left arms;
- *Tiferet* — torso or heart;
- *Neẓaḥ* and *Hod* — legs;
- *Yesod* — generative organ or covenantal channel;
- *Malkhut* — feet, mouth, or receptive feminine presence, depending on the diagram and level.

There is no single timeless chart. Zoharic, Cordoverian, Lurianic, Christian-Cabalistic, Golden Dawn, and modern teaching diagrams differ.

### Emanation through the face

**[Later canonical elaboration]** Vital’s system describes light leaving Adam Kadmon through:

- ears: relatively concealed, high lights;
- nose;
- mouth: associated particularly with *ʿAqqudim*, ten lights in one vessel;
- eyes: associated with *Neqqudim*, the unstable world of points;
- forehead: later light connected with rectification and the name *MaH*.

These are ontological metaphors, not reports of a visible monster discharging physical matter. The *ʿEẓ Ḥayyim* explicitly differentiates inner and surrounding lights and compares their relation to a soul clothed within limbs. [*ʿEẓ Ḥayyim* 2:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.2.3.1)

### Iconography

No ancient statue, temple image, coin, inscription, seal, amulet, or manuscript miniature can securely be identified as Adam Kadmon. The figure has no ancient cult image.

Important later visualizations include:

1. **Sefirotic body diagrams.** Geometric arrangements of the ten *sefirot* imply or explicitly overlay a human body. Their purpose is instructional and contemplative rather than iconic worship.
2. **Athanasius Kircher’s Tree of Life, 1652.** Published in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, this became highly influential in Christian and Hermetic Qabalah. Its arrangement is an early-modern Christian synthesis, not an unchanged ancient Jewish diagram. [Kircher engraving](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AKircher_Tree_of_Life.png)
3. **Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata*, 1677–84.** Its engravings map supernal physiognomy and the *sefirot*, including the famous profile head commonly called Adam Kadmon. The Christian editorial frame equated primordial humanity with Christ. [Historical image record](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/book-kabbalah-unveiled-christian-knorr-von-rosenroth/RwF3RA8GjJemjA?hl=en)
4. **Golden Dawn and Theosophical diagrams, late nineteenth–twentieth centuries.** These add colors, tarot paths, elemental assignments, astrological correspondences, and graded initiatory meanings. Such attributes are modern Western occult elaborations.

### Attributes not securely attested

Adam Kadmon has no documented sacred animal, plant, metal, incense, feast day, weekday, planet, temple color, sacrificial victim, weapon, spouse, mount, or priesthood. Modern tables assigning such correspondences derive from Hermetic Qabalah’s larger correspondence systems, not from the medieval figure’s own cult.

## 4. Genealogy and relations to other divine beings

### Origin rather than parentage

Adam Kadmon has no birth narrative and no parents in the mythological sense.

**[Lurianic system]** Its “origin” is the line of infinite light entering the space after *ẓimẓum*. Depending on redaction, it is the first vessel or comprehensive form of emanation, the first world, or the organized totality of lights and vessels. It is not the biological child of *Ein Sof*.

It has no securely attested spouse or offspring. Lower worlds and configurations proceed through it, but genealogical vocabulary can distort what is an emanational relation.

### Distinctions

- **Ein Sof:** Adam Kadmon is not simply identical to the unknowable Infinite; it is the first comprehensible or configured manifestation after concealment.
- **The ten sefirot:** earlier sources can identify the supernal human with their totality; Lurianic sources distinguish the primordial *sefirot* of Adam Kadmon from their later organization in the lower worlds.
- **Adam ha-Rishon:** the biblical first human is a lower, created analogue, not the same entity.
- **Metatron:** no primary source securely identifies Adam Kadmon as Metatron.
- **Messiah:** *Encyclopaedia Judaica* explicitly warns that Lurianic Adam Kadmon has no necessary identity with the Messiah.
- **Satan/Samael:** Adam Kadmon is not Satan. *Tiqqunei Zohar* and later Kabbalah can contrast holy supernal humanity with *adam beliyyaʿal*, “worthless/evil man,” but this is opposition, not identity.
- **Christ:** the identification belongs to Pauline typology and Christian Cabala, not Jewish Kabbalah.
- **Gnostic Adamas/Anthropos and Manichaean Primal Man:** these are analogues or possible comparative ancestors, not alternate names in their own sources.

### Every documented interaction with another named divine being

Adam Kadmon has almost no narrative encounters because it is a cosmological structure rather than a dramatic person. The following list therefore distinguishes actual textual relations from later equations.

- **Ein Sof — emanational relation:** After *ẓimẓum*, the ray of *Ein Sof*’s light forms or fills Adam Kadmon; Adam Kadmon channels the light toward lower worlds. *ʿEẓ Ḥayyim*, redacted from late-sixteenth-century Lurianic teaching. **[Later canonical elaboration]**
- **The sefirot — configuration:** The ten powers are ordered in, represented by, or emanated through Adam Kadmon. Early Kabbalistic and Zoharic precedents; fully systematized by Vital. **[Secure medieval concept / later canonical elaboration]**
- **Adam ha-Rishon — archetype and image:** Earthly Adam is a lower image or reflection of supernal humanity; no face-to-face meeting occurs. Zoharic and later Kabbalistic interpretation of Genesis. **[Later canonical elaboration]**
- **The Shekhinah/Malkhut — structural inclusion:** Malkhut, often figured as Shekhinah, occupies the receptive or lowest position in anthropomorphic arrangements; this is a relation within the divine structure, not a narrative meeting. **[Medieval Kabbalistic systematization]**
- **Arikh Anpin, Abba, Imma, Zeʿir Anpin, and Nuqva — downstream configurations:** These *parẓufim* emerge in the rectified world below Adam Kadmon. Lurianic writings, late sixteenth century onward. **[Later canonical elaboration]**
- **Samael/Satan — no direct interaction:** No primary Adam Kadmon myth records combat, judgment, seduction, or conversation with Samael. Modern diagrams pairing “holy Adam” and a qliphothic anti-Adam are later occult systematizations. **[Absence of evidence]**
- **Metatron — no direct interaction:** Both can occupy extremely high cosmological positions in different Jewish mystical texts, but no authoritative encounter is attested. **[Absence of evidence]**
- **Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel — no direct interaction:** No relevant Kabbalistic narrative scene is securely attested. **[Absence of evidence]**
- **Christ — identification rather than interaction:** Knorr von Rosenroth and other Christian Cabalists interpreted Adam Kadmon as the preexistent Christ or Logos. *Kabbala Denudata*, 1677–84. **[Syncretistic identification]**
- **Gnostic Adamas/Anthropos — modern comparative relation:** They share the pattern of a heavenly human archetype, but the sources do not have them meet. **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]**
- **Manichaean Primal Man — modern comparative relation:** Similar cosmic-human vocabulary, but different narrative and function; no historical text identifies him as the Jewish Adam Kadmon. **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]**
- **Blake’s Albion — literary analogy:** Albion’s fall, fragmentation, and reintegration parallel cosmic-man imagery; Blake does not stage a meeting with a character named Adam Kadmon. **[Romantic reception / debated influence]**
- **Jung’s Self/Anthropos — psychological equation:** Jung reads Adam Kadmon as an image of psychic totality and transformation. **[Modern psychological reinterpretation]**

No independent combat, romance, theft, judgment, or adventure cycle involving Adam Kadmon is documented.

## 5. Major myths and narrative cycles

### The supernal human as archetype

**[Medieval Kabbalistic core]** The world above is organized in human form; the human below images that supernal order. Biblical “image and likeness,” Ezekiel’s enthroned human likeness, rabbinic cosmic Adam, and *Shiʿur Qomah* body symbolism supplied exegetical materials. The Zohar makes human form the unifying image of what is above and below.

This is not a single narrative with a beginning and conclusion. It is a symbolic complex in which body, cosmos, Torah, divine names, and *sefirot* correspond.

### The Lurianic cosmogony

**[Later canonical elaboration]** This is the principal Adam Kadmon “myth”:

The infinite divine light first leaves a space through concealment or contraction. A line of light enters that space. Its first comprehensive organization is Adam Kadmon. The primordial human form holds circular and linear *sefirot* and differentiates radiances through bodily apertures. From the mouth comes the bound world, where ten lights share a vessel. From the eyes comes the world of points, where the powers stand separately. Its lower vessels cannot sustain the intensity and break. Lights return upward; fragments fall; sparks become trapped in lower materiality and shells. A renewed radiance initiates the world of rectification, where the isolated powers are recomposed into relational *parẓufim*. Human commandments and contemplative intention participate in the recovery and reunion of sparks.

Important qualifications:

- Luria left no systematic book of his own.
- Vital, Ibn Ṭabūl, Jonah, Sarug, and later editors differ on sequence and mechanism.
- Some explanations make the breaking an accident of weak, isolated vessels; others make it a necessary means of exposing and purging judgment or evil.
- Literal “withdrawal” was debated. Later Ḥasidic and non-Ḥasidic interpreters often treated *ẓimẓum* nonliterally—as concealment rather than spatial departure.
- Adam Kadmon is not generally shattered as a person. The breaking concerns vessels of a subsequent emanated stage.

### The two Adams

**[Ancient antecedent / medieval elaboration]** Philo’s intelligible and molded humans, Paul’s heavenly and earthly Adams, and Kabbalah’s upper and lower Adam are historically distinct binary schemes. Later writers often merge them into a single perennial doctrine. The similarities are real; direct textual descent at every step is not demonstrable.

### Androgyny and separation

**[Rabbinic antecedent / later elaboration]** Genesis Rabbah’s double-faced or androgynous Adam explains the creation of the sexes. Kabbalistic systems use masculine and feminine polarity within the divine world, and modern authors commonly call Adam Kadmon androgynous. Yet the rabbinic figure is still Adam ha-Rishon; not every androgynous Adam tradition refers to the technical Adam Kadmon.

## 6. Cult, temples, and ritual practice

### No independent cult

There is no evidence of:

- a temple or sanctuary dedicated to Adam Kadmon;
- an altar, statue, cult image, or priesthood;
- animal or human sacrifice;
- temple prostitution;
- pilgrimage;
- an annual festival;
- a civic or royal cult;
- theophoric personal names invoking Adam Kadmon;
- oracle consultation;
- a liturgical hymn addressed to Adam Kadmon as an independent deity.

The absence is structurally expected: Adam Kadmon is a theosophical configuration within Jewish monotheism, not a separately worshipped god.

### Ritual consequences of the doctrine

**[Later canonical elaboration]** Adam Kadmon nevertheless belongs to a system with ritual consequences. In Lurianic Kabbalah, prayer, commandments, Sabbath observance, dietary acts, sexuality within prescribed bounds, repentance, and contemplative *kavvanot* participate in *tiqqun*. Their immediate ritual objects are God, divine names, *sefirot*, and *parẓufim*, not an independent cult of Adam Kadmon.

Some Lurianic prayers and meditations visualize letters, names, lights, bodily structures, and unions across worlds. These are learned mystical rites preserved in prayer manuals such as *Pri ʿEẓ Ḥayyim* and *Shaʿar ha-Kavvanot*. They do not amount to summoning Adam Kadmon.

### Magic and grimoires

Neither the *Lemegeton* nor *Abramelin* provides a historical cult of Adam Kadmon. Later ceremonial magicians use the sefirotic human as a map of the practitioner’s body or aura. The Golden Dawn’s “Qabalistic Cross” and Middle Pillar practices embody a macrocosm–microcosm logic, but their standardized forms are nineteenth- and twentieth-century occult ritual constructions.

### Sacrifice and contested allegations

The dedicated contested-material search found no evidence connecting Adam Kadmon with human sacrifice, sexual cult, sacrificial violence, or temple economies. Comparisons with the Vedic Puruṣa—whose cosmic body is sacrificed in *Ṛgveda* 10.90—are comparative mythology, not evidence that Adam Kadmon was sacrificed.

## 7. Historical evolution

### Before the first century CE: scriptural materials

Genesis supplied divine-image humanity and two creation accounts; Ezekiel supplied divine human likeness. Neither contains Adam Kadmon.

### First century BCE–CE: philosophical and Christian heavenly humanity

Philo produced the first securely attested Jewish theory of intelligible heavenly humanity contrasted with earthly humanity. Paul made Christ the last Adam and heavenly man. These were later available for comparison and Christian appropriation.

### Third–seventh centuries: rabbinic cosmic Adam

Midrash and Talmud developed a giant, luminous, world-encompassing, sometimes androgynous first Adam diminished after sin. This is an important indigenous Jewish antecedent.

### Late antiquity and early Middle Ages: mystical body traditions

Merkavah, Hekhalot, and *Shiʿur Qomah* texts elaborated throne visions, named limbs, and immeasurable divine stature. The direct line to Adam Kadmon is debated, but the anthropomorphic symbolic vocabulary is historically relevant.

### Twelfth–early thirteenth centuries: emergence of Kabbalah

The *Bahir*, Isaac the Blind, and Provençal/Gerona circles articulated *sefirot*, emanation, divine measure, and symbolic body. The exact name Adam Kadmon is first securely found in *Sod Yediʿat ha-Meẓiʾut*, early thirteenth century.

### Late thirteenth–fourteenth centuries: Zoharic supernal man

The Zoharic literature made divine-human form central to its theosophy. “Upper Adam,” “supernal man,” and anthropomorphic *sefirot* became authoritative images. The Zohar’s claim to second-century authorship is traditional; modern historical study places its principal composition in medieval Castile.

### Sixteenth century: Cordovero and Luria

Cordovero rationalized and harmonized earlier Kabbalah. Luria’s circle recast the cosmos around *ẓimẓum*, Adam Kadmon, breaking, and rectification. This is the decisive promotion of Adam Kadmon from a flexible supernal-human image to a named, technically positioned world above the four worlds.

### Seventeenth–eighteenth centuries: transmission and dispute

Vitalian and Sarugian systems spread through manuscripts. Sabbatian theologians adapted Adam Kadmon within radical messianic metaphysics; this did not make Adam Kadmon itself the Messiah. Christian Cabalists translated and Christologized the figure. Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* made the material accessible in Latin and identified the primordial man with Christ and the highest *sefirot* with Trinitarian structures. Vital’s *ʿEẓ Ḥayyim* reached print in 1782 after roughly two centuries of manuscript transmission.

### Eighteenth–nineteenth centuries: Ḥasidism and Western occultism

Ḥasidic teaching interiorized Lurianic cosmology, relating divine worlds to faculties of soul and worship. Western occultists increasingly detached “Qabalah” from halakhic Jewish practice. Éliphas Lévi, the Golden Dawn, and S. L. MacGregor Mathers treated the sefirotic body as a universal occult map.

### Late nineteenth century: Theosophy

Helena Blavatsky’s *Secret Doctrine* and Theosophical glossaries identified Adam Kadmon with a manifested Logos, androgynous humanity, root-race cosmology, and cross-cultural figures such as Brahmā or Puruṣa. These equations are Theosophical syncretism, not medieval Kabbalah. The 1890 *Key to Theosophy* defines Adam Kadmon as archetypal humanity and the manifested “third Logos.” [Theosophical text](https://www.theosociety.org/pasadena/key/key_to_theosophy.pdf)

### Twentieth–twenty-first centuries

Academic study separated medieval attestations from ancient analogues and occult mergers. At the same time, popular Kabbalah, New Age schools, ceremonial magic, Jungian psychology, comics, games, and online esotericism turned Adam Kadmon into an ideal self, divine human potential, cosmic super-being, or fictional combat entity.

Continuous Jewish study of the Lurianic doctrine exists, but there is no demonstrable continuous worship of Adam Kadmon as a god. Contemporary groups claiming a 3,000-year initiatory lineage centered on “becoming Adam Kadmon” present a modern esoteric program, not a documented ancient institution. One such school explicitly defines its mission as enabling practitioners to become Adam Kadmon-like “god-like beings in the physical.” [Modern Mystery School statement](https://modernmysteryschooluk.com/mission-statement/)

## 8. Modern reception and reinvention

### Christian Cabala

**[Syncretistic identification]** Renaissance and early-modern Christians read Kabbalah as encrypted Christian revelation. Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–84) made the most consequential Adam Kadmon–Christ equation. S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s partial English translation, *The Kabbalah Unveiled* (1887), carried this Christianized reading into ceremonial occultism.

The equation rests on formal similarities among primordial humanity, Logos, divine image, and Pauline “second Adam.” It is not accepted as the plain meaning of Jewish Lurianic sources.

### Golden Dawn and Hermetic Qabalah

**[Modern occult systematization]** The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn placed the human body across the Tree of Life and integrated Adam Kadmon into tarot, astrology, elemental magic, ritual grades, and divine-name practice. Its dictionaries define the figure as the heavenly human or body of God, with Keter above the head and Malkhut at the feet. [Golden Dawn dictionary](https://hermeticgoldendawn.org/h-o-g-d-dictionary/)

These systems materially shaped the image now common in occult publishing: a nude or robed human silhouette with colored *sefirot*, twenty-two paths, planetary symbols, and Hebrew letters. Much of that composite is early-modern or Victorian rather than Zoharic.

### Theosophy and comparative “root races”

Blavatsky treated Adam Kadmon as collective primordial humanity and correlated it with multiple Asian traditions. Claims that Kabbalah “really teaches” Theosophical root races, Lemuria, or the Three Logoi are modern adaptations.

### William Blake

**[Romantic literary reception; influence debated]** Blake never produced a Kabbalistic treatise naming Adam Kadmon as a principal character. The relevant figure is **Albion**, the giant universal human in *Vala, or The Four Zoas* and *Jerusalem: The Emanation of the Giant Albion* (etched from 1804 and developed through about 1820). Albion embodies humanity, Britain, and a cosmic psyche; he falls, sleeps, divides into Zoas and emanations, and is finally reintegrated.

The comparison with Adam Kadmon is strong at the structural level: universal human, macrocosm, bodily totality, division, and restoration. Direct dependence is less secure. Blake had other sources, especially Swedenborg’s *Maximus Homo* or “Grand Man,” Jacob Böhme, biblical prophecy, and Christian mystical anthropology. Scholarship therefore ranges from claims of direct Kabbalistic derivation to more cautious accounts of a shared early-modern esoteric environment. Blake’s text itself should remain primary. [Blake’s *Jerusalem*](https://blake.lib.asu.edu/html/jerusalem.html); [study of Blake and Kabbalah](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/theses/2632/); [study emphasizing Swedenborg](https://bq.blakearchive.org/13.2.paley)

### Carl Gustav Jung

**[Modern psychological reinterpretation]** Jung’s fullest discussion occurs in *Mysterium Coniunctionis* (German volumes, 1955–56; *Collected Works* 14). He treats Adam Kadmon as:

- the Anthropos or “inner man”;
- an archetype of wholeness;
- a symbol of the Self;
- a totality transcending sexual division;
- not merely a completed totality but a process of transformation and differentiation;
- an analogue to alchemical conjunction and the restored “old Adam.”

Jung’s account uses Kabbalah comparatively alongside Gnosticism, Christianity, and alchemy. It is not historical exegesis in the narrow sense; it translates religious symbols into analytical psychology. The International Association for Analytical Psychology summarizes Jung’s quaternity of dark/light Shulamite and old Adam/Adam Kadmon as both alchemical and psychic structures. [IAAP summary](https://iaap.org/resources/academic-resources/collected-works-abstracts/volume-14-mysterium-coniunctionis/)

### New Age and modern esoteric schools

**[Modern invention or adaptation]** Modern teachers variously call Adam Kadmon:

- humanity’s “DNA blueprint”;
- a light-body attainable through ascension;
- an extraterrestrial template;
- an ideal future human;
- a chakra map;
- a personal guardian or higher self;
- a being to invoke.

No medieval source supports DNA, extraterrestrial, or chakra claims. “Becoming Adam Kadmon” can be a modern ethical or mystical metaphor, but claims of an unchanged ancient initiation should be treated as institutional self-presentation.

### Literature, comics, games, and popular media

- **Marvel Comics:** Eternity, the cosmic personification of the universe, has used “Adam Qadmon” as an alias. This adapts macrocosmic humanity into a superhero cosmology. [Marvel character record](https://www.marvel.com/characters/eternity?mobile-app=true&theme=false)
- **Persona 5 Royal** (Atlus/Sega, 2019/2020): Adam Kadmon is Takuto Maruki’s colossal ultimate Persona and visualizes an idealized, reality-ordering human as a golden giant. This is a modern fictional character, not preservation of a Kabbalistic narrative. The game itself is the primary evidence; the developer dates and identifies the title. [Atlus release information](https://atlus.com/persona-5-royal-launch-faq/)
- Numerous comics, games, anime discussions, and occult novels use “Adam Kadmon” for a perfect human, final evolutionary form, cosmic robot, or creator-being. These belong to modern reception even when decorated with Hebrew names.

### Political and extremist appropriation

No major, well-documented nationalist or extremist movement centers on Adam Kadmon. Racialized Theosophical uses of primordial humanity and “root races” provide a potential route into racial mythmaking, but it would be misleading to assign all later Theosophical racial theories to the Kabbalistic figure itself. Claims that Adam Kadmon proves the racial divinity of a particular modern people are modern ideological appropriations.

## 9. Historiography and scholarly debate

### The principal historical problem

The central historiographical error is to combine every “cosmic man” into one transhistorical being. A disciplined history instead distinguishes:

1. biblical divine-image humanity;
2. Philo’s intelligible man;
3. Pauline Adam–Christ typology;
4. rabbinic cosmic Adam;
5. Gnostic Anthropos/Adamas;
6. Manichaean Primal Man;
7. medieval Kabbalistic supernal humanity;
8. Lurianic Adam Kadmon;
9. Christian-Cabalistic Christ;
10. occult, literary, and psychological archetypes.

Formal resemblance is not proof of direct borrowing.

### Gnostic Anthropos and Adamas

**[Ancient parallel]** Several second- and third-century Gnostic systems give “Man,” “Adamas,” or “the perfect Human” a place in the divine fullness.

- The *Apocryphon of John* names Adamas as the perfect, true, holy Human who appears with Autogenes. [Primary translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/apocjn-short.html)
- *Zostrianos* refers to Geradamas, the “first perfect man,” and Seth. [Primary translation](https://gnosis.org/naghamm/zostr.html)
- The *Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit*, commonly called the *Gospel of the Egyptians*, presents incorruptible Adamas in Sethian cosmology.
- Heresiologists including Irenaeus, Hippolytus, and Epiphanius report systems in which Anthropos is an aeon or preexistent human.

These sources demonstrate that divine primordial-human speculation existed long before medieval Kabbalah. They do not demonstrate that a single Gnostic sect transmitted the exact Adam Kadmon doctrine intact into thirteenth-century Spain. Nag Hammadi codices are fourth-century Coptic manuscripts of earlier Greek compositions, discovered in 1945; editions by James M. Robinson, Marvin Meyer, Bentley Layton, and the Coptic Gnostic Library permit comparison. Pearson and Kurt Rudolph remain important for historical contextualization.

### Manichaean Primal Man

**[Ancient parallel, not identity]** In Manichaean cosmology the Father of Greatness summons the **Primal Man**—Middle Persian *Ohrmizd Bay*, Syriac *Nāšā Qadmāyā*—with five luminous sons or elements to confront the powers of Darkness. He is defeated or entrapped; his light becomes mixed with matter, and later divine operations rescue it.

This figure differs sharply from Adam Kadmon:

- he is a warrior sent into combat;
- he suffers defeat or self-offering;
- his light is captured by Darkness;
- he belongs to a dualistic salvation myth;
- he is distinct from the terrestrial Adam.

Lurianic breaking and trapped sparks invite comparison, but the narrative agents, theology, and chronology differ. Direct Manichaean-to-Kabbalistic transmission remains a hypothesis requiring intermediaries, not an established fact. [Encyclopaedia Iranica survey](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-1-general-survey/)

### Mandaean and Islamic comparisons

Mandaean literature has a “first man” or **Gabra Qadmaia**, but manuscript dating and the history of redaction complicate any claim of priority or transmission.

The Qurʾan and hadith richly develop Adam, his creation, angelic prostration, Iblis’s refusal, and prophetic status; neither names Adam Kadmon nor sets Adam above the four Kabbalistic worlds. Al-Ṭabarī and later tafsīr belong to the history of Islamic Adam traditions, not direct primary evidence for Adam Kadmon.

Islamic mysticism’s **al-insān al-kāmil**, “the Perfect Human,” especially in Ibn ʿArabī and ʿAbd al-Karīm al-Jīlī, is another significant comparison. Its bearer manifests divine names and completes cosmic self-disclosure. Similarities to the macrocosmic human encouraged later comparisons, but “perfect human” is not a translation of *Adam Qadmon*, and direct derivation is disputed.

### Was the doctrine derived from Gnosticism?

- **Argument for influence:** ancient Gnostic and Manichaean systems already contain a preexistent divine Human, lower humanity made in its image, cosmic fragmentation, and salvific restoration. Late antique Jewish mystical anthropomorphism arose in a shared environment.
- **Argument for internal Jewish development:** Genesis, Ezekiel, rabbinic cosmic Adam, *Shiʿur Qomah*, *Sefer Yeẓirah*, the *Bahir*, and sefirotic symbolism provide an adequate Jewish chain of conceptual resources.
- **Best-supported conclusion:** Adam Kadmon is a specifically medieval Kabbalistic construction assembled from Jewish scriptural and mystical traditions within a broader Mediterranean and Near Eastern history of cosmic-human speculation. Ancient parallels illuminate the construction; they do not erase its medieval specificity.

### Dating and transmission of the Zohar

Traditional attribution places the Zohar with Simeon bar Yoḥai in the second century. Modern scholarship locates the main corpus in late-thirteenth-century Castile, associated with Moses de León and related circles, while recognizing multiple strata and authors. The corpus’s first print standardization at Mantua and Cremona in 1558–60 affected later reception. Its medieval dating means Zoharic upper-man passages cannot be used as independent second-century evidence for Adam Kadmon.

### Lurianic textual instability

The “teachings of Luria” survive chiefly through disciples. Vital’s works exist in multiple recensions; later editors rearranged notebooks and gates. *Encyclopaedia Judaica* notes contradictions among Ibn Ṭabūl, Moses Jonah, Vital, and Sarug, and even within Vital’s works. [Lurianic Kabbalah overview](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0011_0_10514.html)

Consequently, diagrams presenting one neat, universally canonical sequence are pedagogical simplifications.

### Discredited or unsupported claims still circulating

- Adam Kadmon is named in Genesis. **False.**
- Adam Kadmon is an archangel. **Unsupported.**
- Adam Kadmon is Metatron. **Unsupported as a general identity.**
- Adam Kadmon is the Messiah in Lurianic Kabbalah. **False as a general claim.**
- Isaac the Blind securely coined the term. **Not established.**
- The Zohar supplies the complete Lurianic five-world scheme. **Anachronistic.**
- Adam Kadmon was worshipped in an ancient temple. **No evidence.**
- Adam Kadmon received human sacrifice. **No evidence.**
- Adam Kadmon and the Gnostic Demiurge are the same being. **False; their cosmological functions generally oppose one another.**
- Adam Kadmon is exactly Puruṣa, Gayōmart, the Norse Ymir, Chinese Pangu, Egyptian Atum, or Islamic Perfect Man. **Comparative analogy, not historical identity.**
- The familiar colored Tree of Life is an unchanged ancient Hebrew diagram. **False; its standard occult form owes much to early-modern Christian Cabala and later Hermetic revision.**
- Adam Kadmon is a literal giant robot or superhuman capable of combat. **Modern fiction.**

### State of the evidence

The crucial evidence is manuscript and textual rather than archaeological. Relevant holdings are dispersed among Hebrew manuscript collections in Jerusalem, Oxford, London, Paris, the Vatican, New York, and elsewhere. The documentary chain is uneven:

- ancient antecedents survive in biblical, rabbinic, patristic, Coptic, Syriac, and Manichaean manuscripts;
- early Kabbalistic works often survive anonymously or in late copies;
- Zoharic material survives in numerous variant manuscripts and early printed editions;
- Lurianic teaching survives through competing disciple-redactions;
- early-modern printed diagrams transformed textual metaphors into standardized images.

Gershom Scholem established the dominant historical framework separating Kabbalah’s medieval development from traditional pseudepigraphic dating. Isaiah Tishby, Moshe Idel, Elliot Wolfson, Yehuda Liebes, Joseph Dan, Daniel Abrams, Jonathan Garb, and others have revised particular claims about authorship, embodiment, erotic symbolism, textual plurality, and historical context.

### Source-critical conclusion

The fullest defensible reconstruction is not the history of one ancient god moving unchanged between religions. It is the history of a **medieval Jewish theological name attached to a much older family of cosmic-human images**, then radically repositioned by Lurianic Kabbalah and repeatedly translated into Christian, occult, literary, psychological, and commercial vocabularies. Its earliest secure evidence is textual, not archaeological; its mature “myth” is cosmological, not biographical; and the absence of cult, sacrifice, priesthood, and narrative encounters is itself a central documentary finding.

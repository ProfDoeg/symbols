# Sandalphon: Research Dossier

**Class:** Angel (*malʾakh*), later archangel and throne/chariot angel in Jewish mystical literature; angelic hypostasis of Elijah in one late Kabbalistic identification; archangel of Malkuth in Hermetic Qabalah. Sandalphon is not a god, demon, fallen angel, or Gnostic aeon in the premodern sources examined.

**Earliest secure attestation:** Babylonian Talmud, *Ḥagigah* 13b, in a Hebrew *baraita* embedded in an Aramaic discussion. The Babylonian Talmud was probably redacted around the sixth century CE, though recent scholarship extends possible final redaction into the seventh or eighth century. The passage may preserve earlier tannaitic material, but its formula “it was taught in a *baraita*” does not independently prove a second-century date. The earliest securely datable document is therefore the late-antique Talmudic compilation, not the reputed age of the tradition within it. [The textual tradition of the Babylonian Talmud](https://uplopen.com/en/chapters/e/10.1515/9783111054360-010); [recent debate over final redaction](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009297349.004).

---

## Evidentiary labels

- **Securely attested early material:** explicit in the earliest surviving or securely datable source.
- **Later canonical elaboration:** authoritative within a later religious corpus, without implying historical antiquity.
- **Regional variant:** associated with a particular community, rite, manuscript family, or locality.
- **Learned systematization:** placement created by theologians, Kabbalists, or occult taxonomists.
- **Syncretistic identification:** explicit equation of previously distinguishable figures.
- **Medieval or early-modern accretion:** first securely visible in those periods.
- **Folklore:** customary or popular material whose textual pedigree may be incomplete.
- **Modern scholarly reconstruction:** an inference, not an ancient statement.
- **Modern invention:** a recent attribution unsupported by the older record.

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### Identity

Sandalphon enters the record as an immensely tall angel stationed behind the divine chariot. In *Ḥagigah* 13b he is named while Ezekiel 1:15—“one wheel [*ʾofan*] upon the earth beside the living creatures”—is being interpreted. He exceeds another angel by a “journey of five hundred years” and binds or weaves crowns for his Maker. The text does not call him an archangel, give him wings, make him Elijah, identify him as Metatron’s twin, assign him to Malkuth, or explicitly say that the crowns consist of human prayers. Those are later layers. [*Ḥagigah* 13b](https://www.sefaria.org/Chagigah.13b?with=WILLIAM+DAVIDSON+EDITION+-+ARAMAIC).

### Names and spellings

- **Hebrew:** סנדלפון, normally vocalized *Sandalfon* or *Sandalphon*. Manuscripts and modern publications vary between *Sandalfon*, *Sandalphon*, and occasionally *Sandelfon*.
- **Greek reconstruction:** συνάδελφος, *synadelphos*, “fellow-brother,” “colleague,” or “co-brother.” This is an etymological proposal, not an attested Greek name-form in an ancient Sandalphon text.
- **Arabic:** سندلفون, *Sandalfūn*, appears primarily when Muslim authors report Jewish angelology, rather than in the Qur’an or sound canonical hadith.
- **Japanese:** サンダルフォン, *Sandarufon*, in modern games, manga, and animation.

Wilhelm Bacher and Ludwig Blau defended the Greek derivation from *syn-* plus *adelphos* against Alexander Kohut’s proposed Persian origin. The Greek proposal is philologically plausible, but the attractive interpretation “brother of Metatron” may be partly retrospective: the early Talmudic passage does not pair the two angels. [Bacher and Blau, “Sandalfon”](https://jewishencyclopediadev.sefaria.org/articles/13172-sandalfon).

A derivation from Hebrew/Yiddish *sandek*, the person honored at a circumcision, sometimes circulates because Elijah is associated with circumcision and Sandalphon was later identified with Elijah. No early text derives the name this way. It is best labeled **late folk etymology or modern speculation**.

### Titles and classifications

- **“Angel” (*malʾakh*):** explicit in *Ḥagigah* 13b—**securely attested early**.
- **“Wheel” (*ʾofan*):** the Talmud places Sandalphon within an exegesis of Ezekiel’s wheel, but the syntax does not securely establish *Ophan* as a second personal name. Treating “Ophan” as an unequivocal alias is a **later exegetical inference**.
- **Throne/chariot angel:** an accurate functional description of the Talmudic and midrashic figure—**securely attested early**.
- **Crown-binder:** explicit in *Ḥagigah* 13b and expanded in *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20—**securely attested early/later elaboration**.
- **Angel or prince of prayer:** explicit in medieval liturgical and mystical reception—**medieval elaboration**.
- **“Angel of Glory”:** especially prominent through Henry Wadsworth Longfellow—**modern literary title**.
- **Archangel:** common in Christian occultism and modern popular angelology, but not the Talmudic classification—**late learned systematization**.
- **Master of heavenly song/heavenly cantor:** dependent upon later liturgical material; it is not part of the earliest passage.
- **Archangel of Malkuth:** Golden Dawn and successor Hermetic-Qabalistic classification—**nineteenth-century occult systematization**.
- **“Dark angel” opposite a “bright” Metatron:** not established in the rabbinic or medieval sources located; popularized through modern occult summaries and Japanese media—**modern invention or literary retelling**.
- **Demon:** a game-taxonomy label in *Shin Megami Tensei*, whose “demons” include gods and angels; it is not a historical Jewish classification—**modern commercial taxonomy**.

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### The earliest passage

The received text of *Ḥagigah* 13b reads, in substance:

> A *baraita* teaches that the angel is named Sandalphon; he is taller than his companion by a journey of five hundred years, stands behind the Chariot, and binds crowns for his Maker.

A difficulty immediately follows: how can he put a crown on God if angels do not know God’s “place”? The answer is that Sandalphon causes the crown to utter or be adjured with the divine name, whereupon it rises and rests upon God’s head. This is exegetical throne imagery, not a narrative of an earthly cult. The “five hundred years” is a conventional cosmological measure, not recoverable physical height.

The earliest material therefore establishes:

1. Sandalphon is an angel.
2. He is extraordinarily tall relative to another angel.
3. He stands behind the Chariot.
4. He binds crowns for God.
5. He cannot simply locate or approach God as though God occupied an ordinary place.

It does **not** establish the later prayer-garland, Elijah, twin, Malkuth, music, turquoise, Earth-healing, embryo, or guardian-angel traditions.

### *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20

*Pesiqta Rabbati*, commonly dated in its principal form to sixth- or seventh-century Palestine but textually composite, expands the heavenly journey of Moses. Moses passes Kamuel and is conducted by Hadarniel until they reach Sandalphon’s fire. Hadarniel refuses to proceed lest Sandalphon burn him. Moses is terrified; God descends from the throne and shields or guides him past Sandalphon.

The homily repeats Sandalphon’s five-hundred-year superiority and his work behind the Chariot. It expands the crown’s ascent: the heavenly hosts tremble; the living creatures, seraphim, wheels, Chariot, and throne acclaim God. The crown is thus associated with angelic praise. It is still not explicitly a wreath made from human petitions. [Hebrew text of *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20](https://www.sefaria.org/Pesikta_Rabbati.20.1); [dating discussion](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/pesikta-rabbati).

### Scriptural and Second Temple corpora: negative findings

Sandalphon is **absent by name** from:

- the Hebrew Bible;
- the Septuagint;
- 1 Enoch;
- Jubilees;
- Tobit;
- the securely identified Dead Sea Scrolls;
- the New Testament;
- the principal New Testament apocrypha;
- the Qur’an;
- canonical hadith;
- the Nag Hammadi codices;
- Irenaeus, Hippolytus, and Epiphanius in their catalogues of Gnostic systems.

Accordingly, Sandalphon is not securely a biblical, Second Temple, early Christian, Qur’anic, or Gnostic figure. Ezekiel’s wheels, Enoch’s transformation, Elijah’s ascent, apocalyptic angels, and Gnostic aeons provide later interpreters with comparative material, but none may be relabeled “Sandalphon” without a later source doing so.

No Sandalphon appears in Pseudo-Dionysius’ *Celestial Hierarchy*, whose nine angelic orders profoundly shaped Christian angelology. Thomas Aquinas discusses angels using Scripture, patristic sources, and Pseudo-Dionysius, but does not make Sandalphon part of Catholic dogmatic angelology. Dante and Milton likewise do not name him. Claims that Sandalphon is an established early Christian archangel therefore exceed the evidence.

### Hekhalot and Merkabah literature

Sandalphon belongs conceptually to the chariot-world developed in Hekhalot literature, but precision is essential. “Hekhalot literature” is a modern umbrella for unstable, overlapping textual units—including *Hekhalot Rabbati*, *Hekhalot Zutarti*, *Maʿaseh Merkavah*, *Shiʿur Qomah*, and *Sefer Hekhalot/3 Enoch*—transmitted mainly in medieval manuscripts. Dating individual units between late antiquity and the early Middle Ages remains disputed. [British Academy overview](https://academic.oup.com/british-academy-scholarship-online/book/20140/chapter-abstract/179148960); [Raʿanan Boustan, overview of dating problems](https://rboustan.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf4156/files/rboustan/files/introduction_hekhalot_literature_at_the.pdf).

The strongest early Sandalphon texts remain *Ḥagigah* and *Pesiqta Rabbati*. General statements that he is a central character throughout “the Hekhalot books” should not substitute for manuscript and paragraph citations. In contrast, Metatron is explicitly central to *3 Enoch*. The supposed primeval pair should not be projected backward from later Kabbalah.

### Medieval liturgy

Tosafot on *Ḥagigah* 13b glosses the crowns as made from “the prayers of the righteous” (*mi-tefillatan shel ṣaddiqim*). This is important evidence that the human-prayer interpretation had attached to the Talmudic crown motif in medieval Ashkenaz. [Hebrew *Ḥagigah* page with Tosafot](https://il.talmud-bavli.com/talmud-seder/seder-moed/hagigah/13b).

Sukkot liturgy and later piyyut traditions describe Sandalphon as gathering Israel’s prayers, forming or binding a crown, and adjuring it to ascend before the “King of kings.” Solomon ibn Gabirol is associated in bibliographical tradition with Sandalphon as angel of prayer, but individual poem attribution should be checked against a critical piyyut edition rather than inferred from later anthologies. The documentary shift is nevertheless clear: **Talmudic crown-binder → medieval interpreter of human prayer into the divine crown**.

### Zohar and Kabbalah

The Zoharic corpus emerged in late-thirteenth-century Castile and continued to grow in the early fourteenth century. Traditional attribution to second-century Simeon bar Yoḥai is a religious attribution; historical-critical scholarship places its principal composition around Moses de León and a Castilian circle, with continuing debate over single versus multiple authorship. [Zohar composition debate](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/the-archaeology-of-the-zohar-sifra-ditseni%CA%BDuta-as-a-sample-text/); [Moses de León and the Zohar’s formation](https://academic.oup.com/book/60780/chapter-abstract/528593579).

Zoharic references cited in the older scholarship include II 58a, II 246a, and III 252b. Sandalphon functions in the lower heavenly economy and in the mediation or elevation of prayer. Claims that the Zohar itself straightforwardly calls him “archangel of Malkuth” should be treated carefully: later tabular correspondence systems often turn looser Kabbalistic associations into fixed offices.

### *Sefer Raziel ha-Malʾakh*

This composite Hebrew magical book cannot be treated as a revelation literally written by Raziel for Adam. Much of its recoverable compilation belongs to thirteenth-century Ashkenaz, particularly material associated with Eleazar of Worms and Nehemiah ben Solomon; it incorporates older sources such as *Sefer ha-Razim*. Sandalphon is assigned authority over the month Adar in printed *Sefer Raziel* traditions. This is a **medieval magical correspondence**, not an early Jewish calendar doctrine. [Current research on *Sefer Raziel*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2017/html); [contents and manuscripts](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16509.html).

### Grimoires

Sandalphon does not appear as one of the seventy-two Goetic spirits in the *Lemegeton*, nor is he a fallen spirit in the *Ars Goetia*. Assertions that the *Lemegeton* makes him a demon are false.

The standard *Book of Abramelin* spirit catalogues likewise do not make Sandalphon a major summoned demon. Later ceremonialists may invoke him through Qabalistic tables, but that reflects post-Golden Dawn practice.

The “left-hand feminine cherub of the Ark” is regularly attributed online to the “Greater Key of Solomon.” The precise claim belongs more securely to modern Golden Dawn/Mathers-associated ceremonial literature than to a stable early manuscript recension of the *Key*. It should be labeled **nineteenth-century occult adaptation unless a specific manuscript reading is produced**.

### Attested domains

| Binds crowns for God | Same sources | Securely attested early |

### Form

The earliest source supplies scale and position but almost no anatomy. Sandalphon is taller than his fellow by five hundred years’ journey. *Pesiqta Rabbati* adds consuming fire. Neither provides a stable face, costume, skin color, number of wings, sex, weapon, musical instrument, or animal.

The feminine pronouns sometimes used in modern occultism arise from Malkuth’s feminine symbolism or from the Golden Dawn description “Prince of Prayer (feminine),” not from a rabbinic narrative saying Sandalphon is biologically or consistently female. Jewish grammatical and literary convention generally treats the angel as masculine.

### Iconography

There is no securely identified ancient or medieval cult statue, temple image, mosaic, manuscript portrait, or inscribed figure of Sandalphon comparable to Michael or Gabriel. The absence follows from his textual, esoteric character and from Jewish reservations about anthropomorphic sacred images.

Recognizable modern Sandalphon iconography is therefore derivative:

- a colossal winged humanoid;
- feet on Earth and head among the heavens;
- crowns, garlands, flowers, or written prayers;
- an instrument or musical aura;
- earth tones, black, olive, russet, and citrine from Malkuth;
- turquoise or pink in New Age art;
- a robotic or armored angel in Japanese games.

Only the colossal verticality and crown-making securely descend from the early texts.

## 4. Genealogy and Relations to Other Divine Beings

### Origin and family

The early sources give Sandalphon no parents, birth, consort, offspring, siblings, or earthly biography. He is simply an angel already functioning behind the Chariot.

#### Metatron as twin or brother

The “twin brother of Metatron” is not in *Ḥagigah* 13b, *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20, biblical Enoch literature, or *3 Enoch’s* main Enoch-Metatron narrative. It occurs in later Kabbalistic arrangements. Naphtali Bacharach’s *ʿEmeq ha-Melekh* (1648) and material printed by Adolf Jellinek treat Metatron and Sandalphon as the two “lads” of Genesis 48:16. It is therefore an **early-modern Kabbalistic systematization**, not a Talmudic genealogy. The proposed Greek “co-brother” etymology may have encouraged the pairing, while the pairing in turn made the etymology seem inevitable.

Calling them twins creates a symmetry:

- Enoch ascends and becomes Metatron;
- Elijah ascends and becomes Sandalphon;
- Metatron occupies the upper or Ketherward pole;
- Sandalphon occupies the lower or Malkuthward pole.

That elegant scheme is late. Its symmetry is evidence of system-building, not proof of an ancient common origin.

#### Elijah identification

The Hebrew Bible states that Elijah ascended in a whirlwind with a chariot and horses of fire (2 Kings 2:11); it does not say he became Sandalphon. *Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer* develops Elijah’s continuing heavenly role and also identifies Elijah with Phinehas, but does not by itself securely establish Elijah = Sandalphon.

Moses Cordovero’s sixteenth-century Kabbalistic writings explicitly identify Sandalphon with Elijah; related material is cited from *Maḥzor Vitry* and *Yalqut Ḥadash*. Louis Ginzberg accordingly cautioned that “in the old sources Elijah has nothing to do with Sandalphon.” The equation is a **medieval-to-early-modern syncretistic identification**, not an early tradition. [Bacher and Blau’s source references](https://jewishencyclopediadev.sefaria.org/articles/13172-sandalfon); [Ginzberg’s discussion](https://www.thechristianprince.com/EBOOKS/LegendoftheJews.pdf).

### Every documented interaction with another named divine being

The list distinguishes actual episodes from mere tabular juxtaposition.

- **God / the Holy One:** Sandalphon binds crowns for his Maker; because angels do not know God’s place, he adjures or causes the crown to rise to God. *Babylonian Talmud, Ḥagigah* 13b, redacted ca. sixth century or later. **Securely attested early.**
- **God / the Holy One:** God leaves or descends from the throne and goes before Moses at Sandalphon’s fiery station. *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20, principal compilation conventionally sixth–seventh century but composite. **Later canonical elaboration.**
- **Moses:** Moses sees Sandalphon, becomes terrified, weeps, and nearly falls from the cloud; God protects him. *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20. **Later canonical elaboration.**
- **Hadarniel:** Hadarniel escorts Moses only as far as Sandalphon’s fire and refuses to continue because Sandalphon would burn him. *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20. **Later canonical elaboration.**
- **The ḥayyot, seraphim, ophanim, wheels, Chariot, and Throne:** These collective heavenly beings and structures tremble and acclaim God as Sandalphon’s crown ascends. *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20. **Later canonical elaboration.** They are collective classes, not individually named persons.
- **Metatron:** Sandalphon and Metatron are construed as brothers and as the two “lads” of Genesis 48:16 in later Kabbalah, especially *ʿEmeq ha-Melekh*. No early narrative meeting is supplied. **Early-modern learned systematization.**
- **Elijah:** Sandalphon is identified with, rather than merely meeting, Elijah in Cordoverian and related traditions. **Syncretistic identification; sixteenth century and later.**
- **Enoch:** No direct interaction is attested. Any relationship runs through the late parallel Elijah–Sandalphon / Enoch–Metatron construction. **Absence of evidence.**
- **Michael:** The claim that Sandalphon, “like Michael,” continuously fights Samael is common in modern dictionaries, but no adequately identified primary passage was verified here. **Unestablished.**
- **Samael:** Same claim; it must not be promoted into an ancient episode without a text and locus. **Unestablished/possibly modern conflation.**
- **Shekhinah:** Later Kabbalistic systems position Sandalphon in the lower divine or angelic economy associated with Malkuth/Shekhinah, but a discrete narrative encounter was not established. **Learned association, not an attested episode.**
- **Raziel:** They coexist in later angelological and magical corpora, but no narrative meeting was found. **Absence of evidence.**
- **Lucifer, Satan, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel:** No premodern Sandalphon episode with these beings was securely located. Modern fiction supplies many, but those belong to reception history.

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### The towering crown-binder

**Early attested form:** Ezekiel’s earthly wheel prompts the Talmudic notice of Sandalphon. He stands behind the Chariot, is cosmically tall, and binds crowns for God. The theological problem—God cannot be spatially found by angels—is resolved by making the crown ascend through the divine name.

This is not a biography but an angelological *midrash*: Ezekiel’s obscure wheel becomes a named heavenly functionary; cosmic distance expresses rank; crown-making expresses heavenly praise while retaining divine transcendence.

### Moses and Sandalphon’s fire

**Later canonical elaboration:** In *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20 Moses’ ascent is staged as passage through increasingly dangerous angelic thresholds. Kamuel challenges him; Hadarniel terrifies and then escorts him; Sandalphon’s fire marks a boundary Hadarniel cannot cross. God’s intervention demonstrates Israel’s favored access to Torah: Moses proceeds where even great angels cannot.

The narrative is sometimes retold as “Moses saw Sandalphon in the third heaven.” The source passage itself is more concerned with the ascent sequence and Sandalphon’s fiery station than with a standardized numbered heaven. Assignments to the third, sixth, or seventh heaven in later summaries should therefore remain separate variants.

### Prayers become crowns or flowers

**Medieval elaboration:** Tosafot and liturgical sources identify the crown-material with the prayers of the righteous or of Israel. Sandalphon becomes an angelic liturgist who gathers, binds, and elevates prayer.

**Longfellow’s transformation:** In “Sandalphon,” completed in January 1858 and published in *Birds of Passage*, prayers turn into purple and red flowers and garlands in the angel’s hands. Longfellow calls him both “Angel of Glory” and “Angel of Prayer,” places him at the celestial gates, and associates him with Jacob’s ladder and angels that sing once and expire. These motifs combine rabbinic lore with Romantic Christian imagery of crosses, broken hearts, stars, and longing. The floral metamorphosis has become so influential that it is often misreported as the wording of the Talmud. [Longfellow text and publication information](https://www.hwlongfellow.org/poems_poem.php?pid=142); [journal entry and study of his Jewish themes](https://ajr.edu/wp-content/uploads/Rosenbach-Simon-Jewish-Themes-in-Longfellow-2014-1.pdf).

### Elijah becomes Sandalphon

**Late identification:** Elijah’s biblical ascent provided an available human-to-angel trajectory. Once Enoch’s transformation into Metatron was established in the Enoch-Metatron tradition, Kabbalists could construe Elijah as Sandalphon and the two angels as a complementary pair. The fully symmetrical “twin transformed prophets” story is a synthesis of independent traditions rather than a narrative found intact in an early source.

### Sandalphon of Malkuth

**Modern occult systematization:** The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn mapped divine names, archangels, angelic choirs, colors, planets, elements, and Tarot onto the ten sefirot. Sandalphon became the archangelic power of Malkuth, the Kingdom and manifested world. Golden Dawn materials describe Sandalphon, Metatron, and *Nephesch ha-Messiah* as aspects of Malkuth in Briah; later tables simplify this to “Sandalphon: archangel of Malkuth.” [Golden Dawn manuscript material](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/golden_dawn_manuscripts.html); [Regardie’s collected system](https://scriptaetveritas.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/ISRAEL_REGARDIE_COMPLETE_GOLDEN_DAWN_SYSTEM_OF_MAGIC.pdf).

The resulting modern myth makes Sandalphon the grounding, earthward complement of Metatron. Earth healing, bodily incarnation, environmentalism, manifestation, money, and property are subsequent deductions from Malkuth rather than rabbinic functions.

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

There is no evidence for a temple, priesthood, sacrificial cult, festival founded specifically for Sandalphon, oracle, pilgrimage center, theophoric personal-name tradition, temple treasury, sacred animal, or public statue. No securely identified inscription or archaeological sanctuary attests worship comparable to that of an ancient god.

Consequently:

- **Human sacrifice:** no evidence.
- **Animal sacrifice to Sandalphon:** no evidence.
- **Temple prostitution:** no evidence and not relevant to the attested tradition.
- **Cult violence:** no evidence.
- **Suppression of a Sandalphon cult:** no evidence.
- **Forced conversion away from such a cult:** no evidence.
- **Modern extremist appropriation:** no documented significant movement located.

The evidence concerns prayer, liturgy, exegesis, mystical speculation, amulets, and ceremonial magic—not a public cult.

### Liturgical practice

The medieval Sukkot/Shemini Atzeret prayer tradition makes Sandalphon a heavenly collector and crown-maker. This does not necessarily mean worshippers prayed *to* Sandalphon; often the angel is described in prayer addressed to God. The distinction between narrating an angel’s function and invoking the angel directly is essential.

### Amulets and protective magic

Older scholarship reports Sandalphon’s name:

- on an amulet against miscarriage;
- in a charm for forest protection;
- in material governing Adar or shofar blasts.

These are evidence of practical Jewish angelology, but the surviving object, exact inscription, provenance, date, and collection must be checked individually before treating them as securely archaeological.

Late-antique Jewish incantation bowls from Iraq and western Iran demonstrate the broader material environment in which angels and powerful names were invoked. They date approximately from the fourth through seventh centuries. No bowl naming Sandalphon was securely identified in this research; his inclusion in general discussions of bowl magic must therefore not be assumed. [Dan Levene’s corpus description](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/12270); [recent contextual scholarship](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.32831083).

### Modern ritual

Golden Dawn-derived practitioners invoke or visualize Sandalphon in Malkuth, sometimes using citrine, olive, russet, and black; Earth pentagrams; the divine name *Adonai ha-Aretz*; and the angelic choir *Ishim*. This is historically real modern ritual practice, but it is neither Talmudic worship nor an unchanged ancient Kabbalah.

New Age practitioners address Sandalphon for grounding, music, answered prayer, pregnancy, environmental healing, or material prosperity. The movement is diffuse, commercial, and difficult to quantify; no reliable global adherent count exists.

## 7. Historical Evolution

### Before ca. 500 CE: no named Sandalphon

Biblical throne visions, Ezekiel’s wheels, Enochic angels, Elijah’s ascent, and apocalyptic heavenly journeys supply motifs later used around Sandalphon. None names him. A derivation from Gnosticism, Typhon, or an Islamic angel is not demonstrated by primary evidence.

### Sixth–seventh centuries: emergence in rabbinic throne lore

The Babylonian Talmud records the colossal crown-binder behind the Chariot. Palestinian *Pesiqta Rabbati* supplies the fullest early narrative: Sandalphon’s fire halts Hadarniel during Moses’ ascent. This is the secure foundational stratum.

### Early Middle Ages: liturgical angel of prayer

Interpretation shifts from crowns as angelic praise to crowns made from human prayers. Piyyut and commentary connect Sandalphon with the ascent of Israel’s worship. The figure becomes accessible outside specialist chariot exegesis, though still without an independent cult.

### Thirteenth–fourteenth centuries: Kabbalistic integration

The Zoharic corpus and Ashkenazi magical compilations place Sandalphon within elaborate lower-world, prayer, angelic, calendrical, and magical economies. This stage broadens his functions but does not yet entail the standardized Golden Dawn chart.

### Sixteenth–seventeenth centuries: Elijah and Metatron symmetry

Cordoverian Kabbalah explicitly identifies Sandalphon with Elijah. Later system-builders pair him with Metatron and interpret them as heavenly brothers or the two “lads.” This produces the now-famous transformed-human pairing, but it is chronologically far removed from the Talmud.

### Medieval Muslim notice

Sandalphon is absent from the Qur’an, canonical hadith, al-Ṭabarī’s standard Qur’anic angel narratives, and the core Islamic archangel scheme. Ibn Ḥazm’s eleventh-century *al-Fiṣal fī al-milal wa-l-ahwāʾ wa-l-niḥal* reportedly mentions Sandalfūn while describing or criticizing Jewish beliefs, calling him an angel serving the crown. That is evidence that the Jewish tradition was known in medieval Islamic polemography, not evidence that Sandalphon became a canonical Islamic angel. Islamic Israfil resembles Sandalphon in cosmic scale in some descriptions, but no historical equation is established. [General Islamic angelology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Islam/Angels); [Arabic text repository for Ibn Ḥazm](https://shamela.org/gen/dcffbea7547da2b8605f7323ae548c1e_gen.pdf).

### Nineteenth century: scholarship, Romantic poetry, and occultism

Critical Jewish scholarship catalogued the textual traditions and debated the name’s Greek or Persian origin. Longfellow’s 1858 poem transformed the prayer-bearing angel into a widely recognizable literary image. The Golden Dawn, founded in Britain in 1888, fixed Sandalphon into Malkuth-centered ceremonial correspondences.

### Twentieth–twenty-first centuries: mass-market angel and fictional character

Reference works such as Gustav Davidson’s *Dictionary of Angels* assembled genuine citations alongside unmarked harmonizations and occasionally uncertain attributions. New Age literature extracted Sandalphon from Jewish textual history and recast him as a universally available archangel of music, grounding, wishes, and environmental care.

Japanese media then detached the name further:

- *Neon Genesis Evangelion* uses Sandalphon for an embryonic Angel encountered in a volcano—**modern fictional invention**.
- Kaori Yuki’s *Angel Sanctuary* (1994–2000) makes Sandalphon Metatron’s grotesque, malicious twin and “Nightmare Eater”—**modern fictional invention**.
- *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona* render him as an armored or mechanical summon classified within game systems—**commercial reinvention**.
- Cygames’ *Granblue Fantasy* makes Sandalphon a “primarch,” initially destructive and later redeemed; he became a major recurring character and a playable fighter. The official Japanese character page identifies him as leader of the *tenshi* and keeper of a café aboard the Grandcypher. [Cygames character page](https://granbluefantasy.com/ja/character/arc_2/); [2025 playable release](https://rising.granbluefantasy.jp/en/dlc/character/11).

These adaptations now shape popular recognition more strongly than the Talmudic paragraph.

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Longfellow

Longfellow’s “Sandalphon” is the decisive literary reception. It explicitly announces dependence on “the Talmud” but freely recomposes the tradition. Sandalphon becomes immortal among ephemeral angels and converts suffering prayers into flowers. The poem is not a translation of *Ḥagigah*; it is a nineteenth-century American Christian-Romantic meditation on rabbinic legend.

### Golden Dawn and successors

The Golden Dawn’s table creates a durable occult identity:

- sefirah: Malkuth;
- world: especially Briah in the order’s layered scheme;
- office: archangel or Prince of Prayer;
- lower polarity complementary to Metatron;
- elemental manifestation: Earth/four elements;
- colors: citrine, olive, russet, and black in a divided field;
- choir: Ishim;
- divine name: commonly *Adonai ha-Aretz*.

Later writers—including Aleister Crowley’s correspondence tradition, Dion Fortune, Israel Regardie, and successor orders—made tabular assignments appear timeless. They are not found as a single package in medieval Jewish texts.

### New Age angelology

Claims now regularly made include:

- Sandalphon answers prayers;
- governs music and musicians;
- carries messages through songs;
- grounds spiritual energy;
- heals Earth;
- helps unborn children;
- assigns fetal sex;
- manifests wealth;
- appears in turquoise, pink, brown, or green;
- rules a chakra, weekday, crystal, or musical frequency.

Only prayer mediation has a substantial medieval pedigree. Music is an understandable but late expansion of heavenly praise. Most color, crystal, chakra, prosperity, and environmental assignments are twentieth- or twenty-first-century constructions, frequently author-specific.

### Neopaganism and reconstructionism

No continuous pagan cult exists to reconstruct, because Sandalphon originated as a Jewish angel rather than a pre-Abrahamic deity. Individual occultists, angelolaters, witches, and eclectic pagans may honor or invoke him, but “Sandalphon reconstructionism” would be a category error unless explicitly described as a modern adaptation.

### Politics and commerce

No major nationalist or extremist Sandalphon cult was located. Commercial exploitation is extensive but decentralized: oracle decks, crystals, guided meditations, downloadable sigils, jewelry, artwork, games, manga, anime merchandise, and paid spiritual consultations.

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Dating the first attestation

Bacher and Blau called the notice a second-century *baraita*. Modern Talmud scholarship is more cautious: traditions attributed to tannaim survive through centuries of transmission and redaction. “Early second-century tradition” is possible; “secure second-century document” is not.

### Etymology

- **Greek hypothesis:** *synadelphos*, “fellow-brother”; defended by Levy, Krauss, Bacher, and Blau.
- **Persian hypothesis:** associated with Kohut; rejected by Bacher and Blau.
- **Hebrew *sandek* hypothesis:** late and weak.
- **Unresolved view:** prudent because the exact phonological transmission and original semantic motivation remain uncertain.

The Greek explanation should not be used to date the Metatron brotherhood to the name’s origin.

### Prayer crowns

The phrase “from the prayers of Israel” is often inserted into summaries of *Ḥagigah* 13b. The base Talmudic text says crowns; Tosafot and later liturgy supply prayers. This is a classic case of a commentary becoming indistinguishable from its lemma in popular retelling.

### Elijah

The biblical ascent of Elijah is ancient; Elijah = Sandalphon is not. Cordovero supplies an explicit early-modern witness. The equation may draw upon earlier liturgical or mystical strands, but claims of an original rabbinic identity are unsupported.

### Metatron twinship

The pair’s symmetry is historically late and theologically productive. Andrei Orlov’s work on the Enoch-Metatron tradition is useful precisely because it demonstrates the extensive independent history of Metatron; it does not make Sandalphon an original twin in early Enoch literature.

### Gnostic origin

Neither the Nag Hammadi texts nor the major heresiologists’ reports of Gnostic systems securely contain Sandalphon. Inclusion in a “Gnostic figures pantheon” is a modern classificatory choice based on neighboring late-antique angelologies, not primary Gnostic evidence. Pearson and Rudolph are relevant for defining the Gnostic corpora and their aeons, but they do not furnish an ancient Sandalphon.

### Typhon and Israfil comparisons

Popular articles suggest that Sandalphon’s immense size connects him to Greek Typhon or Islamic Israfil. Similar gigantic scale is insufficient to show borrowing, identity, or common origin. No transmission chain or explicit equation has been demonstrated. These are **comparativist suggestions**, not established genealogy.

### Davidson and reference-book harmonization

Gustav Davidson’s 1967 *Dictionary of Angels* remains influential and useful as a lead-generator. It mixes primary references, literary reception, occult tables, and earlier encyclopedias, however. Every Davidson claim must be returned to its cited locus. Modern sites often cite Davidson without reproducing that verification, causing uncertain claims—embryo sex, specific heavens, battles with Samael—to circulate as settled antiquity.

### State of the evidence

The core sources survive textually rather than archaeologically:

- Talmud manuscripts and printed editions;
- manuscripts and editions of *Pesiqta Rabbati*;
- medieval prayer books and piyyut fragments;
- Hekhalot manuscript miscellanies;
- Zohar manuscripts and early printings;
- composite manuscripts and printed editions of *Sefer Raziel*;
- later amulets and magical compilations.

No known original autograph survives. The great chronological distance between composition, redaction, surviving manuscripts, and print editions makes textual criticism indispensable.

| Sixth century BCE and later | Ezekiel 1 supplies the “wheel upon the earth” later interpreted through Sandalphon; the angel is not named. |

| First century CE | New Testament and early Jewish-Christian apocalyptic texts do not name Sandalphon. |

| Eleventh–thirteenth centuries | Piyyut, prayer books, and commentary increasingly identify Sandalphon’s crowns with human prayer. |

## 11. Sources

### Primary and critical-text priorities

1. *Babylonian Talmud, Ḥagigah* 13b.
2. *Pesiqta Rabbati* 20.
3. Tosafot to *Ḥagigah* 13b.
4. Medieval piyyut and *Maḥzor Vitry* traditions, preferably from critical manuscript editions.
5. Hekhalot textual synopses and manuscript studies rather than undifferentiated anthologies.
6. Zohar II 58a, II 246a, III 252b, with modern critical scholarship on the corpus’s Castilian formation.
7. *Sefer Raziel ha-Malʾakh*, treated as a composite medieval work.
8. Moses Cordovero’s relevant angelological/Kabbalistic writings.
9. Naphtali Bacharach, *ʿEmeq ha-Melekh*.
10. Ibn Ḥazm, *al-Fiṣal*, as a Muslim report about Jewish angelology.
11. Longfellow, “Sandalphon.”
12. Golden Dawn manuscripts and Israel Regardie’s documentary compilation.

### Major secondary works and cautions

- Bacher and Blau’s “Sandalfon” remains the most compact map of the older references, but its source-dating language reflects early-twentieth-century scholarship.
- Gershom Scholem and Peter Schäfer are indispensable for Kabbalah and Hekhalot historiography.
- Andrei Orlov clarifies the independent Enoch-Metatron tradition and helps expose the lateness of the symmetrical twin scheme.
- Gustav Davidson is useful for discovering claims, not as a substitute for verifying their primary loci.
- *Dictionary of Deities and Demons in the Bible* is relevant to biblical divine beings but cannot supply Sandalphon with a biblical attestation.
- Bernard Bamberger, Jeffrey Burton Russell, Henry Ansgar Kelly, and works on Satan or fallen angels help define demonological categories; they offer no reason to classify Sandalphon as fallen.
- Birger Pearson and Kurt Rudolph help delimit Gnostic evidence; that delimitation produces a negative result for Sandalphon.
- The *Encyclopaedia of Islam* and specialist studies of Islamic angels similarly show that Sandalphon is not part of the Qur’anic named angelic roster.

### Documentary conclusions

The reconstructable Sandalphon is a layered textual figure:

1. a late-antique Jewish chariot angel of immense height who binds crowns;
2. a midrashic fiery threshold in Moses’ ascent;
3. a medieval liturgical mediator of human prayer;
4. a Kabbalistic Elijah and counterpart to Metatron;
5. a Golden Dawn archangel of Malkuth;
6. a Romantic angel of flowers and suffering prayer;
7. a New Age earth-and-music guide;
8. a radically reinvented character in Japanese popular media.

Only the first two layers belong to the earliest securely attested record. The prayer function is genuinely medieval, not merely modern; the Elijah and twin traditions are genuine but late; Malkuth’s standardized occult correspondences are nineteenth-century; turquoise, chakras, prosperity, and “dark angel” polarity are principally modern. There is no evidence of an ancient Sandalphon cult, Gnostic aeon, fallen angel, Qur’anic archangel, sacrificial practice, or historical suppression.

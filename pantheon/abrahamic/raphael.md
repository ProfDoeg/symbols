# Raphael: Research Dossier

> **Class:** Named angel/heavenly messenger in Second Temple Jewish literature; later classed as an archangel in Jewish, Christian, and esoteric systems. In one late-antique “Ophite” diagram he is instead an animal-shaped planetary ruler or demon. He is not a god, and no securely identified ancient cult treated him as one.
>
> **Earliest secure attestation:** Raphael appears in the *Book of the Watchers* (1 Enoch 1–36), whose principal composition is generally dated to the third century BCE, and in *Tobit*, normally dated to the third or early second century BCE. Fragments of both works survive among the Dead Sea Scrolls. The relative priority of the two literary portrayals cannot be proved precisely, but the Enochic material is often judged earlier. Raphael is absent by name from the Hebrew Bible.

## 1. Identification, names, and epithets

Raphael is the divine healer and heavenly intermediary whose first recoverable profiles belong to Second Temple Judaism. In 1 Enoch he is one of the senior angels who appeal to God against the Watchers, bind Azazel, heal the corrupted earth, supervise human spirits, show Enoch the places of the dead, and participate in eschatological judgment. In *Tobit* he is the divinely dispatched answer to two human prayers: disguised as a man named Azariah, he escorts Tobias, defeats Asmodeus, arranges Tobias’s marriage to Sarah, and heals Tobit’s blindness. Later Jewish interpretation made him the healer of Abraham and rescuer of Lot; Christianity received him through *Tobit*, developed his liturgical cult and visual identity, and made him a patron of travellers, physicians, the sick, marriage, and guardian angels. Ceremonial magic assigned him planets, elements, directions, colors, and seals. These systems are historically separate and frequently disagree.

### Names

- **Hebrew:** רְפָאֵל, *Rəfāʾēl* or *Rāfāʾēl*.
- **Aramaic:** רפאל, *Rĕpāʾēl/Rāpāʾēl*; the consonantal spelling is attested in Second Temple textual traditions.
- **Greek:** Ῥαφαήλ, *Rhaphāēl*.
- **Latin:** *Raphael*.
- **Syriac:** ܪܦܐܝܠ, *Rāp̄āʾēl*.
- **Coptic:** ⲣⲁⲫⲁⲏⲗ, *Raphaēl*.
- **Geʿez:** ሩፋኤል, commonly transliterated *Rufāʾel*.
- **Arabic:** رَفَائِيل / رفائيل, *Rafāʾīl*. This name must not automatically be replaced with إسرافيل, *Isrāfīl*.
- **Western vernaculars:** Raphael, Raphaël, Rafael, Raffaele, Raffael.

The standard etymology joins the Semitic root רפא, *r-p-ʾ*, “heal, restore,” to the divine element אל, *ʾEl*, “God”: “God has healed,” “God heals,” or “healing of God.” The grammar permits more than one English formulation, but the healing root is secure. *Tobit* exploits the meaning narratively: Raphael accomplishes the healing decreed by God.

### The disguise “Azariah”

In *Tobit* 5:12–13 Raphael claims to be “Azariah” (*Azarias* in Greek and Latin), son of the otherwise unidentified Hananiah/Ananias. Hebrew עֲזַרְיָה, *ʿAzaryāh*, means “YHWH has helped.” This is a narrative alias, not an alternative primordial name. Raphael subsequently explains that his apparent eating and drinking were part of the vision or appearance rather than ordinary bodily consumption (Tobit 12:19).

### Titles and classifications

- **“One of the seven holy angels” / one of seven who stand before God:** *Tobit* 12:15. “Archangel” is a later classificatory shorthand; that verse calls him an angel, not explicitly an *archangelos*.
- **“One of the holy angels”:** 1 Enoch 20.
- **“Over the spirits of men”:** 1 Enoch 20:3 in the usual Ethiopic numbering.
- **Angel “over diseases and every wound of the children of men”:** 1 Enoch 40:9.
- **Guide, healer, binder, intercessor:** functions derived from 1 Enoch and *Tobit*, rather than ancient fixed epithets.
- **Saint Raphael / St Raphael the Archangel:** Christian liturgical usage.
- **Medicus Dei**, “physician/healer of God”: medieval and early-modern Christian devotional description.
- **Patron of travellers, pilgrims, physicians, nurses, pharmacists, the blind, lovers and married couples:** post-biblical devotional elaborations inferred from *Tobit*.
- **Archangel of Air and the East:** chiefly nineteenth-century Golden Dawn ceremonial magic.
- **Archangel of Mercury or the Sun:** competing medieval and early-modern astrological-magical systems; neither is an ancient scriptural attribute.
- **Angel of the emerald-green healing ray:** twentieth-century metaphysical/New Age teaching, not ancient Jewish or Christian evidence.

### Raphael and Isrāfīl

**Finding: they should not be presented as simply the same angel.**

The Qurʾan does not name Raphael or Isrāfīl. It repeatedly describes the sounding of the trumpet at the resurrection without naming its angelic blower. Hadith, exegetical, and *qiṣaṣ al-anbiyāʾ* traditions establish Isrāfīl as the trumpet angel and one of the great angels. Some Islamic reports separately use the recognizable form **Rafāʾīl**, and modern comparative tables often equate Isrāfīl with Raphael. Their names, defining offices, and narrative records do not align securely. Modern scholarship has alternatively compared *Isrāfīl* with Jewish **Sarafiel/Seraphiel**, from the “seraph” complex. Thus:

- **Isrāfīl = Raphael:** late comparative or syncretistic identification; widespread in popular reference works, but not Qurʾanic and not philologically secure.
- **Rafāʾīl in Islamic narrative:** a borrowed or shared angelic name requiring examination source by source.
- **The Qurʾanic trumpet angel:** unnamed; calling him Raphael imports later interpretation into the text.

## 2. Earliest attestation and textual corpus

### The *Book of the Watchers*

**Tier: securely attested early material.**

The oldest literary stratum naming Raphael is probably the *Book of the Watchers*, 1 Enoch 1–36, produced through a third-century-BCE compositional process. Aramaic witnesses from Qumran prove that the work existed in pre-Christian Judaism; the complete collection survives principally in Geʿez, supplemented by Aramaic, Greek, Syriac, and Latin fragments. The work cannot simply be called a medieval Ethiopian invention: the Qumran manuscripts decisively refuted that once-common assumption. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/3161/chapter-abstract/144039804), [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/enoch-books-of/)

Principal passages:

- **1 Enoch 9:1–3:** Michael, Sariel/Uriel, Raphael, and Gabriel look down from heaven, see bloodshed and lawlessness, and hear the dead appealing for their cause.
- **10:4–8:** God orders Raphael to bind Asael/Azazel hand and foot, cast him into a jagged opening in the desert Dudael, cover him with stones and darkness, and heal the earth corrupted by angelic teaching.
- **20:3:** Raphael is one of the named holy angels, “over the spirits of men.”
- **22:** Raphael interprets for Enoch the hollow places where the spirits of the dead await judgment.
- **40:9:** among four presences, Raphael is set over diseases and wounds.
- **54:6:** Michael, Raphael, Gabriel, and Phanuel seize the hosts of Azazel for punishment.
- **90:9:** in the Animal Apocalypse, a manuscript tradition associates Raphael with one of the angelic agents who assist in judgment; translations and name forms vary.

The Enochic Raphael is therefore already more than a medical specialist: he is an intercessor, cosmic custodian, guide to the dead, punitive binder, and agent of environmental restoration.

### *Tobit*

**Tier: securely attested early material; later canonical status differs by community.**

*Tobit* was probably composed between roughly 225 and 175 BCE, though proposals extend from the fourth century to the early second. It survives in four Aramaic manuscripts (4Q196–199) and one Hebrew manuscript (4Q200) from Qumran Cave 4, discovered in 1952 and published in 1995. These fragments generally support the longer Greek recension represented most fully by Codex Sinaiticus rather than the shorter Greek form in Vaticanus and Alexandrinus. They establish a Semitic textual history without proving whether the first edition was Aramaic or Hebrew. [Perrin’s survey](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1476993X14532750), [Qumran manuscript study](https://czasopisma.kul.pl/index.php/ba/article/view/2133), [Oxford Handbook discussion](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/68521/1/263.pdf.pdf)

Raphael’s narrative:

1. Tobit becomes blind after bird droppings fall into his eyes; Sarah is tormented by the demon Asmodeus, who kills each of her seven bridegrooms before consummation.
2. Their prayers reach the heavenly presence. God sends Raphael to heal both sufferers (3:16–17).
3. Raphael assumes the identity of Azariah and is hired as Tobias’s guide from Nineveh toward Media (5).
4. At the Tigris, a fish attacks Tobias. Raphael orders him to seize it and preserve its heart, liver, and gall (6:2–9).
5. Raphael explains that burning the heart and liver repels a demon, while gall treats white films over the eyes.
6. He directs Tobias to marry Sarah as her lawful kinsman and instructs the couple to pray (6–8).
7. The smoke drives Asmodeus away. Raphael pursues him to “upper Egypt” and binds him there (8:2–3).
8. Raphael completes Tobit’s financial business with Gabael (9).
9. Tobias applies the gall; Tobit’s sight returns (11).
10. Raphael refuses payment, commands the family to praise God, reveals his identity as one of the seven angels who present human prayers, and disappears (12).

The story combines wisdom tale, family novella, diaspora piety, exorcism, healing lore, marriage narrative, and angelic epiphany. Modern medical attempts to diagnose Tobit’s eye condition or rationalize fish gall are reconstructions, not statements in the ancient narrative.

### Other Jewish texts

- **Jubilees, late second century BCE:** has an elaborate angelology and angels of the presence but does **not** name Raphael. Associating its unnamed angels with Raphael is modern harmonization.
- **Babylonian Talmud, Bava Metzia 86b, compiled late antiquity:** the three visitors of Genesis 18 receive differentiated missions—announcement, healing Abraham, destruction of Sodom/rescue of Lot. Some textual presentations name Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael; later commentaries make Raphael the healer and rescuer.
- **Genesis Rabbah 48 and 50, redacted approximately fifth century CE:** develops the missions of Abraham’s visitors. The biblical text itself never gives their names.
- **Rashi and Naḥmanides on Genesis 18:** transmit and debate the assignment. Naḥmanides explicitly explains that healing Abraham and rescuing Lot may count as one saving commission. [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Ramban_on_Genesis.18.1?lang=en)
- **Hekhalot/merkabah literature, approximately late antiquity to early Middle Ages:** named angels proliferate, but no single stable Raphael dossier emerges. Individual recensions and magical appendices require separate manuscript control.
- **Sefer ha-Razim, late antique Jewish magical handbook:** invokes hierarchical angels in ritual operations; Raphael participates in the wider late-antique angel-name economy, but planetary and directional assignments vary by recension.
- **Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh:** a medieval compilation, printed at Amsterdam in 1701 from earlier materials. Its title refers to Raziel, not Raphael. Claims that Raziel is simply another name for Raphael are modern esoteric conflations unless a particular recension explicitly makes the equation.
- **Zohar, principally late-thirteenth-century Castile:** integrates Raphael into symbolic camps, body parts, rivers, tribes, and *sefirotic* correspondences. These are medieval Kabbalistic systematizations, not Second Temple attributes.

### Material magical evidence

Late-antique Greek, Jewish Aramaic, Christian, and syncretistic amulets invoke Raphael alongside Michael, Gabriel, Uriel and divine names. A Getty amulet, for example, appeals to the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and names Uriel, Michael, Raphael, Gabriel, and Sariel against an epileptic spirit. Jewish Babylonian incantation bowls from approximately 500–700 CE similarly invoke major angels to bind demons. These objects demonstrate practiced protective religion rather than merely literary speculation. [Getty Museum Journal](https://www.getty.edu/publications/resources/virtuallibrary/0892360305.pdf), [Southampton corpus record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/12270)

### Christian texts

- **New Testament:** Raphael is not named.
- **John 5:3b–4:** later manuscripts explain that an angel stirred the pool of Bethesda and the first entrant was healed. The passage is absent from important early witnesses including P66, P75, Vaticanus, and Sinaiticus. The verse itself never names Raphael. Identifying the angel as Raphael is a later inference from his healing office. [Text-critical discussion](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/29525014/Houghton_OHJS_preprint.pdf)
- **Pseudo-Dionysius, *Celestial Hierarchy*, late fifth or early sixth century:** constructs nine angelic orders in three triads. It does not supply a biography of Raphael; later theologians locate named biblical angels within its hierarchy.
- **Gregory the Great, *Homilies on the Gospels* 34, c. 590:** distinguishes angels and archangels by mission and explains Raphael’s name through healing.
- **Aquinas, *Summa theologiae*, thirteenth century:** systematizes angelic nature using scripture, Augustine, and Pseudo-Dionysius. Raphael is evidence for angelic mission and manifestation, not the object of an independent theological cult.
- **Protestant reception:** because *Tobit* was placed among the Apocrypha rather than the canonical books, Raphael’s status became less doctrinally central, although he remained prominent in art, literature, Anglican usage, and occult traditions.
- **Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox traditions:** 1 Enoch and *Tobit* retain canonical authority, preserving both major ancient Raphael profiles within scripture.

### Gnostic and heresiological evidence

The Nag Hammadi codices contain no securely identified Raphael narrative comparable to *Tobit* or 1 Enoch. The *Hypostasis of the Archons*, *Apocryphon of John*, and *On the Origin of the World* describe rulers such as Yaldabaoth, Sabaoth, and Saklas but do not warrant inserting Raphael into their lists.

The important evidence instead comes from Celsus’s lost *True Doctrine* (c. 175–180 CE), preserved and criticized by Origen in *Contra Celsum* 6.24–38 (c. 248). Origen reports an “Ophite” diagram in which seven rulers bear animal forms. The third is **Raphael, serpent-like or amphibious and hissing**; Michael is lion-like, Suriel bull-like, and Gabriel eagle-like. Origen insists these figures are demons in the diagram and distinguishes them from the honored angels bearing the same names in Jewish and Christian scripture. This is a genuine late-antique variant, but its evidence is hostile and indirect: Celsus and Origen describe a diagram that does not itself survive. [Origen, *Contra Celsum* VI](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/04166.htm)

Irenaeus, Hippolytus, and Epiphanius preserve other reports about groups modern scholars aggregate under “Gnosticism,” but they do not justify a single Gnostic pantheon in which Raphael always has the same place. The Nag Hammadi discoveries confirmed broad archontic myth patterns while also demonstrating that heresiological labels often flatten distinct communities. [Claremont Coptic Encyclopedia](https://ccdl.claremont.edu/digital/collection/cce/id/1001/), [Brill Coptic Gnostic Library](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/cglo/)

## 3. Domain, attributes, and iconography

### Early functions

**Secure early functions:**

- Hearing or presenting human prayer.
- Healing bodily injury, blindness, disease, and the wounded earth.
- Guiding a traveller safely.
- Exorcising and binding dangerous spirits.
- Supervising human spirits and explaining the realm of the dead.
- Punishing rebellious angels.

Raphael does not autonomously heal in the earliest sources: God commissions him. In *Tobit* his medicines and exorcistic instructions work within divine providence, not as an independent magical power.

### Appearance

Neither 1 Enoch nor *Tobit* supplies a stable bodily portrait. In *Tobit*, Raphael looks like an ordinary Israelite man and conceals his identity. Wings, halos, jeweled court dress, and youthful androgynous beauty are artistic conventions.

### Recognizable Christian iconography

- A youthful winged angel accompanying Tobias.
- Tobias holding or carrying a fish.
- A pilgrim’s staff, wallet, bottle, or travelling cloak.
- A medicine box or ointment vessel.
- Raphael touching or leading Tobias, often with Tobias’s small dog.
- In hierarchical ensembles, court dress, orb, sceptre, or staff.

Byzantine mosaics commonly present archangels as imperial courtiers. Western medieval and Renaissance art increasingly narrates the journey. From fifteenth-century Florence onward, “Tobias and the Angel” became a major devotional form connected with safe travel, filial duty, healing, marriage, and guardian angels. Botticini’s *Three Archangels and Tobias* identifies Michael by armor, Gabriel by the lily, and Raphael by his guardianship of Tobias. [Uffizi](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/i-tre-arcangeli-e-tobiolo)

Important Renaissance examples include:

- Antonio and Piero del Pollaiuolo, *Tobias and the Angel*, c. 1460.
- Verrocchio’s workshop, *Tobias and the Angel*, c. 1470–80.
- Perugino, *The Archangel Raphael with Tobias*, c. 1496–1500. [National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/pietro-perugino-the-archangel-raphael-with-tobias)
- Francesco Botticini, *The Three Archangels and Tobias*, c. 1470.
- Titian, *The Archangel Raphael and Tobias*, c. 1512–14.
- Andrea del Sarto, *Raphael with Tobias, St Leonard and a Donor*, early sixteenth century.
- Raphael Sanzio, *Madonna of the Fish*, c. 1513–14: the painter’s name is coincidental, but the work materially reinforced the angel’s visual association with Tobias and the fish.

### Later correspondences

- **Fish and medicine:** directly anchored in *Tobit*.
- **Staff and travel:** reasonable visual development from the journey.
- **Caduceus:** modern or early-modern appropriation of a Greco-Roman emblem; absent from the ancient Raphael texts.
- **Green or emerald light:** modern occult/New Age standardization.
- **Air, East, yellow and violet robes:** Golden Dawn correspondence.
- **Mercury, Wednesday, eight, five, emerald, malachite, particular herbs or crystals:** variable occult and commercial systems, not a single ancient tradition.

No ancient source securely assigns Raphael a sacred animal, temple plant, fixed color, or number.

## 4. Genealogy and relations

Angels in the relevant Jewish and Christian texts are created heavenly beings, not members of a biological divine family. Raphael has no attested parents, consort, children, siblings, mount, or independent household. Calling Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael “brothers” is devotional metaphor or modern fiction.

### Documented divine-being interactions

- **God — receives a commission to bind Azazel and heal the earth.** 1 Enoch 10, third century BCE. **Secure early material.**
- **God — joins Michael, Sariel/Uriel, and Gabriel in appealing over earthly violence.** 1 Enoch 9. **Secure early material.**
- **Azazel/Asael — binds him hand and foot, imprisons him in Dudael, and later participates in judgment on his hosts.** 1 Enoch 10; 54. **Secure early material, with textual variation in the name.**
- **Michael — appears jointly in the four-angel group and later in judgment.** 1 Enoch 9, 40, 54. **Secure early material.**
- **Gabriel — appears jointly in intercession and judgment.** 1 Enoch 9, 40, 54. **Secure early material.**
- **Sariel/Suriel/Uriel — appears jointly in the four-angel appeal; the name varies among witnesses and translations.** 1 Enoch 9. **Secure early material with manuscript variation.**
- **Phanuel — jointly constitutes the four presences and joins eschatological punishment.** 1 Enoch 40, 54, part of the later *Parables of Enoch*, commonly dated around the late first century BCE or first century CE. **Later Enochic elaboration.**
- **The Watchers collectively — Raphael helps expose, punish, and repair the damage caused by their descent and teachings.** 1 Enoch 9–10. **Secure early material.**
- **Asmodeus — expels him from Sarah and binds him in upper Egypt.** *Tobit* 8:2–3, third/early second century BCE. **Secure early material.**
- **The seven angels before God — Raphael identifies himself as one of this unnamed group.** *Tobit* 12:15. **Secure early material; the verse does not name the other six.**
- **Michael and Gabriel — later joins them as the three visitors to Abraham; Raphael heals Abraham and, in dominant commentary, rescues Lot.** Bava Metzia 86b; Genesis Rabbah; medieval commentators. **Late-antique rabbinic elaboration.**
- **Suriel, Michael, and Gabriel — appears beside their animal-shaped counterparts in the Ophite diagram.** Celsus/Origen, second–third centuries CE. **Regional heterodox variant known through hostile testimony.**
- **Christ/Jesus — no ancient narrative meeting is securely attested.** The Bethesda angel is unnamed and textually secondary. **Absence of evidence.**
- **Satan — Raphael narrates Satan’s rebellion and opposes his persuasion in Milton’s *Paradise Lost*.** 1667/1674. **Literary reinvention.**
- **Raziel — no early source establishes identity or interaction.** Assertions that they are one being are **modern conflation**.

## 5. Major myths and narrative cycles

### Binding Azazel and healing the earth

In the *Book of the Watchers*, rebellious heavenly Watchers descend, mate with women, produce giants, reveal forbidden arts, and fill the earth with violence. Human souls cry to heaven. Raphael and other senior angels report the crisis, whereupon God orders Raphael to bind Azazel, throw him into darkness in Dudael, cover him with stones, and preserve him until final judgment. Raphael must then “heal the earth” corrupted by the Watchers.

This is simultaneously punitive and restorative. Azazel is treated as the concentrated bearer of pollution; scholarship has compared the episode with the Levitical scapegoat ritual, but direct dependence and the exact direction of influence remain debated. The binding “hand and foot” also resembles Raphael’s later binding of Asmodeus in *Tobit*, although literary dependence cannot be proved solely from the shared verb.

### Tobias’s journey

*Tobit* supplies the fullest ancient Raphael legend. The narrative’s two sufferers—blind Tobit and demon-afflicted Sarah—pray separately, while the narrator reveals that one angelic mission will resolve both crises. Raphael’s disguise permits him to act as hired guide, marriage broker, exorcist, physician, financial agent, and theological instructor. The fish episode binds the functions together: what threatened Tobias becomes food, exorcistic fumigation, and medicine.

Significant textual differences include:

- Long and short Greek recensions vary in detail and narrative texture.
- Jerome’s Vulgate substantially abbreviates and reshapes the work from an Aramaic intermediary.
- The Qumran evidence generally favors the longer Greek tradition.
- Later art frequently enlarges the fish, adds visible wings throughout the journey, or has Tobias carry the whole fish long after it should have been eaten; these are visual retellings, not textual facts.
- Later devotion shifts attention from the family’s providential deliverance to Raphael as an independently invocable patron.

### Abraham and Lot

Genesis 18–19 names neither the three visitors nor the two angels at Sodom. Rabbinic interpretation assigns one mission to each: Michael announces Isaac, Gabriel destroys Sodom, and Raphael heals circumcised Abraham; Raphael then rescues Lot because rescue is cognate with healing. This is a late-antique exegetical legend built upon Raphael’s established healing profile, not evidence that the Genesis narrator knew his name.

### The Ophite Raphael

In the diagram described by Celsus and Origen, Raphael is a hissing, serpent-like or amphibious ruler among seven animal-shaped powers encountered in the soul’s ascent. The use of revered angelic names for hostile or ambiguous cosmic rulers reflects a deliberate reversal or reuse of Jewish angelology. It should not be harmonized with the benevolent healer. Nor should it be projected indiscriminately onto the Nag Hammadi texts.

### Bethesda

The angel who periodically descends to stir the pool in the expanded text of John 5 became identified with Raphael because healing water suited his name and office. This legend rests on two late moves: insertion of the explanatory verse into the Johannine manuscript tradition and subsequent naming of its anonymous angel. It is therefore a **late Christian accretion**, not a New Testament attestation.

## 6. Cult, temples, and ritual practice

Raphael had no known ancient temple economy, hereditary priesthood, animal sacrifice, or sovereign civic cult comparable to a Mediterranean deity. The material record instead consists of texts, amulets, inscriptions, churches, images, liturgy, prayers, confraternities, hospitals, and personal names.

### Jewish practice

- Late-antique amulets and incantation bowls invoke Raphael for protection, healing, childbirth, and expulsion of demons.
- The bedtime prayer formula asking Michael, Gabriel, Uriel, and Raphael to surround the sleeper became a durable Jewish domestic tradition; ordering varies.
- Medieval magical manuals supplied seals, adjurations, astrological timing, and angelic hierarchies.
- Mainstream rabbinic theology maintained that angels were agents of God, not independent gods; magical texts could nevertheless adjure them with elaborate formulae.

### Christian cult and liturgy

Raphael’s cult grew from scriptural reading of *Tobit*, angelic feast days, healing devotion, and guardian-angel piety.

- Eastern churches commemorate Raphael among the bodiless powers and archangels.
- In the Roman rite Raphael entered the universal calendar as a separate feast on 24 October in 1921.
- The 1969 calendar revision joined Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael on 29 September.
- Catholic guidance warns against inventing or venerating angelic names lacking scriptural warrant; Raphael remains accepted because *Tobit* is canonical in Catholicism. [Vatican directory](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_fr.html)
- Benedict XVI summarized Raphael’s two emblematic tasks as healing and guiding in his 2007 homily. [Vatican homily](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/homilies/2007/documents/hf_ben-xvi_hom_20070929_episc-ordinations.html)

Churches, hospital chapels, medical institutions, confraternities, and travel-related ministries bear his name. The cult produced offerings and commissions, especially paintings requested for protection, healing, or safe travel, but no evidence connects Raphael to human sacrifice, temple prostitution, or an ancient sacrificial priesthood.

### Suppression and control

Raphael himself was not systematically suppressed in churches accepting *Tobit*. What authorities periodically controlled was the proliferation of additional angel names and unauthorized seven-archangel cults. The Roman synod under Pope Zachary in 745 condemned certain angel names used by Adalbert while retaining scripturally grounded Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael. Later scrutiny of the seven-archangel cult likewise distinguished the canonical three from names drawn from apocrypha or magical revelation.

Protestant removal of *Tobit* from the canonical Old Testament reduced Raphael’s doctrinal visibility but did not erase him from art or literature. This is confessional canon differentiation, not evidence of a coordinated attempt to destroy Raphael worship.

## 7. Historical evolution

### Third–second centuries BCE: formation

Named high angels emerge prominently in Jewish apocalyptic literature. Raphael’s earliest recoverable functions are divided between punitive-cosmic healing in 1 Enoch and personal healing/guidance in *Tobit*. Persian, Mesopotamian, biblical, courtly, and Hellenistic environments have all been proposed as contributing contexts for named archangels. Direct derivation from one Zoroastrian being has not been demonstrated.

### First century BCE–first century CE: expansion

Later Enochic strata place Raphael among four presences and eschatological executioners. *Tobit* circulates in several Semitic copies at Qumran. Named angels become increasingly common in Jewish apocalyptic and magical texts.

### Second–fourth centuries CE: differentiation

Christians inherit *Tobit* through Greek scripture while 1 Enoch circulates unevenly. Gnostic or “Ophite” users reverse angelic symbolism, producing the serpent-like Raphael known from Origen. Amulets cross Jewish, Christian, and syncretistic boundaries. Raphael remains unnamed in the New Testament.

### Fifth–eighth centuries: rabbinic and ecclesial systematization

Rabbinic texts assign Raphael to Abraham’s healing. Pseudo-Dionysius supplies the hierarchical architecture within which Christian theology later places archangels. Byzantine art gives senior angels imperial court dress. Jewish and Christian amulets invoke Raphael materially. The Latin church regulates uncontrolled angel-name devotion while retaining Raphael.

### Ninth–thirteenth centuries: mystical and scholastic integration

Hekhalot traditions, magical books, Kabbalistic compilations, and European grimoires multiply Raphael’s correspondences. Scholastic theologians clarify that angels are incorporeal created intellects who can assume visible appearances. The Zohar inserts Raphael into a symbolic, *sefirotic* cosmos.

### Fourteenth–sixteenth centuries: visual flowering

Italian confraternal, mercantile, medical, familial, and travel piety make Tobias and Raphael a favored theme. The angel becomes the prototype of the guardian companion. Renaissance art gives him wings, luxurious robes, delicate youthful beauty, staff, fish, and medicine vessel.

### Seventeenth–nineteenth centuries: literary and occult transformation

Milton gives Raphael an enormous new narrative role in *Paradise Lost*: God sends the “sociable spirit” to warn Adam, recount the war in heaven, explain creation, and discuss celestial bodies and human freedom. This is literary theology, not recovered ancient tradition. Dante, by contrast, does not give Raphael a comparable speaking role; claims that the *Divine Comedy* contains a Raphael cycle are mistaken.

The *Lemegeton* and related grimoires invoke Raphael among governing angels. Éliphas Lévi and the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn consolidate the East–Air attribution. The Golden Dawn’s Lesser Ritual of the Pentagram places Raphael before the practitioner, Gabriel behind, Michael at the right, and Uriel at the left. These correspondences became foundational for later ceremonial magic, Thelema, Wicca, and popular occultism. [*Lemegeton* text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

### Twentieth–twenty-first centuries

Catholic liturgy consolidates Raphael with Michael and Gabriel on 29 September. New Age writers transform the healer into an emerald-green energy, “ray,” chakra assistant, crystal patron, or quasi-therapeutic presence. Some systems date their own alleged revelations: the Summit Lighthouse, for example, says Raphael released a multicolored “healing thoughtform” in 1964. That is a documented modern revelation claim, not an ancient tradition. [Modern example](https://www.healinglightretreat.org/Default.aspx?tabid=1003)

Raphael continues in Jewish prayer, Catholic and Orthodox devotion, ceremonial magic, neopagan angel work, fiction, comics, games, television, and commercial wellness products. No reliable demographic evidence permits a precise count of independent modern “Raphael worshippers.”

## 8. Modern reception and reinvention

### Literature

- **Milton, *Paradise Lost* (1667; revised 1674):** Raphael occupies Books V–VIII as warning messenger, historian of the war in heaven, cosmological teacher, and companionable guest. [University of Toronto summary](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/paradise-lost-books-v-viii-editorial-summary)
- **Longfellow, *The Golden Legend* (1851):** uses Raphael within Christian legendary drama.
- **Modern fantasy:** frequently turns Raphael into a warrior, elemental ruler, fallen angel, romance figure, or sibling of other archangels. These kinships are fictional.

### Art

Renaissance Tobias paintings shaped the popular Raphael more than most theological treatises did. Their recurring fish and youthful guide made Raphael visually distinguishable from Michael’s dragon and Gabriel’s lily. The images also contributed to guardian-angel devotion and reflected the hazards of travel in mercantile Italy. A Borghese Gallery account notes the subject’s vogue among pilgrims and its talismanic associations with safe travel. [Galleria Borghese](https://www.collezionegalleriaborghese.it/en/opere/tobias-and-the-angel-2)

### Occultism and New Age religion

- **Grimoire Raphael:** a powerful angel called to constrain spirits, protect operations, heal, and govern celestial or elemental forces.
- **Golden Dawn Raphael:** East, Air, yellow/violet, caduceus or sword, intellectual clarity.
- **Planetary Raphael:** assigned to Mercury in some systems, the Sun in others. There is no universal occult consensus.
- **New Age Raphael:** emerald ray, heart-centered or cellular healing, crystals, Reiki, medical visualization, abundance, and animal healing.
- **Modern invention markers:** fixed “angel numbers,” universal crystal lists, Atlantis narratives, laser-surgery angels, and precise aura frequencies are twentieth- or twenty-first-century teachings when documentation exists; they are not ancient remnants.

### Political and extremist appropriation

The dedicated search found no significant, well-documented political or extremist movement centered on Raphael comparable to nationalist appropriations of Michael. Raphael can appear incidentally in militant angelic rhetoric, but claiming an organized extremist Raphael tradition would exceed the evidence.

### Contested practices: findings

- **Human sacrifice:** none associated with Raphael’s worship. Azazel’s binding has been interpreted through scapegoat-sacrifice symbolism, but Raphael is the divine agent, not recipient.
- **Temple prostitution:** no evidence.
- **Cult violence:** no institutional Raphael cult violence identified.
- **Forced conversion or suppression:** broader Christianization and confessional conflict affected the texts and communities that transmitted Raphael, but no discrete Raphael-led campaign is documented.
- **Neopagan cult:** small-scale online angelolatry and eclectic ritual work exist; size and continuity are not securely measurable.

## 9. Historiography and scholarly debate

### Priority and origins

The central historical question is not whether Raphael “really existed,” but how a named healing angel emerged from earlier Israelite traditions in which most messengers remain anonymous. Proposed explanations include:

- elaboration of the biblical “angel of YHWH”;
- heavenly-court ideology modeled on royal officials;
- exegetical naming of previously anonymous angels;
- Persian or Babylonian influence on celestial hierarchy;
- literary personification of divine functions;
- priestly/apocalyptic response to pollution, empire, and deferred justice.

No single-origin theory commands consensus. A recent study argues that *Tobit* adapts exodus and angel-of-the-Lord traditions into a “micro-exodus” for suffering diaspora Jews. [Muñoa](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0951820716637148)

### Textual criticism of *Tobit*

The discovery of 4Q196–200 was transformative. It demonstrated a Semitic textual base and vindicated many readings of the long Greek recension. Outstanding disputes concern original language, place of composition, relationship between recensions, folktale sources, and the historical setting concealed beneath the Assyrian narrative.

Critical resources include Joseph Fitzmyer’s *Tobit* commentary; Alexander Di Lella’s work; Stuart Weeks, Simon Gathercole and Loren Stuckenbruck’s synoptic edition; and the Dead Sea Scrolls editions.

### Enochic studies

Qumran discoveries moved substantial parts of 1 Enoch firmly into the pre-Maccabean or early Hellenistic period. George Nickelsburg, James VanderKam, Józef Milik, Loren Stuckenbruck, Annette Yoshiko Reed, and others established the composite nature and complex reception of the corpus. The complete Ethiopic transmission must be compared with earlier Aramaic and Greek evidence rather than treated as a transparent original.

### Gnosticism

Older histories often assumed a coherent “Ophite religion.” Modern scholarship, including Karen King, Birger Pearson, Kurt Rudolph, Michael Williams, and Tuomas Rasimus, stresses that “Gnosticism” and “Ophite” can be heresiological aggregates. Raphael’s serpent form is securely reportable as a feature of the diagram described by Celsus and Origen; it cannot be generalized to all so-called Gnostics.

### Reference works and their limits

Gustav Davidson’s *Dictionary of Angels* is extraordinarily useful as a locator but frequently compiles late occult, literary, and folkloric statements without adequate historical stratification. The *Dictionary of Deities and Demons in the Bible* is stronger on ancient textual history. Bernard Bamberger, Jeffrey Burton Russell, Henry Ansgar Kelly, Birger Pearson, and Kurt Rudolph help situate fallen angels, demons, and Gnostic systems, but none should replace direct checking of the primary passages.

### Discredited or unproved claims still circulating

- Raphael is named in the Hebrew Bible: **false**.
- Raphael is named in the New Testament: **false**.
- John 5:4 originally named Raphael: **false**; even the angel verse is textually secondary.
- Raphael and Isrāfīl are unquestionably identical: **unproved and probably reductive**.
- Raphael’s color has always been emerald green: **modern invention**.
- Raphael belongs to a universally fixed seven-archangel list: **false**; lists and spellings vary.
- Raphael is Raziel: **no early evidence**.
- The Nag Hammadi codices present Raphael as a principal archon: **not established**; the relevant named serpent comes from Origen’s Ophite diagram.
- Raphael’s caduceus is ancient Jewish iconography: **false**.
- Medieval grimoires preserve unchanged antediluvian books authored by Raphael: **legendary attribution, not documentary history**.

## 11. Sources and research assessment

The central primary dossier consists of 1 Enoch 9–10, 20, 22, 40 and 54; *Tobit* 3–12 in its Semitic, Greek and Latin witnesses; Genesis Rabbah and Bava Metzia 86b; medieval Jewish commentary; Origen’s *Contra Celsum* 6.24–38; late-antique amulets and incantation bowls; Gregory the Great; Pseudo-Dionysius; Aquinas; medieval and early-modern grimoires; Renaissance visual objects; and Milton.

The evidence supports a continuous **history of transmission and reinvention**, not an unchanged personality. The earliest Raphael is simultaneously healer and punitive cosmic officer. *Tobit* humanizes him as a companion in disguise. Rabbinic exegesis inserts him into Genesis. Christian art makes him a winged guardian of travellers. Gnostic evidence reverses him into a serpent ruler. Ceremonial magic maps him onto planets and elements. New Age religion turns him emerald green. Each Raphael is historically real as an attested representation, but they are not interchangeable.

# Behemoth: Research Dossier

> **Class:** A singular primordial land-monster or super-beast in Job and early Jewish apocalypse; a divinely created eschatological food-animal in later Jewish tradition; only much later a demon in European demonography. Behemoth is not securely attested as a god, angel, fallen angel, or Gnostic archon.
>
> **Earliest secure attestation:** Hebrew **בְּהֵמוֹת** (*bəhēmōṯ*) in Job 40:15–24. The passage was composed no later than the final formation of Job, conventionally placed somewhere between the sixth and fourth centuries BCE, although the book’s layered composition and deliberate archaism prevent exact dating. The earliest surviving witness relevant to these chapters is the Herodian-period Aramaic Job translation from Qumran, 11Q10/11QtgJob, copied around the turn of the era; the complete Hebrew wording survives in medieval Masoretic codices.

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### Basic identity

In Job 40, YHWH tells Job to look at **Behemoth**, an immense grass-eating creature made by God “with” or “as” Job. Its strength lies in its loins and belly; its bones and limbs resemble bronze and iron; it inhabits reeds, lotuses, mountains, and river country; even a flooding Jordan does not alarm it; humans cannot capture it, but its maker can approach it with the sword. The creature is thus simultaneously zoological and superlative: it resembles a huge herbivorous river animal, yet is “the first” or “chief” of God’s works and functions as a demonstration of divine power beyond human control. The Hebrew text does not call it a demon, angel, evil power, or object of worship. [Hebrew and English text of Job 40:15–24](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.40.15-24?lang=bi&ven=english%7CTanakh%3A_The_Holy_Scriptures%2C_published_by_JPS&vhe=hebrew%7CMiqra_according_to_the_Masorah&with=Translations)

In 1 Enoch 60, Behemoth becomes explicitly one of two primordial monsters: a male land-being paired with the female Leviathan. In 4 Ezra 6 and 2 Baruch 29, the pair were created at the beginning, separated into land and sea, preserved until the end, and—in 2 Baruch explicitly—destined as food for the survivors of the messianic age. Rabbinic texts expand this into Behemoth’s daily feeding on a thousand mountains, its combat with Leviathan, and the banquet of the righteous.

### Names and scripts

- **Hebrew:** בְּהֵמוֹת, *bəhēmōṯ*, conventionally *Behemoth*.
- **Unpointed Hebrew:** בהמות.
- **Greek Job:** θηρία, *thēria*, “wild beasts/animals,” rather than a transliterated proper name. The Old Greek translator apparently read the Hebrew as a common or collective noun. Job 40:15 begins, “Behold, beasts beside you; they eat grass like cattle,” although subsequent pronouns become singular. [Old Greek Job 40](https://biblehub.com/sepd/job/40.htm)
- **Latin Vulgate:** *Behemoth*, preserving the Hebrew form as a name.
- **Geʽez 1 Enoch:** normally represented in modern critical translations as *Behemoth*; 1 Enoch 60 survives completely only through the Ethiopic transmission.
- **Syriac 2 Baruch:** the name is preserved through the Syriac version of a work probably composed in Hebrew and transmitted through Greek.
- **Russian:** Бегемот, *Begemot*. In modern Russian this is also the ordinary word for “hippopotamus,” enabling Bulgakov’s double reference.
- **French:** *Béhémoth*.
- **German:** *Behemoth*.
- **Italian:** *Beemot/Behemot*.
- **Arabic comparandum:** بهموت or بهاموت, *Bahamūt/Bahīmūt*. Medieval Islamic cosmographers use this for a cosmic fish or whale. Its name may ultimately be related to biblical Behemoth, but the Arabic being is not a straightforward Islamic preservation of Job’s land-monster.

### Etymology

**Secure linguistic starting point:** בְּהֵמוֹת has the form of the Hebrew feminine plural of בְּהֵמָה, *bəhēmāh*, “beast, cattle, large animal.” Because Job’s syntax describes a singular creature, the traditional explanation is an intensive, augmentative, or abstract plural: “great beast,” “beast par excellence,” or “colossal cattle.”

**Disputed Egyptian hypothesis:** Earlier scholarship proposed an Egyptian expression reconstructed as *pꜣ-jḥ-mw*, supposedly “the ox of the water,” hence “hippopotamus.” The decisive problem is that the alleged Egyptian expression is not actually attested with that meaning. It can therefore illustrate the long history of identifying Behemoth with a hippopotamus, but cannot presently be treated as a demonstrated etymology. The older Jewish Encyclopedia already noted that Jablonski’s supposed Egyptian *p-ehe-mu* “does not exist.” [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Leviathan and Behemoth”](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/9841-leviathan-and-behemoth)

**Ancient Near Eastern reconstruction:** John Day and other comparativists have proposed that Behemoth’s literary ancestor may be the bull-like monster **Arš** or **ʿAtik**, “the calf of El,” whom Anat defeats near or alongside the Ugaritic dragon Lotan in KTU 1.3. The pairing is attractive because Lotan is the close Northwest Semitic cognate of Hebrew Leviathan. This is a modern scholarly reconstruction, not an ancient identification: no surviving Hebrew or Ugaritic source says “Behemoth is ʿAtik/Arš.”

### Descriptive titles

No ancient cult titles or epithets are securely attached to Behemoth. Later descriptions function as titles:

- **“First/chief of the works/ways of God”** — Job 40:19.
- **Male monster of the dry land** — 1 Enoch 60:7–10.
- **Behemoth of a thousand mountains/hills** — rabbinic interpretation of Psalm 50:10.
- **Food for the elect/righteous or survivors** — 1 Enoch 60:24; 2 Baruch 29:4.
- **Infernal cupbearer, demon of gluttony, night watchman of hell** — early-modern and nineteenth-century European demonography, especially Collin de Plancy. These are not biblical or rabbinic titles.
- **“Another name for Satan”** — some recent demonolatry manuals. This is a modern invention or reclassification, not an ancient equation.

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### Job 40:15–24

**Tier: securely attested early material.**

The sole unambiguous occurrence of Behemoth as a singular named being in the Hebrew Bible is Job 40:15. Elsewhere, *bəhēmōṯ* normally means animals or cattle. The composition history of Job is disputed: proposed dates extend broadly across the first millennium BCE, but linguistic and literary arguments commonly place its final form in the Persian or early Hellenistic period. Exact claims such as “Behemoth was first written in 500 BCE” exceed the evidence.

The text says:

- God made Behemoth “with” or “as” Job.
- It eats grass like cattle.
- Its strength is in its loins and abdominal muscles.
- It makes, stiffens, or bends its tail “like a cedar”; both verb and anatomical implications are debated.
- Its bones and limbs are compared with bronze and iron.
- It is רֵאשִׁית דַּרְכֵי־אֵל, “first/chief of the ways of God.”
- A difficult clause says either that its maker alone can approach it with the sword, that God furnished it with a sword, or something comparable.
- Mountains provide its food; other animals play there.
- It rests under lotuses and among marsh reeds.
- It is unafraid of a surging Jordan.
- Human seizure by the eyes or piercing of its nose is posed as impossible.

The literary function is clearer than the zoology: Job cannot master a being that remains within God’s created order. Michael Fox argues that the pairing with Leviathan and the mythic background matter even if hippopotamus imagery contributed to the description. [Fox, “Behemoth and Leviathan”](https://www.bsw.org/biblica/vol-93-2012/behemoth-and-leviathan/497/article-p262.html)

### Greek Job

**Tier: ancient translation and reinterpretation, approximately second century BCE.**

The Septuagint translates the name as **θηρία**, “beasts.” It also renders Job 40:19 as a creature made “to be played with by his angels,” a statement absent in that form from the Masoretic Hebrew. At 40:20 it places four-footed animals “in Tartarus,” another conspicuous interpretive feature. Greek Job therefore witnesses an early transformation: the singular Hebrew super-beast becomes grammatically unstable between collective animals and an individual monster, while angels and Tartarus enter the scene. [Greek text and English rendering](https://greekdoc.com/DOCUMENTS/lxx-eng/job40.html)

### Dead Sea Scrolls

**Tier: securely attested manuscript transmission.**

Two Aramaic translations of Job were found at Qumran: 4Q157 and the much more substantial **11Q10/11QtgJob**, discovered in Cave 11 in 1956. The latter, copied in a Herodian hand, preserves material from Job 17:14 through 42:11 and is therefore the oldest physical witness extending into Behemoth’s portion of the book, though damaged and incomplete. Its critical edition appears in *Discoveries in the Judaean Desert* XXIII. [11Q10 corpus and edition information](https://tianmu.org/good-work-library/judean/dead-sea-scrolls/fragments/job-targum)

### 1 Enoch 60:7–10, 24

**Tier: Second Temple Jewish apocalyptic elaboration.**

The Book of Parables or Similitudes, 1 Enoch 37–71, is commonly placed near the turn of the era, but remains debated because this Enochic section is absent from the Qumran Enoch manuscripts and survives fully only in Geʽez copies. Chapter 60 describes:

- a **female** monster named Leviathan dwelling in the watery abyss;
- a **male** named Behemoth occupying an invisible desert called **Dundayin/Dunodayin**, east of Eden;
- the two separated on “that day”;
- both monsters being preserved for an eschatological purpose;
- in verse 24, the two becoming food prepared according to God’s greatness for the elect and righteous.

The narrative voice shifts anomalously between Enoch and Noah, one sign of textual layering. [1 Enoch 60 in Charlesworth’s *Old Testament Pseudepigrapha*](https://www.rexresearch1.com/DeadSeaScrolls/OldTestamentPseudepigrapha1Charlesworth.pdf)

### 4 Ezra 6:47–52

**Tier: later canonical/apocalyptic elaboration, approximately 100 CE.**

Known variously as 4 Ezra and, within Latin biblical numbering, 2 Esdras 3–14, the Jewish apocalypse was written after the destruction of Jerusalem in 70 CE. God tells Ezra that on the fifth day he preserved two creatures:

- Leviathan received the watery seventh part of the world.
- Behemoth received the land dried on the third day.
- Behemoth’s territory contains a thousand mountains.
- The sea could not contain both creatures.

Unlike 2 Baruch, the immediate passage does not explicitly say that humans will eat them, although it participates in the same developing eschatological complex.

### 2 Baruch 29:3–8

**Tier: later Jewish apocalyptic elaboration, late first or early second century CE.**

In the messianic age Behemoth will emerge from its place and Leviathan from the sea. God created the two great monsters on the fifth day and preserved them until that time; they will then become food “for all who are left.” The passage adds miraculous vines and inexhaustible agricultural abundance, making the monster-meal part of a transformed creation rather than an isolated curiosity. The complete work survives in a sixth- or seventh-century Syriac manuscript discovered and published by Antonio Ceriani in the nineteenth century. [2 Baruch 29](https://www.biblicalaudio.com/text/baruch2.pdf)

### Rabbinic and midrashic corpus

**Tier: late-antique and early-medieval learned elaboration.**

- **Leviticus Rabbah 13:3**, usually dated around the fifth century CE, says Behemoth and Leviathan will stage an animal combat before the righteous. Behemoth gores with its horns; Leviathan attacks with its fins. God then permits the otherwise irregular slaughter as a special eschatological dispensation. [Vayikra Rabbah 13](https://www.sefaria.org/Vayikra_Rabbah.13)
- **Babylonian Talmud, Bava Batra 74b–75a**, compiled in late antiquity, concentrates mainly on Leviathan’s destruction, flesh, and skin but places Behemoth within the paired eschatological-monster tradition. [Bava Batra 74b](https://www.halakhah.com/bababathra/bababathra_74.html)
- **Midrash Tanchuma** interprets Psalm 50:10 as Behemoth lying upon or feeding from a thousand mountains, whose vegetation renews.
- **Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer**, normally dated to the eighth or ninth century, says Behemoth was brought from the earth on the sixth day, consumes the produce of a thousand mountains each day, drinks the Jordan, and awaits sacrifice for the righteous. [Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer 3–11](https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_DeRabbi_Eliezer.3-11)
- Later compilations place Behemoth with Leviathan and the giant bird Ziz as the land, sea, and air provisions of the final banquet.

### Corpora named in the brief that do not securely attest Behemoth

**Absence of evidence is material here.**

- **Jubilees, Tobit, and the New Testament:** no named Behemoth.
- **Revelation 13:** the beast from the earth is sometimes interpreted by modern scholars as structurally recalling Behemoth opposite the sea-beast, but Revelation never names Behemoth. This is intertextual reconstruction, not an attested identity.
- **Nag Hammadi codices; Irenaeus, Hippolytus, and Epiphanius:** no securely identified Gnostic aeon, archon, angel, or demon called Behemoth. Behemoth consequently does not belong to the reconstructable ancient “Gnostic pantheon.” The Coptic Gnostic corpus is searchable through the scholarly Coptic Gnostic Library and public Nag Hammadi indices. [Coptic Gnostic Library Online](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/cglo/) [Nag Hammadi codex index](https://gnosis.org/naghamm/nhlcodex.html)
- **Pseudo-Dionysius and Aquinas:** neither assigns Behemoth a place in the nine angelic orders. General angelological doctrine in these writers cannot be retroactively applied to it.
- **Qur’an, canonical hadith, and al-Ṭabarī’s major scriptural exegesis:** no Joban Behemoth under that name.
- **Hekhalot literature, Sefer Raziel, and the Zohar:** Behemoth is not a stable named angel or operative spirit in their standard systems. Later references to the rabbinic monster should not be converted into a kabbalistic angel.
- **Lemegeton/Ars Goetia and the Abramelin tradition:** Behemoth is not one of the seventy-two Goetic spirits and has no traditional Goetic seal. Internet “Behemoth sigils” are modern.
- **Milton and Dante:** neither makes the named Behemoth an acting infernal character. Their cosmologies belong to its broad reception environment, not its direct textual dossier.

## 3. Domain, Attributes, and Iconography

### Domains and powers

Behemoth has no securely attested cultic domain. Its functions change by textual period:

1. **Job:** embodiment of immense, untamable terrestrial vitality under God’s control.
2. **Jewish apocalypse:** primordial terrestrial counterpart of Leviathan, spatially separated and preserved for the end.
3. **Rabbinic literature:** prodigious consumer of vegetation, combatant, sacrificial animal, and food of the righteous.
4. **Christian visual tradition:** emblem of divine power, material bulk, or the terrestrial half of the Behemoth–Leviathan pair.
5. **Early-modern political language:** metaphor for civil disorder or a gigantic political body.
6. **European demonography:** gluttony, bodily excess, infernal feasting, and cup-bearing.
7. **Modern English:** any enormous and powerful object, institution, animal, or machine.

### Physical form

Job supplies features rather than an unambiguous species:

- quadrupedal and herbivorous;
- immensely muscular loins and abdomen;
- powerful “tail” compared with a cedar;
- bronze-like bones and iron-like limbs;
- river or marsh habitat;
- mountain pasture;
- uncapturable by ordinary human techniques.

No horns occur in Job. Horns belong to the rabbinic combat tradition and strengthen the bovine form. The later hippopotamus image foregrounds marsh and river details; the mythic-bull interpretation foregrounds “cattle,” mountains, horns, and Ugaritic comparison.

### Hippopotamus, elephant, bull, or composite monster?

**Hippopotamus:** Since Samuel Bochart’s seventeenth-century *Hierozoicon*, this has been the dominant zoological identification. Diet, bulk, marsh vegetation, and indifference to a flooding river fit. The small hippo tail does not readily fit “like a cedar,” although interpreters variously read “tail” euphemistically, understand the verb as stiffening, or treat the cedar comparison as movement rather than dimensions.

**Elephant:** Common in some premodern Christian commentary and visual art because of bulk, herbivory, and the name’s supposed collective magnitude. River and reed imagery fits less well.

**Wild or mythic bull:** The Hebrew name’s connection with cattle, the mountain pasture, later horns, and the proposed Ugaritic parallel with El’s calf ʿAtik make a bovine chaos-monster plausible. This remains a reconstruction, not a translation demanded by the text.

**Composite supernatural monster:** The impossibility of capture, superlative place in creation, and literary pairing with Leviathan support reading Behemoth as a mythic being whose description incorporates natural animals without reducing to a zoological field note.

**Dinosaur:** This is a modern young-earth creationist interpretation, especially involving sauropods and the cedar-like tail. Answers in Genesis and the Institute for Creation Research explicitly defend it. It is not found in ancient Jewish or Christian interpretation, predates no nineteenth-century discovery of dinosaurs, and depends on rejecting mainstream geological and evolutionary chronology. [Answers in Genesis presentation](https://answersingenesis.org/dinosaurs/scholars-mystery-of-behemoth/) [Institute for Creation Research presentation](https://www.icr.org/content/do-dinosaurs-disprove-bible)

### Iconography

No securely identified ancient Israelite, Second Temple, or rabbinic image bears an inscription establishing it as Behemoth. Ancient Egyptian hippopotamus images may illuminate the animal repertoire behind Job but are not images “of Behemoth.”

Important later depictions include:

- **Medieval biblical manuscripts:** Behemoth is variously rendered as ox, elephant, hippopotamus, or composite land monster, usually opposite a serpentine Leviathan.
- **William Blake, *Behemoth and Leviathan*, plate 15 of *Illustrations of the Book of Job*:** Blake’s watercolor cycle began in 1805–1810; the engraved series was published in 1826. Behemoth occupies the terrestrial upper part of a circular abyss, while the serpentine female Leviathan coils below, beneath God, angels, Job, and his companions. [Morgan Library watercolor and dating](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/William-Blakes-World/32) [National Gallery of Victoria engraving, 1823–1826](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/explore/collection/work/27587/)
- **Louis Le Breton’s image for the 1863 *Dictionnaire infernal*:** a pot-bellied, upright, elephant-headed demon holding its abdomen. This image decisively shaped the modern occult Behemoth but illustrates Collin de Plancy’s demon, not Job’s securely recoverable morphology. [Le Breton illustration](https://www.oldbookillustrations.com/illustrations/behemoth/)

No ancient source assigns Behemoth a color, number, weekday, planet, metal, ritual incense, or sigil. Modern lists doing so are recent occult correspondences.

## 4. Genealogy and Relations to Other Divine Beings

### Origin and family

Behemoth has no parents, consort, offspring, or siblings in Job. It is directly made by God. The phrase “whom I made with/as you” aligns it with created humanity without making Job its brother.

In 1 Enoch, Behemoth is male and Leviathan female, but the text calls them paired monsters rather than husband and wife. Later language describing them as spouses or sexual partners is interpretive embellishment. They are primordial counterparts divided by habitat.

4 Ezra says both were formed on the fifth day. Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer instead brings Leviathan from the water on the fifth day but Behemoth from the earth on the sixth. These are genuinely competing chronologies:

- **Fifth-day Behemoth:** 4 Ezra 6; 2 Baruch 29.
- **Sixth-day Behemoth:** Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer.
- **No creation-day specification:** Job.

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### The divine demonstration to Job

**Early attested core.**

YHWH’s second speech challenges Job to assume divine majesty and govern the proud and wicked. Behemoth then becomes the first extended demonstration of a creature beyond Job’s command. It is not fighting God; it calmly eats, rests, and withstands the river. Its very peacefulness intensifies the point: even an herbivore within creation possesses power that renders human pretensions absurd.

Two broad readings compete:

- **Zoological reading:** Behemoth is a rhetorically magnified hippopotamus, elephant, buffalo, or other great animal.
- **Chaos-monster reading:** Behemoth and Leviathan are terrestrial and aquatic embodiments of chaos whose subordination reveals divine sovereignty.
- **Mixed reading:** Job deliberately describes recognizable animals with mythic diction. This best explains why natural and supernatural cues coexist.

Michael Fox observes that the mythic pairing does not require Behemoth to be morally evil. God’s mastery, rather than a simple good-versus-evil combat, controls the scene.

### Separation of the two primordial monsters

**Second Temple elaboration.**

1 Enoch 60 supplies the first explicit sexing and spatial division: female Leviathan occupies the abyss over the springs; male Behemoth occupies Dundayin, an invisible waste east of Eden. 4 Ezra rationalizes the separation cosmographically: the watery portion could not hold them both, so Behemoth received the land.

The texts conflict about geography:

- **Job:** marshes, lotuses, Jordan, mountains.
- **1 Enoch:** invisible desert east of Eden.
- **4 Ezra:** the dry portion of creation with a thousand mountains.
- **Rabbinic tradition:** the thousand mountains themselves, with the Jordan supplying drink.

They should not be harmonized into a single map.

### The eschatological feast

**Second Temple origin; rabbinic expansion.**

1 Enoch 60:24 and 2 Baruch 29 treat Behemoth and Leviathan as food preserved for the elect or messianic survivors. The feast reverses the present human inability to capture them: God, not human hunting skill, delivers primordial abundance.

Andrei Orlov interprets the meal as more than grotesque spectacle. In later Jewish developments the monsters’ consumption can reveal an eschatological Torah or transformed legal order: the righteous eat animals whose mode of slaughter appears irregular by ordinary law because God authorizes the event. [Orlov, “Eschatological Consumption of Leviathan and Behemoth”](https://epublications.marquette.edu/theo_fac/918/)

### The combat of Behemoth and Leviathan

**Rabbinic elaboration, not explicit in Job or 1 Enoch.**

Leviticus Rabbah 13:3 stages a public contest before the righteous. Behemoth strikes Leviathan with its horns; Leviathan attacks with its fins. Their mutual slaughter prepares the banquet. This transforms the older spatial pair into eschatological opponents and provides a narrative mechanism for killing both.

Later art frequently treats the conflict as primordial, but its securely attested narrative form is late antique. Job merely places their descriptions consecutively.

### The thousand mountains

Psalm 50:10 ordinarily means that all cattle on a thousand hills belong to God. Rabbinic wordplay reads the plural *bəhēmōṯ* as the singular monster’s name. Behemoth consumes the vegetation of a thousand mountains every day; it regrows overnight. The Jordan flows into its mouth. The motif is therefore an exegetical expansion built from Psalm 50 and Job 40, not an independent ancient cult myth. [Psalm 50:10 with midrash](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.50.10?lang=bi&with=Midrash)

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

There is **no archaeological, epigraphic, or literary evidence for an ancient cult of Behemoth**.

Specifically, no securely identified evidence documents:

- temples or shrines;
- priests or priestesses;
- festivals or calendar days;
- sacrifices made to Behemoth;
- votive dedications;
- theophoric personal names invoking it;
- oracles or pilgrimage sites;
- sacred prostitution;
- human sacrifice;
- a political state cult;
- ritual suppression or forced conversion of worshippers.

Behemoth is a creature of God within Jewish texts, not a rival deity receiving worship. Its own future killing is food preparation or divine sacrifice, not evidence of sacrifices offered **to** it.

Modern occult practitioners may “work with,” invoke, or venerate a being called Behemoth, but this is not continuity from an ancient cult. Available evidence points to modern demonolatry built from scripture, demon dictionaries, fantasy, and personal revelation. No reliable population statistics establish its scale.

## 7. Historical Evolution of the Figure

### I. Hebrew poetic stage: approximately sixth–fourth centuries BCE

A singular super-beast appears in Job. It is overwhelmingly powerful but created and controlled by YHWH. The text remains ambiguous between animal and mythic monster.

### II. Greek and Aramaic transmission: approximately third century BCE–first century CE

Greek Job translates the name as “beasts,” adds angels and Tartarus, and thus preserves an interpretation different from later proper-name traditions. The Qumran Job targums show that the book, including its final divine speeches, circulated in Aramaic Jewish communities before the rabbinic period.

### III. Second Temple and post-70 Jewish apocalypse: approximately first century BCE–early second century CE

1 Enoch explicitly pairs, sexes, and separates Behemoth and Leviathan. 4 Ezra gives them cosmographic territories. 2 Baruch reserves them for a messianic feast. This is the decisive phase in which Job’s individual marvel becomes an eschatological actor.

### IV. Rabbinic development: approximately fifth–ninth centuries CE

Midrash and Talmud expand the creature’s scale, food supply, combat, slaughter, and banquet. The “thousand mountains” arise through close exegesis of Psalm 50:10. Horns and combat produce a more recognizably bovine monster.

### V. Medieval Jewish and Christian reception

Jewish compilations increasingly coordinate Behemoth, Leviathan, and Ziz into land-sea-sky triads. Christian Bibles and commentaries preserve the Vulgate proper name while debating elephant, hippopotamus, and allegory. Manuscript artists supply forms the text itself does not fix.

There is still no stable placement in scholastic angelology. Pseudo-Dionysius and Aquinas discuss angels and demons extensively without turning Behemoth into a named order-member.

### VI. Islamic cosmographic transformation

The Qur’an and canonical hadith do not name Behemoth. Medieval Arabic cosmographies, especially traditions associated with al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qazwīnī, and al-Damīrī, contain **Bahamūt/Balhūt**, an immense fish or whale beneath the cosmic bull and supporting structures of the earth. The Arabic *ḥūt* means fish or whale.

The name may descend from Behemoth through Jewish–Islamic transmission, but form and function have been radically reassigned: biblical Behemoth is terrestrial, whereas Bahamūt is aquatic. Some scholarship proposes that the names or properties of Behemoth and Leviathan were crossed during transmission. This remains a historical derivation hypothesis, not proof that the Qur’an teaches Behemoth. [Summary of Arabic sources and the transmission problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamut)

### VII. Political metaphor: Hobbes

Thomas Hobbes wrote *Behemoth, or The Long Parliament* around 1668 as a dialogue on the causes and conduct of the English Civil War. Charles II discouraged publication; unauthorized versions circulated in the 1670s, and authorized publication followed after Hobbes’s death in 1681–1682.

The title complements Hobbes’s *Leviathan* but reverses the political valence. *Leviathan* names the huge artificial person of the commonwealth; *Behemoth* evokes the massive, unruly body of rebellion and civil war. Hobbes does not revive a cult or demonology. [Hobbes publication history and text](https://oll.libertyfund.org/title/hobbes-the-english-works-vol-vi-dialogue-behemoth-rhetoric)

### VIII. Demonographic reinvention: seventeenth–nineteenth centuries

Johann Weyer’s sixteenth-century demon catalogue did not include Behemoth among its standard infernal spirits. Later demonographers nevertheless assimilated it to Satan, infernal court hierarchy, appetite, and bodily excess.

Jacques Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal*, first issued in 1818 and repeatedly revised through 1863, consolidated Behemoth as:

- a heavy or stupid demon;
- associated with gluttony and sensual pleasure;
- a singer or feast official;
- chief cupbearer of hell;
- sometimes the devil’s night watchman.

Louis Le Breton’s elephant-headed image in the illustrated 1863 edition gave this reinvention its enduring visual form. [1863 French primary source index and entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Lettre_B)

### IX. Twentieth- and twenty-first-century pluralization

The name now supports several largely independent figures:

- Joban or rabbinic land-monster;
- young-earth creationist dinosaur;
- infernal gluttony demon;
- Arabic-derived fantasy Bahamut;
- enormous black cat from Bulgakov;
- political or technological metaphor;
- entity in games and modern demonolatry.

These should not be merged into a single timeless tradition.

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Bulgakov’s cat

Mikhail Bulgakov’s *The Master and Margarita*, written and repeatedly revised between 1928 and his death in 1940, was first published in censored form in 1966–1967. Its **Бегемот** is an enormous black demonic cat in Woland’s entourage: loquacious, violent, theatrical, fond of vodka, pistols, chess, and grotesque comedy, and capable of appearing in humanoid form.

The name works through Russian polysemy: *begemot* means hippopotamus while also evoking the biblical monster. Bulgakov’s character is not derived from any ancient cat-form of Behemoth; the feline body is a modern literary invention. [Novel and character history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Master_and_Margarita)

### Political and social metaphor

After Hobbes, “behemoth” became a reusable name for massive states, bureaucracies, militaries, corporations, machines, and institutions. Franz Neumann’s *Behemoth: The Structure and Practice of National Socialism* (1942) notably used it for the destructive, lawless organization of Nazi Germany, implicitly contrasting it with the ordered sovereign state of Hobbes’s *Leviathan*.

### Creationism and dinosaurs

Young-earth creationist publications identify Behemoth as a sauropod or another dinosaur living contemporaneously with humans. The chief argument is the cedar-like tail; counterarguments include:

- *zānāb* need not describe tail dimensions;
- the passage uses poetic anatomical comparisons;
- the creature’s river and reed habitat has long prompted a hippopotamus identification;
- no premodern interpreter had access to the modern category Dinosauria;
- Job supplies no diagnostic skeletal anatomy allowing assignment to a dinosaur clade.

This is a documented modern interpretation, not an ancient tradition suppressed by modern scholarship.

### Occultism and demonolatry

Behemoth never acquired the stable ritual dossier possessed by Goetic spirits: there is no agreed ancient seal, office, conjuration, planetary correspondence, or manuscript invocation. Modern sources sometimes call it an earth elemental, prince of gluttony, demon of indulgence, Satanic mask, divinatory spirit, or chthonic dragon. These systems generally descend from Collin de Plancy, twentieth-century occult compilations, fantasy culture, or individual practitioners.

A modern source assigning Behemoth Ouija boards, runes, tarot, crystals, a skeletal-dragon form, or a particular enn is documenting a new religious practice, not recovering lost biblical ritual.

### Popular culture

Materially influential appearances include:

- William Blake’s Job illustrations, fixing the paired terrestrial/aquatic composition.
- Eugène Lourié’s film *The Giant Behemoth* (1959), which uses the name for a radioactive prehistoric monster rather than the Joban form.
- Tabletop and electronic games, especially fantasy traditions influenced by the Arabic Bahamut.
- The Polish extreme-metal band **Behemoth**, founded in 1991, whose later work uses Satanic, Thelemic, and anti-Christian imagery. The band name is reception, not evidence of historic worship.
- Numerous comics, anime, role-playing games, and monster franchises that treat “Behemoth” as a species-name for a gigantic terrestrial creature.

No evidence establishes a continuous Behemoth cult from antiquity to these receptions.

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Principal debates

#### Natural animal versus mythic monster

The older zoological tradition seeks one species. Recent literary study more often asks why Job uses naturalistic details within a mythically intensified divine speech. The most defensible conclusion is not that one side has definitively “solved” the animal, but that Job’s Behemoth exceeds ordinary zoological description while remaining grounded in recognizable large herbivores.

#### Hippopotamus versus mythic bull

Hippopotamus explains the river habitat; bull imagery explains the Hebrew name, mountain pasture, later horns, and proposed Ugaritic background. A composite literary construction can account for both sets of data without positing a biologically coherent unknown species.

#### Ugaritic antecedent

The proposed chain is:

**Lotan + ʿAtik/Arš in Ugaritic poetry → Leviathan + Behemoth in Job.**

Lotan–Leviathan is linguistically strong; ʿAtik/Arš–Behemoth is inferential. It should be cited as a comparativist hypothesis, not translated into a lost Canaanite “Book of Behemoth.”

#### Textual sex and pairing

Job gives Behemoth no sex-marked partner narrative. 1 Enoch makes Behemoth male and Leviathan female. Later interpreters sometimes infer marriage, but neither Job nor 1 Enoch calls them spouses.

#### Date of 1 Enoch’s Parables

The absence of 1 Enoch 37–71 from the Qumran Enoch manuscripts once encouraged late, even Christian, dating. Much contemporary scholarship treats the Parables as Jewish and locates them near the turn of the era, but the exact date remains unsettled. Consequently, 1 Enoch 60 should not be dated to a precise year.

#### Penis or tail in Job 40:17

Some scholars read the conspicuous “tail” and thigh language as sexual display or euphemism, especially because *zānāb* can carry phallic implications in some contexts. Others retain a literal tail. Laura Quick emphasizes the passage’s structured presentation of competing masculine bodies and the erotic/body-description technique comparable to *waṣf*. This is an interpretive debate, not a textual variant. [Quick, “Behemoth’s Penis, Yahweh’s Might”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03090892211040537)

### State of the evidence

- **Hebrew Job:** complete medieval Masoretic witnesses, principally Aleppo and Leningrad codices, supplemented by ancient translations and fragments.
- **Greek Job:** major fourth- and fifth-century biblical codices; the translation itself is older.
- **11QtgJob:** Israel Antiquities Authority/Judean Desert manuscript corpus; critically edited in DJD XXIII.
- **1 Enoch:** complete Geʽez manuscripts, partial Greek and Aramaic witnesses for other sections; no Qumran witness to the Parables.
- **4 Ezra:** lost in its probable original Hebrew; transmitted in Latin and several eastern versions.
- **2 Baruch:** chiefly preserved in Syriac, with minor Greek evidence.
- **Rabbinic sources:** manuscript and printed transmission with the normal textual problems of midrashic compilation.
- **Medieval art:** dispersed through museum and manuscript collections.
- **Demonographic material:** printed books, especially editions of Collin de Plancy.

### Frequently repeated but unsupported claims

- “Behemoth was an ancient god worshipped in Canaan” — not established.
- “Behemoth is a Gnostic archon” — not attested in the surviving Gnostic corpus.
- “Behemoth is a fallen angel in Pseudo-Dionysius or Aquinas” — false.
- “Behemoth is one of the seventy-two Goetic demons” — false.
- “An ancient Behemoth sigil survives” — no evidence.
- “Behemoth and Leviathan were always husband and wife” — later harmonization.
- “The Hebrew unquestionably means hippopotamus” — stronger than the linguistic evidence.
- “The Hebrew unquestionably describes a sauropod” — modern creationist interpretation.
- “Arabic Bahamut is simply the same being unchanged” — ignores the land-to-sea transformation.
- “Behemoth had an ancient sacrificial or human-sacrificial cult” — no evidence.

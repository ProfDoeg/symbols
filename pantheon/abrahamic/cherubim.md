# The Cherubim: Research Dossier

**Class:** Collective class of numinous throne-bearers, guardians, and living heavenly beings in the Hebrew Bible; subsequently classified as an angelic order in Jewish, Christian, Islamic, and esoteric systems. They are not originally individual, named “angels” in the ordinary messenger sense, and they never form an independently worshipped deity or demon.

**Earliest secure attestation:** The Hebrew plural **כְּרוּבִים** (*kərūḇîm*, “cherubim”; singular **כְּרוּב**, *kərūḇ*) occurs in biblical traditions concerning the Jerusalem cult and Ark. The oldest potentially pre-exilic formulations are the divine title “YHWH of hosts, enthroned upon the cherubim” in 1 Samuel 4:4 and 2 Samuel 6:2 and the cherub-throne imagery of 2 Samuel 22:11/Psalm 18:10. Their surviving textual witnesses are much later: fragments among the Dead Sea Scrolls and medieval Masoretic manuscripts. No securely identified Iron Age inscription names a *kərūḇ*. Archaeology supplies analogous Levantine winged sphinxes and composite throne-supporters, not labeled specimens. Consequently, the earliest *datable tradition* may reach the monarchic period, but the earliest surviving physical attestations of the word are Second Temple manuscripts.

**Evidentiary labels used below:** **[Early attested]** Hebrew-biblical or closely associated Iron Age/exilic tradition; **[Later canonical]** later scriptural or canonical reception; **[Regional variant]** a tradition particular to a community or textual recension; **[Learned systematization]** an ordered theological or philosophical scheme; **[Syncretistic identification]** equation with another class, symbol, or being; **[Medieval/early-modern accretion]** post-antique elaboration; **[Folklore]** popular traditional material whose textual pedigree is secondary; **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]** an academic inference rather than an ancient identification; **[Modern invention]** a recent claim presented without adequate ancient support.

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### Core identity

In the Hebrew Bible, cherubim are several related but not fully harmonized things:

- supernatural guardians stationed at Eden’s eastern boundary (Genesis 3:24);
- paired golden figures over the Ark’s **כַּפֹּרֶת** (*kappōret*, traditionally “mercy seat,” more literally the Ark-cover);
- monumental olivewood figures in Solomon’s inner sanctuary;
- the living supports or vehicle of YHWH’s throne;
- the four multi-faced **חַיּוֹת** (*ḥayyôt*, “living creatures”) of Ezekiel 1, explicitly identified as cherubim in Ezekiel 10;
- a singular Edenic “cherub” in the difficult text of Ezekiel 28.

These forms share proximity to divine presence, sacred boundaries, or the heavenly throne, but the texts do not supply one uniform anatomy. Treating them all as a biological “species” with an invariant body is later harmonization. A major modern study concludes that Ezekiel’s four cherubim adapt Jerusalem Temple imagery but innovate by multiplying faces, wings, and directional symmetry; ancient Near Eastern griffins, sphinxes, *lamassu*, and other composite beings provide comparanda, not exact equivalents. [Oxford’s study](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A7e6701bd-90ae-4fa3-b269-79e1dad9f504) and [TheTorah’s iconographic review](https://www.thetorah.com/article/what-kind-of-creatures-are-the-cherubim) summarize the principal evidence.

### Etymology

No etymology is certain.

1. **Akkadian comparison.** Scholars have long compared Hebrew *kərūḇ* with Akkadian **kāribu/kurību**, terms connected with *karābu*, “to pray, bless,” and used of intercessory or protective figures. This remains plausible but does not prove that a Hebrew cherub was simply an Assyrian statue under another name. The sometimes-repeated Akkadian form **kirûbu** is especially precarious; the Chicago Assyrian Dictionary does not list it. **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]**

2. **Composite guardian comparison.** Winged sphinxes, griffins, *šēdu*, *lamassu*, and related figures guarded gateways or supported divine and royal thrones throughout Egypt, Syria-Palest, Anatolia, and Mesopotamia. Biblical cherubim belong to this visual and conceptual environment. “Cherub = lamassu,” however, is too exact: *lamassu* is a distinct Mesopotamian category and the sources never perform the equation. **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]**

3. **Rabbinic wordplay.** b. Sukkah 5b and b. Ḥagigah 13b construe **כרוב** as **כ־רביא**, “like a child/youth,” using Aramaic *ravyā*. This explains why rabbinic cherubim may have youthful faces, but it is a midrashic pun, not historical linguistics. **[Late-antique learned exegesis]**

4. **“Fullness of knowledge.”** Pseudo-Dionysius says that “Cherubim” signifies fullness or outpouring of knowledge. This is theological interpretation, not a defensible Semitic derivation. **[Learned systematization]**

5. **“Those near [God].”** Arabic **q-r-b**, “to be near,” encouraged Islamic and modern devotional explanations of *karūbiyyūn* as angels brought near to God. Hebrew *k-r-b* and Arabic *q-r-b* should not be casually treated as the same etymon. **[Medieval interpretation]**

### Titles and defining formulas

- **“YHWH of hosts, enthroned upon the cherubim”** — 1 Samuel 4:4; 2 Samuel 6:2; 2 Kings 19:15; Isaiah 37:16; compare Psalms 80:1 and 99:1. **[Early attested throne theology]**
- **“Cherubim of glory overshadowing the kappōret”** — Hebrews 9:5. **[Later canonical Christian recollection]**
- **“Anointed/covering/guardian cherub”** — Ezekiel 28:14–16, depending on textual reconstruction. **[Exilic text; disputed reading]**
- **“Holy cherubim”** — Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice, 4Q405. **[Second Temple liturgical development]**
- **“Streams/fullness of wisdom”** — Pseudo-Dionysius, *Celestial Hierarchy* VII. **[Late-antique Christian systematization]**
- **Kerubiel**, “Prince of the Cherubim” — 3 Enoch/Hekhalot tradition. This is an individual angel governing the class, not the collective’s original name. **[Late-antique/early-medieval mystical systematization]**

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### Earliest recoverable stratum

The Ark title in Samuel may preserve monarchic cult language: YHWH is imagined as enthroned above or riding upon paired winged beings. Psalm 18:10/2 Samuel 22:11 says that YHWH “rode upon a cherub and flew,” treating the cherub as a divine mount. Because Samuel and the Psalms underwent long redaction, an exact year cannot be assigned. The monumental Temple cherubim described in 1 Kings 6–8 are attributed narratively to Solomon’s tenth-century-BCE Temple, but Kings reached written and redactional forms centuries later. No excavated remains of Solomon’s sanctuary independently confirm those statues.

Genesis 3:24 belongs to the final Torah’s Eden narrative and probably reached its present form later than the earliest monarchic throne formulas. It cannot therefore be assumed to be the first historical conception simply because Genesis stands first in the canon.

### Hebrew Bible

**Ark and Tabernacle.** Exodus 25:17–22 orders two hammered-gold cherubim at the ends of the *kappōret*, wings spread above it and faces turned inward. There YHWH promises to meet Moses and speak “from between the two cherubim.” Exodus 26:1, 31; 36:8, 35 place woven cherubim on the Tabernacle curtains and veil; Exodus 37:7–9 narrates manufacture. Numbers 7:89 repeats the divine voice from above the Ark-cover. **[Priestly canonical construction]**

**Eden.** Genesis 3:24 says that after expelling “the human,” God stationed “the cherubim” east of Eden together with the flame of a self-turning sword to guard the way to the tree of life. The syntax does not say each cherub held a sword, gives no number, and supplies no anatomy. The familiar singular angel holding a flaming sword is later pictorial harmonization. **[Canonical Eden tradition]**

**Ark narratives and throne formula.** 1 Samuel 4:4, 2 Samuel 6:2, 1 Chronicles 13:6 and related passages connect the Ark with YHWH “enthroned upon the cherubim.” 2 Samuel 22:11/Psalm 18:10 gives the mobile storm-theophany: YHWH rides a cherub and flies upon the wind. **[Early attested / later canonically transmitted]**

**Solomon’s Temple.** 1 Kings 6:23–35 describes two olivewood cherubim, each ten cubits high and ten cubits from wingtip to wingtip, gold-covered and standing side by side across the inner sanctuary; carved cherubim also appear on walls and doors. In 1 Kings 8:6–7 their wings cover the Ark and its poles. 2 Chronicles 3:10–14 and 5:7–8 retell the installation. The cherubim are not idols receiving offerings; they mark the throne-space of the invisible deity. **[Monarchic claim preserved in later historiography]**

**Ezekiel.** Ezekiel 1:4–28 sees four *ḥayyôt*, each with human form, four faces—human, lion, ox, eagle—four wings, straight legs, calf-like feet, human hands, fire, and associated intersecting wheels full of eyes. Ezekiel 10 calls them cherubim, changes the ox face to a “cherub face,” describes coals taken from between them, and has them carry the divine glory out of the condemned Temple. Ezekiel 11:22–23 continues the departure. Ezekiel 41:18–25 gives two-faced cherubim—human and young lion—alternating with palms in the envisioned restored sanctuary. Ezekiel’s configurations are thus internally variable. **[Exilic visionary innovation]**

**Ezekiel 28.** A lament addressed to the king of Tyre depicts an exalted figure in Eden, on God’s holy mountain, associated with an “anointed guardian cherub” or possibly addressed as that cherub. The Masoretic Text, Septuagint, syntax, and vocalization permit competing readings. Its immediate target is Tyrian kingship; identifying the figure as Satan begins in Christian interpretation, notably Tertullian, and is not stated by Ezekiel. Modern work continues to dispute whether the king is himself the cherub, stands beside one, or is cast in an older divine-human Eden pattern. **[Early disputed text; Satanic identity is later accretion]**

### Septuagint and New Testament

The Septuagint normally transliterates rather than translates *kərūḇîm* as **χερούβ/χερουβίμ**, preserving the foreign character of the term. Greek Ezekiel has important differences from the Masoretic wording of Ezekiel 28, one reason reconstructions must remain provisional.

Hebrews 9:5 mentions “cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat” while describing the Tabernacle. It adds no anatomy and explicitly declines to discuss the details. Revelation 4:6–9 presents four living creatures—lion, ox, human, eagle—each with six wings and eyes; it never calls them cherubim. Christian exegesis fused Revelation’s creatures with Ezekiel’s cherubim and Isaiah’s six-winged seraphim. **[Later canonical synthesis, not New Testament nomenclature]**

### Second Temple Jewish literature

**1 Enoch.** This composite apocalypse was composed in stages from approximately the third century BCE through the first century CE; Aramaic portions survive at Qumran, while the complete work survives in Geʿez. In 1 Enoch 14 the fiery throne has wheels and a vision of cherubim. In 20:7 Gabriel is placed over Paradise, the serpents, and the cherubim. Later portions group cherubim with seraphim, ophanim, and other sleepless attendants of the divine throne. They are loyal heavenly beings, not Watchers and not fallen angels. **[Second Temple elaboration]**

**Jubilees.** Jubilees elaborates angelic creation, the angels of the presence and sanctification, and the Watchers, but it does not make cherubim a central independent order. Claims that Jubilees supplies a developed cherubic hierarchy overstate the evidence. **[Absence finding]**

**Tobit.** Tobit gives Raphael a major role but does not develop the cherubim. **[Absence finding]**

**Dead Sea Scrolls.** The *Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice* survive in Qumran copies 4Q400–407 and 11Q17 and a Masada copy. In the twelfth song, 4Q405 describes “holy cherubim,” luminous wheel-beings, and throne-chariots; the cherubim bow, bless, raise their wings, and participate in celestial liturgy. This is a transformation of Ezekiel’s throne vision into communal heavenly worship, not evidence of an earthly cherub cult. The Hebrew text and reconstruction are available in the [4Q405 transcription](https://theancientbible.org/4q405-p1/) and Carol Newsom’s [critical edition](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004369405/9789004369405_webready_content_text.pdf). **[Second Temple liturgical development]**

### Rabbinic and Jewish mystical corpora

**Babylonian Talmud.** b. Sukkah 5b and b. Ḥagigah 13b use the “like a youth” etymology. b. Yoma 54a–b and b. Bava Batra 99a discuss the Temple cherubim as miraculously facing or turning away from one another according to Israel’s relation with God; one tradition says they embraced, and that foreign conquerors exposed them. This erotic-temple symbolism is rabbinic interpretation, not recoverable evidence for the actual statues’ movement or sexual form. [Yoma 54b](https://www.sefaria.org/Yoma.54b?with=Rashi) preserves the received discussion.

**Midrash.** Different collections make cherubim intermediaries in heavenly communication, symbols of Israel’s intimacy with God, or youthful figures. Dates must be assigned to the compilations and their strata, not to the biblical episodes they retell. **[Late-antique/medieval elaboration]**

**Hekhalot and 3 Enoch.** Hekhalot literature, substantially late antique with medieval transmission, develops the throne world into ranks, palaces, seals, hymns, fiery beings, and angelic princes. 3 Enoch introduces **Kerubiel YHWH**, prince over the cherubim, and coordinates cherubim with *ḥayyôt* and *ophanim*. The surviving work’s final form is often placed around the fifth or sixth century, though it incorporates varied materials. **[Mystical systematization]**

**Maimonides.** In *Mishneh Torah*, *Foundations of the Torah* 2:7, Maimonides ranks ten classes: holy living creatures, ophanim, erelim, ḥashmallim, seraphim, angels, elohim, sons of elohim, cherubim, and ishim. Cherubim are therefore ninth, not second. He treats visible bodies as prophetic forms rather than angelic corporeality. The primary text is available through [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Foundations_of_the_Torah.1-6). **[Medieval philosophical systematization]**

**Zohar and later Kabbalah.** Kabbalistic lists differ from Maimonides and from one another. The Zohar connects cherubic and living-creature imagery with the chariot, divine attributes, and the supernal sanctuary; later tables assign chiefs and sephirotic correspondences. No single “Jewish hierarchy” is canonical across Judaism. **[Medieval regional/learned variants]**

**Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh.** This medieval compilation draws on Jewish magical, cosmological, and angelological materials but supplies no historically early cherub doctrine. Modern editions marketed as primordial angelic revelation should not be treated as ancient witnesses to biblical cherubim. **[Medieval compilation; primordial attribution is legendary]**

### Christian theological corpus

Pseudo-Dionysius’ *Celestial Hierarchy* (late fifth or early sixth century) created the most influential Christian arrangement: first triad, Seraphim–Cherubim–Thrones; second, Dominions–Virtues–Powers; third, Principalities–Archangels–Angels. Cherubim signify superabundant knowledge and receive divine illumination immediately. This hierarchy synthesizes biblical names that scripture itself never arranges into nine fixed orders. See [*Celestial Hierarchy* VII](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/dionysius/celestial.viii.html). **[Late-antique learned systematization]**

Thomas Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* I.108, preserves the Dionysian placement of cherubim in the highest triad, below seraphim and above thrones, distinguishing cherubic knowledge from seraphic love. He also accepts patristic readings of Ezekiel 28 in discussion of the devil’s original excellence. See [Aquinas I.108](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1108.htm). **[Medieval scholastic elaboration]**

### Islamic materials

The Qurʾan describes angels around and bearing the Throne, especially at 39:75, 40:7, and 69:17, but it never names a class *karūbiyyūn*. Nor does any sound, widely accepted prophetic hadith securely establish such an order. Medieval exegetes and cosmographers use **الكروبيون** for angels drawn near to God, chiefs of angels, or Throne-bearers. Reports differ and often have weak or non-prophetic chains. Even conservative Arabic source criticism acknowledges that no sound marfūʿ hadith securely defines them; see the Arabic assessment [“هل ثبت شيء في الملائكة الكروبيون؟”](https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/121038). Accordingly, Islamic *karūbiyyūn* are a medieval angelological reception or parallel, not proof that Qurʾanic Throne-bearers are biblical cherubim. **[Medieval learned/syncretistic identification]**

### Gnostic evidence

The Nag Hammadi tractate *Hypostasis of the Archons* (NHC II,4; Coptic manuscript fourth century, Greek composition generally placed in the second or third) says that the arrogant ruler constructs “a huge four-faced chariot of cherubim” with innumerable ministering angels. This appropriates Ezekiel’s vehicle for the ignorant lower ruler rather than treating cherubim as an autonomous Gnostic pantheon. See the [Bentley Layton translation](https://www.gnosis.org/naghamm/hypostas.html). **[Regional Gnostic reinterpretation]**

The surviving Nag Hammadi corpus does not present “The Cherubim” as a uniformly named aeon, archon, or independent divine faction. Irenaeus, Hippolytus, and Epiphanius report many Gnostic cosmologies, but no stable cross-school cherubic pantheon emerges. Irenaeus instead uses the four living creatures to defend the fourfold Gospel. Assertions that cherubim were a major universal Gnostic order are therefore unsupported. **[Absence finding]**

## 3. Domain, Attributes, and Iconography

### Functions

1. **Boundary guardians:** Eden’s inaccessible tree of life, Genesis 3:24.
2. **Sacred-space markers:** Tabernacle veil, Ark-cover, Temple walls and doors.
3. **Throne bearers or supports:** the repeated formula “enthroned upon the cherubim.”
4. **Divine vehicle:** Psalm 18 and Ezekiel’s mobile throne.
5. **Liturgical attendants:** 1 Enoch, Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice, Hekhalot and Christian heavenly liturgy.
6. **Bearers of knowledge:** Pseudo-Dionysian and scholastic Christianity.
7. **Cosmic guardians/equilibrators:** modern Hermetic and Golden Dawn systems.

### Physical forms

- **Ark figures:** winged, paired, inward-facing; otherwise undescribed.
- **Solomonic figures:** enormous winged images, ten cubits high, but with no specified heads or animal bodies.
- **Ezekiel 1:** four faces, four wings, hands, straight legs, calf hooves, fiery radiance.
- **Ezekiel 10:** same class, but “cherub” replaces “ox” in the face list.
- **Ezekiel 41:** two faces, human and young lion.
- **Rabbinic:** youthful or childlike human faces.
- **Byzantine and Orthodox art:** often multi-winged, many-eyed, sometimes only a face amid wings; frequently blended with the tetramorph.
- **Western medieval art:** an angelic order, sometimes winged heads or elaborate composite creatures.
- **Renaissance and Baroque:** commonly represented through *putti*, chubby winged children.
- **Modern “biblically accurate angel” imagery:** an internet-era composite of Ezekiel’s cherubim, ophanim, Isaiah’s seraphim, and Revelation’s living creatures. It is not one biblical description. **[Modern popular synthesis]**

### Wheels and eyes

Ezekiel’s **אוֹפַנִּים** (*ophannim*, wheels) accompany the living creatures and move with their spirit. The text relates them intimately but does not call the wheels cherubim. Later Jewish hierarchies often make *ophanim* a separate order; internet art frequently merges the categories. Eyes signify all-directional perception within Ezekiel’s vision but do not define every biblical cherub.

### The Ark and “mercy seat”

The cherubim do not sit on the Ark as decorative miniature angels in the modern sense. Their wings construct or mark the divine throne-space over the *kappōret*. Whether the invisible throne consisted of the figures, the space between them, or both remains debated. The Ark tradition supports no reliable reconstruction of their complete bodies.

### Mesopotamian and Levantine comparisons

Winged sphinx thrones from the Levant, ivory panels, guardian figures, and monumental *lamassu* illuminate the visual repertoire. They show that mixed human-animal guardians and throne supporters were normal royal and sacred symbols. They do not establish a one-to-one genealogy. The cautious formulation is: biblical cherubim are an Israelite/Judahite realization of a widespread ancient Near Eastern composite-guardian and throne-bearing tradition. **[Modern scholarly reconstruction]**

### The “putto degradation”

Calling the Renaissance change a “degradation” describes a loss of the biblical creature’s terrifying and royal complexity, but not a simple linear corruption.

The Italian **putto** derives principally from classical Eros/Cupid, child-genius, and funerary decorative traditions. Quattrocento artists such as Donatello revived playful *spiritelli* and putti; Christian art then used them as celestial attendants, and viewers increasingly called them cherubs. Raphael’s *Sistine Madonna* (1512–1513) made two wistful putti especially famous. A putto may be wingless; an *amorino* is Cupid-like; neither is automatically a theological cherub. The Getty’s [terminological history](https://www.getty.edu/news/putto-cherub-cupid-difference-art-definiton-terms-classical/) and the nineteenth-century German study [*Die Entwickelung des Putto*](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/weber1898/0013) document the classical-to-Renaissance development. **[Renaissance syncretistic iconography]**

No biblical or patristic text says cherubim are disembodied infant heads. That image is an artistic convention formed by the fusion of classical putti, youthful rabbinic/patristic interpretations, and Christian celestial decoration.

### Unsupported correspondences

No early source gives cherubim fixed sacred colors, weekdays, metals, planets, or zodiacal signs. Such tables belong to medieval or modern occult systems. The lion, bull/ox, human, and eagle are textually secure for Ezekiel; their assignment to fire, earth, air, water or fixed zodiacal signs is later.

## 4. Genealogy and Relations to Other Divine Beings

Cherubim have no parents, spouses, offspring, birth myth, or collective death in the Hebrew Bible. Their “origin” is functional: they appear already belonging to YHWH’s sacred sphere. Jubilees and later theology include angelic creation, but no canonical text narrates the cherubim’s separate generation.

They are not securely identical with seraphim, ophanim, *ḥayyôt*, archangels, Watchers, or *lamassu*. Ezekiel explicitly identifies his *ḥayyôt* with cherubim; the other equations are later or comparative.

Because most cherubim are collective and anonymous, the demonstrable list is short.

No secure Qurʾanic passage records a named prophet or angel interacting with *karūbiyyūn* under that title.

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### A. Guardians of Eden

**Core narrative:** God expels the human pair, stations plural cherubim east of Eden, and places the flame of a turning sword to guard the tree of life (Genesis 3:22–24). Their purpose is not punishment for its own sake but prevention of immortalization after the acquisition of knowledge.

**Variations:**

- Genesis gives neither number nor anatomy.
- Later images commonly make two angels, each with a sword, or one human angel holding the flaming sword. **[Later pictorial harmonization]**
- Ezekiel 28 supplies an Edenic cherub but in a lament about Tyrian kingship; whether it draws on the same Eden complex or another variant is disputed.
- Milton assigns the expulsion to Michael and a cohort of four-faced, many-eyed cherubim and turns “the flame of a sword” into a coordinated military watch. **[Early-modern elaboration]**

### B. The divine throne and Ark

In priestly tradition, paired golden cherubim form the place from which God addresses Moses. In royal and Zion traditions, YHWH is “enthroned upon” them. Solomon’s enormous figures fill the inner sanctuary and overshadow the Ark.

Scholars variously reconstruct:

- a throne with the cherubim as side supports;
- a winged throne above the Ark;
- an invisible throne whose seat is the empty space;
- protective figures flanking a symbolic footstool.

No archaeological recovery of the Ark or Temple figures decides between them. The empty central space accords with Israelite aniconic tendencies but should not be romanticized into total image rejection: the biblical sanctuary itself is described as filled with images of cherubim, palms, flowers, lions, and oxen.

### C. Ezekiel’s mobile throne

Ezekiel sees the storm-cloud manifestation by the Chebar canal. Four living beings support an expanse under the sapphire-like throne. Their wheels permit movement in four directions without turning. In chapters 9–11 the glory abandons the polluted Jerusalem Temple, with the cherubim operating as a mobile throne-chariot.

This is both continuity and transformation: temple throne imagery becomes a vehicle proving that Israel’s God is not trapped in Jerusalem and can appear in Babylonian exile. Ezekiel 10 retrospectively names the chapter 1 creatures cherubim. The wheels are coordinated beings, not “the cherubim themselves.” [Recent Oxford scholarship](https://academic.oup.com/jts/article/77/1/15/8482653) emphasizes Ezekiel’s reshaping of Temple symbolism.

### D. The fallen guardian cherub

Ezekiel 28 laments the king of Tyre through mythic imagery: perfection, Eden, precious stones, God’s mountain, blamelessness followed by violence and expulsion. Textual variants affect whether the king is a cherub or accompanied by one.

- **Historical-literary reading:** a human king is satirically cast as an Edenic/divine figure and expelled for pride or commercial violence.
- **Mythic-background reading:** the poem uses an older divine-being or primordial-human fall tradition.
- **Christian Satanic reading:** beginning clearly with Tertullian, the passage describes Satan’s primordial rank and fall.
- **Popular modern claim:** Satan was certainly “the chief cherub” and heavenly music director. The musical-office claim is not in the Hebrew text and depends on tendentious translations of Ezekiel 28:13. **[Modern invention]**

The scholarly debate over the Hebrew and the figure’s divinity is represented by Lydia Lee’s study, [“You Were the (Divine) Cherub”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0309089216628419).

### E. Heavenly liturgy

1 Enoch and the Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice transpose Temple imagery into heaven. Cherubim, wheels, and angelic priesthoods praise around the throne. Hekhalot mystics elaborate this into dangerous ascent through palaces guarded by angelic beings and seals. This is not one continuous ritual with demonstrable institutional succession; it is a related history of literary and mystical reuse.

### F. Four Gospels and four living creatures

Irenaeus argues that there must be four Gospels because the world has four zones/winds and the throne is borne by four living creatures. Later Christian convention assigns human/angel, lion, ox, and eagle to Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John, though allocations differed before stabilizing. Revelation never calls these figures cherubim; their cherubic identity is exegetical synthesis. **[Patristic systematization]**

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

### No independent cult

There is no securely documented cult *of* the cherubim: no cherub temple, priesthood, festival, sacrifice, oracle, pilgrimage, or theophoric naming tradition centered on them. Biblical law does not instruct worshippers to pray or sacrifice to them. They belong to YHWH’s throne and sanctuary imagery.

### Jerusalem Temple

The biblical record attributes monumental cherubim to Solomon’s Temple and places the Ark beneath their wings. Babylon destroyed the Temple in 586 BCE. No surviving excavation of the Temple Mount has recovered the inner sanctuary or its cherubim, so the literary descriptions cannot be checked directly.

Cherub motifs on walls, doors, and woven fabrics establish them as markers of graduated holiness. The Day of Atonement rite in Leviticus 16 is performed before the *kappōret*, but the sacrifice is directed to YHWH, not to the cherubim.

### Second Temple problem

The Second Temple lacked the recoverable Ark tradition of the First Temple; later Jewish sources remembered the Holy of Holies as Arkless. Rabbinic stories of the conquerors exposing embracing cherubim may preserve symbolic exegesis, memory of other decorations, or legendary Temple history. They are not straightforward archaeological testimony.

### Synagogues and Jewish art

Late-antique and medieval Jewish art could depict the Ark cherubim, Ezekiel’s vision, or chariot motifs, but the aniconic pressures of Exodus and Deuteronomy created interpretive tension. Rabbinic prohibition focused on making heavenly forms for worship; it did not erase literary contemplation of the chariot.

### Christian churches and liturgy

Cherubim became prominent in hymnody and church decoration:

- the Byzantine **Cherubic Hymn** identifies the worshipping congregation mystically with the cherubim as it receives the King of all;
- Eastern domes, apses, liturgical fans, and vestments carry many-eyed cherubic forms;
- Western churches use cherubic heads, tetramorphs, and putti.

These are liturgical representations of heavenly worship, not sacrifices to an independent being.

### Violence, sacrifice, and suppression

No source documents human sacrifice to cherubim, temple prostitution in their cult, or cherub-led religious violence. *Paradise Lost* places Moloch’s human sacrifice in contrast to YHWH “enthroned between the cherubim”; this is Miltonic polemic, not evidence of cherub worship. Cherub images could be destroyed when temples or churches were conquered or during iconoclastic movements, but no historically distinct “suppression of the cherubim cult” occurred because such a cult is not attested.

## 7. Historical Evolution

### Monarchic Israel and Judah

The earliest recoverable cherub traditions concern throne theology, the Ark, royal sanctuary, and storm-theophany. Composite guardians were reworked within YHWH worship. Cherubim were powerful sacred beings or images, not yet one choir within a universal taxonomy.

### Exile and Persian period

Ezekiel radically mobilized the throne and multiplied the creatures’ anatomical and cosmic features. Priestly Tabernacle traditions placed paired cherubim at the center of ideal sacred space. Genesis positioned them at the boundary of lost primordial sacred space. These traditions were canonically juxtaposed without complete harmonization.

### Hellenistic and Roman Judaism

Apocalyptic literature populated heaven more densely. 1 Enoch assigned Gabriel oversight involving the cherubim. The Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice transformed Ezekiel’s machinery into angelic liturgy. Greek translation preserved the foreign name.

### Rabbinic and Hekhalot eras

Rabbinic interpretation humanized their faces, made the Temple pair symbolize divine-Israelite intimacy, and debated their orientation. Hekhalot literature personified angelic governance through Kerubiel and elaborated the chariot-throne world.

### Patristic Christianity

Hebrews retained the Ark figures; Revelation supplied four living creatures; Irenaeus used them to authorize the four Gospels. Pseudo-Dionysius fixed Cherubim as the second choir of the first triad. Tertullian and successors read Ezekiel’s Edenic cherub as the pre-fall devil.

### Medieval Judaism, Christianity, and Islam

Maimonides ranked cherubim ninth and dematerialized angelic bodies philosophically. Kabbalistic systems produced different rankings. Christian scholasticism retained the Dionysian second place and linked cherubim with knowledge. Dante placed them in the eighth celestial sphere and contrasted true cherubic wisdom with fraudulent counsel.

Arabic *karūbiyyūn* emerged in commentary as angels near or around the Throne, but without Qurʾanic naming or sound hadith definition.

### Renaissance and Baroque transformation

Classical putti entered Christian sacred art as celestial attendants. The name “cherub” migrated onto the infant figure, eventually overwhelming biblical composite iconography in Western popular culture. This was iconographic fusion, not a rediscovered ancient description.

### Seventeenth to nineteenth centuries

Milton restored four-faced, many-eyed warrior cherubim to Eden while also permitting Satan to disguise himself as a beautiful young cherub. Assyriological discoveries encouraged comparisons with *lamassu*, *šēdu*, and *kāribu*. Some early comparisons became overly confident equations.

### Modern period

Academic scholarship separates textual strata, analyzes ancient Near Eastern iconography, and emphasizes the variation between Ark figures, Eden guardians, and Ezekiel’s creatures. Popular media simultaneously preserve:

- the winged baby;
- the “biblically accurate” many-eyed monster;
- the high angel of wisdom;
- the Golden Dawn’s four elemental Kerubim.

These are historically different receptions, not interchangeable ancient data.

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Dante

In the *Divine Comedy* (c. 1308–1321), cherubim embody knowledge and govern the eighth celestial sphere. The “black cherub” who claims Guido da Montefeltro for fraudulent counsel provides a fallen counterpart. Dante’s arrangement depends on medieval Christian hierarchy, not Ezekiel alone. **[Medieval literary reception]**

### Milton

In *Paradise Lost* (1667; revised 1674), cherubim are an armed heavenly order. Satan disguises himself as a young cherub before Uriel; Gabriel commands Edenic watches; Michael leads chosen cherubim to expel the human pair; four-faced, many-eyed beings take their stations with the flaming sword. The complete poem documents these uses in [Project Gutenberg’s text](https://mirror.csclub.uwaterloo.ca/gutenberg/2/26/26-h/26-h.htm). **[Early-modern synthesis and invention]**

### Grimoires

The *Lemegeton* or *Lesser Key of Solomon* is a seventeenth-century English compilation drawing on older magical materials. The *Book of Abramelin* survives in early-modern manuscripts and was popularized in the nineteenth century. Neither is an ancient Solomonic record. Cherubic names, directions, seals, or hierarchies appearing in later magical tables represent Christian-Kabbalistic reception, often mediated through Pseudo-Dionysius and Renaissance occultism. Claims that biblical cherubim taught the historical Solomon the extant grimoires are pseudepigraphic legend.

### Golden Dawn and successor traditions

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, founded in Britain in 1888, made four **Kerubim** guardians of the quarters and linked lion, eagle, human, and bull with fixed zodiacal signs, elements, letters of the divine name, and ritual stations. These correspondences combine Ezekiel, Revelation, astrology, Hermeticism, and Christian Kabbalah. Golden Dawn teaching explicitly calls the sphinx a synthesis of the Kerubim; the order’s account is preserved in [its modern exposition](https://hermeticgoldendawn.org/the-kerubim-the-strong-ones/). **[Modern occult synthesis]**

Tarot decks derived from Golden Dawn teaching place the four creatures around the Wheel of Fortune and World cards. Reading them as elemental “cherubim” is an occult convention, not an ancient Tarot tradition.

### New Age and popular angelology

Modern angel books assign cherubim colors, crystals, chakras, rays, healing functions, patronage, and named leaders. Unless a claim is traceable to a specific medieval or occult text, it should be classified as modern invention. Gustav Davidson’s *Dictionary of Angels* is a useful index of literary and magical names but frequently compiles incompatible sources and is not itself primary evidence.

### Literature, film, comics, and games

Fantasy media use “cherub” for:

- infant angels;
- high-ranking warrior angels;
- monstrous four-faced guardians;
- autonomous species;
- fallen or demonic cherubs.

Games often convert a theological order into a combat category. Internet “biblically accurate angel” memes since the late 2010s have materially revived Ezekiel’s eyes and wheels but usually fuse cherubim, ophanim, seraphim, and Revelation’s creatures. **[Modern composite invention]**

### Living religious reception

Cherubim remain active liturgical and theological symbols in Judaism and Christianity, especially in biblical reading, synagogue art, Eastern Christian hymnody, and Orthodox iconography. Islamic discourse occasionally uses *karūbiyyūn*, but its status remains extra-Qurʿanic and hadith-critical. Modern occult practitioners invoke Kerubic guardians, but reliable membership or practitioner numbers are unavailable.

The Nigerian Cherubim and Seraphim movement adopted the angelic classes in its institutional name during the 1920s. This is a modern African Christian appropriation, not survival of an ancient cherub cult.

No substantial organized “cherub reconstructionist” religion is documented.

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Main disputes

**Etymology:** Akkadian *kāribu/kurību* remains plausible, but many neat dictionary derivations are circular or rely on unattested forms.

**Body type:** Scholars variously reconstruct Ark and Temple cherubim as winged humans, winged lions with human heads, griffins, or sphinxes. The Bible gives wings but withholds enough anatomy to prevent certainty. Ezekiel should not automatically be projected backward onto the Ark.

**Throne or guardian:** These functions overlap. Composite beings can guard the sacred boundary while simultaneously supporting or forming a throne.

**Ezekiel’s innovation:** Some see Ezekiel 1 as a visionary description later identified with traditional cherubim in chapter 10; others understand both chapters as a coordinated composition drawing on Temple imagery. Redactional conclusions remain debated.

**Ezekiel 28:** Masoretic, Septuagintal, and reconstructed readings yield a king-as-cherub, king-with-cherub, primordial human, priestly figure, or divine pretender. The Satan reading is historically influential but exegetically later.

**The putto:** The winged baby is not simply a “wrong picture.” It is a traceable Renaissance fusion with classical child spirits and Christian heavenly attendance. It is wrong only when represented as Ezekiel’s own description.

**Islamic continuity:** Similar throne imagery does not prove that Qurʾanic Throne-bearers were historically called cherubim. The name belongs principally to later commentary.

**Gnostic status:** One Nag Hammadi text gives an archon a cherubic chariot, but this does not establish a universal Gnostic cherub pantheon.

### State of the evidence

- No labeled ancient Israelite cherub statue survives.
- No Ark or First Temple cherub has been excavated.
- The Hebrew biblical evidence survives in Second Temple fragments and medieval codices.
- 1 Enoch survives partially in Aramaic and Greek and completely in Geʿez.
- The Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice survive in fragmentary Qumran and Masada manuscripts.
- *Hypostasis of the Archons* survives in Coptic in Nag Hammadi Codex II.
- Hekhalot texts survive through complex medieval manuscript recensions.
- Pseudo-Dionysius survives in Greek manuscript tradition and translations.
- Renaissance and later cherubic art survives abundantly and is often better datable than the ancient iconographic prototypes.

### Influential modern works

T. N. D. Mettinger’s “Cherubim” in the *Dictionary of Deities and Demons in the Bible* remains an important concise study. Alice Wood’s *Of Wings and Wheels* and Mary J. Carruthers-type studies of Ezekiel’s imagery emphasize textual variety; Othmar Keel and Christoph Uehlinger situate Israelite imagery in its Levantine setting. Carol Newsom’s edition anchors study of the Sabbath Songs. Gershom Scholem, Peter Schäfer, and Rachel Elior represent differing approaches to Merkavah and Hekhalot mysticism. Gustav Davidson is a reception-history resource, not a critical substitute for primary sources.

Bamberger, Kelly, and Jeffrey Burton Russell matter chiefly for the later identification of the Ezekiel 28 cherub with Satan. Pearson and Kurt Rudolph illuminate Gnostic cosmology but do not justify turning the cherubim into a major Gnostic race.

### Discredited or unsupported claims still circulating

- “Cherub” literally and certainly means “powerful one.”  
- Cherubim are simply Babylonian *lamassu*.  
- Genesis says one angel carried a flaming sword.  
- Ezekiel’s wheels are themselves cherubim.  
- All cherubim have four faces and six wings.  
- Satan is explicitly called the chief cherub in the Hebrew Bible.  
- Satan directed heavenly music because Ezekiel mentions “pipes.”  
- Cherubim are baby angels in scripture.  
- The Qurʾan names the *karūbiyyūn*.  
- A sound prophetic hadith defines them as a specific order.  
- *Sefer Raziel*, *Abramelin*, or the *Lemegeton* is literally a Solomonic or antediluvian manuscript.  
- Golden Dawn elemental and zodiacal correspondences are ancient Israelite doctrine.  
- “Biblically accurate angels” form one coherent biblical anatomy.

## 11. Sources

### Primary-text base

The indispensable primary witnesses are Genesis 3; Exodus 25–26 and 36–37; Numbers 7; 1 Samuel 4; 2 Samuel 6 and 22; 1 Kings 6–8; 2 Kings 19; Psalms 18, 80, and 99; Isaiah 37; Ezekiel 1, 9–11, 28, and 41; 1 Chronicles 13 and 28; 2 Chronicles 3 and 5; Hebrews 9; Revelation 4; 1 Enoch 14 and 20; 4Q405 and related Sabbath Song manuscripts; b. Sukkah 5b, b. Ḥagigah 13b, b. Yoma 54a–b, and b. Bava Batra 99a; 3 Enoch; *Hypostasis of the Archons*; Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 3.11.8; Tertullian, *Against Marcion* 2.10; Pseudo-Dionysius, *Celestial Hierarchy* VI–VII; Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* I.108; and Milton, *Paradise Lost*.

The evidentiary conclusion is not a single portrait but a sequence of transformations: unnamed royal and sacred composite beings; Ark and Temple throne guardians; Eden boundary keepers; Ezekiel’s mobile, many-faced living chariot; Second Temple liturgical beings; rabbinic youths and symbols of intimacy; Hekhalot officers; Dionysian intelligences; an Islamic extra-scriptural parallel; Renaissance putti; Miltonic warriors; and modern occult or internet composites. “The Cherubim” are therefore best understood as a historically changing family of textual and visual constructions organized around guarded holiness, divine mobility, and proximity to the throne.

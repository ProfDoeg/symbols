# Aker: Research Dossier

**Class:** Masculine Egyptian *nṯr* (“god”), more specifically an earth and netherworld deity or personified cosmic region. The plural or collective *ꜣkrw* (“Akeru”) is textually ambiguous: in different passages it may denote multiple earth-powers, serpent-like beings, or an orthographic/full form of Aker’s name rather than a stable collective species.

**Earliest secure attestation:** The Pyramid Texts in the subterranean chambers of King Unas at Saqqara, late Fifth Dynasty, approximately 2350–2320 BCE. In PT §§324–325 Unas “traverses Aker”; in the Cannibal Hymn, PT §393, “the bones of Aker/Akeru tremble.” These passages establish an earth or subterranean power but not yet the complete later image of two lions labeled “Yesterday” and “Tomorrow.” The Unas corpus is the earliest surviving extensive Egyptian religious corpus, not necessarily the moment when the belief originated. [UCL Digital Egypt](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/literature/religious/pyramid.html), [Allen’s critical translation](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt14jxv34), [TLA lemma ꜣkr](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/852235?lang=en).

## Evidentiary labels

- **Securely attested early:** Old Kingdom Pyramid Text evidence, particularly passages preserved in a datable royal pyramid.
- **Later canonical elaboration:** Middle and New Kingdom funerary compositions that develop Aker’s cosmological functions.
- **Regional or object-specific variant:** A form confined to a particular monument, papyrus, coffin, or local recension.
- **Learned systematization:** Egyptian priestly glosses or theological equivalences, especially the commentaries attached to Book of the Dead Spell 17.
- **Syncretistic identification:** An explicit equation or iconographic overlap with another named deity.
- **Modern scholarly reconstruction:** A conclusion assembled by Egyptologists from dispersed texts and images.
- **Modern invention:** A recent assertion for which no ancient source has been demonstrated.

## 1. Identification, Names, and Epithets

### Basic identity

Aker is the earth conceived simultaneously as solid ground, the enclosing subterranean region, and the passage through which the sun and the transfigured dead travel. His role is therefore double. Earth can hold or seize the deceased, and early mortuary texts seek release from Aker; but Aker can also open his gates, contain dangerous beings, support the solar bark, and deliver the renewed sun from the eastern horizon. This ambivalence is already visible in the Pyramid Texts and becomes a major organizing principle of the New Kingdom underworld books.

Calling Aker simply “the god of the horizon” is convenient but imprecise. The ordinary word and hieroglyph for the horizon is *ꜣḫt*, represented by the sun between two mountain peaks. Aker is *ꜣkr*, an earth-god whose later double form could embody the western and eastern limits through which the sun enters and leaves the earth. The words are not interchangeable, even though artists deliberately combined Aker’s opposed heads or lions with the *ꜣḫt* sign.

### Name and writing

The conventional Egyptological form is **Aker**, transliterated **ꜣkr**. Egyptian hieroglyphic spellings employ the phonograms ꜣ–k–r and several determinatives:

- a strip or tract of earth;
- an earth form terminating in a human head;
- two outward-facing human or leonine protomes;
- a recumbent lion;
- the seated-god determinative.

Fernand Bisson de la Roque documented the variation from a single lion or earth-form to a double lion/sphinx and noted that later copies often replace the pictorial double form with an ordinary divine determinative. The graphic variation is evidence for changing visualization, not proof that every spelling refers to two separately named gods. [Bisson de la Roque, “Notes sur Aker”](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bifao_0255-0962_1931_num_30_1_1915?pageId=T2_576).

The form **Akeru**, conventionally transliterated **ꜣkrw**, requires caution.

1. **Traditional interpretation:** the final *-w* marks a plural, “the Akeru,” a group of earth gods or subterranean serpent-powers.
2. **Alternative modern reconstruction:** because Egyptian *w* can form part of a singular name and is sometimes inconsistently written, some apparent examples of “Akeru” may be fuller spellings of singular Aker. Sjef Willockx argues this explicitly, but it has not displaced the conventional plural analysis in standard editions.
3. **Contextual conclusion:** passages such as Coffin Text 474, referring to earth-gods who existed before Geb, favor a genuine collective in at least some contexts; other occurrences permit a singular reading. The forms should therefore not be normalized indiscriminately. [Willockx, *Two Ancient Egyptian Gods: Aah and Aker*](https://www.egyptology.nl/aah_aker.pdf), [SAOC study citing CT 474](https://gnosis.study/library/%D0%93%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B7%D0%B8%D1%81/%D0%98%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F/ENG/Practicing%20Gnosis.%20Ritual%2C%20Magic,%20Theurgy%20and%20Liturgy%20in%20Nag%20Hammadi,%20Manichaean%20and%20Other%20Ancient%20Literature.pdf).

### Etymology

**Unresolved.** Egyptian lexical sources identify *ꜣkr* as the divine name; some dictionaries also infer “earth” from passages in which Aker parallels *tꜣ*, “land/earth.” That semantic extension rests heavily on PT §§324–325 and is disputed: Aker may be the named personification traversed by the king rather than a common noun for earth. No secure derivation from a productive Egyptian root has achieved consensus.

Claims that Aker is cognate with Sumerian *engar*, Akkadian *ikkaru*, Hebrew *ʾikkār*, Greek Acheron, or unrelated modern names are unsupported by historical phonology. They are **modern folk etymologies**, not established ancient connections.

### Titles and descriptive formulas

Aker has fewer stable cult epithets than major temple gods. Texts instead describe functions:

- **Earth-god / earth itself:** modern lexical description grounded in the Pyramid and Coffin Texts.
- **Opener or possessor of earth’s doors:** PT §796 and related passages speak of Aker’s doors being opened for the king.
- **The double lion / two lions:** a graphic and theological form, but often properly called **Ruty**, *rwty*, “the Two Lions.”
- **He who looks forward and backward:** a modern paraphrase of the opposed orientation; it should not automatically be printed as an ancient fixed epithet without a monument-specific citation.
- **Western and eastern horizon:** later explanatory readings of the lion pair and Book of the Earth cosmography.
- **Yesterday and Tomorrow:** names attached to the two temporal/horizon lions in Book of the Dead Spell 17 vignettes and commentaries. This is a **later canonical and learned identification**, not securely established as the names of two components of Aker in the Old Kingdom.
- **Shu and Tefnut:** another Spell 17 identification of the two lions. This is a priestly equation within an exegetical tradition, not proof that Aker was always identical with Shu-Tefnut.
- **Ruty:** “Two Lions,” associated especially with Spell 17. Ruty and Aker overlap iconographically but should not be treated as timeless synonyms.

### Misleading identifications

There is no securely documented Greek or Roman *interpretatio* that equates Aker with Hades, Chthon, Janus, Aion, Mithras, or Cerberus. Similarities alleged in modern comparative literature—earth, double orientation, solar passage, or a leonine form—do not establish an ancient identification. Likewise, no evidence located supports a historical Aker-Serapis fusion.

## 2. Earliest Attestation and Textual Corpus

### Pyramid Texts: Old Kingdom

The Pyramid Texts were carved in the royal pyramids of Unas and later Fifth–Sixth Dynasty rulers at Saqqara. Unas’s pyramid contains the earliest surviving corpus; later pyramids enlarge, rearrange, and sometimes reinterpret it. Sethe’s *Die altägyptischen Pyramidentexte* remains the basic hieroglyphic concordance, while R. O. Faulkner and James P. Allen provide the principal English translations. Allen’s 2015 revision incorporates newly recovered material and translates monument by monument rather than treating the corpus as a single timeless book. [Allen](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt14jxv34), [UCL bibliography](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/literature/religious/pyramid.html).

Important Aker passages include:

- **PT §§324–325, Unas:** the king travels through Shu and traverses Aker. Aker occupies the terrestrial counterpart to the atmosphere. **Securely attested early.**
- **PT §393, Cannibal Hymn, Unas:** “the bones of Aker/Akeru tremble” when the celestial king appears. The number is grammatically and orthographically disputed. **Securely attested early.**
- **PT §658:** the king is not to be seized by Aker/Akeru. **Early attested hostile or restraining aspect.**
- **PT §796:** “the doors of Aker” and “the doors of Geb” are opened; Anubis’s voice enables the deceased to emerge and become an *akh*, like Thoth. **Securely attested Sixth Dynasty tradition.**
- **PT §§1014 and 1713:** related releases from the earth/Aker support the same mortuary conception.
- **PT §§2201–2202:** Horus removes the king’s bonds so that Aker will not seize him. **Late Old Kingdom Pyramid Text tradition.**

These passages do not supply a continuous myth. They are ritual utterances in which the deceased king crosses, escapes, or overawes the earth. Nor do they securely identify Aker’s lions as Yesterday and Tomorrow.

### Coffin Texts: First Intermediate Period and Middle Kingdom

The Coffin Texts, conventionally numbered in Adriaan de Buck’s seven-volume edition, were written chiefly on nonroyal coffins in Middle Egypt and elsewhere, approximately the late third to early second millennium BCE. They preserve older Pyramid Text material while greatly extending funerary access and netherworld geography.

Aker or Akeru appears roughly two dozen times, depending on how spellings and damaged passages are counted. Significant themes include:

- doors or regions of the earth through which the deceased passes;
- Aker as a dangerous restraining power from whom release is required;
- Aker/Akeru as primordial earth beings;
- serpent or net imagery;
- emergence into daylight;
- solar travel through or over the earth.

**CT 474** names the fishermen of a threatening net as earth-gods/Akeru “who preceded the world and who preceded Geb.” This is important evidence for a plural or collective interpretation and for the claim of primordiality. It does not make them Aker’s children or establish an organized family.

Modern summaries sometimes assert that Aker “replaced Kherty as Ra’s ferryman.” The Coffin Texts do expand Aker’s passage-related role, but “replacement” is a **modern historical reconstruction**, not an explicit Egyptian narrative in which one god transfers an office to another. A spell-by-spell demonstration is needed before treating “ferryman of Ra” as a fixed title.

### Book of Two Ways

The Book of Two Ways, preserved mainly on Middle Kingdom coffins from el-Bersha, maps terrestrial and aquatic routes through the hereafter. Its earth-gates and dangerous guardians provide the larger setting in which Aker-related language occurs. Aker’s association with bounded underground passage fits this corpus, but not every lion, gate, or earth guardian in it is Aker.

### Book of the Dead

The Book of the Dead—Egyptian *rꜣw nw prt m hrw*, “utterances of going forth by day”—developed from Coffin Text traditions during the New Kingdom and survives in highly variable papyrus, linen, coffin, and tomb copies.

**Spell 17** is the central text for the double-lion imagery. It contains a declaration “I am Yesterday; I know Tomorrow,” followed by explanatory glosses identifying Yesterday with Osiris and Tomorrow with Ra. Associated vignettes show two back-to-back lions with the solar horizon between them. Different copies or commentarial strata may interpret the lions as east and west, Yesterday and Tomorrow, or Shu and Tefnut.

Accordingly:

- The two lions are securely attested.
- Their association with solar renewal and time is securely attested in New Kingdom copies.
- Their identification with Ruty is securely attested.
- Their equation with Aker is plausible and traditional but not universal in each manuscript.
- “Aker’s two lions are named Sef and Duau” is an overcompressed modern formula. The spell’s theological gloss is more complicated: *sf* is “yesterday,” *dwꜣw* “tomorrow,” and the lions can simultaneously receive other identities.

The British Museum’s Hunefer papyrus, approximately 1275 BCE, preserves a famous Spell 17 lion-horizon scene; the museum also holds a twentieth-century painted gallery reproduction, EA91057, explicitly derived from that papyrus. The reproduction must not be mistaken for an ancient painting. [British Museum EA91057](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA91057).

### Amduat and related royal underworld books

The Amduat, first substantially preserved in early Eighteenth Dynasty royal tombs, describes twelve hours of the sun’s night journey. In the Fifth Hour’s land of Sokar, a double-sphinx/earth configuration encloses a hidden oval or cavern. It is conventionally identified as Aker. This is an **iconographic and cosmographic identification**, not necessarily a narrative scene in which a text says “Aker did” each depicted action.

The Book of Gates and Book of Caverns further develop earth passages, the confinement and destruction of Apophis, and the reanimation of the dead. Later Books of the Earth draw extensively on this shared repertoire. Aker-like double-earth imagery can appear in these compositions without making every episode a personal myth of Aker.

### Books of the Earth / “Book of Aker”

The term **Book of Aker** is modern. The relevant Ramesside scenes have also been called the **Book of the Earth** and **Creation of the Solar Disk**. Joshua Aaron Roberson’s comprehensive edition uses the plural **Ancient Egyptian Books of the Earth**, because the evidence does not reveal one fixed, ancient-titled book with an invariant sequence.

Scenes occur in Ramesside royal funerary settings, including material associated with Merneptah, Twosret, Ramesses III, Ramesses IV, Ramesses VI, and Ramesses VII. Their most extensive surviving assemblage is in the burial chamber of KV9, begun for Ramesses V and completed and used by Ramesses VI, approximately 1145–1135 BCE. The scenes are arranged in eastern and western groups beneath paired sky compositions. The nocturnal sun enters the western earth, is regenerated among the dead and divine powers, and emerges in the east. [Roberson’s edition](https://www.lockwoodpress.com/product-page/the-ancient-egyptian-books-of-the-earth), [Theban Mapping Project plan](https://thebanmappingproject.com/sites/default/files/plans/KV09_0.pdf).

The composition is not a prose epic. It is a monumental assemblage of captions, hymnic speeches, divine figures, solar disks, crypts, corpses, serpents, mounds, and barks. Aker appears as a double human-headed sphinx or joined terrestrial body supporting the solar bark. The sun’s passage through or over his body expresses the earth as the route of nocturnal regeneration.

Florence Mauric-Barberio has shown that the hurried execution and displaced or imperfect correspondence between text and image in KV9 complicate reconstruction of the original order. Claims about a single canonical plot should therefore be treated cautiously. [Mauric-Barberio](https://www.ifao.egnet.net/publications/catalogue/?nif=BIFAO110_art_11.pdf&nv=0).

### Later funerary transmission

From the Twenty-First Dynasty onward, selected Books of the Earth tableaux were adapted to private coffins, papyri, sarcophagi, and tombs. The arrangement could be changed and new labels added. A Louvre inner coffin of Soutymès includes an Aker solar image within a larger funerary program involving Osiris, Horus, Isis, Nephthys, Anubis, Re’s bark, scarabs, and resurrection scenes. Their co-presence is not automatically a narrative interaction. [Louvre coffin record](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark%3A/53355/cl010028692).

A Louvre faience amulet, AF 2124, represents two opposed lion or bovine protomes with disked horns and is catalogued as Aker. Its database record does not supply a secure date, provenience, or excavated ritual context; it proves collection history and iconographic classification, not a precisely datable cult. [Louvre AF 2124](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark%3A/53355/cl010033024).

### Sources named in the brief in which no secure Aker episode was established

A responsible corpus requires negative findings:

- **Memphite Theology/Shabaka Stone:** no securely identified Aker episode central to the surviving text.
- **Contendings of Horus and Seth:** no direct Aker interaction established.
- **Plutarch, *De Iside et Osiride*:** no clear named discussion of Aker.
- **Greek Magical Papyri:** no securely verified invocation of Aker located in the standard, commonly cited corpus.
- **Turin King List:** a royal historical list, not a source for Aker.
- **Edfu, Dendera, Karnak, and Philae:** these vast temple corpora contain earth, horizon, lion, and underworld theology, but no item-specific inscription located in this research establishes a major independent Aker cult. A generic claim that “Aker was worshipped at all these temples” would be unjustified.

## 3. Domain, Attributes, and Iconography

### Earth and confinement

In the Pyramid Texts, Aker is ground or earth as a domain the king must traverse and escape. The danger is not moral punishment in a later “hell” but entrapment below the terrestrial surface. Thus Aker can “seize” the deceased; his bones tremble before the overwhelmingly powerful king; his doors must open.

### Gates and release

Aker’s doors are paired with Geb’s in PT §796. The dead king emerges when Anubis speaks, becoming an effective spirit like Thoth. This makes Aker a threshold rather than simply a hostile monster. The same structure underlies later imagery: earth encloses the night sun but is also the necessary medium of rebirth.

### Solar night passage

By the New Kingdom, Aker’s body is a cosmic tract connecting sunset and sunrise. A solar bark or disk may appear above or within him. The sun does not merely defeat Aker; it moves through the earth he embodies. That passage unites solar and funerary regeneration: as the sun re-emerges each morning, the deceased king hopes likewise to rise.

### Protective containment

Later sources associate Aker with holding or neutralizing subterranean serpents and poisons. It is safer to describe this as containment than to imagine a warrior-god with a standard weapon. Aker’s massive earth-body encloses what must not escape. Claims that he personally fought three named snakes—Hemtet, Iqeru, and Jagw—are widespread in modern summaries but require a precise primary-text citation; the available evidence supports protection from serpents more firmly than a discrete three-combat myth.

Apophis can be represented as dismembered, restrained, or moving within earth imagery associated with Aker. The destructive agents are frequently other gods or anonymous executioners. Aker’s contribution is spatial and containing, not necessarily the direct killing blow.

### Iconographic development

The recoverable sequence is:

1. **Old Kingdom:** the name may be determined by a land strip, a single lion, or a terrestrial form with a human head. The evidence does not begin with two naturalistic lions named Yesterday and Tomorrow.
2. **Middle Kingdom:** doubled, opposed heads and leonine forms become increasingly intelligible as a two-ended earth/horizon.
3. **New Kingdom:** two back-to-back lions or a double sphinx; between them may be the *ꜣḫt* horizon, sun disk, solar bark, or resurrection emblem.
4. **Ramesside Books of the Earth:** a joined, human-headed double sphinx beneath the solar bark, sometimes extended as the body of the underworld.
5. **Third Intermediate and Late Period:** the motif is excerpted onto coffins, papyri, sarcophagi, and amulets, with altered labels and additional divine equations.

The two lions face in opposite directions because the earth has western and eastern limits. They can be interpreted spatially—west/east—and temporally—yesterday/tomorrow. This is not evidence that Egyptians believed in two independent zoological lions living at geographical mountains.

### Ruty and Aker

The Spell 17 image is often labeled Ruty, “the Two Lions.” The same visual grammar can express Aker. Scholars therefore speak of overlap, assimilation, or an Aker/Ruty motif. A monument that explicitly names Ruty should not silently be relabeled Aker, and a double sphinx explicitly named Aker should not be used to prove that all Ruty scenes are Aker.

### Symbols and associated objects

Securely associated symbols are:

- the earth strip;
- two opposed heads or foreparts;
- recumbent lions;
- double sphinx;
- horizon sign and solar disk;
- solar bark;
- gates or doorways;
- underground serpent forms;
- funerary amulets and coffin imagery.

No securely attested personal weapon, characteristic handheld scepter, sacred plant, festival color, holy number, weekday, zodiacal sign, or exclusive celestial body is known. The sun is associated through passage and rebirth, not because Aker is primarily a solar god.

### Headrests and thresholds

Egyptian headrests could symbolize a head rising like the sun over the horizon, and some examples use paired lions and Shu imagery. A headrest from Tutankhamun’s tomb is frequently interpreted through the Aker/Ruty complex. The more general symbolism is secure; assigning every lion-supported headrest specifically to Aker is not. The Metropolitan Museum notes the broader horizon analogy of the sleeper’s head rising above the support, but its catalogued Middle Kingdom headrest 31.3.12 has no Aker imagery and should not be pressed into the dossier as a cult object. [Metropolitan Museum headrest](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/art/collection/search/544251).

## 4. Genealogy and Relations to Other Divine Beings

### Genealogy

No securely attested Egyptian source gives Aker an ordinary genealogy, birth narrative, spouse, or offspring. He is not consistently placed within the Heliopolitan Ennead. Calling him “older than Geb” derives from CT 474’s primordial Akeru, not from a narrative in which Aker is Geb’s father.

His closest structural relations are not familial:

- **Geb:** another earth god, associated with the living surface, bodily earth, and Heliopolitan genealogy.
- **Ruty:** double-lion form with which Aker overlaps.
- **Shu and Tefnut:** later exegetical identification of the lion pair.
- **Osiris:** yesterday, corpse, and regenerative dead sun in Spell 17 and later underworld theology.
- **Ra/Khepri:** sun entering, crossing, and emerging from Aker.
- **Sokar:** ruler of the cavernous desert-earth region in which Aker imagery appears.
- **Apophis:** chaotic serpent contained or destroyed within the terrestrial underworld.

Geb did not simply “replace” Aker in a documented political decree. **Modern scholarly reconstruction:** the genealogically integrated Geb achieved greater prominence in systematic Heliopolitan theology, while Aker remained important as a cosmic place-form and funerary image.

### Documented divine interactions

The following list separates direct textual actions from theological equation or joint iconography.

- **Shu — spatial traversal.** The deceased king travels through Shu and traverses Aker. Source: PT §§324–325, Unas, approximately 2350–2320 BCE. **Securely attested early.**
- **Geb — parallel opening of earth.** The doors of Aker open and those of Geb are thrown open for the king. Source: PT §796, Sixth Dynasty. **Securely attested early.**
- **Anubis — enables emergence from Aker.** The king comes forth at Anubis’s voice when Aker’s and Geb’s doors open. Source: PT §796. **Securely attested early.**
- **Thoth — model for transformation after emergence.** In the same passage, the released king is made an effective spirit “like Thoth.” This is an indirect joint episode, not a conversation between Aker and Thoth. Source: PT §796. **Securely attested early.**
- **Horus — prevents Aker’s seizure of the king.** Horus cuts the deceased’s cords and removes his obstruction so Aker will not lay hold of him. Source: PT §§2201–2202. **Securely attested Old Kingdom.**
- **Geb — primordial contrast.** The Akeru/earth-gods are said to have preceded Geb. Source: CT 474, Middle Kingdom. **Later canonical elaboration; collective reading.**
- **Ra — nocturnal passage.** The sun god travels over or through Aker’s earth-body and is delivered toward sunrise. Sources: New Kingdom underworld books, especially Ramesside Books of the Earth in KV9. **Later canonical elaboration.**
- **Khepri — emergence or rebirth.** The renewed morning sun, iconographically the scarab Khepri, rises from the terrestrial/horizon complex. Sources: Book of the Dead and royal underworld iconography, New Kingdom onward. **Later canonical/iconographic elaboration.**
- **Osiris — temporal-theological relation.** Spell 17 identifies Yesterday with Osiris while Tomorrow is Ra; the two-lion horizon image supplies the visual setting. Source: Book of the Dead Spell 17, New Kingdom manuscripts. **Learned systematization.**
- **Shu and Tefnut — identification with the two lions.** Spell 17 commentary and related iconography identify Ruty’s paired lions with Shu and Tefnut. Source: New Kingdom Book of the Dead tradition. **Syncretistic identification / learned systematization.**
- **Ruty — iconographic and conceptual assimilation.** Aker’s opposed double form overlaps with Ruty, “Two Lions.” Source: Middle and New Kingdom funerary texts and images. **Later canonical assimilation; not a narrative meeting.**
- **Sokar — shared subterranean region.** Aker’s double-sphinx form encloses or borders the hidden Sokarian cavern in the Fifth Hour of the Amduat. Source: royal tomb copies beginning in the Eighteenth Dynasty. **Later canonical joint iconography.**
- **Apophis — containment in the earth.** Apophis is restrained, cut, or passes through the underworld associated with Aker; Aker supplies the containing earth while other gods or executioners attack the serpent. Sources: Amduat, Book of Gates, Book of Caverns, Books of the Earth. **Later canonical elaboration; direct combat by Aker not securely demonstrated.**
- **Nut — complementary cosmic bodies.** In KV9 the Books of the Earth appear beneath paired celestial compositions of Nut: earth/Aker below, sky/Nut above. Source: Ramesses VI’s burial chamber, approximately 1140 BCE. **Monument-specific theological arrangement, not a narrative encounter.**
- **Heka — shared solar setting.** On later coffins and cosmographic scenes Heka may support Nut or accompany the solar bark above Aker imagery. **Regional/object-specific joint appearance; no independent Aker–Heka myth established.**
- **Seth — no direct interaction securely established.** Seth attacks Apophis in solar-night traditions, but this does not by itself prove a personal Aker–Seth episode.
- **Isis, Nephthys, Hathor, Ptah, Atum, and the Four Sons of Horus — no discrete Aker interaction established.** Their appearance in the same funerary programs is not sufficient evidence of a meeting or relationship.
- **Kherty — no direct transfer episode.** Statements that Aker replaced Kherty as ferryman are scholarly systematizations of changing textual prominence, not a preserved divine encounter.

## 5. Major Myths and Narrative Cycles

### The king crosses and escapes the earth

**Earliest stratum:** Pyramid Texts.

The deceased king confronts the earth not as a passive grave but as a divine, bounded region. He traverses Aker, causes Aker’s bones to tremble, avoids being seized, and passes through opened doors. Horus removes bonds; Anubis calls him forth; he becomes an effective spirit like Thoth.

This cycle has no single narrative order in the ancient corpus. Modern retellings commonly synthesize separate utterances into a story in which Aker first imprisons and then liberates the king. The synthesis captures the conceptual tension but is not itself an ancient continuous tale.

**Function:** to deny permanent burial and make the king’s body, tomb, and subterranean location compatible with celestial resurrection.

### The primordial Akeru and the net

**Middle Kingdom elaboration:** Coffin Texts.

The Akeru appear as ancient earth-powers associated with the dangerous fishermen of a net. The deceased must know their names and avoid capture. CT 474’s statement that they preceded the world and Geb assigns them primordial status.

Two readings remain possible:

- a true plurality of chthonic beings, perhaps serpent-like;
- pluralization or expansion of the singular earth-god’s power.

The context favors multiple agents in the net spell, whereas other orthographies may preserve singular Aker. No surviving source makes the Akeru an organized pantheon with individual biographies.

### The two lions, Yesterday and Tomorrow

**New Kingdom canonical and exegetical elaboration:** Book of the Dead Spell 17.

The deceased identifies with “Yesterday” and knows “Tomorrow.” The commentary associates Yesterday with Osiris and Tomorrow with Ra. The accompanying lions look in opposite directions around the rising sun or horizon.

The image combines:

- west and east;
- sunset and sunrise;
- death and renewal;
- past and future;
- Osirian and solar time.

Some copies identify the lions as Shu and Tefnut; others emphasize Ruty. Aker belongs to this constellation because the opposed lions and intervening horizon reproduce his two-ended earth form. But the widespread modern statement “Aker consists of the lion Sef and the lion Duau” falsely turns flexible annotations into a universal genealogy.

### The sun’s passage through Aker

**New Kingdom royal elaboration:** Amduat, Book of Caverns, and Books of the Earth.

At sunset the solar god enters the western horizon and the subterranean realm. During the night he illuminates the dead, encounters bodies, souls, serpents, crypts, and regenerative powers, and approaches the eastern limit. Aker’s extended, two-headed body is the terrestrial route or container. A bark can travel along his back; the solar disk can lie within the depths he encloses.

In the most elaborate Ramesside form, especially KV9, the sequence is divided broadly into western and eastern halves. The dead sun and Osirian powers are renewed; hostile forces are immobilized; the disk is lifted or reborn toward dawn. The Books of the Earth are therefore less a “myth about Aker” than a cosmography in which Aker is the indispensable body and topology of the myth.

The sun’s apparent swallowing by the western earth gave Aker a potentially threatening aspect. Bisson de la Roque accordingly described Aker as *animal néfaste* in one interpretive context because he swallows the sun at evening. Yet the complete cycle makes the same body the route of morning delivery. [Bisson de la Roque](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bifao_0255-0962_1931_num_30_1_1915?pageId=T2_576).

### Aker and Apophis

Later underworld books depict Apophis bound, dismembered, burned, or contained. Modern summaries often make Aker the serpent’s personal jailer. The evidence supports a more qualified reconstruction:

- the serpent’s defeat occurs within the terrestrial underworld;
- Aker can contain dangerous serpentine powers;
- execution is normally performed by named gods, anonymous divine executioners, ropes, knives, fire, or magical speech;
- therefore “Aker imprisons Apophis” is a defensible cosmographic summary, while “Aker fought and killed Apophis” is not a securely preserved standalone myth.

### No known earthly biography

No ancient source narrates Aker’s birth, reign, marriage, betrayal, death, or resurrection as a divine biography. His mythology is topological and ritual: beings move through him, emerge from him, or are contained within him.

## 6. Cult, Temples, and Ritual Practice

### No demonstrated independent temple cult

No securely excavated temple dedicated primarily to Aker has been established, and no major city can confidently be called his cult center. Assertions that his cult center was Leontopolis or “To Remu” appear to infer a connection from lion cults. Leontopolis supported several lion-associated deities, but leonine form alone cannot establish an Aker temple.

No securely identified office of “high priest of Aker,” temple estate, festival calendar, oracle, sacred herd, or pilgrimage center is known. This absence is significant. Aker functioned predominantly in funerary ritual, cosmography, and protective imagery rather than through a large public institution comparable to the cults of Amun, Ptah, Horus, or Hathor.

### Royal mortuary ritual

The Pyramid Texts were installed inside royal pyramids, where their placement and recitation supported the dead king’s transformation. Aker’s early ritual setting was therefore royal mortuary performance and monumental inscription.

In the New Kingdom, Aker imagery became part of the decorative theology of royal burial chambers. The image did ritual work by turning the chamber itself into the cosmic earth beneath the sky. KV9’s chthonic compositions on the walls and celestial books on the ceiling make the burial chamber a microcosm: sky above, earth and underworld below, renewed king and sun at the center. [ISAC discussion of Ramesside burial chambers](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/Publications/OIS/isacs14.pdf).

### Nonroyal funerary use

Coffin Texts democratized, though did not universally distribute, mortuary knowledge formerly associated with kingship. Book of the Dead papyri, private coffins, sarcophagi, and amulets later transferred the Aker/Ruty horizon symbolism to officials and other purchasers of funerary equipment.

The ordinary ritual aims were:

- avoiding entrapment in the earth;
- knowing dangerous beings and routes;
- passing gates;
- obtaining solar renewal;
- rising from death as the sun rises from the horizon;
- containing serpents and poisons;
- protecting the head or body during vulnerable sleep and death.

### Amulets and protective magic

The Louvre’s Aker amulet confirms that the double form became a portable protective image. Without an excavated context or precise date in its catalog record, its exact ritual use remains inferential. It is consistent with funerary protection and rebirth, but it does not prove a standardized “Aker amulet ritual.” [Louvre AF 2124](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark%3A/53355/cl010033024).

### Offerings and sacrifices

No offering list exclusive to Aker is securely known. Food, incense, libations, and recitations appear in monuments containing Aker images, but they usually belong to general mortuary or temple liturgy.

No evidence located supports:

- human sacrifice to Aker;
- temple prostitution connected with Aker;
- cult violence by an Aker priesthood;
- animal sacrifice uniquely prescribed to him;
- political persecution conducted in his name.

The Cannibal Hymn’s violent consumption of divine powers is ritual-poetic language concerning the dead king, not evidence that humans were sacrificed to Aker.

### Theophoric names and popularity

No substantial corpus of securely identified personal names built from Aker has been demonstrated. The absence of common theophoric names, temples, priestly titles, and public festivals reinforces the conclusion that Aker was a specialized cosmic and funerary power rather than a major civic patron.

## 7. Historical Evolution

### Before approximately 2350 BCE

No secure written attestation has been established. Lions, opposed animals, horizon imagery, and earth symbolism occur earlier in Egyptian art, but assigning them to Aker without an inscription is speculative. The god may predate his first written appearance; that cannot be proved from the surviving record.

### Late Fifth and Sixth Dynasties, approximately 2350–2180 BCE

Aker appears in the Pyramid Texts as terrestrial or subterranean divinity. The king crosses him, escapes his grasp, and exits through his doors. Both single and potentially plural forms occur. The earliest record emphasizes mortuary danger and passage, not public worship or a complete two-lion solar mythology.

### First Intermediate Period and Middle Kingdom, approximately 2180–1650 BCE

Pyramid Text traditions pass onto nonroyal coffins and expand into the Coffin Texts. Aker and Akeru become more deeply embedded in maps, nets, gates, serpentine dangers, and the routes of the dead. The statement that Akeru preceded Geb preserves a primordial theology outside the standard Ennead genealogy.

The opposed or doubled form develops more clearly. Aker’s relation to Ruty and the horizon becomes visually productive. This is an elaboration, not evidence that Old Kingdom Egyptians necessarily knew every later identification.

### Early New Kingdom, approximately 1550–1350 BCE

Royal underworld books monumentalize the sun’s nightly route. Aker’s double-sphinx form participates in the Sokarian cavern and in a cosmic geography of hidden earth. The god’s function shifts from something the king must escape toward the medium through which sun and king are regenerated—without entirely losing the dangerous aspect.

### Later New Kingdom, approximately 1350–1070 BCE

Book of the Dead Spell 17 spreads the two-lion, yesterday/tomorrow, Osiris/Ra, and Shu/Tefnut exegesis. Ramesside royal tombs assemble Books of the Earth scenes in which Aker’s body becomes the great east-west conduit.

KV9 under Ramesses VI provides the fullest surviving monumental recension, although its ordering is imperfect and perhaps hastily executed. Calling this period Aker’s “peak” is justified only iconographically: it produced his largest and most elaborate surviving representations. It does not demonstrate mass public worship.

### Third Intermediate and Late Periods, approximately 1070–332 BCE

Books of the Earth tableaux move into private mortuary equipment. Coffin painters and sarcophagus designers excerpt and rearrange their motifs, sometimes adding new captions. Aker amulets and compact horizon images attest continuing protective and regenerative use.

This is transmission through funerary workshops, not evidence for an expanding independent priesthood.

### Ptolemaic and Roman Egypt, 332 BCE–fourth century CE

Older solar, Osirian, earth, and horizon motifs continued in temple and funerary art. Nevertheless, no securely documented Greco-Roman identification of Aker with a classical god has emerged. Claims of an Aker-Mithras, Aker-Janus, Aker-Cerberus, or Aker-Aion equation are modern comparativism unless an inscription explicitly establishes it.

The reduction of traditional temple institutions under late Roman rule eventually ended the scribal and ritual systems that transmitted compositions such as the Books of the Earth. No documented decree specifically suppressed Aker’s cult; his disappearance belonged to the wider transformation and closure of Egyptian cult institutions.

### Medieval and early-modern periods

No continuous named Aker cult is documented in Coptic Christianity, medieval Egyptian folklore, Islamic magical literature, or European Renaissance esotericism. A generic lion, sphinx, paired guardian, or underworld gate should not be retroactively identified as Aker.

Renaissance Hermetism knew an imagined Egyptian antiquity mainly through Greek and Latin Hermetic texts, hieroglyphic speculation, and monuments, but Aker did not become a significant named Hermetic deity.

### Modern Egyptology and revival

Nineteenth- and twentieth-century publication of the Pyramid Texts, royal tombs, and Book of the Dead papyri restored Aker to scholarly view. Modern translations, museum displays, and illustrated dictionaries then created the compact popular identity “double lion god of yesterday and tomorrow.”

Contemporary Kemetic and neopagan individuals sometimes venerate or “work with” Aker, as modern online testimony shows. Such practice is a genuine modern religious phenomenon but does not demonstrate institutional continuity from antiquity. No reliable census establishes its scale, and no widely recognized Aker-specific reconstructionist organization was located.

## 8. Modern Reception and Reinvention

### Egyptological reconstruction

Modern reference works generally combine three distinct bodies of evidence:

1. Old Kingdom Aker as earth and obstacle;
2. New Kingdom Ruty lions as Yesterday and Tomorrow;
3. Ramesside double-sphinx Aker carrying the sun.

The resulting portrait is broadly coherent but more unified than any single ancient source. It should be labeled a **modern scholarly synthesis**.

### Museum presentation

Museum collections preserve ancient objects but also document modern mediation. The British Museum’s EA91057 is a twentieth-century gallery painting of the Hunefer vignette, not an ancient papyrus fragment. Its record is a useful warning: modern replicas can circulate online detached from catalog metadata and be presented as ancient originals. [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA91057).

Similarly, the opposed-lion amulet in the Louvre is catalogued as Aker but lacks a precise date and archaeological context in the public record. Museum classification is informed expert interpretation, not equivalent to an ancient inscribed label.

### Occult and esoteric traditions

A dedicated search found no significant Aker doctrine in Renaissance Hermetism, the Theosophical Society, the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, Aleister Crowley’s central writings, or the foundational chaos-magic corpus. Egyptian names such as Isis, Osiris, Horus, Thoth, Anubis, and Ra dominate those receptions; Aker remains marginal.

Web pages that supply Aker with prescribed candle colors, crystals, tarot cards, planetary hours, chakras, invocations, or personal psychological “shadow-work” correspondences are **modern inventions** unless they clearly identify themselves as contemporary ritual creativity. No ancient source establishes such correspondences.

### Jungian and psychological readings

The two lions and nightly solar transit lend themselves to interpretations involving memory and anticipation, conscious and unconscious, death and renewal, or the integration of opposites. These are modern psychological interpretations. No demonstrated text by Jung makes Aker a major archetype, and describing the god as “the Jungian guardian of liminality” would be anachronistic.

### Popular culture

Aker has not acquired a dominant literary, cinematic, comic, or game persona comparable to Anubis, Isis, Seth, or Ra. His most influential modern representation remains the illustrated mythology-book or web-entry image of two back-to-back lions with a sun between them.

This popular image frequently introduces three distortions:

- it labels a Ruty/Spell 17 vignette automatically as Aker;
- it turns “Yesterday” and “Tomorrow” into two individual lion names;
- it describes Aker as a gatekeeper who personally fights every serpent encountered by Ra.

### Political and extremist appropriation

No documented nationalist, racialist, or violent extremist movement centered specifically on Aker was found. Egyptian symbols more generally have been appropriated in Afrocentric, occult-nationalist, white-esoteric, and commercial settings, but assigning those movements to Aker without item-specific evidence would reproduce the very conflation this dossier seeks to avoid.

### Commercial and generative reinvention

Modern jewelry, tattoos, prints, role-playing characters, and AI-generated mythology pages increasingly depict Aker as:

- two complete lions joined at the rump;
- a two-headed lion monster;
- a single lion with two heads;
- a sphinx with a sun crown;
- a “guardian of portals” in generalized fantasy.

Some forms resemble ancient motifs; others are new fantasy anatomy. These objects document modern reception, not recovered Egyptian iconography.

## 9. Historiography and Scholarly Debate

### Publication history

The modern recovery of Aker depends on publication of several corpora:

- Kurt Sethe’s edition of the Pyramid Texts established the numbered hieroglyphic corpus.
- R. O. Faulkner produced influential English translations of the Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, and Book of the Dead.
- James P. Allen revised Pyramid Text translations and returned attention to each monument’s local textual program.
- Adriaan de Buck’s seven-volume Coffin Text edition remains the fundamental hieroglyphic source.
- Alexandre Piankoff published major royal underworld compositions and KV9 material.
- Erik Hornung systematized the New Kingdom books of the afterlife while emphasizing that they are distinct compositions.
- Joshua Aaron Roberson collected the dispersed Books of the Earth material and analyzed its architecture, orthography, grammar, vignettes, and textual transmission.

### “Book of Aker” versus Books of the Earth

This is the most consequential terminological debate. “Book of Aker” suggests an ancient book centered on Aker with a fixed title and continuous plot. The surviving scenes instead have:

- no securely preserved ancient title equivalent to that modern name;
- variable arrangements;
- several central figures, especially the solar god, Osiris, and divine *ba* forms;
- monument-specific selections;
- later excerpts with new annotations.

“Books of the Earth” is therefore the safer modern designation. “Book of Aker” remains useful as publication history or shorthand, provided its modern status is stated.

### Is Aker singular or plural?

The writing *ꜣkrw* can support several analyses. Earlier scholarship and dictionaries often render it “Akeru,” plural earth-gods or serpents. Willockx argues that some instances preserve a final semivowel of a singular divine name. CT 474’s multiple fishermen supports a genuine plurality in that passage; other occurrences remain ambiguous.

The evidence does not justify either extreme claim that Akeru are always independent demons or that they are never plural.

### Is Aker “the earth” or an earth-god?

Faulkner’s lexical treatment allows both Aker and “earth itself,” relying partly on parallelism in PT §§324–325. The *Wörterbuch* tradition is more conservative. Egyptian divine names can function metonymically as places or substances without becoming ordinary nouns. The safest translation depends on context: “Aker” in the text, followed by explanation that the god personifies or embodies terrestrial space.

### Aker versus Ruty

Older and popular accounts often collapse them. More cautious iconographic analysis recognizes overlapping functions and deliberate theological identification without eliminating the names’ separate textual histories.

The Spell 17 lions labeled in relation to Yesterday and Tomorrow are securely Ruty-related. Aker’s doubled earth/sphinx form is securely attested elsewhere. The connection is strong, but each object should be read from its own caption and context.

### The antiquity of the double lion

Old Kingdom determinatives already permit doubled or outward-facing earth forms, according to Bisson de la Roque’s analysis. Nevertheless, the fully developed back-to-back lions with sun/horizon and temporal names are best documented later. It is methodologically unsound to transfer the complete New Kingdom interpretation into every Old Kingdom spelling.

### Aker and Geb

Both are earth gods, and PT §796 explicitly juxtaposes their doors. Geb becomes genealogically central in the Ennead; Aker remains topological and funerary. “Geb superseded Aker” is a useful model of relative prominence, but there is no documented reform abolishing one in favor of the other. Egyptian theology normally tolerated overlapping gods.

### Order of the KV9 composition

KV9 is the fullest surviving Books of the Earth assemblage, but not necessarily the pristine archetype. Mauric-Barberio argues that hurried copying and displaced captions disrupt the apparent order. Roberson likewise treats the scenes as related, variably arranged material rather than a uniform book. Reconstructions of an exact sequential plot remain scholarly models.

### Poorly supported theories

The following claims circulate but lack adequate evidence:

- Aker was worshipped in a great temple at Leontopolis.
- He had an organized priesthood and annual public festival.
- His two lions were universally and individually named Sef and Duau from the Old Kingdom onward.
- Greeks and Romans adopted his statues as their standard doorway lions.
- He was equated with Janus, Mithras, Cerberus, or Aion.
- He personally killed Apophis every night.
- He ruled a moral hell and judged human souls.
- He was the father, brother, or son of Geb.
- The Akeru were a fixed race of “hell-hounds.”
- Modern magical correspondences to crystals, planets, chakras, or tarot are ancient.

### State of the evidence

The principal inscriptions remain in situ in the pyramids at Saqqara and royal tombs of the Theban necropolis. Coffin Text manuscripts and Book of the Dead papyri are dispersed among Egyptian and international museums. Late coffins, sarcophagi, and amulets are similarly dispersed, often with incomplete nineteenth-century provenance.

Digital resources improve access but do not eliminate editorial problems:

- TLA provides searchable lemmata and text records.
- UCL Digital Egypt summarizes corpora and editions.
- The Theban Mapping Project documents architecture and decoration.
- Digital Giza is useful for Giza material but is not a principal Aker corpus because Aker’s earliest inscribed attestations are at Saqqara, not Giza.
- Museum databases sometimes catalogue modern reproductions alongside ancient objects; production date and provenance must always be checked.

## 11. Source-critical conclusions

Aker’s securely reconstructable history is not that of a once-popular lion god whose temple and myths were lost. It is the history of a specialized cosmic personification whose importance lies chiefly in mortuary texts and images.

The earliest Aker is the earth crossed or escaped by the royal dead. Middle Kingdom texts multiply or expand this earth-power into Akeru and integrate it with dangerous routes, nets, and primordial beings. New Kingdom theologians and artists place the two-ended earth within a solar geography: west and east, yesterday and tomorrow, burial and emergence. Ramesside tomb designers monumentalize Aker as the double sphinx or vast terrestrial body beneath the night sun.

The familiar modern emblem—two lions looking away from one another with a sun between them—is authentic Egyptian imagery, but its common caption compresses multiple traditions. The lions can be Ruty, east and west, Yesterday and Tomorrow, or Shu and Tefnut; their relation to Aker is strong but historically constructed. The “Book of Aker” is likewise not an ancient canonical title but a modern name for one configuration of the Books of the Earth.

No reliable evidence establishes a separate Aker temple economy, priesthood, public festival, human sacrifice, temple prostitution, violent suppression, Greco-Roman equivalent, medieval survival, major occult lineage, or uninterrupted modern cult. Those absences are not defects in the record; they define the kind of god Aker was—a divine earth, threshold, and cosmic passage whose “cult” was written and pictured primarily around the dead.

# The numeral-system dossier prompt (counting systems and their histories)

Anthony, 2026-09-15: "I want different counting systems and their histories. Roman numerals,
Hindu-Arabic..." One dossier per numeral system. `[NAME]` is the only substitution point; the
per-system brief follows.

---

[NAME]
Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this numeral system, its
history and its use. I want the fullest reconstructable account, from the earliest attestation
to the present, chronologically and in detail. Include, wherever available:

- THE SYSTEM ITSELF: its signs (draw them in words and, where Unicode has them, in the
  characters), the base, whether additive, multiplicative, positional or mixed, how zero or
  its absence is handled, how fractions are written, the largest numbers it can express and
  the words for them, the ordering of signs, the ligatures and abbreviations; a worked table
  of 1 to 20, the tens, hundreds, thousands, and three or four famous numbers written out
- ORIGINS: the earliest surviving inscriptions or tablets with dates, places, museum numbers
  and the scholars who read them (the Ishango and Lebombo tally bones, the Uruk tokens and
  Schmandt-Besserat, the Narmer macehead, the Bakhshali manuscript and its radiocarbon dates,
  the Gwalior zero of 876, the Brahmi numerals at Nana Ghat and Nasik, the Dresden Codex);
  the older system it grew from and the neighbours it borrowed from
- ARITHMETIC AND INSTRUMENTS: how people actually calculated with it (finger reckoning, the
  counting board and abacus, the suan pan and soroban, the Roman calculus and the
  medieval counter-casting, the quipu, the rod numerals, the algorists vs the abacists), the
  surviving textbooks and their worked problems (the Rhind Papyrus, Plimpton 322, the Nine
  Chapters, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta's rules for zero, al-Khwarizmi, Fibonacci's Liber Abaci
  1202, Sacrobosco's Algorismus, Pacioli, Recorde), the words that descend from it
  (calculus, digit, cipher, zero, algorithm, abacus)
- TRANSMISSION: the routes, dates and people by which it travelled or was replaced
  (India to Baghdad to al-Andalus to Italy; the Florentine ban of 1299 on Arabic numerals;
  the persistence of Roman numerals on clocks, monuments, popes and Super Bowls; Chinese
  numerals alongside Arabic in Japan and Korea today; the Maya system's death with the
  codices and its survival in the Long Count)
- PEOPLE: the mathematicians, scribes, merchants, monks, translators and rulers attached to
  it, with dates; the anecdotes (Gerbert's apices, Fibonacci in Bugia, Napier, the Roman
  abacus in the Louvre)
- CULTURE: the system in law, liturgy, chronograms, gematria and isopsephy (Hebrew and
  Greek letter-numerals), the numbers on coins and dates, calendars and clocks, the
  typography of the digits (the old-style figures), the system in literature and art
- CONTROVERSIES: the disputed firsts (who invented zero, the Bakhshali dating, the
  Babylonian placeholder, the Maya zero, the Chinese "counting rods" priority), the
  etymologies, the myths (the "Arabic" numerals that are Indian, the angles-count-the-value
  legend for the digits' shapes, the "Roman numerals have no zero" simplification)

Include the good and the bad without judgment, and legend as fully as fact.
For every significant claim distinguish documented text or artefact, scholarly
reconstruction, tradition, disputed claim, legend and modern invention, and say which sources
carry it, when the reading first appears, who spread it, and what evidence exists.
Do not use fact-checking summaries as substitutes for the sources; trace to the texts and
the objects (museum catalogues, the MacTutor history of mathematics, Menninger's Number Words
and Number Symbols, Ifrah's Universal History of Numbers with its known errors, Chrisomalis's
Numerical Notation 2010 as the standard survey, Plofker for India, Høyrup for Babylon,
Needham for China).

Structure the dossier as: Basic identification (name, base, type, signs, period, region);
The system in detail (with tables); Origins (dated, placed); Arithmetic and instruments;
Transmission and replacement; People; Culture; Controversies and disputes; Open questions;
Sources (full list of URLs and editions consulted).
Length: as long as the material requires; for Hindu-Arabic or Babylonian a thousand lines is
not too many.

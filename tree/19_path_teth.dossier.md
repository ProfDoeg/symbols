The first and most important distinction is historical:

> **[Documented text]** *Sefer Yetzirah* makes Teth one of the twelve simple letters and, in influential recensions, associates it with Leo and Av.  
> **[Hermetic-occult attribution]** The claim that Teth is specifically “Path 19” between Chesed and Geburah comes from the Kircher-derived Western tree. It is not stated in the foundational text of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## Evidentiary labels used below

- **[Documented text]**: explicitly present in a named primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]**: a conclusion of textual, historical, linguistic, or manuscript scholarship.
- **[Kabbalistic tradition]**: a traditional interpretation maintained within Jewish mystical literature, whether or not historians accept its traditional antiquity.
- **[Hermetic-occult attribution]**: a correspondence within Christian Cabala, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, BOTA, or a related Western esoteric system.
- **[Disputed]**: evidence or scholarly opinion is divided.
- **[Modern invention]**: a recent construction presented as a new occult synthesis rather than something found in classical Jewish Kabbalah.

## The name

### Hebrew spelling and pronunciation

**[Documented text]** The ninth Hebrew letter is **ט**, named **טֵית** or **טֵת**. Modern Hebrew pronounces it /t/. Scholarly transliteration often uses **ṭ** to distinguish its historically emphatic consonant from ordinary tav, ת.

The common English forms are:

- **Tet**: modern English/Jewish usage.
- **Ṭet**: linguistic transliteration.
- **Teth**: inherited through Latin and older Christian-Cabalist spelling; dominant in Golden Dawn and Thelemic literature.
- Less commonly: *Teth*, *Theth*, *Teth/Tet*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** In Northwest Semitic, Teth represented an emphatic *ṭ*, probably pharyngealized, glottalized, or otherwise emphatic. Its merger with tav in most modern Israeli pronunciation is later. Some Sephardi and Mizrahi reading traditions preserved a clearer distinction.

### Meaning and etymology

The ancient name’s literal meaning is **not securely known**.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The graphic ancestry runs through an early Northwest Semitic sign, Phoenician **𐤈**, Aramaic forms, Jewish “book hand,” and eventually square Hebrew ט. The angular Phoenician character became increasingly rounded and closed in Aramaic-derived scripts. Unicode encodes both Phoenician and Hebrew descendants but, appropriately, does not adjudicate competing pictographic etymologies.

**[Disputed]** Popular identifications of the early sign as a:

- wheel,
- basket,
- spindle,
- shield,
- coiled snake,
- snake surrounding an egg,
- or crossed enclosure

are hypotheses, not established translations of the letter-name.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** The Golden Dawn and Crowley treat Teth as meaning **“serpent.”** Crowley’s *777* lists “Serpent” for the letter; *Liber Aleph* develops the serpent, lion, solar force, courage, and sexual power symbolically. This is an internally important occult meaning, but it is not a demonstrated Semitic etymology.

**Absence finding:** No securely identified Proto-Sinaitic inscription supplies an unambiguous continuous sequence proving that a particular snake-shaped pictogram became Hebrew Teth. “Teth originally means serpent” should therefore be labeled an occult or speculative reading, not linguistic fact.

### Shape

The square Hebrew ט consists visually of an enclosing curved stroke with an inward-projecting left-hand head or stroke. Jewish homiletics sometimes treat its inwardness as a sign of **concealed good**.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** This symbolism fits the Zoharic teaching that the good of Teth is withheld or concealed for the righteous in the world to come. It is an interpretation of the received square form, not evidence for the letter’s Bronze Age pictographic meaning.

### Numerical value and gematria

**[Documented text]** Teth has numerical value **9**.

Notable later computations include:

- ט = 9.
- טית = 9 + 10 + 400 = **419**, the usual occult full spelling.
- **[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Crowley’s tables also give a “mystic number” of **190** in association with the nineteenth path.
- The first Teth in the Torah occurs in **טוֹב**, *tov*, “good,” in Genesis 1:4.

There is no single classical Jewish “gematria of Teth” that controls all later interpretation. Its value nine supports numerous homilies—pregnancy, completion before a new decade, the nine months of gestation—but these should be dated to the works that make them, not projected into *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## Biblical foundations and limits

### First Chronicles 29:11

The verse reads in part:

> לְךָ יְהוָה הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד … לְךָ יְהוָה הַמַּמְלָכָה

“To You, YHWH, belong greatness, power, beauty, victory, and majesty … Yours, YHWH, is kingship.”

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** This became an important prooftext for the names of the lower sefirot:

- Gedulah/Chesed
- Geburah
- Tiferet
- Netzach
- Hod
- “all,” כל, interpreted as Yesod
- Malkhut

The verse therefore names the **two sefirot joined by Teth on the Kircher tree**, Gedulah/Chesed and Geburah. It does not name Teth or establish a lettered path between them.

### Proverbs 3:19

> יְהוָה בְּחָכְמָה יָסַד־אָרֶץ  
> “YHWH founded the earth through Wisdom.”

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** This verse supports creation through Chokhmah/Wisdom and was integrated into sefirotic interpretation. It is not a biblical attestation of “Path 19.”

### Job 28

Job 28 asks where wisdom is found and declares that God understands its way:

> אֱלֹהִים הֵבִין דַּרְכָּהּ וְהוּא יָדַע אֶת־מְקוֹמָהּ

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Its language of a “way” or path of Wisdom furnished later mystical exegesis. It does not enumerate twenty-two letter-paths or identify Teth with Leo.

### Isaiah

Isaianic passages concerning holiness, divine counsel, wisdom, the messianic spirit, concealed things, and the highway or path of God were repeatedly absorbed into Jewish and Christian mystical interpretation.

**Absence finding:** There is no single Isaianic verse that functions as a primary textual source for Teth = Leo = Strength. Such combinations are later synthetic correspondences.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date and textual history

**[Disputed]** Proposals for the date of *Sefer Yetzirah* have ranged from the early rabbinic period to the early Islamic age. Traditional attribution associates it with Abraham and, in some transmission histories, Rabbi Akiva.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Modern scholarship generally treats it as an anonymous work that grew through textual transmission. Peter Hayman’s critical edition distinguishes three principal recensional families:

1. the **Short Recension**;
2. the **Long Recension**;
3. the **Saadyan Recension**.

Hayman’s edition compares nineteen principal manuscript witnesses. Tzahi Weiss has argued for a setting around the seventh century in a Jewish environment familiar with Syriac Christian cosmological discourse. Other scholars propose earlier strata or somewhat different developmental histories. Consequently, a single date such as “second century” or “sixth century” overstates the evidence.

### The thirty-two paths

The opening reads:

> בִּשְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם נְתִיבוֹת פְּלִיאוֹת חָכְמָה  
> “By thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom…”

It immediately defines them as:

> עֶשֶׂר סְפִירוֹת בְּלִי מָה, וְעֶשְׂרִים וּשְׁתַּיִם אוֹתִיּוֹת יְסוֹד

“Ten sefirot belimah and twenty-two foundational letters.”

The letters are grouped as:

> שָׁלֹשׁ אִמּוֹת וְשֶׁבַע כְּפוּלוֹת וּשְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה פְּשׁוּטוֹת

“Three mothers, seven doubles, and twelve simples.”

**[Documented text]** This arithmetic is foundational:

- 10 sefirot belimah
- 3 mother letters
- 7 double letters
- 12 simple letters
- total: 32

**[Important qualification]** The book does not contain the familiar Kircher diagram, does not number Teth “Path 19,” and does not say that Teth connects Chesed and Geburah. “Thirty-two paths” originally describes the ten sefirot and twenty-two letters as creative modalities, not necessarily twenty-two drawn edges on a fixed tree.

### Teth among the twelve simples

Teth belongs to:

> הו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק  
> He, Vav, Zayin, Chet, Teth, Yod, Lamed, Nun, Samekh, Ayin, Tzaddi, Qof.

**[Documented text]** In an influential recension represented by the Gra version, Teth is associated with:

- **hearing**;
- **Leo**;
- **Av**;
- a **kidney**, commonly the left in this recension.

The creation formula uses *he engraved it, he hewed it, he combined it, he weighed it, he permuted it, and formed through it…* The wording varies among witnesses.

### Recensional variants

The following table separates the commonly cited systems. Exact wording can vary further between manuscripts and printed editions.

| Recension or authority | Faculty attributed to Teth | Zodiac/month | Bodily member | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common/Gra recension | Hearing | Leo / Av | Left kidney | **[Documented text]** in the widely circulated Gra-form text |
| Short recension | Taste or eating in some witnesses | The zodiacal sequence is transmitted with variants | Right kidney in cited comparative tables | **[Documented text/Disputed reading]** |
| Long recension | Hearing | Leo / Av in the received ordering | Right kidney | **[Documented text]** in this family |
| Saadyan recension | Taste/eating, paired with hunger or satiety according to translation | Its own ordered zodiacal scheme | Right kidney | **[Documented text]** |
| Cordoverian reception | Anger | Received zodiacal framework | Right kidney | **[Kabbalistic tradition]** deriving from a different transmitted alignment |
| Later “Ari” tables | Often reproduce Leo/Av while rearranging faculties and organs | Leo / Av | Varies by table | **[Kabbalistic tradition/Disputed]** |

Aryeh Kaplan’s comparative table is useful precisely because it shows that the familiar modern row—Teth/hearing/Leo/Av/left kidney—is not the only textual form. It must not be presented as an uncontested Urtext.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Hayman’s attempt to recover the earliest attainable text suggests that some elaborate astrological and anatomical correspondences may belong to secondary textual growth. “The earliest *Sefer Yetzirah* taught Teth = Leo” is therefore less certain than “several important medieval recensions teach it.”

### Leo and the Sun

**[Documented text]** In the received zodiacal order, Teth forms **Aryeh**, Leo. Leo is governed by the Sun in traditional astrology.

**Important distinction:** Teth is consequently a **zodiacal**, not a planetary, letter. The Sun is Leo’s ruler. It is not the letter’s direct category in *Sefer Yetzirah*, which assigns the seven planets to the seven double letters.

### Av and the tribe

**[Documented text]** The Gra/common recension associates Teth and Leo with the month **Av**.

**Absence finding:** *Sefer Yetzirah* itself does not consistently supply the familiar later table of twelve tribes.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Later Jewish calendars frequently assign **Simeon** to Av. Hearing, *shemi‘ah*, is then linked homiletically with the name **Shim‘on**. Other traditional tables assign Av to Asher or Gad. The tribal attribution must therefore be given with its source rather than treated as universally “the Jewish” correspondence.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Crowley’s *777* assigns **Judah** to Leo. That is an occult zodiac–tribe table and should not be conflated with the common Jewish Av–Simeon calendar.

### The organ: kidney

The kidneys in biblical and rabbinic anthropology symbolize inward counsel. Rabbinic sayings describe the two kidneys as advising the heart.

**[Documented text]** Teth’s organ is a kidney in the major received *Sefer Yetzirah* recensions, although right and left differ.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Golden Dawn/Crowley tables instead emphasize the **heart**, because Leo governs the heart in astrological anatomy. These are two different systems, not a single corrupted list.

## 2. The separate “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom” text

A short enumeration assigning an “Intelligence” to each numbered path circulates with *Sefer Yetzirah* in some editions. Its nineteenth entry begins:

> הנתיב הי״ט נקרא שכל סוד הפעולות  
> “The nineteenth path is called the Intelligence of the secret of the activities…”

The Raavad commentary tradition expands the phrase in terms of the influx or blessing transmitted through the sefirotic order.

Westcott’s 1893 English appendix calls it:

> “The Intelligence of all the activities of the spiritual beings…”

Later occult books regularly abbreviate or reshape this as **“Intelligence of the Secret of all Spiritual Activities.”**

**[Documented text]** The Hebrew “Thirty-two Paths” recension does contain a nineteenth title involving *sod ha-pe‘ulot*, “the secret of the activities/operations.”

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This numbered Intelligence-list is a later text attached to *Sefer Yetzirah*, not part of its recoverable core. Westcott said that he translated it through Johann Stephan Rittangel’s 1642 Latin edition.

**[Critical distinction]** The nineteenth Intelligence does not itself state:

- that it is Teth;
- that it is Leo;
- that it joins Chesed and Geburah;
- or that its tarot image is Strength.

Those equations arise when the later list is superimposed on the Kircher/Golden Dawn tree.

## 3. Talmudic lore

### Shabbat 104a

**[Documented text]** The Talmud interprets sequences of letters mnemonically. Teth and Yod contribute to a teaching about divine goodness, commonly explained through *meitiv*, “He does good” or “bestows goodness.”

This is alphabetic moral interpretation, not a tree-path doctrine.

### Bava Kamma 55a

**[Documented text]** Rabbi Yehoshua is asked whether seeing Teth in a dream is auspicious. The answer connects it with **טוב**, good, because the first Teth in the Torah occurs in Genesis 1:4:

> וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָאוֹר כִּי־טוֹב  
> “God saw that the light was good.”

The same discussion asks why “good” is absent from the account of the first tablets. One answer is that the tablets were destined to break, so the word “good” was withheld lest goodness cease from Israel.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Later treatments combine this with the Zohar’s idea of **concealed good**. Historically, the Talmudic passage does not mention Leo, the nineteenth path, or Strength.

## 4. *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

**[Documented text]** In the medieval alphabet-midrash known as *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*, Teth appeals to its connection with goodness. The text links it with the treasure of good reserved for the righteous, drawing on Psalm 31:20:

> מָה־רַב טוּבְךָ אֲשֶׁר־צָפַנְתָּ לִּירֵאֶיךָ  
> “How abundant is Your goodness, which You have hidden for those who fear You.”

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** This strengthens the symbolic cluster:

- Teth;
- *tov*, good;
- goodness that is hidden rather than immediately displayed;
- the righteous and the world to come.

The work is pseudepigraphically associated with Rabbi Akiva. Its surviving forms are medieval and composite.

## 5. The *Bahir*

**[Documented text]** The *Bahir* interprets the number thirty-two through **לב**, *lev*, “heart,” whose letters total 32. It also develops the divine Glory, Wisdom, the Daughter/Shekhinah, and the ten powers.

**Absence finding:** No sustained Bahiric doctrine corresponding to the Golden Dawn “Path of Teth” was found. Teth appears incidentally in letter-shape discussion—for example, comparison with the belly—but the *Bahir* does not assign “Teth, path 19, Chesed–Geburah, Leo.”

The *Bahir* is important to the development of Jewish sefirotic symbolism, but citing it as evidence for the later Western path layout would be anachronistic.

## 6. The Zohar

### The letters petition to begin creation

In the Zohar’s prologue, the Hebrew letters approach God in reverse order, each asking to become the initial letter of creation.

**[Documented text]** Teth argues from **טוב וישר יהוה**, “YHWH is good and upright.” It is refused because:

1. its goodness is **hidden** and reserved for the world to come;
2. it joins Chet, ח, to form **חט**, the beginning of *ḥet*, “sin”;
3. Chet and Teth do not occur in the names of the tribes.

The textual point is paradoxical: Teth truly carries goodness, but this goodness is too concealed—and too easily entangled with judgment or sin—to serve as the openly manifest first letter of creation.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** This is the most important premodern Jewish mystical lore attached specifically to Teth. It connects goodness, concealment, eschatology, and moral ambiguity. It still does not identify the letter with the Strength card.

### Teth over the Temple

**[Documented text]** In *Zohar*, *Terumah*, Teth is described as suspended over the Temple in a protective context, frustrating a destructive angel. This extends its association with guarded or concealed good.

### Authorship and date

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** The Zohar is attributed to the second-century sage Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Linguistic and historical scholarship places the bulk of the corpus in late-thirteenth-century Castile. Moses de León played a central role. Current scholarship often treats the Zohar not as a single-author forgery but as a layered literary corpus created and redacted by more than one participant.

That historical conclusion does not eliminate the authority the text acquired within Jewish tradition; it answers a different question.

## 7. Gikatilla and *Sha‘arei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla’s *Sha‘arei Orah*, written in late-thirteenth-century Castile, organizes divine names through the ten sefirot.

**[Documented text]** It relates the thirty-two paths to the thirty-two occurrences of **Elohim** in the creation account and traces scriptural names and channels through the sefirotic order.

**Absence finding:** *Sha‘arei Orah* does not provide the Golden Dawn table “Teth—Leo—Strength—Chesed to Geburah.” Nor does it assign to Teth a unique divine name, archangel, angelic order, planet, or virtue.

Its relevance is structural:

- Chesed/Gedulah is associated with **El**;
- Geburah with divine names of power and judgment;
- scriptural verses such as 1 Chronicles 29:11 provide sefirotic language.

The later Western path consequently stands between two complexes that Gikatilla discusses, but the lettered connector is not Gikatilla’s teaching.

## 8. Cordovero: *Pardes Rimmonim* and *Or Ne‘erav*

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (1548)

Moses Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim* is the great pre-Lurianic attempt to systematize competing kabbalistic opinions.

**[Documented text]** It discusses:

- the ten sefirot;
- channels and gates;
- divine names;
- letter combinations;
- cosmology;
- earlier interpretations of *Sefer Yetzirah*;
- the relation between divine unity and differentiated emanation.

Cordovero’s received letter table gives Teth an association with **anger** and a kidney, illustrating that the faculty lists were not fixed across all schools.

**Absence finding:** The *Pardes* does not contain Kircher’s exact twenty-two-edge arrangement, nor the Golden Dawn’s “nineteenth Path of Teth” dossier.

### *Or Ne‘erav*

*Or Ne‘erav* is Cordovero’s more accessible guide to the study and disciplined reception of Kabbalah.

**[Documented text]** It emphasizes preparation, ethical formation, and the ordered study of sefirotic doctrine.

**Absence finding:** It does not establish an independent Teth–Leo–Strength path. References to it in modern pathworking literature tend to import later Western categories.

## 9. Luria through Hayyim Vital: *Etz Chaim*

Isaac Luria left little systematic writing. His teachings were transmitted primarily through disciples, above all Hayyim Vital.

### The Lurianic cosmology

**[Kabbalistic tradition/Documented in Vital’s school]** The usual sequence includes:

1. **Ein Sof**, divine infinity.
2. **Tzimtzum**, contraction or concealment.
3. The **kav**, the ray entering the vacated space.
4. **Iggulim** and **yosher**, circular and linear configurations.
5. **Adam Kadmon**, primordial human configuration.
6. The worlds and configurations of **Akudim**, **Nekudim**, and **Berudim**.
7. **Shevirat ha-kelim**, the breaking of vessels.
8. The fall of sparks and generation of **qelippot**.
9. **Tikkun**, rectification.
10. Reorganization through the **partzufim**:
   - Arikh Anpin;
   - Abba;
   - Imma;
   - Ze‘ir Anpin;
   - Nukva.

The four-world scheme is conventionally expressed as:

- Atzilut, Emanation;
- Beriah, Creation;
- Yetzirah, Formation;
- Assiah, Action.

### Teth’s place—or lack of one

**Absence finding:** Vital’s *Etz Chaim* does not state that “Teth is the nineteenth path connecting Chesed and Geburah.” Its central concern is the dynamics of lights, vessels, worlds, partzufim, rupture, and repair.

**[Disputed/reception history]** Diagrams now called “the Ari tree” arrange the letter-paths differently from Kircher. On a conventional modern Ari diagram, Teth connects **Chokhmah and Tiferet**. This is best described as the later diagrammatic reception of Lurianic structures. It should not be represented as a verbatim proposition from Vital.

Under that layout, a Teth path would symbolize something quite different from the Kircher horizontal path: a descent from Wisdom toward the harmonizing heart of the small countenance, rather than the direct balancing of Mercy and Severity.

## 10. The Gra’s tree

Elijah ben Solomon Zalman, the Vilna Gaon, wrote a highly influential commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah*.

**[Documented text]** The Gra recension preserves Teth as a simple letter connected with hearing, Leo, Av, and a kidney.

**[Kabbalistic tradition/Diagrammatic reconstruction]** In the conventional “Gra tree,” Teth is placed between **Chesed and Tiferet**, not between Chesed and Geburah.

Therefore:

| System | Teth connects |
|---|---|
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Chesed–Geburah |
| Conventional Ari diagram | Chokhmah–Tiferet |
| Conventional Gra diagram | Chesed–Tiferet |

These differences are not trivial artistic variants. They change the symbolic operation assigned to the letter.

- Kircher: reconciliation or transmission between mercy and severity.
- Ari reception: wisdom descending toward beauty/harmony.
- Gra reception: mercy descending toward or expressing itself through harmony.

**[Disputed]** The manuscript and printing history of these Jewish tree diagrams is more complicated than the later labels “the Ari tree” and “the Gra tree” suggest. A diagram can reflect editorial arrangement, a disciple’s school, or a modern standardization rather than an autograph by Luria or the Gra.

## 11. Hasidic readings

### Tanya

**Absence finding:** There is no chapter of *Tanya* devoted to “the Path of Teth.” The request for “the Tanya’s chapter on this element” appears to import a Western path-dossier template into a work organized around the divine and animal souls, the sefirot as intellectual and emotional powers, contemplation, and worship.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Chabad teaching does interpret Teth through:

- *tov*, good;
- concealed goodness;
- the inward-folding form of the letter;
- gestation and emergence;
- the good hidden in apparently adverse experience.

Such interpretations should be cited to later Chabad expositions or alphabet teachings, not called a specific chapter of *Tanya*.

### Rabbi Nachman of Bratslav

**[Documented text]** *Likutey Moharan* invokes the thirty-two paths of Wisdom as Torah and treats hidden goodness and unity in characteristic Hasidic ways.

**Absence finding:** No distinct Nachman teaching establishing Teth as Kircher’s nineteenth Chesed–Geburah path was found. Connections between Nachman’s teachings on hidden good and the Zoharic Teth motif are interpretive comparisons.

## 12. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and calendar

### Prayer

No standard Jewish liturgical rubric assigns Teth a unique daily prayer or blessing.

Relevant textual uses include:

- the Teth stanza of Psalm 119, verses 65–72;
- alphabetic acrostics;
- liturgical meditation on Hebrew letters;
- later kabbalistic kavvanot involving combinations of divine names.

Psalm 119’s Teth stanza repeatedly uses the root טוב, good. This is a poetic alphabetic construction, not proof of the Hermetic path.

### Torah

The first Torah occurrence of Teth is the word **טוב**, “good,” in Genesis 1:4. This fact underlies the Talmudic and later mystical lore.

### Counting the Omer

**Absence finding:** Teth does not have a standard independent assignment in the counting of the Omer.

The kabbalistic Omer system combines the **seven lower sefirot** in a seven-by-seven cycle:

- Chesed
- Geburah
- Tiferet
- Netzach
- Hod
- Yesod
- Malkhut

Because Kircher’s Teth path joins Chesed and Geburah, modern occultists may associate it with their polarity. That is not the same as a traditional Omer day called “Teth.”

### Calendar

In the common *Sefer Yetzirah* arrangement:

- Letter: Teth
- Sign: Leo
- Month: Av
- Faculty: hearing
- Organ: a kidney

Later tribal correspondences vary. Av–Simeon is common in Jewish devotional calendars; Leo–Judah is prominent in Western occultism.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Giovanni Pico della Mirandola included forty-seven “according to the opinion of the Hebrew kabbalists” and seventy-two “according to his own opinion” among his *Conclusiones*.

**[Documented text]** Pico argued that Cabala could confirm Christian doctrines and treated Hebrew mystical traditions as prisca theologia or ancient theological wisdom.

**Absence finding:** Pico’s *Conclusiones* do not contain the later formula:

> Teth = Path 19 = Leo = Strength = Chesed–Geburah.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Pico depended on translations made by Flavius Mithridates and understood Jewish texts through a Christian theological program. His project marks a decisive moment in **Christian Cabala**, but not the first appearance of this path attribution.

**[Contested ethical-historical issue]** Christian Cabala preserved and disseminated Jewish materials while frequently detaching them from rabbinic authority and using them to claim that Judaism secretly proved Christianity. “Transmission,” “reinterpretation,” and “appropriation” are all relevant descriptions, depending on the particular text and power relationship.

---

## 2. Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica* (1517)

Johannes Reuchlin’s Latin dialogue presented Cabala as an ancient theological and linguistic wisdom compatible with Christianity.

**[Documented text]** Reuchlin discusses the Hebrew alphabet, divine names, number, permutation, and Christian theological interpretation. Latin editions use forms such as **Teth** for ט.

**Absence finding:** Reuchlin does not give the mature Golden Dawn attribution of Teth to the nineteenth Chesed–Geburah path and Strength. His importance lies in making a Latin Christian Cabala available to subsequent European readers.

---

## 3. *Portae Lucis* (1516)

*Portae Lucis* is Paolo Riccio’s Latin translation or adaptation of Gikatilla’s *Sha‘arei Orah*.

**[Documented object history]** Its 1516 title page contains the first famous printed sefirotic tree. The woodcut is often associated with Leonhard Beck.

**[Correction of a common claim]** It is not the earliest known kabbalistic tree diagram. Hebrew manuscripts contained cosmological and sefirotic diagrams before 1516. It is more accurately called the **first printed** sefirotic tree.

The image was a major visual step in the European transmission of Kabbalah, but its form should not be conflated with Kircher’s later fixed allocation of all twenty-two letters.

---

## 4. Kircher, *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654)

Athanasius Kircher’s *Systema Sephiroticum* supplied the decisive diagram for the later Western occult Tree of Life.

**[Documented text/image]** Kircher arranged:

- ten sefirot;
- twenty-two connecting paths;
- the Hebrew letters sequentially;
- angelic, divine-name, planetary, and cosmological material.

On this diagram:

- Aleph is path 11;
- Beth path 12;
- and so forth;
- Teth, the ninth letter, becomes **path 19**;
- Teth lies on the horizontal connection between **Chesed and Geburah**.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Kircher synthesized Jewish, Christian-Cabalist, scholastic, astrological, and antiquarian sources. His tree is not simply “the traditional Jewish tree” copied intact.

### Why this matters

The numbering works arithmetically:

- ten sefirot occupy paths or categories 1–10;
- the twenty-two letters occupy 11–32;
- Teth is the ninth letter;
- 10 + 9 = 19.

This is the basis of “Path 19.”

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** The later Golden Dawn inherited this topology. Direct institutional transmission from Kircher to the Golden Dawn may have passed through later occult reproductions rather than a single act of copying from *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*.

---

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–1684)

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* made extensive Zoharic and Lurianic materials available in Latin.

**[Documented text]** Its apparatus contains an alphabetic entry headed **Teth**, spanning several pages in the printed volume, and discusses Hebrew lexical and mystical material.

**Absence finding:** Rosenroth does not present the complete Golden Dawn equation Teth–Leo–Strength–Chesed/Geburah. His significance lies in Latin access to Zoharic and Lurianic sources, later used by Christian esotericists.

The work’s Latin terminology translated Jewish categories into a Christian intellectual environment. That translation widened access while often altering context.

---

## 6. Éliphas Lévi and the tarot alphabet

In *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* (1854–1856), Éliphas Lévi gave decisive impetus to correlating the twenty-two Hebrew letters with the twenty-two tarot trumps.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Lévi’s straightforward numerical order produces:

- Teth, ninth letter;
- tarot trump IX;
- **The Hermit**.

Strength in the traditional Marseille sequence is XI and therefore falls under Kaph in a direct one-to-one sequence.

This is a crucial historical result:

> Lévi did not originate the Golden Dawn’s Teth–Strength correspondence.

The Golden Dawn later altered the card order to preserve astrological correspondences.

## 7. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### The Knowledge Lectures

Founded in 1888, the Golden Dawn synthesized the Kircher tree, *Sefer Yetzirah*, astrology, tarot, Renaissance magic, Egyptianizing symbolism, alchemy, and Enochian material.

The Fourth Knowledge Lecture assigns the twenty-two letters to:

- three elements;
- seven planets;
- twelve zodiacal signs.

Within this system:

- Teth is a simple letter;
- Teth is Leo;
- Teth is path 19;
- path 19 joins Chesed and Geburah;
- its tarot trump is Strength.

### The Strength–Justice transposition

Traditional Marseille numbering normally gives:

- VIII: Justice
- XI: Strength

The Golden Dawn exchanged their numbers while retaining their images:

- VIII: Strength
- XI: Justice

This allowed:

- Teth/Leo → Strength;
- Lamed/Libra → Justice.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** The justification is symbolic and astrological:

- Teth receives Leo from *Sefer Yetzirah*;
- the Strength image contains a lion;
- Lamed receives Libra;
- Justice contains scales;
- the cards are renumbered to align with the zodiacal Hebrew sequence.

**[Disputed]** The surviving Golden Dawn Cipher Manuscripts reflect a transitional situation: letter and astrological correspondences are present while older tarot numerals may still appear. The polished VIII/XI arrangement belongs to the Order’s developed teaching and subsequent decks.

### Internal formula of the path

On the Kircher tree:

- Chesed is Mercy, expansion, Jupiter, and ordered beneficence.
- Geburah is Severity, restriction, Mars, judgment, and force.
- Teth crosses horizontally between them.
- Tiferet, the solar center, lies below.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** The path is consequently interpreted as the force that mediates or equilibrates Mercy and Severity through disciplined vitality. In formulaic Golden Dawn language, it is often expressed as **Jupiter acting through Leo upon Mars**.

The Strength image supplies the ethical key: power becomes effective through conscious mastery, courage, persuasion, and intimacy with instinct—not merely through suppression.

### *Book T*

The Golden Dawn tarot teachings collectively known as *Book T* were circulated privately before appearing through later publications. A substantial public form appeared in *The Equinox* and subsequently in Israel Regardie's Golden Dawn compendium.

The Strength divinatory complex includes:

- courage;
- fortitude;
- power;
- force passing into action;
- endurance;
- command of instinct.

Its adverse side includes:

- abuse of power;
- domination;
- despotism;
- uncontrolled passion.

These are divinatory and initiatory attributions, not virtues and vices taken from Jewish ethical literature.

## 8. The four colour scales

The Golden Dawn assigns each path four colours corresponding to the four worlds and four tarot court principles:

| World | Scale | Teth/Leo colour |
|---|---|---|
| Atzilut | King Scale | Yellow, often specified as greenish yellow |
| Beriah | Queen Scale | Deep purple |
| Yetzirah | Prince Scale | Grey |
| Assiah | Princess Scale | Reddish amber |

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** These colours are used in flashing tablets, temple diagrams, talismans, and pathworking. They are not medieval Jewish colour assignments.

Variations such as “greenish-yellow,” “yellow,” “deep violet,” “purplish,” or “red amber” reflect differences between manuscripts, printed tables, pigments, and modern deck production.

## 10. *Liber 231*

Crowley’s *Liber Arcanorum*, usually catalogued as *Liber 231*, gives brief verses and sigils for the twenty-two paths in their day- and nightside forms.

The Teth-keyed verse invokes Mother Earth and:

> “her lion, even Sekhet, the lady of Asi.”

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Here Teth’s lion becomes the Egyptian lioness goddess Sekhet/Sekhmet. The text’s visionary aphorisms are Thelemic revelation and magical poetry, not translations of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## 11. Crowley’s *Book of Thoth*: Strength becomes Lust

Crowley retained Teth, Leo, and the Kircher connection but renamed the card **Lust** and numbered it XI.

### Why XI?

Crowley restored Strength/Lust to the traditional Marseille position XI while continuing to place it under Teth. He therefore divorced the Hebrew sequence from straightforward numerical order on the cards.

### The image

Frieda Harris’s card shows:

- Babalon;
- riding the seven-headed Beast;
- holding the cup;
- immersed in fiery, ecstatic motion.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Crowley rejects the ordinary moral sense of “strength as repression.” Lust is wholehearted participation in divine energy—“the joy of strength exercised”—and the sanctification of desire through total conscious engagement.

On the tree, Crowley still reads Teth as the influence of Chesed upon Geburah: expansive divine will entering force and severity.

### Strength, Lust, and ethical difference

| System | Card | Principal emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Golden Dawn | Strength VIII | Courage, fortitude, controlled instinct |
| Rider–Waite–Smith | Strength VIII | Gentle mastery, compassion, inner force |
| Crowley–Harris | Lust XI | Ecstasy, desire consecrated to will, Babalon and Beast |
| Lévi’s direct alphabetic order | Hermit IX for Teth | Solitary wisdom rather than leonine force |

## 12. The Tzaddi/Heh controversy

Crowley’s *Liber AL* I:57 states, “Tzaddi is not the Star.” Crowley ultimately exchanged the Hebrew attributions of the Emperor and the Star:

- Emperor → Tzaddi;
- Star → Heh.

**Important finding:** This does **not** directly change Teth. The Teth controversy concerns Strength/Lust’s number VIII or XI. The Tzaddi/Heh controversy concerns the Emperor and Star. They are often conflated because both involve Crowley’s revision of tarot-letter ordering.

## 13. Dion Fortune, *The Mystical Qabalah* (1935)

**Correction:** There is no chapter devoted specifically to Teth, and Teth is not a sefirah.

Fortune’s book contains:

- introductory chapters;
- chapters on the ten sefirot;
- a general treatment of paths and the Tree;
- discussions of the magical image, virtues, vices, symbols, and spiritual experience.

The most relevant chapters are those on Chesed and Geburah and the general discussion of paths.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Applying Fortune’s method, Teth may be understood as the subjective transition or equilibrium between:

- Chesed’s constructive order, mercy, and expansive authority;
- Geburah’s discipline, destruction, judgment, and force.

**Copyright and source point:** Reproducing “the chapter in full” would be inappropriate and would also fail to answer the item correctly, because no Teth chapter exists.

## 14. Israel Regardie

Israel Regardie—listed twice in the requested topics—is one person. His importance is twofold:

1. He published and organized much of the Golden Dawn corpus in *The Golden Dawn*.
2. In *A Garden of Pomegranates*, he presented the paths as practical psychological and magical experiences.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Regardie’s path treatment combines:

- the Hebrew letter;
- tarot;
- astrology;
- mythology;
- psychology;
- ritual symbolism;
- astral vision.

His publications helped make the once-private Golden Dawn Teth complex the twentieth century’s standard English occult account.

## 15. Paul Foster Case

Paul Foster Case preserved much Golden Dawn structure in BOTA while revising emphases, colours, and meditation practices.

**[Documented occult text]** Case assigns:

- Key 8, Strength;
- Teth;
- Leo;
- the nineteenth path;
- the Intelligence involving the secret of spiritual activities.

BOTA practice stresses focused meditation on the tarot image, Hebrew letter, colour, sound, and astrological force. Case interprets the woman’s mastery of the lion as conscious direction of the serpent-power or reproductive life-force.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** This is more explicitly psychological and physiological than the medieval Jewish letter material.

## 16. Gareth Knight

Gareth Knight’s *Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* continues the Fortune–Regardie tradition.

Teth is approached as:

- a dynamic link between the forces of Chesed and Geburah;
- leonine vitality;
- spiritual courage;
- mastery without annihilation of instinct;
- transformation of raw desire into directed will.

**[Modern occult interpretation]** Knight’s value lies in experiential synthesis rather than historical reconstruction.

## 17. Pathworking traditions

### Golden Dawn skrying

A typical pathworking sequence may include:

1. ritual preparation and banishing;
2. visualization of the Tree;
3. concentration on the Teth letter;
4. use of the appropriate colour scale;
5. visualization of Strength;
6. vibration of divine and angelic names belonging to the adjacent sefirot;
7. entry through the tarot image or letter;
8. encounters with leonine, solar, martial, or regal forms;
9. testing of figures by symbols and names;
10. return, grounding, and written record.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** The Golden Dawn treats repeated symbolic coherence and ritual testing as criteria internal to astral work.

**[Scholarly qualification]** Pathworking narratives document practitioners’ experiences and traditions. They do not independently establish claims about ancient Jewish cosmology or objectively existing astral geography.

### Later styles

- Regardie emphasizes symbolic and psychological integration.
- Case/BOTA uses structured tarot meditation.
- Fortune and Knight emphasize balancing the psychospiritual qualities of connected sefirot.
- Thelemic work invokes Babalon, the Beast, Sekhmet, solar force, and ecstatic will.
- Kenneth Grant turns the path toward nightside, dream, sexual, and qliphothic imagery.

# The Qliphah and the tunnel

## Jewish qelippot versus the Western “Tree of Death”

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** In Lurianic Kabbalah, *qelippot*, “shells” or “husks,” arise through cosmic imbalance, particularly in relation to the breaking of vessels, fallen sparks, and forces that conceal holiness. They are not originally a simple black mirror containing one named demon-order for every sefirah and tunnel.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Western occultists systematized ten qliphothic orders opposite the ten sefirot and twenty-two adverse paths opposite the letter-paths.

Therefore:

- **Shalehbiron** is the qliphothic order assigned to the Teth/Leo path in *777*.
- **Temphioth** is Kenneth Grant’s sentinel or tunnel-name for the nightside counterpart of path 19.
- Neither is “the classical Jewish qliphah of Teth.”

## The ten qliphothic orders

| Sefirah | Western qliphothic order | Conventional meaning | Frequently assigned demon-prince | Historical caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Keter | Thaumiel | Dual contending forces | Satan and Moloch | Pairing stabilized in modern occult tables |
| Chokhmah | Ghagiel | Hinderers | Beelzebub | Spellings and rulers vary |
| Binah | Satariel | Concealers | Lucifuge Rofocale | Not a stable classical Jewish assignment |
| Chesed | Gha’agsheblah/Gamchicoth | Breakers in pieces | Astaroth | Variants include different spellings and rulers |
| Geburah | Golachab | Burners | Asmodeus | Western demonological synthesis |
| Tiferet | Thagirion | Disputers/ugly ones | Belphegor | Sometimes associated with a false or black sun |
| Netzach | A‘arab Zaraq | Ravens of dispersion | Baal | Multiple transliterations |
| Hod | Samael | Poisoners/deceivers | Adrammelech | “Samael” has a much older, more complex Jewish history |
| Yesod | Gamaliel | Obscene ones | Lilith | Lilith’s placement varies |
| Malkhut | Lilith/Nahemoth | Evil woman/whisperers | Naamah | Order and ruler frequently interchanged |

**[Documented occult text]** The order-names and glosses occur in Golden Dawn/Crowley tables.

**[Modern invention/Disputed]** The neat list of one Goetic-style “demon prince” per qliphah is a later synthesis. Even Crowley’s tables warn, implicitly or explicitly, that the princes are difficult to assign uniquely. Internet charts often present variant spellings and rulers as if a single ancient Jewish canon existed.

## Shalehbiron

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** *777* assigns the Teth/Leo path to **Shalehbiron**, Hebrewized as שלהבירון in modern tables.

The vocalization, exact morphology, and textual ancestry are uncertain. It belongs to the Golden Dawn/Crowley adverse-path list, not to the Zohar’s Teth narrative.

## Kenneth Grant’s tunnel of Temphioth

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* was published in 1977.

**[Modern invention]** Grant presents the reverse side of the Tree as tunnels associated with sigils, sentinels, sexual-magical formulae, dream states, and Typhonian entities.

For path 19:

- Tunnel/sentinel: **Temphioth** or **Temphiot**.
- Number: **610**.
- Sigil colours: sharp greenish yellow upon an arrow-shaped grey field.
- Imagery: lion-serpent, spermatozoon, female-and-beast union, Sekhet.
- Gematria: Grant connects 610 with transliterated words he understands as desire/lust and a coupling point.
- Magical context: IX° O.T.O.-style sexual symbolism and the Typhonian interpretation of Crowley.

**[Critical classification]** Temphioth is not found in *Sefer Yetzirah*, the *Bahir*, Zohar, *Pardes Rimmonim*, *Etz Chaim*, Kircher, or the early Golden Dawn documents. It is Grant’s twentieth-century Typhonian construction.

# People, works, diagrams, and cultural reception

## Jewish authors and transmitters

### Saadia Gaon

Saadia’s tenth-century commentary provides the earliest extensive surviving interpretation and preserves a distinctive recension. Its Teth correspondences differ from the Gra/common table, particularly in the sense/faculty and organ.

### Shabbetai Donnolo and Dunash ibn Tamim

Their early commentaries testify to medieval scientific, cosmological, philosophical, and astrological receptions of *Sefer Yetzirah*. They are important evidence against treating one late printed recension as the single original text.

### Judah Halevi

The *Kuzari* discusses *Sefer Yetzirah* and its theory of creation, lending philosophical authority to the work without supplying the later Hermetic Teth path.

### Gikatilla

He organized divine names through the sefirot in *Sha‘arei Orah*. His work became especially influential through the Latin *Portae Lucis*.

### Cordovero

He systematized pre-Lurianic Kabbalah in *Pardes Rimmonim* and explained how differentiated sefirot remain expressions of divine unity.

### Luria and Vital

Their school transformed the Tree into a dynamic myth of contraction, emanation, catastrophe, exile, and repair. Their importance to later trees is immense, but they did not teach Kircher’s Teth–Strength path.

### The Vilna Gaon

His recension and commentary became one of the most influential Jewish presentations of *Sefer Yetzirah*. The “Gra tree” differs materially from Kircher.

### Schneur Zalman and Rabbi Nachman

They represent Hasidic interiorization of sefirotic and cosmological ideas. Neither supplied a discrete Western-style Teth path dossier.

## Modern scholarship

- **Gershom Scholem** established the modern historical study of Kabbalah and emphasized the medieval emergence of classical Kabbalah and the historical role of the Zohar.
- **Moshe Idel** challenged overly linear developmental narratives and emphasized multiple ecstatic, theosophical, linguistic, and practical currents.
- **Elliot R. Wolfson** has studied language, embodiment, gender, vision, and symbolism in Jewish mysticism.
- **Joseph Dan** produced broad historical studies of Jewish mysticism and the Christian reception of Kabbalah.
- **A. Peter Hayman** produced the essential critical edition of *Sefer Yesira*.
- **Tzahi Weiss** has advanced a late-antique/early-medieval contextual reconstruction involving Syriac Christianity.
- **Daniel C. Matt** translated the Zohar in the Pritzker Edition with extensive textual annotation.
- **Ronit Meroz** and **Daniel Abrams** have emphasized the Zohar’s layered, evolving textuality.
- **J. H. Chajes** has documented the history of kabbalistic diagrams and *ilanot*.

## Artists

### Early diagrams

**[Documented object history]** Sefirotic and cosmological diagrams appear in Hebrew manuscripts before the age of print. The mature arboreal *ilan* becomes increasingly prominent in the fourteenth century and after.

### *Portae Lucis*

The 1516 title-page woodcut, commonly associated with Leonhard Beck, is the first celebrated printed sefirotic tree. Its importance is typographic and transmissive, not evidence that the tree-form began in 1516.

### Kircher’s plate

Kircher’s *Systema Sephiroticum* is the visual ancestor of the standard Western occult tree. Its balanced geometry, Latin captions, and sequential paths made it exceptionally reproducible.

### Pamela Colman Smith

Smith’s 1909 Strength card for Waite shows a woman gently closing or opening a lion’s jaws, with an infinity sign above her. It became the most familiar visual statement of Teth in popular tarot.

### Frieda Harris

Harris painted Crowley’s Lust card between approximately 1938 and 1943. Babalon rides the many-headed Beast amid radiant, orgasmic, solar motion. The deck was published with *The Book of Thoth* material in the 1940s and became broadly available later.

## Poets and novelists

W. B. Yeats, Arthur Machen, Algernon Blackwood, and Dion Fortune moved in or near Golden Dawn-derived literary cultures. Their works use initiatory, magical, solar, leonine, or tree symbolism.

**Absence finding:** No secure evidence was found that any major poem or novel by these writers was explicitly constructed as a technical treatment of “Path 19, Teth.” General Golden Dawn membership should not be converted into a claim about a specific path without textual evidence.

Dion Fortune’s fiction reflects her occult psychology, but the novels are not source-texts for ancient Teth doctrine.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. When was *Sefer Yetzirah* written?

- **Traditional claim:** Abrahamic origin, sometimes transmitted through Rabbi Akiva.
- **Older scholarly proposals:** early rabbinic centuries.
- **Recent reconstructions:** layered formation, often late antique or early medieval.
- **Weiss’s argument:** approximately seventh century in a Jewish setting conversant with Syriac Christian ideas.
- **Hayman’s contribution:** recensional and manuscript evidence undermines confidence in a single pristine printed text.

No exact date has commanded universal agreement.

## 2. Is Teth originally Leo?

The most accurate answer is qualified:

- **Yes**, in major received recensions including the influential Gra/common form.
- **Uncertain**, if “originally” means the earliest reconstructable stratum of the book.
- Faculties and organs vary substantially, showing secondary rearrangement.
- The zodiacal structure may itself have undergone development.

## 3. Does Teth mean serpent?

- **Occult answer:** Yes; Golden Dawn and Crowley use “serpent.”
- **Linguistic answer:** Not demonstrated.
- **Pictographic answer:** Several early-sign theories exist, none securely proves “serpent.”

## 4. Is path 19 part of *Sefer Yetzirah*?

No, not in the Golden Dawn sense.

The book has thirty-two paths as ten sefirot plus twenty-two letters. A later appendix has thirty-two numbered “Intelligences.” Kircher supplies the familiar geometric sequence, and the Golden Dawn combines the diagram, the appendix, astrology, and tarot.

## 5. Kircher versus Jewish trees

The three common arrangements give Teth three different connections:

- Kircher: Chesed–Geburah.
- Ari reception: Chokhmah–Tiferet.
- Gra reception: Chesed–Tiferet.

Calling Kircher’s pattern “the Kabbalistic Tree of Life” without qualification suppresses genuine Jewish diagrammatic diversity.

## 6. Tarot attribution

- Lévi’s direct numerical scheme makes Teth the Hermit.
- Golden Dawn symbolism makes Teth Strength.
- Waite retains Strength VIII.
- Crowley calls it Lust XI.
- The Tzaddi/Heh exchange affects Emperor and Star, not Teth directly.

No medieval Jewish source assigns tarot to Hebrew letters.

## 7. Christian Cabala as preservation or appropriation

It was both transmission and transformation.

Christian Cabalists:

- learned from Jewish texts and teachers;
- preserved and printed material;
- stimulated Hebrew scholarship;
- but frequently presented Kabbalah as secret proof of Christianity;
- detached it from Jewish law, community, and interpretive authority;
- and incorporated it into universalizing Christian or Hermetic genealogies.

The degree and kind of appropriation vary among Pico, Reuchlin, Kircher, and Rosenroth.

## 8. Zoharic authorship

- **Traditional:** Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and circle.
- **Classical modern scholarship:** Moses de León, late thirteenth century.
- **Recent refinement:** a layered Castilian corpus associated with de León but probably not reducible to a single author.

The historical and religious-authoritative questions should remain distinct.

## 9. Da‘at

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists:

> “Ten and not nine; ten and not eleven.”

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Da‘at is frequently treated as a state, conjunction, or functional manifestation of the upper sefirot rather than a permanent eleventh sefirah. Cordoverian and Lurianic schemes can display Da‘at under particular configurations without abandoning the canonical ten.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** Western diagrams often show Da‘at as an invisible or “false” sefirah in the Abyss. Some modern charts casually call it number eleven. That is a later simplification and conflicts with the foundational formula if taken literally.

## 10. Are qliphothic princes ancient Jewish assignments?

No.

Jewish traditions concerning Samael, Lilith, demonic forces, the “other side,” and qelippot are old and complex. The tidy ten-row table with one demon prince per qliphah is a Western occult synthesis. Grant’s tunnels are later still.

## 11. Does the nineteenth Intelligence belong to Teth?

Only after accepting the Kircher/Golden Dawn numbering. The Hebrew text calls something “the nineteenth path,” but does not identify it with a Hebrew letter. Other tree arrangements disrupt the equation.

# Open questions

1. **The letter-name’s origin:** No proposed etymology of *ṭēt*—wheel, enclosure, basket, spindle, or serpent—is securely proven.

2. **The earliest letter-form:** More epigraphic evidence would be needed to identify an unbroken Proto-Sinaitic ancestry for Teth.

3. **The earliest zodiacal layer of *Sefer Yetzirah*:** Manuscript comparison has not produced universal agreement about which astrological and anatomical attributions belong to the earliest attainable text.

4. **The stemma of the “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom”:** Its exact date, authorship, relation to early commentaries, and route into Rittangel and Westcott warrant further manuscript study.

5. **Jewish tree topology:** The earliest witness explicitly labeling Teth on each connection conventionally called “Ari” or “Gra” remains a more precise research question than simply asking what “the Ari” or “the Gra” taught.

6. **Kircher’s immediate sources:** Kircher’s diagram is documented, but the exact contribution of each Jewish, converted-Jewish, Christian-Cabalist, and printed intermediary remains open to detailed source comparison.

7. **Kircher to the Golden Dawn:** The structural dependence is clear; the precise chain of reproduced diagrams and institutional transmission is less certain.

8. **Tribal calendars:** Av–Simeon, Av–Asher, Av–Gad, and occult Leo–Judah tables require a full source-by-source genealogy.

9. **Shalehbiron:** Its original spelling, vocalization, derivation, and earliest manuscript occurrence remain obscure.

10. **Temphioth:** Grant’s source for the name and some of his 610 gematrias is not transparently documented. It may be creative construction rather than recovered tradition.

11. **Golden Dawn manuscript layers:** The relationship between cipher manuscripts, oral instruction, *Book T*, and later edited publication remains a field for archival work.

12. **Experiential claims:** Pathworking reports may be compared phenomenologically, but they cannot by themselves resolve historical claims about ancient Jewish sources.

---

# Conclusions

The “Path of Teth” is best understood as several historically distinct objects:

1. **A Hebrew letter** with value nine and an uncertain ancient name, richly associated in rabbinic and Zoharic lore with *tov*, goodness—especially goodness concealed.

2. **A simple letter in *Sefer Yetzirah***, assigned in influential recensions to Leo, Av, hearing, and a kidney, but with meaningful recensional variants.

3. **A later numbered Intelligence**, whose Hebrew title concerns the secret of spiritual activities but which does not itself identify Teth or a pair of sefirot.

4. **A Kircherian path**, numbered nineteen because Teth is the ninth letter after the ten sefirot, drawn horizontally between Chesed and Geburah.

5. **A Golden Dawn tarot path**, joining the lion of Leo to Strength, with four colour scales and a Jupiter–Leo–Mars formula.

6. **A Thelemic path of Lust**, transformed by Crowley into the ecstatic union of Babalon and the Beast.

7. **A modern nightside tunnel**, called Temphioth by Kenneth Grant, without a classical Jewish pedigree.

The Jewish and Hermetic accounts intersect through Hebrew letters, *Sefer Yetzirah*, biblical symbolism, and the sefirotic vocabulary. They are nevertheless not interchangeable. Teth as hidden good, Teth as Leo, Teth as a line on one of several Jewish diagrams, Teth as Strength, and Teth as Temphioth entered history at different times and through different textual acts.

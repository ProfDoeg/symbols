# The Path of Qoph: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **[Text]** Directly documented in a primary text or historical edition.
- **[Tradition]** A claim accepted within a Jewish or occult lineage, irrespective of modern historical dating.
- **[Reconstruction]** A conclusion proposed by textual historians, paleographers, or historians of religion.
- **[Hermetic]** A correspondence created or transmitted within Christian Cabala, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, or later occultism.
- **[Disputed]** A claim for which texts, recensions, diagrams, or scholars disagree.
- **[Modern]** A twentieth- or twenty-first-century elaboration, synthesis, or pathworking interpretation.

The principal finding is that “the Path of Qoph” is not one continuous doctrine. It is a stack of historically distinct materials:

1. *Sefer Yetzirah* supplies Qoph as a **simple letter** associated with Pisces and Adar, with varying bodily and experiential correspondences.
2. The “Corporeal Intelligence” comes from the separate, recensionally unstable **Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom** list later appended to editions of *Sefer Yetzirah*.
3. The path connecting Netzach and Malkuth comes from the **Kircher-type diagram**, not from *Sefer Yetzirah* itself.
4. Qoph–Pisces–Trump XVIII, the Moon, is the **Golden Dawn synthesis**.
5. Qulielfi and the “Tunnel of Qoph” belong to Crowley’s *Liber 231* and Kenneth Grant’s twentieth-century Nightside system.

These layers should not be collapsed into “ancient Kabbalah.”

# The name

## Hebrew spelling and pronunciation

The letter is written **ק** and named **קוֹף** or **קוּף**. Scholarly transliterations include *qōp̄*, *qoph*, and *qof*; *kuf/kuph* represents pronunciations in which the historical uvular consonant merged with /k/.

**[Reconstruction]** The consonant was historically a voiceless uvular or emphatic /q/, distinct from kaf כ /k/. Many modern Israeli speakers merge them, while a distinction remains in several Mizrahi and Yemenite pronunciations.

Older Western works employ *Coph*, *Koph*, *Kuph*, and *Qoph*. Mathers explicitly explained that he chose Roman **Q** for Hebrew Qoph/Koph in the introduction to *The Kabbalah Unveiled*. [Mathers’s text](https://hermetics.net/media-library/kabbalah/christian-knorr-von-rosenroth-samuel-liddell-macgregor-mathers-kabbala-denudata-the-kabbalah-unveiled/)

## Meaning and etymology

Three principal glosses circulate:

1. **“Eye of a needle.”**  
   **[Philological]** This is preferred by the Klein dictionary and Gesenius and has Hebrew and Aramaic lexical support. [Klein Dictionary](https://www.sefaria.org/Klein_Dictionary%2C_%D7%A7.1), [Gesenius](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Gesenius%27_Hebrew_Grammar/5._The_Consonants%3A_their_Forms_and_Names)

2. **“Back of the head” or “nape.”**  
   **[Disputed/Hermetic standard]** This became the nearly universal Golden Dawn and English occult gloss. It may be supported by comparison with an Arabic word for the nape, but it is not a securely demonstrated original pictographic meaning.

3. **“Monkey” or “ape.”**  
   **[Homonym/late symbolic interpretation]** קוֹף is the ordinary Hebrew word for monkey. The claim that the alphabetic sign originally depicted a monkey is an older conjecture, not an established paleographic result. Jewish homiletics and modern Adar teachings nevertheless use the monkey as an image of imitation, parody, laughter, or counterfeit holiness. [Modern Chabad presentation](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/137091/jewish/Kuf.htm)

Accordingly, “Qoph means the back of the head” should be labeled an **occult conventional gloss**, not an uncontested etymology.

## The history of the form

**[Reconstruction]** The responsible paleographic sequence is:

- Proto-alphabetic/Proto-Sinaitic antecedents, second millennium BCE: identification of the specific Qoph prototype and the object depicted remains uncertain.
- Phoenician **𐤒**, a circular sign crossed or divided by strokes.
- Paleo-Hebrew forms derived from the Phoenician alphabet.
- Imperial Aramaic forms after the Babylonian exile.
- Jewish “square” or *Ashurit* script during the Second Temple period.
- Rabbinic and medieval manuscript forms.
- Modern square **ק**.

The broad descent from early West Semitic through Phoenician and Aramaic is secure; the tidy internet narrative “a drawing of a monkey became Qoph” is not. The Unicode proposal on Proto-Sinaitic prudently leaves Qoph’s pictorial meaning uncertain. [Unicode Proto-Sinaitic proposal](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2019/19299-revisiting-proto-sinaitic.pdf)

In square script, Qoph has a roof and right body with a separate or nearly separate descending left leg. That leg passes below the normal writing line. This visible descent later became fertile material for rabbinic and occult interpretation.

## Talmudic interpretation of the shape

**[Text]** Babylonian Talmud, *Shabbat* 104a reads ק as the first letter of **קדוש**, “holy,” and ר as **רשע**, “wicked.” The separation and orientation of the letters are interpreted as God’s turning from wickedness, while Qoph’s suspended leg leaves an opening for repentance.

The passage asks why the leg hangs free and answers that, if the wicked person returns, “he may enter” through the prepared opening. This is genuine rabbinic letter lore, but it is not a Netzach–Malkuth path doctrine. [*Shabbat* 104a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104a?lang=en), [Steinsaltz commentary](https://www.sefaria.org/Steinsaltz_on_Shabbat.104a.9)

## Gematria

- ק = **100**.
- Its alphabetic ordinal is **19**.
- Spelled קוף, its ordinary value is 100 + 6 + 80 = **186**.
- **[Jewish homiletic tradition]** 186 also equals מקום, *Makom*, “Place,” a rabbinic divine epithet. *Ben Yehoyada* explicitly exploits this equality: the filled-out Qoph indicates holiness because it equals *Makom*. [*Ben Yehoyada* on *Pesachim* 49b](https://www.sefaria.org/Ben_Yehoyada_on_Pesachim.49b.1)

Qoph is not present in יהוה, אהיה, יה, אל, אלהים, שדי, צבאות, or אדני. Claims that it possesses a unique ancient “divine name” therefore require a later ritual source.

## Biblical occurrences and prooftexts

### Psalm 119:145–152

**[Text]** The Qoph stanza consists of eight verses whose first words begin with ק:

> קָרָאתִי בְכׇל־לֵב — “I call with all my heart.”

Its themes are prayer, the watches of the night, nearness, Torah, and divine truth. The stanza provides a genuine scriptural Qoph cycle and is used in some modern Ari-tree pathworkings, but Scripture does not call it “Path 29.” [Psalm 119:145–152](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.119.145)

### The usual Sefirotic prooftexts

These explain names of Sephiroth, not the name Qoph:

- **1 Chronicles 29:11** supplies the seven lower names or their prooftexts: Gedulah/Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, “all” as Yesod, and Mamlakhah as Malkuth. [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11)
- **Proverbs 3:19** names Wisdom and Understanding in creation. [Proverbs 3:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19)
- **Job 28** treats hidden Wisdom and the path leading to it. [Job 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28)
- **Isaiah 11:2** provides wisdom, understanding, counsel, might and knowledge language later read Sefirotically. [Isaiah 11:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.11.2)

**Absence finding:** none of these verses supplies “Qoph,” “Pisces,” “the Moon,” or “Corporeal Intelligence.”

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date and textual history

**[Tradition]** Jewish tradition ascribes *Sefer Yetzirah* to Abraham, sometimes with transmission or redaction associated with Rabbi Akiva.

**[Reconstruction/Disputed]** The received texts are much later. Peter Hayman’s critical edition prints the Short, Long, and Saadyan witnesses synoptically and emphasizes their already unstable state in the earliest surviving manuscripts, around the tenth century. [Hayman, edition description](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/)

Proposed dates range from the early centuries CE to the early Islamic period. Tzahi Weiss argues for seventh-century Syria, partly from Syriac grammatical vocabulary; Yehuda Liebes and some others have argued for earlier layers. The seventh-century case is influential but not a unanimously final settlement. [Weiss](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv16t6jtm), [Zinner’s assessment](https://brill.com/view/journals/ic/27/4-5/article-p407_9.pdf)

### The thirty-two paths

**[Text]** The book opens with thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom:

- ten ספירות בלימה, *sefirot belimah*;
- twenty-two foundation letters;
- three mothers;
- seven doubles;
- twelve simples.

The word *sefirot* here may still carry numerical, dimensional, or cosmological senses not identical to the fully personified divine emanations of the Bahir, Zohar, and later Kabbalah.

### Qoph as a simple letter

**[Text]** In the standard Sefaria text:

> המליך אות ק׳ וקשר לו כתר וצר בו דגים בעולם ואדר בשנה וקרקבן בנפש

“He made Qoph reign, bound a crown to it, and formed Pisces in the world, Adar in the year, and the craw in the person.” [*Sefer Yetzirah* 5:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.5.2?with=Tanakh+ConnectionsList)

The most stable Qoph correspondences across the major recensions are:

- Qoph;
- Pisces;
- Adar.

The sense and anatomical member are less stable.

### Recensional variants

| Recension or editorial form | Qoph faculty | Body | Sign/month | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sefaria standard text | Not stated in the individual sentence | קרקבן, craw/gizzard | Pisces/Adar | **[Text]** |
| Gra printed version 5:10 | שחוק, laughter | טחול, spleen | Pisces/Adar | **[Text]** [Gra text](https://voices.sefaria.org/sheets/545787) |
| Saadyan recension | Sleep/languor in its recapitulation | Left foot in common translations | Pisces/Adar | **[Text/recensional]** |
| Kaplan’s Gra/ARI-oriented table | Sleep; some adjacent printed passages produce laughter through differing orderings | Left foot or spleen depending textual layer | Pisces/Adar | **[Disputed/editorial synthesis]** |
| Short/Long witnesses | The faculty and organ vary because the lists have been copied, reordered, or harmonized differently | Craw, spleen, or limb | Pisces/Adar | **[Textual instability]** |

Aryeh Kaplan’s comparative table is useful precisely because it shows that “the Qoph correspondence” is not singular. [Kaplan PDF](https://leonbahrmanministries.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Aryeh-Kaplan-Sefer-Yetzirah2.pdf)

The common modern formula “Qoph = Pisces = Adar = laughter = spleen” is the **Gra printed constellation**. “Qoph = sleep = left foot” represents another important line of transmission. Occultists frequently select “sleep” because it supports subconscious and dream interpretations.

### Direction

The twelve simples define the twelve diagonal boundaries of space. Their directional order varies among recensions and commentaries. Consequently, a directional assignment for Qoph cannot be presented as recension-independent.

More importantly, these spatial boundaries are not a statement that Qoph connects two named Sephiroth on the now-familiar ten-circle diagram. That graphical localization is later.

## 2. The “Corporeal Intelligence”

### Hebrew

A Hebrew recension of the separate Thirty-two Paths text reads:

> הנתיב כ״ט נקרא שכל מוגשם … מפני שהוא מתאר כל גשם אשר יתגשם תחת תכונת כל הגלגלים בגידולם

“The twenty-ninth path is called Embodied Intelligence … because it delineates every body embodied beneath the order of all the spheres in its growth.” [Hebrew text](https://or-breslev.co.il/wp-content/uploads/%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%91%D7%93.pdf)

### Westcott

Westcott’s 1887/1893 English version says:

> “The Twenty-ninth Path is the Corporeal Intelligence … it forms every body … beneath the whole set of Worlds.”

The complete sentence continues with their increase or growth. [Westcott’s 1893 edition, p. 31](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1893__westcott___sepher_yetzirah.pdf)

**Critical distinction:** this list is not part of the earliest reconstructable core of *Sefer Yetzirah*. It circulated as a separate or appended text, with wording and numbering variants. Westcott depended heavily on Johann Stephan Rittangelius’s Latin *Liber Jezirah* of 1642 rather than a modern manuscript recension. Calling it simply “what *Sefer Yetzirah* says about Qoph” conceals this textual history.

Nor does the Hebrew passage identify the twenty-ninth intelligence with Qoph, Pisces, the Moon, or Netzach–Malkuth. Those identifications arise when later occultists lay several numbered systems over one another.

## 3. *Sefer ha-Bahir*

**[Text]** The *Bahir*, appearing in Provence in the late twelfth century but incorporating older materials, develops divine powers, channels, letters, the Tree, and the symbolism of thirty-two paths. [*Bahir*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.1?lang=he)

It says that the written and oral Torah are associated with the thirty-two paths and treats letters such as bet and gimel symbolically.

**Absence finding:** the extant *Bahir* does not give a dossier for “Path 29 of Qoph,” does not join Qoph to Netzach–Malkuth, and does not assign it Trump XVIII. Later diagrams sometimes called “Bahir trees” are reconstructions, not plates printed in the medieval text.

## 4. Rabbinic and alphabet-midrash lore

### *Shabbat* 104a

As noted above, Qoph is **Kadosh**, holiness, paired against Resh as **rasha**, wickedness. Its suspended leg symbolizes a path of repentance. This is among the earliest securely transmitted Qoph-specific symbolic readings.

### *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

**[Tradition/Textually unstable]** The *Alphabet of Rabbi Akiva* survives in multiple medieval forms, falsely or pseudonymously attributed to the second-century sage. Its letters petition or reveal divine mysteries. Qoph can claim **קדוש**, holy, but is also vulnerable to words of curse or destructive association beginning with ק.

The exact Qoph speech differs between versions A and B. It should not be quoted as one fixed “ancient teaching of Rabbi Akiva.” [National Library of Israel record](https://www.nli.org.il/en/books/NNL_ALEPH990011568050205171/NLI), [Vatican Hebrew manuscript 228](https://spotlight.vatlib.it/selected-manuscripts-from-the-polonsky-foundation-digitization-project/catalog/Vat_ebr_228)

### The letters seeking to begin the Torah

The best-known sequence is in the Zoharic prologue, not the original stratum of *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*. The letters approach God in reverse order. When Shin appeals through שדי, God rejects it because Shin is enlisted with Qoph and Resh to spell שקר, *sheqer*, “falsehood.” Qoph and Resh subsequently acquire Shin to form קשר, *qesher*, a “conspiracy” or false bond.

**[Text]** In Daniel Matt’s Pritzker translation this occurs near Zohar 1:2b. [Zohar Introduction on Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar%2C_Introduction.1.1-34.260), [Pritzker Zohar, vol. 1](https://big.shi.yt/~mac/zohar/Zohar_Vol_1.pdf)

This is the most important Zoharic Qoph legend. It supplies themes of counterfeit resemblance, false connection, and the danger of holiness mimicked by its shadow. It still does not supply the Kircher path.

## 5. The Zohar

**[Tradition]** The Zohar presents itself as teachings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle in second-century Galilee.

**[Reconstruction]** It appeared in late thirteenth-century Castile through Moses de León. Gershom Scholem argued that de León composed the principal body; recent scholarship more often speaks of a de León-centered circle and multiple strata. Arthur Green’s introduction to the Pritzker edition stresses textual plurality rather than one simple authorial event. [Pritzker front matter](https://www.sup.org/books/pages/4810/Front_Matter.pdf), [Scholem, *Major Trends*](https://www.bard.edu/library/pdfs/archives/Scholem-Major_Trends_in_Jewish_Mysticism.pdf)

The Zohar develops:

- ten Sefirot as the dynamic life of divinity;
- male and female configurations;
- the Other Side, *sitra aḥra*;
- shells, demonic couples, and distorted emanation;
- scriptural and letter symbolism.

But there is no Zoharic teaching that “Qoph is the Moon card on the twenty-ninth path from Netzach to Malkuth.”

## 6. Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

Moses Cordovero systematized earlier Kabbalah into gates dealing with the Sefiroth, divine names, channels, colours, angels, Scripture, and the demonic side. [*Pardes Rimmonim*](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim?lang=he&tab=default)

Gate 8 explains the derivation of the seven lower Sefirotic names from 1 Chronicles 29:11. [Translated selections](https://voices.sefaria.org/sheets/718684)

Cordovero’s discussions of paths are relational channels among Sefiroth. Surviving printed diagrams associated with the *Pardes* differ, and letter labels are not consistently present.

**Absence finding:** no securely identified passage in the 1548 *Pardes* establishes the Golden Dawn equation:

> Qoph = path 29 = Netzach–Malkuth = Pisces = Moon.

### *Or Ne’erav*

*Or Ne’erav* is a concise introduction to the study and ethical prerequisites of Kabbalah. [*Or Ne’erav*](https://www.sefaria.org/Ohr_Ne%27erav%2C_Introduction.25)

It does not contain a Golden Dawn-style Qoph pathworking. Its relevance is methodological: Kabbalah is approached through Torah, commandments, ethical purification, prayer, and authorized study, rather than by astral travel through Tarot images.

## 7. Gikatilla’s *Sha’arei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla’s *Sha’arei Orah*, composed in the late thirteenth century, proceeds through the ten principal divine names from Malkuth upward and shows how scriptural names channel prayer.

Its lower Sefirotic associations include:

- Malkuth: אדני, *Adonai*;
- Yesod: אל חי or שדי in overlapping traditions;
- Netzach and Hod: the Tzevaot names;
- Tiferet: יהוה;
- Chesed: אל;
- Gevurah: אלהים.

These are Sefirotic and divine-name structures. They do not yield a specific divine name for Qoph.

The Latin *Portae Lucis* of 1516, translated by the convert Paulus Ricius, placed an early printed Sefirotic image on its title page. [Historical study](https://www.sephardichorizons.org/Volume15/Issue1/Heller-Gikatilla.html)

## 8. Luria through Chaim Vital

### Historical system

Isaac Luria taught in Safed from approximately 1570 until his death in 1572. Chaim Vital recorded and repeatedly reorganized his teachings; *Etz Chaim* is therefore a Vitalian textual monument to the Lurianic school rather than a book written by Luria himself. [*Etz Chaim*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.1.1-5.4)

Central doctrines include:

- **Tzimtzum:** the primordial withdrawal or concealment that permits finite differentiation.
- **Adam Qadmon:** the primordial configuration through which lights emerge.
- **Four worlds:** Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, Assiah, used in complex nested arrangements.
- **Shevirat ha-kelim:** the breaking of vessels, particularly in the world of points/Niqqudim.
- **Nitzotzot:** sparks trapped among the shells.
- **Tikkun:** reconfiguration and repair.
- **Partzufim:** divine configurations or “faces,” including Arikh Anpin, Abba, Imma, Zeir Anpin, and Nukva.

Qoph may participate in Lurianic letter permutations, prayer intentions, and calendar meditations, but *Etz Chaim* does not present a chapter titled “The Path of Qoph.”

### The so-called Ari tree

A common modern reconstruction attributed to the Ari preserves:

- three horizontal mother-letter paths;
- seven vertical double-letter paths;
- twelve diagonal simple-letter paths;
- only one connection into Malkuth, from Yesod.

On Kaplan’s Ari-type arrangement, **Qoph joins Binah and Chesed**. This replaces the Kircher tree’s lower Netzach–Malkuth Qoph line with a diagonal across the upper structure.

**[Disputed/Reconstruction]** “The Ari tree” is not one universally fixed plate personally drawn and authorized by Luria. Lurianic manuscripts and ilanot contain concentric, anthropomorphic, nested, linear, and highly elaborate diagrams. Modern simple line diagrams are interpretive normalizations.

## 9. The Gra’s tree

The Vilna Gaon, Elijah ben Solomon Zalman (1720–1797), produced an influential recension and commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah*.

The Gra configuration rearranges the usual vertical positions: Tiferet appears high in the place frequently occupied by Da’at, with Yesod and Malkuth correspondingly elevated. In the Kaplan reconstruction:

- mothers occupy horizontal paths;
- doubles occupy verticals;
- simples occupy diagonals;
- **Qoph joins Hod and Malkuth**.

Thus:

| Diagram | Qoph joins |
|---|---|
| Ari reconstruction | Binah–Chesed |
| Gra reconstruction | Hod–Malkuth |
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Netzach–Malkuth |

This disagreement alone demonstrates that “the ancient path of Qoph joins Netzach and Malkuth” is historically indefensible.

The comparative material is discussed in Kaplan’s *Sefer Yetzirah* and reproduced in Don Karr’s survey. [Karr](https://www.digital-brilliance.com/contributed/Karr/Maat/AKM.pdf)

## 10. Hasidic readings

### Tanya

**Absence finding:** *Tanya* has no chapter devoted to Qoph, Pisces, or “Path 29.” The familiar request for “the Tanya chapter on this element” appears to transfer a Sephirah-dossier template to a letter-path.

*Tanya* does give a Hasidic psychology of:

- divine and animal souls;
- thought, speech, and action;
- concealment and kelippah;
- the vivifying force of Hebrew letters;
- descent for the purpose of ascent.

Those concepts can be brought into dialogue with Qoph’s themes, but doing so is comparative interpretation, not a Qoph chapter by Shneur Zalman.

### Other Hasidic material

**[Text/Tradition]** *Peri Tzadik* explicitly links Adar, Qoph, holiness, and the defeat of Amalek. It contrasts the ק of קדושה with the “ape-like” imitation of sanctity associated with the destructive side. [*Peri Tzadik*, Mishpatim 9](https://www.sefaria.org/Peri_Tzadik%2C_Mishpatim.9.1)

**[Hasidic tradition]** Adar is developed through laughter, Purim reversals, concealment, sleep, and hidden providence. Naphtali, associated with swiftness and “beautiful words,” is the most common later tribal attribution; some calendars instead assign Adar to Joseph, particularly when explaining the duplication of Adar in leap years.

### Rebbe Nachman

Nachman of Breslov often treats:

- joy as a religious obligation;
- holy foolishness;
- transformation of melancholy;
- concealment within concealment;
- midnight Torah and prayer;
- dreams and imaginative faculties.

These themes resemble later Qoph pathworkings. No securely located passage establishes a Breslov “Path of Qoph” equivalent to the Golden Dawn path.

## 11. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and calendar

### Prayer and Torah

The most direct Qoph prayer-text is Psalm 119:145–152. Its repeated calling, night watches, nearness, and fidelity to Torah support Jewish contemplative readings without importing Tarot.

### Counting the Omer

The Kabbalistic Omer cycles the seven lower Sefiroth through one another over forty-nine days. Qoph is not assigned a canonical Omer day. Adar occurs before the Omer season, which begins in Nisan.

Any “Qoph day of the Omer” is consequently a modern private scheme, not the standard Lurianic count.

### Calendar

| Category | Qoph attribution |
|---|---|
| Month | Adar |
| Zodiac | Pisces/Dagim |
| Faculty | Laughter or sleep |
| Body | Spleen, craw/gizzard, or left foot |
| Tribe | Usually Naphtali in later Ari/Hasidic calendars; Joseph in some alternative calendars |
| Festival complex | Purim, joy, disguise, reversal, concealed providence |
| Leap year | Adar I and Adar II provoke later Joseph/Ephraim-Manasseh interpretations; this is not stated by the base *Sefer Yetzirah* |

The tribe is absent from the terse Qoph sentence in *Sefer Yetzirah*. It is a later calendrical overlay.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* contain forty-seven theses “according to the secret teaching of the Hebrew Cabalists” and seventy-one conclusions “according to my own opinion.” [Latin text](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Conclusiones_philosophicae%2C_cabalisticae_et_theologicae), [edited Latin](https://www.esotericarchives.com/pico/conclus.htm)

**[Text]** Pico divides Cabala into the science of Sefiroth and the science of Names and argues that Cabala and magic confirm Christian mysteries.

**Absence finding:** Pico does not attest:

- Path 29 as Qoph;
- Qoph joining Netzach–Malkuth;
- Qoph as the Moon trump;
- a Qoph colour scale;
- Qulielfi.

Pico’s historical significance is Christian reinterpretation of Jewish texts, not creation of the later path correspondence.

## 2. Johannes Reuchlin, 1517

Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* presents a dialogue among a Pythagorean, a Muslim, and a Jewish teacher. It integrates Hebrew divine names, number, language, Pythagoreanism, and Christian theology. [1517 scan](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105)

Reuchlin also defended Jewish books against proposed destruction, although his Cabala remained Christological.

**Absence finding:** no Qoph–Moon path system appears. Latin alphabet tables could spell the letter *Coph*, but this is not the name of a named occult path.

## 3. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paulus Ricius’s Latin translation of Gikatilla appeared one year before *De arte cabalistica*. Its Burgkmair title-page woodcut is generally identified as the first printed Sefirotic tree image. It has eighteen connecting channels rather than the later Kircher system and no Golden Dawn path attributions. [Study and image history](https://www.sephardichorizons.org/Volume15/Issue1/Heller-Gikatilla.html)

Earlier Jewish manuscript diagrams existed; 1516 is the earliest known **printed** image, not the invention of Sefirotic diagrams.

## 4. Athanasius Kircher, 1652

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I, contains the influential *Systema Sephiroticum* and *Derivatio Cabalistica*. [Kircher transcription and plate](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm), [Cornell plate](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272)

On this diagram:

- the ten Sefiroth assume the now-familiar three-column geometry;
- Malkuth receives three paths;
- the twenty-two letters are written on the channels;
- **Coph/Qoph is placed between Netzach and Malkuth**.

This is the earliest securely located printed attestation of the exact Qoph endpoint assignment used by the Golden Dawn.

**[Disputed]** Kircher did not invent the entire geometry from nothing. The diagram has Jewish, converted-Jewish, and Christian-Cabalist antecedents, including Philippe d’Aquin and printed *Pardes* traditions. What Kircher securely documents is this particular lettered synthesis.

Kircher did not assign Qoph to Tarot XVIII. Tarot–letter systems came later.

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

*Kabbala Denudata* was the largest early modern Latin anthology of Kabbalah. It translated and excerpted:

- Zoharic material;
- Lurianic writings;
- Cordoverian and Gikatillan materials;
- Abraham Cohen de Herrera;
- Sefirotic and qliphothic terminology.

[1677–84 scans](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/10759091)

Knorr’s importance for later occultism is transmission. Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* translated selected portions from Knorr rather than directly from Zoharic manuscripts.

**Absence finding:** the *Kabbala Denudata* does not contain the Golden Dawn Qoph–Moon Tarot attribution. Its Latin/Hebrew apparatus supplied vocabulary and diagrams later reorganized by occult orders.

## 6. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* made the twenty-two Tarot trumps into a symbolic alphabet and publicly cemented the Tarot–Hebrew-letter project. [French scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ADogme_et_rituel_de_la_haute_magie_%28IA_b24884340_0001%29.pdf)

Lévi’s sequential scheme differs from the Golden Dawn:

- the unnumbered/zero Fool is displaced;
- Aleph begins with the Magician;
- the nineteenth Hebrew letter, Qoph/Coph, corresponds to the nineteenth numbered trump, **the Sun**, not the Moon.

Thus Qoph–Moon is not simply “Lévi’s attribution.”

Papus retained a French-style formula in which the “nineteenth letter Coph” accompanies the Sun while also acknowledging its zodiacal Pisces attribution. [Papus in Spanish translation](https://web.seducoahuila.gob.mx/biblioweb/upload/Papus%20-%20El%20Tarot%20De%20Los%20Bohemios%20%28Parte%203%29.pdf)

## 7. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### Formation of the synthesis

The Golden Dawn, founded in 1888 by Westcott, Mathers, and Woodman, combined:

- Kircher-type path geometry;
- the letter classes of *Sefer Yetzirah*;
- astrology;
- Tarot;
- alchemy;
- Rosicrucian and Masonic initiation;
- Egyptianizing ritual imagery;
- four-world colour scales.

For Qoph this produces:

> Path 29 → Qoph → Pisces → Moon XVIII → Netzach–Malkuth.

### Knowledge Lectures and ritual

The Philosophus material says that Netzach is reached through Qoph from Malkuth, Tzaddi from Yesod, and Peh from Hod. [Golden Dawn “Garden of Eden” lecture](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Garden_of_Eden.html)

The ritual calls Qoph the Corporeal Intelligence and explains that the waters of Chesed flow down the Pillar of Mercy through Netzach toward Malkuth. The Moon card’s drops, dogs, towers, winding path, water, and crayfish become a visual epitome of the path. [Philosophus ritual](https://www.magicgatebg.com/Books/Aleister%20Crowley/Philosophus%20by%20Aleister%20Crowley.pdf)

### Tarot rationale

The Golden Dawn’s logic was principally combinatorial:

1. Qoph is the last of the twelve simples.
2. The twelve simples are matched to the zodiac.
3. Qoph is Pisces.
4. The twenty-two Tarot trumps are redistributed to match three elements, seven planets, and twelve zodiacal signs.
5. Trump XVIII is assigned to Pisces/Qoph.
6. Kircher’s Qoph line supplies the endpoints Netzach–Malkuth.

Symbolic reinforcement was then read back into the card:

- Pisces and the pool;
- lunar tides, “flux and reflux”;
- animal and human consciousness in dog and wolf;
- life emerging from water;
- a perilous path between towers;
- reflected rather than direct light;
- dreams, sleep, automatic bodily intelligence, sexuality, and reproduction.

These explanations justify the correspondence internally; they do not prove that historical Tarot designers encoded Qoph.

### Book T

The Golden Dawn Tarot manuscript known as *Book T* gives the Moon:

- Qoph;
- Pisces;
- “Ruler of Flux and Reflux; Child of the Sons of the Mighty.”

Substantial portions became public through Crowley’s *Equinox* I:8 in 1912. [*Equinox* I:8 scan](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/equinox_1_8_1912/equinox_v1_8_text.pdf), [online text](https://hermetic.com/crowley/equinox/i/viii/eqi08016)

### Four colour scales

| World | Golden Dawn scale | Qoph colour |
|---|---|---|
| Atziluth | King | Crimson, sometimes qualified “ultra-violet” |
| Briah | Queen | Buff, flecked silver-white |
| Yetzirah | Prince/Emperor | Light translucent pinkish-brown |
| Assiah | Princess/Empress | Stone colour |

These colours are Golden Dawn magical technology, not Jewish scriptural attributes. [*777*, 1909 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1909__crowley___777.pdf)

### *Liber 231*

*Liber 231* supplies two series of twenty-two sigils and genii: the “Houses of Mercury” and the “Cells of the Qliphoth.”

For Qoph:

- luminous-side name: **Qani-Δ-nayx-ipamai**, in the printed occult orthography;
- qliphothic genius: **Qulielfi**;
- the verse corresponding to Trump XVIII invokes the scarab Kheph-Ra, divided waters, spells, and destroyed illusion.

Crowley’s brief line reads:

> “By her spells she invoked the Scarab, the Lord Kheph-Ra.”

[*Liber 231*](https://sacred-texts.com/oto/lib231.htm)

Qulielfi therefore predates Kenneth Grant’s tunnel exposition. Grant did not invent the name, but he greatly enlarged its mythology.

### *The Book of Thoth*

Crowley’s *Book of Thoth* retains Qoph–Pisces–Moon. Lady Frieda Harris’s card depicts:

- twin dark towers;
- Anubian guardians;
- a blood-tinged path or stream;
- drops descending from the Moon;
- the scarab carrying the Sun through darkness.

Crowley interprets the card through nocturnal passage, sexuality, fear, illusion, mutation, and the Sun’s concealed rebirth. [*Book of Thoth* scan](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/crowley/equinox/volume3/vol_3_no_05.pdf)

### The Tzaddi/Heh question

*Liber AL* I:57 states, “Tzaddi is not the Star.” Crowley eventually reassigned the Emperor and Star symbolism between Heh and Tzaddi.

This does **not** directly change Qoph:

- Qoph remains Pisces.
- The Moon remains XVIII.
- The path remains Netzach–Malkuth in Crowley’s ordinary tree.

It changes neighboring interpretive geometry and the ordering logic of the trumps, not Qoph itself.

### Thelemic grades

Qoph spans the lower-grade movement from Malkuth toward Netzach:

- Malkuth: Zelator 1°=10□ in A∴A∴ terminology;
- Netzach: Philosophus 4°=7□.

The Golden Dawn Philosophus ritual treats Qoph as one of the three avenues entering Netzach. This is an initiatory attribution, not a Jewish grade system.

## 9. Dion Fortune

*The Mystical Qabalah* was published in 1935 and is organized primarily by Sephiroth, not by the twenty-two individual paths.

**Absence finding:** there is no chapter “on Qoph” to reproduce. A request for “the chapter on this Sephira in full” is inapplicable because Qoph is not a Sephirah and Fortune did not devote a full chapter to it.

Fortune’s relevant contribution is her psychology of:

- Netzach as emotion, instinct, art, nature, and group-mind;
- Malkuth as physical manifestation;
- Yesod as the astral and reproductive foundation;
- pathworking as an interaction of symbols rather than a bare list of equivalents.

A full chapter would in any event be unnecessary and copyright-sensitive; the evidentiary fact is that no Qoph chapter exists. [1935 text](https://www.awakening-intuition.com/FortuneDion-TheMysticalQabbalah.pdf)

## 10. Israel Regardie

In *A Garden of Pomegranates* (1931), Regardie makes the familiar psychological synthesis:

- Qoph, 100, “back of the head”;
- Corporeal Intelligence;
- Pisces;
- Moon XVIII;
- Netzach–Malkuth;
- Khephra, Neptune/Poseidon, Matsya, fish and dolphin;

[*A Garden of Pomegranates*, pp. 88–89](https://lib.wildla.in/book/A%20Garden%20of%20Pomegranates%20-%20An%20Outline%20of%20the%20Qabalah%20by%20Israel%20Regardie.pdf)

His later publication of Golden Dawn papers made this synthesis far more accessible. He did not independently document it in pre-Golden Dawn Jewish sources.

## 11. Paul Foster Case

Case’s B.O.T.A. teaching retains Qoph–Pisces–Moon and interprets the path through subconscious “body-building” intelligence, sleep, automatic functions, and evolution.

His joint *Dissertation Concerning the Thirty-two Paths* claims that the Corporeal Intelligence organizes physical bodies. Its speculation that “ape” might anticipate Darwinian evolution is a conspicuous modern projection. [Case and Whitty](https://hermetics.net/media-library/kabbalah/paul-foster-case-and-michael-whitty-a-dissertation-concerning-the-thirty-two-paths-of-wisdom/)

In *The Tarot* (1947), Case interprets Key XVIII as the organization of subconscious life, with the pool associated with the lunar foundation and the path as evolutionary ascent. [Case, *The Tarot*](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1947__case___the_tarot.pdf)

## 12. Gareth Knight

Knight’s *Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* continues the Fortune–Regardie tradition. Qoph is treated as a channel from physical consciousness into the emotive and imaginative power of Netzach, mediated by lunar-Piscean symbolism.

His importance is pedagogical and experiential rather than historical. [Knight, volume II](https://maestrosdelsaber.com/wp-content/uploads/ftp-files/Kabbalah/Kab%20en%20General/Kab%20Knight%2C%20Gareth%20-%20A%20Practical%20Guide%20to%20Qabalistic%20Symbolism%20Vol%202.pdf)

## 13. Modern pathworking

Modern Golden Dawn-derived pathworking usually proceeds from Malkuth upward:

1. Entry through a stone, lunar, watery, or twilight landscape.
2. Encounter with dogs, jackals, Anubis, a fish-being, Oannes, Khephra, or the Moon card’s towers.
3. Exposure to fear, dream distortion, bodily memory, sexuality, and unconscious images.
4. Testing images against divine names and known correspondences.
5. Emergence into Netzach as clarified intuition, imagination, or devotion.

Chic and Tabatha Cicero’s *Skrying on the Tree of Life* presents Qoph as body consciousness, reproduction, dream, intuition, and unconscious fear. [Cicero, pp. 104 onward](https://logoilibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/skrying_on_the_tree_of_life-tabatha_cicero.pdf)

These accounts are experiential occult literature. Similarities among visions may result from shared imagery and scripts and are not independent evidence for an objectively existing ancient path.

# The Qliphah or tunnel

## Jewish qelippah versus an inverted occult tree

קליפה, *qelippah*, means shell, husk, rind, or covering. Jewish sources use shells and the *sitra aḥra* for parasitic, obstructive, or distorted forces. Lurianic Kabbalah relates them to broken vessels and displaced sparks.

The exact mirror-tree of ten named qliphoth with one demon prince per sphere is a later systematization. It should not be substituted for the varied demonologies of the Zohar, the *Treatise on the Left Emanation*, Cordovero, Herrera, and Vital.

## Qoph’s zodiacal adverse order

In Golden Dawn/*777* material, Pisces is assigned the **Neshimiron/Nashimiron**, typically glossed “malignant women.” The source spelling varies. The Golden Dawn lecture describes them as watery blue, skeletal female beings combined with serpents and fishes. [Golden Dawn Qliphoth lecture](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html)

This order is distinct from:

- the tenth qliphah Lilith;
- the ninth qliphah Gamaliel;
- Crowley’s individual Qoph genius Qulielfi;
- Grant’s Tunnel of Qoph.

## Qulielfi

**[Text/Hermetic]** Crowley’s *Liber 231* lists **Qulielfi** as the Qoph genius of the “Cells of the Qliphoth.” No prose mythology accompanies the name in Crowley’s text.

## Kenneth Grant’s Tunnel of Qoph

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* appeared in 1977. It treats the reverse sides of the twenty-two paths as Tunnels of Set and builds extensive visions around Crowley’s genii.

For Qoph/Qualifi/Qulielfi, Grant supplies:

- number **266**;
- a sigil likened to a silvery slug-track on a circular stone;
- twenty-two inverted crescents;
- Hekt/Heqet, the frog-headed Lady of Transformation;
- frogs and batrachian life;
- mangrove and swamp imagery;
- Neshimiron;
- menstrual, lunar, biological, and prehuman imagery;
- “Universe B,” Grant’s reversed or Nightside reality.

[Grant, *Nightside of Eden*, Qoph tunnel chapter](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf)

**[Modern invention]** This detailed tunnel mythology is Grant’s Typhonian construction. It is not found in the Zohar, *Pardes Rimmonim*, or *Etz Chaim*. Its sources include Crowley’s sigils, Golden Dawn adverse correspondences, Lovecraftian aesthetics, Tantric material, and Grant’s own claimed magical experiences.

## The ten qliphoth and chiefs

The following is the conventional Golden Dawn/*777* list. The source itself warns that the chiefs cannot be confined neatly to one sphere.

| No. | Sephirah’s shadow | Name and usual gloss | Conventional chief(s) | Source status |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keter | Thaumiel/Thamiel, “dual contending forces” | Satan and Moloch | **[Hermetic compilation]** |
| 2 | Chokmah | Ghagiel/Zogiel/Oghiel, “hinderers” | Beelzebub or Lucifuge, depending table | **[Disputed Hermetic list]** |
| 3 | Binah | Satariel/Harasiel, “concealers” | Lucifuge or Beelzebub, depending column/order | **[Disputed]** |
| 4 | Chesed | Gha’agsheblah/Gagh Shekelah/Gamchicoth, “disturbers/devourers” | Astaroth | **[Hermetic]** |
| 5 | Gevurah | Golachab/Golahab, “burners” | Asmodeus | **[Hermetic]** |
| 6 | Tiferet | Thagirion/Zamiel, “disputers” or “ugly ones” | Belphegor | **[Hermetic]** |
| 7 | Netzach | A’arab Zaraq/Ghoreb Zereq/Harab Serapel, “dispersing ravens” | Baal | **[Hermetic]** |
| 8 | Hod | Samael, “deceivers” or “poison” | Adramelech | **[Hermetic; Samael is much older than this placement]** |

That inconsistency should be preserved rather than “corrected.” [Golden Dawn list](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html)

For the Kircher Qoph path, the adjacent adverse spheres are therefore:

- Netzach → A’arab Zaraq/Harab Serapel;
- Malkuth → Lilith/Nahemoth.

Grant’s tunnel is not merely one of these spheres; it is the reverse of the connecting path.

# People and works

## Jewish authors, transmitters, and scholars

- **Saadia Gaon:** earliest surviving extensive commentary recension; reads the work philosophically and cosmologically.
- **Shabbetai Donnolo:** early commentary associated with the Long recension.
- **Judah Halevi:** summarizes the letter classes and creation doctrine in *Kuzari* IV:25.
- **Judah ben Barzillai:** important medieval commentator and transmitter.
- **Eleazar of Worms and Ashkenazi pietists:** magical and linguistic reception of *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- **Abraham Abulafia:** letter permutation and prophetic Kabbalah; aware of different Yetzirah versions.
- **Joseph Gikatilla:** divine names and Sefirotic gates.
- **Moses Cordovero:** systematized Sefiroth, paths, divine names, colours, and qelippot.
- **Isaac Luria and Chaim Vital:** cosmological rupture, partzufim, repair, and complex trees.
- **Vilna Gaon:** recension, commentary, and distinctive reconstructed tree.
- **Shneur Zalman:** Hasidic internalization of letters, Sefiroth, souls, and kelippah; no Qoph chapter.
- **Nachman of Breslov:** joy, concealment, dreams, imagination, and prayer; no Golden Dawn Qoph path.
- **Gershom Scholem:** historical dating of Kabbalah and de León’s role in the Zohar.
- **Moshe Idel:** challenged overly linear Scholemian histories and emphasized experiential, linguistic, and ecstatic Kabbalah.
- **Joseph Dan:** scholarship on the Bahir, early Kabbalah, and changing reception of *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- **Elliot R. Wolfson:** language, embodiment, gender, and visuality in Kabbalah.
- **Peter Hayman:** critical synoptic edition of the Yetzirah recensions.
- **Tzahi Weiss:** seventh-century Syriac-context thesis.
- **Daniel C. Matt:** manuscript-based Pritzker translation of the Zohar.
- **Arthur Green:** historical introduction to the Pritzker Zohar.
- **J. H. Chajes:** major study of ilanot and Kabbalistic visual culture.

## Christian Cabalists and occultists

- **Pico della Mirandola:** 1486 Christian Cabalistic theses.
- **Johannes Reuchlin:** 1517 Christian-Pythagorean Cabala and defense of Hebrew literature.
- **Paulus Ricius:** Latin *Portae Lucis*.
- **Athanasius Kircher:** earliest securely printed Qoph-on-Netzach–Malkuth arrangement.
- **Christian Knorr von Rosenroth:** Latin anthology and transmission of Zoharic/Lurianic material.
- **Éliphas Lévi:** public Tarot–Hebrew synthesis, but Qoph–Sun rather than Qoph–Moon.
- **Papus:** extended the French occult Tarot tradition.
- **William Wynn Westcott:** English *Sepher Yetzirah* and Golden Dawn co-founder.
- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers:** Golden Dawn system, *Kabbalah Unveiled*, Tarot and Goetia redactions.
- **Aleister Crowley:** *777*, *Liber 231*, *Book of Thoth*.
- **Dion Fortune:** psychological and devotional Qabalah organized by Sephiroth.
- **Israel Regardie:** disseminated Golden Dawn manuscripts and psychological path interpretations.
- **Paul Foster Case:** B.O.T.A. body-consciousness and subconscious readings.
- **Gareth Knight:** post-Fortune symbolic and pathworking synthesis.
- **Kenneth Grant:** Qulielfi as the Nightside Tunnel of Qoph.
- **Chic and Tabatha Cicero:** modern Golden Dawn pathworking manuals.

## Artists and literary figures

- **Hans Burgkmair the Elder:** title-page woodcut of *Portae Lucis*.
- **Pamela Colman Smith:** Rider–Waite Moon imagery; Waite’s deck does not print Hebrew Qoph on the card, but Golden Dawn symbolism underlies its esoteric reception.
- **Lady Frieda Harris:** Thoth Moon; perhaps the most concentrated visual statement of Crowley’s Qoph.
- **Steffi Grant:** Nightside sigils and visual culture associated with Kenneth Grant.
- **W. B. Yeats:** Golden Dawn initiate whose poetry repeatedly uses lunar, tower, mask, dream, and gyre symbolism, although no major poem is a straightforward exposition of Qoph.
- **Arthur Machen, Algernon Blackwood, and Dion Fortune:** members or heirs of the occult revival whose fiction explores astral and subconscious borderlands; direct Qoph dependence must be demonstrated work by work.
- **Alan Moore and J. H. Williams III, *Promethea*:** a modern graphic traversal of Hermetic Qabalah; relevant as visual-literary reception, not historical Kabbalah.

## Diagrams and manuscripts

1. Medieval Jewish diagrams and ilanot precede print and display considerable diversity.
2. *Portae Lucis* (1516) supplies the first generally recognized printed Sefirotic tree.
3. Printed *Pardes Rimmonim* editions from the 1590s contain divergent trees.
4. Kircher’s 1652 plate provides the decisive lettered prototype for Qoph on Netzach–Malkuth.
5. *Kabbala Denudata* reproduces and transmits several Jewish and Christian-Cabalist figures.
6. Golden Dawn Cipher Manuscript diagrams show an evolving arrangement, including a “perfected” tree involving Da’at.
7. Golden Dawn coloured trees apply four complete colour scales to the same geometry.
8. Kaplan’s modern Ari and Gra figures are pedagogical reconstructions, not facsimiles of one universally normative Jewish tree.

The Ilanot Project is the most useful digital entry point for surviving Jewish diagrammatic traditions. [Ilanot Project](https://www.ilanot.org/pages?page=about), [Ilanot portal](https://ilanot.info/en/)

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. The date of *Sefer Yetzirah*

- **Traditional:** Abraham composed or received it.
- **Older scholarship:** dates ranging from the first to sixth centuries.
- **Hayman:** earliest reconstructable textual situation is already recensionally divided by about the tenth century.
- **Weiss:** seventh-century Syrian environment with Syriac grammatical influence.
- **Open issue:** whether older cosmological materials lie behind the received composition.

Qoph–Pisces–Adar is early within the recoverable recension history, but its exact body/faculty correspondence is not stable.

## 2. The identity of the “Thirty-two Paths”

The opening “thirty-two paths” of *Sefer Yetzirah* means ten Sefiroth plus twenty-two letters. The prose list naming each path an “Intelligence” is another text.

Westcott and Golden Dawn writers often print the latter as though it were simply the concluding portion of *Sefer Yetzirah*. Manuscript history does not support that seamless identification.

Consequently:

- “Qoph is simple/Pisces/Adar” is a Yetzirah claim.
- “The twenty-ninth path is Corporeal Intelligence” is from the appended path list.
- “The twenty-ninth is Qoph” results from aligning two scales.
- “It joins Netzach–Malkuth” comes from Kircher.
- “Its Tarot is the Moon” comes from the Golden Dawn.

## 3. Zoharic authorship

- **Tradition:** Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle.
- **Scholem:** principal authorship by Moses de León in thirteenth-century Castile.
- **Current scholarship:** de León remains central, but several Zoharic strata point toward collective composition and later additions.

The Qoph–Resh–Shin falsehood story is textually Zoharic regardless of whether one accepts its traditional or critical date. The date determines whether it is treated as tannaitic revelation or medieval Castilian literature.

## 4. Christian Cabala and appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Ricius, Kircher, and Rosenroth preserved and publicized Jewish material, sometimes with considerable linguistic learning. They also:

- detached it from rabbinic practice;
- used it to prove Christianity;
- transformed Hebrew divine names into Christian magical or Christological signs;
- relied on converts and Jewish teachers whose agency is easily erased;
- created diagrams and syntheses later presented as “the Hebrew tradition.”

“Appropriation” accurately describes the power-laden theological conversion of Jewish material, but it should not erase differences among the Christian Cabalists. Reuchlin, for example, simultaneously Christianized Kabbalah and defended Jewish books from destruction.

## 5. Who created the Kircher tree?

The extremes are both misleading:

- “Kircher invented the Tree of Life” ignores medieval Jewish diagrams, *Portae Lucis*, Cordoverian trees, Philippe d’Aquin, and converted-Jewish intermediaries.
- “Kircher merely copied the ancient Jewish tree” ignores the lack of an earlier securely identified diagram with his complete path lettering.

The strongest formulation is:

> Kircher’s 1652 publication is the earliest securely documented printed source for the exact Qoph-on-Netzach–Malkuth configuration later normalized by the Golden Dawn.

## 6. Kircher versus Ari and Gra

Kircher’s tree violates the later Jewish interpretive rule that:

- three mothers should occupy three horizontals;
- seven doubles seven verticals;
- twelve simples twelve diagonals.

On Kircher’s tree, the alphabet runs sequentially through a geometry not organized consistently by those three classes.

The Ari and Gra reconstructions try to make the geometry conform to the classes, but disagree about the Sefirotic arrangement and the ordering of simple letters.

No neutral textual rule establishes one diagram as the uniquely correct drawing of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## 7. Tarot attribution

There is no evidence that medieval Jewish Kabbalists knew Tarot trumps as embodiments of Hebrew letters.

- Lévi publicly mapped letters to trumps in the 1850s.
- His Qoph corresponds to the Sun.
- The Golden Dawn restructured the sequence and gave Qoph the Moon.
- Crowley retained Qoph–Moon despite changing Heh/Tzaddi.

Qoph–Moon is therefore a late nineteenth-century Hermetic achievement, not a hidden doctrine recoverable from the Zohar.

## 8. “Back of the head” and neuropsychology

Golden Dawn-derived writers locate automatic life in the cerebellum or medulla and read Qoph as the subconscious rear brain.

- “Back of the head” is a disputed etymology.
- The cerebellum/medulla theory reflects nineteenth- and early twentieth-century physiology.
- The subconscious framework reflects modern psychology.
- “Reptilian brain,” genetic memory, and cellular consciousness are later popular elaborations.

These can function as ritual metaphors but should not be presented as ancient Hebrew science.

## 9. Pisces versus the Moon

Pisces is not the Moon’s zodiacal domicile. In traditional astrology it is ruled by Jupiter; modern astrologers add Neptune.

The Golden Dawn assigns the **Moon card** to Pisces because the card is part of the twenty-two-trump distribution. This is different from saying that the planetary Moon rules Pisces. The system already assigns the Moon as a planet to Gimel and the High Priestess.

## 10. Qoph’s body and faculty

Laughter/spleen is commonly quoted as though universal. Sleep/left foot and craw/gizzard are also documented.

The variants probably result from:

- different original ordering systems;
- transmission errors;
- deliberate harmonization;
- movement of summaries between recensions;
- attempts to align cosmology, medicine, astrology, and alphabetic order.

A dossier that gives only one combination suppresses the textual evidence.

## 11. Da’at

*Sefer Yetzirah* emphatically says ten Sefiroth, “ten and not nine, ten and not eleven.”

Da’at is therefore not simply an eleventh independent Sephirah. In later Kabbalah it can be:

- an emergent conjunction of Chokhmah and Binah;
- the inner dimension or replacement-count for Keter;
- a functional state of consciousness;
- an apparent location in a diagram;
- a partzufic or developmental principle.

Golden Dawn trees often draw or imply Da’at at the Abyss. Ari and Gra diagrams handle the location differently. Da’at’s presence does not authorize eleven simultaneously counted Sefiroth.

## 12. The qliphothic mirror-tree

Jewish sources genuinely contain qelippot, Samael, Lilith, Naamah, the Other Side, demonic palaces, and broken vessels. What is disputed is the claim that they form one ancient, universally fixed reverse tree identical to modern occult diagrams.

The ten-name list, demon chiefs, twenty-two tunnels, and Tarot correspondences were assembled in stages:

- Jewish demonological and Kabbalistic source material;
- Christian-Latin compilation;
- Golden Dawn tables;
- Crowley’s *777* and *Liber 231*;
- Grant’s 1977 tunnel narratives;
- later Left-Hand-Path systems.

# Open questions

1. **What did the earliest Qoph sign depict?**  
   Needle-eye, nape, monkey, bow, and other proposals exist. The available proto-alphabetic evidence does not settle the question.

2. **How old is the Qoph–Pisces–Adar sentence?**  
   It is secure within the medieval recensional tradition, but no surviving manuscript takes it directly back to late antiquity.

3. **What was Qoph’s original faculty and organ?**  
   Sleep, laughter, craw, spleen, and left foot compete across witnesses and harmonized editions.

4. **When was the Thirty-two Intelligences list composed?**  
   Its Hebrew and Latin histories need a full manuscript stemma independent of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

5. **Who first placed Qoph specifically on Netzach–Malkuth?**  
   Kircher is the earliest securely located printed witness. An earlier manuscript source may yet be identified.

6. **How much did Kircher take from Philippe d’Aquin or Jewish informants?**  
   The geometry has antecedents, but the pathway from those sources to Kircher’s lettering remains incompletely documented.

7. **Were the simple-letter diagrams attributed to the Ari and Gra historical drawings or later reconstructions?**  
   The teachings are rooted in their commentarial traditions, but many familiar plates were normalized by modern editors.

8. **When exactly did the Golden Dawn finalize Qoph–Moon?**  
   It belongs to the order’s early curriculum, but surviving Cipher Manuscript, ritual, and later publication layers should be distinguished.

9. **What is the correct spelling of the qliphothic genius?**  
   Qulielfi, Qualifi, and other forms reflect typography, transcription, and Grantian adaptation. Crowley’s printed form is Qulielfi.

10. **Are recurring Qoph pathworking visions independent?**  
    Most practitioners have seen the same Moon card, colours, deity lists, and scripts. Similarity cannot by itself distinguish shared suggestion from an independent visionary environment.

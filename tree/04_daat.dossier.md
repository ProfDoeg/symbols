# Da'at: Research Dossier

## Editorial method and evidentiary legend

This dossier keeps Jewish Kabbalah and Christian/Hermetic Qabalah historically distinct. Similar vocabulary does not establish identical doctrine, and later occult correspondences are not projected backward into the *Sefer Yetzirah*, Bahir, Zohar, Cordovero, or Luria.

Labels used throughout:

- **[DT] Documented text:** directly attested in the cited primary source.
- **[KT] Kabbalistic tradition:** a theological or exegetical claim made within Jewish tradition.
- **[SR] Scholarly reconstruction:** historical-critical conclusion based on manuscripts, language, bibliography, or reception history.
- **[HO] Hermetic-occult attribution:** a correspondence or teaching belonging to Christian Cabala, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, or later occultism.
- **[D] Disputed:** attribution, date, genealogy, or interpretation remains contested.
- **[MI] Modern invention:** a late synthesis lacking an identified precedent in the older sources.
- **[NF] Negative finding:** the requested attribution is absent from the examined source.

Translations are mine unless an edition is named. Brief quotations are supplied rather than reproducing copyrighted chapters in full. In particular, Dion Fortune’s still-copyrighted discussion is given as a complete source-critical synopsis, not a verbatim chapter. More importantly, *The Mystical Qabalah* contains no separate Da’at chapter: Da’at is discussed across several chapters.

## Basic identification

| Field | Source-critical identification |
|---|---|
| Hebrew | **דַּעַת / דָּעַת**, *daʿat*: knowledge, cognition, knowing |
| Root | ידע, *y-d-ʿ*, “to know”; biblical usage ranges from information and skill to recognition, intimate acquaintance, and sexual knowing |
| Other spellings | *Daʿat, Da’at, Daat*; Christian/Hermetic sources commonly use *Daath* or Crowley’s *Daäth* |
| Gematria | ד 4 + ע 70 + ת 400 = **474** |
| Number | **None in the classical decade.** It is sometimes called the “eleventh Sephira,” but systems that count it normally omit or supernalize Keter so that the operative count remains ten |
| Jewish position | A function or configuration between Chokhmah and Binah; in other contexts the integrating “brain” of Zeʿir Anpin, carrying intellect into the six emotional sefirot |
| Western position | An invisible point on the Middle Pillar below Chokhmah and Binah, above Chesed and Gevurah, in or at the Abyss |
| Status | Neither simply “a Sephira” nor simply “not a Sephira.” Its status varies by textual system: Cordovero rejects it as an additional eleventh; Lurianic texts use several forms of Da’at within partzufic configurations; Western occultism turns it into an invisible station or threshold |
| Divine name | **No independent classical name.** יהוה אלוה ודעת, *YHVH Eloah ve-Daʿat*, belongs to **Tiferet** in Western tables |
| Archangel | **None canonically assigned to Da’at** in *Shaʿarei Orah*, *Pardes Rimmonim*, the Golden Dawn’s basic tenfold table, or *777* |
| Angelic order | **None canonically assigned** |
| Planet or “mundane chakra” | **None canonically assigned.** Sirius, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto, or a trans-Saturnian “hidden planet” are divergent twentieth-century or later proposals |
| Body | No single universal assignment. Lurianic texts locate particular Da’at configurations in the brain, between the shoulders, or temporarily at the throat/chest of Zeʿir Anpin. Western sources often give the nape or throat |
| Tarot | **None.** Da’at is not a Hebrew letter or one of the twenty-two Golden Dawn paths |
| Four colours | A supplementary Western list gives lavender; grey-white or silvery grey; pure violet; grey flecked gold/yellow. These are not a row in the earliest canonical colour table |
| Qliphah | **No canonical Da’at Qliphah.** Choronzon is Crowley’s demon/personification of dispersion in the Abyss, not an eleventh member of the ten named Qliphoth |
| Tunnel | **No “tunnel of Da’at” in Grant’s twenty-two tunnels.** The path crossing the Abyss centrally is Gimel; Grant’s corresponding tunnel is Gargophias. Da’at is instead the gate or pylon into the Nightside |

The most important preliminary finding is therefore negative: Da’at does not possess the full classical package—number, divine name, archangel, choir, planet, tarot card, virtue, vice, plant, stone, animal—that the ten numbered Sephiroth possess. Tables that supply all of these are late attempts to normalize an anomalous point.

## The name

### Hebrew form and etymology

**[DT]** דַּעַת, *daʿat*, is a biblical noun from ידע, *yadaʿ*, “to know.” The root does not inherently mean “gnosis” or mystical union. Its semantic range includes factual knowledge, discernment, practical skill, recognition, relationship, and intimacy.

Genesis 4:1 says, “וְהָאָדָם יָדַע אֶת־חַוָּה אִשְׁתּוֹ”—“The human knew Eve his wife.” [Genesis 4:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.4.1) became important for later kabbalistic and Hasidic explanations of Da’at as joining, attachment, or fruitful union. That symbolism is a genuine exegetical development, not the only lexical meaning of the root.

The spelling consists of:

- **ד**, *dalet*, 4
- **ע**, *ʿayin*, 70
- **ת**, *tav*, 400

Hence the gematria **474**, used by Crowley for *Liber CDLXXIV, Os Abysmi vel Daath*.

### Biblical constellation

The biblical passages are sources for later symbolism, not evidence that the Bible already contained a Sephira named Da’at.

#### Proverbs 3:19–20

**[DT]** The triad is explicit:

> יְהוָה בְּחָכְמָה יָסַד־אָרֶץ … בִּתְבוּנָה … בְּדַעְתּוֹ תְּהוֹמוֹת נִבְקָעוּ

“YHWH founded the earth by wisdom … by understanding … by His knowledge the depths split open.” [Proverbs 3:19–20](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19-20)

**[KT]** Medieval Kabbalists could therefore read Chokhmah, Binah/Tevunah, and Da’at as a triadic structure of creation. The verse does not call any of them a numbered Sephira.

#### Proverbs 24:3–4

This is especially important in the Zohar:

- a house is built by wisdom;
- established by understanding;
- its chambers filled by knowledge.

**[KT]** Zoharic and Lurianic texts apply this to Abba, Imma, and the Da’at or mochin of Zeʿir Anpin. Some old English occult editions incorrectly cite the passage as Proverbs 29.

#### Job 28

**[DT]** Job asks where wisdom and understanding are found and answers that their way is hidden from creatures: “God understands its way; He knows its place.” [Job 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28)

**[KT]** Later readers found here a scriptural grammar for concealed supernal knowledge. **[NF]** Job 28 does not name a Sephira Da’at or describe an eleventh emanation.

#### Isaiah

Two passages became especially relevant:

- Isaiah 11:2 places “the spirit of knowledge and fear of YHWH” beside wisdom, understanding, counsel, and might. [Isaiah 11:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.11.2)
- Isaiah 33:6 joins wisdom and knowledge to stability and salvation. [Isaiah 33:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.33.6)

These passages supply biblical language for later sefirotic associations but no diagram or enumeration.

#### 1 Chronicles 29:11

David’s doxology lists:

> הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד … הַמַּמְלָכָה

Greatness, might, beauty/glory, victory, majesty, “all,” and kingdom. [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11)

**[KT]** Kabbalistic exegesis maps these words to the seven lower qualities:

| Biblical word | Later sefirotic reading |
|---|---|
| *Gedullah* | Chesed |
| *Gevurah* | Gevurah |
| *Tiferet* | Tiferet |
| *Netzach* | Netzach |
| *Hod* | Hod |
| *kol ba-shamayim u-va-aretz* | Yesod |
| *mamlakhah* | Malkhut |

**[NF]** Da’at is not among them. The verse supports the lower seven, not an independent eleventh.

#### Exodus 31:3

Bezalel is filled with “wisdom, understanding, and knowledge” for skilled workmanship. [Exodus 31:1–3](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.31.1-3)

This shows that the biblical triad can be practical and artisanal. A mystical reading is possible, but should not erase the ordinary sense.

## The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*: ten, not eleven

### Date and recensions

**[D/SR]** The date of *Sefer Yetzirah* remains unsettled. Proposals range from the second century CE to the early Islamic period. The secure evidence is that distinct forms existed by the early tenth century, when Saadia Gaon and other commentators addressed them. A. Peter Hayman’s critical edition distinguishes Short, Long, and Saadian recensional families and emphasizes the instability of the text. [Hayman’s edition](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/) describes the work as first securely emerging in the early tenth century; many current historians place final redaction between the seventh and ninth centuries, while allowing older materials.

The familiar “Ari” and “Gra” versions are later received or normalized recensions. They should not be treated as independent ancient manuscripts contemporary with the work’s first composition.

### The thirty-two paths

**[DT]** The opening formula is:

> בִּשְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם נְתִיבוֹת פְּלִיאוֹת חָכְמָה

“By thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom…” [*Sefer Yetzirah*, Gra version 1:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.1.1)

The thirty-two comprise:

- ten **sefirot belimah**;
- twenty-two foundation letters;
- three mothers, אמש;
- seven doubles, בגדכפרת;
- twelve simples.

The text’s own categorical division is arithmetical-cosmological. Its *sefirot* are not simply identical with the fully named medieval emanations Keter through Malkhut.

### Da’at’s absence

**[DT/NF]** The decisive wording is “ten and not nine; ten and not eleven.” No recension supplies Da’at as an eleventh Sephira. No recension identifies one of the twenty-two letters as Da’at.

Consequently Da’at has, in the *Sefer Yetzirah* itself:

- no letter;
- no mother/double/simple class;
- no planet, zodiacal sign, or element;
- no month or tribe;
- no bodily organ;
- no line connecting two Sephiroth;
- no “Yetziratic Intelligence” text.

### The “thirty-two Intelligences”

**[SR]** The prose texts called the “First Path, Admirable Intelligence,” “Second Path, Illuminating Intelligence,” and so forth are not part of the recoverable text of *Sefer Yetzirah*. They belong to a later list transmitted in medieval and Christian Cabalistic contexts and printed by Kircher. Westcott’s English “Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom” popularized them as if they were integral to the *Yetzirah*. [Kircher’s Latin list](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm) begins *Prima semita… Intelligentia admirabilis*.

**[NF]** There is no “Da’at Intelligence” among those thirty-two because the list consists of ten Sephiroth plus twenty-two paths, and Da’at is neither.

### Saadia, Ari, and Gra readings

- **Saadia Gaon, tenth century:** interprets the text within philosophical cosmology and creation theology. He does not install an eleventh Sephira Da’at.
- **Ari recension:** a later kabbalistically edited form associated with the Lurianic tradition; still says ten, not eleven.
- **Gra recension:** the form edited or selected in the Vilna Gaon’s circle; still preserves the ten-plus-twenty-two structure.

**[D]** Modern “Ari Tree” and “Gra Tree” charts should not be confused with those textual recensions. A recension is a form of a text; a tree is a diagrammatic interpretation.

## 2. *Sefer ha-Bahir*

**[SR]** The Bahir circulated in Provence in the late twelfth century, probably around 1170–1200. **[KT]** Tradition attributes it to the tannaitic sage Nehunya ben ha-Qanah; historical scholarship regards it as a composite medieval work containing older motifs.

The Bahir transforms the ten *maʿamarot*, divine powers, or lights into a far more mythic-theosophical system than the *Sefer Yetzirah*. It develops:

- divine powers expressed through biblical verses;
- male and female symbolism;
- a tree, channels, limbs, and light;
- Wisdom and Understanding;
- the thirty-two paths.

Bahir §124/125 connects the thirty-two paths with hidden Torah and primordial light. [Bahir 124–125](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.124)

**[NF]** In the examined text, Da’at does not appear as a clearly delimited hidden or eleventh Sephira. The Bahir is an indispensable ancestor of sefirotic symbolism, but “the Bahir teaches Da’at as the child of Chokhmah and Binah” is not an adequately sourced claim.

## 3. The Zoharic corpus

### Date and authorship

- **[KT]** The Zohar presents its teachings through Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and his second-century circle.
- **[SR]** The main corpus emerged in late-thirteenth-century Castile, principally in the orbit of Moses de León.
- **[D]** Scholem’s strong Moses-de-León authorship thesis remains foundational, but later scholars—including Yehuda Liebes, Ronit Meroz, Daniel Matt, and others—have emphasized compositional layers, collaborators, and a broader Castilian circle. “Written entirely by one man” and “an intact second-century book” are both too simple.

Daniel Matt’s Pritzker translation retains traditional Zohar pagination while treating the work as medieval literature. [Matt’s introduction](https://supress.sites-pro.stanford.edu/sites/supress/files/media/file/introduction_by_daniel_matt.pdf)

### Da’at in the Zohar

**[DT]** The Zohar’s *Idra Zuta* associates Wisdom, Understanding, and Knowledge with father, mother, and their unifying issue. A passage at Zohar III 291a invokes Isaiah 33:6 and describes concealed matters carried into the world to come. [Zohar III 291a](https://www.zoharonline.org/zohar/volume-3/idra-zuta/daf-291a/)

Another Zoharic passage explains that father and mother prepare the house and that the chambers are filled through Da’at, invoking Proverbs 24:3–4. [Zohar, Ha’azinu](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Ha%27azinu/chapters/26)

A particularly clarifying formulation says:

> “Da’at is no more than the inclusion of Chokhmah and Binah.”

[Zohar, Ha’azinu 88](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Ha%27azinu/verses/88-88)

This does not create eleven independently counted entities. It treats Da’at as conjunction, inclusion, issue, or operative manifestation.

### “The child of Chokhmah and Binah”

**[KT]** The familial symbolism is genuinely Zoharic: Abba and Imma, Father and Mother, unite; the son or Vav carries their influx into the lower configuration. Da’at can name the conjunction and its fruit.

**[D]** Saying simply “Da’at is the son” can obscure passages where the son is Tiferet or Zeʿir Anpin and Da’at is the son’s interior consciousness. The symbols overlap by perspective; they are not a single fixed pedigree.

### “The Sephira that is not counted”

The concept has authentic roots, but the exact slogan is often modernized.

- Zoharic discourse sometimes counts Keter beyond or above the manifest grades.
- Da’at can enter the operative decade when Keter is not counted.
- Da’at is described as concealed or as an inclusion of Chokhmah and Binah.

**[D]** The polished formula “There are eleven Sephiroth, but never more than ten at once” is an explanatory summary, not a quotation from the Zohar.

## 4. Joseph Gikatilla: *Shaʿarei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla composed *Shaʿarei Orah*, “Gates of Light,” around the 1290s. It organizes ten gates by divine names and sefirotic relationships.

In the Fifth Gate’s treatment of the sixth Sephira, Tiferet, Da’at is:

- the middle line;
- the Vav of YHWH;
- the “third” that mediates Wisdom and Understanding;
- the power that joins the upper and lower system.

[*Shaʿarei Orah*, Fifth Gate, Sixth Sephira §74](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Fifth_Gate%2C_Sixth_Sefirah.74)

**[DT]** Here Da’at belongs to the symbolic complex of Tiferet and the Middle Pillar. It is not given a separate gate.

### Classical correspondences

**[NF]** Gikatilla gives Da’at no independent:

- divine name;
- angel;
- order of angels;
- planet;
- bodily member.

The name *YHVH Eloah ve-Da’at* is therefore not evidence for a separate Da’at correspondence: in later Western tables it belongs to Tiferet and preserves Da’at’s Tiferetic role.

## 5. Moses Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

Cordovero’s discussion is one of the strongest correctives to modern oversimplification.

In Gate 2, chapter 3, he reports the view of Shem Tov ben Shem Tov:

1. Keter should not be counted;
2. the decade begins with Chokhmah;
3. Da’at is placed beneath Chokhmah and Binah, above Chesed and Gevurah.

Cordovero then rejects this as an additional independently emanated Sephira. He insists that all authorities agree on ten and argues that Zoharic Da’at is the concealed reality of Tiferet between Father and Mother. [*Pardes Rimmonim* 2:3:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.2.3.1)

His conclusion may be summarized from the Hebrew:

> Da’at is the hidden essence of Tiferet, between Father and Mother.

That is far more precise than “Cordovero omitted Da’at.”

In Gate 8, chapter 17, he explains that:

- Tiferet has a concealed root before its manifest emanation;
- this root unites Chokhmah and Binah;
- it is called Da’at;
- Da’at may clothe or animate Tiferet “as a soul.”

[*Pardes Rimmonim* 8:17](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.8.17)

Elsewhere he calls Da’at the third or deciding principle between Wisdom and Understanding and reads Proverbs 3:20 accordingly. [*Pardes Rimmonim* 8:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.8.5), [4:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.4.9)

### Cordovero’s position

- **[DT]** The Sephiroth are ten.
- **[DT]** Da’at is real as a relation, concealed root, or inner mode.
- **[DT]** It is not an independently added eleventh emanation.
- **[KT]** Its principal role is union and mediation: Chokhmah and Binah, and through Tiferet the upper and lower structure.
- **[D]** Calling the familiar Western chart “the Cordovero Tree” is misleading. Cordovero discusses channels and competing diagrams, but the exact Kircher/Golden Dawn arrangement is not simply his authoritative drawing.

### *Or Neʿerav*

Cordovero wrote *Or Neʿerav* as a concise pedagogical entrance to Kabbalah; it was printed posthumously at Venice in 1587. Its framework presupposes the traditional tenfold sefirotic order and directs the student toward the more elaborate *Pardes*. [National Library of Israel record](https://www.nli.org.il/he/books/NNL_ALEPH990020541790205171/NLI)

**[NF]** It supplies no separate Da’at planet, angelic choir, or numbered station. Claims that it contains a full modern Da’at correspondence table could not be substantiated.

## 6. Luria through Ḥayyim Vital

### Historical setting

Isaac Luria taught in Safed chiefly in 1570–1572 and wrote little systematic prose. His doctrines were preserved in competing records by disciples, above all Ḥayyim Vital. *Etz Ḥayyim* is therefore a Vitalian redaction of Lurianic teaching, transmitted in manuscripts and edited after Luria’s death.

**[SR]** “What Luria said” is mediated by Vital’s redaction and by later editorial arrangements. The technical literature is not reducible to the simple ten-circle classroom tree.

### Tzimtzum, Adam Kadmon, worlds, and partzufim

The Lurianic drama includes:

1. **Tzimtzum:** the Infinite withdraws or constricts its light, making possible a differentiated domain.
2. **Qav:** a line or ray of light enters the vacated space.
3. **Adam Kadmon:** the primordial anthropomorphic configuration.
4. **Olam ha-Nekudim:** a world of isolated “points.”
5. **Shevirat ha-kelim:** vessels fail to contain the light and shatter.
6. **Qelipot:** shells form around fallen sparks and fragments.
7. **Tikkun:** repair reorganizes the sefirotic points into relational *partzufim*.

The principal partzufim are:

| Partzuf | Principal sefirotic matrix |
|---|---|
| Arikh Anpin | Keter |
| Abba | Chokhmah |
| Imma | Binah |
| Zeʿir Anpin | Chesed through Yesod, especially Tiferet as center |
| Nukva | Malkhut |

Da’at is not merely inserted as a permanent eleventh circle. It operates as connection, interior mentality, and transmitted mochin within these configurations.

### Upper and lower Da’at

**[DT]** *Etz Ḥayyim* 25:1 distinguishes:

- **Da’at Elyon**, the upper Da’at mediating or deciding between upper Chokhmah and Binah;
- **Da’at Taḥton**, lower Da’at containing the two crowns or complexes of ḥasadim and gevurot.

[*Etz Ḥayyim* 25:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.25.1)

This distinction is indispensable. “Da’at” in Lurianic literature does not always denote the same location or function.

### Da’at as mochin of Zeʿir Anpin

**[DT]** *Etz Ḥayyim* 23:1 speaks of the four mochin of Zeʿir Anpin—Chokhmah, Binah, and Da’at inclusive of ḥasadim and gevurot. [*Etz Ḥayyim* 23:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.23.1)

Da’at:

- integrates the intellectual influx from Abba and Imma;
- includes differentiated mercy and severity;
- conducts cognition into the emotional body of Zeʿir Anpin;
- is necessary for mature relational consciousness.

The developmental imagery is exact. [*Etz Ḥayyim* 22:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.22.1) says that the fetus possesses Chokhmah and Binah without mature Da’at; nursing gives incomplete Da’at; maturation completes it. Da’at is therefore not “facts known” but the power by which cognition becomes an integrated, affective person.

### Da’at in the shattering of the vessels

**[DT]** In *Etz Ḥayyim* 9:1–2, the vessel called Da’at is the first of the lower “kings” to shatter. Its vessel falls into Beriah, while lights and residues move through the subsequent vessels. [*Etz Ḥayyim* 9:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.1), [9:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.2)

This is the strongest textual basis for associating Da’at with:

- unstable knowledge;
- separation;
- the dangerous transition from supernal mind to differentiated personality;
- the need for repair.

**[D]** It is not yet Crowley’s Abyss, Choronzon, or destruction of the ego. Those are Western transformations of the motif.

### The Tree of Knowledge

In *Etz Ḥayyim* 50:3, Adam’s sin affects the third moach of Zeʿir Anpin, Da’at, with its ḥasadim and gevurot. Da’at descends; qelipat nogah becomes the mixed Tree of Knowledge of good and evil. [*Etz Ḥayyim* 50:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.50.3)

This supports a genuine Lurianic link between:

- Da’at;
- human moral mixture;
- descent;
- the Tree of Knowledge;
- repair through discrimination and restored union.

### Was Da’at “the throat of Adam Kadmon”?

**[NF/Correction]** This formulation could not be verified in the examined Vitalian source.

What the sources do say is more specific:

- *Shaʿar ha-Kavvanot* describes the Da’at/moach of **Zeʿir Anpin** enclosed in the yesod of Imma at the throat, later descending toward the chest. [*Shaʿar ha-Kavvanot*, Passover 1:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27ar_HaKavanot%2C_Sermons_on_Passover.1.2)
- Other technical texts place Da’at between the shoulders or distinguish it from Binah at the throat of Arikh Anpin.

Thus “Da’at is the throat of Adam Kadmon” is at best a later diagrammatic compression of several partzufic anatomies. It should not be quoted as Vital’s universal assignment.

### Four worlds

Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Assiah are worlds or levels of emanation, creation, formation, and action. Lurianic configurations recur and interpenetrate across them.

**[NF]** There is no simple rule in *Etz Ḥayyim* giving Da’at one planet, one colour, and one angel for each world. The Western four-colour scales are a Golden Dawn construction laid over a different metaphysical grammar.

## 7. The Vilna Gaon and the “Gra Tree”

Elijah ben Solomon Zalman, the Vilna Gaon (1720–1797), wrote influential commentaries on *Sefer Yetzirah* and Zoharic literature.

### Text versus diagram

**[DT]** The Gra recension of *Sefer Yetzirah* preserves:

- ten *sefirot belimah*;
- twenty-two letters;
- three mothers;
- seven doubles;
- twelve simples.

**[D]** The chart now marketed as “the Gra Tree” is a later diagrammatic convention based on Gra-related exegesis. Surviving Jewish *ilanot* show far more diversity than the three-brand taxonomy “Cordovero/Ari/Gra” suggests. J. H. Chajes’s study documents a many-branched manuscript culture rather than one canonical Jewish diagram. [The Ilanot Project](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/), [Chajes, *The Kabbalistic Tree*](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/978-0-271-09345-1.html)

A common modern contrast says:

- Ari-type diagrams leave a conspicuous interval where Da’at is drawn;
- Gra-type diagrams place Tiferet higher, so Da’at is less easily imagined as a missing circle;
- Kircher/Golden Dawn diagrams assign Hebrew letters sequentially to geometrical paths.

This is useful as a teaching comparison but not an exhaustive history.

### Path layout

The *Sefer Yetzirah* says three mothers, seven doubles, and twelve simples. It does **not** itself draw:

- three horizontal lines;
- seven vertical lines;
- twelve diagonals;
- named Sephirotic endpoints.

Jewish commentators constructed such diagrams in differing ways. The exact Ari and Gra endpoints are therefore interpretive and recension-dependent.

## 8. Hasidism and Chabad

### Shneur Zalman of Liadi and the *Tanya*

The *Tanya* was first printed in 1796. Chapter 3 gives Da’at exceptional psychological centrality.

The divine soul has three intellectual faculties:

- Chokhmah: seminal insight;
- Binah: expansion and comprehension;
- Da’at: attachment and integration.

The chapter appeals to Genesis 4:1: “Adam knew Eve.” Da’at means that the mind binds itself strongly and persistently to what it understands; this union generates the emotional “children,” love and fear. [*Tanya*, chapter 3](https://www.chabad.org/library/tanya/tanya_cdo/aid/7210147/jewish/Tanya-Chapter-3-Part-1.htm)

**[DT]** Da’at is not mere verbal comprehension. The *Tanya*’s characteristic language is *hitkashrut* and *hitḥabrut*: binding and joining.

Chapter 42 sharpens the distinction: knowledge is not merely knowing that something exists but fixing the mind upon it until it becomes existentially present. [*Tanya*, chapter 42](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.42)

### Why Chabad is ḤaBaD

חב״ד abbreviates:

- חכמה, Chokhmah;
- בינה, Binah;
- דעת, Da’at.

**[KT]** Chabad makes the three intellective powers the preferred route to inward divine service. Da’at bridges thought and the middot; insight and analysis do not transform character until bound into consciousness.

Chabad teachings commonly distinguish:

- **Da’at Elyon:** the higher, concealed unification of Chokhmah and Binah, correlated with Keter;
- **Da’at Taḥton:** the lower bridge from intellect to emotion.

[Chabad, “Ten Powers of the Soul”](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/462959/jewish/Ten-Powers-of-the-Soul.htm)

**[NF]** Chabad’s centrality of Da’at does not mean it adds an eleventh independent divine power. When Keter is counted, Da’at is usually not; when conscious faculties are counted, Da’at appears in Keter’s place.

### Naḥman of Bratslav

Naḥman’s *Liqquṭei Moharan* gives Da’at existential and redemptive roles:

- the essential human distinction is Da’at;
- Moses reveals knowledge of God;
- Torah communicates life-force through Da’at;
- recognizing that God is concealed within concealment can transform concealment into Da’at;
- Da’at makes unions and matches.

Relevant passages include [*Liqquṭei Moharan* II 7:1:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan%2C_Part_II.7.1.3), [I 56:3:13–15](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.56.3.13), and [II 89:1:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan%2C_Part_II.89.1.2).

The common Hasidic ground is that Da’at makes belief, insight, or doctrine inwardly operative.

## 9. Prayer, Omer, calendar, body, and Torah

### Prayer

**[DT]** The weekday Amidah’s fourth blessing is *Ḥonen ha-Daʿat*, asking God graciously to grant knowledge, understanding, and discernment.

Berakhot 33a connects Havdalah with this blessing because distinction requires knowledge: “If there is no knowledge, whence distinction?” [Berakhot 33a](https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.33a)

This is rabbinic liturgy and ethics, not evidence that the prayer addresses an eleventh Sephira. Kabbalistic prayer intentions may interpret it through the intellective triad.

### Counting the Omer

The seven weeks are conventionally assigned to combinations of the seven lower middot:

Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, Malkhut.

**[NF]** Da’at is not an eighth week or independently counted day. It functions, in Hasidic psychology, as the interior consciousness that animates the middot. [Chabad on the Omer](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/636385/jewish/Count-and-Wave.htm)

### Month, tribe, and twelve simple letters

**[NF]** None belongs to Da’at. Month, tribe, zodiacal sign, and bodily function are assignments of the twelve simple **letters** in recensions of *Sefer Yetzirah*. Da’at is a word and symbolic configuration, not a twenty-third letter.

### Torah

Da’at is related to Torah through several distinct registers:

- biblical wisdom and skilled knowledge;
- rabbinic discernment;
- Zoharic knowledge of divine unity;
- Lurianic restoration of mochin;
- Hasidic inward attachment;
- Naḥman’s Torah-derived life-force.

These should not be homogenized into “Da’at means secret Torah.”

## The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* contained forty-seven propositions “according to the Hebrew Cabalists” and seventy-two propositions “according to my own opinion.” He distinguished the *scientia Sephiroth* from the science of divine names and argued that Cabala confirms Christian Trinity and incarnation. [Latin *Conclusiones*](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Conclusiones_sive_Theses_DCCCC)

His Latin terminology includes *numerationes* for Sephiroth, *sapientia* for wisdom, and *intelligentia* for understanding.

**[NF]** Pico does not enumerate *Daath* as an eleventh Sephira in the *Conclusiones*.

**[SR]** Pico’s importance is conceptual rather than Da’at-specific: he inaugurated a Christian Cabala that extracted Jewish linguistic and sefirotic techniques into a Christological apologetic. [Stanford Encyclopedia on Pico](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2012/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

---

## 2. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paulus Ricius’s Latin *Portae Lucis* translated Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*. Its Augsburg title page, printed by Johann Miller with a woodcut usually attributed to Hans Burgkmair, is the first known **printed** Sephirotic tree. [CJA record](https://cja.huji.ac.il/browser.php?id=40577&mode=set), [historical study](https://www.sephardichorizons.org/Volume15/Issue1/Heller-Gikatilla.html)

**[SR]** It is not the first Sephirotic diagram altogether. Hebrew manuscripts contain diagrams from the late thirteenth and fourteenth centuries; examples include Paris, BnF hébreu 763 and later *ilanot*. [Biblissima manuscript record](https://portail.biblissima.fr/en/ark%3A/43093/ifdata70fbdaeec9d1ae238f5f80c07a3a026d4e76447a)

**[NF]** The title-page tree does not establish Da’at as an eleventh numbered node.

---

## 3. Johannes Reuchlin, 1517

*De arte cabalistica* presents Cabala through a Latin dialogue combining Jewish materials, Pythagoreanism, and Christian theology. Reuchlin cites *Sefer Yetzirah*, repeats “ten and not eleven,” and calls the Sephiroth *numerationes*. [Frankfurt scan](https://sammlungen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/freimann/content/titleinfo/404942)

**[NF]** In the edition examined, Reuchlin does not supply *Daath* as a separate eleventh Sephira with an angel or planet. Generic Latin *scientia* and *cognitio* should not automatically be capitalized into the later technical Da’at.

---

## 4. Athanasius Kircher, 1652–1654

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, II.1, printed the celebrated *Systema Sephiroticum*. [Cornell plate](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272), [Latin transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm), [digitized volume](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/doi/10.3931/e-rara-47641)

The plate integrates:

- ten Sephiroth;
- divine names;
- angelic orders;
- celestial spheres;
- twenty-two connecting paths;
- Latin descriptions of the thirty-two “Intelligences.”

### Was it the tree “the Golden Dawn adopted”?

**[SR/D]** Broadly, yes: the conventional Golden Dawn geometry and sequential path numbering descend through the Kircher/Christian-Cabalist diagram tradition. But “adopted unchanged” is false.

The Golden Dawn altered or standardized:

- planetary and elemental correspondences;
- path-letter assignments;
- tarot trumps;
- initiatory grades;
- colours;
- ritual meanings.

Kircher’s direct dependence may include Philippe d’Aquin’s 1625 work and other Jewish/Christian diagrams. His sequential derivation of letters may also contain original systematization.

### Da’at on Kircher’s plate

**[NF]**

- Da’at is not a numbered circle.
- No path terminates at Da’at.
- It has no Kircherian planet, angelic order, or divine name.
- The vertical Keter–Tiferet path crosses the region where later diagrams place Da’at, but that geometric intersection is not a Sephira.

Thus Kircher is essential to the Western *shape* of Da’at’s later location but does not yet give it Western Da’at doctrine.

---

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* translated and excerpted Zoharic, Cordoveran, Lurianic, and related texts into Latin.

Here Da’at is explicit:

> “Daath, seu Cognitio”—“Da’at, or cognition.”

Another passage says that when Chokhmah and Binah unite, “*gignitur … Daath*,” Da’at is generated. [*Kabbala Denudata* scan](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/1284080.pdf)

The work also describes Da’at as dwelling in Zeʿir Anpin like soul and life, joining Wisdom and Understanding and containing mercies and severities. [Second relevant scan](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/1284066.pdf)

Latin equivalents vary:

- *cognitio*—cognition or knowledge;
- *scientia*—knowledge or science;
- *Daath sive cognitio*—Da’at, that is, cognition.

**[SR]** Knorr was a decisive bridge into nineteenth-century occultism. Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* translated portions of Knorr, transmitting Da’at’s parental and anthropomorphic imagery to the Golden Dawn.

**[D]** Knorr’s Christian framing and selections are not neutral replicas of Jewish Kabbalah. They preserve material while rearranging it toward Christian esotericism.

---

## 6. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

In *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*, Lévi asserted a systematic analogy between:

- twenty-two Hebrew letters;
- twenty-two paths or occult keys;
- twenty-two tarot trumps.

[French/English digitization](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/ceremonial/classics/pdf/transcendental/dogma_et_rituel_part_2.pdf)

**[HO]** This was a crucial step toward occult tarot-Qabalah.

**[NF]** Lévi did not give Da’at a tarot trump. The twenty-two-card scheme belongs to letters and paths, not a hidden Sephira.

**[D]** Lévi’s actual sequence differs from the Golden Dawn’s later assignments. The historical Tarot originated as a fifteenth-century Italian card game; Hebrew-letter correspondences are eighteenth- and nineteenth-century occult constructions, not medieval Tarot doctrine.

## 7. Westcott, Mathers, and the Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn was founded in 1888. Its synthesis joined:

- Kircher-derived tree geometry;
- *Sefer Yetzirah* categories;
- Knorr/Mathers Zoharic material;
- astrology;
- elemental magic;
- tarot;
- Enochian material;
- four worlds and four colour scales;
- a graded initiatory ascent.

### Knowledge Lectures

The Fourth Knowledge Lecture says that horns on the fallen Adamic diagram spring from Da’at, which:

> is not properly a Sephira, but a conjunction of Chokmah and Binah.

[Regardie, *The Complete Golden Dawn*, Fourth Knowledge Lecture](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/R/Regardie%20-%20The%20Complete%20Golden%20Dawn.pdf)

This closely resembles Cordovero and Knorr but is embedded in a distinct Western ritual cosmology.

Another Golden Dawn diagram, “The Garden of Eden after the Fall,” places the dragon’s heads near Da’at and associates the region with fallen or infernal waters. [Regardie, *Complete Golden Dawn System of Magic*](https://scriptaetveritas.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/ISRAEL_REGARDIE_COMPLETE_GOLDEN_DAWN_SYSTEM_OF_MAGIC.pdf)

### The Abyss

The Golden Dawn distinguishes:

- the Veil of Paroketh below Tiferet;
- the Abyss between the supernal triad and the lower seven;
- Da’at as an invisible conjunction or threshold in that interval.

**[HO]** Da’at’s position in the Abyss becomes much more spatial and initiatory than in Cordovero or Vital.

**[D]** Choronzon’s fully developed role as “Dweller in the Abyss” is primarily Crowleyan, not a uniformly attested Golden Dawn doctrine.

### Book T and tarot

**[NF]** *Book T* contains:

- twenty-two trumps for twenty-two letter paths;
- sixteen court cards;
- thirty-six decan cards;
- forty numbered small cards.

It contains no Da’at card. Da’at has neither a trump nor four numbered cards.

### Four colour scales

The Golden Dawn’s general fourfold scheme is:

| Scale | World | Elemental formula |
|---|---|---|
| King | Atzilut | Wands/Yod/Fire |
| Queen | Beriah | Cups/Heh/Water |
| Prince or Knight | Yetzirah | Swords/Vav/Air |
| Princess or Knave | Assiah | Pentacles/final Heh/Earth |

[Golden Dawn manuscripts on the colour scales](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/golden_dawn_manuscripts.html)

The original row-based system has ten Sephiroth and twenty-two paths. Da’at has no ordinary row. A supplementary list published by Regardie gives:

- King: lavender;
- Queen: grey-white or silvery grey;
- Prince/Knight: pure violet;
- Princess/Knave: grey flecked gold or yellow.

**[HO/D]** These are authentic to a later published Golden Dawn-derived corpus, but their precise order-history is less secure than the principal thirty-two-row table.

### Kircher versus Jewish layouts

The familiar Golden Dawn path geometry is:

| Path | Letter | Golden Dawn endpoints |

| 11 | Aleph | Keter–Chokhmah |

| 18 | Cheth | Binah–Gevurah |

By contrast, Ari- and Gra-related Jewish schemes try in different ways to embody the *Sefer Yetzirah*’s three mothers, seven doubles, and twelve simples. No single “Jewish tree” gives one universally accepted competing list.

## 8. Crowley

### *777*

Crowley’s *777* was first privately published in 1909, largely systematizing Golden Dawn correspondences.

**[NF]** There is no Da’at row. Rows 1–10 are the ten Sephiroth; row 11 is **Aleph/Air/The Fool**, not Da’at. Consequently no complete set of *777* columns exists “for Da’at.”

This means that the following are absent as Da’at entries:

- Hebrew letter;
- key scale;
- planet or sign;
- tarot trump;
- divine name;
- archangel;
- angelic order;
- magical image;
- virtue and vice;
- plant;
- perfume;
- stone;
- animal;
- weapon;
- Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Hindu, or Scandinavian deity;
- geomantic figure;
- Goetic decan or quinance;
- Qliphothic order.

[*Liber 777*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib777), [facsimile PDF](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/0777-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf)

Da’at appears only in explanatory or cross-referential prose, not as a new table key. The revised commentary explains *YHVH Eloah ve-Da’at* under Tiferet as referring to Tiferet’s relation to the child and to Da’at. [*777 Revised*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf)

### Crowley’s definitions

*Book 4* glosses Da’at in two ways:

> “child of Chokmah and Binah”; and “home of Choronzon.”

[Crowley, *Book 4* glossary](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-4/aba2)

In “Knowledge,” *Little Essays Toward Truth*, Crowley is deliberately paradoxical:

> “Daäth—Knowledge—is not a Sephira.”

He identifies knowledge with the Ruach’s dualistic propositions and treats its apparent crown as the location of the Abyss. [“Knowledge”](https://hermetic.com/crowley/little-essays-towards-truth/knowledge)

This is neither Cordovero’s exact position nor modern anti-intellectualism. For Crowley, discursive knowledge breaks reality into subject and predicate; crossing the Abyss entails surrendering this constructed center.

### *Liber Os Abysmi vel Daath*, 474

Published in *The Equinox* I.7 in March 1912, *Liber CDLXXIV* prescribes a severe intellectual curriculum for the Exempt Adept:

- logic;
- Kant;
- Hume;
- Hegel;
- Buddhist analysis;
- reflection on causality and contradiction.

[*Liber Os Abysmi vel Daath*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib474), [1912 *Equinox* facsimile](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/equinox_1_7_1912/equinox_1_7_text.pdf)

**[HO]** The point is to exhaust the claim that reason can supply an ultimate self-grounding truth. Da’at is the mouth or threshold of the Abyss, not a reward for accumulating occult facts.

### Choronzon and the Tenth Aethyr

Crowley and Victor Neuburg recorded an operation near Bou-Saâda, Algeria, on 6 December 1909. Crowley sat within a triangle while Neuburg acted as scribe and guardian. Their text identifies Choronzon with dispersion, contradiction, and the restless forms of the Abyss. [*Liber 418*, Tenth Aethyr](https://sacred-texts.com/oto/418/aetyr10.htm)

**[Documented history]** A written ritual narrative exists, dated and situated at Bou-Saâda.

**[HO claim]** Crowley interpreted it as an actual evocation and as part of his crossing of the Abyss.

**[D]** The supernatural event cannot be historically verified. Later testimony attributed to Neuburg differs, and the survival history of the notebook raises questions. The record proves that Crowley and Neuburg constructed and reported the operation, not that an objective demon appeared.

Choronzon says or embodies “dispersion”; the text connects the serpent’s head rising into Knowledge with the Abyss. Crowley’s Third Aethyr later calls Choronzon the shell or refuse of paths whose heads rise into Da’at. [*Liber 418*, Third Aethyr](https://hermetic.com/crowley/the-vision-and-the-voice/aethyr3)

### Thelemic grades

The A∴A∴ grade structure is:

| Grade | Sephirotic attribution |
|---|---|
| Adeptus Exemptus 7°=4□ | Chesed |
| Babe of the Abyss | Transitional condition, not a numbered Sephirotic grade |
| Magister Templi 8°=3□ | Binah |
| Magus 9°=2□ | Chokhmah |
| Ipsissimus 10°=1□ | Keter |

**[NF]** There is no formal “Da’at grade.” The Babe of the Abyss has poured out the contents of the self into the Graal of Babalon and awaits formation as a Master of the Temple. Da’at represents the threshold or disintegrating false crown of the Ruach.

### *Liber 231*

*Liber CCXXXI* was published in *The Equinox* I.7 in 1912. It gives sigils and names for:

- twenty-two houses or genii of Mercury;
- twenty-two cells or genii of the Qliphoth.

[Primary text](https://hermetic.com/crowley/equinox/i/vii/eqi07009)

**[NF]** It has no Da’at entry because its units are the twenty-two letter paths. Grant later used its averse sigils as a basis for the Tunnels of Set.

## 9. The Tzaddi/Heh question

*Liber AL* I:57 says, “Tzaddi is not the Star.” Crowley eventually exchanged the **cards’** positions so that:

- The Emperor corresponds to Tzaddi/Aquarius;
- The Star corresponds to Heh/Aries;

while maintaining the underlying letter paths in a revised scheme. [Crowley, *The Book of Thoth*](https://otohungary.org/documents/publication/english/Book_of_Thoth.html)

**[NF]** This dispute does not give Da’at a path. It affects two of the twenty-two tarot-letter correspondences only.

## 10. Dion Fortune, 1935

*The Mystical Qabalah* was published in 1935.

### Bibliographic correction

**[NF]** There is no chapter titled “Da’at” and no chapter devoted exclusively to it. Fortune gives chapters to the ten numbered Sephiroth and discusses Da’at throughout her account of the Tree, especially the Middle Pillar, consciousness, Tiferet, and the Abyss. [Complete text](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html)

### Complete synopsis of Fortune’s Da’at teaching

Fortune describes Da’at as:

1. the mysterious or invisible Sephira;
2. a point on the Middle Pillar where the Abyss intersects it;
3. a product of the conjunction of Chokhmah and Binah;
4. a station belonging to another plane from the Tree presently being considered;
5. the point of contact between microcosm and macrocosm;
6. dawning apprehension rather than fully individualized consciousness;
7. a place where force passes into another mode of manifestation;
8. a dangerous focus for undirected meditation;
9. physiologically symbolized at the nape, where skull and spine meet;
10. associated with generation, regeneration, and the polarity of opposites.

Her exact formulation includes:

> “Daath, the invisible Sephirah, is also on the Central Pillar.”

She explicitly says it “belongs to another plane” from the one under consideration. This is why it can appear in one projection and disappear in another.

Fortune also clarifies that *YHVH Eloah ve-Da’at* is the God-name of **Tiferet**, not Da’at. It associates Tiferet with the invisible point above it and with consciousness made manifest.

**[HO]** Fortune turns Da’at into a psychologically intelligible change of state between supernal archetype and individualized awareness.

**[MI/D]** Popular claims that she assigned Da’at a complete set of standard symbols equivalent to Keter or Tiferet go beyond her text.

## 11. Israel Regardie

- *A Garden of Pomegranates* appeared in 1932 and was later revised;
- *The Golden Dawn* published large bodies of Order material;
- *The Complete Golden Dawn System of Magic* supplied expanded tables and commentary.

He treats Da’at as:

- an invisible or “false” Sephira;
- conjunction of Chokhmah and Binah;

## 12. Paul Foster Case

Paul Foster Case founded Builders of the Adytum in 1922 and retained the ten-Sephira/twenty-two-path system, with tarot keys assigned to Hebrew letters. [BOTA history](https://bota.org/bota/history)

**[NF]** In Case’s publicly accessible major tables, Da’at has no tarot key or numbered Sephirotic grade. His distinctive tarot corrections do not turn it into a twenty-third path.

## 13. Gareth Knight

Gareth Knight’s *A Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* was first published in 1965 and, unlike Fortune’s book, contains a chapter explicitly titled “Daath—Knowledge.” [Google Books contents](https://books.google.com/books?id=Tw-B_Hzcp9MC&num=20)

Knight develops Da’at as:

- union of force and form;
- a point of transition from the supernal to the manifest;
- a higher analogue of Yesod;
- an initiatory threshold;
- cosmic justice or impersonal balance;
- the double-faced standpoint symbolized by Janus;
- the possibility of vision across the Abyss.

He uses the colours lavender, silvery grey, pure violet, and grey flecked yellow. He also discusses Prometheus, Perseus, Galahad, and Janus as analogues.

**[HO/MI]** These are creative twentieth-century extensions of the Golden Dawn system. They are not correspondences from the Zohar or Vital.

## 14. Kenneth Grant and the Nightside

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* appeared in 1977.

Grant treats Da’at as:

- a black hole or gateway to the “other side” of the Tree;
- a pylon of the Nightside;
- the entrance to a parallel magical universe;
- the reflected throat or Word;
- the locus of Choronzon and Shugal;
- a gate associated with the Typhonian current, Set, Sirius, and extra-human consciousness.

He equates Da’at with the **Vishuddha** throat chakra and joins Choronzon 333 to Shugal 333, producing 666. [*Nightside of Eden* text](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf)

**[HO]** Grant’s Da’at is far more ontologically independent and chthonic than the Golden Dawn’s conjunction of Chokhmah and Binah.

### Does Da’at have a tunnel?

No.

Grant’s twenty-two Tunnels of Set underlie the twenty-two paths. The tunnel beneath the central Gimel path crossing the Abyss is **Gargophias**. Da’at is the pylon or gateway through which the Nightside becomes accessible, not a twenty-third tunnel.

Grant also connects Da’at to the tunnel **Shalicu** through a secondary network of Choronzonic or Mercurial resonances, but that does not make Shalicu “the tunnel of Da’at.”

**[MI]** Calling a particular spirit “the Da’at tunnel demon” collapses Grant’s distinct categories.

## The Qliphah or tunnel

## Jewish qelipot and Western Qliphoth

In Lurianic Kabbalah, *qelipot* are shells or husks formed through imbalance, separation, and the shattering of vessels. Their structure is embedded in a complex account of sparks, holiness, mixture, and repair.

The late Western “Tree of Death” is a rearranged demonological correspondence system. It combines:

- Zoharic and Lurianic shells;
- Kings of Edom;
- Knorr von Rosenroth;
- Golden Dawn manuscripts;
- Crowley’s *777*;
- grimoire demonology;
- later authors’ rulers.

These should not be treated as a single ancient Jewish list.

## The ten standard Golden Dawn/Crowley orders

| Sephira | Qliphothic order | Common translation |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 Keter | Thaumiel/Thamiel | Two contending forces |
| 2 Chokhmah | Ghagiel/Ghogiel | Hinderers |
| 3 Binah | Satariel | Concealers |
| 4 Chesed | Gha’agsheblah/Agshekeloh | Breakers or disturbers |
| 5 Gevurah | Golachab | Burners or incendiaries |
| 6 Tiferet | Thagirion/Tagiriron | Disputers or painful movers |
| 7 Netzach | Aʿarab Zaraq/Gharab Tzerek | Ravens of dispersion/death |
| 8 Hod | Samael | Poison of God |
| 9 Yesod | Gamaliel | Obscene or polluted ones |
| 10 Malkhut | Lilith | Night spectre |

[Golden Dawn Qliphoth paper](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html), [*777*, column VIII](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/0777-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf)

### Demon princes

The widely circulated sequence—

Thaumiel–Satan/Moloch; Ghagiel–Beelzebub; Satariel–Lucifuge; Gha’agsheblah–Astaroth; Golachab–Asmodeus; Thagirion–Belphegor; Aʿarab Zaraq–Baal; Samael–Adrammelech; Gamaliel–Lilith; Lilith–Naamah—

is a **modern composite**. Crowley’s column CVIII does not map all those rulers cleanly one-to-one in that order; some entries span several rows, and editions differ. [*777* columns CVI–CIX](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/0777-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf)

### Da’at’s Qliphah

**[NF]** Da’at is not in the ten-order list. No classical row supplies:

- an eleventh Qliphah;
- an eleventh prince;
- a mirror planet;
- an eleventh hell.

### Choronzon

- **[HO]** Crowley: Choronzon is dispersion and the Dweller in the Abyss.
- **[HO]** Grant: Choronzon-Shugal guards or constitutes the Da’at pylon.
- **[NF]** Neither fact makes Choronzon one of the ten Qliphoth.
- **[D]** Modern tables variously name Choronzon, Belial, Leviathan, or Choronzon-Shugal as “ruler of Da’at.” These are system-dependent inventions, not a stable Golden Dawn or Jewish attribution.

## Paths: why no path dossier exists for Da’at

The path template does not apply.

Da’at has:

- no single Hebrew letter;
- no letter shape;
- no letter value;
- no pictographic meaning such as ox, house, or camel;
- no mother/double/simple classification;
- no *Sefer Yetzirah* planet, sign, or element;
- no two fixed Sephirotic endpoints;
- no Golden Dawn trump;
- no Tzaddi/Heh problem;
- no Westcott “Intelligence” text;
- no Goetic quinances.

In Western pathworking, practitioners may approach Da’at through:

- Gimel, Keter–Tiferet, crossing the Abyss centrally;
- Daleth, Chokhmah–Binah, as supernal conjunction;
- meditations on the Middle Pillar;
- Crowley’s Abyssal curriculum;
- Grant’s Da’at pylon.

Those are ritual strategies, not proof that Da’at is itself a path.

## People, works, diagrams, and cultural reception

### Jewish kabbalists and texts

- **Joseph Gikatilla:** Da’at as Middle-Pillar mediation within Tiferet; *Shaʿarei Orah*.
- **Moses de León/Zoharic circle:** Da’at as inclusion, conjunction, inheritance, and concealed cognition.
- **Moses Cordovero:** rejects Da’at as an added eleventh while affirming it as Tiferet’s concealed root and the union of Chokhmah/Binah.
- **Isaac Luria and Ḥayyim Vital:** multiple Da’ot, mochin, development of Zeʿir Anpin, shattering and repair.
- **Vilna Gaon:** recension and commentary emphasizing the ten-plus-twenty-two architecture.
- **Shneur Zalman:** Da’at as binding cognition and source of transformed emotion.
- **Naḥman of Bratslav:** Da’at as human distinctiveness, Torah-derived vitality, union, and recognition within concealment.

### Christian Cabalists and occultists

- **Pico:** Christianized Cabala; no Da’at Sephira in the *Conclusiones*.
- **Reuchlin:** ten *numerationes*; no independent Da’at.
- **Ricius and Burgkmair:** *Portae Lucis* and the first printed Sephirotic tree.
- **Kircher:** standard Western diagram and Latin “Intelligences”; no Da’at node.
- **Knorr von Rosenroth:** *Daath sive cognitio*, major conduit into occultism.
- **Éliphas Lévi:** letters and tarot; no Da’at card.
- **Westcott and Mathers:** Golden Dawn synthesis; Da’at as conjunction/invisible point.
- **Aleister Crowley:** Da’at as false crown of reason, Abyss, and home of Choronzon.
- **Victor Neuburg:** scribe and participant in the 1909 Bou-Saâda operation.
- **Dion Fortune:** psychological and macrocosmic point of contact.
- **Israel Regardie:** principal publisher and organizer of Golden Dawn material.
- **Paul Foster Case:** retained the ten-and-twenty-two system; no Da’at key.
- **Gareth Knight:** first major English chapter-length occult synthesis of Da’at.
- **Kenneth Grant:** Da’at as gateway to the Nightside.

### Scholars

- **Gershom Scholem:** historical differentiation of ancient cosmology, medieval Kabbalah, and later reception; major Moses-de-León thesis.
- **Moshe Idel:** comparative and phenomenological correction to overly linear histories of Kabbalah.
- **Elliot R. Wolfson:** gender, embodiment, language, erotic union, and theosophical symbolism.
- **Joseph Dan:** historical formation of Jewish mysticism and the Zohar.
- **A. Peter Hayman:** critical edition and recension history of *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- **Daniel C. Matt:** Pritzker Zohar translation and historical-literary commentary.
- **Yehuda Liebes and Ronit Meroz:** layered and collective dimensions of Zoharic composition.
- **J. H. Chajes:** history of *ilanot* and the plurality of Jewish kabbalistic diagrams.

### Manuscripts and diagrams

1. **Late-thirteenth/fourteenth-century Hebrew diagrams:** the earliest surviving Sephirotic schemata; they precede print and are not uniform.
2. **Paris BnF hébreu 763:** contains an early diagram often discussed in the history of *ilanot*.
3. **Portae Lucis, 1516:** first known printed Sephirotic tree.
4. **Hebrew *Pardes Rimmonim*, Kraków 1592:** among the earliest printed Hebrew presentations of sefirotic diagrams.
5. **Kircher, 1652–54:** the plate that became archetypal for Christian and Hermetic Qabalah.
6. **Knorr, 1677–84:** Latin diagrams and text transmitted to Mathers.
7. **Golden Dawn coloured trees:** combine four worlds, tarot, astrology, and initiation; Da’at may be indicated invisibly or by a supplementary point.
8. **Lurianic *ilanot*:** map partzufim, worlds, lights, vessels, and bodily structures far beyond the ten-circle classroom chart.

- **Hans Burgkmair:** the *Portae Lucis* woodcut, foundational in printed visual reception.
- **W. B. Yeats:** Golden Dawn initiate whose magical practice and “talismanic poetry” developed together. ## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is Da’at a Sephira?

There are at least five historically different answers:

1. **Sefer Yetzirah:** no named Da’at; exactly ten *sefirot belimah*.
2. **Zohar:** Da’at is conjunction, inclusion, concealed cognition, or fruit of Chokhmah and Binah.
3. **Cordovero:** an independently added eleventh is impossible; Da’at is Tiferet’s concealed root.
4. **Luria/Vital:** Da’at is technically real in several partzufic configurations but does not simply create an eleven-member list.
5. **Western occultism:** Da’at becomes an invisible station in the Abyss.

The statement “Da’at is the eleventh Sephira” is therefore acceptable only as shorthand accompanied by qualification.

## 2. Dating *Sefer Yetzirah*

- **Traditional:** Abraham, sometimes mediated through Rabbi Akiva.
- **Older scholarship:** dates as early as the second through fourth centuries.
- **Current critical tendency:** final form in late antiquity or the early Islamic period, securely attested by the early tenth century.
- **Open issue:** older units may predate final redaction.

No proposed date changes the negative finding: all major recensions insist on ten, not eleven.

## 3. Zohar authorship

- **Traditional:** Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle.
- **Scholem:** predominantly Moses de León, late thirteenth century.
- **Recent scholarship:** layered Castilian composition, with de León central but not necessarily alone.

The traditional attribution has theological authority within Kabbalah; the medieval dating has much stronger historical evidence.

## 4. Christian Cabalistic appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Kircher, and Knorr:

- preserved and translated Jewish materials;
- expanded access to Hebrew sources;
- also subordinated those materials to Christian Trinity, Christology, universal magic, or Egyptological speculation.

“Transmission” and “appropriation” are both accurate, provided neither is used to erase the other.

## 5. Kircher versus Jewish trees

There was no single Jewish tree that Kircher simply copied. Medieval and early modern *ilanot* vary substantially.

The Golden Dawn’s tree is:

- historically related to Christian Cabalistic diagrams;
- structurally useful for its grade system;
- not the sole or original visual meaning of the thirty-two paths;
- not identical with either a Lurianic or Gra-related Jewish diagram.

## 6. Tarot attributions

- Tarot cards existed before their Hebrew-letter attribution.
- Lévi created an influential nineteenth-century parallel.
- The Golden Dawn produced the standard occult mapping.
- Crowley revised the Heh/Tzaddi card positions.

None gives Da’at a trump.

## 7. The Abyss

Jewish Kabbalah certainly has gaps, veils, worlds, concealments, *tehom*, and dangerous transitions. But the fixed diagrammatic Abyss containing Da’at and Choronzon is a Western development.

Equating:

- Lurianic shattering,
- Christian Cabalistic abyss,
- Golden Dawn Abyss,
- Crowley’s ego-dissolution,
- Grant’s Nightside gateway

as one unchanged doctrine is historically indefensible.

## 8. Is Choronzon the Qliphah of Da’at?

No primary table examined says so.

A more accurate formulation is:

- Choronzon is Crowley’s personification or demon of dispersion in the Abyss;
- Da’at is the Abyssal threshold or false crown of divided knowledge;
- Grant turns Choronzon-Shugal into the guardian or polarity of the Da’at pylon;
- later authors simplify this to “Da’at’s demon.”

## 9. Does Da’at have a planet?

No classical one.

Proposals such as Sirius, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto reflect different modern theories:

- Sirius: hidden sun, Set, or extra-solar intelligence;
- Uranus: sudden supernal insight;
- Neptune: dissolution of rational boundaries;
- Pluto: death, transformation, underworld, black-hole symbolism.

These are mutually competing analogies. None is the Golden Dawn’s missing official planet.

## 10. Is Da’at the throat of Adam Kadmon?

Not as a general statement in the sources examined. Vitalian texts give throat symbolism to specific moments in the anatomy of Zeʿir Anpin or other partzufim. Grant later makes the throat/Vishuddha equation explicit. “Throat of Adam Kadmon” is an overgeneralized modern diagram label.

## Open questions

1. **Earliest technical use:** A complete lexical study across all Zoharic layers may locate earlier or more precise uses of Da’at as a named configuration than the famous *Idra* passages.
2. **Diagram genealogy:** The exact line from particular Jewish *ilanot* through Philippe d’Aquin to Kircher and the Golden Dawn remains more complex than standard occult histories admit.
3. **Golden Dawn colour provenance:** Da’at’s supplementary four colours appear in the published Regardie corpus, but their exact date and status within different temples deserve manuscript comparison.
4. **Reuchlin’s vocabulary:** A searchable diplomatic edition could determine every technical versus generic occurrence of *scientia* and *cognitio*.
5. **Or Neʿerav:** The text’s dependence on the *Pardes* is clear, but a critical English edition would permit more exact assessment of its Da’at terminology.
6. **Zoharic plurality:** Different strata relate Da’at variously to Tiferet, Zeʿir Anpin, Father/Mother, the “key including six,” and concealed cognition. These should be mapped rather than harmonized prematurely.
7. **Vitalian recensions:** Da’at’s position varies with partzuf, developmental stage, world, and editorial recension. A single static chart cannot contain every use.
8. **BOTA:** Restricted lesson material makes claims about Case’s private Da’at doctrine difficult to verify.
9. **Choronzon notebooks:** The differences between published *Liber 418*, surviving manuscript evidence, and later Neuburg anecdotes merit a dedicated archival study.
10. **Modern Jewish reception:** Contemporary Chabad diagrams frequently explain Keter and Da’at as alternate manifestations of a single unifying principle. The exact genealogy from Lurianic texts through later Chabad discourses remains a substantial study in itself.
11. **The “eleventh” slogan:** Its first exact printed occurrence in English occult literature remains to be established. The doctrine is older than the slogan, but the slogan may be quite late.
12. **Modern tables:** A bibliographic stemma is needed for the now-common Da’at assignments—Sirius, Uriel, lavender, prism, empty room, Janus, and “vision across the Abyss”—to separate Fortune, Regardie, Knight, Grant, and internet-era combinations.

## Conclusions

Da’at’s history is not the history of a forgotten eleventh ball waiting to be restored to a universal diagram.

In Jewish sources it develops as a grammar of relation:

- the Bible supplies knowledge, skill, intimacy, and a Chokhmah–Binah–Da’at triad;
- *Sefer Yetzirah* insists on ten and contains no technical Da’at;
- the Bahir prepares the theosophical field without clearly establishing a Da’at Sephira;
- the Zohar makes Da’at the conjunction, inclusion, issue, or hidden cognition of Father and Mother;
- Gikatilla associates it with Tiferet and the Middle Pillar;
- Cordovero explicitly rejects an added eleventh while affirming Da’at as Tiferet’s concealed root;
- Vital gives it several precise functions in the mochin, development, shattering, and repair of partzufim;
- Hasidism internalizes it as attachment—the power by which understanding becomes lived emotion and action.

In the Western tradition it becomes a grammar of threshold:

- Renaissance Christian Cabala transmits the ten Sephiroth but does not yet install Da’at;
- Knorr’s Latin makes *Daath sive cognitio* widely accessible;
- the Golden Dawn places an invisible conjunction in the Abyss;
- Crowley makes it the false crown of divided knowledge and the home of Choronzon;
- Fortune psychologizes it as cross-plane contact;
- Knight gives it a full chapter and symbolic mythology;
- Grant turns it into the pylon of the Nightside.

The anomaly is the doctrine. Da’at’s lack of a fixed number, planet, card, angel, or Qliphah is not missing data to be filled indiscriminately. It records the fact that Da’at names a relation, a state of integration, a passage between levels, or—especially in Western occultism—the failure of knowledge to become ultimate unity.

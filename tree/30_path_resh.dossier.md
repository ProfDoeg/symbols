# The Path of Resh: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary legend

The central historical finding is straightforward:

> **“The Path of Resh” as Path 30, joining Hod to Yesod, attributed to the Sun and Tarot Trump XIX, is a Hermetic-Qabalistic construction assembled between Kircher’s seventeenth-century path diagram and the Golden Dawn’s late-nineteenth-century correspondence system. It is not a discrete path doctrine in classical Jewish Kabbalah.**

Resh itself is ancient; its classification among the seven “double” letters is documented in *Sefer Yetzirah*. But its planet, bodily aperture, contrary qualities, position on a diagram, Tarot card, colors, and qliphotic tunnel belong to different textual strata and sometimes contradict one another.

Claims below use these labels:

- **[DT] Documented text:** stated in the named primary source.

- **[SR] Scholarly reconstruction:** an inference from manuscripts, paleography, textual criticism, or historical context.

- **[KT] Kabbalistic tradition:** a received Jewish attribution whose historical origin may be later than its traditional dating.

- **[HO] Hermetic-occult attribution:** a correspondence created or transmitted in Christian Cabala, ritual magic, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, BOTA, or related movements.

- **[D] Disputed:** manuscripts, recensions, scholars, or occult schools disagree.

- **[MI] Modern invention:** an attribution first securely evidenced in modern occultism, even if presented as ancient.

## The name

### Hebrew, transliteration, meaning, and etymology

The letter is **ר**, conventionally vocalized **רֵישׁ** or **רֵשׁ**. English spellings include *resh*, *reish*, *res*, and older Latinizing *resch*. Its name is cognate with Hebrew **רֹאשׁ** (*rosh*), Aramaic **רישא** (*resha*), and other Northwest Semitic words meaning “head,” “top,” “chief,” or “beginning.” **[SR]**

The usual historical reconstruction is:

1. An Egyptian or Proto-Sinaitic profile-head sign was given the acrophonic value /r/ from a Semitic word for “head.”

2. It developed into Proto-Canaanite and Phoenician **𐤓**.

3. Phoenician *rōš/rēš* passed into Aramaic script.

4. Imperial Aramaic and Jewish book hands gradually produced square Hebrew **ר**.

5. Greek borrowed it as rho, Ρ/ρ; Latin R ultimately belongs to the same alphabetic lineage.

This is a paleographic reconstruction, not a claim made in *Sefer Yetzirah*. The direct equation “the shape of modern ר is a picture of a head” is therefore too simple: the pictorial ancestry is reconstructed from much earlier signs, while the square form is the product of later scribal evolution. See the [Penn Museum history of the alphabet](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/the-alphabet/), the [Jewish Encyclopedia entry on Resh](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/12687-resh), and the [Unicode Phoenician chart](https://www.unicode.org/charts/nameslist/n_10900.html).

### Shape and scribal significance

Square-script **ר** differs from **ד** (*dalet*) primarily in the rounded rather than angular upper-right corner. Rabbinic scribal law treats confusion of the two as potentially meaning-changing. This is particularly vivid in:

- **אחד**, *eḥad*, “one,” in the *Shema*;

- **אחר**, *aḥer*, “another.”

Confusing dalet and resh can transform the confession of divine unity into a reference to “another.” That is genuine Jewish scribal and homiletic lore; it is unrelated to the Golden Dawn’s solar attribution.

### Resh as “head”

The semantic field includes:

- anatomical head;

- beginning or first principle;

- summit or top;

- leader or chief;

- aggregate or total, as in “head-count.”

Occult writers exploited all of these senses. Crowley’s *777* simply gives “Head” as the English meaning. Paul Foster Case expands “head” into consciousness, direction, solar centrality, and the organizing focus of personality. Those are Hermetic interpretations, not lexical necessities.

### Numerical values

- **ר = 200** in standard gematria. **[KT]**

- The spelling **ריש = 510** in standard additive gematria. **[KT]**

- Crowley’s table associates Path 30 with **465**, but this is the “mystic number” of 30, the sum 1+2+…+30. It is not the gematria of ריש. **[HO]**

Resh occurs in countless Hebrew words and divine epithets. Examples include **רחום**, “compassionate,” and **רם**, “exalted.” No classical source establishes a unique “divine name of the Path of Resh.”

## Biblical frame

### The biblical sources of the sefirotic vocabulary

The verses specified in the brief concern the sefiroth or divine Wisdom, not a Path of Resh.

#### 1 Chronicles 29:11

The verse reads in part:

> **לְךָ יְהוָה הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד... לְךָ יְהוָה הַמַּמְלָכָה**

It explicitly supplies the names Gedulah, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, and Mamlakhah. Kabbalistic exegesis reads **כי כל**, “for all,” as an allusion to Yesod and **הממלכה**, “the kingdom,” as Malkhut. **[KT]** The verse therefore helped furnish the names of the lower seven sefiroth, but it says nothing about Resh or an edge between Hod and Yesod. [Sefaria: 1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11?lang=bi).

#### Proverbs 3:19

“YHWH founded the earth by wisdom; He established the heavens by understanding.” This became an important proof text for Chokhmah and Binah and for creation through divine Wisdom. **[KT]** [Sefaria: Proverbs 3:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19?lang=bi).

#### Job 28

Job 28 asks where Wisdom can be found and distinguishes it from created treasures. It became a major source for Jewish speculation about primordial Wisdom but offers no Resh correspondence. [Sefaria: Job 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28?lang=bi).

#### Isaiah

Several Isaianic texts entered later kabbalistic interpretation:

- Isaiah 11:2 lists wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear of YHWH.

- Isaiah 45:7 speaks of forming light, creating darkness, making peace, and creating evil/calamity. Its polarity of **שלום / רע** is relevant to some versions of *Sefer Yetzirah*’s paired qualities.

Again, neither passage names a Path of Resh.

### Resh in the Torah

The Torah begins with **ב**, not ר. Resh is the second letter of **בראשית**, *bereshit*, and belongs to the root **ראש**, “beginning/head,” but no early source identifies this occurrence with a solar path.

The later alphabet legends explain why Resh was not chosen as the first letter of creation. That is aggadic letter lore, treated below, rather than an explanation of the Golden Dawn attribution.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. The alphabet before Kabbalah

Hebrew ר is attested as a letter long before the emergence of the kabbalistic Tree. The history from Northwest Semitic inscriptions through Paleo-Hebrew, Aramaic, and square script belongs to epigraphy and paleography. **[SR]**

The word **ראש**, “head,” is biblical. The mystical claim that the letter is an ontological channel through which creation is sustained is much later.

## 2. *Sefer Yetzirah*: date and structure

### Date

Traditional Jewish attributions connect *Sefer Yetzirah* with Abraham or Rabbi Akiva. **[KT]** Neither attribution is accepted as historical proof by critical scholarship.

Modern datings range widely:

- Gershom Scholem located its materials broadly in late antiquity and treated it as earlier than the medieval Kabbalah. **[SR]**

- Peter Hayman’s manuscript analysis emphasizes that the recoverable work is composite and that its final textual emergence may not be earlier than the ninth or early tenth century. **[SR/D]**

- Tzahi Weiss has argued for approximately the seventh century and a milieu interacting with Syriac-Christian cosmology. **[SR/D]**

Accordingly, “second century,” “sixth century,” and “tenth century” often refer to different things: hypothetical early strata, the composition of a recognizable work, or the earliest recoverable recensional form. See [Hayman’s critical edition](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/) and [Weiss’s study](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv16t6jtm).

### Thirty-two paths

The familiar opening defines thirty-two paths as:

- ten **sefiroth belimah**;

- twenty-two foundational letters.

The twenty-two are divided into:

- three mothers: אמש;

- seven doubles: בג״ד כפר״ת;

- twelve simples: הוזחטילנסעצק.

The phrase **עשר ולא תשע, עשר ולא אחת עשרה**, “ten and not nine, ten and not eleven,” is crucial for the later Da’at controversy. [Westcott’s 1887 translation](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/yetzirah.htm) and the [Sefaria Hebrew text](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1?lang=bi) provide accessible witnesses.

The core conception does **not** say that each letter is an edge on one fixed ten-node diagram. It presents letters and sefiroth as the thirty-two formative principles. Mapping each letter onto a specific line of a Tree is later.

## 3. Resh among the seven double letters

The seven are written בג״ד כפר״ת. Their “doubleness” is associated with paired pronunciation and paired qualities. Resh is anomalous because later Tiberian Hebrew does not ordinarily display the same stop/fricative alternation seen in letters such as ב, כ, and פ. **[D]**

Proposed explanations include:

- an older dialect distinguished two realizations of /r/;

- Resh could receive a limited dagesh in some traditions;

- the seven-letter group became schematic and was preserved after the phonetic distinction weakened;

- “double” may have cosmological as well as phonetic significance.

No single explanation is universally accepted.

### Planetary recensions

The most important source-critical result is that Resh was not always the Sun.

| Recension or occult redaction | Resh’s planet | Day | Bodily aperture | Paired quality |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Long recension | Mercury | Fifth day | Right ear | Beauty/Ugliness |
| Saadia recension | Mercury | Fifth day | Right ear | Beauty/Ugliness |
| Common early planetary order | Mercury | Fifth in the sequence | Varies in mixed printed texts | Varies |
| Gra recension | Saturn | Sixth day/Friday | Left nostril | Peace/Evil or Peace/War, depending translation |
| Westcott’s printed supplement | Saturn | Sixth day | Left nostril | Peace/Evil |
| Golden Dawn redaction | Sun | Sunday | Left nostril | Fruitfulness/Sterility |

The Long and Saadia sequences follow the classical descending planetary order:

**Saturn—Jupiter—Mars—Sun—Venus—Mercury—Moon**

assigned respectively to:

**Bet—Gimel—Dalet—Kaf—Peh—Resh—Tav**.

Thus Resh = Mercury in these witnesses. Hayman’s reconstructed text gives, in substance, Mercury, the fifth weekday, the right ear, and beauty/ugliness for Resh. A useful comparison, adapted from Aryeh Kaplan’s tables, is available at [Psyche’s recension comparison](https://www.psyche.com/psyche/yetsira/sy_planetaryattributions.html).

The Gra order instead produces:

| Letter | Gra planet |
|---|---|
| Bet | Moon |
| Gimel | Mars |
| Dalet | Sun |
| Kaf | Venus |
| Peh | Mercury |
| **Resh** | **Saturn** |
| Tav | Jupiter |

[Sefaria’s Gra version](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.4.1?lang=bi) accordingly places Resh under Saturn.

### The “Ari recension”

“Ari version” is used imprecisely in modern literature. There is no universally agreed independent Ari recension standing on the same textual footing as the Saadia and Gra witnesses. Aryeh Kaplan and later diagram-makers often speak of an “Ari-Gra” textual or graphic tradition because the Gra’s form was interpreted through Lurianic principles. **[D]**

It is therefore safer to say:

- Saadia’s recension documents Resh–Mercury.

- The Gra’s recension documents Resh–Saturn.

- “Ari” attributions in modern charts require identification of the particular printed recension or commentator.

- Resh–Sun is the Golden Dawn ordering, not an uncontested Lurianic reading.

## 4. Why the Golden Dawn changed the planets

The Golden Dawn wanted its seven double letters to match a particular occult planetary scheme. Its redacted *Sepher Yetzirah* openly notes that planetary allocations in editions were confused and then supplies the order used by the Order:

| Letter | Golden Dawn planet |
|---|---|
| Bet | Mercury |
| Gimel | Moon |
| Dalet | Venus |
| Kaf | Jupiter |
| Peh | Mars |
| **Resh** | **Sun** |
| Tav | Saturn |

That sequence is late and ritual-systematic. It should not be silently substituted into translations of the Jewish text. The relevant Golden Dawn redaction is preserved at [Sacred Texts](https://sacred-texts.com/oto/yetzirah.htm).

## 5. The “Collecting Intelligence”

### A separate medieval list

The thirty-two named “Intelligences” are not the same textual layer as the core recensions of *Sefer Yetzirah*. They come from a separate Hebrew tract or appendix generally titled **ל״ב נתיבות חכמה**, “The Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom.” **[DT/SR]**

Path 30 reads:

> **נתיב ל נקרא שכל כללי...**

That is, “The thirtieth path is called *Sekhel Kelali*,” after which the text relates it to astrologers’ judgments about stars, constellations, and their revolutions. The Hebrew is available in a manuscript-based transcription at [Mr. Kaplan’s Thirty-Two Paths](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Hebrew/32-paths-of-wisdom.html) and in manuscript records at the [National Library of Israel](https://www.nli.org.il/en/discover/manuscripts/hebrew-manuscripts/itempage?docId=PNX_MANUSCRIPTS990000541870205171&vid=MANUSCRIPTS).

### Translation of שכל כללי

- **שכל**, *sekhel*: intellect, intelligence.

- **כללי**, *kelali*: general, inclusive, comprehensive, collective.

“Collecting Intelligence” is therefore Westcott’s traditional English label, but “General” or “Comprehensive Intelligence” better represents the adjective’s ordinary sense.

Kircher translates it:

> **Intelligentia collectiva**

and explains that astrologers collect or derive their judgments concerning stars and celestial signs from it. [Kircher transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

Westcott’s 1887 rendering begins:

> “The Thirtieth Path is the Collecting Intelligence…”

and associates it with astrological judgment and knowledge of celestial revolutions. [Westcott appendix](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/yetzirah.htm).

### Does the Hebrew appendix identify Path 30 with Resh?

Not explicitly in its basic wording. The identification depends on the later arrangement:

- Paths 1–10 = sefiroth.

- Paths 11–32 = letters Aleph–Tav.

On that arithmetic, Resh, the twentieth letter, is Path 30. This ordering became standard in Hermetic Qabalah. It should not be projected back automatically into every Jewish witness of the list.

## 6. The *Bahir*

The *Bahir* probably reached its surviving form in Provence in the late twelfth century, though it contains older motifs. **[SR]** Traditional attribution to Neḥunya ben Haqanah is not historical evidence.

The work treats:

- divine powers and attributes;

- letters as formative symbols;

- the thirty-two paths of Wisdom;

- masculine and feminine dimensions;

- cosmic tree imagery.

It does **not** supply the later package “Resh, Path 30, Hod–Yesod, Sun, Trump XIX.” Searches in the [Sefaria text of the *Bahir*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.1?lang=bi) disclose general alphabetic and cosmological material, not a separate Resh path doctrine. Absence here is significant.

## 7. Talmudic lore of Resh

In *Shabbat* 104a, the alphabet is interpreted homiletically. **ק** is associated with *qadosh*, holiness, and **ר** with *rasha*, wickedness. The letters’ orientations and proximity generate a lesson about the wicked turning from God and the possibility of repentance. [Sefaria: Shabbat 104a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104a?lang=bi).

This is genuine rabbinic letter exegesis. It is not a qliphotic identification of Resh and does not establish Resh as intrinsically evil. The interpretation depends upon one word beginning with the letter.

## 8. *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

The medieval *Alphabet of Rabbi Akiva* presents letters petitioning or expounding their mysteries. It is traditionally linked to Rabbi Akiva but is not treated by critical scholarship as a second-century composition. **[KT/SR]**

In versions of the Resh legend, Resh appeals through favorable words such as:

- **ראש**, head or beginning;

- **רחום**, compassionate.

It is rejected because it also introduces **רשע/רשעה**, wickedness. Different recensions vary. See the [Hebrew Academy textual database](https://maagarim.hebrew-academy.org.il/Pages/PMain.aspx?mishibbur=74000&page=4) and the [Encyclopedia.com overview](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/alphabet-rabbi-akiva).

## 9. The Zohar and the letters petitioning for creation

### Authorship and date

Traditional attribution makes Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle the source of the *Zohar*. **[KT]**

Critical scholarship places its principal literary emergence in late-thirteenth-century Castile, with Moses de León central to its production or circulation. Scholem argued strongly for de León’s authorship; Yehuda Liebes, Ronit Meroz, Daniel Matt, and others have emphasized multiple voices, strata, or a broader circle without returning to literal second-century authorship. **[SR/D]** See Daniel Matt’s [Pritzker Edition front matter](https://supress.sites-pro.stanford.edu/sites/supress/files/media/file/4810_Front_Matter.pdf).

### Resh in the creation alphabet

In the Zoharic alphabet procession, letters approach God in reverse order asking that creation begin through them. Resh is embedded in the treatment of **ק–ר–ש** and **שקר**, “falsehood.”

The symbolic construction is approximately:

- ק and ר belong to an unstable or “other-side” formation;

- ש is recruited to make their falsehood sustainable;

- the resulting word שקר is a reason not to create through those letters.

This story does not make Resh a solar letter. Nor does it place Resh between Hod and Yesod. It belongs to the Zohar’s mythopoetic analysis of Hebrew words. See [Zohar, Introduction 6:34](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar%2C_Introduction.6.34?lang=bi) and the [Sulam commentary](https://www.sefaria.org/Sulam_on_Zohar%2C_Introduction.25).

## 10. Cordovero: *Pardes Rimmonim* and *Or Ne’erav*

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

Moses Cordovero attempted a systematic reconciliation of earlier Kabbalah. The *Pardes* treats:

- ten sefiroth and their relations;

- divine names;

- paths and channels;

- letters and vocalization;

- colors;

- worlds and angelic structures;

- contradictions among earlier authorities.

Relevant sections include the discussion of the [thirty-two paths](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.8.5), the [gate on letters](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.27.27), and the work’s [divine-name classifications](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.1.10).

Cordovero does not supply a Golden Dawn-style entry reading:

> Resh—Path 30—Hod to Yesod—Sun—The Sun—orange/gold/amber.

His “paths” are channels of divine relationship within a Jewish theosophical system, not Tarot-bearing initiatory roads.

### *Or Ne’erav*

*Or Ne’erav*, composed in the sixteenth century, introduces the prerequisites and proper approach to Kabbalah. It emphasizes religious discipline and the interdependence of the sefiroth. It contains no independent Path of Resh doctrine. [Sefaria: *Or Ne’erav*](https://prod.sefaria.org/Ohr_Ne%27erav%2C_Introduction.61).

## 11. Luria and Vital’s *Etz Chaim*

### Historical situation

Isaac Luria left comparatively little writing. The system called “Lurianic Kabbalah” is known chiefly through the records and redactions of disciples, especially Ḥayyim Vital. *Etz Chaim* was compiled from material stemming from Luria’s teaching in Safed during the 1570s. **[KT/SR]**

### Major doctrines

The work’s characteristic narrative includes:

1. **Ein Sof**, the infinite divine reality.

2. **Tzimtzum**, divine contraction or concealment.

3. The **kav**, a line or ray entering the vacated space.

4. **Adam Qadmon**, primordial configuration.

5. The four worlds: Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Assiah.

6. The emergence of vessels.

7. **Shevirat ha-kelim**, shattering of vessels.

8. Descent and entrapment of sparks.

9. Reconfiguration into **partzufim**, dynamic divine countenances.

10. **Tikkun**, repair or rectification through divine and human action.

See [Sefaria’s *Etz Chaim*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim) and its [gate on the breaking of the vessels](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.22.1).

These doctrines profoundly affected later drawings of the sefirotic structure. They do not, however, teach a Resh–Sun–Tarot path. Lurianic lines are processes of emanation, coupling, ascent, descent, and repair; the Golden Dawn’s paths are initiatory and correspondence-bearing.

## 12. Ari and Gra trees versus Kircher’s tree

Modern comparison charts often describe three layouts:

| Layout | Commonly assigned endpoints for Resh | Evidentiary caution |
|---|---|---|
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Hod—Yesod | Clearly visible on Kircher-derived Hermetic trees |
| “Ari” tree | Tiferet—Yesod | Later diagrammatic attribution; one must identify the manuscript or printed source |
| “Gra” tree | Tiferet—Yesod | Common in modern Gra-tree reconstructions |
| Core *Sefer Yetzirah* | No endpoint stated | The text classifies letters but does not prescribe this diagram |

The Ari/Gra organization is often summarized as:

- mothers on horizontal connections;

- doubles on vertical connections;

- simples on diagonal connections.

This elegantly matches the letter classes, but the precise graphic attribution is partly reconstructive. A comparative table is preserved at the [Arcane Archive](https://arcane-archive.org/tarot/tzaddi-is-not-the-star-1.php); a useful critical discussion of competing layouts appears in [*Kabbalistic Structural Trees*, Part III](https://quaker.zebby.org/tarot/kst/KST-Part-III.pdf).

The responsible formulation is therefore not “the Ari definitively placed Resh here,” but “later Ari/Gra diagrammatic traditions commonly place it here.”

## 13. The Vilna Gaon

The Vilna Gaon, Elijah ben Solomon Zalman (1720–1797), produced an influential commentary and recension of *Sefer Yetzirah*. His arrangement gives Resh to Saturn, not the Sun. [Sefaria: Gra version, chapter 4](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.4.1?lang=bi).

The Gra’s interpretation is Jewish, textual, and cosmological. The modern visual “Gra tree” should still be distinguished from the exact wording of the recension.

## 14. Hasidic readings

### Tanya

There is no chapter of *Tanya* devoted to “the Path of Resh.” That premise is a template mismatch.

*Tanya*, *Shaar ha-Yiḥud ve-ha-Emunah* chapter 11, does treat the twenty-two letters as distinct channels through which divine vitality enters creation. The shape and combinations of letters signify differentiated modes of transmission. [Sefaria: *Tanya*, Part II, chapter 11](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_II%3B_Shaar_HaYichud_VehaEmunah.11).

This supplies a Hasidic ontology of letters but not:

- Resh = Sun;

- Resh = Hod–Yesod;

- Resh = Tarot XIX;

- Resh = Raflifu.

*Tanya* chapter 22 discusses the emergence of apparent separateness and the kelipot, but it does not give the Golden Dawn ten-order qliphoth table. [Sefaria: *Tanya*, Part I, chapter 22](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.22).

### Naḥman of Bratslav

Rabbi Naḥman repeatedly treats the letters of Torah and prayer as vessels of vitality, thought, and divine union. Examples include:

- *Likkutei Moharan* 9, on the letters of prayer;

- teaching 38, involving the twenty-seven written letter forms and numerical interpretation;

- teaching 65, on joining prayer’s letters and words.

See [*Likkutei Moharan* 9](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.9), [38](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.38.1), and [65](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.65.2.1).

No securely documented Bratslav doctrine makes Resh the thirtieth solar path.

## 15. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and calendar

### Prayer and Psalms

Resh heads alphabetic verses in the Hebrew Bible:

- Psalm 119:153–160 forms the Resh stanza.

- Psalm 145:19 begins the Resh line **רצון יראיו יעשה**, “He fulfills the will of those who fear Him.”

These are alphabetic-liturgical uses, not Tree-path attributions. [Sefaria: Psalm 119](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.119?lang=he-en).

### Counting the Omer

Later kabbalistic Omer practice maps the forty-nine days to the seven lower sefiroth nested within one another: Ḥesed of Ḥesed through Malkhut of Malkhut. Resh has no standard Omer day. **[KT]**

### Calendar, month, and tribe

Months, tribes, and zodiacal signs belong to the twelve simple letters in *Sefer Yetzirah*. Resh is a double letter. Therefore:

- month: **not applicable**;

- tribe: **not applicable**;

- zodiacal sign: **not applicable in the Jewish classification**.

Resh instead receives a planet, weekday, bodily aperture, and pair of contrary qualities—and these vary by recension.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones nongentae* include forty-seven “according to the secret teaching of the wise Hebrews.” He used Hebrew names, letter permutations, number symbolism, and kabbalistic doctrines as evidence for Christian theological conclusions. **[DT/HO]**

Pico does not provide:

- a Path 30 diagram;

- Resh joining Hod and Yesod;

- Resh assigned to the Sun;

- Tarot correspondences.

His work is nevertheless a decisive stage in the creation of Christian Cabala. It reframed Jewish esoteric materials as confirmation of Christological propositions. This is historically influential and also a form of appropriation: the material was detached from Jewish law, liturgy, and communal authority. See the [Brown digital edition of Pico’s theses](https://cds.library.brown.edu/projects/pico/singleThesis.php?expand=sub10528&myID=p1052833) and the [Latin/Spanish text](https://spiritusmundi.com/es/obras/conclusiones-nongentae).

## 2. Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica*, 1517

Johannes Reuchlin presented Cabala through a Latin philosophical dialogue, combining:

- Hebrew divine names;

- number and letter techniques;

- Pythagorean philosophy;

- Christian theology.

Latin editions use transliterations such as *Res*, *Resch*, or forms governed by their Hebrew type and local orthography; “head” is Latin *caput*. No canonical Latin phrase “Path of Resh” emerges here. Reuchlin did not join Resh to the Tarot or securely establish Resh–Sun. See the [1517 digital scan](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105) and the [Folger catalogue record](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/92921).

## 3. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

*Portae Lucis* is Paulus Riccius’s Latin adaptation of Gikatilla’s *Sha’arei Orah*. Its title-page woodcut, attributed to Leonhard Beck, is among the earliest printed European presentations of a sefirotic structure.

The image is historically important for the visual transmission of Kabbalah into Christian Europe, but it is not a Golden Dawn Tree and does not depict Tarot-bearing paths. See the [1516 title page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3APortae_Lucis_1516.jpg).

## 4. Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, 1652–1654

### The decisive diagrammatic step

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part 1, presents a famous “Tree of the Sephiroth.” On the later Kircher-derived reading of its lines, Resh joins:

> **Hod—Yesod**

This is the layout adopted by the Golden Dawn and now often called the “Kircher Tree.” See the [Cornell digital plate](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272) and the [scanned volume](https://www.esonet.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Oedipus_Aegyptiacus02.pdf).

### Path title in Latin

Kircher gives the thirtieth path as:

> **Trigesima semita dicitur Intelligentia collectiva**

“the thirtieth path is called the Collective Intelligence.” He then relates it to astrologers’ judgments of stars, signs, and celestial revolutions. [Latin transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

### What Kircher did not yet supply

Kircher’s achievement should not be inflated. His plate supplies the path layout and Latinized “Intelligences,” but not the complete later formula:

- Resh = Sun;

- Resh = Tarot XIX;

- four Golden Dawn color scales;

- Crowley’s magical correspondences;

- Grant’s tunnel Raflifu.

Those stages came later.

### Difference from Ari/Gra trees

The Kircher layout does not consistently express the simple scheme of:

- mothers horizontal;

- doubles vertical;

- simples diagonal.

Later Ari/Gra diagrams attempt to embody that classification more strictly. Kircher’s is a Christian-syncretic diagram assembled within his Egyptian, Neoplatonic, astrological, and Catholic project.

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* made extensive Jewish kabbalistic writings available in Latin. The first volumes appeared in 1677–1678 and the second major installment in 1684.

It includes or summarizes material from:

- the Zoharic corpus;

- Cordoveran Kabbalah;

- Lurianic writings;

- commentarial and diagrammatic traditions.

Its importance is transmission. Later Christian esotericists who lacked Hebrew could encounter sophisticated Jewish theosophy through Knorr. It did not originate the Resh–Sun–Tarot correspondence. See the [Latin scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AKabbala_Denudata_Seu_Doctrina_Hebraeorum_Transcendentalis_Et_Metaphysica_Atque_Theologica_Opus_Antiquissimae_Philosophiae_Barbaricae_variis_speciminibus_refertissimum_%28IA_bub_gb_QzBBAAAAcAAJ%29.pdf) and the [scan index](https://www.billheidrick.com/Orpd/KRKD/index.htm).

Mathers’s 1887 *Kabbalah Unveiled* is not a translation of the whole *Kabbala Denudata*; it translates selected treatises.

## 6. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* made the twenty-two Tarot trumps correspond to the twenty-two Hebrew letters. This was foundational for modern occult Tarot. **[HO]**

But Lévi’s ordering was not the Golden Dawn’s. In the commonly understood one-based scheme:

- Qoph corresponds to Trump XIX, the Sun;

- **Resh corresponds to Trump XX, Judgment**;

- Shin corresponds to the Fool;

- Tav corresponds to the World.

Therefore the statement “Lévi taught Resh = the Sun” is false or at least dependent on a later reordering. See the [French text](https://www.arbredor.com/ebooks/Dogme.pdf) and [English translation](https://www.hermetikon.com/library/books/levy_transcendental_magic).

Papus preserved this general sequence in *The Tarot of the Bohemians*, explicitly treating Resh with Judgment and Saturn rather than the Golden Dawn Sun. [Sacred Texts: Papus](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/tob/index.htm).

## 7. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### Construction of the final classical occult package

Between approximately 1888 and 1900, Golden Dawn documents consolidated:

- Kircher’s Tree layout;

- the twenty-two Hebrew letters;

- a revised Tarot ordering;

- astrological attributions;

- the thirty-two “Intelligences”;

- four color scales;

- grades and initiatory journeys.

The result was:

> **Path 30 — Resh — Sun — Trump XIX, The Sun — Hod to Yesod — Collecting Intelligence.**

This is the earliest securely evidenced integrated system matching the modern phrase “Path of Resh.”

### Why Resh became the Sun trump

The Golden Dawn placed:

- Fool = 0 = Aleph;

- Magician = 1 = Bet;

- subsequent trumps in alphabetic order.

This shift from Lévi’s one-based sequence moves Trump XIX, the Sun, to the twentieth Hebrew letter, Resh.

*Book T* consequently calls the card:

> “The Sun—Lord of the Fire of the World—Resh—Sun.”

See the [Golden Dawn *Book T* scan](https://media-01.imu.nl/storage/divona.be/17547/book-t.pdf).

### Path position and initiation

In the Golden Dawn Practicus ritual:

- Practicus is the grade **3=8**, associated with Hod;

- the aspirant symbolically approaches Hod from Yesod by the thirtieth path;

- Resh and the Sun card are displayed and explained;

- the Sun card’s central solar image and the cross of thirteen squares receive ritual interpretation.

The ritual movement is Yesod → Hod, although the geometric path itself need not be treated as one-way. See the [Practicus ritual](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Ritual_Practicus.html) and [Fourth Knowledge Lecture](https://osogd.org/education/the-fourth-or-practicus-knowledge-lecture/).

### Four color scales

| World | Scale | Resh color |
|---|---|---|
| Atzilut | King | Orange |
| Beriah | Queen | Gold-yellow |
| Yetzirah | Prince/Emperor | Rich amber |
| Assiah | Princess/Empress | Amber rayed red |

These are Golden Dawn color correspondences, not colors stated by Jewish *Sefer Yetzirah*. [Path Resh color compilation](https://keepsilence.org/xxii/path-resh.html).

Some electronic transcriptions of *777* give “dark brown” in the Prince scale. Comparison with Golden Dawn color tables supports **rich amber**; “dark brown” appears to be a transcription error.

## 8. Aleister Crowley

### *Liber 777* (1909; revised posthumously)

Crowley retained the Golden Dawn attribution:

- Key Scale 30;

- Resh;

- Hod–Yesod;

- Sun;

- Trump XIX;

- Collecting Intelligence.

The table was largely compiled from Golden Dawn materials. Crowley later stated that he wrote much of it from memory and was dissatisfied with errors in the original edition. [Crowley’s account](https://hermetic.com/crowley/confessions/chapter57).

A facsimile of the correspondence tables is available at [Keep Silence](https://keepsilence.org/the-equinox/777/table-of-correspondences_low.pdf), with the revised text at [Hermetic.com](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf).

### *Liber 231*

Crowley’s *Liber Arcanorum* associates each letter-path with a brief poetic oracle and a sigil. The Resh verse begins:

> “Then the sun did appear unclouded…”

and joins the mouths of Asi and Asar in a solar-Egyptian image. [Resh page and *Liber 231* extract](https://keepsilence.org/xxii/path-resh.html).

The work was received in 1907 and first published in *The Equinox* in 1912. Its sigil/name **Raflifu** later became the basis of Kenneth Grant’s thirtieth tunnel. Crowley himself did not yet present the full Grantian tunnel system.

### *Liber Resh vel Helios*

*Liber Resh* instructs the magician to adore the Sun at:

- dawn;

- noon;

- sunset;

- midnight.

The title joins Resh’s value 200 with the Golden Dawn solar attribution. The rite is Thelemic and Hermetic, not an ancient Jewish prayer. [Thelemapedia: *Liber Resh*](https://thelemapedia.org/index.php/Liber_Resh).

### The Tzaddi/Heh question

Crowley’s statement “Tzaddi is not the Star” led him to exchange the Tarot attributions of Heh and Tzaddi:

- Emperor ↔ Star.

That revision does **not** change Resh’s attribution. Resh remains the Sun in Crowley’s system. Invoking the Tzaddi controversy to question Resh–Sun confuses two different problems.

### Thelemic grades

Golden Dawn and A∴A∴ grade structures associate:

- **3=8 Practicus** with Hod;

- **2=9 Theoricus** with Yesod.

Because Path 30 joins those spheres in the Kircher system, it can represent the transition from Yesod to Hod. There is no universally independent “degree of Resh”; it is part of a grade-transition structure.

## 9. Dion Fortune, 1935

*The Mystical Qabalah* is organized principally by sefiroth, not by twenty-two path chapters. There is therefore no “chapter on the Sephira Resh”: Resh is a letter-path, not a sephira.

Fortune’s chapters on Yesod and Hod help interpret its endpoints:

- Yesod as the image-forming, lunar foundation and “Treasure House of Images”;

- Hod as language, form, intellect, classification, and ceremonial magic.

An occult reading of Resh may therefore describe solar consciousness illuminating and organizing the image-field between Yesod and Hod. That is a synthesis from Fortune’s sephirotic chapters, not a full chapter she wrote on Resh.

The complete online text is at [Global Grey](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html). A purported “full Fortune chapter on Resh” was not found.

## 10. Israel Regardie

Regardie’s *A Garden of Pomegranates* gives the established Golden Dawn interpretation:

- thirtieth path;

- Resh, “head”;

- Hod–Yesod;

- Sun;

- Ra, Helios, Apollo, and Surya;

- orange/yellow solar colors;

- lion and sparrowhawk;

- sunflower, heliotrope, and laurel;

- frankincense and cinnamon;

- chrysolite;

- Collecting Intelligence.

Regardie’s work is an especially important vector because it moved Golden Dawn teaching into public occult literature. See [*A Garden of Pomegranates*](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06numerologia_cabala/Isreael_Regardie_A_Garden_Of_Pomegranates_2Nd%20Ed.pdf).

## 11. Paul Foster Case and BOTA

Paul Foster Case retained Resh–Sun and interpreted the “Collecting Intelligence” through solar accumulation and distribution:

- the Sun collects and concentrates cosmic energy;

- personality becomes a vehicle for a central life-power;

- Hod supplies articulate interpretation;

- Yesod supplies images and subconscious patterning.

In *The Book of Tokens*, the Resh meditation begins:

> “I am the Face which shineth ever…”

This is a twentieth-century occult meditation written in a biblical-kabbalistic voice, not an ancient Hebrew text. See Case’s [*Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom*](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/PFCase/32-Paths-of-Wisdom.html) and [*Book of Tokens*](https://www.gwotton.ca/library/720-3.pdf).

## 12. Gareth Knight

Gareth Knight’s *A Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* continued the Fortune–Regardie line. Its approach is psychological, magical, and initiatory: paths are experiential transitions as well as correspondence lists.

Knight’s treatment is influential in modern pathworking but does not establish historical Jewish antiquity for the Resh–Sun attribution. See the [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1350874M/A_practical_guide_to_Qabalistic_symbolism) and [Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books?id=Tw-B_Hzcp9MC&num=20).

## 13. Pathworking traditions

Modern Resh pathworkings commonly use some combination of:

- an amber or orange corridor;

- a solar disk;

- Trump XIX;

- the divine name or archangel borrowed from Tiferet;

- descent into Yesod’s image-world or ascent toward Hod’s articulate intellect;

- exposure of illusion by direct light;

- regulation of pride, self-image, or the desire for recognition;

- Crowley’s Resh sigil;

- Grant’s Raflifu tunnel as a shadow-working counterpart.

These are modern magical practices and frequently report dreams, visions, animals, landscapes, or personal messages. Such reports are evidence for practitioners’ experiences and communities of interpretation, not evidence about ancient Jewish Kabbalah. Examples include [Light in Extension’s Resh pathworking](https://lightinextension.wordpress.com/2018/03/22/pathworking-on-the-30th-path-of-resh/) and [Tarot Pathwork’s account](https://tarotpathwork.blogspot.com/2010/03/30th-path-resh-sun.html).

# The Qliphah or tunnel

## Jewish kelipot versus occult qliphoth

Jewish Kabbalah uses **קליפות**, *qelippot*, “shells” or “husks,” for forces of concealment, impurity, separation, or residual material surrounding holiness. Zoharic, Lurianic, and Hasidic sources do not present one completely uniform ten-name table.

The familiar list of ten qliphotic orders belongs to later Hermetic systematization. **[HO/D]**

## The standard occult list

Spellings vary extensively because Hebrew and Aramaic names passed through Latin, German, French, and English occult publications.

| Sephira | Qliphotic order | Common ruler or chief in later tables |
|---|---|---|
| Keter | Thaumiel/Thamiel | Satan and Moloch |
| Chokhmah | Ghagiel/Ghogiel | Beelzebub |
| Binah | Satariel | Lucifuge Rofocale |
| Chesed | Gha’agsheblah/Gamchicoth | Astaroth |
| Gevurah | Golachab/Golab | Asmodeus |
| Tiferet | Thagirion/Tagiriron | Belphegor |
| Netzach | A’arab Zaraq/Harab Serapel | Baal |
| Hod | Samael | Adrammelech |
| Yesod | Gamaliel | Lilith |
| Malkhut | Lilith/Nehemoth | Naamah |

The Golden Dawn’s “Qliphoth” paper gives variant spellings and warns that the demon chiefs cannot always be referred cleanly to one sephira. [Golden Dawn qliphoth paper](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html). Crowley’s *777* further stabilized a version of the list.

## The tunnel of Resh: Raflifu

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* (1977), pp. 246–247, places the thirtieth tunnel under the aegis of **Raflifu**. **[MI]**

Its main features are:

| Feature | Grantian attribution |
|---|---|
| Tunnel | 30 |
| Genius/guardian | Raflifu |
| Qliphotic endpoints | Samael—Gamaliel |
| Diurnal counterpart | Path of Resh, Hod—Yesod |
| Color | Amber, with the sigil rayed or traced in red |
| Planetary force | A blackened, eclipsed, or averse solar current |
| Animal | Leopard in Grant’s expanded table |
| Magical power | Acquiring wealth; red tincture |
| Source of name/sigil | Crowley’s *Liber 231* |
| Date of tunnel system | 1977 publication |

Grant’s “tunnels of Set” are not found in the Zohar, Cordovero, Luria, or the Golden Dawn’s original papers. They are a Typhonian elaboration combining:

- the reverse side of the Kircher Tree;

- Crowley’s *Liber 231* sigils;

- qliphotic orders;

- Lovecraftian, Egyptian, tantric, and personal visionary material.

The scan of Grant’s discussion is available in [*Nightside of Eden*](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/G/Grant%20-%20Nightside%20of%20Eden.pdf).

### Is Raflifu a demon prince?

Not in classical Jewish demonology. Grant calls such figures genii or powers of the tunnels. “Demon prince of Resh” is therefore misleading.

A more precise hierarchy is:

- **Raflifu:** Grantian tunnel genius.

- **Samael:** qliphotic order corresponding to Hod.

- **Adrammelech:** later occult ruler associated with Samael/Hod.

- **Gamaliel:** qliphotic order corresponding to Yesod.

- **Lilith:** later ruler associated with Gamaliel/Yesod.

Modern websites that describe Raflifu as an ancient vampire, autonomous goetic prince, or historically Jewish demon go beyond the evidence. **[MI]**

# People and works

## Jewish authors and transmitters

### Saadia Gaon

His recension/commentary is essential because it preserves Resh–Mercury rather than Resh–Sun. It is among the strongest witnesses against presenting the Golden Dawn order as the single ancient one. [Saadia translation PDF](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/judaism/saadia.pdf).

### Shabbetai Donnolo

An early commentator on *Sefer Yetzirah*, important to its medieval cosmological reception. His presence helps show that *Sefer Yetzirah* was interpreted before the classical sefirotic Tree had stabilized.

### Moses de León and the Zoharic circle

Important for the alphabetic creation legend and the mythic treatment of ק–ר–ש and שקר, not for Resh–Sun.

### Joseph Gikatilla

His *Sha’arei Orah* organized divine names and sefiroth as gates. The work influenced Christian Cabala through *Portae Lucis*. It does not provide the Golden Dawn path correspondence.

### Moses Cordovero

Systematized competing earlier kabbalistic materials. His discussions of letters, colors, names, and paths became a reservoir for later adaptation.

### Isaac Luria and Ḥayyim Vital

Their cosmology transformed the meaning of emanation and repair. Later diagram-makers frequently invoked their authority, sometimes more confidently than the documentary evidence permits.

### Vilna Gaon

His recension establishes a major Jewish Resh–Saturn tradition.

### Schneur Zalman of Liadi

Offers a Hasidic theology in which letters are channels of continuous divine creation, but no special solar Resh path.

### Naḥman of Bratslav

Develops experiential practices involving prayer’s letters, again without the occult path complex.

## Christian Cabalists and Hermetic occultists

### Giovanni Pico della Mirandola

Introduced Cabala into Renaissance Christian apologetic; no Resh path.

### Johannes Reuchlin

Expanded Christian Cabalistic study of Hebrew names and letters; no Tarot attribution.

### Paulus Riccius and Leonhard Beck

Riccius transmitted Gikatilla in Latin; Beck is associated with the memorable *Portae Lucis* image.

### Athanasius Kircher

Supplied the decisive diagram on which Resh lies between Hod and Yesod and transmitted the thirtieth intelligence in Latin.

### Christian Knorr von Rosenroth

Made Zoharic and Lurianic material accessible to Latin readers.

### Éliphas Lévi

Joined Hebrew letters to Tarot, but placed Resh with Judgment rather than the Sun.

### Papus

Popularized Lévi-style occult Tarot and likewise preserves a non-Golden-Dawn Resh attribution.

### William Wynn Westcott

Translated *Sefer Yetzirah*, appended the thirty-two Intelligences, and helped build the Golden Dawn synthesis. His translation should be read as an occult edition, not a modern critical text.

### Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers

Co-created Golden Dawn Tarot and path attributions and transmitted a partial English version of *Kabbala Denudata* material.

### A. E. Waite and Pamela Colman Smith

Their Tarot’s Sun card became the most familiar modern image attached to Resh, although most users of the deck do not employ Hebrew path correspondences.

### Aleister Crowley and Lady Frieda Harris

Crowley retained Resh–Sun; Harris’s Thoth Sun rendered it as a dynamic Thelemic image. The paintings were made principally from 1938 to 1943; the deck was published later.

### Dion Fortune

Interpreted Hod and Yesod psychologically and magically, furnishing a framework for later Resh pathworking without writing a dedicated Resh chapter.

### Israel Regardie

Publicly codified the Golden Dawn Resh correspondences.

### Paul Foster Case

Developed a solar-consciousness and “cosmic storage” interpretation in BOTA.

### Gareth Knight

Extended the path into modern ritual psychology and guided visualization.

### Kenneth Grant

Created the Raflifu tunnel as the nightside inverse of Resh.

## Scholars

### Gershom Scholem

Established the modern critical study of Jewish mysticism and argued for the medieval setting of the Zohar.

### Moshe Idel

Challenged overly linear accounts of Kabbalah and emphasized multiple experiential, linguistic, and ecstatic currents.

### Joseph Dan

Clarified distinctions among early Jewish mysticism, medieval Kabbalah, and later myth-making.

### Peter Hayman

Produced a critical synopsis of *Sefer Yetzirah*’s principal manuscripts, indispensable for planetary variants.

### Tzahi Weiss

Proposed a later and more culturally entangled setting for *Sefer Yetzirah* than older accounts.

### Daniel C. Matt

Produced the Pritzker *Zohar*, combining a reconstructed Aramaic text, translation, and detailed source annotation.

### Elliot R. Wolfson

Explored language, embodiment, gender, visualization, and temporality in Jewish mysticism. His work cautions against reducing Hebrew letters to simple pictorial or psychological “meanings.”

### J. H. Chajes

His work on *ilanot*—kabbalistic trees and scrolls—demonstrates that Jewish diagrams form a varied historical genre, not one timeless universal Tree. See the [Ilanot project](https://ilanot.info/en/) and [National Library overview](https://blog.nli.org.il/en/djm_ilanot/).

## Artists, poets, and novelists

- **Leonhard Beck:** associated with the *Portae Lucis* woodcut.

- **Pamela Colman Smith:** painted the Rider–Waite–Smith Sun.

- **Lady Frieda Harris:** painted Crowley’s Thoth Sun.

- **W. B. Yeats:** a Golden Dawn initiate whose poetry and occult notebooks reflect Hermetic and kabbalistic interests. No securely documented poem devoted specifically to Path 30 was found.

- **Alan Moore and J. H. Williams III:** *Promethea* uses the Hermetic Tree as narrative architecture. It is a modern artistic engagement with the occult Tree, not evidence for Jewish Resh doctrine.

No well-documented major novelist whose work is specifically and explicitly structured around the Path of Resh, rather than the Tree generally, emerged from the source review. That absence should not be filled with speculative associations to “sun imagery.”

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Dating *Sefer Yetzirah*

| Position | Nature of claim |
|---|---|
| Abrahamic authorship | Traditional attribution |
| Rabbi Akiva or early rabbinic authorship | Traditional attribution |
| Late-antique core | Older scholarly reconstruction |
| Seventh-century milieu | Weiss’s reconstruction |
| Composite work reaching recoverable form around the ninth–tenth centuries | Hayman’s text-critical emphasis |

The variation in Resh’s planet is itself evidence that the work circulated in multiple recensions.

## 2. Zoharic authorship

| Position | Status |
|---|---|
| Taught by Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai in the second century | Kabbalistic tradition |
| Written principally by Moses de León in late-thirteenth-century Castile | Scholem’s influential scholarly thesis |
| Produced through de León plus a circle, multiple authors, or successive strata | Current disputed scholarly refinements |

Traditional sanctity and historical authorship are different questions.

## 3. Was Resh originally Mercury, Saturn, or the Sun?

- **Mercury:** strongest early explicit recensional support in Long and Saadia.

- **Saturn:** Gra and Westcott’s Gra-like supplement.

- **Sun:** Golden Dawn redaction and its descendants.

Thus “Resh is the Sun according to *Sefer Yetzirah*” is too broad. The accurate claim is “Resh is the Sun in the Golden Dawn’s rearrangement of the seven doubles.”

## 4. Is the thirtieth Intelligence part of *Sefer Yetzirah*?

The “Collecting Intelligence” is traditionally printed with occult editions of *Sefer Yetzirah*, but manuscript and textual history distinguish it from the core work. **[D]**

Calling it “the Yetziratic text” is conventional occult usage. Calling it an uncontested sentence of the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah* is misleading.

## 5. Kircher versus Jewish trees

Kircher’s layout became dominant in Western occultism because it accommodated a rich matrix of Tarot, astrology, alchemy, and initiation. It is not the only kabbalistic diagram.

Jewish *ilanot* vary by:

- period;

- school;

- manuscript;

- Cordoveran or Lurianic purpose;

- whether they represent emanation, partzufim, worlds, divine names, or ritual intention.

The Kircher Tree’s apparent universality is a consequence of occult reproduction, not proof that it was the original Jewish Tree.

## 6. Tarot attributions

No evidence places the Hebrew-letter/Tarot system in ancient Judaism or medieval Kabbalah.

The documented sequence is:

1. Tarot develops in Renaissance Italy.

2. Eighteenth- and nineteenth-century French occultists propose ancient Egyptian and kabbalistic origins.

3. Lévi systematically correlates letters and trumps.

4. Golden Dawn reorders the Fool and creates Resh–Sun.

5. Crowley modifies Heh/Tzaddi but retains Resh–Sun.

Therefore the Resh–Sun trump is a nineteenth-century occult attribution.

## 7. The Tzaddi/Heh controversy

It concerns the Emperor and Star, not the Sun. It is relevant as evidence that occult Tarot mappings were actively revised, but it does not create an alternative Crowleyan card for Resh.

## 8. Christian Cabala and appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Kircher, and Knorr preserved and disseminated Jewish materials, sometimes with considerable philological effort. They also reframed them within Christian theological agendas.

The contested issues include:

- reading Kabbalah as proof of the Trinity or Incarnation;

- removing it from halakhic and liturgical practice;

- treating Jews as custodians of truths supposedly completed by Christianity;

- combining Jewish sources with Egyptian, Hermetic, Neoplatonic, or magical systems and calling the result ancient Hebrew doctrine.

“Transmission” and “appropriation” are not mutually exclusive descriptions.

## 9. Da’at’s status

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists on ten sefiroth, “not nine and not eleven.” Da’at therefore cannot simply be inserted as an eleventh coequal sephira without explanation.

Later kabbalistic systems commonly use two enumerations:

- Keter included, Da’at not counted independently;

- Da’at counted when Keter is concealed or omitted.

Hermetic diagrams often show Da’at as a dotted, hidden, or “non-sephira” position. This is not evidence that ancient Kabbalah possessed an ordinary eleventh sphere. A Jewish explanation of the two counts is given by [Chabad’s “Two Systems of Ten Sefirot”](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/137112/jewish/Two-Systems-of-Ten-Sefirot.htm).

Da’at does not directly alter the standard Hod–Yesod placement of Resh, though alternative trees can alter path numbering more generally.

## 10. Is Resh really a double consonant?

Its inclusion in בג״ד כפר״ת is textual fact. The nature of its phonetic doubling is disputed.

Later Hebrew pronunciation does not give it the familiar plosive/fricative opposition of Bet, Kaf, or Peh. Historical dialect evidence and exceptional forms suggest that more than one realization of resh may once have existed, but the exact connection to *Sefer Yetzirah* remains uncertain.

## 11. “Path 20” versus “Path 30”

Some modern writers call Resh “the twentieth path” because it is the twentieth letter. In Golden Dawn key-scale numbering it is Path 30 because:

- 1–10 are sefiroth;

- Aleph begins at 11;

- Resh is 30.

Both usages can be deciphered, but “Path 30” is the historically standard Golden Dawn formulation.

## 12. Qliphotic antiquity

The kelipot are Jewish. The exact table Thaumiel through Lilith is Hermetic standardization. Raflifu as the tunnel of Resh is specifically Grantian.

Collapsing all three layers into “ancient Kabbalah says Raflifu rules Resh” is historically false.

# Open questions

1. **What was the original phonetic “doubling” of Resh?**  
   Manuscript and dialect evidence has not produced a universally accepted reconstruction.

2. **How early is the explicit planet-by-letter assignment in *Sefer Yetzirah*?**  
   Some witnesses omit the one-to-one allocations; the surviving recensions may preserve different redactional stages.

3. **What is the precise date and provenance of the Hebrew Thirty-Two Intelligences list?**  
   It is medieval and textually distinct from the core *Sefer Yetzirah*, but its exact origin remains uncertain.

4. **Which diagrams can securely be attributed to Luria or the Gra themselves?**  
   Modern charts frequently reproduce later normalized versions without tracing a manuscript stemma.

5. **Did Kircher derive every path-line directly from a Jewish exemplar?**  
   His diagram reflects Jewish sources but also his own Christian and syncretic organization. Its precise graphic genealogy needs manuscript-by-manuscript comparison.

6. **When exactly was the Golden Dawn planetary rearrangement first finalized?**  
   The Order’s papers establish the late-nineteenth-century system, but dating individual instructional documents can be difficult because surviving copies are later.

7. **Who first described Resh’s qliphotic inverse explicitly as a “tunnel”?**  
   Crowley supplied the name and sigil; Grant supplied the mature tunnel cosmology. Intermediate private Typhonian development is poorly documented.

8. **Are Ari/Gra Resh endpoints primary tradition or retrospective diagrammatic regularization?**  
   The classification logic is strong, but printed comparative charts often provide weaker manuscript provenance than their labels imply.

9. **How much of *777*’s Resh row is Golden Dawn inheritance versus Crowley’s addition?**  
   The basic path, planet, trump, colors, and standard natural correspondences are largely Golden Dawn; revised columns add Crowleyan comparative material.

10. **Which modern pathworking reports influenced later published manuals?**  
    Much of this material circulated privately, orally, or within initiatory orders, making lines of dependence difficult to establish.

# Conclusions

1. **The authentic ancient element is the letter Resh, not the complete occult path complex.**

2. **Its earliest explicit planetary assignment is better evidenced as Mercury than Sun.** The Gra gives Saturn.

3. **The core *Sefer Yetzirah* does not place Resh on a Hod–Yesod line.**

4. **“Collecting Intelligence” comes from a separate medieval list of thirty-two Intelligences.**

5. **Kircher supplies the decisive Hod–Yesod diagram and the Latin *Intelligentia collectiva*.**

6. **Lévi joins Tarot to Hebrew but assigns Resh to Judgment.**

7. **The Golden Dawn creates the now-standard Resh–Sun–Trump XIX package.**

8. **Crowley preserves and greatly expands it; his Heh/Tzaddi revision does not affect Resh.**

9. **Raflifu is a twentieth-century Typhonian development based on Crowley’s *Liber 231*, not a Zoharic demon.**

10. **Jewish and Hermetic interpretations can be compared fruitfully, but treating the Hermetic table as an unchanged ancient Jewish teaching erases the actual textual history.**

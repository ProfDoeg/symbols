# Hod: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary legend

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of claim:

- **[PT — documented text]**: explicitly attested in a primary text or diagram.
- **[SR — scholarly reconstruction]**: a historical or philological conclusion argued by modern scholars.
- **[JT — Jewish tradition]**: a received Jewish-kabbalistic interpretation, whether or not historically original to the cited biblical or rabbinic text.
- **[HO — Hermetic-occult attribution]**: a Christian Cabalist, Golden Dawn, Thelemic, or later occult correspondence.
- **[D — disputed]**: competing texts, recensions, dates, or interpretations exist.
- **[MI — modern invention or synthesis]**: a system first demonstrably assembled in the modern Western occult tradition.

“Hod” is used for the Jewish sefirah; “Hermetic Qabalah” for the Western occult system. Similar words and diagrams do not imply identical theology.

### Findings on the item-specific uncertainties

- **Elohim Tzeva’ot:** securely Jewish and specifically associated with the Netzach–Hod complex in Gikatilla and Cordovero.
- **Michael or Raphael:** both occur, but at different taxonomic levels. Michael is Hod’s Sephirotic archangel in Agrippa/Golden Dawn tables. Raphael is the archangel invoked for the planet Mercury. Modern charts often collapse the two.
- **Benei Elohim:** part of Agrippa’s and the Golden Dawn’s angelic hierarchy; not found as a complete Hod correspondence in the principal Jewish sources inspected.
- **Mercury/Kokhav:** a Christian Cabalist/Hermetic correspondence. Pico’s 1486 scheme instead assigned the eighth sefirah to Venus and the ninth to Mercury.
- **Aaron or Moses:** Aaron is the standard Hod figure, Moses the standard Netzach figure, but the Zoharic corpus contains reversals. Later commentators explain these through crossed roots, emergence, and channels.
- **Perfect Intelligence:** not in the core *Sefer Yetzirah*. It belongs to a later list appended by Christian and occult editors.
- **Practicus:** Golden Dawn invention, later adopted by Crowley’s A∴A∴.
- **Names, versicles, apron:** Golden Dawn/Crowley symbols subsequently interpreted at length by Dion Fortune.
- **Intellect and magic:** characteristic Western occult interpretations, especially Fortune, Regardie, Case, and Knight—not a definition supplied by the Zohar or Cordovero.

## The name

### Hebrew, meaning, and etymology

**[PT]** The spelling is **הוד**; with Masoretic pointing, **הוֹד**. Its consonants have the numerical value:

- ה = 5
- ו = 6
- ד = 4
- Total = **15**

That gematria is real but does not explain why Hod is the eighth sefirah.

Biblical lexica give a semantic field including splendor, majesty, glory, honor, magnificence, and vigor. BDB treats it as a noun whose deeper verbal root is uncertain. The same noun can describe divine majesty, royal dignity, physical vigor, the grandeur of a voice, or radiance conferred upon a leader.

**[JT]** Hasidic and devotional writers frequently connect *Hod* with **הודאה, hoda’ah**, acknowledgment, thanksgiving, or confession. This is spiritually important: Hod becomes the power to acknowledge what exceeds one’s comprehension and to say “thank you.”

**[SR]** That connection is best treated as homiletical wordplay, not a demonstrated historical etymology. *Hoda’ah* belongs morphologically to the root י־ד־ה, while the historical derivation of הוד is uncertain.

### Biblical sources

#### 1 Chronicles 29:11

**[PT]** David’s prayer contains five later sefirah-names in sequence:

> לְךָ יְהוָה הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד

“To you, YHWH, belong greatness, power, beauty, eternity/victory, and splendor.”

**[JT]** Medieval Kabbalah reads the verse as a scriptural register of the seven lower sefirot:

| Verse term | Kabbalistic reading |
|---|---|
| הַגְּדֻלָּה, “the greatness” | Chesed/Gedullah |
| הַגְּבוּרָה, “the power” | Gevurah |
| הַתִּפְאֶרֶת, “the beauty” | Tiferet |
| הַנֵּצַח, “the victory/eternity” | Netzach |
| הַהוֹד, “the splendor” | Hod |
| כֹל, “all” | Yesod |
| הַמַּמְלָכָה, “the kingdom” | Malkhut |

**[SR]** The verse itself does not present a Tree, enumerate emanations, or say that *kol* means Yesod. That is medieval mystical exegesis imposed upon a biblical doxology.

#### Numbers 27:20

**[PT]** Moses is commanded concerning Joshua:

> וְנָתַתָּה מֵהוֹדְךָ עָלָיו

“Place some of your *hod* upon him.”

Rashi stresses “some, but not all”: Moses is likened to the sun and Joshua to the moon. This is a biblical use of *hod* as conferred dignity or radiance, not an explicit reference to the eighth sefirah.

#### Proverbs 3:19, Job 28, and Isaiah

These passages matter to the vocabulary of the Tree but do not name Hod as the eighth emanation.

- **[PT]** Proverbs 3:19 associates creation with **חכמה**, Wisdom, and **תבונה**, Understanding—the vocabulary later attached to Chokhmah and Binah.
- **[PT]** Job 28 asks where Wisdom and Understanding can be found. It supplies the language of concealed divine wisdom, not Hod’s name or position.
- **[PT]** Isaiah 11:2 lists wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear of YHWH. These became important to later systems of divine powers.
- **[PT]** Isaiah 30:30 uses **הוֹד קוֹלוֹ**, “the majesty of His voice.” It is relevant to Hod’s semantic range but not proof of a sefirah.
- **[JT]** Kabbalists subsequently read such passages through an already-developed sefirah system.

### The Reuchlin problem

**[PT]** Reuchlin’s 1517 printing reads **consessio laudis**. *Consessio* ordinarily suggests a sitting together, assembly, or session, while later literature regularly reports **confessio laudis**, “confession of praise.”

**[D]** There are three possibilities:

1. Reuchlin intentionally wrote *consessio*.
2. The compositor produced a typographical error for *confessio*.
3. Later occultists normalized an obscure reading into a more theologically familiar expression.

No decisive correction was supplied in the edition inspected. Therefore “Reuchlin translated Hod as *confessio laudis*” should not be repeated without qualification.

## The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*: the system before Hod was named

### Date and recensions

**[SR/D]** The date remains contested. Proposals range from late antiquity—often third through sixth centuries—to an early medieval compilation. Peter Hayman’s critical edition emphasizes that the securely recoverable textual record emerges in the early tenth century, particularly with Saadia Gaon’s commentary.

Hayman distinguishes major textual families conventionally called:

- the Short recension;
- the Long recension;
- the Saadyan recension.

Recent work by Avishai Bar-Asher has challenged the older assumption that Saadia’s form is necessarily a late expansion and argues that it may preserve early structural features.

**[JT/D]** “Ari recension” and “Gra recension” are practitioner names for early-modern edited/redacted forms associated respectively with Isaac Luria’s school and the Vilna Gaon. They should not be treated as independent ancient manuscript recensions on the same evidentiary footing as Hayman’s text families.

### The thirty-two paths

**[PT]** *Sefer Yetzirah* 1:1 begins:

> בִּשְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם נְתִיבוֹת פְּלִיאוֹת חָכְמָה

“With thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom…”

These are then analyzed as:

- ten **sefirot belimah**;
- twenty-two foundation letters;
- three Mothers: א מ ש;
- seven Doubles: ב ג ד כ פ ר ת;
- twelve Simples: ה ו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק.

The precise letter groupings and their astrological assignments vary between recensions.

### The ten *sefirot belimah*

**[PT]** In the core text they are cosmic numbers or dimensions, not the later named sequence. A representative recension proceeds through:

1. Spirit of the living God
2. Spirit from Spirit
3. Water from Spirit
4. Fire from Water
5–10. Above, below, east, west, north, south

**[Negative finding]** The words Hod, Netzach, Yesod, or Malkhut do not name these ten in the foundational text.

**[SR]** Consequently, statements such as “the *Sefer Yetzirah* teaches that Hod is Mercury” conflate at least three later developments:

1. the medieval replacement of the cosmic sefirot with named divine powers;
2. the later *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom* intelligence-text;
3. the Renaissance planetary hierarchy.

### “The Perfect Intelligence”

**[PT/D]** The sentence—

> “The Eighth Path is called the Absolute or Perfect Intelligence…”

—is from the separate *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom*, circulated in Hebrew and Latin forms and appended by William Wynn Westcott to his English *Sepher Yetzirah*. The text continues by connecting the eighth intelligence with the hidden roots of Gedullah.

**[SR]** Don Karr’s bibliographical work and modern textual scholarship distinguish this appendix from the core *Sefer Yetzirah*. Its precise date and Hebrew textual genealogy remain unsettled, but it is medieval or later—not part of the earliest recoverable *Yetzirah*.

**Finding:** “Hod is the Perfect Intelligence according to the *Sefer Yetzirah*” is historically misleading. More accurately: “Golden Dawn and Thelemic tables number Hod eight and pair it with the eighth entry of a later appendix traditionally printed with *Sefer Yetzirah*.”

## 2. The *Bahir*

### Date and authorship

**[JT]** The *Bahir* is traditionally associated with Rabbi Nehuniah ben ha-Qanah and other tannaitic sages.

**[SR/D]** Gershom Scholem placed its public appearance in Provence near the end of the twelfth century while arguing that it incorporated older eastern materials. Avishai Bar-Asher’s 2023 reassessment argues that this confident stratigraphic narrative rests on much weaker evidence than scholars formerly admitted. The work’s formation is therefore still obscure.

### Hod in the *Bahir*

**[SR/D]** Scholem observed that Hod as a named sefirah appears only in a passage quoted as *Bahir* by Todros Abulafia and questioned whether that quotation belonged to the authentic text. The standard surviving *Bahir* does not provide a stable, systematic Hod doctrine.

What it does provide is crucial background:

- divine powers interpreted through scriptural symbols;
- limbs and cosmic structures;
- male/female polarities;
- lower powers associated with righteous figures, channels, supports, and divine governance.

**[SR]** Arthur Green and other interpreters suggest that the weakly differentiated lower powers which later became Netzach and Hod may have developed from paired angels or supports—sometimes compared with Metatron and Sandalphon—and were shifted as the tenfold order stabilized.

**Finding:** calling the *Bahir* the source of a fully formed eighth sefirah named Hod overstates the textual evidence.

## 3. Thirteenth-century consolidation: Gikatilla and the Zohar

### Joseph Gikatilla, *Sha’arei Orah*

Gikatilla composed *Sha’arei Orah* around 1293. Its organizing concern is the hierarchy of divine names and the proper direction of prayer.

**[PT/JT]** Gikatilla treats the seventh and eighth sefirot together because their divine names are paired:

- Netzach: **YHWH Tzeva’ot**
- Hod: **Elohim Tzeva’ot**

The word **צבאות, Tzeva’ot**, “Hosts,” signifies the ordered multitudes or forces governed through these channels.

**[PT/JT]** In the Hod material, the sefirah is associated with the operation of Gevurah and judgment: divine splendor becomes the force by which enemies are humbled and deliverance occurs. Thanksgiving for miracles and the *Modim* blessing are therefore placed in Hod by later transmitters of Gikatilla’s teaching.

**Important boundary:** Gikatilla supplies Elohim Tzeva’ot and the prayer-theology of Hod. The inspected text does not supply the complete Michael–Benei Elohim–Mercury table later attributed to “the Kabbalah.”

### The Zoharic corpus

**[JT]** Traditional attribution identifies Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle, in the second century.

**[SR/D]** From the nineteenth century onward, textual and historical scholarship—especially Scholem—located the main literary emergence in late-thirteenth-century Castile and associated much of it with Moses de León. Current scholarship often speaks of composite “Zoharic literature”: de León was pivotal, but a simple one-author forgery model is no longer the only scholarly reconstruction.

### Hod and Netzach

**[PT/JT]** Zoharic texts regularly identify Netzach and Hod as:

- the two thighs or legs;
- supports of the body;
- channels by which higher powers extend into manifestation;
- prophetic powers;
- paired with Moses and Aaron;
- associated with Samuel and the establishment of kingship.

*Tikkunei Zohar* gives the familiar anthropomorphic sequence: Chesed and Gevurah are the arms, Netzach and Hod the thighs.

These are symbolic dimensions of the divine configuration, not statements that God has a material body.

### Aaron or Moses?

The conventional chart is:

| Sefirah | Figure |
|---|---|
| Netzach | Moses |
| Hod | Aaron |

Aaron fits Hod through priestly liturgy, verbal confession, thanksgiving, ordered ritual, and the splendor of priestly vestments.

**[D]** The Zoharic corpus also contains reversals. *Mikdash Melekh* explicitly notices that some passages place Moses in Hod and Aaron in Netzach.

Later solutions include:

- Moses is rooted on the right/Netzach but emerges through Hod;
- Aaron is rooted in Hod but manifests through the opposing channel;
- each includes the other because the pair functions jointly;
- roots, branches, and historical manifestation need not carry the same attribution.

This is not simply a scribal mistake that modern tables have “corrected.” It is a real internal complexity within the tradition.

### Samuel

**[PT/JT]** Zoharic material associates Samuel with Hod, particularly in his prophetic and royal-anointing functions. This complements rather than displaces Aaron: several biblical figures can embody a sefirah under different relations.

## 4. Cordovero: synthesis before Luria

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

Moses Cordovero completed *Pardes Rimmonim* in Safed in 1548. It is a large synthetic and comparative treatment of earlier Kabbalah, organized in thirty-two gates. The first printing was at Salonica in 1584; a 1592 Cracow edition supplied an important printed Hebrew sefirah diagram.

### Hod’s Cordoverian profile

**[PT/JT]** Cordovero presents Hod as inseparable from Netzach but differentiated from it:

- Netzach and Hod are the two supports or legs.
- Hod is the left-side support.
- Netzach is associated more with bone or enduring strength; Hod with flesh and the receptive or judgmental side.
- Hod receives from Gevurah and therefore has a stronger relation to *din*, judgment.
- Both participate in prophecy.
- Both are lower courts or instrumental powers through which higher decisions are implemented.
- Some authorities associate Netzach with day and Hod with night.

### Speech, prophecy, and *hashmal*

**[PT/JT]** In *Pardes* 23:8, Cordovero interprets **חשמל, hashmal**, through a division in which silence and speech are distributed across Netzach and Hod. Hod is particularly linked to articulated prophetic speech.

This is an important Jewish antecedent for—but not identical with—the occult idea of Hod as language and intellect.

### Tzeva’ot

**[PT/JT]** Cordovero treats **צבא**, host, as implicating Netzach and Hod, while the final **אות**, “sign/letter,” can be related to Yesod. Thus Tzeva’ot describes ordered divine forces passing through the lower structure.

### *Or Ne’erav*

Cordovero’s *Or Ne’erav*, first printed in Venice in 1587, is an introduction and defense of kabbalistic study rather than a separate occult correspondence manual. It condenses the conceptual discipline underlying *Pardes*: the sefirot must be understood as divine modalities without dividing God into separate beings.

Ira Robinson’s English translation, *Moses Cordovero’s Introduction to Kabbalah: An Annotated Version of His Or Ne’erav* (Yeshiva University Press, 1994), remains the principal English scholarly edition.

### Diagrammatic qualification

**[D]** Cordovero discusses channels in prose, and diagrams transmitted with *Pardes* do not all agree. Correctors and printers sometimes replaced or regularized drawings. It is therefore unsafe to infer an exact immutable “Cordovero Tree” from a single modern redraw.

## 5. Luria through Hayyim Vital

### Textual history

Isaac Luria taught in Safed until his death in 1572 and left no systematic book of his own. Hayyim Vital arranged teachings recorded from Luria; several recensions and compilations followed. Meir Poppers produced the influential organization known as *Derekh/Etz Hayyim*. It circulated in manuscript and was first printed at Korets in 1782–83.

Thus “the Ari says in *Etz Chaim*” normally means a Vitalian literary transmission of Lurianic teaching.

### The four worlds

**[JT]** Hod operates within every world in which a complete configuration of ten sefirot is contemplated:

1. **Atzilut** — Emanation
2. **Beriah** — Creation
3. **Yetzirah** — Formation
4. **Assiyah** — Action

The worlds are ontological registers. They are not originally the Golden Dawn’s four color scales, although the Golden Dawn later superimposed colors upon them.

### Contraction, vessels, shattering, and repair

Lurianic cosmology centers upon:

- **tzimtzum**, primordial concealment or contraction;
- the line of light entering the vacated space;
- configurations of lights and vessels;
- the world of Nekudim/Tohu;
- the death or shattering of the lower “kings” or vessels;
- the fall and intermixture of sparks;
- the reorganization of the sefirot into **partzufim**;
- **tikkun**, repair through divine reconfiguration and human religious action.

**[JT]** Hod participates in this history as one of the lower seven powers. It is not merely a static colored sphere. Its vessel, lights, interinclusion with the other sefirot, and placement within the partzuf determine its operation.

### Hod in Ze’ir Anpin

In the repaired structure, the six sefirot Chesed through Yesod form the principal body of **Ze’ir Anpin**:

- Chesed and Gevurah: arms;
- Tiferet: torso;
- Netzach and Hod: legs;
- Yesod: generative covenant/channel.

Malkhut/Nukva receives through this configuration.

**[JT]** Hod therefore serves as a left-sided lower extension of Ze’ir Anpin. It transmits higher configurations toward embodied action and Malkhut while remaining paired and interincluded with Netzach.

### Hod’s internal dynamics

*Etz Chaim* contains technical discussions of “reaching and not reaching” (*mati ve-lo mati*), descent and withdrawal of lights, and the construction of the lower vessels. These passages resist translation into the later slogan “Hod equals intellect.” In Lurianic terms, Hod is a dynamic position within a changing anatomy of lights, vessels, faces, and worlds.

### Tikkun

**[PT/JT]** Vitalian texts connect mitzvot with the clarification and elevation of sparks and the repair of vessels. Hod’s repair is consequently part of Torah observance, prayer, ethical action, and cosmic restoration—not chiefly a pathworking visualization.

## 6. The Gra and the problem of “the Gra Tree”

The Vilna Gaon, Elijah ben Solomon Zalman (1720–1797), commented on the *Sefer Yetzirah*, *Sifra de-Tzniuta*, and related literature.

**[JT]** His school sought to correlate the Tree’s channels more rigorously with the *Yetzirah*’s letter classes:

- Mothers as one geometrical class;
- Doubles as another;
- Simples as another.

In modern “Gra Tree” drawings, the three Mothers are commonly placed on horizontal relations, the seven Doubles on verticals, and the twelve Simples on diagonals.

**[D]** A “Gra Tree” is already an interpretation of textual and diagrammatic material. Neither the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah* nor the Gra’s prose supplies one universally standardized modern poster. Aryeh Kaplan’s diagrams are influential reconstructions, not photographs of an ancient archetype.

In the most common Gra/Ari-type topology, Hod connects with Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, and Yesod, but not directly with Malkhut. The missing two lower diagonals are compensated by upper crossed channels between Chokhmah–Gevurah and Binah–Chesed.

## 7. Hasidic readings

### *Tanya*

There is no main numbered chapter of *Likkutei Amarim* exclusively devoted to Hod. The principal treatment is **Iggeret ha-Kodesh 15**.

**[PT/JT]** Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi describes Netzach and Hod through two anatomical pairs:

- kidneys, which “counsel”;
- testicles, which prepare and refine the seed transmitted through Yesod.

The analogy means that Netzach and Hod adapt an intellectual or spiritual influence so it can be transmitted and received. Hod differentiates, qualifies, limits, acknowledges, and prepares.

**[JT]** In Chabad interpretation:

- Netzach is the soul’s capacity to prevail or persist;
- Hod is surrender, acknowledgment, and gratitude;
- Hod admits the reality of what exceeds direct understanding;
- unhealthy Hod can become passive submission, empty convention, or confession without inward transformation.

*Iggeret ha-Kodesh* 5 also relates Netzach and Hod to the supporters of Torah, drawing on their anatomical identity as the legs and loins.

### Rabbi Nachman of Bratslav

**[PT/JT]** *Likkutei Moharan* I:32 associates Netzach and Hod with the legs and with their elevation through joy, song, and dancing. The heart’s joy raises and animates the feet.

This is not Mercury symbolism. It is a Hasidic embodiment of the Zoharic anatomy: affective and devotional transformation reaches the lower supports of action.

### Later Hasidic readings

Works such as *Peri Tzadik* connect Hod with:

- Aaron and priesthood;
- Chanukah and the Temple;
- the eighth quality and what exceeds a completed natural cycle;
- Lag ba-Omer, especially Hod within Hod;
- acknowledgment and thanksgiving after deliverance.

These are homiletical-theosophical developments, not uniform doctrines shared by all Hasidic schools.

## 8. Prayer, Omer, calendar, and Torah

### Prayer

**[JT]** Gikatilla’s tradition and later authorities place the *Modim* thanksgiving blessing of the Amidah in Hod. The association rests on *hoda’ah*: acknowledgment, confession, thanksgiving, and bowing.

**Boundary:** the statutory Amidah does not name Hod. The sefirah attribution is a kabbalistic layer of intention.

### Counting the Omer

The Torah commands counting seven complete weeks but does not assign sefirot to the days.

**[JT]** Later kabbalistic practice maps the forty-nine days as a 7×7 interinclusion of:

Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, Malkhut.

Hod is the fifth week:

| Omer day | Quality |
|---:|---|
| 29 | Chesed within Hod |
| 30 | Gevurah within Hod |
| 31 | Tiferet within Hod |
| 32 | Netzach within Hod |
| 33 | **Hod within Hod** |
| 34 | Yesod within Hod |
| 35 | Malkhut within Hod |

**[JT/D]** Lag ba-Omer, day 33, is consequently Hod shebe-Hod in this system. The exact date at which the now-standard printed 7×7 meditative grid first took its present form remains less securely documented than modern manuals imply.

### Calendar, month, tribe, and simple letter

**[Negative finding]** Hod, being a sefirah, has no intrinsic month, tribe, or one of the twelve Simple letters in the core *Sefer Yetzirah*. Those belong to a different classification.

Later synthetic charts may associate Mercury with Gemini or Virgo and then borrow the months/tribes assigned to their letters. That is an indirect occult construction, not “Hod’s month” in classical Jewish Kabbalah.

### Torah

The most direct Torah occurrence is Numbers 27:20, Moses placing some of his *hod* upon Joshua. Its later sefirah reading is traditional interpretation, not the verse’s plain grammatical meaning.

## The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* introduced detailed Cabalist claims into a public Christian philosophical program.

**[PT]** Pico maintains that Cabala confirms Christian doctrines, including the Trinity and Christ. That is a Christian theological reinterpretation of Jewish materials.

### Hod and the planets

A crucial negative finding is that Pico does not provide the later Hod–Mercury bundle. His conclusion 40.48 gives:

> “Venus octaue, Mercurius none, Luna decime.”

“Venus to the eighth, Mercury to the ninth, the Moon to the tenth.”

Thus:

| Number | Pico, 1486 | Later Agrippa/GD |
|---:|---|---|
| 8 | Venus | Mercury/Hod |
| 9 | Mercury | Moon/Yesod |
| 10 | Moon | Elements/Earth/Malkhut |

**Finding:** Pico is foundational to Christian Cabala, but not to the standard Golden Dawn planetary order.

---

## 2. Reuchlin, 1517

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* was printed at Hagenau in 1517. It presents Cabala through a Christian philosophical dialogue and integrates Hebrew learning with Pythagorean, Neoplatonic, and Christian claims.

Hod’s Latin gloss, as printed at fol. 62r, is **consessio laudis**. Later reports commonly change the first word to *confessio*.

**[HO/SR]** Reuchlin helped make the sefirot accessible to Christian readers but reoriented them toward Christian salvation history, including the construction YHShVH/Jesus from the Tetragrammaton plus Shin. This is not Jewish Kabbalah’s interpretation of the divine name.

---

## 3. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paulus Ricius’s Latin abridgment/translation of Gikatilla appeared at Augsburg one year before Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica*.

**[PT]** Its title page, designed by Hans Burgkmair the Elder, contains what the Center for Jewish Art identifies as probably the first printed image of the sefirot. A second tree appears inside the book.

The image is historically pivotal:

- it translated Jewish sefirah lore into Latin print culture;
- it visually stabilized relationships that earlier manuscripts represented in multiple ways;
- it circulated before the first printed Hebrew sefirah diagram in the 1592 Cracow *Pardes Rimmonim*.

It should not be mistaken for the earliest Tree diagram of any kind. Hebrew kabbalistic manuscripts with diagrams survive from the late thirteenth century; the iconic arboreal genre developed through the fourteenth century.

---

## 4. Agrippa, 1533: the decisive correspondence bundle

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa drafted *De occulta philosophia* around 1510 and published its complete form in 1533.

Book III, chapter 10 supplies the earliest securely located integrated hierarchy matching the later occult Hod:

- eighth sefirah: Hod;
- glosses: praise, confession, honor, fame;
- divine name: Elohim Sabaoth;
- angelic order: Archangels, called Benei Elohim;
- celestial sphere: Mercury;
- governing intelligence: Michael;
- human manifestation: elegance and consonance of speech;
- exemplary ruler: Solomon.

**[HO]** This is a synthesis of Christian Cabala, angelology, astrology, and Renaissance magic. It is the proximate ancestor of the Golden Dawn table.

### Michael and Raphael resolved

Agrippa and later occultism use more than one hierarchical grid:

1. **Sephirotic hierarchy:** Hod → Michael → Benei Elohim → Mercury.
2. **Planetary hierarchy of Mercury:** divine name and angelic names assigned specifically to Mercury, including Raphael, Tiriel, and Taphthartharath.

Modern correspondence charts often merge these grids and then report an apparent contradiction. In the original logic, they are overlapping but different levels.

Agrippa’s own Hod exemplar is Solomon, not Aaron. Aaron enters the Latin comparative tradition through Jewish/Kabbalistic correspondences transmitted by later compilers.

---

## 5. Kircher, 1652

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I, appeared in 1652. Its celebrated plate presents the sefirot, divine names, celestial orders, and twenty-two lettered channels.

### The plate

**[PT]** Hod is labeled **Gloria**. The diagram has the topology later adopted by the Golden Dawn:

- both Netzach and Hod connect directly to Malkhut;
- there are no crossed upper paths Binah–Chesed and Chokhmah–Gevurah;
- the Hebrew letters are placed sequentially along the twenty-two channels in the now-familiar Hermetic arrangement.

### Sources and originality

**[SR/D]** Kircher did not simply invent the entire diagram. His sources included Jewish kabbalistic materials, Christian Cabalist adaptations, and Philippe d’Aquin’s *Interprétation de l’arbre de la Cabale* (1625). Daniel Stolzenberg has shown that Kircher’s work is a learned but confessional Christian reconstruction.

The accurate formulation is:

> The Golden Dawn adopted the Kircher-family path topology and combined it with other systems.

It did not adopt every feature of Kircher’s theology or derive all its correspondences from that one plate.

---

## 6. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* was published in two major stages, Sulzbach 1677–78 and Frankfurt 1684.

It translated and excerpted important Jewish kabbalistic materials, including Zoharic and Lurianic texts, into Latin while placing them within a Christian editorial frame.

### Hod in Latin

**[PT]** The comparative apparatus gives:

> “Hod, id est, laus, honor, gloria.”

“Hod, that is, praise, honor, glory.”

It further associates Hod with:

- Elohim Tzeva’ot;
- the left foot or column;
- a cherub;
- Aaron;
- grinding millstones;
- Michael;
- Mercury;
- transmission to archangels.

This is one of the clearest sources for the later Aaron–Michael–Mercury package.

### Historical effect

Rosenroth became a major conduit between Jewish Kabbalah and European occultism. S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* translated portions of Rosenroth, not the complete Zohar or complete Lurianic corpus. Consequently, many English occultists encountered Jewish texts already selected, Latinized, Christianized, and rearranged.

---

## 7. Lévi and the Tarot

Éliphas Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* appeared in 1854–56.

**[HO/MI]** Lévi argued that the twenty-two Tarot trumps correspond to the twenty-two Hebrew letters. This was not a recovered ancient Jewish teaching. Tarot arose in fifteenth-century European card culture; no medieval Jewish source connects it to the *Sefer Yetzirah*.

Lévi’s sequencing is not identical with the Golden Dawn’s:

- Lévi begins the series with the Magician and Aleph;
- the Fool is placed anomalously near the end;
- the Golden Dawn instead assigns the Fool to Aleph and the Magician to Beth.

Papus popularized related constructions in French occultism, sometimes treating Tarot as an encoded “book” of ancient wisdom. The historical antiquity claimed for this system is unsupported.

---

## 8. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

The Golden Dawn was founded in 1888 by William Wynn Westcott, S. L. MacGregor Mathers, and William Robert Woodman.

### Hod and the grade system

**[PT/HO]** Hod is the station of **Practicus 3°=8□**. The grade number means the aspirant stands in the third initiated grade corresponding to the eighth sefirah.

The surviving Cipher Manuscript already places Practicus in Hod, although its divine-name line may reflect a transcription or cipher inconsistency. The developed ritual opens under **Elohim Tzeva’ot**.

### Water and Mercury

The Practicus grade combines:

- Hod/Mercury as its sefirah and planetary symbolism;
- Water as the grade’s elemental initiation.

This is not necessarily a contradiction. The grade traverses the elemental sequence while being stationed on a planetary sefirah.

### Paths introduced in the ritual

The Practicus ritual particularly presents the paths rising into Hod from below:

- Shin from Malkhut;
- Resh from Yesod.

The initiate has already encountered or will subsequently study the other paths adjoining the sphere.

### The four worlds and colors

| World | Golden Dawn scale | Hod color |
|---|---|---|
| Atziluth | King Scale | Violet-purple |
| Briah | Queen Scale | Orange |
| Yetzirah | Prince/Emperor Scale | Red-russet |
| Assiah | Princess/Empress Scale | Yellow-brown, flecked white |

Dion Fortune prints a darker Assiah variant, often “yellowish black, flecked with white.” Manuscripts, paint recipes, and printed editions do not always reproduce the same hue.

### The four Eights in *Book T*

| Suit | Golden Dawn title | Astrological decan |
|---|---|---|
| Eight of Wands | Lord of Swiftness | Mercury in Sagittarius, first decan |
| Eight of Cups | Lord of Abandoned Success | Saturn in Pisces, first decan |
| Eight of Swords | Lord of Shortened Force | Jupiter in Gemini, first decan |
| Eight of Pentacles | Lord of Prudence | Sun in Virgo, first decan |

Crowley later renamed or nuanced some titles, most notably treating the Eight of Swords as “Interference.”

These cards combine Hod’s number eight with the elemental suit and decan ruler; they are not four independent portraits of the Jewish sefirah.

### Names, versicles, and the apron

**[HO]** Golden Dawn and Crowley tables give Hod:

- names;
- versicles;
- the apron.

“Names” signify formulas of definition, classification, and invocation. “Versicles” signify patterned sacred speech. The apron is a craft and Masonic emblem, both protective and connected with disciplined work at the lower center of the body.

No evidence was found for this triad in Gikatilla, the Zohar, or Cordovero.

### Hod’s number

The theosophical or “mystic” sum of eight is:

1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8 = **36**.

For the Mercury magic square:

- order: 8×8;
- number of cells: 64;
- each row/column: 260;
- total: 2080;
- intelligence: Tiriel, whose Hebrew value is conventionally made 260;
- spirit: Taphthartharath, conventionally 2080.

These belong to planetary Mercury magic, not to medieval Jewish Hod doctrine.

### *Liber O*

Crowley’s Mercury-working instructions use:

- a Mercurial god-form such as Thoth;
- Elohim Tzeva’ot among the divine names;
- Raphael as planetary archangel;
- Benei Elohim;
- Tiriel;
- Taphthartharath;
- Mercurial colors and implements.

This confirms rather than disproves the Michael/Raphael distinction: Crowley’s Sephirotic table says Michael; his planetary Mercury operation says Raphael.

### *Liber 231*

*Liber 231* consists of sigils and brief verses for twenty-two path-genii. It is not a chapter-by-chapter treatment of the ten sefirot and supplies no independent ancient Hod doctrine.

Its genii later became source material for Kenneth Grant’s Nightside tunnels.

### A∴A∴ grade

The A∴A∴ retained **Practicus 3°=8□**. In *One Star in Sight* and related grade papers, the Practicus is expected to complete intellectual training, particularly in the Qabalah.

This is a twentieth-century Thelemic curriculum, not a Jewish initiatory grade.

## 10. Dion Fortune, 1935

Fortune’s *The Mystical Qabalah* was first published in 1935. Chapter XXIII is devoted to Hod.

A full verbatim reproduction cannot be supplied, but the chapter’s complete argument can be reconstructed as follows.

### Fortune’s tabulation

She gives:

- title: Hod, Glory;
- spelling: Heh–Vav–Daleth;
- magical image: hermaphrodite;
- location: foot of Severity;
- Yetziratic title: Perfect Intelligence;
- divine name: Elohim Tzeva’ot;
- archangel: Michael;
- order: Benei Elohim;
- mundane chakra: Kokhav, Mercury;
- spiritual experience: Vision of Splendour;
- virtue: truthfulness;
- vice: falsehood and dishonesty;
- body: loins and legs;
- symbols: names, versicles, apron;
- Tarot: four Eights;
- four color scales.

### Her interpretation

**[HO]** Fortune understands the lower triad as a magical and psychological mechanism:

- Netzach supplies force, desire, image, and affect;
- Hod supplies form, naming, concept, formula, and organization;
- Yesod combines these into an operative astral image;
- Malkhut manifests the result.

Hod is therefore not “pure reason.” It is the sphere in which force is given intelligible form.

Magic, in Fortune’s account, depends on:

1. understanding the form or name of a force;
2. building a symbolic vehicle appropriate to it;
3. charging that form through emotion and imagination;
4. balancing Hod’s formal intelligence with Netzach’s living power.

She warns that Hod without Netzach produces sterile intellectualism, while Netzach without Hod produces diffuse emotionality and superstition.

### The hermaphrodite

The image signifies the joining of force and form and Mercury’s ambiguous or mediating sexuality. It is not a claim about Jewish descriptions of Hod’s angel.

### Michael and the serpent

Fortune interprets Michael’s conquest of the serpent as disciplined consciousness mastering disordered instinct. This is Christian-Hermetic symbolism layered over the Hod table.

### Truth and deception

Mercury governs both communication and trickery. Hence:

- virtue: truthfulness, accurate formulation, intellectual honesty;
- vice: falsehood, sophistry, rationalization, manipulative speech.

### Names and versicles

Fortune treats divine names as compressed philosophical and magical formulae. Versicles discipline consciousness through patterned sacred language. The apron signifies skilled, ordered work and the protective regulation of generative force.

### Historical evaluation

**[MI]** Fortune’s chapter is a powerful synthesis of Golden Dawn symbolism, contemporary psychology, ceremonial magic, and Christian esotericism. It is not a neutral exposition of the Zohar. Some of its psychology and cultural generalizations reflect the 1930s and should not be accepted uncritically.

## 11. Regardie, Case, and Knight

### Israel Regardie

*A Garden of Pomegranates* appeared in 1932; later editions revised it. Regardie presents Hod as Mercury, intellect, language, magic, and formal study.

In his initiatory account, the Practicus must complete intellectual training in philosophy, metaphysics, and particularly Qabalah; some versions add instruction in evocation and construction of the magical Cup.

Regardie’s importance is primarily transmissive: he published and systematized Golden Dawn teachings for a public readership.

### Paul Foster Case

Case participated briefly in an American Golden Dawn successor and founded the Builders of the Adytum.

His BOTA system retained the Tree, Hebrew letters, Tarot, color, and meditative correspondences but revised details. Case treats Hod as the field of intellect and planning and Mercury as the transformer or mediator. The Magician’s gesture—one hand above, one below—expresses the conversion of intention into structured manifestation.

Case’s system is not identical to the Golden Dawn’s in every path assignment, color detail, or Tarot doctrine.

### Gareth Knight

*A Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* first appeared in two volumes in 1965.

Knight characterizes Hod as the sphere of the concrete mind: language, classification, symbols, science, esoteric philosophy, and formal magic. True forms allow human consciousness to approach realities that are themselves formless.

Knight strongly emphasizes that Hod must work with Netzach. Concepts without living intuition become sterile; intuition without adequate conceptual form cannot be communicated or reliably used.

### Recensional qualifications

- **[PT]** The Mother/Double/Simple classes originate in *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- **[D]** Planetary assignments among the seven Doubles vary by recension.
- **[MI]** The trump assignments are modern occult constructions.
- **[D]** The placement of the letters on these particular graphical edges is not given by the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### Kircher versus Ari/Gra topology

| Diagram family | Hod’s connections |
|---|---|
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Yesod, **Malkhut** |
| Common Ari-type | Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Yesod; normally no direct Hod–Malkhut |
| Common Gra-type | Same four neighboring nodes in the lower structure; letters redistributed according to geometrical classes |

The Ari/Gra-type trees preserve twenty-two channels by placing two additional crossed channels higher on the Tree. Manuscripts and printed diagrams vary, so these are diagram families, not single uncontested originals.

### “Yetziratic” path texts

The traditional intelligence-titles used by Westcott and the Golden Dawn are:

- 23rd: Stable Intelligence;
- 26th: Renovating Intelligence;
- 27th: Exciting Intelligence;
- 30th: Collecting Intelligence;
- 31st: Perpetual Intelligence.

These belong to the later *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom* list, not to the ancient strata of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### Tzaddi and Heh

The Golden Dawn assigns:

- Heh → Aries → Emperor;
- Tzaddi → Aquarius → Star.

*The Book of the Law* I:57 states that Tzaddi is “not the Star.” Crowley eventually assigned:

- Tzaddi → Emperor;
- Heh → Star.

**[D/HO]** Crowley’s explanations leave interpretive questions about whether the cards alone, the zodiacal bundles, or the full path-correspondences exchange places. The issue does not directly change any of the five paths touching Hod, but it affects the integrity of the wider Tree and its Tarot sequence.

### Pathworking

Golden Dawn-derived pathworking normally entails:

1. ritual purification and banishing;
2. visualization of the appropriate letter and color;
3. use of divine and angelic names;
4. entry through the trump’s symbolic landscape;
5. testing encountered figures by names, signs, or symbols;
6. recording the vision;
7. interpretation against the established correspondences.

**[HO/MI]** This is a nineteenth- and twentieth-century magical technique. It should not be projected back into Gikatilla’s prayer instructions or Vital’s Lurianic contemplations.

## The Qliphah and Nightside tunnels

## 1. Jewish qelippot

The Hebrew **קְלִפּוֹת, qelippot**, means husks, shells, or peels.

**[JT]** Jewish Kabbalah uses the term for forces of concealment, impurity, or obstructed vitality. In Lurianic teaching, qelippot are bound up with the shattering of vessels and the captivity of sparks.

This does not automatically entail a geometrically mirrored Tree with exactly ten named anti-sefirot and one demon king assigned to each.

## 2. The Hermetic mirrored hierarchy

The familiar list is:

| Sefirah | Qliphah |
|---:|---|
| 1 Keter | Thaumiel |
| 2 Chokhmah | Ghagiel |
| 3 Binah | Satariel |
| 4 Chesed | Gha’agsheblah |
| 5 Gevurah | Golachab |
| 6 Tiferet | Thagirion |
| 7 Netzach | A’arab Zaraq |
| 8 Hod | **Samael** |
| 9 Yesod | Gamaliel |
| 10 Malkhut | Lilith/Nahemoth, depending table |

Spellings vary because the Hebrew or Aramaic names passed through Latin, German, French, and English transliterations.

**[SR/D]** Some names derive from Zoharic or Tikkunei Zohar discussions of impurity. Knorr von Rosenroth’s *De decem numerationibus impuris* in *Kabbala Denudata* was a major conduit. The completely regular mirror-table and its demon rulers are nevertheless products of Christian Cabalist and Golden Dawn systematization.

## 3. Samael as Hod’s qliphah

The Golden Dawn paper on the Qliphoth places Hod in the sixth infernal palace and assigns **Samael**, described as “Deceivers” or “Jugglers,” imagined as dull, demon-headed doglike beings.

This represents Hod’s functions inverted:

| Hod | Samaelic distortion |
|---|---|
| Accurate naming | Mislabeling |
| Truthful communication | Lying |
| Analysis | Rationalization |
| Symbolic mediation | Manipulation |
| Adaptable intelligence | Evasive cleverness |
| Ritual order | Empty formalism |
| Classification | Sophistry that mistakes categories for reality |

The traditional Jewish Samael is older and more complex—accuser, angel of death, adversarial power, and in some later traditions partner of Lilith. The narrow identification “Samael = shell of Hod” is specifically Hermetic.

## 4. Adramelech

The Golden Dawn assigns Hod’s qliphah the ruler **Adramelech**.

Adramelech originates in 2 Kings 17:31 as a deity worshiped by the Sepharvites. Later Christian demonology transformed the name into a demon and supplied varying offices and forms. The Golden Dawn then placed that demon into its qliphic Tree.

Its own qliphic paper qualifies the ruler list as an opinion and admits that the attributions cannot be sharply restricted. Adramelech’s rulership is therefore a late occult convention, not a Zoharic teaching.

## 5. Kenneth Grant’s tunnels

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* appeared in 1977. It reinterprets the twenty-two sigils of Crowley’s *Liber 231* as tunnels joining the qliphic spheres.

On the nightside reflection of the Kircher Tree, five tunnels touch Samael/Hod:

| Dayside path | Grantian tunnel | Nightside connection |
|---|---|---|
| Mem | Malkunofat | Golachab–Samael |
| Ayin | A’ano’nin | Thagirion–Samael |
| Peh | Parfaxitas | A’arab Zaraq–Samael |
| Resh | Raflifu | Samael–Gamaliel |
| Shin | Shalicu | Samael–Nahemoth |

**[MI]** The names, spellings, and precise experiences differ among editions and later practitioners. The tunnel system is Typhonian occultism, not Jewish Kabbalah and not an original teaching of *Liber 231* itself.

### Shem ha-Mephorash quinances

Golden Dawn-derived tables place two five-degree Shem angels within each decan. They are quinance rulers, not Goetic demons and not direct Hod correspondences.

| Eight | Common Golden Dawn spellings |
|---|---|
| Wands | Nithahiah; Haaiah |
| Cups | Vavaliah; Yelahiah |
| Swords | Umabel; Iah-hel/Yahhel |

Spellings differ because the Hebrew names are reconstructed and transliterated in multiple systems. Goetic day/night demons are a separate decanic table and should not be called the “angels of Hod.”

## Diagrams and visual culture

1. **Late-thirteenth-century manuscripts:** early kabbalistic diagrams, including “Tree of Wisdom” forms.
2. **Fourteenth-century ilanot:** increasingly arboreal diagrams of divine structure.
3. **1516 *Portae Lucis*:** probably the first printed sefirah image.
4. **1592 Cracow *Pardes Rimmonim*:** first printed sefirah diagram in a Hebrew book.
5. **1652 Kircher plate:** influential Latin-Christian Tree with the Hermetic path topology.
6. **Golden Dawn colored Trees:** paths and spheres differentiated in four world-scales.
7. **Waite–Smith Tarot:** Arthur Edward Waite and Pamela Colman Smith rendered Golden Dawn-influenced symbolic material in pictorial form.
8. **Thoth Tarot:** Crowley and Frieda Harris supplied a specifically Thelemic visual revision.
9. **BOTA Tarot:** Paul Foster Case and Jessie Burns Parke produced another initiatory Tarot recension.
10. **Nightside art:** Kenneth and Steffi Grant helped create a distinct Typhonian visual culture around qliphic paths.

## Poets and novelists

W. B. Yeats, Arthur Machen, Algernon Blackwood, and Florence Farr belonged to or worked close to the Golden Dawn environment. Their writings participate in the Order’s larger magical-symbolic world, although it would be misleading to call each an author of an independent Hod doctrine.

Later fiction frequently borrows the Tree as a structure of chapters, worlds, characters, or psychic ascent. Unless an author explicitly identifies Hod, resemblance to intellect, Mercury, or the number eight is not sufficient evidence of deliberate use.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Dating the *Sefer Yetzirah*

- **Traditional:** attributed to Abraham or ancient sages.
- **Older scholarship:** commonly late antique.
- **Hayman’s evidentiary caution:** securely visible through early-tenth-century recensions and commentaries.
- **Current position:** composition and redaction may span periods; an exact date is unproved.

Hod cannot be dated to the putative earliest layer because it is not named there.

## 2. Was Hod already in the *Bahir*?

- A quotation attributed to the *Bahir* by Todros Abulafia names Hod.
- Scholem questioned its authenticity.
- The standard text does not make Hod a securely recurring technical term.
- Recent reassessment makes confident reconstruction of the *Bahir*’s stages even more difficult.

The earliest secure named Hod belongs to the thirteenth-century consolidation of the sefirotic system, but its exact first manuscript occurrence remains open.

## 3. Zohar authorship

- **Traditional:** revelation by Shimon bar Yochai and companions.
- **Scholem:** principal composition by Moses de León in late-thirteenth-century Castile.
- **Recent scholarship:** de León remains central, but the corpus shows multiple strata, genres, and possibly contributors.

Both the traditional attribution and the scholarly literary history should be identified when citing Hod material.

## 4. Christian appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Ricius, Kircher, and Rosenroth preserved, translated, and disseminated Jewish materials. They also:

- detached them from Jewish ritual and interpretive communities;
- used them to prove Christian dogmas;
- rearranged them within Neoplatonic, astrological, and angelological systems;
- often relied upon Jewish converts operating under Christian institutions;
- helped create “Cabala” as a universal Christian wisdom.

“Transmission” and “appropriation” are not mutually exclusive descriptions.

## 5. Is the Kircher Tree the Jewish Tree?

No.

- Jewish manuscripts contain multiple diagrams and channel systems.
- Kircher published an influential Christian synthesis.
- Ari/Gra-type diagrams usually omit direct Hod–Malkhut and Netzach–Malkhut paths.
- Golden Dawn practice fixes the Kircher topology because its letters, Tarot, astrology, ritual grades, and pathworkings depend upon it.

The dispute is not resolved by declaring one picture “the original Tree.”

## 6. Tarot attributions

No evidence connects the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah* with Tarot.

The historical sequence is:

1. fifteenth-century Tarot card culture;
2. eighteenth-century Egyptianizing speculation;
3. Lévi’s systematic letters/trumps;
4. Golden Dawn reassignment;
5. Crowley’s Tzaddi/Heh revision;
6. later BOTA and independent variants.

These are occult constructions, not recoveries of an ancient Hebrew Tarot.

## 7. Michael versus Raphael

The evidence supports a layered resolution:

- Michael: archangel of Hod in the Sephirotic hierarchy.
- Raphael: archangel of Mercury in planetary ritual.
- Benei Elohim: angelic order in Hod’s hierarchical world.
- Tiriel and Taphthartharath: Mercury’s planetary intelligence and spirit.

A chart listing only one name without its hierarchy is incomplete.

## 8. Aaron versus Moses

The conventional Aaron/Hod and Moses/Netzach assignment is well established, but Zoharic reversals are genuine. Later kabbalists resolve them through interinclusion and crossed channels. “Aaron only” is a useful table entry, not the entire textual history.

## 9. Is Hod “the intellect”?

- Jewish sources emphasize acknowledgment, prophecy, articulated transmission, ritual, judgment, and the legs/supports.
- Renaissance sources add eloquence and Mercury.
- Golden Dawn writers make Hod formal intellect.
- Fortune and Knight make it concrete mind and symbolic form.

Thus “Hod is intellect” is principally a Hermetic interpretation with some analogical Jewish antecedents.

## 10. Da’at

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists on “ten and not nine, ten and not eleven.”

Cordovero explicitly defends the count of ten and warns against removing Keter or Malkhut. Other kabbalistic systems treat Da’at as:

- the mediation or union of Chokhmah and Binah;
- an internal state of the partzuf rather than an independent emanation;
- counted when Keter is not counted;
- displayed diagrammatically while not increasing the essential number beyond ten.

The popular occult “eleventh sefirah in the Abyss” is only one presentation. Da’at’s appearance does not establish an original eleven-sefirah Tree.

## 11. Qliphic genealogy

Jewish Kabbalah contains qelippot, named adverse powers, Samael, Lilith, and the “other side.” Nevertheless, the exact ten-row mirror with demon rulers is not found in one simple ancient source. Rosenroth and the Golden Dawn are critical stages in its present form.

## 12. Side and orientation

Hod is normally the left leg of the divine/human figure. A diagram viewed from the front may place it on the viewer’s right unless the illustrator adopts the figure’s own perspective. Modern diagrams vary. This geometrical issue has generated false claims that sources reverse Hod and Netzach.

## Open questions

1. What is the earliest securely datable manuscript occurrence of הוד as the technical name of the eighth sefirah?
2. Does Todros Abulafia preserve a lost *Bahir* reading or a later attribution to the *Bahir*?
3. How exactly did the *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom* develop before its Latin and Westcott forms?
4. Was Reuchlin’s **consessio laudis** intentional, or a printing error for **confessio laudis**?
5. Which precise manuscripts or intermediaries supplied each feature of Kircher’s channel arrangement?
6. How much of the qliphic tenfold list derives directly from Zoharic sources, and how much from Rosenroth’s editorial harmonization?
7. When did the now-standard 7×7 Omer meditation first appear in its complete daily printed form?
8. How were early colored Jewish ilanot actually used—study aid, contemplative device, mnemonic tool, ritual object, or several of these?
9. How much of the Michael–Benei Elohim–Mercury hierarchy Agrippa compiled from Jewish sources, and how much came from Christian angelology and astrological magic?
10. Which variants of the “Ari Tree” derive from Vitalian manuscripts, and which were imposed by later editors or illustrators?
11. Did the Golden Dawn consciously distinguish every time between Michael as Sephirotic archangel and Raphael as planetary archangel, or was the theoretical distinction sometimes blurred in practice?
12. How much of Crowley’s revised *777* represents Golden Dawn manuscript teaching, and how much is his later comparative editorial work?

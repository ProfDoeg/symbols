# The Path of Zayin: Research Dossier

## Evidentiary key

- **[TEXT]** Directly documented in the cited primary text, manuscript, early printing, or order paper.
- **[TRADITION]** A claim made by Jewish religious tradition about a text, figure, or practice.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]** A modern historical, philological, or textual reconstruction.
- **[HERMETIC]** An attribution internal to Christian Cabala, Hermetic Qabalah, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, B.O.T.A., or a related occult school.
- **[DISPUTED]** A claim for which sources or recensions disagree.
- **[MODERN]** A modern synthesis, reconstruction, or invention without demonstrable ancient or medieval authority.
- **[ABSENCE]** A requested correspondence that does not occur in the relevant source.

A central distinction governs the dossier:

> **[TEXT/SCHOLARSHIP]** Jewish sources know the letter Zayin, the thirty-two *netivot*, the sefirot, and many letter correspondences. They do **not** identify a Binah–Tiferet channel called “the Path of Zayin.” That composite results from placing the seventh Hebrew letter on the seventeenth line of the Kircher-derived occult Tree and then adding Golden Dawn astrology and Tarot.

# The name

## Hebrew, meaning, and etymology

**[TEXT]** The name is **זַיִן**, *zayin*. The letter belongs to a Semitic series represented by Phoenician 𐤆, Paleo-Hebrew 𐤆, Imperial Aramaic 𐡆, Syriac ܙ, Hebrew ז, and Arabic ز. Its consonantal value is /z/. The seventh-place numerical value appears in Jewish alphabetic numeration and later gematria.

**[TEXT]** In rabbinic and medieval Hebrew, **זַיִן** and especially **כְּלֵי זַיִן**, *kelei zayin*, mean “weaponry” or “implements of war.” This is the meaning exploited by the Zohar when it connects the letter with sword and spear. Ernest Klein’s dictionary accordingly glosses the letter name through “weapon,” while recognizing its Semitic antiquity. See [Klein Dictionary, זַיִן](https://www.sefaria.org/Klein_Dictionary%2C_%D7%96%D6%B7%D6%BD%D7%99%D6%B4%D7%9F_%E1%B4%B5.1?lang=bi).

**[DISPUTED/SCHOLARSHIP]** “Zayin originally meant sword” is less secure than “*zayin* meant weapon in later Hebrew.” The original acrophonic object represented by the Proto-Sinaitic sign is uncertain. Proposals include a weapon, fetter, or agricultural implement; another reconstruction compares Greek *zeta* and proposes an earlier name related to *zayit*, “olive.” The evidence does not permit the familiar occult claim that a clearly identifiable Proto-Sinaitic sword picture evolved continuously into ז.

### Palaeographic development

**[SCHOLARSHIP]**

1. A proposed Proto-Sinaitic/Proto-Canaanite ancestor is visually and semantically uncertain.
2. Phoenician 𐤆 usually has a vertical stem crossed by horizontal strokes.
3. Paleo-Hebrew retains the Northwest Semitic form family.
4. Imperial Aramaic develops a more cursive form.
5. Jewish “square” or Assyrian script develops from Aramaic during the Second Temple period.
6. The modern printed ז has a short roof and descending vertical stroke.
7. Torah scribal forms may add three ornamental strokes, *tagin*.

The University of Chicago’s palaeographic materials and the Mnamon writing-system database document this development without requiring the “sword pictogram” hypothesis: [Early Hebrew scripts](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/study_writng.pdf), [Mnamon—Hebrew writing](https://mnamon.sns.it/index.php?id=8&lang=en&page=Scrittura).

**[TRADITION]** Later letter mysticism interprets the square form as a **Vav crowned by a Yod**, a vertical instrument surmounted by a crown, or the image of a weapon. These are symbolic readings of the mature script, not historical explanations of its origin.

## Scribal crowns and rabbinic form

**[TEXT]** In *Menachot* 29b, Zayin belongs to the mnemonic **שעטנ״ז ג״ץ**, the seven letters given three *tagin*. The same passage recounts Moses finding God tying crowns to the Torah’s letters and being shown Rabbi Akiva’s future derivations from them. The short formulation is:

> **שבעה אותיות צריכות שלשה זיונין** — “Seven letters require three crownlets.”

See [Menachot 29b](https://www.sefaria.org/Menachot.29b?with=Talmud).

**[TEXT]** *Shabbat* 104a also presupposes Zayin’s graphic form: removing the roof of a Chet can leave two Zayins. This is halakhic evidence about recognizable letter shape, not an occult path doctrine. See [Shabbat 104a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104).

## Gematria

**[TEXT]**

- ז = 7.
- זין = 7 + 10 + 50 = **67**.
- In systems assigning elevated values to final letters, זי״ן may be reckoned 7 + 10 + 700 = **717**.

**[HERMETIC]** Crowley and Kenneth Grant exploit both 67 and 717. Grant observes that 717 is one less than 718, Crowley’s number for the Stele of Revealing. This is Typhonian numerology, not a classical Jewish doctrine about Zayin.

**[ABSENCE]** Zayin is not present in יהוה, אהיה, אדני, שדי, or אלהים. It appears in innumerable other sacred words and names, including **ישראל**, but no classical source establishes “the divine name of Path Zayin.”

## Biblical foundations—and their limits

The biblical passages requested belong to the vocabulary from which later sefirotic interpretation was built. None names Zayin as a Binah–Tiferet path.

### 1 Chronicles 29:11

**[TEXT]**

> **לְךָ יְהוָה הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד ... לְךָ יְהוָה הַמַּמְלָכָה**

“Yours, YHWH, are greatness, might, beauty, victory, and majesty … yours is kingship.” See [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11).

**[TRADITION]** Kabbalists read the verse as naming the seven lower sefirot: Gedullah/Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, the connective **כי כל** as Yesod in some Zoharic readings, and Malkhut. It supplies the word **Tiferet**, one endpoint of Kircher’s path, but does not identify Zayin with it.

### Proverbs 3:19

**[TEXT]**

> **יְהוָה בְּחָכְמָה יָסַד־אָרֶץ כּוֹנֵן שָׁמַיִם בִּתְבוּנָה**

“YHWH founded earth by wisdom; by understanding He established heaven.” See [Proverbs 3:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19).

**[TRADITION]** The verse became a key proof text for Chokhmah and Binah as creative principles. It does not locate an alphabetic path between Binah and Tiferet.

### Job 28

**[TEXT]** Job 28 repeatedly asks where wisdom and understanding may be found, finally declaring that God understands wisdom’s way:

> **אֱלֹהִים הֵבִין דַּרְכָּהּ וְהוּא יָדַע אֶת־מְקוֹמָהּ**

See [Job 28:23](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28.23).

**[TRADITION/HERMETIC]** Its words for way, place, wisdom, darkness, and hidden treasure furnished later imagery for *netivot*. Westcott’s appended “Paths” text cites Job 38:9 for Path 15, not Job 28 as a Zayin proof text.

### Isaiah

**[TEXT]** The opening of *Sefer Yetzirah* incorporates biblical epithets including **רם ונשא**, “high and exalted,” and **שוכן עד וקדוש שמו**, “inhabiting eternity, whose name is holy,” from [Isaiah 57:15](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.57.15). Isaiah 11:2 juxtaposes the spirit of wisdom and understanding.

**[ABSENCE]** No verse in Isaiah establishes Zayin = Gemini = Lovers = Binah–Tiferet.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date, authorship, and recensions

**[TRADITION]** Medieval manuscripts and commentators ascribe *Sefer Yetzirah* to Abraham; some traditions place its transmission through an angel or through patriarchal revelation.

**[SCHOLARSHIP/DISPUTED]** Its date remains unresolved. Proposals range from Roman antiquity to the early Islamic period. Peter Hayman’s critical edition treats the work as first securely visible in the early tenth century and reconstructs fluid Saadyan, Short, and Long textual families. Recent scholarship often favors formation between roughly the seventh and ninth centuries, although earlier materials may underlie it. See Peter Hayman, *Sefer Yesira: Edition, Translation and Text-Critical Commentary* (Mohr Siebeck, 2004), publisher’s description at [Mohr Siebeck](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/), and the manuscript-oriented overview at [Hekhal](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-yetzirah-opening/).

“Saadia,” “Ari,” and “Gra” are not three autograph editions by those persons:

- **Saadia recension:** text represented by Saadia Gaon’s Arabic commentary, written around 931.
- **Ari recension:** a medieval text selected or corrected in the Safed environment and associated with Isaac Luria.
- **Gra recension:** the version edited and interpreted in the school of Elijah of Vilna; printed editions are later than the Gra.
- **Short and Long recensions:** modern textual categories based on manuscript families.

### The thirty-two paths

**[TEXT]** The opening reads:

> **בשלשים ושתים נתיבות פליאות חכמה חקק יה יהוה צבאות**

“With thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom, Yah, YHWH Tzevaot, engraved …”

The book then defines its basic inventory as:

> **עשר ספירות בלימה ועשרים ושתים אותיות יסוד**

“Ten sefirot belimah and twenty-two foundation letters.”

The letters divide into:

- Three mothers: א מ ש
- Seven doubles: ב ג ד כ פ ר ת
- Twelve simples: **ה ו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק**

See [Sefer Yetzirah, Gra version](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.5).

**[TEXT/SCHOLARSHIP]** In the text itself, the thirty-two are an inventory of ten sefirot plus twenty-two letters. *Sefer Yetzirah* does **not** draw a Tree, name 22 edges between ten nodes, or say “Zayin joins Binah and Tiferet.” The diagrammatic conversion of the letters into connecting lines is medieval and varies from school to school.

### Zayin as a simple letter

The best-known Gra/Kaplan text states:

> **המליך אות ז׳ בהלוך ... וצר בהם תאומים בעולם וסיון בשנה ורגל שמאל בנפש**

“He made Zayin king over motion … and formed Gemini, Sivan, and the left foot.” See [Sefer Yetzirah 5](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.5).

### Recensional comparison

| Recension or printed tradition | Cosmic sign | Month | Faculty/action | Body member | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| Saadia’s text | Gemini | Sivan | In one Saadyan sequence, smell | Spleen | [TEXT/DISPUTED] Manuscripts and translations require care; see Saadia’s [Arabic commentary translation](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/judaism/saadia.pdf) |
| Common Short/Long sequence | Gemini | Sivan | Sequence varies by witness | One of the “directors” or organs | [TEXT/DISPUTED] No single eclectic internet text should be treated as the autograph |
| Gra/Kaplan printed version | Gemini | Sivan | **Motion/walking**, הלוך | **Left foot** | [TEXT] [Sefaria Gra 5:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.5) |
| Ari-associated printed version | Gemini | Sivan | Commonly motion/walking | Frequently printed as right foot | [TRADITION/DISPUTED] The label “Ari version” masks editorial transmission |
| Some Gra commentary arrangements | Letter associations are reassigned during commentary | — | Sight or motion can shift to adjacent letters | Right hand/left foot variants occur | [DISPUTED] Evidence of textual correction and harmonization |

**Finding:** Gemini and Sivan are comparatively stable for Zayin in the standard ordered zodiac/month series. Faculty and bodily member are much less stable.

### Twelve diagonal boundaries

**[TEXT]** *Sefer Yetzirah* calls the simples the foundation of the **twelve diagonal boundaries**, גבולי אלכסון. These are spatial-cosmological limits—east-upper, east-north, and so forth—not a list of twenty-two drawn links between later named sefirot.

**[DISPUTED]** Later diagrammers inferred that the three mothers should occupy horizontal paths, seven doubles vertical paths, and twelve simples diagonal paths. That rule produces Ari and Gra trees unlike Kircher’s. Kircher’s arrangement visibly violates the classification rule in several places because it assigns the alphabet consecutively according to his own path order.

### Tribe

**[ABSENCE]** The core *Sefer Yetzirah* gives signs, months, and bodily “directors,” but not Israelite tribes.

**[TRADITION]** Later calendars combine the twelve months and twelve tribes. A widespread arrangement associates Sivan/Zayin with **Zebulun**, often through encampment order or birth order. Crowley’s *777*, however, prints **Manasseh** for row 17. Neither should be projected into the early text.

## 2. The “Seventeenth Path” text is not the core *Sefer Yetzirah*

The phrase “Disposing Intelligence” comes from a separate medieval list commonly printed with *Sefer Yetzirah*.

**[TEXT]** The Hebrew reads:

> **נתיב י״ז נקרא שכל ההרגש והוא מוכן לחסידי האמונה להתלבש ברוח קדושה**

“The seventeenth path is called the intelligence of sensation; it is prepared for the faithful saints to be clothed in holy spirit.”

It continues by calling it **יסוד התפארת במעמד העליונים**, “the foundation of Tiferet/excellence among the supernal standings.” See the Hebrew in [Sefer ha-Peliah, Path 17](https://mobile.tora.ws/html/15019-249.html) and the manuscript/edition text at [Mikdash Melech](https://mikdashmelech.co.il/media/drive-stream?id=1vsu_aEOWj1NR5425KTk_lV8VnsVEQ6Qc).

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** The key noun **הרגש**, *hargesh*, means sensation, feeling, or sensible awareness. Nothing in it literally means “disposing.” The English title arose through Latin transmission.

**[TEXT/HERMETIC]** Kircher prints:

> **Decima septima semita dicitur Intelligentia dispositiua, quæ aptat piis fidelitatem**

“The seventeenth path is called the disposing intelligence, which prepares faithfulness for the pious.”

Kircher continues with clothing in the Holy Spirit and the “foundation of beauty” among higher things. See *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, II.1, around p. 314, [Cabala Hebraeorum](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

**[HERMETIC]** Westcott translates:

> “The Seventeenth Path is the Disposing Intelligence, which provides Faith to the Righteous…”

See W. W. Westcott, *Sepher Yetzirah*, 1893 ed., p. 30, [IAPSOP scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1893__westcott___sepher_yetzirah.pdf).

**[DISPUTED]** The Hebrew title should therefore be distinguished from the Latin/English title:

- Hebrew: *Sekhel ha-hargesh* — sensible, sentient, or affective intelligence.
- Kircher: *Intelligentia dispositiva* — disposing/ordering intelligence.
- Westcott: “Disposing Intelligence.”

**[ABSENCE]** The Hebrew text does not say “Zayin,” “Gemini,” “Lovers,” or “Binah–Tiferet.” Those identifications result from later superimposition.

## 3. *Sefer ha-Bahir*

### Date

**[TRADITION]** The *Bahir* is attributed to the first-century sage Nehunya ben ha-Kanah.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** It first emerges in Provence in the late twelfth century, probably incorporating earlier Jewish mystical materials.

### Zayin passages

**[TEXT]** Sections 81–83 connect Zayin with seven:

> **זיי״ן ... כמנין ימי השבוע**

“Zayin … according to the number of the days of the week.”

The text then relates seven days, seven powers, bodily members, and the ear as a receiver of wisdom. See [*Bahir* §§81–83](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-81-83-targum).

**[TRADITION]** The seventh letter thus evokes Sabbath, the sevenfold structure of time, and the coordination of human microcosm and celestial powers.

**[ABSENCE]** The *Bahir* does not call Zayin “the Lovers,” assign it Gemini, or put it between Binah and Tiferet.

## 4. Talmud, midrash, and alphabet lore

### Letters petitioning to begin creation

**[TEXT]** The famous reverse-alphabet procession occurs in the Zohar’s introduction, not securely in the early recensions of *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*. Zayin petitions:

> **דְּבִי נָטְרִין בְּנָיךְ שַׁבָּת ... זָכוֹר אֶת יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת**

“Through me your children keep Sabbath … ‘Remember the Sabbath day.’”

God refuses because the letter contains:

> **קְרָבָא וְחַרְבָּא דְּשִׁנְנָא וְרוֹמָחָא**

“War, a sharpened sword, and a spear.”

See [Zohar, Introduction 6:34](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar%2C_Introduction.6.34?with=Sulam).

**[DISPUTED]** Modern books often attribute every alphabet-petition motif to *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*. Alphabetic personification is indeed older and widespread, but the verified source for this exact Zayin/Sabbath/weapon scene is the Zoharic introduction.

## 5. The Zohar

### Authorship and date

**[TRADITION]** The Zohar records teachings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yohai and his circle in the second century.

**[SCHOLARSHIP/DISPUTED]** The principal Zoharic corpus appeared in late-thirteenth-century Castile. Gershom Scholem argued for Moses de León as principal author; later scholars have proposed multiple writers, textual strata, and editorial processes while retaining de León as central. Daniel Matt’s Pritzker Edition is based on early manuscripts rather than merely reproducing the first printed editions. See Matt’s textual statement, [“Establishing the Text of the Zohar”](https://supress.sites-pro.stanford.edu/sites/supress/files/media/file/intaram.pdf).

### Zayin’s Zoharic complex

**[TEXT]** In the petition scene, Zayin carries two opposed associations:

- Sabbath through **זכור**, *zakhor*, “remember.”
- Warfare through **כלי זין**, weapons.

**[TRADITION]** Later Zohar commentaries read the square Zayin as Yod over Vav: a feminine crown above the masculine six, or the Nukba crowning Zeir Anpin on Sabbath.

**[IMPORTANT DISTINCTION]** The long commentary on Sefaria under “Sulam” is Yehuda Ashlag’s twentieth-century interpretation. Its technical explanation in terms of the matured Nukba, Zeir Anpin, and weekday warfare must not be quoted as though it were the thirteenth-century Zoharic text.

**[ABSENCE]** The Zohar does not identify a numbered 17th channel from Binah to Tiferet with Gemini and the Lovers.

## 6. Gikatilla and *Sha’arei Orah*

**[TEXT]** Joseph Gikatilla’s *Sha’arei Orah*, written in late-thirteenth-century Castile, organizes ten gates around divine names and sefirot. It is a major source for correspondences among divine names, biblical language, sefirot, and prayer.

**[ABSENCE]** It does not supply a unique divine name, archangel, angelic order, or planet for a “Path of Zayin.” Those categories are principally **sefirah-level** categories in later Christian and Hermetic tables.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** The 1516 Latin *Portae Lucis*, translated by Paulus Ricius, helped transmit Gikatilla to Christian readers. Its celebrated title-page image is one of the earliest printed European depictions of a sefirotic tree, but it is not the Kircher/Golden Dawn 22-path diagram. See the [1516 title page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3APortae_Lucis_1516.jpg) and the [Center for Jewish Art catalogue](https://cja.huji.ac.il/gross/browser.php?id=40577&mode=set).

## 7. Moses Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (1548)

**[TEXT]** Moses Cordovero completed *Pardes Rimmonim* in Safed in 1548. It systematizes competing kabbalistic teachings on the sefirot, divine names, letters, channels, colors, and cosmology. See the Hebrew [*Pardes Rimmonim* contents](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimonim?lang=he&tab=schema).

**[TEXT]** Gate 27 treats letters and their forms. Cordovero records interpretations of Zayin through seven, Sabbath, covenant, Yesod, and the crowned Vav. In one explicitly sexual-symbolic reading, the crown corresponds to the corona of the covenantal organ. This is a medieval/early-modern Jewish body-symbolism of the letter, not a Tarot interpretation. A translated extract appears in [“All Male and Female”](https://cojs.org/all_male_and_female_texts/).

**[TEXT]** Cordovero also comments on the list of thirty-two named intelligences. His discussion of the seventeenth, **שכל ההרגש**, concerns the subtle body or garment in which righteous souls are clothed in Eden and the transmission of Binah’s spirit through the sefirotic structure. The Hebrew is visible in the [*Pardes* scan](https://zkolel.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%93%D7%A1-%D7%A8%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9C%D7%A8%D7%9E%D7%A7.pdf).

**[ABSENCE]** Cordovero discusses:

1. Zayin as a letter;
2. numbered intelligences;
3. channels among sefirot.

He does not combine all three into “Zayin, Path 17, Binah–Tiferet.”

### *Or Ne’erav*

**[TEXT]** *Or Ne’erav*, a shorter guide to Kabbalah, outlines prerequisites, sefirotic relations, and modes of study. See [*Or Ne’erav*](https://www.sefaria.org/Ohr_Ne%27erav%2C_Introduction.25).

**[SCHOLARSHIP/DISPUTED]** Cordoverian diagrams printed or copied with these works do not all have the same number or disposition of connecting channels. This instability is positive evidence against claims of a single ancient “Jewish Tree” identical to the Golden Dawn glyph.

## 8. Luria through Hayyim Vital

**[TRADITION/TEXT]** Isaac Luria taught orally in Safed between roughly 1570 and his death in 1572. Hayyim Vital and other disciples recorded and reorganized the teachings. *Etz Hayyim* is therefore a Vitalian literary construction transmitting Lurianic doctrine, not a book personally written and published by Luria.

Its major structures include:

- **Tzimtzum:** primordial contraction/concealment.
- **Kav:** the line or ray entering the vacated space.
- **Adam Kadmon:** primordial configuration.
- **Four worlds:** Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, Assiah.
- **Partzufim:** developed divine configurations—Arikh Anpin, Abba, Imma, Zeir Anpin, Nukva.
- **Shevirat ha-kelim:** shattering of vessels.
- **Nitzotzot:** scattered sparks.
- **Tikkun:** restoration through reconfiguration and human religious action.

A Hebrew edition begins at [*Sefer Etz Chaim*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.5).

**[ABSENCE]** Searching *Etz Hayyim* does not produce a Golden Dawn-style exposition of “Path 17, Zayin, Gemini, the Lovers.” Individual Hebrew letters appear in technical discussions of partzufic forms and divine names, but that is a different operation.

### The Ari Tree

**[TRADITION/DISPUTED]** Diagrams conventionally called “the Ari Tree” enforce the *Sefer Yetzirah* classification:

- three mother letters on horizontal paths;
- seven doubles on vertical paths;
- twelve simples on diagonals;
- Malkhut often linked only through Yesod;
- upper diagonals Chokhmah–Gevurah and Binah–Chesed replace the Hermetic lower diagonals Hod–Malkhut and Netzach–Malkhut.

In a commonly reproduced Ari assignment, **Zayin joins Chokhmah and Gevurah**, not Binah and Tiferet. A comparative table is preserved in the [Arcane Archive](https://arcane-archive.org/tarot/tzaddi-is-not-the-star-1.php).

**[CAUTION]** Calling this “Luria’s exact autograph Tree” is too strong. The diagrams are products of a transmitted ilan tradition, editorial reconstruction, and later print.

## 9. The Gra Tree

**[TRADITION]** Elijah ben Solomon Zalman, the Vilna Gaon or Gra (1720–1797), wrote a major commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah*. His school’s arrangement places Tiferet in an elevated central position and likewise preserves the mother/horizontal, double/vertical, simple/diagonal classification.

**[DISPUTED]** In the commonly reproduced comparison, Zayin joins **Chokhmah–Tiferet**; Chet joins Binah–Tiferet. See the Ari/Gra table in the [Arcane Archive](https://arcane-archive.org/tarot/tzaddi-is-not-the-star-1.php).

**[CAUTION]** Modern diagrams labeled “Gra Tree” are not always identical. Some internet diagrams simply relabel the Kircher Tree and are demonstrably not Gra arrangements. The secure conclusion is that the Gra system is structurally different from Kircher’s and does not provide Jewish authority for Zayin = Binah–Tiferet.

### Comparative topology

| System | Zayin connects | Organizing principle |
|---|---|---|
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | **Binah–Tiferet** | Alphabet assigned successively to Kircher’s 22-line geometry |
| Common Ari reconstruction | **Chokhmah–Gevurah** | Mothers horizontal, doubles vertical, simples diagonal |
| Common Gra reconstruction | **Chokhmah–Tiferet** | Same letter-class principle, but different sefirah geometry |
| *Sefer Yetzirah* itself | No named sefirah pair | Zayin is a simple letter and one of twelve diagonal boundaries |

## 10. Hasidic readings

### *Tanya*

**[ABSENCE]** There is no chapter of Shneur Zalman of Liadi’s *Tanya* devoted to Zayin, Gemini, or the seventeenth path. The request for “the Tanya’s chapter on this element” has no identifiable textual referent.

**[TEXT]** *Tanya* uses Lurianic sefirot, letters, divine speech, and the four worlds throughout, but it does not adopt the Kircher/Golden Dawn path grid. See the complete [*Tanya*](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya?lang=he).

### Rabbi Nachman of Breslov

**[TEXT]** *Likutei Moharan* 94 offers a genuine Zayin meditation. Faith becomes “the seventh” completing the six weekdays:

> **העשיה היא ששת ימי החול, והאמונה היא השביעית**

“Doing is the six weekdays, and faith is the seventh.”

Nachman calls this seventh principle Zayin and then multiplies Zayin by Yod: 7 × 10 = 70, Ayin, associated with the eye and wisdom. See [Likutei Moharan 94:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.94.2.4).

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** Elsewhere Nachman describes prayer as the Messiah’s weapon or two-edged sword. This makes an authentic Breslov constellation of Zayin, faith, Sabbath, sight, wisdom, and prayer. It still has no Tarot or Binah–Tiferet connection.

## 11. Prayer, Omer, calendar, and Torah

### Prayer

**[ABSENCE]** Standard Jewish liturgy does not contain a rite of ascending “Path 17.” Letter permutation, divine-name meditation, and kavvanot exist, especially in ecstatic and Lurianic Kabbalah, but a Zayin–Gemini–Lovers pathworking belongs to Western occultism.

**[TRADITION]** Zayin’s weapon symbolism can be spiritualized as prayer, particularly in Breslov teaching.

### Counting the Omer

**[TRADITION]** Kabbalistic Omer practice combines the seven lower sefirot across seven weeks: Chesed through Malkhut, producing 49 daily combinations.

**[ABSENCE]** The practice concerns seven sefirot, not the seventh letter as Path 17. Zayin’s value 7 makes later homiletic comparison possible, but it is not the structural key to the Omer.

### Month and Torah

**[TEXT/TRADITION]**

- Zayin → Gemini → Sivan in standard *Sefer Yetzirah* arrangements.
- Shavuot occurs in Sivan and commemorates the giving of Torah in rabbinic tradition.
- Later Jewish month symbolism connects Sivan with walking toward Sinai, the “twins,” or the union of written and oral Torah.
- Zebulun is a common later tribal attribution; other tables differ.

**[ABSENCE]** These are calendar and homiletic associations, not evidence that the biblical Torah contains a concealed Lovers card.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

**[TEXT]** Giovanni Pico della Mirandola included Cabalistic conclusions among his 900 *Conclusiones* prepared for public disputation in Rome in 1486. His sources included Latin translations made by Flavius Mithridates. See the [Latin *Conclusiones*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/pico/conclus.htm) and the [Brown University Pico Project](https://cds.library.brown.edu/projects/pico/latest.php).

**[TEXT/HERMETIC]** Pico interprets Jewish sefirotic and messianic materials through Christian theology, arguing that Cabala confirms doctrines concerning Christ, Trinity, and original sin.

**[ABSENCE]** Pico does not attest:

- Zayin as Path 17;
- Binah–Tiferet as its endpoints;
- Gemini–Lovers;
- Tarot on the Tree.

**[SCHOLARSHIP/CONTROVERSY]** Pico’s project founded Christian Cabala partly through genuine philological engagement and partly through conversionist appropriation: Jewish texts were made to testify to Christian conclusions their Jewish transmitters did not accept.

---

## 2. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

**[TEXT]** Paulus Ricius’s Latin translation of Gikatilla’s *Sha’arei Orah* was printed as *Portae Lucis* in Augsburg in 1516.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Its title-page tree is historically important as an early printed European sefirotic image. It is not a 22-letter path diagram and does not display a Zayin Binah–Tiferet line. See [Portae Lucis 1516](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3APortae_Lucis_1516.jpg).

---

## 3. Johannes Reuchlin, 1517

**[TEXT]** Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* describes thirty-two paths extending from highest to lowest and gives Latin titles to the named intelligences. The 1993 Martin–Goodman translation renders his account at approximately pp. 287–89. A first-edition record and scan are available through [Emory/Pitts Digital Collections](https://digital.pitts.emory.edu/s/paradise-lost/item/4771); parallel excerpts are at [Spiritus Mundi](https://spiritusmundi.com/es/obras/de-arte-cabalistica).

**[TRADITION AS REPORTED BY REUCHLIN]** Reuchlin says the paths were transmitted to Abraham, in one account by the angel Zadkiel.

**[ABSENCE]** Reuchlin does not establish the later Zayin–Gemini–Lovers synthesis. His Cabala is explicitly Christian and Pythagorean, but pre-Tarot in this specific sense.

---

## 4. Philippe d’Aquin and Athanasius Kircher

### Philippe d’Aquin, 1625

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Philippe d’Aquin, a Jewish convert to Christianity, published *L’Interprétation de l’arbre de la Cabale* in 1625. Kircher used its tree as an important visual template, while altering and expanding it. The relationship is illustrated in Paula Findlen’s edited volume, [*Athanasius Kircher: The Last Man Who Knew Everything*](https://kirchernetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/findlen-p-athanasius-kircher-the-last-man-who-knew-everything-2004.pdf).

### Kircher, 1652–1654

**[TEXT]** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I, prints a **Systema Sephiroticum** with ten sefirot, 22 connecting lines, Hebrew letters, angelic and divine names, and astrological symbols. Cornell provides the [Tree of Sephiroth plate](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272).

**[TEXT]** On that plate, Zayin is path 17, connecting Binah to Tiferet. Kircher’s path description is:

> **Decima septima semita dicitur Intelligentia dispositiua**

“The seventeenth path is called the Disposing Intelligence.”

See [Kircher, *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Kircher did not create the ten sefirot, the 22 letters, or the named intelligences. His innovation was a particular European synthesis and graphical alignment. His diagram became the ancestor of the Golden Dawn’s standard Tree.

**[TEXT/CONTROVERSY]** Kircher’s own language about Jewish Kabbalah is polemical and frequently anti-Jewish. He accuses rabbis of corrupting divine mysteries while simultaneously appropriating their textual traditions into a Christian-Egyptian universal history.

---

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

**[TEXT]** Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* translated and anthologized Zoharic, Cordoverian, Lurianic, and related materials in Latin. Its two major publication phases appeared in Sulzbach and Frankfurt between 1677 and 1684.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Rosenroth was enormously influential because readers without Hebrew could encounter texts and terminology associated with the Zohar and Luria. He also transmitted Latin materials on pure and impure sefirot later mined by occultists.

**[ABSENCE]** Rosenroth supplies no historical Jewish evidence for the Lovers card on Zayin. His importance is transmissional: Latin Cabala became source material for later occult synthesis.

## 6. Tarot enters the story

### Historical Tarot

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Tarot arose as a fifteenth-century Italian card game. Surviving early decks and documents provide no evidence that its trumps were invented as Hebrew letters or kabbalistic paths.

### Court de Gébelin and Comte de Mellet, 1781

**[SCHOLARSHIP/TEXT]** The first influential printed claims of ancient Egyptian and alphabetic significance appear in Antoine Court de Gébelin’s *Monde primitif* and Comte de Mellet’s accompanying essay. De Mellet correlates 22 trumps and 22 Hebrew letters.

**[MODERN INVENTION]** This is the historical beginning of the Tarot–Hebrew alphabet theory, not the recovery of an attested ancient system.

### Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

**[TEXT/HERMETIC]** In *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*, Lévi systematizes the 22-letter/22-trump correspondence. See the [French text](https://www.arbredor.com/ebooks/Dogme.pdf).

**[IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE]** Lévi’s direct alphabetic ordering gives:

- Vav, sixth letter → Trump VI, the Lovers.
- Zayin, seventh letter → Trump VII, the Chariot.

Therefore **Zayin = Lovers is not Lévi’s attribution**.

## 7. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### Formation of the synthesis

**[HERMETIC]** The Golden Dawn, founded in 1888, combined:

1. Kircher’s lettered path geometry;
2. *Sefer Yetzirah*’s letter astrology;
3. a revised Tarot sequence beginning Aleph with the Fool;
4. elemental, planetary, color, angelic, Egyptian, magical, and initiatory correspondences.

The resulting chain is:

> Zayin → seventh letter → path 17 → Gemini → Trump VI, the Lovers → Binah–Tiferet.

This exact composite is a late-nineteenth-century occult construction.

### *Book T*

**[TEXT/HERMETIC]** *Book T* calls Trump VI:

> “The Children of the Voice Divine; the Oracles of the Mighty Gods.”

It assigns Zayin and Gemini. See [Golden Dawn *Book T*](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/mathers-and-felkin-golden-dawn-book-t-the-tarot-1888.pdf) and Regardie's [*Complete Golden Dawn System*](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06Numerologia_Cabala/I.Regardie_Complete_Golden_Dawn_%28II%20ed.deluxe%29.pdf).

Its divinatory complex includes inspiration, intuition, intelligence, second sight, indecision, contradiction, superficial union, and instability. The “Lovers” becomes as much a card of polarity, choice, and inspired mediation as romantic attachment.

### Golden Dawn reasoning

**[HERMETIC]**

- Gemini supplies twins, doubleness, interchange, and Mercury.
- Zayin’s sword supplies division and discrimination.
- Binah supplies supernal understanding and form.
- Tiferet supplies solar beauty, harmony, the divine child, and the adept’s center.
- Their connection is interpreted as supernal understanding communicated to the solar self.
- The Lovers’ pair becomes division seeking reconciliation.

This reasoning is coherent inside Golden Dawn symbolism, but retrospective claims that it comes directly from *Sefer Yetzirah* are historically false.

|---|---|---|
| Atzilut | King | **Orange** |
| Briah | Queen | **Pale mauve** |
| Yetzirah | Prince/Emperor | **New yellow leather** |
| Assiah | Princess/Empress | **Reddish grey inclining to mauve** |

These appear in *777* and Golden Dawn color papers. See the [four-color-scale compilation](https://steve-p.org/cards/The%20Four%20Colour%20Scales%20of%20The%20Golden%20Dawn.pdf).

**[DISPUTED]** Some later Whare Ra and deck-making tables substitute “marigold,” violet, red-grey-violet, and pale grey-white tinged purple. These are lineages or practical color revisions, not proof that the original scale had only one immutable hue.

# The Qliphah and the tunnel

## Jewish qelippot versus an occult shadow Tree

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** Hebrew **קליפה**, *qelippah*, means shell, husk, or peel. Zoharic and Lurianic texts use shells and the *sitra ahra*, “Other Side,” for structures that conceal or feed upon holiness. Lurianic qelippot are bound to the shattering of vessels, sparks, sin, exile, and tikkun.

**[ABSENCE]** Jewish sources do not provide:

- a demon called Zamradiel guarding a Zayin tunnel;
- a complete mirror diagram with one demonized path for each Hebrew letter;
- a Tarot trump for each qelippotic tunnel.

That system is modern Western occultism.

## The ten occult qliphoth

Crowley’s *777* and Golden Dawn papers give the following order names. The demon-prince column comes from a Golden Dawn paper that explicitly warns the names cannot be confined neatly to single sefirot.

| No. | Sefirah shadowed | Qliphah/order | Common Golden Dawn chief |

| 2 | Chokhmah | Ghagiel/Ghogiel | Beelzebub |
| 3 | Binah | Satariel/Sathariel | Lucifuge |
| 4 | Chesed | Gha’agsheblah/Gha’agsheklah | Astaroth |
| 5 | Gevurah | Golachab | Asmodeus |
| 6 | Tiferet | Thagirion/Thagiriron | Belphegor |

See Crowley’s [*777*, columns VIII and related notes](https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/crowley/liber/libers/liber777.pdf) and the Golden Dawn paper [“The Qliphoth”](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html).

## Zamradiel

**[TEXT/HERMETIC]** Crowley’s *Liber 231* provides the name Zamradiel and a sigil opposite Zayin.

**[MODERN/HERMETIC]** Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* (1977), pp. 190–97 in the commonly circulating Skoob scan, turns this entry into the seventeenth “Tunnel of Set.” Grant writes that it transmits the shadow of the Lovers and prescribes:

- vibration of **Zamradiel** in musical key D;
- a hoarse, cackling vocalization;
- the sigil in “new leather yellow” on a mauve vesica;
- number **292**;
- raven/evening/gold wordplay;
- a crescent pierced by an arrow;
- Set–Horus or Set–Anubis twin symbolism;
- Da’ath, death, astral shells, sexual fluids, and Typhonian imagery.

See [Grant, *Nightside of Eden*, Zamradiel](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/G/Grant%20-%20Nightside%20of%20Eden.pdf).

**[MODERN INVENTION]** Grant’s derivations are creative Typhonian occultism. His Hebrew metatheses, Atlantean allusions, Setian physiology, and sexual-kala doctrine should not be represented as translations of the Zohar or Luria.

### Which spheres does the tunnel join?

**[HERMETIC LOGIC]** If the nightside mirrors Golden Dawn path 17, its terminals should be:

- Binah → Satariel;
- Tiferet → Thagirion.

Thus **Satariel–Thagirion**.

**[DISPUTED/MODERN]** Some online tables print **Ghagiel–Thagirion**, importing Chokhmah’s shell into the Zayin row. Grant’s prose calls the originating zone the “Black power-zone,” Binah, supporting Satariel. The Ghagiel reading is best treated as a later diagrammatic or transcription error unless an edition’s actual plate demonstrates otherwise.

### Tzalalimiron versus Zamradiel

These are not synonymous.

- **Tzalalimiron** is the qliphotic order assigned to Gemini in *777*.
- **Zamradiel** is the individual qliphotic genius in *Liber 231* and the tunnel guardian in Grant.
- **Satariel** and **Thagirion** are the qliphotic spheres at the tunnel’s ends.

# Pathworking traditions

## Golden Dawn

**[HERMETIC]** Original Golden Dawn work employed Tarot contemplation, color visualization, divine and angelic names, ritual signs, and “rising on the planes.” Surviving papers do not provide one universally fixed travelogue for Zayin.

A historically responsible Zayin pathworking therefore distinguishes:

1. **Order correspondences:** orange/mauve/new-leather/reddish-grey, Gemini, Lovers, Binah–Tiferet.
2. **Card imagery:** Lovers, twins, prophet or angel, choice.
3. **Interpretive development:** sword as discrimination; supernal understanding descending to Tiferet.
4. **Personal vision:** practitioner report, not doctrine.

## Regardie and twentieth-century occult schools

**[HERMETIC]** Regardie encouraged systematic meditation on letter, color, Tarot, astrology, and connected sefirot. B.O.T.A. placed greater emphasis on repeated Tarot meditation and the Cube of Space. Gareth Knight and later Servants of the Light writers introduced more explicitly psychological and initiatory narratives.

## Typical modern script

**[MODERN]** Later pathworking manuals often ask the practitioner to:

- enter through the Lovers card;
- encounter twins, a forked road, or an angelic mediator;
- test discrimination and commitment;
- cross a symbolic Abyss;
- receive understanding from Binah;
- return that understanding to Tiferet.

No single such narrative is canonical, and visionary reports are evidence about practitioners, not evidence for ancient Jewish cosmology.

## Nightside pathworking

**[MODERN/HERMETIC]** Grantian and post-Grantian practitioners reverse or tunnel beneath the same symbols, using Zamradiel, mauve and new-leather color, raven imagery, harsh laughter, and experiences of doubleness or disintegration. Linda Falorio and later authors added Tarot cards and guided practices not present in *Liber 231*.

## Diagrams and manuscripts

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Kabbalistic diagrams, *ilanot*, survive from the medieval period onward. They are not all variants of one 22-line pattern. J. H. Chajes documents trees, circles, bodies, garments, chains of worlds, and enormous scroll diagrams. See [*The Kabbalistic Tree*](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/978-0-271-09345-1.html) and the [Ilanot database](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/).

Important visual landmarks are:

1. Medieval manuscript diagrams—diverse, often not 22-letter networks.
2. *Portae Lucis* title page, 1516—printed sefirotic imagery without Golden Dawn paths.
3. D’Aquin’s Tree, 1625—direct predecessor to Kircher.
4. Kircher’s plate, 1652—lettered geometry behind the occult Tree.
5. Golden Dawn colored Trees—four color scales and ritual correspondences.
6. Waite–Smith and Thoth Lovers—two dominant pictorial interpretations.

## Poets, artists, and novelists

**[DOCUMENTED]**

- Crowley was both poet and occult writer; Zayin appears in his Tarot, visions, tables, and *Liber 231*.
- Pamela Colman Smith and Frieda Harris produced the two most influential modern Lovers images.
- W. B. Yeats was a Golden Dawn member and used broad occult and cyclic symbolism.

**[ABSENCE]** A writer’s general interest in Kabbalah is insufficient evidence that they “worked with the Path of Zayin.” No specific Zayin dossier should be attributed to Borges, Joyce, Yeats, or modern novelists without a text, diary entry, image, or letter explicitly making the connection.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Date of *Sefer Yetzirah*

- **[TRADITION]** Abrahamic authorship.
- **[EARLIER SCHOLARSHIP]** Dates as early as the second century.
- **[CURRENT SCHOLARSHIP]** Often seventh–ninth century, with earlier materials possible.
- **Evidence:** absence of secure early citations; early-tenth-century commentaries; vocabulary, cosmology, and divergent recensions.
- **Unresolved:** whether distinct strata can be dated separately.

## 2. Zohar authorship

- **[TRADITION]** Shimon bar Yohai and disciples.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]** Late-thirteenth-century Castile; Moses de León central.
- **[CURRENT DEBATE]** Single author versus literary circle, multiple authors, and later strata.
- **Evidence:** medieval linguistic features, contemporaneous concepts, manuscript variation, and historical reports about de León.

## 3. Is “Zayin” originally a sword pictogram?

- **[TRADITION/OCCULT]** The form is a sword.
- **[SECURE LEXICAL FACT]** *Kelei zayin* means weapons.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]** The Proto-Sinaitic object and original letter name remain uncertain.
- **Conclusion:** use “weapon/sword” as a documented Jewish and occult interpretation, not certain Bronze Age palaeography.

## 4. Does *Sefer Yetzirah* describe the modern Tree?

- **[POPULAR CLAIM]** Its 32 paths are the modern ten circles plus 22 lines.
- **[TEXT]** It enumerates ten sefirot and 22 letters.
- **[ABSENCE]** It gives no Kircher topology.
- **Conclusion:** the modern diagram is an interpretation of the number 32, not an illustration transmitted inside the earliest text.

## 5. Kircher versus Jewish trees

- Kircher: Zayin = Binah–Tiferet.
- Common Ari: Zayin = Chokhmah–Gevurah.
- Common Gra: Zayin = Chokhmah–Tiferet.
- *Sefer Yetzirah*: no named pair.

The dispute is not merely over where to draw one line. It reflects incompatible principles for placing the mother, double, and simple letters.

## 6. Who originated Kircher’s Tree?

**[DISPUTED]** Older occult literature calls it “Kircher’s Tree” as if invented wholly by Kircher. Historical comparison shows substantial dependence on Philippe d’Aquin’s 1625 Tree, itself embedded in Jewish and Christian diagrammatic traditions. Kircher’s distinctive contribution is the expanded syncretic plate and letter/path alignment.

## 7. Tarot attribution

- Historical Tarot has no demonstrated Kabbalistic origin.
- De Mellet: early 22-letter theory, 1781.
- Lévi: Zayin = Chariot VII.
- Golden Dawn: Zayin = Lovers VI.
- Crowley: retains Zayin/Lovers; changes Heh/Tzaddi elsewhere.

Thus “Zayin has always been the Lovers” is false.

## 8. “Disposing” versus “sensible” intelligence

- Hebrew: **שכל ההרגש**, intelligence of sensation/feeling.
- Latin: **Intelligentia dispositiva**.
- Westcott: Disposing Intelligence.

“Disposing” reflects the Latin reception rather than a literal translation of *hargesh*.

## 9. Da’ath

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** *Sefer Yetzirah* insists on ten sefirot—“ten and not eleven.” In later Kabbalah, Da’ath can function as the conjunction or revealed condition of Chokhmah and Binah; systems often count either Keter or Da’ath in particular enumerations, not eleven independent sefirot.

**[HERMETIC]** Golden Dawn and Thelemic diagrams often draw Da’ath as an unnumbered or “invisible” sphere at the Abyss.

**[ABSENCE]** Path 17 does not terminate at Da’ath. Modern art may make its line pass through the Abyssal region, but that does not make Da’ath an endpoint or prove an eleventh sefirah.

## 10. Christian Cabala and appropriation

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Pico, Reuchlin, d’Aquin, Kircher, and Rosenroth transmitted real Jewish material while subordinating it to Christian theological claims. Conversion, censorship, anti-Jewish polemic, and unequal access to Hebrew learning are part of that history.

**[HERMETIC DEVELOPMENT]** The Golden Dawn further universalized the material by adding Egyptian gods, Greek gods, Tarot, Enochian magic, geomancy, and color theory.

**Conclusion:** influence and creativity can be acknowledged without treating the synthesis as either ancient Judaism or a neutral transcription of it.

## 11. Qliphothic rulers

**[DISPUTED]** The ten-order and ten-demon-prince tables combine Jewish terms, Latin Cabala, medieval demonology, Golden Dawn redaction, and twentieth-century occultism. Even the Golden Dawn paper says the chiefs cannot strictly be limited to one sefirah.

## 12. The contested-material search finding

Searches centered on “Path of Zayin,” dispute, attribution, Kircher, Golden Dawn, Ari, Gra, dating, and the Tzaddi controversy produced three recurring errors:

1. Modern pages state that *Sefer Yetzirah* itself places Zayin between Binah and Tiferet.
2. Kircher’s diagram is called the uniquely authentic Jewish Tree.
3. Crowley’s Tzaddi correction is said to change Zayin.

None survives comparison with the primary texts.

# Open questions

1. **The earliest recoverable form of the “Thirty-two Intelligences” list.** Its Hebrew manuscript history, relationship to *Sefer ha-Peliah*, and routes into Reuchlin and Kircher require a dedicated stemmatic study.

2. **Exact Ari and Gra diagram transmission.** Modern tables give clear differing placements, but an edition-by-edition survey of manuscript *ilanot* is needed before attributing one final diagram personally to Luria or the Gra.

3. **The original acrophonic name of Zayin.** Weapon, fetter, agricultural implement, and olive-related theories remain palaeographically contested.

4. **Saadyan bodily/faculty sequences.** The surviving Arabic commentary, Hebrew retroversions, Short and Long texts, and later corrected editions need side-by-side manuscript collation. Internet labels such as “Saadia version” frequently conceal mixed texts.

5. **Kircher’s immediate textual source for *Intelligentia dispositiva*.** Reuchlin, Rittangelius, d’Aquin, medieval Hebrew lists, and Kircher’s own harmonization all warrant comparison.

6. **The origin of each Golden Dawn color.** The four-scale doctrine is documented, but the practical pigments, manuscript variants, and Whare Ra revisions need codicological comparison.

7. **The exact relationship between Crowley’s *Liber 231* sigils and its oracles.** The printed typography is unstable, original manuscript evidence is incomplete, and later interpretations often assume more systematic pairings than the publication explains.

8. **Zamradiel’s terminals.** Grant’s prose supports Binah/Satariel–Tiferet/Thagirion; some derivative tables print Ghagiel–Thagirion. The plates of each authorized edition should be collated.

9. **Pathworking reports as historical evidence.** Diaries from Golden Dawn, Stella Matutina, Whare Ra, A∴A∴, B.O.T.A., and Servants of the Light could show when now-standard Zayin imagery first appeared in actual practice.

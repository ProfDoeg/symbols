# The Path of Gimel: Research Dossier

## Editorial key

The following labels distinguish kinds of evidence throughout:

- **[Text]** Directly attested in a cited primary text, manuscript, printed edition, or organizational paper.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** A conclusion of historical, philological, or palaeographic scholarship.
- **[Jewish tradition]** A traditional attribution accepted within a Jewish interpretive lineage but not necessarily historically demonstrable.
- **[Hermetic attribution]** A teaching of Christian Cabala, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, or a related occult school.
- **[Disputed]** A claim for which texts, recensions, or scholars materially disagree.
- **[Modern invention]** A comparatively recent synthesis that should not be projected into ancient or medieval Jewish Kabbalah.

Two distinctions govern the dossier:

1. *Sefer Yetzirah* teaches ten *sefirot belimah* and twenty-two creative letters, but it does not supply the familiar Kircher/Golden Dawn diagram assigning every letter to a particular line between two sefirot.
2. “Gimel = Moon = Kether–Tiphareth = High Priestess” is a coherent Golden Dawn correspondence. It is not the uniform—or even the predominant early—Jewish attribution of Gimel.

### The central historical finding

**[Text]** The common short/long *Sefer Yetzirah* tradition makes Gimel form Jupiter:

> המליך אות ג׳ ... וצר בו צדק בעולם

“He made Gimel reign … and formed with it Jupiter in the World.” [*Sefer Yetzirah* 4:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.4.6)

The same recension gives the Moon to Tav. The Gra recension rearranges the series, normally giving Gimel Mars. The Golden Dawn instead gives Gimel the Moon. A comparison derived from Aryeh Kaplan’s recension tables displays the difference particularly clearly: early short, long, and Saadia forms have Gimel–Jupiter; Gra has Gimel–Mars; Golden Dawn has Gimel–Moon. [Comparative attribution table](https://www.psyche.com/psyche/yetsira/sy_planetaryattributions.html)

## The name

### Hebrew spelling and transliteration

**[Text]** The normal modern Hebrew spelling is **גימל**, vocalized **גִּימֶל**, *Gimel*. English occult literature also uses *Gimmel*, *Gimel*, *Gimel/Gemel*, and older *Ghimel*, the last spelling attempting to preserve the historical fricative pronunciation of undageshed ג.

In *Sefer Yetzirah*, the letter normally appears simply as **ג׳** within the sequence **בג״ד כפר״ת**. The full occult spelling **גמל**, GML, is especially important in Crowley because it totals 73. It is a defective spelling of the letter-name rather than the usual modern orthography.

### Meaning and etymology

**[Text]** Hebrew **גָּמָל**, *gamal*, means “camel.” The related verbal root **גמל** can mean to deal with, recompense, bestow, mature, ripen, or wean. Thus several historically distinct associations became available:

- camel as the conventional name of the letter;
- *gemul*, recompense or benefit;
- *gomel*, one who bestows;
- *gemilut ḥasadim*, acts of loving-kindness.

The lexical evidence is collected under גמל in [BDB-linked lexical data](https://lemma.gebible.app/w/H1581) and the [Klein Dictionary](https://www.sefaria.org/Klein_Dictionary%2C_%D7%92).

**[Disputed]** The familiar assertion that the original sign was literally a drawing of a camel or camel’s neck is possible but not proven. Older alphabet histories often repeat it; more recent historians have proposed a throwing stick, staff-sling, or another implement. Even general palaeographic tables sometimes cautiously print “camel(?)”. The secure points are the consonantal continuity and name-family, not a uniquely demonstrable pictorial prototype.

### Form and graphic history

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The likely line of development is:

```text
Proto-alphabetic sign (identification disputed)
        ↓
Proto-Canaanite / early alphabetic forms
        ↓
Phoenician 𐤂
        ↓
Paleo-Hebrew forms
        ↓
Imperial Aramaic 𐡂
        ↓
Jewish square script ג
```

Phoenician *gīml* also gave Greek **Γ, gamma**. Through western Greek and Etruscan transmission, related forms contributed to Latin **C**, from which **G** was later differentiated.

The Proto-Sinaitic corpus is conventionally dated around the second millennium BCE, but individual sign readings remain debated. Unicode consequently treats Phoenician as a historical-script block embracing Phoenician and closely related palaeo-Hebrew traditions without claiming that every reconstructed Proto-Sinaitic sign is certain. See the [Unicode Hebrew and Phoenician specification](https://unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-10/) and the [Hebrew Palaeography Project](https://www.hebrewpalaeography.com/data/graphicelements/25170/).

### Sound and “double” status

**[Text]** *Sefer Yetzirah* places Gimel among seven “doubles,” whose two pronunciations are described through such contrasts as soft/hard or strong/weak. In the Tiberian tradition:

- גּ, with dagesh: /g/;
- ג, without dagesh: conventionally reconstructed as the fricative /ɣ/.

Modern Israeli Hebrew normally pronounces both as /g/, though some traditional pronunciations retain a distinction.

**[Disputed]** The seven-letter *Sefer Yetzirah* group includes Resh, whereas the later standard grammatical mnemonic *begadkefat* normally comprises six letters. The book’s “double” theory therefore cannot simply be reduced to the familiar modern rule about six letters taking dagesh.

### Gematria

- ג alone = **3**.
- גימל = **83**.
- גמל = **73**.

**[Hermetic attribution]** Crowley favors GML = 73 and develops correspondences from that number. These calculations depend upon the selected spelling. Treating 73 as the one intrinsic “number of Gimel” suppresses the ordinary spelling גימל and is therefore an occult convention, not a neutral philological fact.

### Gimel in divine names

**[Text]** Gimel does not occur in the Tetragrammaton יהוה, אהיה (*Ehyeh*), אל (*El*), or שדי (*Shaddai*). It does occur in descriptive names and epithets such as **אלהים גבור**, *Elohim Gibbor*, “Mighty God,” used by the Golden Dawn for Geburah.

**Finding:** no classical Jewish source discovered in this research assigns a special divine name, archangel, or angelic order to a line called “the Path of Gimel.” Such names belong primarily to sefirot, palaces, planets, or angelic hierarchies. Transferring Gabriel to Gimel because both are lunar in the Golden Dawn system is a secondary Hermetic operation.

## Biblical vocabulary behind the Tree and its “paths”

These verses are important to the vocabulary of the sefirotic tradition, but none identifies Gimel as a Kether–Tiphareth path.

### 1 Chronicles 29:11

**[Text]**

> לך יהוה הגדלה והגבורה והתפארת והנצח וההוד ... לך יהוה הממלכה

The verse names greatness, power, beauty, victory, splendour, “all,” and kingdom. Kabbalists read these as a scriptural series corresponding to:

- Gedulah/Chesed;
- Gevurah;
- Tiferet;
- Netzach;
- Hod;
- *kol*, often interpreted as Yesod;
- Malkhut.

See [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11).

**[Jewish tradition]** This is a major proof-text for the seven lower sefirot.

**Finding:** it is not an etymological or historical source for the thirteenth path or the letter Gimel.

### Proverbs 3:19

> יהוה בחכמה יסד ארץ כונן שמים בתבונה

“YHWH founded the earth by Wisdom; by Understanding He established the heavens.” [Proverbs 3:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19)

This became important for Chokhmah and Binah. Proverbs 3:17 also speaks of Wisdom’s *netivot*, “paths,” as peace.

### Job 28 and Isaiah

Job 28:7 speaks of a **נתיב**, a hidden path unknown to the bird of prey. [Job 28:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28.7)

Isaiah 43:16 speaks of God making “a path in mighty waters,” **ובמים עזים נתיבה**. [Isaiah 43](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.43)

**[Text]** Cordovero cites the language of Job and Isaiah in *Pardes Rimmonim* 8:5 to distinguish a concealed *netiv* from a more open road. [*Pardes Rimmonim* 8:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.8.5)

These are sources for the mystical vocabulary of hidden channels, not for the later numbered Kircher paths.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date and textual condition

**[Jewish tradition]** The book is traditionally attributed to Abraham; other traditions connect its transmission or interpretation with Rabbi Akiva.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The surviving text is anonymous and composite. Proposed dates range widely, from late antiquity into the early Islamic period. Its first secure reception is in the tenth century, notably in the commentaries of Saadia Gaon and Shabbetai Donnolo. Peter Hayman describes the textual form as emerging into documented history in the early tenth century and produced the first full critical synoptic edition of the principal manuscripts. [Hayman, *Sefer Yeṣira*](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/)

Tzahi Weiss has argued for a seventh-century milieu partly from Syriac grammatical terminology. Other scholars retain somewhat earlier or later datings. [Weiss, “Sefer Yeṣirah and Its Contexts”](https://brill.com/view/journals/ic/27/4-5/article-p407_9.pdf)

A recent textual study argues that Saadia’s recension may preserve features of an early textual form despite Saadia’s rearrangement of the material. [Hebrew University research record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-earliest-version-of-sefer-yeirah-text-format-structure-and-ge/)

### The thirty-two paths

**[Text]** The book begins with “thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom” and immediately analyzes them as:

> עשר ספירות בלימה ועשרים ושתים אותיות

“Ten sefirot belimah and twenty-two letters.” [*Sefer Yetzirah*, Gra 1:1–2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.1)

The letters divide into:

- three mothers: אמש;
- seven doubles: בג״ד כפר״ת;
- twelve simples.

**Crucial finding:** the text does not say that the third letter must be drawn as one particular geometrical line from Kether to Tiferet. It provides creative-letter classes, cosmological attributions, permutations, directions, planets, times, and bodily “gates.” The fixed letter-labelled Tree familiar from Golden Dawn literature is a later construction.

### Gimel as a double

**[Text]** The common recension begins chapter 4:

> שבע כפולות בג״ד כפר״ת

“Seven doubles: BGD KPRT.” [*Sefer Yetzirah* 4:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.4)

These letters generate seven planets, seven days, and seven bodily gates. The chapter also supplies seven polarities:

- life/death;
- peace/evil or war;
- wisdom/folly;
- wealth/poverty;
- grace/ugliness;
- fertility/desolation;
- dominion/servitude.

The exact pairing of each polarity with each letter varies by recension.

### Recension comparison

| Witness or received recension | Gimel’s planet | Polarity commonly paired with Gimel | Bodily gate | Assessment |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Common short recension | Jupiter | Peace/evil or peace/war | Left eye | **[Text]** Sefaria 4:6 |
| Long recension | Jupiter | Usually peace/war | Recensional variation | **[Text]** |
| Saadia recension | Jupiter | Often wealth or peace depending how the reordered text is tabulated | Right/left-eye and day details vary | **[Text/Disputed]** |
| Gra recension | Mars | Wealth/poverty in the usual Gra ordering | Commonly right ear | **[Text]**, but its redaction is late |
| “Ari” recension | Not a securely independent autograph; often conflated with an Ari–Gra redaction | Variable in modern tables | Variable | **[Disputed]** |
| Golden Dawn | Moon | Peace/war in occult tables | Left eye in *777* | **[Hermetic attribution]** |

The early planetary sequence is normally the descending Chaldean order:

```text
Bet   Saturn
Gimel Jupiter
Dalet Mars
Kaf   Sun
Peh   Venus
Resh  Mercury
Tav   Moon
```

The Gra order usually begins:

```text
Bet   Moon
Gimel Mars
Dalet Sun
Kaf   Venus
Peh   Mercury
Resh  Saturn
Tav   Jupiter
```

The Golden Dawn order is:

```text
Bet   Mercury
Gimel Moon
Dalet Venus
Kaf   Jupiter
Peh   Mars
Resh  Sun
Tav   Saturn
```

**Conclusion:** the statement “Gimel is the Moon in *Sefer Yetzirah*” is false without the qualification “in the Golden Dawn’s adaptation of Yetziratic letter classes.” The Moon is Tav in the common early sequence and Bet in the Gra recension.

### The “Uniting Intelligence”

Westcott’s 1893 English *Sepher Yetzirah* includes an appendix called “The Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom.” Its thirteenth item reads:

> “The Thirteenth Path is named the Uniting Intelligence … the Essence of Glory.”

W. Wynn Westcott, *Sepher Yetzirah*, 1893, appendix, p. 28. [Scanned edition](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1893__westcott___sepher_yetzirah.pdf)

A Hebrew form commonly printed for this title is:

> השכל המנהיג האחדות  
> *ha-sekhel ha-manhig ha-aḥdut*

“The intelligence directing/leading unity.”

Longer recensions continue with language describing it as the “essence of glory” and “completion of the truth” of unified spiritual beings.

**[Textual caution]** Westcott explicitly says his appendix was translated from the Hebrew text printed by Johannes Stephanus Rittangelius in 1642. It is not part of the core ancient text represented by the principal *Sefer Yetzirah* manuscripts. Its “thirty-two intelligences” constitute a separate medieval or early-modern appendix traditionally attached to the book.

**[Hermetic attribution]** Golden Dawn and later occult writers combined:

1. this appendix’s thirteenth intelligence;
2. Gimel as the third letter;
3. Kircher’s thirteenth connecting line;
4. the Moon;
5. the High Priestess.

The fivefold identity does not appear as a unit in ancient *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## 2. Talmudic alphabet lore

### Shabbat 104a

**[Text]** Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 104a, preserves a children’s alphabet homily:

> גימ״ל דל״ת — גמול דלים

“Gimel-Dalet: bestow upon the poor.” [Shabbat 104a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104a)

The extended leg of Gimel runs toward Dalet because a benefactor should pursue the poor. Dalet turns its face away so charity can be given discreetly.

This interpretation depends upon wordplay:

- ג = גמול, bestow;
- ד = דלים, the poor.

**[Jewish tradition]** The teaching makes Gimel an ethical emblem of active loving-kindness.

**Finding:** it is letter exegesis, not a planetary or tree-path attribution.

## 3. *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

**[Jewish tradition]** The *Alphabet of Rabbi Akiva* is attributed pseudepigraphically to Rabbi Akiva.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Its extant forms are late-antique or early-medieval alphabet midrashim, transmitted in varying recensions and printed much later. They elaborate every Hebrew letter through acrostics, divine attributes, numerical analogies, and the shapes of letters.

Gimel is associated with *gemilut ḥasadim*, divine beneficence, recompense, and words beginning with ג. These traditions helped stabilize the ethical interpretation of Gimel as giving or reward.

**Finding:** no early recension securely identifies Gimel with a Kether–Tiferet line, the Moon, or the High Priestess.

## 4. *Sefer ha-Bahir*

The *Bahir* first became visible as a circulating text in twelfth-century Provence, though it presents itself as much older teaching associated with Rabbi Nehuniah ben ha-Qanah.

### Gimel in §§19–20

**[Text]** The *Bahir* asks why Gimel is third and interprets it through the root גמל: accomplishing, bestowing, growing, or sustaining. Its form is read dynamically: the upper part receives, while its “tail” or conduit distributes below.

This is a genuinely kabbalistic development beyond the Talmudic alphabet mnemonic. Gimel becomes an image of transmission:

```text
source above
    ↓
Gimel receives
    ↓
Gimel bestows/sustains below
```

See *Sefer ha-Bahir*, §§19–20, in the [Kaplan-based PDF edition](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Sepher-Bahir.pdf) and [Hebrew/French text](https://kabbale.eu/wp-content/files/Sefer_Habbahir.pdf).

**[Jewish tradition]** This can be compared conceptually with a channel or path.

**Finding:** the *Bahir* does not state “Gimel is path 13 from Keter to Tiferet.” That geometry is absent.

## 5. The Zohar

### Letters petitioning to begin creation

**[Text]** In the Zoharic prologue, the letters approach God in reverse order, each asking to be used to create the world. Gimel and Dalet appear together. God tells them to remain together because the poor will always require a benefactor: Dalet signifies *dal*, poor, and Gimel the one who sustains it. See [Zohar, Introduction 6:34–36](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar%2C_Introduction.6.34).

This is closely related to Shabbat 104a but integrated into Zoharic creation theology.

### Other Zoharic uses

The Zohar repeatedly interprets letters as powers, forms, channels, and components of divine names. Gimel may be paired with Dalet in discussions of bestowal and receptivity, or combined with other letters in permutations. There is no single, universal Zoharic “Gimel doctrine.”

**Finding:** no passage in the classical Zohar gives the complete later identity:

```text
Gimel = path 13 = Kether–Tiphareth = Moon = High Priestess
```

### Authorship and date

**[Jewish tradition]** The Zohar records the teachings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle in the second century CE.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Linguistic and documentary evidence places the main body in late-thirteenth-century Castile. Moses de León is generally considered its central author or redactor, possibly working with additional sources or collaborators. Modern scholarship does not reduce every layer to one moment or one hand. Daniel C. Matt’s Pritzker edition treats the text’s strata and sources in detail. For the debate see the [Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Scholem](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2026/entries/scholem/) and [University College London scholarship on Zoharic authorship](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10088406/).

## 6. Gikatilla and *Shaʿarei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*, composed in late-thirteenth-century Castile, organizes divine names and biblical language by the ten sefirot.

**[Text]** Its basic structure is ten “gates” ascending through the sefirotic world. It supplies divine names and relations among sefirot, not a list assigning divine names, archangels, or angelic orders to twenty-two lettered connecting lines. [*Sha’arei Orah* on Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Shaarei_Orah)

For the later Kircher/Golden Dawn Gimel line, the relevant endpoint names can be compared:

- Keter: אהיה, *Ehyeh*;
- Tiferet: the Tetragrammaton and its harmonizing manifestations.

But these are names of the endpoint sefirot, not a documented name “of Gimel.”

## 7. Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

Moses Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim* is the great pre-Lurianic synthesis of Safedian Kabbalah.

**[Text]** Cordovero insists upon ten sefirot and discusses the thirty-two paths as the ten sefirot plus twenty-two letters. He also treats the 231 gates generated by letter combinations. [*Pardes Rimmonim*](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim); see especially [30:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.30.5).

In 8:5 he uses Job 28:7 and Isaiah 43:16 to explain a *netiv* as a hidden channel. This is important evidence for Jewish path language. It is not evidence for Kircher’s particular letter layout.

**Finding:** Cordovero does not teach the Golden Dawn Path of Gimel as a Moon/High Priestess line from Keter to Tiferet.

### *Or Neʿerav*

*Or Neʿerav* is an introduction to the study and disciplined transmission of Kabbalah. Its headings “Letter Aleph,” “Letter Bet,” “Letter Gimel,” and so forth belong to an alphabetically ordered index or glossary in modern digital presentation, not to a series of twenty-two Tree paths. [*Or Ne’erav*](https://www.sefaria.org/Ohr_Ne%27erav)

**Negative finding:** the apparent “Letter Gimel” heading is not a Cordoverian chapter on the thirteenth path.

## 8. Luria through Hayyim Vital

Isaac Luria, the Ari (1534–1572), left little writing. His doctrines were transmitted chiefly through disciples, especially Hayyim Vital. *Etz Chaim* is a posthumously organized Vitalian presentation.

Its central themes include:

- **tzimtzum**, primordial contraction or concealment;
- the ray entering the vacated space;
- Adam Qadmon;
- the four worlds—Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, Assiah;
- the configurations called *partzufim*;
- *shevirat ha-kelim*, the shattering of vessels;
- sparks in the lower worlds;
- *tikkun*, restoration or reconfiguration.

See [*Sefer Etz Chaim*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim) and Sefaria’s [tzimtzum source collection](https://www.sefaria.org/topics/tzimtzum1).

**[Text]** Vitalian writings contain many diagrams, letter permutations, divine-name structures, and channels among partzufim.

**Finding:** *Etz Chaim* does not present the later Golden Dawn equation of path 13, Gimel, Moon, High Priestess, and Keter–Tiferet.

### What “the Ari tree” means

**[Disputed]** Modern diagrams called “the Ari Tree” attempt to place the three mothers on horizontal paths, seven doubles on vertical paths, and twelve simple letters on diagonals. This is a reconstruction from classificatory principles and later diagram traditions, not necessarily a plate authored by Luria.

In the common modern Ari layout:

- Gimel joins **Binah–Gevurah**;
- Dalet joins **Keter–Tiferet**.

This differs materially from Kircher.

## 9. The Gra

The Vilna Gaon’s recension reorganizes *Sefer Yetzirah* and was printed with diagrams in later editions. Aryeh Kaplan’s English edition gives this system wide modern circulation.

In the common reconstructed Gra tree:

- Gimel joins **Chokhmah–Chesed**;
- Bet joins **Keter–Tiferet**.

The associated Gra planetary series gives Gimel Mars, not Moon.

| Line | Common Ari reconstruction | Common Gra reconstruction | Kircher/Golden Dawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keter–Tiferet | Dalet | Bet | **Gimel** |
| Chokhmah–Chesed | Bet | **Gimel** | Vav |
| Binah–Gevurah | **Gimel** | Dalet | Cheth |

**[Disputed]** “Ari tree” and “Gra tree” are not completely uniform categories. Editions and modern redrawings sometimes differ. The table identifies the widespread Kaplan-derived reconstructions, not an uncontested ancient geometry.

## 10. Hasidic readings

### Tanya

**Finding:** *Tanya* contains no chapter devoted to “the Path of Gimel.” Chapter 3—identified by the Hebrew chapter number ג—discusses the intellectual faculties Chokhmah, Binah, and Daʿat and the emotions born from them. The numeral ג in the chapter heading does not make it a treatise on the letter Gimel. [*Tanya*](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya)

Later Chabad teaching does interpret Gimel through *gomel dalim*, generosity, and the camel as a bearer across barren space. These are homiletic developments, not evidence that Shneur Zalman taught the Golden Dawn path. An example is the modern Chabad essay [“Gimmel”](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/137075/jewish/Gimmel.htm).

### Rabbi Nachman of Breslov

Rabbi Nachman makes extensive use of letters, vocalization, divine names, charity, Torah, imagination, and the paths of spiritual return. No canonical teaching has been found in *Likutei Moharan* that identifies a “Path of Gimel” joining Keter and Tiferet. [*Likutei Moharan*](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan)

### Prayer, Torah, calendar, Omer

- **Prayer:** no standard Jewish liturgical rite is assigned to the Kircher Path of Gimel.
- **Psalm 119:** verses 17–24 form the Gimel stanza; every verse begins with ג.
- **Torah:** Gimel is a sacred scribal letter and participates in traditional letter interpretation, but the Torah does not contain the Golden Dawn path attribution.
- **Omer:** the third day is often contemplated in later devotional systems as Tiferet within Chesed. That is a day-three sefirotic permutation, not a classical Gimel path attribution.
- **Month and tribe:** none for Gimel in *Sefer Yetzirah*, because months, tribes, zodiacal signs, and directional limbs belong to the twelve simple letters.
- **Planet/day/body:** these do apply to Gimel as a double, but differ among recensions.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Giovanni Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones philosophicae, cabalisticae et theologicae* introduced Christian readers to a programmatic Cabala obtained substantially through Jewish converts and translators.

**[Text]** Pico treats Hebrew letters, divine names, the ten numerations, and Cabala as confirming Christian mysteries. [Latin *Conclusiones*](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Conclusiones_philosophicae%2C_cabalisticae_et_theologicae)

**Finding:** the 1486 conclusions contain no documented doctrine of “Path 13, Gimel, Kether–Tiphareth, Moon, High Priestess.” Tarot is absent.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Pico’s significance lies in the Christian reinterpretive framework that made later Latin Cabala possible, not in originating the Golden Dawn layout.

## 2. Johannes Reuchlin, 1517

Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* presents Cabala through a Christian philosophical dialogue.

**[Text]** Reuchlin transmits Hebrew letter names in Latin transliteration—*Gimel/Ghimel*—and discusses alphabets, number, angelic hierarchies, divine names, and letter permutations. In one synthetic hierarchy Gimel can be connected with the third angelic rank. [Digitized/transcribed *De arte cabalistica*](https://spiritusmundi.com/es/obras/de-arte-cabalistica)

**Finding:** Reuchlin has no tarot attribution and no Golden Dawn Path of Gimel.

### The Latin name

No stable Latin translation displaced the Semitic name. Writers could gloss it with **camelus**, “camel,” but continued to use *Gimel/Ghimel* as the technical letter-name.

## 3. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paulus Ricius’s Latin abridgment of Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah* was published as *Portae Lucis*. Its title-page tree is probably the earliest printed sefirotic tree. The design is often associated with Hans Burgkmair.

See the [Center for Jewish Art record](https://cja.huji.ac.il/browser.php?id=40577&mode=set) and [digitized image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sefirot_(Paulus_Ricius).jpg).

**[Text]** The tree shows ten sefirotic medallions and connecting lines.

**Negative finding:** it does not label a set of twenty-two paths with the Hebrew alphabet and therefore does not yet give Gimel to Keter–Tiferet.

## 4. Athanasius Kircher, 1652–1654

Kircher published *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* in several volumes between 1652 and 1654. His famous “Tree of the Sephiroth” appears in the Cabalistic section of volume II, part I.

- [Latin transcription and plate](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)
- [Cornell digital image](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272)
- [e-rara scan](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/13487220.pdf)
- [scan index](https://www.billheidrick.com/Orpd/AKir/AKOeAeII.htm)

### Kircher’s innovation and sources

**[Text]** The plate places twenty-two Hebrew letters on twenty-two lines. Gimel is on the central line from the crown to the solar middle, Kether–Tiphareth.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Kircher did not invent sefirotic diagrams. Jewish *ilanot* had existed for centuries, and Renaissance Christian diagrams preceded him. His specific arrangement belongs to an early-modern Christian schematic tradition, probably indebted to sources such as Philippe d’Aquin and to printed or manuscript Jewish trees.

The University of Haifa’s Ilanot Project notes that Jewish kabbalists began producing developed arboreal diagrams in the fourteenth century and that these were flexible cosmological maps rather than one immutable icon. [Maps of God](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/)

### Difference from Jewish trees

Kircher’s order is visually simple: he places letters in a largely sequential scheme over the lines of a familiar three-column tree. This does not preserve the classificatory principle later used to construct Ari/Gra arrangements:

- mothers on horizontal lines;
- doubles on vertical lines;
- simples on diagonals.

Kircher therefore makes Gimel Keter–Tiferet, whereas the common Ari and Gra reconstructions place another double there.

### What the Golden Dawn adopted

**[Hermetic attribution]** The Golden Dawn adopted Kircher’s basic line-letter geometry. It did not merely reproduce Kircher. It added or standardized:

- *Sefer Yetzirah* element/planet/zodiac classes;
- tarot trumps;
- four colour scales;
- Egyptian, Greek, Christian, magical, and alchemical correspondences;
- initiatory pathworking;
- the Abyss and grade symbolism.

Thus “the Golden Dawn adopted Kircher’s tree” is broadly correct for the geometry but misleading if taken to mean that Kircher already possessed the Golden Dawn system.

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* appeared in Sulzbach in 1677–1678 and Frankfurt in 1684. It translated and excerpted Zoharic, Cordoverian, and Lurianic materials into Latin and became a central conduit to later Christian and occult readers. [Digital edition index](https://www.billheidrick.com/Orpd/KRKD/index.htm)

The letter remains *Gimel/Ghimel* in Latin transliteration; *camelus* may be supplied as a gloss.

**Finding:** *Kabbala Denudata* contains no tarot High Priestess attribution. It is an important source for the sefirotic and Lurianic background later mined by Mathers and others, but a complete Golden Dawn “Path of Gimel” should not be retrojected into it.

## 6. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* was decisive in aligning the twenty-two Hebrew letters with the twenty-two tarot trumps. [French edition](https://www.arbredor.com/ebooks/Dogme.pdf)

His straightforward series differed from the Golden Dawn:

| Letter | Lévi’s sequential trump | Golden Dawn trump |
|---|---|---|
| Aleph | Magician I | Fool 0 |
| Bet | High Priestess II | Magician I |
| **Gimel** | **Empress III** | **High Priestess II** |
| Dalet | Emperor IV | Empress III |

Lévi placed the unnumbered or late-numbered Fool differently from the Golden Dawn. Once the Golden Dawn assigned Fool to Aleph as zero, the remaining early trumps shifted by one letter.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** There is no historical evidence that medieval tarot was designed as a Hebrew-alphabet cipher. The correspondence is an occult interpretation originating centuries after the first tarot cards.

## 7. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

The Golden Dawn was founded in 1888. Its Knowledge Lectures and “Book T” papers systematized the Kircher tree, astrology, tarot, colour, and ritual symbolism.

### Formal attribution

**[Text]** The Fourth Knowledge Lecture lists path 13 with Trump II, the High Priestess. [Fourth Knowledge Lecture](https://osogd.org/education/the-fourth-or-practicus-knowledge-lecture/)

The canonical Golden Dawn cluster is:

```text
13 — Gimel — Moon — High Priestess
Kether ↕ Tiphareth
Uniting Intelligence
```

### Why the High Priestess?

The reasoning is cumulative:

1. Gimel is the third letter and therefore path 13.
2. Kircher placed the third letter on Kether–Tiphareth.
3. The Golden Dawn’s planetary sequence assigned Luna to Gimel.
4. Its tarot sequence assigned the High Priestess to Gimel.
5. The High Priestess’s veil, silver colour, memory, receptivity, and concealed knowledge were read as lunar.
6. The Middle-Pillar line made her the mediator of supernal light into the solar centre.

**[Hermetic attribution]** The camel becomes the animal able to cross the “desert” of the Abyss. This is a powerful later interpretation but is not stated in ancient *Sefer Yetzirah* or the *Bahir*. Its explicit development belongs chiefly to Crowley, Regardie, Case, and later pathworking literature.

### Four colour scales

| World | Golden Dawn scale | Gimel colour |
|---|---|---|
| Atziluth | King | Blue |
| Briah | Queen | Silver or silvery white |
| Yetzirah | Prince | Very cold pale blue |
| Assiah | Princess | Silver rayed sky-blue |

A modern online transcription of *777* incorrectly prints “brilliant flame” for the Prince scale; Golden Dawn colour tables give **very cold pale blue**. Compare the [Whare Ra colour papers](https://hermetic.com/gdlibrary/whare_ra/6-5_preparation) and [Golden Dawn colour-table reproduction](https://pdfcoffee.com/coloring-the-classic-golden-dawntarot-pdf-free.html).

### The Golden Dawn High Priestess image

In the Golden Dawn/Mathers design, she is a lunar priestess before or within a veiled sanctuary. Later designs accentuate different details:

- Pamela Colman Smith: seated between black and white pillars, Torah scroll, lunar crown, veil of palms and pomegranates;
- Frieda Harris: a more abstract veiled goddess, bow, light lattice, crystals, fruits, and camel;
- BOTA: subconsciousness, memory, lunar fluidity, and balanced pillars.

These are related but not interchangeable iconographies.

## 8. Westcott and the thirteenth intelligence

Westcott’s translation spread “Uniting Intelligence” throughout English occultism. The expression was subsequently treated as if it were the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah*’s own name for the Kircher line.

**[Disputed/clarified]** The phrase is a real translated text, but from the appended “Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom,” not from the core clauses classifying Gimel as a double. Westcott’s combination of the two textual layers facilitated their later fusion.

## 9. Aleister Crowley

### *Liber 777* (1909)

Crowley’s row 13 preserves the Golden Dawn system:

- Hebrew: גמל;
- name: Gimel;
- meaning: camel;
- line: 1–6;
- intelligence: Uniting;
- tarot: High Priestess;
- astrology: Luna.

The accessible [row-by-row transcription of *777*](https://hermeticus.info/doku.php?id=table_01) should be checked against printed editions because online versions contain transcription errors.

### *Liber LVIII*

Crowley reads GML as 73 and treats Gimel as the path joining Kether and Tiphareth, corresponding to the “Priestess of the Silver Star.” [*Liber LVIII*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib58)

### *The Book of Thoth*

Crowley’s Priestess retains Gimel and the Moon. He emphasizes that the path joins the supernal source to the solar hexad and occupies the precise central balance of the Tree. [*The Book of Thoth* in *Equinox* III.5](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/crowley/equinox/volume3/vol_3_no_05.pdf)

Crowley differs from the original Golden Dawn deck artistically and Thelemically, but not in the primary Gimel–Moon–Priestess attribution.

### *Liber 231*

The Gimel/Atu II utterance is the verse numbered 2:

> “Now hath Nuit veiled herself, that she may open the gate of her sister.”

[*Liber CCXXXI*](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/publication/english/Liber_CCXXXI.html)

The accompanying second series supplies qliphotic genii and sigils. Kenneth Grant later turned those genii into the guardians of twenty-two “Tunnels of Set.”

### The Tzaddi/Heh question

*Liber AL vel Legis* I:57 declares, “All these old letters of my Book are aright; but צ is not the Star.”

Crowley eventually exchanged the tarot associations of Heh and Tzaddi:

- Emperor ↔ Star;
- Heh ↔ Tzaddi.

**Finding:** this change does not alter Gimel, Moon, or the Priestess. Claims that Crowley “changed the Path of Gimel” through the Tzaddi reform are mistaken.

### Thelemic grades

No formal A∴A∴ grade is assigned solely to Gimel. In the standard grade-sefira scheme:

- 5°=6□, Adeptus Minor: Tiphareth;
- 7°=4□, Adeptus Exemptus: Chesed;
- Babe of the Abyss: transitional state;
- 8°=3□, Magister Templi: Binah;
- 9°=2□: Chokhmah;
- 10°=1□: Kether.

**[Thelemic interpretation]** Gimel symbolizes a direct Middle-Pillar transit from the solar centre toward the Crown and therefore the crossing of the Abyss, but it is not the ordinary sequential grade route from Tiphareth directly to Kether.

## 10. Dion Fortune

*The Mystical Qabalah* was published in book form in 1935 from earlier articles.

**Negative finding:** there is no chapter devoted to Gimel. Its contents contain chapters on the Tree, the paths generally, and each of the ten sefirot, not twenty-two separate path chapters. The relevant discussion is principally chapter X, “The Paths upon the Tree,” and the chapters on Kether and Tiphareth. [Contents and text](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/ebooks/previews/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah.pdf)

Fortune’s path method treats the paths as channels of divine influence and as stages to be harmonized in the practitioner’s consciousness. Her discussion helped popularize psychological pathworking, but a purported “Dion Fortune chapter on the Path of Gimel” is a bibliographic error, sometimes caused by conflation with later books based on her system.

## 11. Israel Regardie

Regardie published the Golden Dawn papers between 1937 and 1940 and thereby made the system widely available. [*The Complete Golden Dawn*](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/R/Regardie%20-%20The%20Complete%20Golden%20Dawn.pdf)

In *A Garden of Pomegranates*, Gimel is:

- camel;
- Moon;
- High Priestess;
- Kether–Tiphareth;
- the central path bearing supernal influence toward the solar centre.

[*A Garden of Pomegranates*](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06Numerologia_Cabala/Isreael_Regardie_A_Garden_Of_Pomegranates_2Nd%20Ed.pdf)

## 12. Paul Foster Case

Case retained the Golden Dawn Gimel–Moon–High Priestess correspondence in BOTA. He emphasized:

- subconsciousness and memory;
- receptive yet active mediation;
- lunar rhythm;
- the veil between self-consciousness and superconsciousness;
- the path’s activation of Beauty/Tiferet.

See Case and Michael Whitty, [“A Dissertation Concerning the Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom”](https://hermetics.net/media-library/kabbalah/paul-foster-case-and-michael-whitty-a-dissertation-concerning-the-thirty-two-paths-of-wisdom/).

## 13. Gareth Knight

Knight’s *Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism*, first published in the 1960s, interprets the upper paths through initiatory psychology. Gimel is the lunar passage into the supernal darkness, frequently described through the camel/desert and priestess/veil complexes.

This is mature twentieth-century Hermetic path symbolism, not a recovery of an ancient Jewish rite. [Bibliographic preview](https://books.google.com/books?id=Tw-B_Hzcp9MC)

## 14. Pathworking traditions

**[Modern Hermetic practice]** Pathworking as a structured guided visualization is principally a nineteenth- and twentieth-century occult development. Typical practice includes:

1. preliminary banishing or ritual protection;
2. contemplation or visualization of ג;
3. use of the path’s colour;
4. entry through the High Priestess card as a symbolic “door”;
5. lunar landscape, temple, veil, desert, water, or astral imagery;
6. encounter with a guide or priestess figure;
7. ascent toward Kether or return toward Tiphareth;
8. deliberate retracing and closing;
9. recording in a magical diary.

Regardie and later Cicero materials formalized such work. See [*Skrying on the Tree of Life*](https://logoilibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/skrying_on_the_tree_of_life-tabatha_cicero.pdf).

**Evidentiary caution:** pathworking reports are experiential literature. Similar imagery may result from shared instructions, tarot pictures, expectations, and group culture; it does not independently prove ancient continuity or objective geography.

# The Qliphah or tunnel

## The classical Jewish background

Jewish kabbalistic sources discuss *qelippot*, “shells” or “husks,” forces of obstruction, impurity, judgment, or estrangement. Their systems are not uniform.

**Finding:** classical Jewish Kabbalah contains no entity called “Gargophias, demon of the thirteenth Path of Gimel.” That is a twentieth-century Typhonian construction.

## Golden Dawn and Crowley qliphoth

The Golden Dawn and Crowley tabulated ten qliphotic orders opposite the ten sefirot. Spellings vary substantially:

| Sefira | Common qliphah name | Typical interpretation |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 Kether | Thaumiel | Contending or dual heads |
| 2 Chokhmah | Ghagiel/Chaigidel | Hinderers/confusion |
| 3 Binah | Satariel | Concealers |
| 4 Chesed | Gha’agsheblah/Gamchicoth | Devourers/disturbers |
| 5 Geburah | Golachab | Burners |
| 6 Tiphareth | Thagirion | Disputers, false or distorted beauty |
| 7 Netzach | A’arab Zaraq/Harab Serapel | Ravens of dispersion |
| 8 Hod | Samael | Poison of God |
| 9 Yesod | Gamaliel | Obscene or distorted lunar images |
| 10 Malkuth | Lilith/Nahemoth | Night spectres/material shells |

See the Golden Dawn qliphoth paper reproduced at [Thelemagick Library](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html) and Crowley’s [*Liber 777* scan](https://astrumargentum.org/ht/libri/Liber_777.pdf).

### Demon princes

Later tables commonly attach:

- Thaumiel: Satan and Moloch;
- Ghagiel: Beelzebub;
- Satariel: Lucifuge Rofocale;
- Gha’agsheblah: Astaroth;
- Golachab: Asmodeus;
- Thagirion: Belphegor;
- A’arab Zaraq: Baal;
- Samael: Adrammelech;
- Gamaliel: Lilith;
- Nahemoth/Lilith: Naamah.

**[Disputed/modern synthesis]** This neat one-prince-per-sphere series is not a uniform Jewish or Golden Dawn list. It combines Crowley’s infernal rulers, older demonologies, later occult tables, and shifting spellings. It should be cited as a modern synthetic hierarchy, not as “the Kabbalistic doctrine.”

## Kenneth Grant’s Gargophias

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* appeared in 1977. He treated the reverse of the Tree’s twenty-two paths as the “Tunnels of Set.”

For path 13:

| Field | Grant’s attribution |
|---|---|
| Tunnel | **Gargophias** |
| Letter | Gimel |
| Obverse path | Kether–Tiphareth |
| Nightside endpoints | Thaumiel–Thagirion |
| Trump | High Priestess |
| Planetary character | Lunar |
| Sigil colours | Silver on black |
| Number assigned by Grant | 393 |
| Sound/key | G-sharp |
| Major imagery | Kala, blood, pearl/oyster, Hecate/Diana, dream and nightmare, lunar sexuality, Typhonian goddess symbolism |

Grant begins the chapter:

> “The Thirteenth path is charged with the lunar kala.”

Kenneth Grant, *Nightside of Eden*, chapter “Gargophias.” [Searchable PDF](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf)

**[Modern invention]** Grant’s numerical and sexual interpretations combine Crowley, Egyptian mythology as understood by Gerald Massey, tantra, Lovecraftian or Typhonian imagery, and Grant’s own gematria. They are not historical findings about Egyptian religion or Jewish Kabbalah.

### Gargophias versus Gamaliel

These are often confused:

- **Gargophias**: Grant’s tunnel corresponding to Gimel/path 13.
- **Gamaliel**: qliphah of Yesod, the ninth sphere.

Gamaliel may seem “Gimel-like” because it is lunar, but assigning Gamaliel as the qliphah “of Gimel” confuses a sphere with a path. Grant’s own tunnel table gives Gimel to Gargophias. [Tunnel table](https://www.qliphoth-archive.com/en/correspondences.html)

# People, works, diagrams, and cultural reception

## Jewish authors and transmitters

### Saadia Gaon

His early-tenth-century commentary is one of the first secure witnesses to *Sefer Yetzirah*. His recension retains the old planetary order in which Gimel belongs with Jupiter.

### Shabbetai Donnolo

Donnolo’s tenth-century commentary is another early witness to the book’s cosmological and astrological reception.

### Judah ben Barzillai

His extensive medieval commentary preserves traditions and textual variants important for reconstructing *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### Abraham Abulafia

Abulafia’s prophetic Kabbalah made intensive meditative use of Hebrew letters, permutations, breathing, and divine names. It does not supply the later Kircher Gimel path but is crucial background for practical letter contemplation.

### Joseph Gikatilla

Gikatilla’s divine-name and sefirotic synthesis passed into Christian Cabala through *Portae Lucis*.

### Moses Cordovero

Cordovero systematized sefirot, channels, names, colours, and letter permutations before Luria.

### Isaac Luria and Hayyim Vital

Their cosmology transformed the meaning of divine structure through tzimtzum, shattering, partzufim, and tikkun. Later occultists frequently borrowed Lurianic language while retaining Kircher’s incompatible path geometry.

### Elijah, Gaon of Vilna

His redaction and commentary underlie the influential Gra/Ari-Gra reconstruction in which Gimel is not Kether–Tiferet.

### Shneur Zalman and Rabbi Nachman

Both are important to Hasidic interiorization of sefirotic and letter symbolism, but neither teaches the Golden Dawn Path of Gimel.

## Christian Cabalists and occultists

- Pico della Mirandola: Christian Cabala program, 1486.
- Johannes Reuchlin: Hebrew letters and Cabalistic philosophy, 1517.
- Paulus Ricius: *Portae Lucis*, 1516.
- Athanasius Kircher: decisive printed letter-path layout, 1652–1654.
- Knorr von Rosenroth: Latin transmission of Zoharic and Lurianic sources, 1677–1684.
- Éliphas Lévi: letters and tarot, but Gimel–Empress rather than Gimel–Priestess.
- Papus: popularized French occult Kabbalah and tarot correspondences.
- W. Wynn Westcott: *Sepher Yetzirah* and “Uniting Intelligence.”
- S. L. MacGregor Mathers: Golden Dawn synthesis and *Kabbalah Unveiled*.
- Aleister Crowley: *777*, *Liber 231*, *Book of Thoth*.
- Dion Fortune: psychological and initiatory Tree interpretation.
- Israel Regardie: publication and systematization of Golden Dawn material.
- Paul Foster Case: BOTA tarot and subconsciousness interpretation.
- Gareth Knight: later initiatory/pathworking synthesis.
- Kenneth Grant: Gargophias and the Nightside tunnel.

## Scholars

- Gershom Scholem established modern historical study of Kabbalah and argued for the medieval composition of the Zohar.
- Moshe Idel challenged overly linear models and emphasized ecstatic, linguistic, and experiential forms of Kabbalah.
- Elliot R. Wolfson analyzed gender, language, embodiment, vision, and eros in kabbalistic symbolism.
- Joseph Dan surveyed the historical plurality of Jewish mystical movements.
- Peter Hayman produced the critical synoptic *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- Daniel C. Matt produced the annotated Pritzker Zohar translation.
- J. H. Chajes and the Ilanot Project study kabbalistic diagrams as historically variable artifacts rather than one timeless Tree.

## Artists

### Hans Burgkmair and *Portae Lucis*

The title-page image is associated with the earliest printed sefirotic tree, although attribution and design responsibility require caution.

### Pamela Colman Smith

Her High Priestess in the Rider–Waite–Smith deck—pillars, veil, Torah, pomegranates, lunar crown—became the most influential popular image used in Gimel pathworking. Waite’s deck is Golden Dawn-derived but not identical to the order’s original tarot papers.

### Lady Frieda Harris

Her Thoth Priestess visually embodies Crowley’s Gimel doctrine: veil, lunar geometry, bow, crystal forms, fruit, and camel.

### Steffi Grant

Her artwork and sigil renderings became integral to Kenneth Grant’s Typhonian presentation of the tunnels.

## Poets and novelists

W. B. Yeats and Arthur Machen participated in Golden Dawn culture, while Dion Fortune and Aleister Crowley wrote occult fiction informed by the Tree. Evidence that any particular poem or novel was designed specifically as a working of Path 13 must be established work by work. General membership in an occult order is not proof of a Gimel-specific composition.

# Diagrams and manuscripts

## Medieval Jewish ilanot

The earliest kabbalistic trees were not standardized Golden Dawn diagrams. Late-thirteenth- and fourteenth-century manuscripts experimented with circles, concentric forms, branching trees, bodies, chains, and networks. The University of Haifa’s [Ilanot Database](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/Ilanot_Site/database.html) documents this diversity.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Developed arboreal ilanot became especially prominent in the fourteenth century. Their channels could carry descriptions, divine names, processes, or relationships, but did not universally bear the twenty-two letters in Kircher’s order.

## *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Probably the first printed sefirotic tree. It has connecting lines but no complete Hebrew-letter path attribution.

## *Pardes Rimmonim*, Kraków 1592

The printed *Pardes* helped disseminate a Jewish three-column sefirotic arrangement. Its diagrammatic tradition is an important possible background to Christian trees, but it does not establish the Golden Dawn Gimel line.

## Kircher’s plate

Kircher’s plate is the decisive early printed witness for Gimel on Kether–Tiphareth.

## Lurianic trees

Lurianic ilanot visualize worlds, partzufim, faces, skulls, lights, vessels, and interpenetrating structures. Reducing these cosmologies to one ten-circle Kircher diagram loses much of their distinctive architecture.

## Golden Dawn coloured trees

Golden Dawn temples and manuscripts used trees coloured in four scales corresponding to Atziluth, Briah, Yetzirah, and Assiah. These are ritual-magical diagrams, not reproductions of medieval Jewish illumination.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Dating *Sefer Yetzirah*

| Position | Status |
|---|---|
| Authored by Abraham | Jewish traditional attribution |
| Transmitted or authored by Rabbi Akiva | Traditional attribution |
| Composed in late antiquity | Major scholarly family of hypotheses |
| Seventh-century setting | Modern argument supported by Syriac linguistic evidence |
| Text stabilized only near the tenth century | Strong manuscript/reception evidence |
| Current printed text represents one original | Not sustainable; multiple recensions exist |

The safest statement is that the work contains late-antique cosmological and linguistic materials but becomes securely visible in its varied textual forms around the tenth century.

## 2. Zoharic authorship

The traditional Shimon bar Yochai attribution and the scholarly Moses de León/Castilian account belong to different evidentiary frameworks. Modern scholars also recognize sources, interpolations, later strata, and possibly multiple hands. “Written entirely by de León in one operation” is too simple; “historically proven second-century transcript” is likewise unsupported.

## 3. Christian Cabala as transmission and appropriation

Christian Cabalists preserved, translated, printed, and disseminated Jewish material. They also:

- detached texts from halakhic and Jewish ritual settings;
- used Kabbalah to prove Christian doctrines;
- recoded sefirot as Trinitarian or Christological mysteries;
- introduced philological errors;
- treated Jewish texts as incomplete anticipations of Christianity.

These are not mutually exclusive judgments: the tradition was both a major vehicle of textual transmission and an appropriative transformation.

## 4. Kircher versus Ari and Gra

There is no single, timeless “correct Tree” on which all kabbalists agreed.

| System | Gimel connection |
|---|---|
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Kether–Tiphareth |
| Common Ari reconstruction | Binah–Gevurah |
| Common Gra reconstruction | Chokhmah–Chesed |

Kircher’s layout optimizes visual and sequential order. Ari/Gra reconstructions attempt to preserve the structural letter classes. Which is “correct” depends upon whether one is reconstructing Jewish textual principles or practicing a particular Hermetic system.

## 5. Gimel’s planet

This is the most important attribution dispute:

- common early *Sefer Yetzirah*: Jupiter;
- Gra: Mars;
- Golden Dawn: Moon.

Therefore Gimel’s Moon attribution cannot be cited as “what the *Sefer Yetzirah* says.” It is what the Golden Dawn says while adapting the *Sefer Yetzirah*’s seven-double framework.

## 6. Tarot attribution

- Lévi: Gimel–Empress;
- Golden Dawn: Gimel–High Priestess;
- Crowley: retains Gimel–Priestess;
- Crowley’s Tzaddi/Heh reform: does not affect Gimel.

No evidence connects Hebrew letters with the original fifteenth-century manufacture of tarot cards.

## 7. The “Uniting Intelligence”

The title is textually attested in the later “Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom” appendix. Its application simultaneously to Gimel and the Kether–Tiphareth line depends on the occult numbering system. It is not proof that the earliest *Sefer Yetzirah* named Gimel “Uniting Intelligence.”

## 8. Daʿat

**[Text]** *Sefer Yetzirah* insists: ten, not nine; ten, not eleven.

Jewish Kabbalah may speak of Daʿat as:

- knowledge;
- the union or mediation of Chokhmah and Binah;
- a state in which Keter is not separately counted;
- a configuration in particular partzufic conditions.

It is not normally an eleventh independent sefirah added to a permanent set of ten.

**[Hermetic attribution]** Golden Dawn and Thelemic diagrams locate Daʿat in the Abyss and interpret Gimel as passing through that region. This is not the same as a classical Jewish assertion that a lunar path literally pierces an eleventh sphere.

## 9. The camel crossing the Abyss

This is symbolically elegant:

- camel = Gimel;
- desert = Abyss;
- stored water = preserved consciousness or grace;
- Tiphareth-to-Kether = initiatory crossing.

But the image is a later Hermetic interpretation. It is absent from the earliest letter texts, Talmud, *Bahir*, and classical Zohar passages examined here.

## 10. “Dion Fortune’s chapter on Gimel”

No such chapter exists in *The Mystical Qabalah*. Fortune discusses paths generally and treats the ten sefirot individually. Dedicated Gimel chapters belong to other, later pathworking books.

## 11. “Gamaliel is the qliphah of Gimel”

This is a category mistake. Gamaliel is the qliphah of Yesod. Gargophias is Grant’s tunnel corresponding to Gimel.

## 12. Contested-material audit

Searches combining “Path of Gimel” with *dispute*, *dating*, *attribution*, *Kircher*, *Golden Dawn*, *Ari*, and *Gra* produced several recurring unsupported claims:

1. **“The Sefer Yetzirah places Gimel between Kether and Tiphareth.”**  
   No: the core text does not supply that diagram.

2. **“Gimel is traditionally the Moon.”**  
   Only in the Golden Dawn and descendants; early recensions give Jupiter.

3. **“Kircher invented the Tree of Life.”**  
   No: Jewish ilanot and printed trees predate him. His particular letter layout is what matters here.

4. **“The Golden Dawn simply copied Kircher.”**  
   Incomplete: it retained the geometry but added tarot, astrology, colour, and ritual.

5. **“The Ari recension is a single autograph text by Luria.”**  
   Unsupported: “Ari” and “Ari-Gra” label later redactions and reconstructions.

6. **“Gargophias is an ancient qliphah.”**  
   No: it is Grant’s twentieth-century tunnel guardian, developed from Crowleyan sigils.

7. **“Pathworking accounts demonstrate an ancient initiatory practice.”**  
   No continuous documentation establishes that lineage.

# Open questions

1. **The earliest date of the Hebrew “Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom.”**  
   Its textual history before the early-modern Rittangelius printing needs clearer manuscript mapping.

2. **Kircher’s immediate source for the exact letter sequence.**  
   Earlier Christian and Jewish diagrams supplied much of his material, but the precise chain behind every line remains incompletely reconstructed.

3. **The authority of named “Ari trees.”**  
   Modern diagrams should be traced to particular printed editions and manuscripts rather than attributed generically to Luria.

4. **Gimel’s Proto-Sinaitic picture-name.**  
   Camel, camel-neck, throwing stick, and related proposals remain debated.

5. **The seven doubles’ original phonology.**  
   The inclusion of Resh and the history of soft/hard pronunciation require analysis beyond the later six-letter *begadkefat* rule.

6. **The first explicit “camel crosses the Abyss” formulation.**  
   The image is widespread in twentieth-century occultism, but its first precisely datable printed formulation still warrants bibliographic research.

7. **Grant’s derivation of Gargophias.**  
   Crowley supplies the sigil/genius series, but the etymology of the name and Grant’s exact transformation of it into a tunnel guardian remain opaque.

8. **Local manuscript variation.**  
   Planet, day, bodily gate, and polarity should always be cited from a specified *Sefer Yetzirah* manuscript or recension rather than from an undifferentiated “tradition.”

# Conclusions

The fullest historically responsible account consists of several superimposed but distinct Gimels.

### Jewish Gimel

The Jewish textual Gimel is:

- the third Hebrew letter;
- a “double” in *Sefer Yetzirah*;
- ordinarily Jupiter in early recensions and Mars in the Gra recension;
- a sign of bestowal, recompense, growth, and sustaining generosity;
- paired with Dalet in Talmudic, *Bahiric*, and Zoharic lore;
- a creative letter with planetary, temporal, and bodily dimensions;
- not securely assigned to one universal line on a sefirotic tree.

### Christian-Cabalistic Gimel

Christian Cabala transmitted the letter, Hebrew cosmology, divine names, and Tree diagrams into Latin. Kircher’s decisive contribution was the printed geometry putting Gimel on Kether–Tiphareth. Tarot and lunar priestess symbolism were not yet present.

### Golden Dawn Gimel

The Golden Dawn created the classic modern identity:

```text
Gimel
Path 13
Kether–Tiphareth
Moon
High Priestess
Uniting Intelligence
Blue / silver / pale blue
```

That system is internally coherent and historically influential, but composite.

### Thelemic and Nightside Gimel

Crowley deepened the priestess, veil, camel, Abyss, and lunar symbolism. Kenneth Grant reversed the line into the tunnel of Gargophias between Thaumiel and Thagirion. The latter is a twentieth-century Typhonian mythology, not an ancient Jewish qliphah.

The historical task is consequently not to choose one attribution and call every other one corrupt. It is to identify which textual community made each association, when it entered the record, and what earlier material it transformed.

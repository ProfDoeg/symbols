# The Tree of Life: Research Dossier

## Basic identification

| Field | Identification |
|---|---|
| Hebrew name | עֵץ חַיִּים; normally printed unpointed as עץ חיים |
| Transliteration | *ʿēṣ ḥayyīm*; common forms: *Etz Chaim*, *Etz Hayyim*, *Ets Ḥayyim* |
| Literal meaning | “Tree of Life”; grammatically, “tree of living/life,” with plural-form חַיִּים used as an abstract noun |
| Object treated here | The entire sefirotic system and its diagrams—not one Sephirah or one letter-path |
| Basic number | Ten Sephiroth plus twenty-two Hebrew letters: thirty-two constituents in the later diagrammatic synthesis |
| Position | The whole articulated order, from Keter to Malkhut; Daʿat is conditionally represented but is not normally an eleventh coequal Sephirah |
| Classical Jewish form | Multiple textual and visual arrangements; there is no single canonical geometry |
| Standard Hermetic form | Ten circles in three columns connected by twenty-two lettered paths, descended principally from early-modern Christian Cabala and systematized by the Golden Dawn |
| Divine name | None singular. The entire Tree includes the ten principal divine-name correspondences |
| Archangel/order/planet | None singular. Those are distributed among the ten Sephiroth in Hermetic tables |
| Tarot trump | None singular. The twenty-two trumps are assigned to the twenty-two connecting paths in the Golden Dawn system |
| Colour | None singular. The Golden Dawn gives every Sephirah and path four colours, corresponding to Atziluth, Briah, Yetzirah and Assiah |
| Qliphah/tunnel | None singular. The late Hermetic “averse Tree” contains ten Qliphoth and, in Kenneth Grant’s system, twenty-two tunnels |
| Principal caution | The *Sefer Yetzirah* supplies ten *sefirot belimah* and twenty-two letters, but not the familiar ten-circle/twenty-two-line picture. That identification is a later reconstruction. |

### Evidentiary labels

- **[DT] Documented text:** stated directly in the cited primary source.
- **[SR] Scholarly reconstruction:** a historical or philological conclusion based on manuscripts, language or material evidence.
- **[KT] Kabbalistic tradition:** a received Jewish attribution, whether or not historically demonstrable.
- **[HO] Hermetic-occult attribution:** an operative correspondence taught in Christian Cabala, the Golden Dawn, Thelema or related schools.
- **[D] Disputed:** competing sources or interpretations exist.
- **[MI] Modern invention/development:** a demonstrably recent system, even if constructed from older materials.

## The name

### Biblical foundations

The expression עֵץ חַיִּים is biblical, but the Bible does not contain the later sefirotic diagram.

1. **Genesis 2:9; 3:22–24 — [DT].** The primordial עֵץ הַחַיִּים, “tree of life,” stands in Eden. After the transgression, access to it is guarded. Later Jewish and Christian interpreters made Eden, its two trees and its river into major symbolic frameworks.

2. **Proverbs 3:18 — [DT].** Wisdom is called:

   > עֵץ־חַיִּים הִיא לַמַּחֲזִיקִים בָּהּ  
   > “She is a tree of life to those who grasp her.”

   In Jewish reception, “she” is Wisdom and, increasingly, Torah. This verse—not Proverbs 3:19—is the direct lexical source for the name *Etz Chaim*. [Proverbs 3:18](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.18?lang=bi)

3. **Proverbs 3:19 — [DT/KT].** “YHWH founded the earth by wisdom; He established the heavens by understanding.” Kabbalists read חָכְמָה and תְּבוּנָה as resonant with Chokhmah and Binah. In the biblical poem, however, they are divine wisdom and understanding, not labels on a diagram. [Proverbs 3:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19?lang=bi)

4. **Job 28 — [DT/KT].** Job repeatedly asks, `וְהַחָכְמָה מֵאַיִן תִּמָּצֵא`—“From where shall wisdom be found?” Kabbalists could hear in מֵאַיִן, “from where/nothing,” an allusion to *Ayin* and the emergence of Chokhmah; the chapter also couples חכמה and בינה. That sefirotic reading is interpretive, not Job’s historical-literal sense. [Job 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28?lang=bi)

5. **1 Chronicles 29:11 — [DT/KT].** The verse gives the verbal sequence later used for the seven lower Sephiroth:

   > הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד … הַמַּמְלָכָה

   Kabbalistic parsing reads Gedullah/Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, “all” (*kol*) as Yesod, and Mamlakhah as Malkhut. The biblical verse itself is a royal doxology. [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11?lang=bi)

6. **Isaiah — [DT/KT].** There is no single Isaiah “Tree of Life” proof-text. Several passages enter the system:

   - Isaiah 11:2 supplies wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge and fear.
   - Isaiah 57:15 supplies language reproduced in *Sefer Yetzirah*: “high and exalted, dwelling eternally, holy is His name.”
   - Isaiah 43:7—“created … formed … made”—is used in discussions of Briah, Yetzirah and Assiah.
   - Isaiah 6:3 supplies the threefold *Qadosh* used in sefirotic and angelological interpretation.

### How the name changes meaning

- **[DT] Bahir and Zoharic literature:** “tree” may mean a divine configuration, a central channel, Torah, a cosmic structure or specifically Tiferet.
- **[DT] Gikatilla:** Tiferet is called עץ החיים because Binah, “life,” is united to it. [*Shaʿarei Orah*, Fifth Gate](https://www.sefaria.org.il/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Fifth_Gate%2C_Sixth_Sefirah.54)
- **[DT] Cordovero:** “tree” without qualification is frequently Tiferet; “Tree of Life” can mean Tiferet specifically and Atziluth collectively. [*Pardes Rimmonim* 23](https://www.sefaria.org.il/Pardes_Rimmonim.23.16.68)
- **[DT/KT] Vital:** *Etz Chaim* is the title given to the great redaction of Lurianic cosmology. Here it names a book and a total dynamic system, not merely the familiar flat glyph.
- **[HO] Christian and Hermetic usage:** *arbor*, *arbor vitae*, *arbor sephirotica*, “Tree of Life,” “Tree of the Sephiroth,” and transliterations such as *Otz Chiim* become names for the diagram.

### Latin and occult nomenclature

| Author/work | Actual or characteristic usage |
|---|---|
| Pico, *Conclusiones* (1486) | Discusses the *decem numerationes* and individual Latin sefirotic glosses; no surviving standard “Tree of Life” plate |
| Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica* (1517) | *Cabala*, divine names, letters and *Sephiroth*; not yet the later Kircher/Golden Dawn glyph |
| Riccio/Gikatilla, *Portae Lucis* (1516) | “Gates of Light”; title page supplies the first printed sefirotic tree |
| Kircher, *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54) | *Systema Sephiroticum*; the plate is commonly called Kircher’s Tree |
| Knorr von Rosenroth, *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–84) | *arbores seu tabulae* and related diagrammatic language; numerous Lurianic configurations |
| Golden Dawn | “Tree of Life,” “Sephirotic Tree,” and *Otz Chiim* |
| Crowley | “Tree of Life,” “Qabalistic Tree,” *Otz Chiim*; the master index of *Liber 777* |

## The Jewish tradition: dated and sourced

## 1. The *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date and textual state

- **[KT]** Traditionally attributed to Abraham.
- **[SR/D]** Its date remains unsettled. Proposals range from the early rabbinic period to the early Islamic age.
- Gershom Scholem generally located its core in late antiquity.
- A. Peter Hayman emphasizes the fluid manuscript tradition and the fact that the work emerges into securely datable light with tenth-century commentaries.
- Tzahi Weiss has argued for a seventh-century Syriac-Christian linguistic and intellectual setting. This is an important proposal, not an uncontested closure of the debate. [Hayman’s critical edition](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/); [2023 discussion of Weiss](https://brill.com/view/journals/ic/27/4-5/article-p407_9.pdf)

Hayman’s edition prints the Short, Long and Saadyan recensions from nineteen selected manuscripts. Saadia’s commentary dates to about 931; Dunash ibn Tamim’s to 955/56. The later “Gra recension” is a Kabbalistically revised recension associated with the Vilna Gaon’s commentary, not an independent ancient manuscript family of equal status.

### The thirty-two paths

*Sefer Yetzirah* 1:1 begins:

> בִּשְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם נְתִיבוֹת פְּלִיאוֹת חָכְמָה  
> “By thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom…”

It then defines the total as:

- ten *sefirot belimah*;
- twenty-two foundation letters;
- three mothers;
- seven doubles;
- twelve simples.  
  [*Sefer Yetzirah* 1:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.1?lang=bi&with=Kabbalah)

**Critical finding:** the text does not say “ten circles plus twenty-two lines between them.” Its letters and Sephiroth are co-ordinate instruments of creation. Turning every letter into a connector between two named later Sephiroth is a medieval-to-early-modern diagrammatic interpretation.

### *Sefirot belimah*

בלימה (*belimah*) is obscure. Suggested senses include:

- “without what/without substance”;
- “restrained,” based on Job 26:7’s “hangs the earth upon nothing”;
- ineffable or non-material numerical principles.

The work commands the reader to restrain mouth and heart and insists: “ten and not nine; ten and not eleven.” Its ten are not yet the later Keter–Malkhut list. They include spirit, spirit from spirit, water from spirit, fire from water and the six spatial directions. [*Sefer Yetzirah* 1:13](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.13)

### The letters

| Class | Letters | Explicit *Sefer Yetzirah* domain |
|---|---|---|
| Three mothers | א מ ש | Air/spirit, water, fire; balance in world, year and human |
| Seven doubles | ב ג ד כ פ ר ת | Seven planets, days, apertures of the head and paired opposites |
| Twelve simples | ה ו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק | Zodiac, months and twelve bodily faculties/directors |

The mother/planet/zodiac scheme is textual. Its exact letter-to-planet and organ assignments vary between recensions. [Westcott’s historical translation](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/yetzirah.htm); [Gra recension 5](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.5.3?lang=bi)

### Recensional variants

| Version | Character |
|---|---|
| Short | One of the two principal medieval recensions; known through manuscripts and Dunash’s commentary |
| Long | Expanded arrangement transmitted with early commentaries |
| Saadyan | Saadia’s reordered Arabic recension and commentary |
| Ari recension | Kabbalistically corrected form attributed to Isaac Luria’s school |
| Gra recension | The Vilna Gaon’s Kabbalistic textual arrangement, familiar through later printings |

A particularly consequential variant concerns the seven doubles. The Short/Long/Saadyan tradition commonly follows the geocentric order:

- Bet—Saturn
- Gimel—Jupiter
- Dalet—Mars
- Kaf—Sun
- Pe—Venus
- Resh—Mercury
- Tav—Moon

The Gra system instead gives:

- Bet—Moon
- Gimel—Mars
- Dalet—Sun
- Kaf—Venus
- Pe—Mercury
- Resh—Saturn
- Tav—Jupiter

The Golden Dawn gives yet another sequence: Bet–Mercury, Gimel–Moon, Dalet–Venus, Kaf–Jupiter, Pe–Mars, Resh–Sun, Tav–Saturn. Consequently, “the *Sefer Yetzirah* attribution” must always identify its recension.

### The thirty-two “Intelligences”

The familiar list in which the First Path is the “Admirable or Concealed Intelligence,” the Second “Illuminating,” and so forth is **not part of the early *Sefer Yetzirah***. In Westcott it appears explicitly as an appendix, “The Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom.” [Westcott/Sacred Texts](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/yetzirah.htm)

Its conventional sequence is:

1. Admirable/Concealed  
2. Illuminating  
3. Sanctifying  
4. Cohesive or Receptacular  
5. Radical/Root  
6. Mediating  
7. Hidden  
8. Perfect  
9. Pure  
10. Resplendent  
11. Scintillating  
12. Transparent  
13. Uniting  
14. Illuminating  
15. Constituting  
16. Triumphal/Eternal  
17. Disposing  
18. House of Influence  
19. Intelligence of the Secret Activities  
20. Will  
21. Conciliation  
22. Faithful  
23. Stable  
24. Imaginative  
25. Probation  
26. Renovating  
27. Exciting  
28. Natural  
29. Corporeal  
30. Collecting  
31. Perpetual  
32. Administrative

**[D]** The list’s medieval Hebrew textual history and its precise relationship to the diagram require separate manuscript study. Occult books often misidentify it as the text of *Sefer Yetzirah* itself.

## 2. The *Bahir*: late twelfth century

- **[KT]** Attributed to the tanna Neḥunya ben ha-Qanah.
- **[SR]** The work appeared in Provence between approximately 1150 and 1200, apparently incorporating older eastern materials. [Encyclopaedia Judaica summary](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0011_0_10514.html)

The *Bahir* transforms numerical and letter cosmology into symbolic, mythic relationships. Section 120 identifies the heart, לב = 32, with thirty-two paths and gives the parable of a king’s thirty-two chambers:

> “A heart is thirty-two … with them the world was created.”

This is a conceptual map of creation, knowledge and royal access—not a surviving twenty-two-line Tree diagram. [*Sefer ha-Bahir* 120](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.120)

The *Bahir* also develops:

- ten divine utterances/powers;
- male and female symbolism;
- the righteous as a conduit;
- the cosmic tree whose roots and branches invert ordinary physical expectation;
- the association of divine potencies with body, commandments and letters.

## 3. Gerona and Castile: thirteenth century

Early theosophical Kabbalah stabilized the familiar names and dynamic relations of the ten Sephiroth. Yet writers differed about whether the Sephiroth were divine essences, instruments, names, attributes, garments, lights or powers.

Joseph Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*, composed in the late thirteenth century, organizes ascent through ten gates and divine names. In ascending order:

| Sephirah | Principal Gikatillan name |
|---|---|
| Malkhut | אדני, Adonai |
| Yesod | אל חי / שדי, El Ḥai / Shaddai |
| Hod | אלהים צבאות, Elohim Tzevaot |
| Netzach | יהוה צבאות, YHWH Tzevaot |
| Tiferet | יהוה, YHWH |
| Gevurah | אלהים, Elohim |
| Chesed | אל, El |
| Binah | YHWH pronounced as Elohim |
| Chokhmah | יה, Yah |
| Keter | אהיה, Ehyeh |

Gikatilla calls YHWH the trunk to which other names attach like branches and places YHWH on the middle line between El on the right and Elohim on the left. [*Shaʿarei Orah*, Gates 1–5](https://www.sefaria.org/Sheet.146165); [Fifth Gate 85–87](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Fifth_Gate%2C_Sixth_Sefirah.85?with=all)

This is a strong medieval source for the divine-name array. It is not a source for the complete Golden Dawn archangel, tarot, virtue/vice and colour tables.

## 4. The Zohar: late thirteenth century and later strata

### Authorship

- **[KT]** Revealed by Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle in the second century.
- **[SR]** The main corpus appeared in late-thirteenth-century Castile and is centrally associated with Moses de León.
- **[D]** Scholem argued for de León as the main or substantially single author of the core. Yehuda Liebes, Ronit Meroz, Daniel Abrams and other later scholars have emphasized a literary circle, multiple compositional strata and continuing redaction. [Current linguistic review](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10088406/); [Daniel Matt, Pritzker Edition front matter](https://supress.sites-pro.stanford.edu/sites/supress/files/media/file/4810_Front_Matter.pdf)

### The Tree in the Zohar

The Zohar’s Godhead is fluid, dramatic and exegetical. It contains:

- right, left and central columns;
- masculine and feminine polarities;
- rivers, trees, gardens, lights and colours;
- the body of the primordial human;
- unions of Tiferet and Malkhut/Shekhinah;
- upper mother and father;
- the “Other Side.”

“Tree of Life” frequently means **Tiferet as the central column**, standing in the garden of Malkhut. [Zohar, *Shemot*](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Shemot/verses); [Zohar, *Bemidbar*](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Bemidbar/chapters/7)

Thus a caption reading “the Zoharic Tree of Life” can conceal two different claims:

1. **[DT]** The Zohar has elaborate sefirotic tree/body/column symbolism.
2. **[Not demonstrated]** The Zohar contains the later standard ten-circle, twenty-two-connector diagram.

The second claim lacks evidence.

## 5. Early diagrams and the *ilan* genre

### Earliest material record

J. H. Chajes’s Ilanot Project provides the decisive correction to histories that begin with Kircher:

- late-thirteenth-century manuscripts already contain sefirotic and cosmological diagrams;
- fourteenth-century Kabbalists began producing large parchment sheets called *ilanot*, “trees,” or *yeriʿot*, “sheets”;
- they were mnemonic, exegetical, contemplative and sometimes ritual objects;
- their geometry is diverse. [Ilanot Project](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/); [Chajes, *The Kabbalistic Tree*](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/978-0-271-09345-1.html)

The diagrams include:

- concentric celestial spheres;
- medallions and channels;
- human figures;
- menorahs;
- Temple objects;
- rivers and Eden;
- angelic hierarchies;
- Lurianic worlds and partzufim.

They are therefore better understood as visual cosmological commentaries than as imperfect precursors of one “correct” occult Tree.

### *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paulus Riccius’s Latin translation of Gikatilla, printed at Augsburg in 1516, bears the first printed woodcut of the Sephiroth. The image shows a scholar holding a branching structure with ten labelled medallions. It is traditionally associated with the artistic circle of Hans Burgkmair and sometimes with Reuchlin’s intellectual influence. [Bibliographical description](https://www.sephardichorizons.org/Volume15/Issue1/Heller-Gikatilla.html); [image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ATree_of_life.png)

Important corrections:

- it is the first **printed** sefirotic tree, not the first Jewish diagram;
- it has seventeen visible connecting strokes, not the later canonical twenty-two paths;
- it does not give the Golden Dawn’s alphabetic path sequence.

## 6. Moses Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim*, completed 1548

Cordovero’s *Pardes* is the great pre-Lurianic synthesis. Its thirty-two gates treat the number and nature of the Sephiroth, channels, names, colours, angelic matters and worlds.

Central doctrines include:

- the Sephiroth are ten, neither nine nor eleven;
- Keter is included, while Daʿat is not normally an independent eleventh;
- the Sephiroth are entirely spiritual, not material bodies;
- all interpenetrate and are included in one another;
- channels are modes of directed influx, not physical pipes;
- Tiferet mediates between right and left;
- four worlds articulate degrees of emanation and creation.  
  [*Pardes Rimmonim* Gate 1](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim%2C_Index.1); [Gate 7, “Channels”](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.7); [four worlds](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.2.6)

Cordovero’s printed diagrams have a complicated transmission history. Copyists and printers could omit, duplicate or redraw channels. A “Cordovero Tree” extracted from one posthumous edition is therefore not automatically Cordovero’s autograph geometry. [Eugene Matanky’s manuscript study](https://brill.com/view/journals/ima/15/1/article-p27_3.pdf)

### *Or Neʿerav*

*Or Neʿerav* is Cordovero’s shorter methodological introduction. It emphasizes preparation, disciplined study and the ordered relationship of the Sephiroth. Its discussion of Daʿat agrees with the principle that one counts Keter or speaks of Daʿat contextually, but does not add an eleventh power.

## 7. Luria through Ḥayyim Vital

Isaac Luria taught in Safed principally during 1570–72 and wrote little systematic exposition. Ḥayyim Vital recorded and organized his teaching; later editors, especially Jacob Ṣemaḥ and Meir Poppers, created the forms of *Etz Chaim* that circulated and were printed. Poppers’s redaction dates to the mid-seventeenth century; the first printing appeared at Korets in 1782. [Textual-history evidence](https://d-nb.info/1370283172/34)

### Tzimtzum and the line

Vital’s text states that Ein Sof “contracted” its light from a central point, leaving an empty space, after which a straight line, קו אחד ישר, entered the void. [*Etz Chaim* 1:2:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.2.3)

- **[KT/D] Literalist reading:** an actual primordial withdrawal.
- **[KT] Nonliteral reading:** concealment or limitation from the receiver’s perspective; Ein Sof remains omnipresent.
- **[SR]** The text itself uses spatial diagrams while also speaking from the creature’s perspective. Later schools disagree over how literally its geometry should be taken.

### Igulim and yosher

Lurianic cosmology uses:

- **עיגולים, circles:** concentric, non-hierarchical-environmental relation;
- **יושר, uprightness/straightness:** the human-shaped, relational configuration;
- **Adam Kadmon:** the primordial configuration through which subsequent worlds unfold.

This is not adequately represented by the standard flat Hermetic Tree.

### Breaking and repair

The sequence, greatly abbreviated, is:

1. Ein Sof and infinite light.
2. Tzimtzum and the vacant space.
3. The *kav*, line of measured light.
4. Adam Kadmon.
5. Akudim, Nekudim and Berudim.
6. The lights of the world of points exceed the capacity of the vessels.
7. The vessels of the lower configuration break.
8. Fragments and sparks descend and become entangled with the Qelippot.
9. *Tikkun* rearranges the isolated points into relational *partzufim*.
10. Prayer, commandments and ethical-religious action participate in raising sparks.

Vital distinguishes degrees in the breaking; it was not one simple explosion in which every vessel broke identically. [*Etz Chaim* 9:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.2)

### Partzufim

The Sephiroth are reconfigured as complete “faces/personae,” each internally containing ten:

- Atik Yomin and Arikh Anpin—Keter’s ancient and long-faced configurations;
- Abba—Chokhmah;
- Imma—Binah;
- Zeʿir Anpin—the six middle Sephiroth;
- Nukva/Rachel—Malkhut.

They interact through gestation, nursing, maturation, turning back-to-back and face-to-face, coupling and renewed influx. The partzufim are therefore processes and relationships, not decorative figures placed around a static chart.

### Four worlds and four Trees

| World | Hebrew | Function in mature Kabbalah |
|---|---|---|
| Emanation | אצילות, Atziluth | Divinity in articulated emanation |
| Creation | בריאה, Briah | Throne and created intellect/soul |
| Formation | יצירה, Yetzirah | Angelic and formative order |
| Action | עשיה, Assiah | Cosmic action, including material existence |

Vital repeatedly states that every world contains ten Sephiroth and internal/external levels. [*Etz Chaim* 40:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.40.11)

The frequently reproduced four interlocking Trees called “Jacob’s Ladder” are **[MI]** a twentieth-century reformulation most strongly associated with Zʾev ben Shimon Halevi/Warren Kenton. They use authentic four-world material but are not a recovered medieval master-diagram.

## 8. The Gra’s Tree

Elijah ben Solomon Zalman, the Vilna Gaon (1720–97), produced a major commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah*. His diagrammatic tradition arranges the letters so their textual classes remain geometrically evident:

- three mother paths as one class;
- seven double paths as one class;
- twelve simple paths as one class.

Common reconstructions describe these as three horizontal, seven vertical and twelve diagonal relations. This geometry differs radically from the Kircher/Golden Dawn Tree, where mother, double and simple letters are interspersed among diagonal, horizontal and vertical links.

The Gra version also assigns the twelve simples in order to Aries–Pisces and Nisan–Adar. [Gra recension 5:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.5.3?lang=bi)

## 9. The Tree in body and soul

### Adam Kadmon and the body

**[KT]** The human body is both analogy and theurgic image. In widespread Jewish arrangements:

| Region | Sefirotic symbolism |
|---|---|
| Crown/skull | Keter |
| Fatherly/right intellectual potency | Chokhmah |
| Motherly/left intellectual potency | Binah |
| Right arm | Chesed |
| Left arm | Gevurah |
| Torso/heart | Tiferet |
| Right and left legs/kidneys | Netzach and Hod |
| Covenant/generative organ | Yesod |
| Mouth, feet, receiving body or Shekhinah | Malkhut, depending text and context |

This is symbolic embodiment, not anatomy. Elliot Wolfson’s work is particularly important for showing that Kabbalistic body and gender imagery cannot be reduced to a simple male-right/female-left code. [Wolfson, “The Body in the Text”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/25470090)

### Five soul levels

| Soul level | Common world/configuration |
|---|---|
| Nefesh | Assiah; vital/acting soul |
| Ruach | Yetzirah; affective-spiritual soul |
| Neshamah | Briah; higher/intellectual soul |
| Chayah | Atziluth; living supernal soul |
| Yechidah | Adam Kadmon/Keter; singular unity |

The assignments are nested: each world, partzuf and soul level can itself contain all five.

## 10. Hasidic readings

### Tanya

*Tanya*, Part I, chapter 3, internalizes the sefirotic array. Each divine soul possesses ten faculties:

- Chokhmah, Binah and Daʿat as three intellectual powers;
- seven *middot* as affective powers.

Daʿat binds intellectual apprehension to lived emotion. This is not a commentary on the lines of a Tree diagram but a psychological-theological appropriation of the sefirotic structure. [*Tanya* I.3](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.3.14)

### Naḥman of Bratslav

Naḥman uses Ein Sof, worlds, souls, channels, concealment and the righteous as a cosmic tree, but does not present one systematic “Tree of Life chapter.” His teaching is homiletic and experiential rather than diagram-manual literature. [*Likkutei Moharan* 24](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.24.1.2)

## 11. Prayer, Torah, Omer and calendar

### Torah

- **[DT]** Proverbs calls Wisdom a Tree of Life.
- **[KT]** Rabbinic liturgy applies the verse directly to Torah: `עץ חיים היא למחזיקים בה`.
- **[KT]** Zoharic and later Kabbalistic texts read Torah’s written and oral dimensions through Tiferet and Malkhut and its commandments as branches, leaves or fruit.

### Prayer

No universal “Tree prayer” exists. Different Kabbalistic siddurim map:

- divine names;
- four worlds;
- vocalizations;
- bodily gestures;
- daily prayer sections;
- unifications of Tiferet and Shekhinah.

The recital of 1 Chronicles 29:11 in *Vayevarekh David* became an obvious liturgical location for the seven lower attributes.

### Counting the Omer

The forty-nine days are arranged as seven weeks of seven lower Sephiroth:

- Chesed within Chesed through Malkhut within Malkhut.

This is a later Kabbalistic devotional superstructure upon the biblical counting command, not its plain textual meaning.

## The Hermetic and occult tradition: dated and sourced

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones nongentae* contain forty-seven conclusions “according to the Hebrew Cabalists” and seventy-two of his own “according to his own opinion.” He used translations made principally by Flavius Mithridates.

Pico’s sefirotic Latin glosses include:

- *Fatum supremum*;
- *Sapientia*;
- *Intelligentia*;
- *Amor/Pietas*;
- *Iudicium/Potentia*;
- *Clementia* or Tiferet;
- *Aeternitas*;
- *Decor*;
- *Fundamentum/Iustus*;
- *Regnum*.

**[HO/DT]** Pico’s objective was Christian apologetic: he claimed that Cabala confirmed Trinity, Incarnation and Jesus’ divinity. His conclusions do not exhibit the later Tree glyph and cannot document a fifteenth-century tarot-on-the-Tree system. [Latin *Conclusiones*](https://cds.library.brown.edu/projects/pico/latest.php); [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2021/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

## 2. Reuchlin, 1517

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica libri tres* presents a dialogue among Jewish, Muslim and Christian interlocutors. It develops:

- the ten Sephiroth;
- Hebrew letters and divine names;
- Pythagorean number symbolism;
- a Christian reading of the Tetragrammaton and Pentagrammaton.

Reuchlin helped make Kabbalah a Christian learned art. He did not simply transmit Jewish Kabbalah unchanged. [1517 digital copy](https://digital.pitts.emory.edu/s/paradise-lost/item/4771); [modern critical edition](https://www.frommann-holzboog.de/editionen/1706/170600211?lang=de)

## 3. Riccius and *Portae Lucis*, 1516

The Latin Gikatilla translation is older than *De arte cabalistica* by one year. Its woodcut was enormously influential as a printable visual emblem, but it lacks the later twenty-two-letter connector system.

## 4. Kircher, 1652–54

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I, contains the plate titled *Systema Sephiroticum*. It draws upon Philippe d’Aquin’s early-seventeenth-century Christian Cabalistic diagram tradition and combines:

- ten Sephiroth;
- Latin glosses;
- Hebrew names;
- astrological symbols;
- divine names;
- twenty-two connecting paths;
- Daʿat as a liminal or hidden point.

[Kircher’s plate at Cornell](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272); [Latin transcription and images](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

### Kircher versus Ari and Gra

| Feature | Ari/Gra-type Jewish diagrams | Kircher/Golden Dawn lineage |
|---|---|---|
| Primary rationale | Preserve *Sefer Yetzirah*’s 3/7/12 letter classes; visualize Lurianic processes | Produce an encyclopedic twenty-two-letter network on a three-column Tree |
| Geometry | Several competing layouts; often mother/double/simple classes remain visibly grouped | The familiar ten-circle arrangement with three routes to Malkuth |
| Letters | Variable; tied to Jewish recensions | Placed successively in a single path order |
| Daʿat | Conditional knowledge/configuration, not an eleventh | Often visibly marked at the Abyss |
| Use | Exegesis, contemplation, Lurianic cosmography | Universal correspondence system and map of initiation |

The Golden Dawn inherited Kircher’s topology and alphabetic premise, but altered details. “The Golden Dawn copied Kircher exactly” is too strong.

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–84

*Kabbala Denudata, seu doctrina Hebraeorum transcendentalis et metaphysica atque theologica* made large portions of Jewish and Lurianic Kabbalah available in Latin. It includes:

- Zoharic passages;
- Cordoverean and Lurianic material;
- extensive terminological lexica;
- multiple *arbores* and *tabulae*;
- Christian interpretive material.

It did not contain one single authoritative Tree. Its multiple diagrams testify to an already plural Jewish and Christian-Cabalistic visual culture. [1677 volume](https://books.google.com/books/about/KABBALA_DENUDATA_Seu_DOCTRINA_HEBRAEORUM.html?id=psQRGAjKNKgC); [e-rara scans](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/10759091)

S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* (1887) translates selections, especially the *Siphra di-Tseniutha* and Idrot. It is not a translation of the whole *Kabbala Denudata*. [Mathers’s introduction](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/tku/tku03.htm)

## 6. Lévi and tarot

Antoine Court de Gébelin and the Comte de Mellet made the late-eighteenth-century claim that tarot preserved Egyptian wisdom. De Mellet explicitly paralleled twenty-two trumps and twenty-two Hebrew letters.

Éliphas Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* (1854–56) systematized this association. His sequence was not the Golden Dawn’s later assignment; notably, his placement of the Fool and the relationship of Aleph to the Magician differ.

- **[SR]** Tarot cards are documented first as European playing cards.
- **[HO/MI]** Their Egyptian antiquity and Mosaic origin are occult constructions without medieval evidence.
- **[HO]** Their integration with the Tree became an internally coherent magical system under the Golden Dawn.  
  [Lévi scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ADogme_et_rituel_de_la_haute_magie_%28IA_b24884340_0001%29.pdf)

## 7. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

Founded in 1887–88 by William Wynn Westcott, S. L. MacGregor Mathers and William Robert Woodman, the Golden Dawn made the Tree the structural map of its curriculum.

### Knowledge Lectures

The Tree organizes:

- grades;
- elements and planets;
- Hebrew;
- tarot;
- geomancy;
- alchemy;
- divine and angelic names;
- ritual stations;
- visionary ascent.

The Second Knowledge Lecture describes the Serpent of Wisdom as binding the twenty-two paths without touching the Sephiroth, while the Flaming Sword links the Sephiroth in numerical order. [Golden Dawn lecture study](https://hermetic.com/gdlibrary/the-nature-structure-and-role-of-the-soul/the-structure-of-the-golden-dawn-and-its-rituals)

### Three pillars

In standard Golden Dawn English:

- Pillar of Mercy: Chokmah–Chesed–Netzach;
- Pillar of Severity: Binah–Geburah–Hod;
- Middle Pillar/Mildness: Keter–Tiferet–Yesod–Malkuth.

Earlier Jewish sources possess right, left and middle columns. The exact English names, colour coding and ritualized pillar system are Hermetic developments.

### Three triads and the pendant

A characteristic Hermetic analysis is:

1. Supernal triad: Keter, Chokmah, Binah.
2. Ethical/mental triad: Chesed, Geburah, Tiferet.
3. Astral/personality triad: Netzach, Hod, Yesod.
4. Malkuth as pendant or material synthesis.

This is pedagogically central in occult Qabalah, but not a universal Jewish division.

### The Abyss and Daʿat

Jewish sources distinguish the upper three from lower configurations and discuss Daʿat as mediation. The Golden Dawn turns the interval into an initiatory Abyss. Crowley intensifies it as the ordeal of Choronzon and the transition to Magister Templi. This is an occult dramatization of older materials, not a direct quotation from the Zohar.

### The Tzaddi/Heh problem

Golden Dawn:

- He—Aries—Emperor;
- Tzaddi—Aquarius—Star.

*Liber AL* I:57 states that Tzaddi “is not the Star.” Crowley eventually interpreted this by exchanging the trumps:

- He—Aries—Star;
- Tzaddi—Aquarius—Emperor.

His published explanations and tables are not perfectly consistent about whether trump alone, trump-plus-sign or path position is being exchanged. The *Book of Thoth*’s final table gives Emperor–Tzaddi–Aquarius and Star–He–Aries. [*Book of Thoth*](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/crowley/equinox/volume3/vol_3_no_05.pdf)

## 9. Crowley

### *Liber 777*

*777* is a system of keyed rows:

- 1–10: Sephiroth;
- 11–32: letter-paths.

Its approximately 180 columns correlate gods, colours, perfumes, plants, stones, magical weapons, bodies, spirits, scriptures and other taxonomies. The whole Tree therefore has no single “777 row”; it is the index that contains all rows. “Every column for the Tree” means the entirety of the published tables, which is better consulted directly than reproduced without their notes. [*777 Revised*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf)

### *Liber 231*

*Liber Arcanorum* gives twenty-two terse path visions; *Liber Carcerorum Qliphoth* gives twenty-two averse genii and sigils. It does not supply an ancient Jewish “tunnel” system. Kenneth Grant later turned its averse genii into the Tunnels of Set. [*Liber 231*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib231)

### A∴A∴

The A∴A∴ preserves the numerical Tree grades while recasting them through Thelemic attainment:

- the Holy Guardian Angel is centered upon the Adeptus Minor/Tiferet attainment;
- the Abyss separates Adeptus Exemptus from Magister Templi;
- Binah, Chokmah and Keter correspond to Magister Templi, Magus and Ipsissimus.

The grades are initiatory claims internal to Thelema, not historical Jewish offices.

## 10. Fortune, Regardie, Case and Knight

### Dion Fortune, *The Mystical Qabalah* (1935)

There is no chapter “on this Sephirah” because the subject is the whole Tree. The relevant opening chapters cover:

- the Western Qabalah;
- the Tree as a method;
- negative existence;
- three pillars;
- ten Sephiroth;
- twenty-two paths;
- the Tree as a mental classification system.

Fortune openly states that she is presenting a Western initiatory development rather than rabbinic Kabbalah as history. She treats gods of many pantheons as being sorted into ten sefirotic “pigeon-holes.” [Complete online text](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html)

Her achievement was to make the Tree intelligible as a psychological and magical filing system. Her method is also universalizing and sometimes supersessionist: Jewish materials become raw data for a putatively universal Western occult science.

### Israel Regardie

Regardie’s *A Garden of Pomegranates* (1932), *The Tree of Life* (1932) and *The Golden Dawn* (1937–40) disseminated:

- the complete grade-Tree;
- pathworking;
- colour scales;
- ritual correspondences;
- psychological interpretations.

He is a transmitter and synthesizer of Golden Dawn Qabalah, not a neutral historian of Jewish Kabbalah. [*The Tree of Life*](https://www.invisiblehouse.org/ref/The_Tree_of_Life.pdf)

### Paul Foster Case

Case founded Builders of the Adytum in 1922. BOTA retained the Golden Dawn letter/tarot Tree, emphasized meditation and tarot colouring, and rejected Crowley’s Tzaddi exchange. BOTA’s claim to possess the uniquely “correct” attributions is an initiatory claim, not historical proof. [BOTA history](https://bota.org/bota/history)

### Gareth Knight

Knight’s *A Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* (1965) presents the Tree as a universal symbolic relation-system and treats pathworking as guided entry into its inner images. It is explicitly practical, comparative occultism rather than manuscript scholarship. [Edition record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1350874M/A_practical_guide_to_Qabalistic_symbolism)

## The Qliphah or tunnel

## 1. Jewish Qelippot and the Sitra Aḥra

קליפה, *qelippah*, means husk, peel or shell; plural קליפות. The Aramaic סטרא אחרא, *Sitra Aḥra*, means “Other Side.”

In Zoharic and Lurianic sources, evil can be figured as:

- a left side detached from balance;
- shells surrounding or concealing holiness;
- counterfeit palaces;
- parasitic reception of divine vitality;
- the domain of Samael and the feminine demonic;
- fragments that imprison sparks after the breaking of the vessels.

The Zohar itself uses more than one spatial model: the Other Side may mirror holy columns and palaces, or appear as concentric shells surrounding holiness. [Eliyahu Berman’s study](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1435419/1/Berman%20-%20Improper%20Twins%20-%20The%20Ambivalent%20Other%20Side%20in%20the%20Zohar.pdf)

A perfectly symmetrical, inverted ten-circle “Tree of Death” is therefore a later diagrammatic systematization.

### Art and literature

- **Leonhard Beck/Hans Burgkmair circle:** attribution of the *Portae Lucis* woodcut remains bibliographically discussed.
- **Kircher:** turned the Tree into encyclopedic Baroque information design.
- **Golden Dawn artists:** coloured Trees, tarot designs and tracing boards made the correspondences materially visible.
- **Moina Mathers:** contributed significantly to Golden Dawn visual culture, although assigning every colour innovation to her exceeds surviving evidence.
- **W. B. Yeats:** practiced Golden Dawn ritual for roughly two decades; Tree, path and antinomial symbolism inform his occult prose and parts of his poetry. [Study of Yeats’s Golden Dawn Kabbalah](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/8/9/182)
- **Arthur Machen, Algernon Blackwood and Dion Fortune:** incorporated Golden Dawn or cognate initiatory themes into fiction. [Literary study](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.2307/jj.18254351.4)
- **Steffi Grant:** produced Tree-related occult art, though the attribution of at least one surviving museum diagram is uncertain. [Museum record](https://museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk/object/tree-of-life-diagram-by-steffi-grant/)
- **Elliot Wolfson:** combines scholarship with poetry and painting concerned with Kabbalistic language and vision.
- **Jorge Luis Borges:** worked intensively with Kabbalistic language, infinity and creation, but not every “tree” in Borges is a technical sefirotic diagram.
- **“Lyons”:** no securely identified artist or canonical Tree work matching this lead was located. It may refer to the Martinist *Leçons de Lyon*, to a modern illustrator, or to a misspelled name. This remains open.
- **Gustav Klimt:** his *Tree of Life* should not be called a Kabbalistic Tree without independent provenance; resemblance of title is insufficient.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is the familiar Tree ancient?

**Finding:** no.

- *Sefer Yetzirah* supplies 10 + 22, but not the standard picture.
- The *Bahir* and Zohar supply tree, body and column imagery.
- Manuscript diagrams become visible in the late thirteenth and fourteenth centuries.
- The familiar Hermetic geometry is early modern and was standardized in the nineteenth century.

Calling the Golden Dawn Tree “the diagram from *Sefer Yetzirah*” collapses nearly a millennium of development.

## 2. Dating *Sefer Yetzirah*

- **Tradition:** Abraham.
- **Scholem and many twentieth-century scholars:** late antiquity.
- **Hayman:** manuscript history securely visible from the tenth century; the text grew through recensional transmission.
- **Weiss:** seventh-century Syriac setting.
- **Open:** the date and unity of the earliest recoverable core.

## 3. Zoharic authorship

- **Tradition:** Shimon bar Yoḥai and companions.
- **Scholem:** Moses de León as main author of the core.
- **Later scholarship:** a de León-centered circle, multiple strata and redaction.
- **Evidence:** artificial medieval Aramaic, Castilian geography, dependence on medieval texts, parallels with de León’s Hebrew writings and late-thirteenth-century circulation.

Traditional sacred attribution and historical authorship are different kinds of claim; neither should be silently substituted for the other.

## 4. Christian appropriation

Christian Cabalists preserved and disseminated important Jewish texts but also:

- translated them through Christian theological categories;
- claimed that Jewish esotericism proved Christianity;
- detached techniques and names from halakhic life;
- worked in societies where conversionary pressure and legal inequality were real.

“Transmission” and “appropriation” are therefore both applicable, depending the precise actor and act. The power imbalance is part of the evidence, not a modern distraction.

## 5. Kircher versus Jewish Trees

There is no single “Jewish Tree” against which Kircher can simply be marked right or wrong. Nevertheless:

- Kircher’s alphabetical path sequence does not preserve the visible 3/7/12 geometry favored in Ari/Gra reconstructions;
- it produces a powerful and consistent Christian-Hermetic filing system;
- the Golden Dawn’s success made it look universal.

Its ritual effectiveness within Hermeticism does not establish its antiquity within Judaism.

## 6. Tarot attributions

- Playing-card history does not support an ancient Egyptian or Mosaic tarot.
- Twenty-two-letter/trump equations appear in late-eighteenth-century occultism.
- Lévi, Papus, the Golden Dawn, Waite, Case and Crowley disagree.
- Strength/Justice and He/Tzaddi are the best-known fault lines.
- Tarot-on-the-Tree is a major Hermetic creation, not a teaching of the *Bahir* or Zohar.

## 7. Daʿat

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists on ten, not eleven. Cordovero argues strongly for counting Keter and not Daʿat as an independent Sephirah. [*Pardes Rimmonim* 3:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.3.3.1?lang=bi)

Daʿat can nevertheless signify:

- the synthesis of Chokhmah and Binah;
- the internal knowledge of a partzuf;
- the conscious manifestation of Keter;
- a temporary counting substitute when Keter is not enumerated;
- in Hermeticism, an invisible sphere at the Abyss.

A dotted Daʿat does not make eleven ordinary Sephiroth.

## 8. The three veils

Ein Sof and infinite light are classical Kabbalistic terms. The fixed sequence:

1. Ain;
2. Ain Soph;
3. Ain Soph Aur

as three graphic “veils of negative existence” is principally Golden Dawn/Fortune-era systematization. It should not be attributed wholesale to the Zohar or Luria.

## 9. Assyrian origins

Simo Parpola proposed that the Neo-Assyrian sacred tree encoded a structure ancestral to Kabbalistic monotheism and the Sephiroth. [Parpola’s article](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/373622)

The proposal is disputed because:

- the iconographic parallels require interpretive reconstruction;
- no continuous chain of transmission to medieval Kabbalistic diagrams has been demonstrated;
- similar tree, body and celestial hierarchies occur widely.

Chajes considers medieval diagram genres—Porphyrian trees, celestial-sphere maps and contemporary Jewish visual practices—more immediate sources. Similarity is evidence for comparison, not automatically genealogy.

## 10. Qliphoth as an inverted Tree

- **Classical evidence:** shells, palaces, left side, demonic couples and Sitra Aḥra.
- **Later synthesis:** ten exactly mirrored Qliphoth.
- **Modern development:** twenty-two Tunnels of Set.

Calling Grant’s system “Lurianic Qliphoth” is historically inaccurate even where it borrows Lurianic vocabulary.

## Open questions

1. What is the earliest securely datable manuscript in which all twenty-two Hebrew letters are placed on connectors between ten named Sephiroth?
2. How much of each surviving Cordovero diagram belongs to Cordovero, and how much to scribes, engravers or printers?
3. Which precise Philippe d’Aquin source or intermediate image did Kircher use?
4. Which details of the Golden Dawn path layout derive directly from Kircher, and which entered through Knorr, French occultism or Mathers’s own reconstruction?
5. What is the full manuscript genealogy of the “Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom” appendix?
6. Can the Ari and Gra diagrams be described as single Trees, or only as families of diagrams associated with particular recensions and printers?
7. When did the fixed “Ain–Ain Soph–Ain Soph Aur” graphic triad first appear in exactly its modern form?
8. Which demon-prince table first fixed the now-popular Thaumiel-to-Lilith sequence without the Golden Dawn lecture’s reversals and qualifications?
9. Does “Lyons” identify a particular artist, the *Leçons de Lyon*, or another modern source?
10. How did coloured Jewish *ilanot* influence, if at all, the Golden Dawn’s four colour scales?
11. To what extent did Jewish practitioners use *ilanot* performatively—as contemplative devices, amulets, teaching maps or ritual objects—in different communities?
12. How should historians distinguish legitimate cross-traditional adaptation from supersessionist appropriation when a system has acquired centuries of independent Hermetic use?

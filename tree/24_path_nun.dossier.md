# The Path of Nun: Research Dossier

## Evidentiary key

The following labels distinguish kinds of evidence throughout:

- **[Documented text]** Directly attested in a cited primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** Historical or philological conclusion argued by modern scholarship.
- **[Kabbalistic tradition]** A received Jewish mystical interpretation, not necessarily historically demonstrable.
- **[Hermetic-occult attribution]** A correspondence created or transmitted within Christian Cabala, Hermetic Qabalah, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, or related occult systems.
- **[Disputed]** Attribution, date, authorship, textual reading, or genealogy remains contested.
- **[Modern invention]** A correspondence first demonstrable in modern esoteric literature and not in the early Jewish sources.

“Path” requires special care. In *Sefer Yetzirah*, the thirty-two paths are the ten *sefirot belimah* plus twenty-two letters. The text does not number Nun “24,” draw it between Tiferet and Netzach, or pair it with a tarot card. Those identifications belong to the Kircher–Golden Dawn arrangement.

# The name

## Hebrew spelling, pronunciation, and numerical value

**[Documented linguistic fact]** Nun is written נ medially or initially and ן at the end of a word. Its usual sound is /n/. It is the fourteenth Hebrew letter and has the numerical value fifty.

The traditional fully vocalized name is נוּן, *nūn*. In ordinary Hebrew gematria:

\[
נון = 50 + 6 + 50 = 106
\]

In *mispar gadol*, where the five final letters can receive values 500–900, final ן may be 700, producing 756. That extended valuation is not the default in biblical or rabbinic numerical interpretation.

Nun appears in divine names, most conspicuously in אדני, *Adonai*, whose ordinary value is 65:

\[
אדני = 1+4+50+10=65
\]

That fact does not create a classical “divine name of the Path of Nun.” Such a title would be a later magical construction.

## Meaning and etymology

**[Documented traditional lexicography]** The conventional Semitic interpretation is “fish.” Phoenician 𐤍 was called *nūn*; comparable words include Aramaic נוּן and Akkadian *nūnu*, “fish.” Biblical Hebrew ordinarily uses דג, *dag*, for fish, although נון survives as the personal name Nun, father of Joshua.

**[Disputed paleographic reconstruction]** The meaning of the earliest pictorial sign is less certain than occult manuals imply. Proto-Sinaitic discussions have identified the ancestor of /n/ either with a fish or with a snake, the latter through the acrophonic word *naḥash*. In some reconstructions, the obvious fish pictogram instead supplied /d/. “Nun is shaped like a fish” is therefore a traditional mnemonic, not an uncontested account of the Proto-Sinaitic sign.

## Development of the letter-form

A defensible paleographic sequence is:

1. **[Scholarly reconstruction]** Proto-Sinaitic/Proto-Canaanite sign, second millennium BCE: a sinuous or pictorial sign whose identification as fish or snake is disputed.
2. **[Documented epigraphy]** Phoenician 𐤍: an angular zigzag or bent form.
3. **[Documented epigraphy]** Paleo-Hebrew: a locally developed version of the Phoenician form.
4. **[Documented epigraphy]** Imperial Aramaic 𐡍: increasingly cursive.
5. **[Scholarly reconstruction]** Jewish “square” script, developed from Imperial Aramaic during the Persian and Hellenistic periods.
6. **[Documented scribal development]** נ and final ן: positional forms became standard in the Jewish script. The medial letter bends; the final descends below the writing line.

Greek nu, Ν/ν, and ultimately Latin N derive through the Phoenician alphabetic line. The resemblance between modern Latin N and the ancient sign is historical, though altered by transmission through Greek.

## Rabbinic interpretation of its two forms

**[Documented text]** *Shabbat* 104a interprets bent נ and straight final ן homiletically:

> נאמן כפוף, נאמן פשוט  
> “A faithful person bent; a faithful person upright.”

The bent Nun represents faithfulness under present humility; the extended final Nun suggests the faithful person ultimately made upright. This is rabbinic alphabetic exegesis, not historical etymology.

## Biblical wisdom language and the name “Path”

Several biblical passages supplied later kabbalists with a vocabulary of wisdom, understanding, paths, powers, and the Tree of Life. None names a “Path of Nun.”

- **[Documented text]** 1 Chronicles 29:11 enumerates “greatness,” “power,” “beauty/glory,” “victory,” and “majesty.” Medieval kabbalists eventually read these as names or scriptural supports for the lower seven sefirot. This is a later mystical exegesis, not the verse’s historical-linguistic meaning.
- **[Documented text]** Proverbs 3:19 says that YHWH founded the earth “by wisdom” and established the heavens “by understanding.” Proverbs 3:18 calls wisdom “a tree of life.”
- **[Documented text]** Job 28 repeatedly asks for the דרך or מקום of Wisdom and Understanding: “Whence does wisdom come?”
- **[Documented text]** Isaiah 11:2 enumerates spirit, wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear of YHWH. Later Christian and Jewish systems could read these schematically, but Isaiah does not identify the seven lower sefirot.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** The language נתיבות פליאות חכמה, “wondrous paths of wisdom,” at the opening of *Sefer Yetzirah* belongs to the broader poetic wisdom vocabulary of Proverbs, Job, and Psalms.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. Biblical and early rabbinic foundations

Nun has three especially important rabbinic complexes.

### Nun missing from *Ashrei*

**[Documented text]** Psalm 145 is alphabetic but omits Nun. *Berakhot* 4b explains the omission through Amos 5:2:

> נפלה לא תוסיף קום בתולת ישראל  
> “Fallen, no more to rise, is virgin Israel.”

Nun is associated with נפילה, *nefilah*, “falling.” The following Samekh verse—“YHWH supports all who fall” (Psalm 145:14)—then supplies restoration. This fall/support sequence also informs the Zoharic alphabet legend.

This is the earliest securely attested “fall and restoration” lore surrounding Nun. It is not yet Scorpio, Death, or a Tiferet–Netzach path.

### The inverted Nuns in Numbers

**[Documented text]** Numbers 10:35–36 is enclosed in the Masoretic tradition by two reversed or inverted Nun-like signs. *Shabbat* 115b–116a calls them סימניות, “signs,” and reports two explanations:

- the passage is not in its proper place;
- the marked unit constitutes a separate book, yielding a traditional count of seven books of the Torah.

**[Disputed]** The marks’ graphic ancestry and original textual-critical function remain uncertain. Parallels have been proposed with ancient scribal signs used to mark displaced or questionable text. Calling them literal “backward Nuns” is conventional, but the Talmud itself emphasizes their function as signs.

### Fifty gates of understanding

**[Documented text]** *Nedarim* 38a says that fifty gates of understanding were created and that Moses received all but one. The number is naturally connected to Nun’s value fifty in later teaching.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Forty-nine attainable gates plus an inaccessible or divinely bestowed fiftieth became an important motif.

**[Modern Hasidic homily]** The forty-nine days of the Omer and the fiftieth day, Shavuot, are frequently related to Nun/50. No classical Omer rite assigns a particular day to a “Path of Nun.”

### Scribal crowns

**[Documented text]** *Menachot* 29b includes Nun among the letters of שעטנז ג״ץ that receive three ornamental crowns or *tagin* in Torah scroll calligraphy. This is scribal law and midrashic lore, not an astrological attribution.

## 2. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date and textual history

**[Disputed]** *Sefer Yetzirah* has been dated anywhere from late antiquity to the early medieval period. Older scholarship sometimes proposed a date as early as the second or third century. Current textual study more often treats it as a layered work whose recoverable forms crystallized between approximately the third and seventh centuries, with major medieval redaction and commentary.

Peter Hayman’s synoptic critical edition demonstrates that no single medieval witness can simply be equated with an “original text.” Ronit Meroz and other scholars have examined its affinities with biblical Wisdom literature. Recent Genizah research has reopened the possibility that a form resembling Saadia’s recension preserves especially early structural features.

### The thirty-two paths

**[Documented text]** The opening says:

> בשלשים ושתים נתיבות פליאות חכמה חקק יה יהוה צבאות  
> “With thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom, Yah, YHWH of Hosts, engraved…”

The system consists of:

- ten *sefirot belimah*, עשר ספירות בלימה;
- twenty-two foundational letters, כ״ב אותיות יסוד;
- three mothers, אמש;
- seven doubles, בג״ד כפר״ת;
- twelve simples, הוזחטילנסעצק.

**[Documented text]** The work insists:

> עשר ולא תשע, עשר ולא אחת עשרה  
> “Ten and not nine; ten and not eleven.”

This statement later becomes important in arguments over whether Daʿat may be counted as an eleventh sefirah.

### Nun as a simple letter

**[Documented text, received recensions]** Nun is one of the twelve simple letters. In the commonly printed Gra text:

> המליך אות נ׳ בריח וקשר לו כתר וצר בו עקרב בעולם ומרחשון בשנה ודקין בנפש זכר ונקבה

A literal rendering is:

> “He made the letter Nun king over smell, bound a crown to it, combined them with one another, and formed through it Scorpio in the universe, Marḥeshvan in the year, and the intestines in the person, male and female.”

The wording varies, and “combined/permuted them” is sometimes omitted or differently placed.

### Recensional variants

Aryeh Kaplan’s comparative table, based on the Gra, Short, Long, Saadia, and Ramak forms, illustrates the instability:

| Recension/commentarial form | Faculty | Body | Sign | Month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gra | Smell | Intestines | Scorpio | Ḥeshvan |
| Printed “Ari” tradition | Usually smell | Usually intestines | Scorpio | Ḥeshvan |
| Short version | Motion | Spleen | Scorpio | Ḥeshvan |
| Long version | Smell | *Kivah*, stomach | Scorpio | Ḥeshvan |
| Saadia | Motion | *Kivah*, stomach | Scorpio | Ḥeshvan |
| Ramak/Cordoverian ordering | Thought | Spleen | Scorpio | Ḥeshvan |

**Finding:** Scorpio and Marḥeshvan are comparatively stable. The sensory faculty and bodily organ are not.

**[Disputed]** “Ari version” does not mean that Isaac Luria wrote an autograph recension of *Sefer Yetzirah*. It is a received edited form associated with Lurianic circles. Likewise, the “Gra version” represents the Vilna Gaon’s recension and commentary, not an ancient Gra manuscript.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Hayman’s experimental “earliest recoverable text” excludes much of the detailed month–organ–faculty material. Consequently, the complete Nun–smell–intestines formula should be cited as medieval recensional evidence, not confidently projected into the text’s earliest layer.

### Tribe

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** *Sefer Yetzirah* does not assign the twelve letters to Israel’s twelve tribes.

**[Later kabbalistic/Hasidic attribution]** In common modern Jewish month tables, Marḥeshvan/Scorpio/Nun is paired with Manasseh. That system is a later harmonization of months, tribes, senses, and letters. It should not be represented as a statement of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### “The Imaginative Intelligence”

The text usually presented as the “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom” says:

> הנתיב הכ״ד נקרא שכל דמיוני. ונקרא כן לפי שהוא נותן דמות וצורה לכל הדמיונים אשר נבראו בדמיונם לפי צביונם

Approximately:

> “The twenty-fourth path is called the Imaginative Intellect, because it gives image and form to all representations created in their imagination according to their nature.”

Kaplan renders שכל דמיוני as “Apparitive Consciousness”; Westcott used “Imaginative Intelligence.”

Kircher’s Latin is:

> *Vigesima quarta semita dicitur Intelligentia imaginativa, eò quòd illa det similitudinem omnibus similitudinibus creatorum.*

That is, “The twenty-fourth path is called Imaginative Intelligence because it gives likeness to all likenesses of created things.”

Westcott’s 19th-century translation reads:

> “The Twenty-fourth Path is the Imaginative Intelligence…”

**[Text-critical correction]** This numbered list is not part of the secure core of *Sefer Yetzirah*. It is an independent medieval tract appended to some manuscripts and editions and transmitted with a commentary attributed to the Raavad.

**[Disputed]** The commentary’s attribution to Abraham ben David of Posquières is not universally accepted.

**[Hermetic-occult attribution]** The equation

\[
\text{24th Intelligence} = \text{Nun} = \text{Tiferet–Netzach}
\]

requires three additional Western steps:

1. count the ten sefirot as paths 1–10;
2. count Aleph as 11, making Nun 24;
3. place the letters successively on Kircher’s twenty-two lines.

Neither *Sefer Yetzirah* nor the medieval “Thirty-two Intelligences” tract itself supplies that complete equation.

## 3. *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

**[Documented medieval text]** The *Alphabet of Rabbi Akiva* survives in several pseudepigraphic forms. Its medieval compilations were first printed in Kraków in 1578, though constituent traditions are earlier.

The Nun section develops Proverbs 20:27:

> נר יהוה נשמת אדם  
> “The lamp of YHWH is the human soul.”

It treats souls, judgment, Eden, Gehinnom, and the distinction between human and animal life.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** The work attributes vast cosmological meanings to the shapes, names, and numerical values of individual letters.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** It does not supply the Kircher path, Scorpio–Death synthesis, or a Tiferet–Netzach correspondence.

## 4. The *Bahir*

The *Bahir* probably emerged in Provence in the late twelfth century, although it presents itself as ancient rabbinic teaching.

**[Documented text]** In the passage commonly numbered §97, Nun is explained through the spinal axis:

> ומהי נון … המוח הוא עיקר חוט השדרה ומשם שואב … והוא נון כפופה … נון ארוכה לעולם בהשלמת המלה

In summary: the brain is the root of the spinal cord; the bent Nun represents a channel or continuing structure; the long Nun occurs at a word’s completion. The long form contains or completes the bent form, and the passage introduces male–female symbolism.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Nun therefore becomes a figure of transmission, extension, polarity, and completion.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** The *Bahir* does not call Nun the twenty-fourth path, locate it between Tiferet and Netzach, or associate it with tarot Death.

## 5. The Zohar

### Date and authorship

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** The Zohar is attributed to the second-century sage Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The principal corpus first circulated in late thirteenth-century Castile through Moses de León. Current scholarship treats its composition as complex: Moses de León was central, but multiple strata, voices, and possibly collaborators are evident. Daniel Matt’s Pritzker edition is especially valuable for tracing those layers.

### Nun in the alphabet procession

In the Zohar’s prologue, the letters approach God in reverse order, each asking that creation begin with it.

**[Documented text]** Nun appeals through phrases such as:

- נורא תהלות, “awesome in praises,” from Exodus 15:11;
- נאוה תהלה, “praise is comely,” from Psalms 33:1 and 147:1.

God sends Nun back:

> נו״ן תוב לאתרך דהא בגינך תבת סמ״ך לאתרה והוי סמיך עליה

Approximately:

> “Nun, return to your place, for because of you Samekh returned to its place; remain supported by it.”

The allusion is again to Nun/falling and Samekh/supporting.

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Nun becomes the soul or righteous community vulnerable to descent yet sustained by divine support.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** Zoharic Nun is not Death XIII. The alphabet legend supplies a potent fall-and-restoration motif that later occultists could read alongside Death, but the historical link runs from modern synthesis back to the Zohar—not from the Zohar forward to tarot.

## 6. Gikatilla: *Shaʿarei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*, written in the late thirteenth century, organizes divine names by the ten sefirot.

**[Documented text]**

- Tiferet is associated above all with the Tetragrammaton and divine compassion or balanced judgment.
- Netzach and Hod are treated as paired channels, frequently figured as legs, prophetic powers, or extensions through which upper forces operate.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** Gikatilla does not assign Nun an archangel, angelic order, planet, or a single path between Tiferet and Netzach. Those are later occult correlations.

The 1516 Augsburg Latin translation, *Portae Lucis*, by Paulus Ricius, carries the name:

> *Portae Lucis … Haec est porta Tetragrammaton*

Its title-page woodcut, associated with Hans Burgkmair, is often described as the earliest printed European image of a Sephirotic tree. It depicts sefirot and connective branches, not a Golden Dawn letter-tarot diagram.

## 7. Moses Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

Cordovero attempted a systematic reconciliation of earlier kabbalistic doctrines.

**[Documented text]** *Pardes Rimmonim* discusses:

- the ten sefirot and their names;
- divine names;
- colors;
- angelic and cosmic relations;
- the channels or paths connecting sefirot;
- permutation and symbolic uses of Hebrew letters.

Its discussions of the Tree do not yield the later universally familiar Kircher arrangement. Verbal descriptions and posthumously printed diagrams differ, and early Cordoverian trees do not consistently place all twenty-two Hebrew letters on twenty-two fixed edges.

### *Or Neʿerav*

This shorter methodological work explains the proper study of Kabbalah and summarizes sefirotic structure.

**[Documented text]** It presents the sefirot as an organic interdependent system rather than isolated magical objects.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** Neither *Pardes Rimmonim* nor *Or Neʿerav* securely attests “Nun = path 24 = Tiferet–Netzach = Scorpio = Death.”

Cordovero’s relevance is nevertheless substantial: his systematization of colors, names, channels, and worlds supplied material that later Christian Cabalists rearranged.

## 8. Luria through Ḥayyim Vital

Isaac Luria left little systematic writing. His teachings were redacted chiefly by Ḥayyim Vital in *Etz Ḥayyim* and related works.

### Lurianic cosmology

**[Documented Lurianic tradition]** The sequence includes:

1. *Ein Sof*, the limitless divine.
2. *Tzimtzum*, divine contraction or concealment.
3. The *kav*, a ray entering the vacated conceptual space.
4. Adam Qadmon.
5. The worlds or configurations of *Akudim*, *Nekudim*, and *Berudim*.
6. *Shevirat ha-kelim*, the shattering of the vessels.
7. The descent and entrapment of sparks.
8. Reconfiguration as *partzufim*.
9. *Tikkun*, repair or restoration.

Major *partzufim* include Atik Yomin, Arikh Anpin, Abba, Imma, Zeʿir Anpin, and Nukva. The four principal worlds are Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Assiah.

**[Documented text]** *Etz Ḥayyim* knows the arithmetic of ten sefirot plus twenty-two letters as thirty-two paths and connects fifty gates with the five final letters, מנצפ״ך.

**[Documented caution]** Vital also stresses that bodily diagrams, letters, and spatial relationships are analogies for supernal structures, not literal anatomy above.

### Does the Ari assign Nun to a particular line?

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** The core *Etz Ḥayyim* does not provide an autograph Lurianic twenty-two-edge diagram assigning Nun to one fixed pair of sefirot.

Modern sources nevertheless speak of “the Ari Tree.” This can mean:

- a diagram inferred from Lurianic *partzufim*;
- a later printed diagram associated with an Ari recension of *Sefer Yetzirah*;
- a tree designed according to the rule that mothers are horizontal, doubles vertical, and simples diagonal.

These are not always identical.

### Modern “Ari” placements of Nun

**[Disputed reconstruction]**

- One commonly circulated Ari table places Nun between Netzach and Yesod.
- Other reconstructions following three horizontal mothers, seven vertical doubles, and twelve diagonal simples place Nun differently, including Gevurah–Tiferet.

The conflict is itself evidence that “the Ari’s placement of Nun” is not a single securely transmitted fact.

## 9. The Gra’s tree

The Vilna Gaon’s recension seeks a diagrammatic arrangement consonant with *Sefer Yetzirah*’s letter classes.

**[Documented later diagrammatic tradition]** In the commonly reproduced Warsaw 1884 Gra diagram, republished by Aryeh Kaplan, the sefirot have a geometry and numbering unlike Kircher’s. Tiferet may appear as the fourth geometric center rather than Kircher’s sixth sefirah.

**[Reconstructed placement]** In commonly published transcriptions of that Gra diagram, Nun joins Gevurah and Yesod.

This produces the central comparison:

| Diagrammatic tradition | Nun joins | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Tiferet–Netzach | Secure in Western occult diagrams |
| Common modern Ari table | Netzach–Yesod | Later reconstruction; not an Ari autograph |
| Alternative “Ari-rule” diagram | Gevurah–Tiferet | Conflicting reconstruction |
| Commonly reproduced Gra tree | Gevurah–Yesod | Based on the later printed Gra diagram |

The Jewish diagrams do not make Nun “path 24” in the Golden Dawn’s sense.

## 10. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and Hasidism

### Prayer

The clearest liturgical presence is Nun’s absence from Psalm 145 in *Ashrei* and the rabbinic explanation that Samekh supports the fallen Nun. Some liturgical traditions add verses around *Ashrei*, but no normative Jewish prayer invokes a “twenty-fourth Path of Nun.”

### Torah

The inverted signs around Numbers 10:35–36 are Nun’s principal graphic peculiarity in the Torah scroll tradition.

### Counting the Omer

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** The Omer’s seven weeks are commonly mapped to the seven lower sefirot, producing forty-nine combinations from Ḥesed she-be-Ḥesed to Malkhut she-be-Malkhut.

**[Later homiletic association]** Nun/50, the fifty gates, and Shavuot as the fiftieth day provide a natural symbolic cluster.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** There is no classical Omer day designated “the Path of Nun.”

### *Tanya*

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** *Tanya* has no chapter devoted to Nun or Path 24. It treats:

- divine speech and Hebrew letters;
- the ten sefirot;
- the four worlds;
- concealment, vitality, and transformation.

These themes can inform a Hasidic meditation on Nun, but identifying a “Tanya chapter on Nun” is erroneous.

### Naḥman of Breslov

Rebbe Naḥman repeatedly treats descent as potentially preparatory to ascent and emphasizes refusing despair.

**[Hasidic interpretation]** Later Breslov teaching can associate this with נפל, Nun’s fall, and Samekh’s support.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** Naḥman did not create a systematic Scorpio–Death–Path 24 doctrine.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones nongentae* presented 900 theses, including propositions “according to the opinion of the Hebrew Kabbalists.”

**[Documented text]** Pico reread Jewish Kabbalah as confirming Christian doctrines, including Trinity, Incarnation, and the divinity of Jesus.

**[Scholarly assessment]** This marks a decisive emergence of Christian Cabala: Jewish texts and techniques were appropriated into a Christian apologetic program.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** Pico does not attest:

- Nun as Path 24;
- Tiferet–Netzach;
- Nun–Scorpio–Death;
- a tarot correspondence.

There is consequently no meaningful Pico Latin title for “the Path of Nun.” Where Hebrew letters occur, Nun is simply the Hebrew letter or its transliteration.

---

## 2. Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica*, 1517

Johann Reuchlin’s dialogue was the first large-scale Christian exposition of Cabala by a major Christian Hebraist.

**[Documented text]** He treats Hebrew language, divine names, letter permutation, number, and Pythagorean-Christian metaphysics.

**[Scholarly assessment]** Reuchlin knew more Hebrew than most occult successors, but his purpose remained Christian and concordist.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** Reuchlin supplies no Nun–Death or fixed Tiferet–Netzach path. *Nun* remains the Hebrew letter name; *via Nun* or *semita Nun* is not a developed Reuchlin doctrine.

---

## 3. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

The Latin Gikatilla translation slightly predates Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica*.

**[Documented visual history]** Its title-page woodcut presents ten named emanations with organic branches. It helped make the Sephirotic tree visible in Christian print culture.

**[Disputed priority]** It is often called the earliest printed Sephirotic tree, though manuscript *ilanot* are centuries earlier and the precise definition of “tree diagram” affects priority claims.

No alphabetic path sequence or tarot occurs on the woodcut.

## 4. Athanasius Kircher, 1652–1654

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part 1, contains a *Cabala Hebraeorum* and the plate that became decisive for Western occultism.

### Kircher’s path layout

**[Documented text and plate]** Kircher represented ten sefirot connected by twenty-two paths and placed the Hebrew letters consecutively along them. In this arrangement Nun connects Tiferet and Netzach.

Because the ten sefirot are treated as paths 1–10 and Aleph begins the letter paths at 11:

\[
10 + 14 = 24
\]

Thus Nun becomes Path 24.

Kircher’s Latin title for the associated intelligence is:

> *Vigesima quarta semita dicitur Intelligentia imaginativa.*

### Historical significance

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Kircher did not invent every component of his tree, but his printed plate stabilized a particular topology for Christian readers. It does not reproduce a single authoritative Ari or Gra diagram.

**[Hermetic-occult inheritance]** The Golden Dawn adopted essentially this connectivity. That is why its Nun runs from Tiferet to Netzach.

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, *Kabbala Denudata*, 1677–1684

Knorr translated and excerpted Zoharic, Cordoverian, and especially Lurianic materials into Latin. The title is:

> *Kabbala denudata, seu doctrina Hebraeorum transcendentalis et metaphysica atque theologica.*

**[Documented historical role]** It became the most influential Latin reservoir of Jewish Kabbalah for later European esotericism.

**[Textual finding]** The Latin vocabulary includes formulations such as *semita* for path and *intelligentia* for intellect. The “twenty-fourth Imaginative Intelligence” circulates in this Latinizing environment.

**[Absence-of-evidence finding]** Knorr does not originate Nun = Death. Tarot is not part of his translated Lurianic system.

S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s *The Kabbalah Unveiled* (1887) translated selections from Knorr and helped deliver this Christian-Lurianic hybrid to English occultism.

## 6. Tarot before Lévi

**[Documented history]** Tarot cards originated as fifteenth-century European playing cards. Death was already a familiar triumphal image, usually a skeleton or personification of death. The card was often unnumbered or differently numbered in early packs.

**[Scholarly consensus]** There is no historical evidence that the earliest tarot designers encoded *Sefer Yetzirah*, Hebrew letters, or an Egyptian initiatory book.

**[Occult legend]** Eighteenth-century writers such as Court de Gébelin and Etteilla asserted Egyptian or primordial origins. These claims transformed tarot into an esoteric book but are not supported by the card game’s material history.

## 7. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

In *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*, Lévi connected the twenty-two Hebrew letters to twenty-two tarot trumps.

**[Documented occult attribution]** Lévi’s sequence did not yet produce Golden Dawn Nun–Death. Beginning Aleph with the Magician made:

- Mem correspond to Death XIII;
- Nun correspond to Temperance XIV.

**[Historical significance]** Lévi established the influential structural claim “twenty-two letters = twenty-two trumps,” but the Golden Dawn revised his ordering.

Therefore the statement “Lévi assigned Nun to Death” is false.

## 8. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### Construction of the correspondence

The Golden Dawn, founded in 1888 by William Wynn Westcott, S. L. MacGregor Mathers, and William Robert Woodman, combined:

1. Kircher’s tree topology;
2. *Sefer Yetzirah*’s letter classes and zodiac;
3. the medieval list of thirty-two intelligences;
4. Lévi’s letter–tarot principle;
5. a revised tarot order with the Fool as 0/Aleph.

That synthesis yielded:

\[
\text{Nun} \rightarrow \text{Scorpio} \rightarrow \text{Death XIII}
\]

and

\[
\text{Path 24} \rightarrow \text{Tiferet–Netzach}
\]

This is a **[Hermetic-occult attribution]**, not a Jewish one.

### Golden Dawn reasoning

The combination is symbolically coherent inside the Golden Dawn system:

- Scorpio was traditionally ruled by Mars.
- Scorpio’s scorpion, serpent, eagle, and regenerative symbolism lent itself to cycles of death and transformation.
- The Death trump already depicted mortality and change.
- Tiferet represents solar selfhood, sacrifice, and integration.
- Netzach represents desire, instinct, emotion, attraction, and Venusian nature.
- Nun therefore dramatizes the transformation of instinctual-emotional nature on the approach to Tiferet.

This reasoning is synthetic and initiatory; it is not an inference made by the ancient author of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### *Book T*

The Golden Dawn title is:

> “Child of the Great Transformers; Lord of the Gates of Death.”

Its imagery includes:

- a skeleton and scythe;
- severed or fallen crowned heads;
- scorpion, serpent, eagle, and transformed forms;
- fermentation, putrefaction, dissolution, and regeneration.

Its divinatory meaning includes time, age, involuntary transformation, change, destruction, and—only under appropriately confirming conditions—physical death. Responsible tarot practice does not automatically predict literal death from the card.

### Four color scales

| World/scale | Golden Dawn color |
|---|---|
| Atzilut, King scale | Green-blue |
| Briah, Queen scale | Dull brown |
| Yetzirah, Prince scale | Very dark brown |
| Assiah, Princess scale | Livid indigo-brown, “like a black beetle” |

**Textual variant:** Some *Liber 777* tables print “Venetian red” for the Prince scale. Golden Dawn color manuscripts and Regardie-derived tables commonly give very dark brown. Both readings should be reported rather than silently harmonized.

### Portal ritual

The Portal material associates Nun with Scorpio and Water, while also emphasizing Scorpio’s fiery or Martian transformative power. Nun, Samekh, and Ayin form part of the set of paths confronted before entry into the Adeptus Minor/Tiferet grade.

### Pathworking

Golden Dawn-derived pathworking typically proceeds by:

1. establishing the corresponding spheres;
2. visualizing the tarot trump as a doorway;
3. vibrating appropriate names;
4. entering a landscape formed from the card, colors, and zodiac;
5. testing encountered images through pentagrams, divine names, or symbolic consistency;
6. recording the vision and returning by the same path.

**[Modern ritual tradition]** These procedures descend from Golden Dawn “Rising on the Planes” and skrying practices. They are not Jewish prayer or classical *merkavah* ascent.

For Nun, recurrent modern reports include caverns, tombs, marshes, autumnal fields, putrefying vegetation, scorpions, serpents, skeletal guardians, and emergence into sunrise or renewed growth. Such reports are experiential literature, not independently verifiable geography.

# The Qliphah and the tunnel

## Jewish background

קליפה, *qelippah*, literally means a shell, peel, or husk.

**[Documented Jewish mystical tradition]** In Zoharic and Lurianic sources, the *qelippot* are forces or structures of concealment, impurity, fragmentation, and unbalanced judgment. They belong to the *sitra aḥra*, the “other side.”

**[Historical caution]** Jewish sources do not present one universally fixed “Tree of Death” with ten English-transliterated orders, one demon prince per sphere, and twenty-two named tunnels.

## The ten Hermetic qliphotic orders

Crowley’s *777* transmits the familiar ten names. Golden Dawn papers give a related list of “evil chiefs,” while warning that their placement cannot always be made strictly.

| Sefirah | Qliphotic order | Common occult gloss | Common chief/prince | Evidentiary status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Keter | Thaumiel | Twins/contending forces of God | Satan and Moloch | Later Hermetic table |
| Chokhmah | Ghagiel | Hinderers/confusion | Beelzebub | Mapping varies |
| Binah | Satariel | Concealers | Lucifuge Rofocale | Mapping varies |
| Ḥesed | Ghaʿagsheblah/Gamchicoth | Devourers or breakers | Astaroth | Later Hermetic |
| Gevurah | Golachab/Golab | Burners or incendiaries | Asmodeus | Later Hermetic |
| Tiferet | Thagirion | Disputers/ugly or false beauty | Belphegor | Later Hermetic |
| Netzach | Aʿarab Zaraq | Ravens of dispersion | Baal | Later Hermetic |
| Hod | Samael | Poison of God | Adrammelech | Later Hermetic |
| Yesod | Gamaliel | Obscene or polluted forces | Lilith | Later Hermetic |
| Malkhut | Lilith/Nehemoth | Night specters/whisperers | Naamah | Later Hermetic |

The Golden Dawn chief-list sometimes reverses or confuses the Chokhmah/Binah order. The source itself attributes the chiefs only “according to the opinion of some,” which is good evidence against treating the chart as an ancient unanimity.

## Which qliphah belongs to Nun?

Strictly, Nun is a path, not a sefirah. It therefore has no one member of the tenfold qliphah list.

Its relevant layers are:

1. **[Golden Dawn/Crowley]** The adverse order Necheshthiron, “Brazen Serpents.”
2. **[Crowley, *Liber 231*]** The qliphotic genius Niantiel.
3. **[Grant, 1977]** Niantiel becomes the named twenty-fourth Tunnel of Set between the reverse of Thagirion and Aʿarab Zaraq.
4. **[Endpoint chiefs]** Belphegor and Baal belong to the corresponding adverse spheres in the later chief-list.

These layers should not be collapsed into the claim “the Jewish demon of Nun is Niantiel.” No such Jewish textual attribution has been found.

## Diagrams and manuscripts

### Medieval *ilanot*

**[Documented material history]** Diagrammatic scrolls and trees existed in Jewish manuscript culture well before Kircher. Surviving examples include fourteenth-century Spanish and Italian *ilanot*. Their structures, uses, and symbolic conventions vary.

### *Portae Lucis*, 1516

The Augsburg title-page is a milestone in printed Christian reception. Its branches do not amount to the Golden Dawn’s twenty-two numbered paths.

### Cordoverian print trees

Posthumous editions of Cordovero’s writings contain diagrams, but printed versions are not always identical and cannot simply be treated as an authorial master-image.

### Kircher’s plate

Kircher’s plate is the direct diagrammatic ancestor of the most familiar Western occult Tree. The Golden Dawn superimposed color scales, tarot, astrology, and initiatory grades upon this topology.

### Golden Dawn colored trees

Order manuscripts represented the Tree separately in the four color scales of Atzilut, Briah, Yetzirah, and Assiah. These are practical magical diagrams, not reconstructions of ancient Jewish manuscript art.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Date of *Sefer Yetzirah*

The range extends from Roman-period proposals to early medieval redaction. Its terse core, recensional instability, and late manuscript evidence prevent a precise date. Nun–Scorpio is old within the received recension history, but smell/intestines cannot safely be assigned to the earliest recoverable layer.

## 2. Is the “Thirty-two Intelligences” list part of *Sefer Yetzirah*?

Probably not.

Its literary form differs from the core work, it is appended only in parts of the transmission, and its letter alignment varies in later editions. Calling “Imaginative Intelligence” the original *Sefer Yetzirah* name of Nun is misleading.

## 3. Zoharic authorship

Traditional attribution to Shimon bar Yoḥai and scholarly attribution to Moses de León’s late thirteenth-century milieu express different kinds of claims. The former is sacred-authorial tradition; the latter rests on language, historical circulation, manuscript evidence, literary development, and testimony surrounding de León.

## 4. Fish or snake?

“Fish” is the securely transmitted letter-name. The claim that the earliest pictograph was a fish is less secure; Proto-Sinaitic evidence permits a snake interpretation. Letter-name etymology and graphic ancestry are related questions, not identical ones.

## 5. Kircher versus Jewish trees

Kircher’s layout is neither “the ancient Tree” nor a faithful reproduction of one uncontested Jewish diagram. Ari, Gra, Cordoverian, Zoharic, and manuscript *ilan* arrangements differ.

The Golden Dawn’s success made Kircher’s topology appear normative in English-language occultism.

## 6. Does the Ari put Nun on Netzach–Yesod?

No autograph tree settles the question. “Ari tree” is a modern umbrella for several reconstructions. Netzach–Yesod is one widespread table; it should be cited as a reconstruction, not an uncontested teaching of Luria.

## 7. Tarot antiquity

The Death card is medieval-European, while Nun–Death is a late nineteenth-century Golden Dawn synthesis. Lévi’s earlier system actually assigned Nun to Temperance.

Claims of an ancient Egyptian or Mosaic tarot are occult legends unsupported by the surviving cards and documents.

## 8. The Tzaddi/Heh correction

Crowley’s exchange of Emperor and Star changes Heh and Tzaddi. It does not change Nun, Scorpio, or Death.

## 9. Mars versus Pluto

Mars is the classical ruler of Scorpio and therefore the only ruler relevant to *Sefer Yetzirah*, Renaissance astrology, the Golden Dawn, and early Crowley. Pluto was discovered in 1930. Its status as Scorpio’s modern ruler is a twentieth-century astrological innovation.

## 10. Is Death the meaning of Nun?

No at the philological level: Nun means “fish.”

Death enters through tarot. Jewish Nun lore instead emphasizes fish, faithfulness, falling and being supported, fifty gates, soul, spinal transmission, and completion. Transformation provides a genuine thematic bridge, but that bridge is interpretive rather than etymological.

## 11. The qliphotic “Tree of Death”

Jewish Kabbalah has *qelippot* and the *sitra aḥra*. The rigid ten-order chart with fixed demon princes is a later Christian-Hermetic systematization. The twenty-two Tunnels of Set are later still, chiefly Grant’s 1977 elaboration of Crowley’s *Liber 231* names.

## 12. Daʿat

*Sefer Yetzirah* says “ten and not eleven.” In later Kabbalah, Daʿat can be:

- a mode or manifestation of consciousness;
- the conjunction of Ḥokhmah and Binah;
- counted when Keter is not counted;
- represented diagrammatically without being an independent eleventh sefirah.

Western occult diagrams sometimes call it the “false” or “invisible” sefirah. Its status does not alter Nun’s Kircher endpoints, though alternative diagrams around the Abyss can change the overall path topology.

## 13. Christian appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Kircher, and Knorr preserved and transmitted Jewish materials while subordinating them to Christian theology, universalism, Hermetic Egyptology, or missionary claims. The resulting “Cabala” is historically significant but cannot be cited as if it were simply Jewish Kabbalah in Latin.

## 14. “A thousand-year-old Path of Nun”

This phrase conflates separate layers:

- ancient and late-antique Hebrew letter traditions;
- medieval Jewish letter mysticism;
- a medieval list of intelligences;
- Kircher’s seventeenth-century diagram;
- eighteenth- and nineteenth-century occult tarot;
- Golden Dawn correspondences;
- Crowley’s syncretic tables;
- Grant’s twentieth-century tunnels.

The complete Path of Nun system is late nineteenth-century in its recognizable form.

# Open questions

1. Which *Sefer Yetzirah* reading of Nun’s faculty and organ best represents the earliest recension? Existing evidence does not settle this.
2. When exactly did the “Thirty-two Intelligences” tract first acquire its fixed numbered form?
3. Was its pseudo-Raavad attribution the work of one redactor or a gradual accretion?
4. Which manuscript first explicitly aligned the twenty-fourth intelligence with Nun?
5. What precise diagrammatic sources mediated between Renaissance Jewish *ilanot* and Kircher’s published plate?
6. How much of Kircher’s path arrangement came through Jewish converts, Christian Hebraists, or his own graphic systematization?
7. Which Golden Dawn manuscript first gives the complete Nun–Death color-scale table?
8. Is “Venetian red” in *777* an authorial Crowley variant, a manuscript divergence, or a typographic corruption of the Golden Dawn’s “very dark brown”?
9. What is the definitive Hebrew spelling intended behind Grant’s “Niantiel,” and how is his assigned value 160 calculated? Grant does not provide a philologically compelling derivation.
10. Can an autographic or early Lurianic source be found that fixes Nun to one pair of sefirot? No such source was identified in this investigation.
11. How early is the tribe-Manasseh attribution? It is common in modern Jewish teaching but absent from *Sefer Yetzirah* itself.
12. To what extent did Golden Dawn adepts know the recension problems in *Sefer Yetzirah*, as opposed to relying on Westcott’s English edition?
13. How did modern psychological tarot readings of Death feed back into later Jewish-themed popular Kabbalah? This remains a reception-history question rather than a question about classical texts.

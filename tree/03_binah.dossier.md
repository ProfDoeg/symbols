# Binah: Research Dossier

## Method and evidence key

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **[TEXT]** Directly documented in a primary text or historical edition.
- **[TRADITION]** A teaching transmitted within Jewish Kabbalah, including interpretations that may postdate the text to which they are attached.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]** A historical or philological reconstruction by modern scholars.
- **[HERMETIC]** A Christian Cabalist, Golden Dawn, Thelemic, or later occult attribution.
- **[DISPUTED]** A claim whose date, authorship, reading, or provenance remains contested.
- **[MODERN]** A recent synthesis or invention not documented in the older sources.

The word “classical” is used cautiously. A correspondence may be early in Western occultism without being ancient, Jewish, or present in *Sefer Yetzirah*.

# The name

## Hebrew spelling, grammar, and etymology

**[TEXT]** The standard form is **בִּינָה**, *bīnāh* in precise academic transliteration, normally *Binah* or *Bina* in English. It is a feminine noun from the Hebrew root **ב־י־ן**, associated with understanding, discernment, perceiving distinctions, and considering relations. The root also underlies **בֵּין**, “between,” but the popular explanation “Binah means separation because it comes from *between*” is an interpretive association, not a complete historical etymology. The basic lexical sense is “understanding” or “discernment.” See the [BDB entry for בין](https://www.sefaria.org/BDB%2C_%D7%91%D6%B4%D6%BC%D7%99%D7%9F).

The final **־ה** is the normal feminine ending. It is not, by itself, evidence for the later personification of Binah as Mother. The grammatical gender made that symbolism easier, but the maternal theology is a medieval kabbalistic development.

## Biblical sources and proof-texts

### Proverbs 3:19

**[TEXT]**

> יְהוָה בְּחָכְמָה יָסַד־אָרֶץ כּוֹנֵן שָׁמַיִם בִּתְבוּנָה  
> “YHWH founded the earth by wisdom; He established the heavens by understanding.”

The verse uses **תְּבוּנָה**, *tevunah*, a cognate of Binah, not the exact noun **בינה**. Medieval kabbalists nevertheless read it as a scriptural pairing of Ḥokhmah and Binah. [Proverbs 3:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19)

### Job 28

**[TEXT]**

> וְהַחָכְמָה מֵאַיִן תִּמָּצֵא וְאֵי זֶה מְקוֹם בִּינָה  
> “Where shall wisdom be found, and where is the place of understanding?”

Job 28:12 and 28:20 pair Ḥokhmah and Binah while stressing their hidden divine origin. The verse originally concerns inaccessible wisdom, not a mapped third sefirah. Its sefirotic application is **[TRADITION]**. [Job 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28)

The phrase **מֵאַיִן**, “from where/from nothing,” was also susceptible to kabbalistic rereading: Wisdom comes “from *Ayin*,” Nothingness, associated with Keter or the hidden divine. That reading is mystical wordplay, not the plain syntax of Job.

### Proverbs 2:3 and the Mother pun

**[TEXT]**

> כִּי אִם לַבִּינָה תִקְרָא  
> “If you call out to understanding…”

**[TRADITION]** Kabbalists read **אִם**, “if,” as though it were **אֵם**, “mother”: “Call Understanding Mother.” This pun underlies Bahiric and later maternal symbolism. The consonantal Torah text permits the association, but the Masoretic vocalization distinguishes the words.

### Isaiah

Several Isaianic verses enter the Binah complex:

- **Isaiah 6:10:** “his heart will understand, return, and be healed.” **[TRADITION]** This supports the triad Binah–heart–teshuvah.
- **Isaiah 11:2:** the messianic spirit includes **רוּחַ חָכְמָה וּבִינָה**, “a spirit of wisdom and understanding.”
- **Isaiah 33:7:** **אֶרְאֶלָּם**, usually “their heroes/valiant ones,” became relevant to the angelic name *Erelim/Aralim*.
- **Isaiah 40:28:** God’s understanding is unsearchable, another source for the immeasurability of Binah.

[Isaiah 6](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.6) and [Isaiah 33](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.33)

### 1 Chronicles 29:11

**[TEXT]**

> לְךָ יְהוָה הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד … לְךָ יְהוָה הַמַּמְלָכָה

This verse supplies names for the seven lower sefirot: Gedullah/Ḥesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod through “all” or *kol*, and Malkhut. **It does not name Binah.** Cordovero therefore treats the repeated **לְךָ יהוה**, “Yours, YHWH,” as a hint to the three supernals rather than finding Binah among the seven named qualities. [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11), [Pardes Rimmonim 1:8:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.1.8.1)

Cordovero adds a gematria: **לך** equals fifty, suggesting Binah’s Fifty Gates. This is **[TRADITION]**, not the verse’s historical meaning.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*: before Binah became “the third sefirah”

### Date and recensions

**[DISPUTED/SCHOLARSHIP]** *Sefer Yetzirah* cannot be dated securely. Proposals range from late antiquity into the early Islamic period. Its linguistic and conceptual strata may not all be contemporaneous. Saadia Gaon commented on it in the early tenth century, establishing a firm terminus before which a substantial form existed.

Peter Hayman’s critical edition compares nineteen manuscripts and distinguishes Short, Long, and Saadyan textual families. Avishai Bar-Asher has challenged the older assumption that the Saadyan recension is merely a late artificial version, pointing to early Genizah evidence. [Hayman’s critical edition](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/), [Bar-Asher’s study](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/the-earliest-version-of-sefer-yeirah-text-format-structure-and-ge/)

The so-called **Ari recension** is an early-modern kabbalistic recension associated with Lurianic circles; the **Gra recension** reflects the Vilna Gaon’s eighteenth-century textual and interpretive work. Neither is an untouched “original” ancient text.

### The thirty-two paths

**[TEXT]** *Sefer Yetzirah* 1:1 begins with:

> בִּשְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם נְתִיבוֹת פְּלִיאוֹת חָכְמָה  
> “With thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom…”

These are then enumerated as:

- Ten **ספירות בלימה**, *sefirot belimah*.
- Twenty-two foundation letters.
- Three Mothers: **א מ ש**.
- Seven Doubles: traditionally **ב ג ד כ פ ר ת**.
- Twelve Simples: the remaining twelve letters.

[Sefer Yetzirah 1:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.1)

### What the ten sefirot are in this text

**[TEXT]** The ten are not yet the familiar named sequence Keter–Ḥokhmah–Binah–… . They are “ten sefirot belimah,” associated with number, measure, spirit, water, fire, and the six directions.

A representative sequence is:

1. Spirit of the living God.
2. Spirit/breath from spirit.
3. Water from breath.
4. Fire from water.
5–10. The six directional seals.

[Sefer Yetzirah 1:12](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.12)

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Scholem and many later historians therefore distinguish the numerical-cosmological sefirot of *Sefer Yetzirah* from the divine attributes or potencies of medieval Kabbalah.

### Is Binah present?

**[TEXT]** Binah is present as an intellectual action:

> הָבֵן בְּחָכְמָה וַחֲכַם בְּבִינָה  
> “Understand with wisdom, and be wise with understanding.”

But **[ABSENCE OF EVIDENCE]** the work does not say “the third sefirah is Binah.” That alignment belongs to the medieval theosophical rereading of the older tenfold scheme.

### Saadia, Ari, and Gra

- **Saadia Gaon:** reads the book philosophically and cosmologically, within Jewish monotheism; its sefirot are created measures, not parts of God.
- **Ari recension:** harmonizes the text with early-modern Kabbalah and is often used in later devotional and occult translations.
- **Gra recension:** attempts a tightly structured reading of letters, directions, organs, planets, and signs.

**[DISPUTED]** Popular books often speak as if the Ari and Gra each inherited a single ancient, fully drawn “Tree.” In fact, the surviving textual traditions, commentaries, and later printed diagrams have to be distinguished.

### The “Sanctifying Intelligence”

**[HERMETIC]** The famous third-path paragraph is not part of the ancient recension:

> “The Third Path is the Sanctifying Intelligence…”

Westcott printed it in the appendix to his 1893 *Sepher Yetzirah*, translating a line of transmission through Rittangelius’s 1642 Latin edition. Kircher likewise gives:

> *Tertia semita vocatur intelligentia santificans…*

It describes the third path as the basis of primordial wisdom and parent of faith. This is an appended medieval/early-modern “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom” text, subsequently fused with *Sefer Yetzirah* in Christian and occult reception. [Westcott, 1893, pp. 27–28](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1893__westcott___sepher_yetzirah.pdf), [Kircher’s Latin text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

## 2. *Sefer ha-Bahir*: Binah becomes Mother

### Date and provenance

**[TRADITION]** The *Bahir* is attributed to Rabbi Neḥunya ben ha-Qanah, a first-century sage.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Its extant form is anonymous and composite, and it appeared in Provence in the late twelfth century. It is usually treated as the first surviving work in which the ten sefirot become a dynamic map of the divine interior rather than merely cosmological numbers. [Complete *Bahir* overview](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir/), [Joseph Dan’s historical introduction](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/sefer-ha-bahir-the-book-of-brilliance/)

### Binah as Mother

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** The *Bahir* exploits Proverbs 2:3, reading “if” as “mother.” The mother is associated with the world to come and the children with the sevenfold created order. In the bird’s-nest interpretation, the “mother” is Binah and the children are lower powers.

Numbering varies among editions. The familiar English phrase “you shall call Understanding a Mother” is associated with Kaplan’s arrangement, but it should not be treated as a literal translation of every manuscript at “§75”; Sefaria’s current §75 is a different paragraph because its numbering follows another textual arrangement. [Sefaria’s *Sefer HaBahir*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir), [Daniel Abrams’s critical-edition discussion](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~kazhdan/Shneider/Abrams.Bahir.1-54.pdf)

This discrepancy is itself important: **quotation and section number must be tied to an edition.**

### Historical significance

The *Bahir* supplies several enduring Binah motifs:

- Mother of the seven lower powers.
- The hidden source from which manifest qualities emerge.
- The world to come.
- A mediating feminine potency inside the divine world.

It does not yet supply the Golden Dawn package of Saturn, Tzaphqiel, Aralim, four colours, and tarot cards.

## 3. Gerona and thirteenth-century Kabbalah

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** In the schools of Isaac the Blind, Azriel of Gerona, Ezra of Gerona, and their successors, the ten sefirot became an increasingly ordered divine pleroma. Ḥokhmah and Binah were treated as paired intellectual principles, often related as seed and development, father and mother, or undifferentiated wisdom and articulated understanding.

This period is decisive for the movement from:

> ten cosmological numbers → ten named divine potencies → diagrammed relationships.

The terminology was not absolutely uniform. Different authors could count, name, or describe the upper powers differently, and graphical trees were not yet standardized.

## 4. Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla composed *Shaʿarei Orah* around the end of the thirteenth century. It ascends through divine names and sefirot, beginning below and moving upward.

### The divine name

**[TEXT]** Gikatilla states:

> ושמה המיוחד לה בתורה הוא יהו״ה … וניקוד אותיות הוא ניקוד אלהים  
> “Its distinctive name in the Torah is YHWH … with the vocalization of Elohim.”

[Shaʿarei Orah, Fifth Gate](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Fifth_Gate%2C_Sixth_Sefirah.85)

This is the strongest Jewish source behind the later formula **YHWH Elohim**. It is more precise than simply treating the two words as a spoken compound. In Jewish liturgical practice the Tetragrammaton is not pronounced “Jehovah” or “Yahweh.”

### First *heh* and judgment

**[TEXT/TRADITION]**

- Yod corresponds to Ḥokhmah.
- The first **ה** corresponds to Binah.
- Binah is the beginning or root of articulated judgment.
- Judgment is not evil: it is the differentiation and limitation necessary for ordered creation.

This is one ancestry of the later Hermetic idea that Binah is “form,” but the Hermetic force/form polarity should not be substituted for Gikatilla’s own theological language.

### Angelic and bodily lists

Gikatilla’s project concerns divine names and sefirotic channels. It does not present the complete later Golden Dawn row. In particular, **Tzaphqiel + Aralim + Saturn + black + myrrh + yoni** is not a single list in *Shaʿarei Orah*.

## 5. The Zohar

### Date and authorship

**[TRADITION]** The Zohar is attributed to Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle in second-century Galilee.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Its central corpus emerged in late thirteenth-century Castile and was circulated by Moses de León. Scholem argued strongly for de León as the principal author. More recent scholars—including Yehuda Liebes, Ronit Meroz, Daniel Abrams, Elliot Wolfson, and others—have emphasized multiple strata, circles, and redactional processes. Daniel Matt’s Pritzker edition translates the Zohar as a medieval Aramaic corpus while taking its pseudepigraphic world seriously as literature.

### Binah as the Sea

**[TEXT]** In *Zohar*, Bo:

> “Afterward He made a great vessel and called it Sea, and called it Binah.”

The passage first speaks of the small vessel Yod/Wisdom and then the great vessel Sea/Understanding, followed by seven vessels representing the lower powers. [Zohar, Bo 15:228](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar%2C_Bo.15.228)

This is a major source for:

- **The Great Sea**.
- Binah as a capacious vessel.
- The emergence of the seven lower sefirot from the supernal pair.
- The mother-and-children structure.

### Supernal Mother

**[TRADITION]** Zoharic names and images include:

- **אימא עילאה**, *Imma Ilaʾah*, Supernal Mother.
- Mother of the children.
- Jubilee/Freedom.
- World to come.
- Palace or womb of gestation.
- The upper *heh* of YHWH.
- Teshuvah, return.

The language of father, mother, king, queen, son, and daughter is symbolic and relational. It does not imply that medieval Kabbalists believed in separate gods.

### Jubilee and Fifty Gates

Binah’s association with Jubilee rests on the fiftieth year of liberation and the tradition of the **Fifty Gates of Understanding**. The lower seven are expanded through sevenfold structures, while the fiftieth is a return to their source.

### Judgment and mercy

Binah is not simply “harsh Saturn.” It can be the root from which differentiated judgment becomes possible, but when united with Ḥokhmah it is also freedom, mercy, redemption, and fecundity. Later occult descriptions of a uniformly gloomy Binah select only one side of this older polarity.

## 6. Cordovero: *Pardes Rimmonim* and *Or Neʾerav*

### Historical position

Moses Cordovero completed *Pardes Rimmonim* in Safed in 1548. It was first printed posthumously at Cracow in 1592. It is the most systematic synthesis of pre-Lurianic Kabbalah.

### Fifty Gates and “Yours, YHWH”

**[TEXT]** Cordovero reads **לך**, “to You/Yours,” as fifty by gematria and associates it with Binah’s Fifty Gates. [Pardes Rimmonim 1:8:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.1.8.1)

### Jubilee

**[TEXT]** Binah is Jubilee because the fiftieth year releases bonds and restores freedom. When Binah joins Ḥokhmah, mercy and redemption predominate. [Pardes Rimmonim 23:10:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.23.10.2)

### The first *heh*

Cordovero repeats the widespread assignment of the first *heh* of YHWH to Binah. The opening yod is Ḥokhmah; the expanded heh is the articulation and development of what was concentrated in the yod.

### Mother crouching over her children

In Cordovero’s reading of the letter aleph, the second yod can signify Binah turned downward:

> “the mother crouching over the children to nurse them.”

[Pardes Rimmonim 27:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.27.4)

### Saturn

**[TEXT]** Cordovero explicitly relates Binah to Saturn:

> “its rule is in Saturn … for Binah is also the Great Sabbath.”

[Pardes Rimmonim 27:15](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.27.15)

This is critical evidence. Saturn–Binah is not merely a nineteenth-century invention. Yet Cordovero’s Sabbath/Saturn interpretation does not automatically validate every later Saturnian colour, metal, perfume, demon, and tarot correspondence.

### Judgment and the two mothers

Cordovero records disagreement over whether Ḥokhmah and Binah should be characterized primarily as mercy and judgment. Binah contains red and white aspects: differentiated judgment and the mercy received from Ḥokhmah. [Pardes Rimmonim 8:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.8.5)

This is an older basis for the later contrast:

- **Ama**, dark/sterile mother.
- **Aima**, bright/fertile mother.

But the exact English opposition “dark sterile mother/bright fertile mother” is the Golden Dawn’s formula, not a quotation from Cordovero.

### Aralim

Cordovero discusses traditional lists of ten angelic ranks, including **אראלים**, *Erelim*. [Pardes Rimmonim 24:15](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.24.15)

However, the cited passage does not itself securely establish the full equation:

> Binah = Aralim = Christian Thrones = Tzaphqiel.

That cross-system row is clearest in Agrippa.

### *Or Neʾerav*

*Or Neʾerav*, first printed in Venice in 1587, is an introduction to the prerequisites, discipline, and proper study of Kabbalah. **[ABSENCE OF EVIDENCE]** It does not supply a substantially new independent Binah correspondence table. Its importance is methodological: sefirotic learning must be rooted in Torah, ethics, and disciplined transmission.

## 7. Luria through Ḥayyim Vital’s *Etz Chaim*

### Historical qualification

Isaac Luria wrote little systematic prose. His teachings were recorded and organized by disciples, above all Ḥayyim Vital. “The Lurianic doctrine” therefore reaches us through layered manuscripts and editions, with differences among Vitalian recensions and later redactions.

### Tzimtzum and the worlds

**[TRADITION]** Lurianic cosmology describes:

1. The contraction or concealment called *tzimtzum*.
2. The ray entering the vacated space.
3. Adam Qadmon and primordial configurations.
4. Worlds of Emanation, Creation, Formation, and Action.
5. The breaking of vessels.
6. Reconfiguration and repair.

The four worlds are:

- **אצילות**, Atzilut, Emanation.
- **בריאה**, Beriah, Creation.
- **יצירה**, Yetzirah, Formation.
- **עשיה**, Assiah, Action.

Each world contains a complete tenfold sefirotic structure. Therefore “Binah equals Beriah” is an analogical correspondence, not a claim that Binah exists only in Beriah. [Etz Chaim 1:4:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.4.11), [Etz Chaim 40:13](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.40.13)

### Partzufim

The sefirot become organized as **partzufim**, configurations or “countenances”:

- Keter: Arikh Anpin and Atik.
- Ḥokhmah: Abba.
- Binah: Imma, with further differentiation as Imma Ilaʾah and Tevunah.
- Six lower emotional sefirot: Zeʿir Anpin.
- Malkhut: Nukva.

Binah is no longer merely a static sphere. As **Imma**, it receives from Abba, gestates the lower configurations, clothes their minds, and supplies expanded consciousness.

### Gestation of the seven kings

**[TEXT]** *Etz Chaim* describes the seven kings as being in the womb of Binah and rising as feminine waters. When the vessels fail and break, the configurations of Abba and Imma are affected and turn back-to-back. [Etz Chaim 9:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.1)

This is part of the doctrine of **shevirat ha-kelim**, the breaking of the vessels.

### Shevirah and tikkun

The vessels associated with the primordial world of points cannot sustain the light. Their breaking disperses sparks into lower existence. **Tikkun**, repair, reorganizes the sefirot into relational partzufim capable of giving and receiving.

Binah/Imma is central to tikkun because she:

- Gestates configurations.
- Mediates consciousness to Zeʿir Anpin.
- Encloses and clothes lower levels.
- Transforms isolated points into relationships.

### The “two mothers”

Lurianic literature differentiates higher Imma and Tevunah. This is one source behind later occult distinctions among mother forms. It should not be reduced to a simple binary of “good fertile mother” and “evil barren mother.” Both higher and lower maternal configurations participate in divine order.

## 8. The Gra and the Jewish Tree

The Vilna Gaon, Elijah ben Solomon Zalman (1720–1797), wrote commentaries on *Sefer Yetzirah*, *Sifra di-Tzeniuta*, and other kabbalistic works.

### The Gra recension

**[TRADITION/SCHOLARSHIP]** The Gra’s recension seeks to recover a coherent textual structure from variant readings. Its diagrammatic interpretation places strong weight on:

- Three Mothers.
- Seven Doubles.
- Twelve Simples.
- Directions, organs, planets, signs, and letter order.

The familiar printed “Gra Tree” is documented in the Warsaw 1884 edition and reproduced in modern discussions such as Aryeh Kaplan’s *Sefer Yetzirah*. [Kaplan’s reproduction and discussion](https://leonbahrmanministries.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Aryeh-Kaplan-Sefer-Yetzirah2.pdf)

### Ari, Gra, and Hermetic trees

A useful distinction is:

| System | Primary organizing rule |
|---|---|
| Ari-style diagram | Three Mothers on horizontal channels, seven Doubles on verticals, twelve Simples on diagonals; usually only one lowest path into Malkhut. |
| Gra-style diagram | Also seeks a 3/7/12 geometrical arrangement, but distributes letters and several channels differently. |
| Kircher/Golden Dawn diagram | Twenty-two lines arranged in three pillars and three horizontal levels, with Hebrew letters assigned sequentially from Aleph to Tav in occult path order. Malkhut receives three paths. |

**[DISPUTED]** Neither “the Ari Tree” nor “the Gra Tree” should be treated as one universally agreed autograph plate. Modern redrawings differ, and authors sometimes conflate topology—the actual connected nodes—with letter assignment.

## 9. Hasidic interpretations

### *Tanya*, chapter 3

**[TEXT]** Shneur Zalman of Liadi maps the soul’s ten faculties to the ten sefirot. The first three are Ḥokhmah, Binah, and Daʿat.

Binah is the expansion of the initial flash of Ḥokhmah into length, breadth, detail, and intelligible structure. Ḥokhmah and Binah are father and mother because contemplation gives birth to love and awe. [Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 3](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.3.3)

This is one of the clearest Jewish psychological definitions:

- Ḥokhmah: seminal intuition.
- Binah: developed comprehension.
- Daʿat: attachment and internalization.

### Binah and teshuvah

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** *Iggeret ha-Teshuvah* identifies Binah with higher teshuvah and quotes the image of the mother crouching over the chicks. [Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah 9](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_III%3B_Iggeret_HaTeshuvah.9.7)

### Binah as heart

**[TEXT]**

> “Binah is the heart, and with it the heart understands.”

[Tanya 43](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.43.11)

The “heart” here is not simply emotion. It is contemplative understanding that becomes lived feeling.

### Naḥman of Bratslav

Naḥman repeatedly develops:

- Binah as the mother/womb in which an idea or spiritual state is formed.
- Binah as repentance and the heart’s return.
- Torah study as conception, gestation, birth, and nursing.
- The Zoharic ascent from **זאת**, “this,” toward **מי**, “who,” a name associated with Binah.

Relevant passages include [Liqquṭei Moharan 48](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.48.1), [49:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.49.6.4), [29:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.29.4.2), and [105](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.105.1).

## 10. Prayer, Omer, calendar, and Torah

### Prayer

**[TEXT]** The weekday Amidah asks God to grant **חכמה בינה ודעת**, wisdom, understanding, and knowledge, in the blessing *Atah Ḥonen*.

**[TRADITION]** Kabbalistic prayer manuals may direct intention toward the supernal intellectual triad, but the precise kavvanot differ among schools.

The Tetragrammaton is not pronounced according to speculative vowel combinations in ordinary Jewish prayer.

### Counting the Omer

**[TRADITION]** The conventional kabbalistic Omer count maps seven weeks and seven days to the seven lower sefirot, giving forty-nine combinations from Ḥesed through Malkhut.

**Binah is not one of the seven counted qualities.**

Its connection is indirect:

- Forty-nine accessible gates.
- A fiftieth gate beyond ordinary attainment.
- Shavuot as a return to the higher source.
- Binah as Jubilee and liberation.

### Month, tribe, and simple letter

**[CATEGORY CORRECTION]** Binah is a sefirah, not one of the twelve simple letters. Consequently there is no ancient or canonical Binah:

- Month.
- Tribe.
- Simple-letter zodiacal assignment.
- Goetic zodiacal quinance.

Such fields apply to the twelve simple letters and their zodiacal paths. Saturn’s day, Sabbath, or the month-sign governed by Saturn in some astrological system is not the same thing as a Jewish *Sefer Yetzirah* month for Binah.

### Torah

Binah is linked to Torah in several senses:

- Discernment required for interpretation.
- Development of the seminal point of wisdom into explanation.
- The oral unfolding of concentrated revelation.
- Teshuvah and inward comprehension.
- In Lurianic and Hasidic teaching, the maternal matrix from which emotional service of God is born.

There is no canonical single Torah portion “belonging to Binah.”

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones nongentae* include seventy-one Cabalistic conclusions based heavily on Hebrew materials translated for him by Flavius Mithridates.

### Latin vocabulary

**[TEXT]** Pico generally calls the sefirot **numerationes**, “enumerations.” *Intelligentia* is the natural Latin equivalent for Binah, although Pico does not give the later standardized ten-row table.

### The forty-eighth Cabalistic conclusion

Pico writes:

> *Quicquid dicant caeteri Cabalistae, ego decem sphaeras sic decem numerationibus correspondere dico…*

“Whatever other Cabalists may say, I say that the ten spheres correspond to the ten numerations as follows…”

Crucially, Pico assigns:

- Jupiter to the fourth numeration.
- Mars to the fifth.
- Sun to the sixth.
- **Saturn to the seventh**, not the third.
- Firmament to the third.
- Primum mobile to the second.
- Empyrean to the first.

[Pico’s complete *Conclusiones*](https://cds.library.brown.edu/projects/pico/latest.php)

Therefore:

> **Pico is not evidence for the later Saturn = Binah table.**

That combination is clearer in Agrippa.

### Christianization

Pico argues that Cabala confirms Christianity, including Christological and Trinitarian claims. This is **[TEXT] Christian Cabala**, not Jewish Kabbalah. The method was intellectually productive but also appropriative: Jewish texts were detached from Jewish interpretive authority and made witnesses for doctrines their Jewish authors rejected.

---

## 2. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paolo Riccio’s abbreviated Latin translation of Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah* was printed at Augsburg as *Portae Lucis*.

Its title page contains what is probably the first printed image of the sefirot. The woodcut is commonly attributed to Hans Burgkmair the Elder, though catalogues are not entirely uniform about the design’s authorship. [Center for Jewish Art catalogue](https://cja.huji.ac.il/index.php?id=40577&mode=set), [historical description](https://www.sephardichorizons.org/Volume15/Issue1/Heller-Gikatilla.html)

This printed image is important, but it is not yet the Golden Dawn Tree with twenty-two tarot-letter paths.

---

## 3. Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica*, 1517

Johannes Reuchlin’s dialogue made Cabala part of Christian humanist philosophy. He transmitted:

- Ten sefirot.
- Hebrew divine names.
- Letter mysticism.
- Pythagorean and Christian readings.

**[TEXT/HERMETIC]** Binah is rendered through Latin intellectual vocabulary, especially *intelligentia*. Reuchlin’s Cabala is Christian and philosophical; it should not be quoted as though it were a neutral translation of a single Jewish source. [1517 digital edition](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105)

Reuchlin also defended Hebrew books against Johannes Pfefferkorn’s campaign, making his historical position more complex than simple hostility. Nevertheless, his theological purpose remained Christian.

## 4. Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia*, 1533

Agrippa’s *Three Books of Occult Philosophy* supply the earliest clear European table closely resembling the later occult Binah row.

In Book II, chapter 13, the “scale of ten” gives:

- Binah.
- Jehova Elohim.
- Thrones.
- Aralim.
- Zaphkiel.
- Sabbathi, sphere of Saturn.
- Spleen in the human microcosm.
- “Vessels of iniquity” in the infernal world.

[Agrippa, Book II, scale of ten](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp2b.htm)

This is a composite:

- Hebrew sefirot and divine names.
- Maimonidean Jewish angel ranks.
- Pseudo-Dionysian Christian choirs.
- Planetary cosmology.
- Renaissance macrocosm/microcosm.
- Christian demonological categories.

It is historically inaccurate to call the entire table “the ancient Hebrew correspondences.”

---

## 5. Kircher, *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, 1652–1654

Kircher’s *Cabala Hebraeorum* appears in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I.

### The third path

Kircher writes:

> *Tertia semita vocatur intelligentia santificans… respondet autem tertiae Sephirae… Binah seu intelligentia.*

“The third path is called the Sanctifying Intelligence … and corresponds to the third sefirah, called Binah or Intelligence.”

[Kircher’s transcribed Latin](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

### The diagram

Kircher’s *Systema Sephiroticum* plate gives a three-column Tree whose topology became enormously influential in Western occultism. [Cornell’s Kircher plate](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272)

### Did the Golden Dawn simply adopt Kircher’s Tree?

**[DISPUTED]** “The Golden Dawn adopted Kircher’s Tree” is broadly true genealogically but imprecise.

- Kircher supplied a major printed ancestor.
- Similar topologies appeared before and after him.
- Knorr von Rosenroth helped transmit the scheme.
- Nineteenth-century occultists standardized letter, tarot, colour, and astrological assignments that Kircher did not possess in Golden Dawn form.
- The Golden Dawn’s working Tree is therefore a systematized descendant, not an unchanged copy.

---

## 6. Knorr von Rosenroth, *Kabbala Denudata*, 1677–1684

Knorr’s vast Latin anthology includes:

- A Hebrew-Latin kabbalistic lexicon.
- Zoharic texts.
- *Sifra di-Tzeniuta*.
- *Idra Rabba* and *Idra Zuta*.
- Lurianic materials.
- Works associated with Cordovero, Vital, Abraham Cohen de Herrera, and others.
- Christian Cabalist interpretation.

Its standard formula is **Binah / Intelligentia**. [Public-domain 1677–1684 edition](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/10759091)

Knorr was one of the chief conduits by which Zoharic and Lurianic vocabulary entered European occultism. S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* translates only a small fraction of the whole. [Mathers’s extracts](https://www.sacred-texts.com/jud/tku/index.htm)

### Qliphothic significance

The *Kabbala Denudata* includes material on the ten “impure numerations.” That material helped make possible the later Golden Dawn mirror-tree of ten qliphoth. It is nevertheless misleading to imagine one stable “Tree of Death” running unchanged from the Zohar to Knorr to Crowley.

## 7. Éliphas Lévi and the Tarot

In *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* (1854–1856), Lévi organized each volume into twenty-two chapters and drew a systematic relationship among:

- Twenty-two Hebrew letters.
- Twenty-two occult keys.
- Twenty-two tarot trumps.

[French first-volume scan](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/eliphas-levi-dogme-et-rituel-tome-1.pdf)

Lévi did not use the later Golden Dawn trump order. Most importantly:

- Lévi generally begins Aleph with the Magician.
- The Golden Dawn begins Aleph with the Fool.
- The Golden Dawn redistributed the trumps to match its preferred astrological-letter system.

Thus the Golden Dawn tarot Tree is not simply ancient Kabbalah plus medieval Tarot. It is a nineteenth-century occult construction made from older ingredients.

## 8. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### Standard Binah attribution

The Golden Dawn system gives:

- Binah, Understanding.
- Number 3.
- YHVH Elohim.
- Tzaphqiel.
- Aralim/Thrones.
- Shabbathai/Saturn.
- Black in the Queen Scale.
- The four Threes.

[Golden Dawn correspondence summary](https://hermetic.com/jwmt/v1n3/treeoflife), [Golden Dawn grade table](https://hermetic.com/gdlibrary/gd-faq)

### Ama and Aima

Golden Dawn transliterations distinguish:

- **AMA**, the “dark sterile mother.”
- **AIMA**, the “bright fertile mother.”

**[HERMETIC]** These formulas use Hebrew/Aramaic mother vocabulary but are specifically occult doctrinal slogans. They are not simple quotations from the Zohar.

The underlying principle is that Binah can be considered:

- Apart from the influx of Ḥokhmah: restriction, latency, darkness.
- Pregnant with that influx: formation, gestation, fecundity.

### Form and force

Golden Dawn cosmology makes Ḥokhmah primordial force and Binah primordial form. Their union generates the lower Tree.

This is a useful Hermetic abstraction, but Jewish sources speak more variously of:

- Wisdom and understanding.
- Father and mother.
- Point and palace.
- Seed and womb.
- Mercy and judgment.
- Yod and heh.
- Spring and sea.

[Book T in *The Equinox*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/equinox/i/viii/eqi08016)

The four cards express Binah through four worlds:

- Wands—Atziluth.

## 9. Crowley

### *Liber 777*

Crowley’s *777*—first published in 1909 and later revised—condenses Golden Dawn tables together with Crowley’s additions. The relevant Binah entries are audited below.

### Great Sea, Mother, sorrow, and understanding

Crowley repeatedly identifies Binah as:

- Mother.
- Great Sea.
- Throne.
- Saturn.
- Black.
- Myrrh.
- Sorrow.
- Understanding.

[“The Temple of Solomon the King”](https://hermetic.com/crowley/equinox/i/v/eqi05007)

### Magister Templi

The A∴A∴ grade attached to Binah is **8°=3□ Magister Templi**.

Crowley’s *One Star in Sight* says the aspirant beyond Adeptus Exemptus must surrender even the Holy Guardian Angel, becoming a Babe of the Abyss; the Magister’s principal work is understanding the universe and tending a “garden” of disciples. [One Star in Sight](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-4/app2)

### City of the Pyramids

**[HERMETIC]** In *Liber 418*, especially the Fourteenth Aethyr:

- The City of the Pyramids lies symbolically in Binah beyond the Abyss.
- Its inhabitants are Masters of the Temple.
- Each is represented as a heap of dust beneath a pyramid.
- The personal name has been surrendered.
- NEMO, “No Man,” is a characteristic title.
- Babalon receives the adept’s blood in her cup.

[Liber 418 introduction and Aethyr sequence](https://hermetic.com/crowley/the-vision-and-the-voice/intro418)

*Liber Cheth* states:

> “The angels shall lay thy dust in the City of the Pyramids.”

[Liber Cheth](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib156)

This is Crowley’s initiatory myth, not a Jewish teaching about Binah.

### Babalon and the cup

Crowley superimposes Babalon on Binah’s receptive and maternal symbolism. The adept pours all individual life into her cup. This develops the Golden Dawn cup/yoni correspondence into a Thelemic theology of ego-annihilation and universal life.

### *Liber 231*

*Liber 231* gives twenty-two obverse and reverse spirits or sigils associated with Hebrew letters. It is not a prose treatise on Binah. Its relevance is indirect: the four letters on Golden Dawn paths touching Binah have reverse spirits later transformed by Kenneth Grant into “Tunnels of Set.” [Liber 231](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib231.pdf)

### Daʿat and the Abyss

Crowley says explicitly:

> “Daath is not on the Tree of Life.”

[Liber 58](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib58)

For Crowley, Daʿat is the divided intellectual awareness of the Abyss. This dramatization is not equivalent to the subtler Jewish uses of Daʿat as unification, knowledge, or a state counted when Keter is concealed.

## 10. Dion Fortune, *The Mystical Qabalah*, 1935

Fortune devotes chapter XVII to Binah.

A complete verbatim reproduction cannot be supplied here, but the chapter’s substantive argument can be reconstructed in detail. The original is available through [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL114617W/The_Mystical_Qabalah?edition=ia%3Amysticalqabalah00fort_0).

### Fortune’s formal attribution list

Fortune gives:

- Title: Understanding.
- Magical image: mature woman, matron.
- Position: head of the Pillar of Severity.
- Yetziratic title: Sanctifying Intelligence.
- Divine name: YHVH Elohim.
- Archangel: Tzaphkiel.
- Order: Aralim, Thrones.
- Mundane chakra: Saturn.
- Spiritual experience: Vision of Sorrow.
- Virtue: Silence.
- Vice: Avarice.
- Symbols: yoni, kteis, vesica piscis, cup, outer robe.
- Tarot: the four Threes.

### Main argument

Fortune treats Ḥokhmah as force and Binah as form. Form is not dead matter but balanced limitation. Without resistance and boundary, force cannot become a determinate universe.

From this she derives a double symbolism:

- Binah is Mother because she gives form and birth.
- Binah is also the root of death because every determinate form excludes alternatives and must eventually dissolve.
- Saturn signifies limitation, duration, time, age, and structure.
- Sorrow is the recognition that manifestation entails separation and loss.
- Silence is the mature containment of understanding.
- Avarice is diseased retention: form refusing circulation.

### Great Sea and Mother

Fortune presents the Great Sea as the universal matrix. The primordial point of Ḥokhmah is received, delimited, and gestated in Binah. Malkhut is the final material expression of what begins formally in Binah.

### The four Threes

Fortune reads them as four consequences of established form:

- Wands: power stabilized.
- Cups: abundance and fecundity.
- Swords: sorrow inherent in limitation.
- Pentacles: completed material work.

### Historical limitations

Fortune’s chapter employs early twentieth-century sexual psychology and a binary male-force/female-form model. These are **[HERMETIC]** interpretations conditioned by her period, not neutral translations of Jewish Kabbalah.

# The Qliphah and the tunnels

## Satariel

### Name

Common spellings include:

- Satariel.
- Sathariel.
- Satorial.
- **סאתאריאל**, Crowley’s form.
- **סתריאל**, a Hebrew-normalized modern form.

The usual glosses are “Concealers,” “Those Who Hide,” or “Concealment of God.”

### Golden Dawn description

The Golden Dawn qliphoth paper says:

> “Unto Binah are referred the Satorial or Harasiel, the Concealers and Destroyers…”

It depicts gigantic black veiled heads with horns and hideous eyes, followed by evil centaurs. [Golden Dawn “Qliphoth” paper](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html)

### Jewish precedent and occult transformation

**[TRADITION]** Jewish Kabbalah contains:

- *Qelippot*, shells or husks.
- The *sitra aḥra*, “other side.”
- Demonic palaces and impure powers.
- Lurianic doctrines of trapped sparks.

**[HERMETIC]** The neat tenfold mirror-tree with one named qliphah opposite each holy sefirah is an early-modern and Golden Dawn systematization. It cannot simply be labeled “the Zohar’s Tree of Death.”

### Satariel and the Enochic Watcher

A name resembling Satariel appears in Ethiopic *1 Enoch* among the Watchers. **[DISPUTED]** The linguistic form, manuscript tradition, and meaning differ. There is no evidence that the Golden Dawn’s qliphah was historically derived from that Watcher merely because English spellings coincide.

### Demon prince: Lucifuge Rofocale

Lucifuge Rofocale belongs to modern European grimoire demonology, especially the *Grand Grimoire*. He is not named ruler of Satariel in:

- *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- *Bahir*.
- Zohar.
- *Pardes Rimmonim*.
- *Etz Chaim*.
- Agrippa’s Binah table.
- The foundational Golden Dawn Satariel description.
- Crowley’s original row in *777*.

The association **Lucifuge = ruler of Satariel** is therefore **[MODERN]**, widespread in later Left-Hand-Path and demonolatry literature but inadequately traced to a primary early source. Contemporary reference works acknowledge that demon rulers were mapped onto the qliphoth after the fact. [Modern provenance warning](https://www.divinityatlas.com/qliphoth/satariel)

# Paths adjoining Binah on the Hermetic Tree

Binah is a sefirah, so it has no single Hebrew letter or trump. On the Golden Dawn/Kircher-derived Tree, four letter-paths touch it.

| Path | Letter | Value and literal name | *Sefer Yetzirah* class | Golden Dawn attribution | Trump | Joins |
|---:|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | ב Beth | 2, “house” | Double | Mercury | Magician | Keter–Binah |
| 14 | ד Daleth | 4, “door” | Double | Venus | Empress | Ḥokhmah–Binah |
| 17 | ז Zayin | 7, “weapon/sword” | Simple | Gemini | Lovers | Binah–Tiferet |
| 18 | ח Cheth | 8, “fence/enclosure” | Simple | Cancer | Chariot | Binah–Gevurah |

## *Sefer Yetzirah* attributions and variants

- Beth and Daleth belong to the seven Doubles. Their precise planetary order differs among recensions and commentators.
- Zayin and Cheth belong to the twelve Simples.
- The familiar Zayin–Gemini and Cheth–Cancer assignments belong to the zodiacal sequence used by the Golden Dawn and supported by particular recension traditions.
- *Sefer Yetzirah* does not say that these letters join these particular named sefirot.

That final mapping is Hermetic.

## Westcott’s path titles

In the appended “Thirty-two Paths,” Westcott gives:

| Path | Intelligence |
|---:|---|
| 12 | Intelligence of Transparency |
| 14 | Illuminating Intelligence |
| 17 | Disposing Intelligence |
| 18 | Intelligence of the House of Influence |

These titles are not the native descriptions of Beth, Daleth, Zayin, and Cheth in the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah*. The Golden Dawn combines two enumerations:

- Paths 1–10 as sefirot.
- Paths 11–32 as Hebrew-letter channels.

## Ari and Gra comparison

The same letters do not necessarily connect the same sefirot in Ari-style and Gra-style diagrams. Those systems try to honor the geometric classification:

- Mothers = horizontal.
- Doubles = vertical.
- Simples = diagonal.

The Golden Dawn instead assigns Aleph through Tav sequentially to its inherited twenty-two-line topology. Consequently a statement such as “Beth is naturally the Keter–Binah path” is true only inside the Hermetic convention.

## Tzaddi/Heh controversy

The original Golden Dawn attribution was:

- Heh = Aries = Emperor.
- Tzaddi = Aquarius = Star.

Crowley interpreted *Liber AL* I:57, “Tzaddi is not the Star,” as requiring an exchange:

- Emperor receives Tzaddi.
- Star receives Heh.

This is **[HERMETIC/DISPUTED]** and concerns the Chokmah–Tiferet and Netzach–Yesod regions in the standard Tree. It does **not directly change any of the four paths adjoining Binah** listed above.

## Pathworking

Golden Dawn and later pathworking generally employs:

1. Ritual purification and banishing.
2. Visualization of the two endpoint sefirot.
3. The Hebrew letter in its world-specific colour.
4. The associated trump and astrological force.
5. Vibrated divine and angelic names.
6. Symbolic “rising on the planes” or skrying.
7. A recorded vision tested against established correspondences.

This is a modern ritual practice. Jewish Kabbalah has contemplative letter-combination, divine-name, prayer, and ascent traditions, but not the Golden Dawn method of walking a tarot-coloured Kircher path.

## Diagrams and manuscripts

### Early *ilanot*

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Kabbalists began producing elaborate *ilanot*, “trees,” especially from the fourteenth century onward. These were not merely pictures but:

- Mnemonic diagrams.
- Maps of emanation.
- Ritual-contemplative instruments.
- Compendia of texts and relationships.

The [Ilanot Project](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/) documents these manuscripts.

### *Portae Lucis*, 1516

The first printed sefirotic image appears on the title page of Riccio’s Latin Gikatilla. It is not the oldest manuscript diagram and not yet the modern occult Tree.

### *Pardes Rimmonim*, 1592

The Cracow edition contains the first printed sefirotic image in a Hebrew book, seventy-six years after *Portae Lucis*. Different early printed diagrams are not topologically identical.

### Kircher’s plate

Kircher’s plate is the most influential early printed ancestor of the modern Hermetic Tree.

### Golden Dawn coloured Trees

The Golden Dawn added four world-colour systems, tarot, astrology, elemental symbolism, grades, divine names, magical weapons, and pathworking instructions to the inherited topology.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Dating *Sefer Yetzirah*

- **[TRADITION]** Attributed to Abraham.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]** Composite late-antique or early-medieval work.
- **Evidence:** diverse recensions, language, cosmology, early commentaries, Genizah witnesses.
- **Unresolved:** exact date and relative priority of the principal textual families.

The safest conclusion is that it precedes the mature medieval doctrine of ten named divine sefirot.

## 2. Is Binah really the third sefirah in *Sefer Yetzirah*?

No—not explicitly.

The ancient text has a third *sefirah belimah*, often water from breath, and separately uses “understanding” as a cognitive term. The identification of that third measure with the named medieval Binah is retrospective.

## 3. Authorship of the Zohar

- **[TRADITION]** Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and companions.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]** Late thirteenth- and early fourteenth-century Castilian corpus, with Moses de León central.
- **[DISPUTED]** Single principal author versus several authors, circles, textual strata, and later supplements.

The historical conclusion does not determine the work’s religious authority for traditional readers.

## 4. Christian Cabalist appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Riccio, Kircher, and Knorr preserved and disseminated Jewish materials. They also:

- Reframed them as witnesses to Christianity.
- Identified the supernal triad with the Trinity.
- Read Adam Qadmon Christologically.
- Detached texts from Jewish legal and liturgical life.
- Mixed Jewish angelology with Pseudo-Dionysian Christianity and Greco-Roman planetary schemes.

“Transmission” and “appropriation” are both historically warranted descriptions.

## 5. Saturn’s provenance

- Not explicit as Binah in *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- Pico assigns Saturn to the seventh numeration.
- Cordovero explicitly relates Saturn to Binah through the Great Sabbath.
- Agrippa places Saturn in the third-sefirah row.
- Golden Dawn and Crowley standardize it.

Therefore Saturn–Binah has genuine Jewish early-modern support, but the complete Saturnian occult dossier is composite.

## 6. Kircher versus Jewish trees

The Kircher/Golden Dawn Tree is not “the” universal Jewish Tree.

Differences include:

- Which sefirot are connected.
- Whether Malkhut has one or three paths.
- Whether letter classes determine the geometry.
- Whether paths are numbered 11–32.
- Whether tarot trumps are present.
- Whether Daʿat is drawn.
- Whether the diagram represents a single world, all worlds, partzufim, or an initiatory ladder.

The Ari and Gra arrangements are themselves complex textual-diagrammatic traditions, not one uncontested pair of pictures.

## 7. Tarot attributions

No historical evidence places tarot in:

- *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- *Bahir*.
- Zohar.
- Gikatilla.
- Cordovero.
- Luria.

The 22 letters/22 trumps analogy becomes systematic with Lévi and is restructured by the Golden Dawn. Crowley’s Tzaddi/Heh exchange is a further Thelemic revision.

## 8. Is “Sanctifying Intelligence” ancient?

No secure evidence supports that claim.

It belongs to the appended *Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom* tradition transmitted through Latin and early-modern occult editions. Westcott’s placement beside *Sefer Yetzirah* made it appear integral to that work.

## 9. Ama versus Aima

- Mother terminology is genuinely Jewish and Zoharic.
- Different maternal configurations are important in Lurianic Kabbalah.
- “Dark sterile Mother” and “bright fertile Mother” are Golden Dawn English formulas.
- Treating Ama as simply “evil” is a distortion: occult authors generally intend latent versus impregnated form, not two independent goddesses.

## 10. Satariel and its ruler

Satariel as Binah’s adverse counterpart is securely documented in Golden Dawn and Crowleyan material. Lucifuge Rofocale as its demon prince is not securely documented there.

The latter should be labeled a modern demonological overlay unless a dated primary edition is produced.

## 11. Daʿat’s status

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists on “ten and not nine, ten and not eleven.”

Later Jewish systems sometimes count:

- Keter without Daʿat.
- Daʿat as operative when Keter is concealed.
- Daʿat as the union or internalization of Ḥokhmah and Binah.

Daʿat is therefore not normally an independent eleventh sefirah. The diagrammatic “hidden sefirah” and Crowley’s abyssal pseudo-sefirah are later models.

## 12. Gender

Jewish Kabbalah uses intensely gendered symbolism, but sefirotic gender is relational:

- A sefirah can be receptive toward what is above and giving toward what is below.
- Binah is feminine relative to Ḥokhmah but maternal and generative toward the lower sefirot.
- Mercy/judgment and male/female do not form perfectly fixed binaries in every source.

The rigid equation “male = force, female = passive form” is especially characteristic of modern Hermetic summaries.

# Open questions

1. **Earliest manuscript use of Binah as a fixed third sefirah:** the transition between early *Sefer Yetzirah* commentary, the *Bahir*, and Gerona materials requires manuscript-by-manuscript study.

2. **Bahir numbering:** the maternal Binah passage circulates under different section numbers. Quotations should specify Abrams, Kaplan, or another edition.

3. **Exact genealogy of the Kircher topology:** Kircher’s plate has identifiable precursors, including Philippe d’Aquin and earlier printed trees. “Invented by Kircher” is too strong.

4. **The earliest exact Tzaphqiel–Binah assignment:** Agrippa is a firm printed witness, but earlier manuscript sources may underlie his table.

5. **Aralim’s route into Christian Thrones:** Maimonides supplies a tenfold Jewish hierarchy and Pseudo-Dionysius a ninefold Christian one; the exact stages of their table-by-table fusion merit further study.

6. **The impure numerations before Knorr:** Cordovero and earlier Zoharic/Tiqqunic materials contain demonic names and structures, but their relation to the later fixed mirror-tree remains textually complex.

7. **Lucifuge and Satariel:** no consulted early source established the now-common rulership. Its first demonstrable printed occurrence remains to be identified.

8. **Ari and Gra diagrams:** modern redrawings should be checked against named editions and manuscripts rather than cited generically.

9. **Crowley’s comparative pantheon:** many *777* identifications reflect analogy, Theosophical universalism, or Crowley’s editorial preference rather than historical contact between traditions.

10. **Contemporary Jewish use:** Binah remains active in Hasidic teaching, prayer commentary, gender studies, contemporary art, and new diagrammatic practice, but these living interpretations cannot be reduced to the Golden Dawn row.

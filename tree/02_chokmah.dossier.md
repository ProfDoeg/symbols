# Chokmah: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary legend

This dossier keeps Jewish Kabbalah and Christian/Hermetic Qabalah historically distinct. Similar vocabulary does not imply identical doctrine.

Claims are marked as follows:

- **[DT] Documented text:** stated in a cited primary source.
- **[SR] Scholarly reconstruction:** a conclusion of modern historical or philological scholarship.
- **[KT] Kabbalistic tradition:** a received interpretation internal to Jewish Kabbalah, which may claim antiquity beyond its earliest surviving witness.
- **[HO] Hermetic-occult attribution:** part of Christian Cabala, Golden Dawn, Thelemic, or later Western occultism.
- **[D] Disputed:** evidence, dating, translation, or lineage is contested.
- **[MI] Modern invention:** an attribution first documented in modern occultism, rather than ancient or medieval Jewish sources.
- **[AE] Absence of evidence:** the requested attribution could not be established in the named early sources.

Primary-text quotations are necessarily brief. Dion Fortune’s still-copyrighted chapter is therefore analyzed comprehensively but not reproduced verbatim.

> **2 · Chokmah · Yah · Raziel · Auphanim · Mazloth · Four Twos · pure soft blue/grey/pearl-grey/white flecked with the primaries · Magus 9°=2□ · Ghagiel.**

Almost half of that formula is specifically Hermetic rather than an undifferentiated inheritance from ancient Judaism.

# The name

## Hebrew spelling, pronunciation, and etymology

**[DT]** The word is **חָכְמָה**, vocalized ḥokhmāh and conventionally translated “wisdom.” It derives from the Semitic root **ח־כ־ם**, whose verb and adjectival forms concern being wise, skillful, competent, or judicious. Biblical *ḥokhmāh* can mean:

- intellectual or moral wisdom;
- practical skill;
- artistic craftsmanship;
- political prudence;
- technical expertise;
- the wisdom by which God orders creation.

The noun is grammatically feminine. That grammatical fact should not be confused with the later kabbalistic convention in which Chokmah is symbolically “Father” and transmits a seed to Binah, “Mother.” Hebrew grammatical gender and sefirotic erotic symbolism are different systems.

Standard transliterations include:

- **Ḥokhmāh**, the most precise scholarly form;
- **Hokhmah** or **Hokhma**;
- **Chokhmah**, common in English Jewish writing;
- **Chochmah**, especially in Ashkenazi/Hasidic English;
- **Chokmah**, standard in Golden Dawn and Thelemic literature;
- **Hochma, Chochma, Hhokmah**, found in older Latin, German, French, and occult transcriptions.

The gematria is **73**. Crowley draws additional analogies from that number, but the arithmetic itself is simply the ordinary numerical value of the Hebrew letters.

## Biblical foundations

### Proverbs 3:19

**[DT]**

> **יְהוָה בְּחָכְמָה יָסַד־אָרֶץ כּוֹנֵן שָׁמַיִם בִּתְבוּנָה**  
> “YHWH founded the earth by wisdom; He established the heavens by understanding.”

This is the most direct scriptural pairing behind the later Chokmah–Binah polarity. Chokmah is associated with the originary founding act; *tevunah/binah* with establishment and articulation. See [Proverbs 3:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19).

**[KT]** Kabbalists do not merely read “wisdom” and “understanding” here as human or divine qualities. They read them as names of successive modes within divine manifestation.

### Proverbs 8

**[DT]** Proverbs 8 personifies Wisdom as a female speaker who precedes or accompanies creation:

> **יְהוָה קָנָנִי רֵאשִׁית דַּרְכּוֹ**  
> “YHWH created/acquired/possessed me at the beginning of His course.”

The verb **קנני**, *qānānî*, permits several translations—“created me,” “acquired me,” or “possessed me.” The translation was already theologically consequential in ancient Jewish and Christian exegesis. Wisdom says she existed before the primordial depths, mountains, earth, and heavens, and was present when God established the world. See [Proverbs 8:22–31](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.8.22).

**[KT]** Medieval Kabbalah uses this personified, pre-cosmic Wisdom as a scriptural warrant for Chokmah.

**[D]** Proverbs itself does not present a ten-sefirah system or identify Wisdom as “sefirah number two.” That is a later mystical rereading.

### Job 28

**[DT]** Job asks:

> **וְהַחָכְמָה מֵאַיִן תִּמָּצֵא**  
> “But wisdom—from where shall it be found?”

See [Job 28:12](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28.12) and the chapter’s conclusion at [Job 28:28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28.28).

**[KT]** The ordinary interrogative **מֵאַיִן**, *me-ʾayin*, “from where?”, was reread ontologically as “from **Ayin**, Nothing.” Thus Wisdom issues “from Nothing,” with Ayin associated with Keter or the concealed divine source. Ezra ben Solomon of Gerona already makes this reading in the early thirteenth century: wisdom derives from *ayin*. See [Ezra ben Solomon’s introduction to his Song of Songs commentary](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezra_ben_Solomon_on_Song_of_Songs%2C_Introduction.26).

**[D]** This is brilliant kabbalistic wordplay, not the historical-grammatical meaning demanded by the syntax of Job.

### Isaiah

The strongest Isaianic thread is Isaiah 11:2:

> **רוּחַ חָכְמָה וּבִינָה**  
> “A spirit of wisdom and understanding.”

See [Isaiah 11:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.11.2).

Isaiah 28:29 also calls God “wonderful in counsel, great in wisdom.” These passages contributed to Jewish and Christian wisdom theology, but **[AE]** neither verse enumerates Chokmah as the second sefirah.

### 1 Chronicles 29:11

**[DT]** The verse lists:

> “Greatness, might, glory, victory, majesty … all … kingdom.”

See [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11).

**[KT]** Later kabbalists read this verse as a chain of the lower seven sefirot:

- Gedulah/Chesed;
- Gevurah;
- Tiferet;
- Netzach;
- Hod;
- “all,” understood as Yesod;
- Malkhut.

**[AE]** It is not the source of the name Chokmah. Chokmah’s main biblical anchors are Proverbs, Job, Genesis reread through *reshit*, and wisdom passages such as Isaiah 11.

## Genesis and *reshit*

Genesis begins **בְּרֵאשִׁית**, *bereshit*. Psalm 111:10 states **רֵאשִׁית חָכְמָה**, “the beginning of wisdom” or “the beginning is wisdom/fear of YHWH,” depending how the phrase is construed.

**[KT]** The *Bahir* turns *reshit* into a technical symbol: “beginning” is Wisdom. Creation “in the beginning” thus becomes creation “through Chokmah.” This became one of the foundational exegetical moves of the sefirotic system.

## Kabbalistic decomposition: *koaḥ mah*

**[KT]** The Zoharic and Hasidic wordplay divides **חכמה** into:

- **כח**, *koaḥ*, “power”;
- **מה**, *mah*, “what?” or “what-ness.”

Chokmah becomes **כח מה**, “the power of ‘what?’”—a faculty whose identity is self-effacing openness rather than completed conceptual knowledge. The *Tanya* uses this reading to explain Chokmah’s *bittul*, nullification before the Infinite.

**[D]** This is not historical etymology. The actual word derives from ח־כ־ם. *Koaḥ mah* is an intentional mystical disassembly of the spelling.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. Pre-kabbalistic foundations

### Biblical Wisdom

**[DT]** Biblical Chokmah is not yet a hypostasis in a tenfold emanational diagram. It can nevertheless be personified, especially in Proverbs 8, and it functions as God’s creative instrument in Proverbs 3:19.

**[SR]** Historians distinguish this wisdom literature from medieval sefirotic Kabbalah. The later system is an exegetical construction upon the biblical material, not the Bible’s explicit cosmology.

### Rabbinic lists of creative qualities

**[DT]** *Ḥagigah* 12a lists qualities through which the world was created and supports them with scriptural verses. Wisdom and understanding are proven from Proverbs 3:19; knowledge from Proverbs 3:20; strength, rebuke, righteousness, justice, kindness, and mercy follow from other verses.

This rabbinic list is not yet the canonical medieval sequence Keter–Chokmah–Binah–Chesed and so forth. **[SR]** It nevertheless supplied both vocabulary and proof-texting habits later employed by kabbalists.

## 2. Sefer Yetzirah

### Date and textual history

**[D/SR]** *Sefer Yetzirah* is notoriously difficult to date. Proposals range from late antiquity to the early Islamic period. Sefaria’s overview summarizes a common linguistic estimate of approximately the third through sixth centuries, with later additions. Peter Hayman’s critical edition emphasizes that “the text” survives as several manuscript families rather than one transparent original.

Hayman edited three principal forms:

- the Short recension;
- the Long recension;
- the Saadyan recension.

His edition collates nineteen manuscripts: Peter Hayman, *Sefer Yeṣira: Edition, Translation and Text-Critical Commentary*, TSAJ 104 (Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck, 2004). See the [publisher’s description](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/).

**[D]** A more recent revisionist argument by Avishai Bar-Asher proposes that a Genizah/Saadyan-form text may preserve the earliest recoverable format, contrary to the older preference for a short recension. See “The Earliest Version of *Sefer Yeṣirah*,” *Aleph* 23 (2023), 7–50, [Hebrew University record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-earliest-version-of-sefer-yeirah-text-format-structure-and-ge/).

### The thirty-two paths

**[DT]** The work begins:

> “With thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom Yah, YHWH of Hosts … engraved and created His world…”

See [*Sefer Yetzirah* 1:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.1?lang=bi&with=Kabbalah).

The thirty-two are:

- **ten sefirot belimah**;
- **twenty-two foundation letters**.

The letters divide into:

- three Mothers: א מ ש;
- seven Doubles: ב ג ד כ פ ר ת;
- twelve Simples: ה ו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק.

See [*Sefer Yetzirah* 1:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.5).

### What the second sefirah is

This is the central historical correction.

**[DT]** In the early cosmological sequence the second sefirah is **“breath from breath”**—*ruaḥ me-ruaḥ*. It is associated with the engraving of the twenty-two letters. It is not called Chokmah in the surviving core recensions.

The Gra recension reads in 1:10:

> “Two: Breath from Breath. With it He engraved and carved twenty-two foundation letters: three Mothers, seven Doubles, and twelve Elementals.”

See the [Gra recension and commentary](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.1) and [Sefaria’s Gra material](https://www.sefaria.org/HaGra_on_Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.1.1).

**[SR]** Gershom Scholem therefore warned against projecting the fully developed medieval sefirotic names directly into *Sefer Yetzirah*. Its “wisdom” characterizes the thirty-two paths collectively; it is not yet the named second hypostasis familiar from the Zohar.

### “Ten and not nine; ten and not eleven”

**[DT]** *Sefer Yetzirah* insists:

> “Ten sefirot belimah: ten and not nine; ten and not eleven.”

This maxim later became decisive in arguments about Da’at. It does not prevent kabbalists from discussing both Keter and Da’at, but it prevents counting both as independent sefirot in an eleven-member series.

### Saadia, Ari, and Gra “versions”

- **Saadia Gaon, tenth century [DT]:** Saadia translated/commented on an Arabic-form recension. His philosophical interpretation resists treating the sefirot as divine persons or emanated members of a godhead.
- **“Ari version” [D]:** The expression often refers to a recension or reordering harmonized with Zoharic/Lurianic teaching. A complete independent autograph of Isaac Luria’s own recension is not available.
- **Gra version [DT/KT]:** The Vilna Gaon’s eighteenth-century recension and commentary became highly influential in later Jewish study. Modern books sometimes call it the “Gra-Ari” text because it incorporates Lurianic/Zoharic corrections.
- **[D]** Aryeh Kaplan’s popular presentation synthesizes these histories and diagrams. It is valuable but should not be mistaken for a diplomatic edition of one ancient archetype.

### The letters and cosmic attributions

The Mothers correspond to elemental principles; the Doubles to planets and polar qualities; the Simples to zodiacal signs, months, bodily functions, or “leaders.”

**[D]** The details vary. The early planetary sequence often corresponds:

- Bet—Saturn;
- Gimel—Jupiter;
- Dalet—Mars;
- Kaf—Sun;
- Peh—Venus;
- Resh—Mercury;
- Tav—Moon.

The Golden Dawn instead uses an astrological sequence in which Dalet is Venus. Westcott’s Victorian text presents still another arrangement. A comparison of manuscript evidence is collected in [this recension table, citing Kaplan and Hayman](https://www.psyche.com/psyche/yetsira/sy_planetaryattributions.html).

### No drawn tarot paths

**[AE]** *Sefer Yetzirah* does not say:

- that Aleph joins Keter to Chokmah;
- that Dalet joins Chokmah to Binah;
- that tarot trumps belong on those lines;
- that the second sefirah has Raziel, Ophanim, or a blue/grey color.

It supplies the numerical architecture—ten plus twenty-two—not the Golden Dawn diagram.

## 3. Sefer ha-Bahir

### Date and status

**[SR]** The *Bahir* became publicly known in Provence in the late twelfth century. Traditional attributions connect it to the tannaitic Rabbi Neḥunya ben ha-Qanah, but historians treat it as a composite medieval work containing older traditions.

Joseph Dan describes it as the first disseminated book to use the dynamic symbolism that defines theosophical Kabbalah. See Dan’s [overview of the *Bahir*](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/sefer-ha-bahir-the-book-of-brilliance/).

### Beginning is Wisdom

**[DT]** *Bahir* §60 interprets the Torah’s initial Bet, blessing, and *reshit*:

> “Beginning is nothing other than Wisdom.”

It invokes “The beginning of wisdom is fear of YHWH” and turns *reshit* into a divine grade. See [*Sefer ha-Bahir* §60](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.60).

This is one of the earliest surviving textual bridges between biblical Wisdom and the developed sefirah Chokmah.

### The thirty-two paths and the heart

**[DT]** *Bahir* §120 observes that **לב**, *lev*, “heart,” has the numerical value thirty-two. The thirty-two hidden paths become internal to the divine glory and are figured through the parable of the king and his daughter. See [*Bahir* §120](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.120).

### Gender

**[D/KT]** The *Bahir* does not yet present the rigid Western equation “Chokmah = male force; Binah = female form.” Its images move among daughter, bride, mother, blessing, Torah, and glory. Later kabbalists stabilize some relations that remain fluid in the *Bahir*.

## 4. Provence and Gerona

In the late twelfth and early thirteenth centuries, Isaac the Blind, Azriel of Gerona, Ezra ben Solomon, and their circles transformed the older sefirotic vocabulary into a systematic account of divine emanation.

### Chokmah from Ayin

**[KT]** Ezra ben Solomon’s rereading of Job 28 makes Chokmah emerge from *Ayin*, Nothing. The idea can be written schematically:

> Ein Sof / Ayin → Keter → Chokmah → Binah.

**[D]** Not every early kabbalist counted Keter in the same way. Some begin the intelligible enumeration with Chokmah; some bring Keter into close proximity with Ein Sof. This later underlies the Keter-versus-Da’at counting problem.

### Point, seed, and thought

Chokmah becomes:

- the primordial point;
- undifferentiated thought;
- a seed containing all later differentiation;
- the first discernible moment after concealed will.

Binah is the expansion, palace, or womb in which this seed becomes articulated.

## 5. The Zohar

### Date and authorship

**[KT]** Traditional attribution identifies Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle in the second century.

**[SR]** The work first circulated publicly in late-thirteenth-century Castile. Gershom Scholem argued that Moses de León authored most of the central body. More recent scholarship often speaks of de León and a literary/mystical circle, while distinguishing different strata. The *Ra’aya Meheimna* and *Tikkunei Zohar* are generally treated as later compositions.

Useful orientation is supplied by the [National Library of Israel](https://www.nli.org.il/en/discover/judaism/jewish-bookshelf/zohar), Daniel Matt’s [Pritzker Edition](https://www.sup.org/books/jewish-studies/zohar-volume-1), and the [Pritzker front matter](https://supress.sites-pro.stanford.edu/sites/supress/files/media/file/4810_Front_Matter.pdf).

### First beginning and the thirty-two paths

**[DT]** *Zohar*, *Ha’azinu*, describes Chokmah as the first beginning:

> “This Chokmah is the first beginning; from it thirty-two paths expand.”

It calls Chokmah “father to fathers,” containing everything, and links its thirty-two paths with the Torah’s twenty-two letters and ten creative utterances. See [Zohar, *Ha’azinu*, chapter 23 §73](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Ha%27azinu/chapters/23).

### The point and the Father

Chokmah is frequently represented as:

- **נקודה**, the point;
- **אבא**, Abba/Father;
- **י**, the Yod;
- **ראשית**, Beginning;
- a concealed or supernal thought.

The tiny Yod contains extension potentially; Binah unfolds what the point holds without differentiation.

### Chokmah and Torah

**[KT]** Torah is said to proceed from, be contained within, or embody divine Wisdom. This does not mean a printed Torah scroll “is the planet Chokmah.” It means Torah’s primordial ordering principle is located in Chokmah.

### Dynamic gender

**[KT/D]** Chokmah is Father relative to Binah, but kabbalistic gender is relational. Every sefirah may receive from above and transmit below. Elliot Wolfson’s scholarship shows that the system is highly gendered and frequently phallocentric, but also capable of gender crossing and reversals. See Wolfson, *Circle in the Square* ([JSTOR edition](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.18253571)).

## 6. Joseph Gikatilla: Sha’arei Orah

*Sha’arei Orah*, composed around 1290, ascends through the sefirot by examining their divine names.

### Yah

**[DT]** In the gate of Chokmah, Gikatilla identifies its characteristic divine name as **יָהּ**, Yah. He draws on:

- Job 28:12: Wisdom from Ayin;
- Proverbs 3:19: earth founded through Wisdom;
- the relation of Yah’s Yod and Heh to Wisdom and Understanding.

See [*Sha’arei Orah*, Ninth Gate, Second Sefirah](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Ninth_Gate%2C_Second_Sefirah.6).

### Yah versus YHVH

The historical result is not “Yah or YHVH, one must be wrong.”

- **[DT/KT]** Gikatilla’s principal Chokmah-name is **Yah**.
- **[KT]** Yod, the first letter of YHVH, is Chokmah.
- **[KT]** The full Tetragrammaton can symbolize a multi-sefirotic or four-world process.
- **[HO]** Mathers lists both Yah and YHVH under Chokmah.
- **[HO]** Fortune calls the God-name “Jehovah,” reflecting her broad use of the Tetragrammaton.

### Archangel, order, and planet

**[AE]** The surviving Chokmah gate does not provide the complete later formula “Yah—Raziel—Ophanim—Mazloth” as a four-world table. Gikatilla’s primary organizing concern is divine names and their scriptural operations.

The association of the twelve constellations with permutations and influence from YHVH appears elsewhere in *Sha’arei Orah*, but that is not identical to the Golden Dawn’s “mundane chakra of Chokmah.” See [Fifth Gate, Sixth Sefirah §98](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Fifth_Gate%2C_Sixth_Sefirah.98).

## 7. Cordovero

### Pardes Rimmonim, 1548

Moses Cordovero composed *Pardes Rimmonim* in Safed in 1548. It sought to reconcile earlier kabbalistic opinions into a coherent non-Lurianic system.

### Fixed enumeration

**[DT]** Cordovero enumerates:

1. Keter;
2. Chokmah;
3. Binah;
4. Gedulah/Chesed;
5. Gevurah;
6. Tiferet;
7. Netzach;
8. Hod;
9. Yesod;
10. Malkhut.

He explicitly states that the name of Chokmah is **Yah**. See [*Pardes Rimmonim* 1:10](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.1.10).

### Hidden Keter and two beginnings

**[DT]** Cordovero reads *Bereshit* as containing two levels of beginning:

- hidden Keter;
- manifest Chokmah.

See [*Pardes Rimmonim* 3:3:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.3.3.5).

### Job 28 and emanational order

**[DT]** Cordovero invokes “Wisdom, from where shall it be found?” to relate Ayin, Chokmah, and Binah. See [*Pardes Rimmonim* 1:8:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.1.8.1).

### Body

**[DT]** Cordovero identifies **moaḥ**, the brain, with Chokmah. The skull or covering of the brain corresponds to Keter; within the brain the powers Chokmah, Binah, and Da’at can be differentiated. See [*Pardes Rimmonim* 23:13:36](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.23.13.36).

Cordovero expressly warns that anthropomorphic language does not imply an actual divine body: bodily structures exist below because their roots exist non-corporeally above. See [*Pardes Rimmonim* 2:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.2.1.1-14).

### Da’at

**[DT]** Cordovero does not count Da’at as a separate eleventh sefirah. It is the hidden mediating reality—often the unification or offspring of Chokmah and Binah—manifesting in relation to Tiferet. See [*Pardes Rimmonim* 2:3:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.2.3.1).

### Channels and diagrams

Cordovero says that infinitely many relationships are possible but enumerates principal channels. In *Or Ne’erav*, Part VI, chapter 2, Chokmah has channels to:

- Binah;
- Chesed;
- Gevurah;
- Tiferet;

and receives from Keter. This yields five immediate relationships if the incoming Keter channel is counted.

Ira Robinson translates the passage in *Moses Cordovero’s Introduction to Kabbalah: An Annotated Translation of Or Ne’erav* (1994), pp. 120–21. A comparative discussion and quotation appears [here](https://latinsefiroth.blogspot.com/2015/08/paths-planets-and-golden-dawn.html).

**[D]** Manuscript and printed diagrams do not always agree with Cordovero’s prose. Some printed *Pardes* trees have twenty rather than twenty-two visible lines. That discrepancy is evidence against imagining a single unchanged “Jewish Tree” handed down from antiquity.

### Or Ne’erav

*Or Ne’erav* is a shorter pedagogical introduction, written after *Pardes* and printed in Venice in 1587. Its central aim is to prepare a qualified student ethically and intellectually for Kabbalah. See the [Sefaria text](https://www.sefaria.org/Ohr_Ne%27erav).

## 8. Luria through Ḥayyim Vital

Isaac Luria left relatively little writing. The principal literary form of “Lurianic Kabbalah” comes through Ḥayyim Vital and later editors. *Etz Chaim* is therefore Vital’s presentation of Lurianic teaching, not a book personally authored and finalized by Luria.

### Tzimtzum and the line

**[KT]** Lurianic cosmology begins with Ein Sof, the contraction or concealment called **tzimtzum**, and the entry of a measured **kav**, line or ray, into the vacated space.

Chokmah itself is not simply identical to the cosmic line. The line precedes and produces complex worlds and partzufim in which Chokmah appears repeatedly.

### Four worlds

The four principal worlds are:

- **Atzilut**—Emanation;
- **Beriah**—Creation;
- **Yetzirah**—Formation;
- **Assiah**—Action.

**[KT]** Each world contains a complete ten-sefirah configuration. It is therefore misleading to say that Chokmah exists only in Atzilut. Chokmah of Atzilut, Chokmah of Beriah, and so on are distinguishable levels.

In Western correspondence tables the worlds are often placed into four color scales and mapped onto YHVH. That is an occult ritual system built from, but not identical with, Lurianic metaphysics.

### Chokmah as Abba

In the world of Tikkun, the sefirot become dynamic **partzufim**, configurations or “countenances”:

- Keter → Arikh Anpin and Atik;
- Chokmah → **Abba**;
- Binah → **Imma**;
- the six central sefirot → Ze’ir Anpin;
- Malkhut → Nukva.

Developed analysis further distinguishes:

- **Abba Ila’ah**, Supernal Father;
- **Yisrael Sava**, Israel the Elder.

Chokmah is therefore not erased by the partzuf Abba. Abba is Chokmah reorganized as a relational, internally articulated configuration.

### Shattering of the vessels

**[KT]** In the world of Nekudim/Tohu, the vessels cannot sustain the intensity and disunity of the lights.

A necessary nuance:

- the seven lower vessels undergo the principal shattering;
- the upper configurations are not simply untouched;
- the “backs” or lower aspects of Abba and Imma fall or are impaired.

See [*Etz Chaim* 9:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.2).

### Tikkun

Tikkun is the reorganization of isolated points into relational partzufim, including the restored union of Abba and Imma. Sparks are elevated through commandments, prayer, study, and ethical-religious action. See [*Etz Chaim* 26:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.26.1).

**[D]** It is reductive to translate tikkun as merely personal self-improvement. In Vital it is cosmic, ritual, anthropological, and theurgical.

### Chokmah in Etz Chaim

Examples include:

- concentric or circular configurations containing Keter and Chokmah: [*Etz Chaim* 1:4:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.4.11);
- Abba/Imma and their damaged backs: [9:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.2);
- the relation of the four worlds and reception through Keter and Chokmah: [ABYA, Seventh Gate](https://otzar.shaulmolcho.com/library/etz-chayim-part-three/abya-the-seventh-gate).

## 9. The Gra’s tree and interpretation

Elijah ben Solomon Zalman, the Vilna Gaon, 1720–1797, wrote an influential commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### Intellectual meaning

**[DT/KT]** The Gra treats Chokmah as the undifferentiated readiness of intellect to apprehend; Binah articulates a definite conception. He associates Chokmah with the Yod, the opening of the paths, and the brain under the skull. See [the Gra’s commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah* 1:1](https://www.sefaria.org/HaGra_on_Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.1.1).

### Diagram

The widely reproduced “Gra tree,” especially through Aryeh Kaplan, arranges:

- the three Mothers on horizontal channels;
- the seven Doubles on vertical channels;
- the twelve Simples on diagonals.

See Kaplan, *Sefer Yetzirah*, figure 5, reproduced in [this searchable edition](https://leonbahrmanministries.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Aryeh-Kaplan-Sefer-Yetzirah2.pdf).

In this reconstruction Chokmah is joined to:

- Keter;
- Binah;
- Chesed;
- Tiferet.

Thus its immediate graph-neighbors resemble the Kircher graph, but the letter placement does not. Most importantly:

- Chokmah–Binah is the Mother Shin/Fire in the Gra scheme;
- it is Dalet/Venus/Empress in the Golden Dawn scheme.

**[D]** “The Gra tree” is a modern name for diagrams reconstructed from the Gra’s textual principles. It should not be treated as an ancient picture contained in the earliest *Sefer Yetzirah* manuscripts.

## 10. Hasidic readings

### Shneur Zalman of Liadi: Tanya

The most relevant discussion is not confined to a single chapter.

#### Chapters 18–19

**[DT/KT]** Chapter 18 locates the hidden love of God in the soul’s Chokmah. Chokmah is “above understanding and comprehension,” a point of faith and inherited attachment to God.

Chapter 19 develops the image of a flame that naturally rises and seeks absorption in its source. Chokmah is complete nullification before Ein Sof. See [*Tanya*, Likkutei Amarim 18–19](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.18-19).

The wordplay is **koaḥ mah**: the power of “What am I?” or self-nullification.

**[KT]** This gives Chokmah a devotional and psychological register: not accumulated information, but the non-discursive point that permits self-transcending fidelity.

#### Iggeret ha-Kodesh

Letter 5 develops the Zoharic formula “Father founded the daughter,” Chokmah flowing into speech/Malkhut. See [*Tanya*, Iggeret ha-Kodesh 5](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_IV%3B_Iggeret_HaKodesh.5).

Letter 28 discusses Abba, the supernal mazal, and Torah proceeding from Chokmah. See [Iggeret 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_IV%3B_Iggeret_HaKodesh.28).

### Rabbi Naḥman of Bratslav

**[DT/KT]** *Likkutei Moharan* 35 teaches that repentance returns a thing to its root in Chokmah, invoking Psalm 104:24, “You made them all with wisdom.” See [*Likkutei Moharan* 35:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.35.1).

Lessons 49:1 and 49:4 locate creation through Chokmah in the heart; corrupt thought damages that creative order. See [49:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.49.1) and [49:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.49.4).

Lesson 21 treats the transcendent intellects or *makifin*, relevant to Chokmah as cognition not yet domesticated by discursive understanding. See [21:1–5](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.21.1-5).

## 11. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and calendar

### Prayer

**[KT]** Lurianic prayer manuals map divine names, vowels, words, and ritual intentions onto complex partzufic structures. Chokmah may be activated as Abba, Yod, or a moḥin-state.

**[AE]** There is no single universally binding “Chokmah prayer” equivalent to the Golden Dawn’s one-line correspondence.

Jewish kabbalists repeatedly warn that prayer is addressed to God, not to a sefirah as an independent deity. The sefirot are channels, names, or modalities of divine action.

### Torah

**[KT]** Torah is frequently rooted in Chokmah:

- it is primordial Wisdom;
- its letters unfold from the point of Yod;
- its undifferentiated totality precedes articulated interpretation in Binah;
- mitzvot elevate sparks in the work of tikkun.

### Counting of the Omer

**[AE]** The standard kabbalistic Omer count uses the **seven lower sefirot**, seven weeks each containing seven days:

Chesed through Malkhut.

Chokmah has no separate week or day in this 7×7 count. It may be invoked as the supernal intellect that illuminates the lower attributes, but “Chokmah of Chokmah” is not one of the conventional forty-nine Omer combinations. See this [traditional account of the seven emotional attributes](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/67788/jewish/Discourse-25.htm).

### Month and tribe

**[AE]** Months and tribes attach to the twelve Simple letters, not to Chokmah as a sefirah. Any table giving Chokmah a single Hebrew month or tribe is combining different correspondence rows.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* introduced “Cabala” as a major source for Christian theological demonstration.

**[DT]** Pico distinguishes:

> “scientia Sephiroth et Semot”

—the science of the sefirot and the divine names. He calls the sefirot **numerationes**, “numerations,” and states:

> **Ensoph non est aliis numeracionibus connumeranda**  
> “Ein Sof is not to be numbered with the other numerations.”

See the Latin [*Conclusiones*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/pico/conclus.htm).

**[HO]** Pico interpreted Jewish material Christologically, especially in his seventy-one conclusions “confirming the Christian religion.”

**[AE]** His 1486 theses do not provide the later complete Chokmah dossier—Yah, Raziel, Ophanim, Zodiac, colors, tarot, and grade.

**[SR/D]** Pico’s importance is therefore foundational but indirect. He legitimized a Christian Cabala in which later thinkers could reinterpret Chokmah as divine *sapientia*, Logos, Son, or a Trinitarian mystery.

## 2. Portae Lucis, 1516

Paulus Ricius’s Latin translation of Gikatilla’s *Sha’arei Orah* appeared as *Portae Lucis* in Augsburg in 1516.

The title-page woodcut is associated with Hans Burgkmair. See the [Center for Jewish Art record](https://cja.huji.ac.il/index.php?id=40577&mode=set).

**[SR]** It is among the earliest printed sefirotic trees, not the earliest surviving tree diagram. J. H. Chajes’s research dates the ilanot diagram genre to the fourteenth century. See the [Ilanot Project](https://www.ilanot.org/pages?page=about) and Chajes, *The Kabbalistic Tree* ([Penn State University Press](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/978-0-271-09345-1.html)).

The woodcut contains fewer than the later canonical twenty-two lines and cannot be treated as a Golden Dawn path diagram.

## 3. Johannes Reuchlin, 1517

*De arte cabalistica* was published at Hagenau in 1517. It is a dialogue among a Jewish kabbalist, a Pythagorean, and a Muslim interlocutor.

Reuchlin renders wisdom through Latin **sapientia** and presents:

- *maʿaseh bereshit* as wisdom/science of nature;
- *maʿaseh merkavah* as wisdom of divinity;
- Hebrew Kabbalah as the source of Pythagorean number wisdom.

See the [1517 scan](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105) and the modern Latin-German critical edition: Widu-Wolfgang Ehlers and Fritz Felgentreu, eds., *Sämtliche Werke II/1: De arte cabalistica libri tres* (2010), [publisher record](https://www.frommann-holzboog.de/editionen/1706/170600211?lang=de).

**[AE]** Reuchlin does not yet give the Golden Dawn Chokmah row. His importance lies in transmitting Hebrew names, number symbolism, divine-name speculation, and Christian theological recoding.

## 4. Agrippa, 1533

Although not named in the requested sequence, Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa is essential to the correspondence-table tradition.

His *De occulta philosophia* organizes natural, celestial, and intellectual worlds; lists angelic orders; and provides planetary names, divine names, symbols, and magical operations. See Joseph Peterson’s edition of [Book III](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agrippa3.htm).

Agrippa knows Raziel as an angelic name and gives hierarchical material concerning Ophanim, but **[AE]** his work does not yet securely document the complete later identification “Raziel specifically rules Chokmah.”

## 5. Kircher, 1652–1654

Athanasius Kircher printed his influential Tree in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I, class VII, syntagma III, around p. 305.

The plate labels Wisdom in Latin as **Sapientia**, with a Hebrew/transliterated form of Chokmah. See:

- the [digitized *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp67641);
- [Google Books scan](https://books.google.be/books?id=0M5TAAAAcAAJ);
- [high-resolution reconstruction of Kircher’s Tree](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tree_of_life_Kircher_Hebrew.svg).

### Kircher’s layout

In the graph adopted by the Golden Dawn, Chokmah joins:

- Keter;
- Binah;
- Chesed;
- Tiferet.

The Golden Dawn assigns:

- Aleph to Keter–Chokmah;
- Dalet to Chokmah–Binah;
- Heh to Chokmah–Tiferet;
- Vav to Chokmah–Chesed.

### How it differs from the Ari tree

The common Cordoverian/Lurianic or “Ari” graph:

- adds Chokmah–Gevurah;
- adds Binah–Chesed;
- removes Netzach–Malkhut;
- removes Hod–Malkhut;
- leaves Malkhut receiving graphically through Yesod alone.

Thus Ari/Cordovero Chokmah touches Keter, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, and Tiferet.

### How it differs from the Gra tree

The popular Gra reconstruction has the same immediate Chokmah neighbors as the Kircher graph—Keter, Binah, Chesed, Tiferet—but distributes the letter classes structurally:

- Mothers horizontally;
- Doubles vertically;
- Simples diagonally.

Consequently Chokmah–Binah is Shin/Fire in the Gra scheme, not Dalet/Venus/Empress.

### Provenance dispute

**[D]** Kircher did not invent every line from nothing. His diagram probably depends partly on Philippe d’Aquin’s 1625 tree and on Jewish printed/manuscript sources. Yet the letter-to-line ordering used by the Golden Dawn is not established as an ancient Jewish arrangement.

The strongest conclusion is:

> Kircher standardized one early-modern Christian synthesis. The Golden Dawn adopted its geometry and imposed its own consistent letter-tarot-astrology sequence upon it.

## 6. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* appeared in Sulzbach in 1677–78 and Frankfurt in 1684.

Its Latin title is:

> *Kabbala denudata, seu doctrina Hebraeorum transcendentalis et metaphysica atque theologica*.

It includes Latin translations or summaries of:

- Zoharic texts;
- Cordovero;
- Gikatilla;
- Abraham Cohen de Herrera;
- Lurianic material associated with Vital;
- later Christian Cabala.

See the [complete scan index](https://www.billheidrick.com/Orpd/KRKD/index.htm) and [e-rara scan](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/1284225.pdf).

Typical Latin terminology is **Chochma, Sapientia**—Chokmah, Wisdom. Knorr transmits the descriptions:

- masculine active potency;
- **AB**, Father;
- **YH**, Yah;
- the Yod of the Tetragrammaton.

**[SR]** Recent scholarship has identified Hebrew manuscript sources underlying at least one of Knorr’s Lurianic translations. See the 2024 study, [“Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s Translation of a Lurianic Treatise”](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2016/html?lang=en).

**[D]** Knorr’s work is neither simply fabricated nor a neutral window onto “the authentic Kabbalah.” It is an enormous Christian-edited anthology with selective translation, reorganization, and theological framing.

Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* translates only a small part—approximately five percent—of the full *Kabbala Denudata*. See [Mathers’s introduction and text](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/tku/tku03.htm).

## 7. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

Lévi’s *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* was published in two volumes in 1854 and 1856. Its twenty-two-chapter structure mirrors the twenty-two tarot trumps and Hebrew letters.

**[DT/HO]** Lévi states:

> “The twenty-two keys of the Tarot are the twenty-two letters…”

See the [French/English scan](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/ceremonial/classics/pdf/transcendental/dogma_et_rituel_part_2.pdf).

His ordering begins Aleph with trump I, the Magician, rather than the Golden Dawn’s Aleph–Fool arrangement. Lévi’s system therefore does not simply equal the later Golden Dawn sequence.

**[HO]** Lévi’s decisive innovation was to make the correspondence between twenty-two trumps, twenty-two letters, and kabbalistic paths programmatic.

**[D]** Lévi believed Tarot preserved ancient Egyptian/Hermetic wisdom. Modern tarot history does not support that origin legend.

## 8. Golden Dawn

### Knowledge Lectures

The late-nineteenth-century Golden Dawn system gives Chokmah:

- God-name: **Yah**;
- archangel: **Ratziel**;
- angelic choir: **Auphanim**;

**[D]** It is not the wording of the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah*. ## 10. Dion Fortune, 1935

Fortune’s *The Mystical Qabalah* was published by Williams and Norgate in 1935, 306 pages. Chapter XVI is “Chokmah, the Second Sephirah.” See the [1935 bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Mystical_Qabalah.html?id=YkCxFg6efM4C) and [chapter text](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html).

In the 1998 Society of the Inner Light pagination, the chapter begins around p. 118; pagination varies by edition.

### Full analytical account of the chapter

Fortune begins with the inherited tabulation:

- bearded male figure;
- Pillar of Mercy;
- Illuminating Intelligence;
- Power of Yetzirah;
- Abba/Supernal Father;
- Tetragrammaton and Yod;
- Jehovah;
- Ratziel;
- Auphanim;
- Mazloth;
- vision of God face to face;
- devotion;
- left side of face;
- lingam, phallus, standing stone, tower, rod, straight line;
- the four Twos;
- the four colors.

Her argument then proceeds as follows.

1. **Keter as point, Chokmah as line.**  
   Keter is potential; Chokmah is its dynamic extension. A point moving produces a line. Chokmah is therefore the first “positive” or outgoing force.

2. **Force and form.**  
   Chokmah is paired with Binah. Chokmah is energy; Binah gives limitation, organization, and form. Neither is creative alone.

3. **The Great Stimulator.**  
   Chokmah is the impulse causing latent capacities elsewhere on the Tree to become active. It is not primarily a storehouse of information.

4. **Sexual symbolism.**  
   Fortune interprets phallus, lingam, standing stone, tower, wand, and rod as symbols of outgoing generative force. She insists that cosmic polarity is broader than physical sexuality, although her explanation remains saturated with sexual categories.

5. **The Supernal Father.**  
   Abba is the universal paternal or fecundating principle. The Yod is a seed-point and the initiating letter of YHVH.

6. **Zodiac.**  
   Mazloth represents the total stellar field rather than one planet. Chokmah is a universal force expressed through the whole Zodiac.

7. **Vision and devotion.**  
   The “vision of God face to face” belongs to Chokmah, and the proper virtue is devotion. The experience is not discursive knowledge but an overwhelming direct encounter.

8. **Colors.**  
   Pure blue is the primary force; grey and pearl-grey express the mistlike mediation of Wisdom; Assiah’s white flecked with red, blue, and yellow shows the latent primaries in manifestation.

9. **Tarot Twos.**  
   The Twos signify balanced polarization: force has met its complement but has not yet entered the fuller complexity of subsequent numbers.

10. **No ordinary ritual attainment of the Supernals.**  
    Fortune warns that direct operative contact with the Supernals is not achieved by routine ceremonial invocation. Their principles are known through their reflection and participation in lower levels.

11. **The Magus.**  
    Although Fortune does not reproduce Crowley’s complete A∴A∴ doctrine, Chokmah is the sphere of the creative Magus, Word, and outgoing power.

### Critical assessment

- **[HO]** The chapter is one of the clearest syntheses of Golden Dawn Chokmah symbolism.
- **[D]** Fortune’s presentation sometimes calls late occult attributions simply “Qabalistic,” obscuring their recent lineage.
- **[D]** Her fixed male/female psychology reflects early-twentieth-century essentialism. It is not a neutral statement of either contemporary psychology or all Jewish Kabbalah.
- **[D]** “Jehovah” differs from the Golden Dawn’s more precise Yah.
- **[D]** An apparent wording or typesetting error in some editions assigns a nearby sentence’s spiritual experience to Keter, though the table and argument clearly mean Chokmah.

## 11. Regardie, Case, and later expositors

### Israel Regardie

Regardie published Golden Dawn papers in *The Golden Dawn* from 1937 onward and thereby transformed private order material into the standard public curriculum.

His accounts preserve:

- Yah;
- Ratziel;
- Auphanim;
- Mazloth;
- the four color scales;
- the Magus/Chokmah relationship;
- ritual use of the Tree.

Regardie did not independently discover these correspondences; he disseminated and systematized Golden Dawn teaching.

### Paul Foster Case

Case, through the Builders of the Adytum, retained much of the Golden Dawn letter-tarot structure while revising symbolism, pathworking methods, and color teaching. B.O.T.A. strongly emphasizes Chokmah as archetypal Wisdom and as the source of the Kings.

Unlike Crowley, Case rejected the Tzaddi–Heh exchange.

### Gareth Knight

Knight’s *A Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* (1965) develops Fortune’s approach into a fuller magical psychology. Chokmah is dynamic archetypal force, known through polarity with Binah and reflected through the whole Tree.

### Pathworking

The now-familiar practice of entering a tarot-colored landscape, meeting symbolic figures, and traveling from one sefirah to another is **[HO/MI]**. It developed through Golden Dawn astral work, Case, Regardie, Fortune’s successors, and Knight.

It is not described in this form by *Sefer Yetzirah*, the *Bahir*, or the Zohar.

# The Qliphah or tunnel

## Jewish qelippot

**[DT/KT]** Hebrew **קליפה**, *qelippah*, means shell, husk, or peel. Zoharic and Lurianic texts use qelippot for forces of concealment, impurity, judgment, or parasitic existence.

In Lurianism the qelippot are deeply connected with:

- the world of Tohu;
- shattered vessels;
- fallen sparks;
- mixtures of good and evil;
- their rectification through commandments.

**[AE]** Classical Jewish sources do not ordinarily present the exact modern list of ten shadow spheres, each with a demon prince, as a practical “Tree of Death” for initiation.

## The tenfold Hermetic list

The standard later list is:

| Sefirah | Qliphah | Common translation |
|---|---|---|
| Keter | Thaumiel/Thamiel | Dual Contending Forces |
| Chokmah | Ghagiel, Ghogiel, Ogiel, Oghiel, Zogiel | Hinderers |
| Binah | Satariel/Sathariel | Concealers |
| Chesed | Gha’agsheblah, Gamchicoth | Breakers/Devourers |
| Gevurah | Golachab/Golab | Burners |
| Tiferet | Thagirion/Tagiriron | Disputers |
| Netzach | A’arab Zaraq/Harab Serapel | Ravens of Dispersion |
| Hod | Samael | Deceivers/Poison of God |
| Yesod | Gamaliel | Obscene Ones |
| Malkhut | Lilith/Nahemoth | Evil Woman/Whisperers |

The list has roots in early-modern Kabbalistic/Christian-Cabalistic compilations, including *Kabbala Denudata*, but its modern standardized English form is chiefly Golden Dawn and Crowleyan.

## Ghagiel

### Name and form

The Golden Dawn paper gives:

- Zogiel;
- Ghogiel;
- Oghiel;
- association with Og of Bashan;
- Dukes of Edom;
- lying and material appearances;
- black giants with serpents.

See [“The Qliphoth”](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html).

Crowley’s *777* calls them the **Hinderers**.

**[D]** Proposed Hebrew spellings and etymologies vary sharply. “Hinderers” is the received occult translation, not a secure lexicographical derivation from one transparently attested Hebrew noun.

### Demon ruler controversy

The Golden Dawn source explicitly warns that demon names cannot be restricted neatly to single sefirot, then gives:

- Keter—Satan and Moloch;
- Binah—Beelzebub;
- **Chokmah—Lucifuge**;
- Chesed—Ashtaroth;
- Gevurah—Asmodeus;
- Tiferet—Belphegor;
- Netzach—Baal;
- Hod—Adrammelech;
- Yesod—Lilith;
- Malkhut—Nahemah.

It also says **Belial** unifies the force of the averse Chokmah in a separate threefold arrangement.

Therefore:

- **[DT/HO]** Lucifuge is the clearest Chokmah ruler in the cited Golden Dawn paper.
- **[DT/HO]** Belial is also attached to averse Chokmah, but in another hierarchy.
- **[MI/D]** Beelzebub as Ghagiel’s ruler is widespread in later demonological tables, especially post-Grant and Karlsson.
- **[AE]** A secure primary-text chain making Beelzebub ruler of Ghagiel before the later occult synthesis was not established.
- The claim that Mathers cited a “magical papyrus” saying Beelzebub “bars the light” appears to be a modern fabricated quotation.

## Kenneth Grant and the Tunnels of Set

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* was published in 1977. It transforms the Golden Dawn/Crowley qliphoth into a Typhonian system of twenty-two “Tunnels of Set,” using Crowley, Austin Osman Spare, sexual magic, extraterrestrial imagery, and Grant’s visionary interpretation.

See the [1977 text](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf).

A sefirah has no single tunnel. Ghagiel is a node adjoining several tunnels in Grant’s nightside arrangement:

| Letter/path | Tunnel | Connection in Grant’s scheme |
|---|---|---|
| Aleph | Amprodias | Thaumiel–Ghagiel |
| Dalet | Dagdagiel | Ghagiel–Satariel |
| Vav | Uriens | Ghagiel–Gha’agsheblah |
| Zayin | Zamradiel | Ghagiel–Thagirion |

See the comparative [Tunnels of Set table](https://www.qliphoth-archive.com/en/tunnels.html).

**[MI]** These tunnel names and pathworking doctrines are twentieth-century Typhonian occultism. They are not in the Zohar, *Pardes Rimmonim*, or Vital.

# Paths touching Chokmah

Chokmah is a sefirah, not a letter-path. The path-specific portion of the brief therefore applies only to the lines touching it in the relevant diagrams.

## Comparative graph

| System | Sefirot directly joined to Chokmah |
|---|---|
| Cordovero/*Or Ne’erav* | Keter, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet |
| Common “Ari tree” | Keter, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet |
| Popular Gra reconstruction | Keter, Binah, Chesed, Tiferet |
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Keter, Binah, Chesed, Tiferet |

The crucial difference between Gra and Golden Dawn is often not the four neighboring nodes but the assignment of letters to the lines.

## Kircher/Golden Dawn paths

| No. | Letter | Name/meaning | Class | Golden Dawn attribution | Trump | Joins |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | א, Aleph, value 1 | Ox; historically from an ox-head letter-form | Mother | Air | Fool | Keter–Chokmah |
| 14 | ד, Dalet, value 4 | Door | Double | Venus | Empress | Chokmah–Binah |
| 15 | ה, Heh, value 5 | Window; “behold” is a separate wordplay | Simple | Aries | Emperor in Golden Dawn | Chokmah–Tiferet |
| 16 | ו, Vav, value 6 | Hook, peg, nail | Simple | Taurus | Hierophant | Chokmah–Chesed |

### Sefer Yetzirah comparison

- Aleph is a Mother and Air in the central recension tradition.
- Dalet is one of the seven Doubles, but its planet varies by recension. Early manuscript traditions often make it Mars; Golden Dawn makes it Venus.
- Heh and Vav are Simples. The common zodiacal sequence gives Heh Aries and Vav Taurus.
- *Sefer Yetzirah* does not say which pair of sefirot any of these letters joins.

### Westcott’s “Intelligences”

| Path | Westcott title |
|---|---|
| 11, Aleph | Scintillating Intelligence |
| 14, Dalet | Illuminating Intelligence |
| 15, Heh | Constituting Intelligence |
| 16, Vav | Triumphal or Eternal Intelligence |

This means “Illuminating Intelligence” occurs both as the title of sefirah 2 and, in different wording, path 14 in the appended list. The duplication is one symptom of its composite and unstable textual transmission.

## Tzaddi/Heh problem

Golden Dawn:

- Heh = Aries = Emperor on path 15, Chokmah–Tiferet;
- Tzaddi = Aquarius = Star on path 28, Netzach–Yesod.

Crowley’s *Liber AL* I:57 declares:

> “Tzaddi is not the Star.”

In *The Book of Thoth*, Crowley says Tzaddi is the Emperor and counterchanges trumps IV and XVII. See the [1944 text](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/crowley/equinox/volume3/vol_3_no_05.pdf).

The resulting evidence is internally awkward:

- Crowley’s prose keeps the Emperor’s Aries symbolism while calling the card Tzaddi.
- His “double loop” diagram implies Emperor–Tzaddi–Aquarius and Star–Heh–Aries.
- Other Thelemic diagrams effectively swap letters/trumps while retaining familiar signs.
- The Thoth card imagery preserves strong Aries symbolism for the Emperor.

**[D]** There is therefore no wholly unambiguous single “Crowley solution” across every text and diagram. The clearest doctrinal statement is: Emperor belongs to Tzaddi and Star to Heh. Whether the signs travel with the letters, cards, or geometric paths is handled inconsistently.

For Chokmah this matters because path 15 is one of its four Golden Dawn connections:

- Golden Dawn: Heh–Aries–Emperor;
- one common Thelemic reading: Heh–Aquarius–Star;
- strict double-loop reading: Heh–Aries–Star.

## Pathworking traditions

Typical Golden Dawn-derived practice includes:

1. establish the sefirah through divine and angelic names;
2. visualize the path in its color scales;
3. use the Hebrew letter and tarot image as gates;
4. test visions by correspondences and divine names;
5. return and record the vision.

**[HO]** Aleph pathworking toward Chokmah often emphasizes the Fool, Air, primal consciousness, and the leap beyond rational form.

**[HO]** Dalet emphasizes Venus and the Empress as the door uniting Father and Mother.

**[HO]** Heh emphasizes authority, sight, Aries, and creative command—or the Star under Crowley’s revision.

**[HO]** Vav emphasizes the Hierophant, Taurus, hearing, doctrine, and transmission from archetypal Wisdom into Chesed.

**[AE]** These are not traditional Jewish techniques for ascending Cordovero’s channels.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Dating Sefer Yetzirah

- Traditional accounts attribute it to Abraham or Rabbi Akiva.
- Older scholarship proposed dates as early as the second century.
- Linguistic and conceptual studies often place substantial formation between the third and sixth centuries.
- Hayman stresses a fluid textual tradition known through later witnesses.
- Bar-Asher argues for a different earliest recoverable form close to the Saadyan/Genizah text.

No exact date commands consensus.

## 2. Does Sefer Yetzirah teach Chokmah as sefirah two?

**No, not in its earliest recoverable wording.**

It teaches:

- thirty-two paths of wisdom;
- ten sefirot belimah;
- second: breath from breath;
- engraving of twenty-two letters.

The equation “second sefirah = Chokmah” is the result of later Kabbalistic interpretation.

## 3. Zohar authorship

- **Tradition:** Rabbi Shimon bar Yoḥai.
- **Historical consensus:** late-thirteenth-century Castile, with Moses de León central.
- **Current refinement:** multiple compositional strata and probably a literary circle, rather than a simplistic single-act forgery model.

The traditional persona remains part of the Zohar’s religious meaning; it is not the same kind of claim as historical authorship.

## 4. Christian appropriation

Christian Cabalists preserved, translated, and publicized Jewish texts. They also:

- removed them from Jewish law and liturgy;
- reinterpreted them as proofs of Trinity and Christ;
- sometimes participated in conversionary polemics;
- combined them with Pythagoreanism, Neoplatonism, Hermetism, astrology, and magic.

“Transmission” and “appropriation” are both historically justified descriptions. The balance depends on the author and context.

## 5. Kircher versus Jewish trees

There never was one universally fixed Jewish picture.

Nevertheless:

- Cordoverian/Lurianic paths commonly cross Chokmah–Gevurah and Binah–Chesed;
- Kircher instead gives Malkhut three connections;
- Golden Dawn follows Kircher’s graph;
- Gra-style attribution follows the Mother/Double/Simple classes geometrically rather than alphabetical path order.

Calling Kircher’s diagram “the ancient Hebrew Tree” is unsupported.

## 6. Tarot attribution

- Tarot trumps originated as early modern playing cards.
- Lévi made the Hebrew-letter connection systematic.
- Golden Dawn moved the Fool before the Magician and developed the modern occult sequence.
- Crowley challenged Heh–Emperor and Tzaddi–Star.
- Case and Waite retained Golden Dawn’s basic Heh/Tzaddi scheme.

There is no medieval Jewish tarot attribution to Chokmah.

## 7. Da’at

*Sefer Yetzirah* says ten, not eleven.

Kabbalistic resolutions include:

- count Keter, do not count Da’at;
- when Keter is concealed, count Da’at as the manifest integration of Chokmah and Binah;
- describe Da’at as a state, conjunction, or internal aspect rather than a separate vessel.

Cordovero’s treatment supports the non-independent status of Da’at. A useful later summary is [“Two Systems of Ten Sefirot”](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/380536/jewish/Two-Systems-of-Ten-Sefirot.htm).

The occult practice of drawing Da’at as an eleventh dotted sphere is a graphical convention. It does not prove that Jewish Kabbalah has eleven sefirot.

## 8. Yah versus YHVH

- Yah is the most textually secure specific name for Chokmah in Gikatilla, Cordovero, and Golden Dawn tables.
- Yod of YHVH is Chokmah.
- YHVH can symbolize a wider four-stage system.
- Knorr/Mathers list Yah and YHVH.
- Fortune says Jehovah.

The alternatives belong to different levels of symbolism; they should not be flattened into a single textual attribution.

## 9. Raziel and the Auphanim

Raziel is an old Jewish angelic name and is prominent in traditions surrounding *Sefer Raziel*. Ophanim derive from the “wheels” of Ezekiel’s chariot and become an angelic rank.

What is not established is that the early Jewish works named in the brief already contained the complete fixed equation:

> Chokmah = Raziel = Ophanim.

Its secure, fully tabulated form belongs to Christian/Hermetic correspondence literature and the Golden Dawn.

## 10. Mazloth and modern planets

- Mazloth means constellations, astral configurations, or the Zodiac.
- It is not a single planet.
- Uranus was discovered in 1781, Neptune in 1846, and Pluto in 1930.
- Assigning any of them to Chokmah is necessarily modern.
- Crowley’s own rearrangements were inconsistent.

## 11. Phallus, wand, and straight line

- Jewish Kabbalah calls Chokmah Father, seed, point, and Yod.
- Strong erotic symbolism is genuinely present in Zoharic and Lurianic literature.
- The exact tabulation “lingam, phallus, tower, uplifted rod, straight line” is Golden Dawn/Fortune/Crowley.
- “Lingam” imports Hindu terminology and should not be attributed to medieval Jewish Kabbalah.

## 12. Virtue and vice

“Devotion” is an occult initiatory attribution, not the lexical meaning of Chokmah.

The inherited table leaves its vice blank. Modern assignments such as pride, fanaticism, delusion, addiction, or reckless force are interpretive inventions. “Insanity” appears in *777* as a pathological correspondence, not a clinical claim that wisdom causes mental illness.

## 13. Fortune’s gender psychology

Fortune’s force/form model is influential and internally coherent. Its claims about essential male and female psychology are historically situated and disputed. Jewish kabbalistic symbolism is itself gendered, but it is more relational and textually variable than Fortune’s binary presentation suggests.

## 14. Ghagiel’s ruler

The sources do not support one timeless answer:

- Golden Dawn: Lucifuge;
- separate averse triad: Belial;
- later popular occultism: Beelzebub;
- Jewish Kabbalah: no such fixed table.

Any source asserting simply “Ghagiel has always been ruled by Beelzebub” erases the documented variation.

# Open questions

1. **Earliest named occurrence:** Precisely which *Bahir* stratum first identifies *reshit* with Chokmah remains difficult because the work is composite and its manuscript history is complex.

2. **Diagram transmission:** More manuscript study is needed to trace the exact channel graph from early ilanot through Cordovero, Philippe d’Aquin, Kircher, and Knorr.

3. **Raziel’s assignment:** The exact first printed witness for the complete Raziel–Chokmah equation should be established through a systematic comparison of Hebrew angelological works, Renaissance tables, Kircher, and Knorr.

4. **Ophanim’s assignment:** Biblical Ophanim, medieval angelic orders, and the Golden Dawn’s four-world hierarchy are related but not identical. The bridge deserves a dedicated stemmatic study.

5. **Ari recension:** The common phrase “Ari version of *Sefer Yetzirah*” obscures the difference between Lurianic teaching, later redaction, the Gra’s recension, and modern Kaplan diagrams.

6. **Kabbala Denudata terminology:** A complete concordance of Knorr’s Chochma/Sapientia entries across all volumes would clarify which later Golden Dawn correspondences derive directly from Knorr and which entered through other sources.

7. **Qliphothic demon rulers:** The path from Knorr’s “ten impure numerations” to Mathers’s competing ruler lists and later Beelzebub attribution is not adequately documented in popular literature.

8. **Crowley’s Heh/Tzaddi solution:** Crowley’s prose, tarot images, tables, and diagrams do not yield one perfectly consistent rule for whether trumps, letters, zodiacal signs, or geometrical paths are exchanged.

9. **Color manuscripts:** Surviving Golden Dawn colored Trees vary in pigment, copying, and nomenclature. Modern digital reproductions can conceal manuscript differences.

10. **Modern Jewish reception:** Chabad, Breslov, Ashlagian Kabbalah, contemporary yeshivot, and popular “Kabbalah” organizations do not use Chokmah identically; a full contemporary ethnography would exceed a text-history dossier.

# The Path of Samekh: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary legend

This dossier separates three things often fused in modern occult literature:

1. **Samekh as a Hebrew letter** and its Jewish exegetical, liturgical, cosmological, and kabbalistic history.
2. **The twenty-fifth “Intelligence of Probation”**, a medieval Jewish path-text later printed with *Sefer Yetzirah* but not part of its earliest recoverable text.
3. **Samekh as the Tiphareth–Yesod path**, a Renaissance/early-modern diagrammatic arrangement systematized by the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn.

Claims below are marked as follows:

- **[TEXT]** directly stated in the cited primary source.
- **[TRADITION]** a received Jewish or occult attribution, whether or not historically demonstrable.
- **[RECONSTRUCTION]** a conclusion of modern textual, historical, or iconographic scholarship.
- **[HERMETIC]** an attribution originating within Christian Cabala, ceremonial magic, Tarot occultism, or Thelema.
- **[DISPUTED]** evidence or scholarly interpretation is contested.
- **[MODERN]** a twentieth- or twenty-first-century development or synthesis.
- **[ABSENCE]** the requested attribution was not found in the relevant source and should not be retrojected into it.

### Core finding

The recoverable Jewish tradition knows Samekh principally as:

- the letter of “support”;
- the closed letter miraculously sustained in the tablets;
- the support following fallen Nun in Psalm 145;
- a simple letter associated with Sagittarius and Kislev;
- in one influential recension, the faculty of sleep.

It does **not** identify Samekh as a fixed line from Tiphareth to Yesod.

The familiar Path 25 is a composite:

```text
Sefer Yetzirah:
Samekh → Sagittarius/Kislev/(sleep; bodily locus varies)

Medieval “Thirty-Two Paths” list:
Path 25 → Sekhel Nisyoni, Intelligence of Probation

Kircher-line diagram + Golden Dawn:
Path 25 → Tiphareth–Yesod → Samekh → Sagittarius → Temperance
```

Those three layers are historically related but not coeval.

# The name

## Hebrew spelling, pronunciation, and number

**[TEXT]** The square-script form is **ס**. Its name is written **סָמֶךְ**, conventionally pronounced *SAH-mekh* in modern Israeli Hebrew. English occult literature varies among *Samekh*, *Samech*, *Samek*, and occasionally *Sameck*. Westcott generally used **Samech**; Crowley preferred **Samekh**.

The letter is fifteenth in alphabetic order and has the ordinary numerical value **60**. The full letter-name:

\[
סמך = ס(60)+מ(40)+ך(20)=120.
\]

Using the later extended values sometimes assigned to final letters would change the calculation, but 120 is the normal value in standard gematria. Samekh has no separate final form.

## Etymology

**[ETYMOLOGY]** The most secure comparison is with the Semitic root **ס־מ־ך**, “to support, uphold, sustain, lean against.” Biblical examples include:

- “the Lord **upholds** all who fall,” סוֹמֵךְ יְהוָה לְכָל־הַנֹּפְלִים, Psalm 145:14;
- “You sustain me,” תִּסְמְכֵנִי, Psalm 51:14;
- “wisdom sustains the wise,” Ecclesiastes 7:19.

Hence the traditional English occult gloss **prop** or **support** is linguistically reasonable.

**[DISPUTED]** It is less certain that the letter’s Bronze Age prototype actually pictured a prop. Proposed pictorial originals include:

- a post, support, or tent-peg;
- an Egyptian *djed*-pillar;
- a fish or fish-skeleton;
- an unidentified lattice-like object.

The Phoenician sign **𐤎** is secure; the claim that a particular Proto-Sinaitic glyph “means prop” is reconstruction, not a deciphered caption. The complex history of the Semitic sibilants makes Samekh one of the less straightforward alphabetic genealogies.

## Development of the form

**[RECONSTRUCTION]**

1. **Proto-Sinaitic/Proto-Canaanite, second millennium BCE:** possible ancestors have been proposed, but the exact identification and pictorial referent remain disputed.
2. **Phoenician, early first millennium BCE:** **𐤎**, a vertical crossed by several horizontal strokes.
3. **Paleo-Hebrew:** retained the Phoenician-type form.
4. **Imperial Aramaic:** the crossed form was simplified and progressively rounded.
5. **Jewish “square” script:** the Aramaic-derived sign developed into the closed **ס**; the fully closed shape becomes clear in late Second Temple/Hasmonean scripts.
6. **Rabbinic consequence:** because square Samekh encloses an island of parchment, the Talmud could describe the centre of Samekh in the tablets as standing miraculously. That interpretation presupposes the square form and is not evidence for the shape of a Mosaic-era alphabet.

Greek **Xi**, Ξ, descends from the Phoenician Samekh position/form, though its phonetic history diverged. Arabic lost Samekh as an independent letter during the evolution from Nabataean, while related names/forms survive in Aramaic and Syriac.

## The central biblical pun: support after falling

Psalm 145 is an alphabetic acrostic but omits Nun. The next verse begins with Samekh:

> סוֹמֵךְ יְהוָה לְכָל־הַנֹּפְלִים וְזוֹקֵף לְכָל־הַכְּפוּפִים  
> “The Lord supports all who fall and raises all who are bowed down.”

**[TEXT]** Berakhot 4b explains that Nun was omitted because it suggested Israel’s נפילה, “falling.” David then “supported them through the holy spirit”—חזר דוד וסמכן ברוח הקודש—with the Samekh verse. This is the oldest securely datable Jewish interpretive complex connecting Samekh, falling, and support. [Berakhot 4b](https://www.sefaria.org/Berakhot.4b?lang=bi)

The Zohar later dramatizes the same pun in its procession of letters.

## The requested biblical wisdom and sephirotic prooftexts

These verses belong to the history of the sefirotic Tree, but **not specifically to the name Samekh**.

### 1 Chronicles 29:11

> לְךָ יְהוָה הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד ... לְךָ יְהוָה הַמַּמְלָכָה  
> “Yours, YHWH, are greatness, power, beauty, victory, and majesty … Yours is kingship.”

**[TRADITION]** Kabbalists read this as a biblical sequence for the lower seven sefirot: Ḥesed/Gedullah, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, “all” or Yesod, and Malkhut. It does not assign letters to the connecting channels. [1 Chronicles 29:11](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11)

### Proverbs 3:19–20

> יְהוָה בְּחָכְמָה יָסַד־אָרֶץ כּוֹנֵן שָׁמַיִם בִּתְבוּנָה׃ בְּדַעְתּוֹ תְּהוֹמוֹת נִבְקָעוּ  
> “YHWH founded the earth by Wisdom, established the heavens by Understanding; by His Knowledge the depths were split.”

**[TRADITION]** This supported the triad Chokhmah–Binah–Daʿat and the creative meaning of Yesod, “foundation.” It is not a Samekh attribution. [Proverbs 3:19–20](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19)

### Job 28

Job asks repeatedly where Wisdom and Understanding are found and speaks of a נתיב, “path,” unknown to birds of prey. **[TRADITION]** Later kabbalists and occultists treated the chapter as a biblical archetype of hidden Paths of Wisdom; no verse identifies the twenty-fifth path or Samekh. [Job 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28)

### Isaiah

Isaiah 11:2 lists spirit, wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear of YHWH. **[TRADITION]** These became sources for kabbalistic and Christian-Cabalist hierarchies. Again, there is no Samekh path. [Isaiah 11:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.11.2?lang=bi)

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. Rabbinic letter lore

### Samekh and final Mem in the tablets

**[TEXT]** Shabbat 104a reports Rav Ḥisda’s saying:

> מ״ם וסמ״ך שבלוחות בנס היו עומדין  
> “The Mem and Samekh in the tablets stood miraculously.”

Because the letters were cut through the tablets, the inner pieces of closed Samekh and final Mem should have fallen out. [Shabbat 104a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104a?lang=en)

This is theological typography, not paleography. The statement depends on the closed square-script forms used by the rabbis.

### Samekh in alphabetic moral instruction

Shabbat 104a also interprets letter sequences homiletically. Samekh and Ayin are read as **סמוך עניים**, “support the poor.” This belongs to rabbinic mnemonic exegesis, not to the later diagrammatic Tree.

### *Otiyyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

**[TRADITION/DISPUTED TEXTUAL HISTORY]** The several recensions of the *Alphabet of Rabbi Akiva* expand letter names and shapes into divine discourses. Samekh is commonly expounded through סמך, support, and through God as the one who supports the lowly or fallen. The work is pseudepigraphic, composite, and medieval in its surviving forms; “Rabbi Akiva taught” is its literary frame, not a demonstrated authorship.

Claims sometimes quoted online such as “do not read Samekh but *somekh makh*, supporter of the lowly” belong to this fluid midrashic family. They should not be cited without identifying a recension.

## 2. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date and authorship

**[TRADITION]** Jewish tradition variously connected *Sefer Yetzirah* with Abraham and, later, Rabbi Akiva.

**[RECONSTRUCTION/DISPUTED]** No scholarly consensus fixes its date:

- Gershom Scholem placed its core broadly in Palestinian late antiquity, approximately the third–sixth centuries.
- Yehuda Liebes argued for earlier, tannaitic possibilities.
- A. Peter Hayman’s critical edition demonstrates a highly unstable manuscript tradition rather than a single recoverable vulgate.
- Tzahi Weiss argues that its formation is best contextualized around the seventh century.
- Other proposals extend into the early Islamic period.

The tenth-century commentaries of Saadia Gaon, Shabbetai Donnolo, Dunash ibn Tamim, and others are the first firm anchors for major textual forms. See Hayman’s critical edition and Weiss’s monograph. [Tzahi Weiss, *Sefer Yeṣirah and Its Contexts*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv16t6jtm)

### The 32 paths

The opening reads:

> בשלשים ושתים נתיבות פליאות חכמה חקק יה  
> “By thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom, Yah engraved…”

**[TEXT]** These are immediately analyzed as ten sefirot belimah and twenty-two foundational letters. The work does not initially mean twenty-two lines on a familiar modern Tree; “paths” is its total creative architecture.

### Ten sefirot *belimah*

**[TEXT]** The text insists:

> עשר ספירות בלימה, עשר ולא תשע, עשר ולא אחת עשרה  
> “Ten sefirot belimah: ten and not nine, ten and not eleven.”

Here *sefirot* are enigmatic measures, enumerations, or dimensions—not yet the fully personified ten emanations of the Zohar and Luria.

### Twenty-two letters

**[TEXT]**

- Three Mothers: א מ ש
- Seven Doubles: ב ג ד כ פ ר ת
- Twelve Simples: ה ו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק

Samekh is the ninth letter in the simple-letter series. [*Sefer Yetzirah*, Gra Version 5](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.5)

### Samekh in the common/Gra-Ari recension

The relevant Hebrew is:

> המליך אות ס׳ בשינה וקשר לו כתר וצרפן זה בזה וצר בו קשת בעולם וכסלו בשנה ומרה בנפש זכר ונקבה

A close translation is:

> “He made the letter Samekh reign over sleep, bound a crown to it, combined them with one another, and formed with it the Bow in the World, Kislev in the Year, and the gall in the Person, male and female.”

**[TEXT]**

| Dimension | Samekh |
|---|---|
| Faculty | שינה, sleep |
| World | קשת, Bow/Sagittarius |
| Year | כסלו, Kislev |
| Person | מרה, gall or gallbladder |

The word **מרה** literally denotes gall/bile and can designate the gallbladder. “Stomach,” found in Westcott and subsequent occult tables, is an imprecise translation or a reflection of another bodily table. [*Sefer Yetzirah* 5:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.5.2?with=Tanakh+ConnectionsList)

### Samekh in Saadia’s recension

Saadia’s recension reads:

> המליך את סמך וקשר לו כתר וצרפן זה עם זה וצר בו קשת בעולם וכסלו בשנה יד ימין בנפש

> “He made Samekh reign, attached a crown to it, combined them with one another, and created with it Sagittarius in the world, Kislev in the year, and the right hand in the person.”

**[TEXT]** Sagittarius and Kislev remain stable, but the bodily locus is **right hand**, not gall. In the Saadia form, the faculty “sleep” is not affixed to Samekh in the same explicit formula. [Saadia recension, Hebrew and French](https://www.biblioj.fr/saadia-gaon-sefer-yetzira-commentaire-lambert-chapitre-6/) and [English translation](https://nirvjam.tripod.com/txts/Saadia.htm).

### Gra, Ari, Short, Long, and Saadia

The labels require care:

- **Short and Long recensions** are manuscript-text families, neither simply “original.”
- **Saadia recension** is the reorganized form embedded in Saadia Gaon’s tenth-century Judeo-Arabic commentary.
- **Gra recension** is the text edited according to Elijah of Vilna’s eighteenth-century understanding.
- **Ari recension** means a text or arrangement transmitted under Lurianic authority; it is not a sixteenth-century critical edition in the modern sense.
- “Gra-Ari” is sometimes used loosely by occultists for a harmonized text. This conceals real differences.

**Finding:** Sagittarius–Kislev–Samekh is unusually stable. Sleep and bodily locus are less stable.

### Is Samekh a planet?

No. **[TEXT]** A simple letter forms a zodiacal constellation/sign. Planets belong to the seven double letters. Jupiter enters the Samekh complex only because later astrology treats Jupiter as ruler of Sagittarius.

### Twelve diagonal boundaries

**[TEXT]** The Gra recension calls the simple letters “twelve diagonal boundaries.” This supplied later interpreters with a reason to place them on diagonal lines of a Tree or on the edges of a “Cube of Space.” But the text itself does not say:

> “Samekh is the line from Tiphareth to Yesod.”

Indeed, a vertical Middle-Pillar line is not naturally a “diagonal boundary,” one reason the Golden Dawn arrangement should not be presented as a literal diagram of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## 3. The separate “Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom”

The sentence called the “Yetziratic text” of Path 25 is **not part of the earliest recoverable *Sefer Yetzirah***.

### Hebrew

Cordovero preserves:

> נתיב כ״ה הוא שכל נסיוני מפני שהוא הנסיון הקדמון אשר בו הבורא ית׳ שמו מנסה כל החסידים

> “The twenty-fifth path is the Experimental/Probative Intelligence, because it is the primordial trial by which the Creator—blessed be His name—tests all the pious.”

[*Pardes Rimmonim*, Gate 12](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.12.2.1)

### Westcott

Westcott rendered it:

> “The Twenty-fifth Path is the Intelligence of Probation, or is Tentative, and is so called because it is the primary temptation, by which the Creator (blessed be He) trieth all righteous persons.”

[*The Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom*, Westcott translation](https://hermetics.net/media-library/kabbalah/william-wynn-westcott-the-thirty-two-paths-of-wisdom/)

### History

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** The list is usually attributed by scholarship to the late-thirteenth-century Castilian “Circle of Contemplation” or a related stratum. It was transmitted alongside *Sefer Yetzirah* and reproduced by Cordovero, but its terminology and literary history are later.

**Crucial distinction:** In Cordovero, “Path 25 = Probative Intelligence” does not entail “Path 25 = Samekh = Tiphareth–Yesod.” That synthesis is Hermetic.

## 4. *Sefer ha-Bahir*

**[TRADITION]** The *Bahir* is attributed within tradition to R. Neḥunya ben ha-Qanah.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** It surfaced in Provence in the second half of the twelfth century, probably incorporating older eastern materials. [Academic discussion of the *Bahir*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/light-through-darkness-the-ideal-of-human-perfection-in-the-zohar/0427DF77AFA145B8346936CCC10F81D0)

The *Bahir* develops:

- the sefirot as divine powers;
- letter-shape exegesis;
- channels, trees, limbs, and cosmic directions;
- interpretations of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

**[ABSENCE]** A search of the Hebrew text found no securely identifiable doctrine in which Samekh is a numbered Tiphareth–Yesod path. Modern claims that *Bahir* §89 makes Samekh’s circular form the Tiphareth–Yesod channel are not supported by the standard text. [Hebrew *Bahir*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir)

## 5. The Zohar

### Letters petition to begin creation

In the Zoharic prologue, the letters approach God in reverse order and ask that creation begin with them.

Samekh says:

> עָאלַת ס אָת אָמְרָה קַמֵּיהּ ... דְּאִית בִּי סְמִיכָא לִנְפָלִין, דִּכְתִיב סוֹמֵךְ ה׳ לְכָל הַנּוֹפְלִים

> “The letter Samekh entered and said before Him … ‘In me is support for the fallen, as is written, “YHWH supports all who fall.”’”

God answers:

> עַל דָּא אַנְתְּ צָרִיךְ לְאַתְרָךְ, וְלָא תָזוּז מִנֵּיהּ  
> “For that very reason you must remain in your place and not move from it.”

When Nun appears, God sends it back because Nun, the sign of falling, must depend on Samekh. [Zohar, Prologue §28–29](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Prologue/chapters/6)

This is Samekh’s most important specifically Zoharic myth. It deepens the Berakhot 4b interpretation but does not install Samekh between Tiferet and Yesod.

### Authorship and date

- **[TRADITION]** The Zohar records the teachings of second-century R. Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle.
- **[RECONSTRUCTION]** Its main body appeared in late-thirteenth-century Castile, with Moses de León a major author, redactor, or disseminator.
- **[DISPUTED]** Scholem’s strong single-author model has been modified by Yehuda Liebes, Ronit Meroz, Daniel Matt, and others, who discern multiple literary strata and possibly a circle of authors.

Daniel Matt’s Pritzker edition establishes a critical Aramaic text from manuscripts rather than simply reproducing the standard Mantua/Cremona print. [Stanford, *Zohar: Pritzker Edition*](https://www.sup.org/books/jewish-studies/zohar)

## 6. Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla’s late-thirteenth-century *Gates of Light* organizes the ten sefirot through divine names. It is important for Samekh’s future Hermetic endpoints but supplies no path-specific Samekh hierarchy.

For **Yesod**, Gikatilla’s names include:

- אל חי, El Ḥai;
- אלהים חיים, Elohim Ḥayyim;
- אל שדי, El Shaddai;
- מקור מים חיים, “Source of Living Waters.”

He calls Yesod the channel drawing life into Adonai/Malkhut. [*Shaʿarei Orah*, Gate Two](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Second_Gate%2C_Ninth_Sefirah.5)

**[ABSENCE]** Gikatilla does not assign Samekh a divine name, archangel, angelic choir, or Tiphareth–Yesod line. Later Golden Dawn tables combine Gikatilla-like divine names with angelological lists from other sources.

## 7. Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed c. 1548)

Moses Cordovero systematized earlier kabbalistic teachings before the spread of Lurianism.

Relevant gates include:

- Gate 7: the channels connecting the sefirot and disputes about their arrangement;
- Gate 12: the thirty-two paths and their names;
- later gates: divine names, angels, letter permutations, colours, and symbolic epithets.

**[TEXT]** Cordovero reproduces the twenty-fifth path as שכל נסיוני, the Probative Intelligence. He also knows a channel between Tiferet and Yesod as part of the sefirotic network.

**[ABSENCE]** These facts do not amount to an explicit statement that the channel Tiferet–Yesod bears the letter Samekh. [*Pardes Rimmonim*, Gate 7](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.7) and [Gate 12](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.12)

### *Or Neʿerav*

Cordovero’s *Or Neʿerav*, composed as an accessible introduction, teaches proper preparation, the relation of Torah to Kabbalah, the sefirot, and the four worlds. It directs advanced readers toward the larger *Pardes*.

**[ABSENCE]** It contains no developed Samekh–Temperance doctrine and no occult pathworking system.

## 8. Luria through Ḥayyim Vital

### Lurianic cosmology

Isaac Luria’s oral teaching was recorded in several recensions, above all those associated with Ḥayyim Vital. *Etz Ḥayyim* describes:

- **Ein Sof**, the limitless divine;
- **tzimtzum**, the primordial withdrawal or concealment;
- **Adam Qadmon**;
- emanating lights and vessels;
- the worlds of Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah;
- **shevirat ha-kelim**, shattering of the vessels;
- the fall of sparks;
- **tikkun**, rectification;
- the reorganization of isolated sefirot into relational **partzufim**.

Vital explicitly explains that concealment of light enables vessels to bear it and that repair reorganizes what was broken. [*Etz Ḥayyim* 9:1–2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.1) and [2:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.2.2.1)

### Samekh in *Etz Ḥayyim*

Vital does use Samekh in letter symbolism. One passage interprets the miraculous Mem and Samekh of the tablets in relation to the completion of a partzuf in Imma: the “six aspects” suggested by ס׳ become a complete tenfold configuration “by way of miracle.”

**[TEXT]** This is Lurianic exegesis of Samekh and the tablets.

**[ABSENCE]** It is not a doctrine of “Path 25, Tiphareth–Yesod, Sagittarius, Temperance.” Neither Temperance nor Tarot belongs to Vital’s system.

### The so-called Ari Tree

Later diagrams reconstructed from Lurianic principles place:

- the three Mothers on horizontals;
- the seven Doubles on verticals;
- the twelve Simples on diagonals.

In the arrangement reproduced by Aryeh Kaplan as “according to the Ari,” **Samekh connects Chokhmah and Tiferet**, not Tiferet and Yesod.

**[RECONSTRUCTION/TRADITION]** This is a later graphic application of Lurianic and *Sefer Yetzirah* principles. It should not be described as a labelled diagram drawn by Luria.

## 9. The Gra’s Tree

Elijah, the Vilna Gaon, produced a recension and spatial interpretation designed to accord more closely with *Sefer Yetzirah*.

In the commonly reproduced Gra arrangement, **Samekh connects Netzach and Yesod**. The Gra tree also relocates Tiferet in relation to the other sefirot; it is not merely the Kircher tree with its letters shuffled.

A useful comparison is:

| System | Samekh joins | Rationale/status |
|---|---|---|
| Ari reconstruction | Chokhmah–Tiferet | Simple letter placed on a diagonal |
| Gra reconstruction | Netzach–Yesod | Simple letter placed in ordered diagonal-boundary scheme |

## 10. Later Jewish Samekh–Yesod associations

The eighteenth-century Ḥayyim Yosef David Azulai, citing R. Moses Zacuto, says:

> סמך הוא היסוד שסומך כל העולמות  
> “Samekh is Yesod, which supports all the worlds.”

**[TRADITION]** This is an explicit Jewish association of Samekh with Yesod through the idea of support. It remains a sefirah-letter symbolism, not a Golden Dawn line between Yesod and Tiferet. [*Devash LeFi*, Samekh](https://www.sefaria.org/Devash_LeFi%2C_Letter_Samekh.13)

## 11. Hasidic readings

### Tanya

**[ABSENCE]** There is no chapter of the *Tanya* devoted to Samekh or “Path 25.”

Chapter 48 discusses **סובב כל עלמין**, *sovev kol almin*, divine light that “surrounds all worlds.” Although *sovev* begins with Samekh and circular Samekh is attractive to later homiletics, the chapter does not derive its doctrine from the letter Samekh. [*Tanya*, chapter 48](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.48)

Chabad letter teachings later emphasize:

- Samekh’s enclosure as divine protection;
- sixty as transcendence of ordinary sixfold extension;
- the Psalm 145 support motif;
- modern acronyms such as סלח–מחל–כפר, “forgive–pardon–atone.”

These are **[TRADITION/MODERN HOMILETIC]**, not passages in *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### Naḥman of Bratslav

R. Naḥman repeatedly treats spiritual sleep, awakening, fall, support, and the raising of consciousness. *Liqquṭei Moharan* I.60 is especially concerned with people “sleeping away their days.”

**[ABSENCE]** The discourse number 60 and theme of sleep are suggestive, but the text does not establish a Samekh–Sagittarius–Kislev path. Treating it as a Samekh pathworking is a modern comparative reading, not R. Naḥman’s stated teaching.

## 12. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and calendar

### Prayer

Samekh is ritually encountered every day in Psalm 145:14, part of **Ashrei**, normally recited three times daily. Its support of fallen Nun is therefore not merely literary but liturgical.

The Amidah’s second blessing praises God as:

> סוֹמֵךְ נוֹפְלִים וְרוֹפֵא חוֹלִים וּמַתִּיר אֲסוּרִים  
> “supporter of the fallen, healer of the sick, and releaser of the bound.”

The root סמך is liturgically central; no standard Jewish liturgy visualizes a Tiphareth–Yesod Samekh path.

### Torah

Samekh appears as an ordinary consonant throughout the Torah. Its first occurrence is generally identified in Genesis 2:11, הַסֹּבֵב, “that which winds around.” Later circular interpretations use this suggestively, but Scripture itself offers no letter doctrine.

### Omer

**[ABSENCE]** Classical counting of the Omer has no Samekh day. Later kabbalistic practice maps the seven weeks and forty-nine days onto combinations of the seven lower sefirot. Samekh is not part of that canonical 7 × 7 system.

### Calendar and tribe

**[TEXT]** Samekh forms Sagittarius and Kislev in *Sefer Yetzirah*.

**[TRADITION]** Benjamin is assigned to Kislev in later tribal-month arrangements, frequently attributed to the Ari. Benjamin’s biblical archery helps reinforce Sagittarius, but the tribe is absent from the *Sefer Yetzirah* clause. [Traditional Kislev exposition](https://inner.org/times/kislev/kislev.htm)

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* contain forty-seven theses “according to the secret teaching of the Hebrew sages” and seventy-two “according to my own opinion.”

A representative Latin thesis says:

> Nullae sunt litterae in tota lege quae in formis, coniunctionibus, separationibus … et ordine, decem numerationum secreta non manifestent.

> “There are no letters in the whole Law which do not manifest, through their forms, conjunctions, separations … and order, the secrets of the ten numerations.”

[Pico, *Conclusiones* I.28.33](https://cds.library.brown.edu/projects/pico/singleThesis.php?expand=sub10528&myID=p1052833)

**[HERMETIC]** Pico made Hebrew letters instruments of Christian theological interpretation, including arguments for Trinity and Christ.

**[ABSENCE]** Pico has no Path 25, no Samekh–Tiphareth–Yesod line, and no Tarot attribution.

## 2. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paulus Ricius’s Latin adaptation of Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*, **Portae lucis: haec est porta Tetragrammaton**, appeared at Augsburg in 1516.

Its title page, attributed to Leonhard Beck, shows a Jewish sage beside ten connected sefirotic medallions. It is probably the first printed sefirotic *ilan*. [British Museum record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1870-1008-1942)

**[ABSENCE]** It is not the Kircher/Golden Dawn Tree, does not show twenty-two lettered paths, and does not identify Samekh with Tiferet–Yesod.

## 3. Johannes Reuchlin, 1517

Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica libri tres* is a Latin dialogue among a Jewish kabbalist, a Muslim, and a Pythagorean. It integrates Hebrew Kabbalah, Pythagoreanism, and Christian doctrine. [1517 scan](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105)

Reuchlin transliterates Hebrew letters and discusses divine names, number, and letter combination. Latin convention generally rendered Samekh as **Samech**.

**[ABSENCE]** No evidence was found that Reuchlin called a Tiphareth–Yesod line *Via Samech*, associated it with Sagittarius, or linked it to Temperance.

## 4. Athanasius Kircher, 1652–54

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I, p. 286, printed the celebrated **Systema Sephiroticum**. [Cornell facsimile and catalogue](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272)

### The diagram

It contains:

- ten equal sefirotic circles;
- three columns;
- twenty-two connecting paths;
- Malkhut linked to Yesod, Hod, and Netzach;
- astrological symbols and Latin/Hebrew labels.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** The configuration was influenced by earlier Jewish and Christian-Cabalist diagrams, especially a 1625 design associated with Philippe d’Aquin. Calling it “Kircher’s invention” is therefore too strong; Kircher’s publication made it widely accessible.

### Samekh

On the later alphabetic reading of this geometry, the fifteenth letter becomes the twenty-fifth path and the central Tiferet–Yesod line.

**Caution:** “Kircher’s path 25” is convenient shorthand. The complete Golden Dawn package—number 25, Samekh, Sagittarius, Temperance, four colour scales—was not printed by Kircher.

Kircher’s own attitude was explicitly Christian and polemical. He distinguished what he considered an ancient Mosaic Cabala from what he condemned as later rabbinic corruption. His appropriative framework is therefore visible in the primary text itself. [Kircher, *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–84

*Kabbala Denudata* translated and excerpted Zoharic, Cordoverian, Gikatillan, and Lurianic material into Latin. Its vocabulary includes:

- *Sephiroth*;
- *viae* or paths;
- divine names;
- partzufic configurations;
- qlippothic material.

Knorr helped make Lurianic Kabbalah accessible to European Christian readers and ultimately to Mathers.

**[ABSENCE]** The work does not yet contain the Golden Dawn Samekh–Temperance system. Nor was a distinctive Latin title equivalent to “Path of Samekh” found. The medieval Path 25 may be represented through Latin ideas such as *intelligentia probationis/tentationis*, but the secure primary Hebrew witness is Cordovero’s שכל נסיוני.

## 6. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–56

Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* decisively connected the twenty-two Tarot trumps and twenty-two Hebrew letters.

His arrangement was not the later Golden Dawn sequence. Under the straightforward numbered alignment:

- Aleph corresponded to Trump I;
- Samekh, the fifteenth letter, corresponded to Trump XV, **the Devil**;
- the unnumbered Fool was displaced toward the end.

**[HERMETIC]** Samekh–Devil is therefore the Lévi attribution, not Samekh–Temperance. [Gareth Knight’s historical table reproducing Lévi’s sequence](https://maestrosdelsaber.com/wp-content/uploads/ftp-files/Kabbalah/Kab%20en%20General/Kab%20Knight%2C%20Gareth%20-%20A%20Practical%20Guide%20to%20Qabalistic%20Symbolism%20Vol%202.pdf)

Lévi’s great innovation was the assertion of a hidden Tarot-Hebrew equivalence. No medieval Tarot evidence demonstrates that the trumps were designed as Hebrew letters; historical Tarot arose as an Italian card game.

## 7. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### Construction of Path 25

The Golden Dawn placed the Fool at zero/Aleph, then advanced the numbered trumps:

- Aleph = Fool 0;
- Beth = Magician I;
- …
- Nun = Death XIII;
- Samekh = Temperance XIV;
- Ayin = Devil XV.

It then combined this with *Sefer Yetzirah*’s letter astrology:

- Samekh = Sagittarius;
- therefore Temperance = Sagittarius;
- and on the adopted tree, Samekh = Tiphareth–Yesod.

This is more systematic than Lévi but historically later.

### Knowledge Lectures

The Golden Dawn described the connecting paths as twenty-two Hebrew letters completing the thirty-two Paths of Wisdom. Samekh became the direct route through the lower Middle Pillar.

A frequently transmitted instruction says:

> “Therefore, by the straight and narrow path of Samekh, let the Philosophus advance like the arrow from the Bow of Qesheth.”

[Golden Dawn manuscript collection](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/golden_dawn_manuscripts.html)

This combines:

- Samekh’s vertical line;
- Sagittarius/Qesheth;
- the aspirant’s one-pointed advance toward Tiphareth;
- the probationary Yetziratic title.

### Book T

*Book T* calls Temperance:

> “The Daughter of the Reconcilers; the Bringer-Forth of Life.”

Its general meaning is:

> “Combination of forces, realization, action based on accurate calculation; the way of escape.”

The card represents reconciliation rather than merely mildness. Water poured between vessels becomes an emblem of rhythmic circulation; the centaur unites animal and human natures; mutable Fire can be directed by the archer’s aim. [Golden Dawn *Book T* scan](https://jantetteroo.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Mathers_and_felkin_golden_dawn_book_t_th.pdf)

### Four colour scales

| World/scale | Golden Dawn name | Samekh colour |
|---|---|---|
| Atziluth | King Scale | Blue |
| Briah | Queen Scale | Yellow |
| Yetzirah | Prince/Emperor Scale | Green |
| Assiah | Princess/Empress Scale | Dark vivid blue |

These are ritual colour correspondences, not Jewish descriptions of the four worlds.

### Initiatory position

The path lies between:

- Yesod, assigned to Theoricus 2=9;
- Tiphareth, assigned to Adeptus Minor 5=6.

In Golden Dawn symbolism, Samekh is the principal central ascent through the Veil of Paroketh toward Tiphareth. The Philosophus 4=7 is exhorted to advance through it, but the path should not be reduced to a single formal grade.

## 8. Crowley

### *Liber 777* (1909)

Crowley published and expanded Golden Dawn correspondence tables. For line 25 he gives Samekh, “Prop,” Sagittarius, the Tiphareth–Yesod connection, Probative Intelligence, Temperance, and the four colours. [1909 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1909__crowley___777.pdf)

### *Liber CCXXXI* (published 1912)

Crowley’s Samekh verse reads:

> “And a mighty angel appeared as a woman, pouring vials of woe upon the flames, lighting the pure stream with her brand of cursing. And the iniquity was very great.”

This belongs to *Liber Arcanorum*, Crowley’s brief visionary verses and sigils for the twenty-two scales of the serpent and qliphoth. It later became Grant’s textual warrant for Saksaksalim. The work appeared in *The Equinox* I:7, March 1912.

### *Liber Samekh*

*Liber Samekh*—Liber DCCC—is a ritual for the Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy Guardian Angel, developed from the “Bornless” invocation transmitted through Golden Dawn practice and ultimately related to a Greek magical papyrus.

**[HERMETIC]** The title resonates with Samekh’s route from lunar Yesod to solar Tiphareth and with the attainment associated with Tiphareth.

**[ABSENCE]** It is not a prose commentary on the twenty-fifth path. Its title also participates in Crowley’s gematria: Samekh spelled in full equals 120, while DCCC is its formal class number. [*Magick in Theory and Practice*, Appendix IV](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1929__crowley___magick_in_theory_and_practice.pdf)

### Temperance becomes Art

Crowley renamed XIV **Art**. The card, painted by Frieda Harris, is the fulfilment of Atu VI, the Lovers:

- Lovers separates or stages the opposites;
- Art combines them through alchemical *coniunctio*;
- white and red, fire and water, lion and eagle, male and female are exchanged and recombined;
- the figure is androgynous rather than merely a conventional temperance angel.

Crowley explains Sagittarius through the Moon–huntress–bow complex:

> “Sagittarius bears the arrow which pierces the rainbow, for his path leads from the Moon of Yesod to the Sun of Tiphareth.”

[Crowley, *Book of Thoth*, “Art”](https://qbl.thelemistas.org/en/Tarot/14-art)

The “last three paths spell Qesheth” doctrine is a Hermetic wordplay on קשת, bow/rainbow, not a teaching in *Sefer Yetzirah*.

### The Tzaddi/Heh question

Liber AL I:57 says:

> “All these old letters of my Book are aright; but צ is not the Star.”

Crowley eventually exchanged the Star and Emperor’s letter positions, producing the well-known Heh/Tzaddi controversy.

**Finding:** The correction does not change Samekh. XIV remains Samekh–Sagittarius–Art. The controversy matters because it shows that Crowley regarded the Golden Dawn correspondences as revisable through revelation rather than historically fixed.

## 9. Dion Fortune, 1935

*The Mystical Qabalah* is organized primarily by the ten sefirot, not by twenty-two full path chapters. There is therefore no “chapter on the Sephira Samekh”: Samekh is not a sefirah.

Fortune’s relevant teachings are distributed among discussions of:

- the thirty-two paths;
- choice of a path;
- Yesod and Tiphareth;
- the Middle Pillar;
- Tarot and astrology as aspects of the Tree;
- astral symbolism and the hazards of taking inner images literally.

She regarded the path system as a practical map of relations between macrocosm and microcosm and warned that visions are symbolic intermediaries rather than literal personal messages from God. [Complete text](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html)

A complete chapter reproduction is neither item-applicable nor necessary; the work’s Samekh material is summarized here rather than reproduced.

## 10. Paul Foster Case

Case retained Samekh–Temperance–Sagittarius–Tiphareth/Yesod but renamed its consciousness **Verification** or “Intelligence of Probation or Trial.”

His *Book of Tokens* presents each Hebrew letter as a first-person meditation. Samekh begins by identifying itself as the support upon which created existence depends. In his 1947 *Tarot*, the rainbow, pool, and path between the solar and lunar spheres are made explicit. [Case, *The Tarot*, pp. 149 ff.](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1947__case___the_tarot.pdf)

B.O.T.A. additionally standardized:

- colour: blue;
- tone: G-sharp/A-flat;
- function: testing imagination through experience;
- Tarot keyword: Verification.

These are twentieth-century esoteric teaching devices.

## 11. Israel Regardie

Regardie’s publications between 1937 and 1940 made Golden Dawn papers widely available. His importance is transmissive:

- preservation of Knowledge Lectures;
- dissemination of Book T;
- detailed ritual grades;
- colour scales;
- path and Tarot tables.

The standard modern “Path of Samekh” is largely the Regardie-published Golden Dawn path. [*Complete Golden Dawn System of Magic*](https://www.invisiblehouse.org/ref/Complete_Golden_Dawn_System_of_Magic.pdf)

## 12. Gareth Knight

Knight’s *A Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism* (first published 1965) treats the twenty-two paths as psychological and spiritual relationships. Samekh is the progressive quieting and organization of the astral personality so that it can respond to the Tiphareth centre.

Knight also clearly records the historical difference between Lévi’s Samekh–Devil arrangement and the Golden Dawn’s Samekh–Temperance. His approach is Christian-Hermetic and depth-psychological, not Jewish textual commentary.

## 13. Pathworking

### Golden Dawn and descendants

A conventional Samekh pathworking may include:

1. Establishment in the purple/lunar sphere of Yesod.
2. Visualization of blue Samekh or a blue path rising centrally.
3. Sagittarius, bow, arrow, rainbow, horse, or centaur imagery.
4. Temperance/Art as gate image.
5. Testing of images through divine names, signs, and symbolic consistency.
6. Emergence into the gold/solar field of Tiphareth.
7. Return and written record.

This is **[HERMETIC PRACTICE]**, not Jewish liturgy.

### Crowleyan reading

Samekh is the directed arrow of aspiration and the ordeal of bringing the lunar/astral personality into alignment with the solar True Will or Holy Guardian Angel. The process is not simple repression: Art recombines conflicting forces until a new integrated substance is produced.

### Practitioner testimony

Modern practitioners report:

- heightened dream activity;
- tests of certainty and self-image;
- “chapel perilous” experiences in which symbolic and literal interpretations become difficult to separate;
- oscillation between aspiration and inflation.

Such reports are experiential literature, not historical verification. An example is [“Limping towards heaven: Walking pathway 25, Samekh”](https://www.spiralnature.com/magick/limping-towards-heaven-walking-pathway-25-samekh/).

# The Qliphah or tunnel

## Three systems that must not be confused

### 1. Jewish qelippot

קְלִפּוֹת, *qelippot*, means peels, husks, or shells.

In Zoharic and Lurianic Kabbalah they are forces of concealment, impurity, separation, or misappropriated vitality. In Luria, broken vessels and fallen sparks provide the cosmological setting for their power.

The classical scheme includes:

- three wholly impure shells, drawn from Ezekiel’s storm-wind, great cloud, and flashing fire;
- *qelippat nogah*, a mixed or translucent shell capable of elevation;
- the *sitra aḥra*, “Other Side.”

It does not provide Kenneth Grant’s tunnels.

### 2. Golden Dawn/Crowley qliphotic orders

The Golden Dawn and *777* arrange ten orders opposite the ten sefirot and twelve further zodiacal orders. For Samekh/Sagittarius, *777* gives **Necheshiron**.

Necheshiron is the qliphotic order of the zodiacal row. It is **not** Saksaksalim and not the qliphah of Yesod or Tiphareth.

### 3. Kenneth Grant’s tunnel

In *Nightside of Eden* (1977), Grant calls **Saksaksalim** the sentinel of the twenty-fifth tunnel. It connects:

- **Thagirion**, the qliphotic counterpart of Tiphareth;
- **Gamaliel**, the qliphotic counterpart of Yesod.

Grant assigns or discusses:

- number 300, linking the entity with Shin and “false fire”;
- a high-pitched electrical crackling sound;
- the musical key G-sharp;
- a bright-yellow sigil upon dark vivid blue;
- distorted lunar, sexual, serpentine, and alchemical symbolism;
- the *Liber 231* image of a woman pouring vials of woe;
- transmutation and the Vision of the Universal Peacock;
- Necheshiron as the corresponding qliphotic host.

See *Nightside of Eden*, pp. 226–28, in the [digitized scan](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf).

**[MODERN]** Grant’s tunnels are a Typhonian reinterpretation of Crowley’s sigils and path structure, influenced by Austin Osman Spare, sexual magic, fictional mythology, and Grant’s claimed magical experience. They are not recoverable from the Zohar, Cordovero, or Vital.

## Major diagrams and visual witnesses

1. **Late-thirteenth- and fourteenth-century manuscripts:** early sefirotic diagrams and “Trees of Wisdom,” diverse and not standardized.
2. **1516, *Portae Lucis*:** probably the first printed sefirotic *ilan*; ten medallions, not the Golden Dawn path map.
3. **1591, printed *Pardes Rimmonim*:** editorial and Cordoverian diagrams helped circulate channel arrangements.
4. **1652–54, Kircher, p. 286:** the principal ancestor of the standard Western Tree.
5. **Golden Dawn coloured Trees:** projected the four colour scales across Atziluth, Briah, Yetzirah, and Assiah.
6. **Waite–Smith Temperance, 1909:** angel, two cups, one foot on land and one in water, path toward a crown.
7. **Crowley–Harris Art, painted 1938–43:** alchemical fusion, arrow, rainbow, lion, eagle, androgynous operator.
8. **Ithell Colquhoun’s Tarot:** combined Golden Dawn colour theory with surrealist morphology. [Tate record](https://shop.tate.org.uk/ithell-colquhoun-15.-the-daughter-of-the-reconcilers-the-bringer-forth-of-life-from-taro-major-arcana/ithcol2515.html)

## Poetry and fiction

- W. B. Yeats knew Golden Dawn grades and Tree symbolism, but no securely documented work can be reduced to a Samekh path allegory.
- Dion Fortune’s occult novels dramatize initiation, astral glamour, polarity, and solar integration more directly than *The Mystical Qabalah*’s brief path material.
- Ron Weighell’s modern tale “The Tunnel of Saksaksalim” is a literary afterlife of Grant’s tunnel, not an independent kabbalistic tradition.
- Modern Tarot poetry and visionary diaries frequently identify Samekh with ordeal, dream, alchemy, and the Holy Guardian Angel; these are reception history rather than ancient witnesses.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. When was *Sefer Yetzirah* written?

There is no secure authorial date.

- Abraham or Rabbi Akiva: **traditional attribution**.
- First century: defended by some scholars, notably Yehuda Liebes.
- Third–sixth centuries: Scholem’s influential range.
- Sixth–seventh centuries: several linguistic/contextual proposals.
- Around the seventh century: Weiss.
- Early Islamic period: minority contextual theories.

Hayman’s critical work shows that asking for “the original Samekh attribution” may exceed what the manuscript evidence permits. Sagittarius–Kislev is stable; sleep and bodily organ are not.

## 2. Is the “Intelligence of Probation” in *Sefer Yetzirah*?

Not in its early recoverable text.

The thirty-two numbered Intelligence descriptions are a separate medieval work. Westcott printed them with his *Sefer Yetzirah*, encouraging later occultists to call them “the Yetziratic Text.” Cordovero’s preservation proves Jewish transmission, but not late-antique authorship.

## 3. Who wrote the Zohar?

- R. Shimon bar Yoḥai: traditional sacred authorship.
- Moses de León: central figure in historical reconstruction.
- A Castilian circle/multiple authors: increasingly influential scholarly model.
- Earlier sources: possible and, for some passages, probable; this does not establish second-century authorship for the whole corpus.

## 4. Did Kircher invent the occult Tree?

Not outright.

Kircher printed an exceptionally influential version, but it drew upon earlier Jewish and Christian-Cabalist diagrams, including Philippe d’Aquin. “Kircher Tree” properly names a publication lineage and geometry, not a creation ex nihilo.

## 5. Which path does Samekh occupy?

There is no single transhistorical answer.

| Framework | Samekh |
|---|---|
| *Sefer Yetzirah* | A simple letter/Sagittarius/Kislev; not a sefirah-to-sefirah line |
| Ari reconstruction | Chokhmah–Tiferet |
| Gra reconstruction | Netzach–Yesod |
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Tiferet–Yesod |
| Grant’s Nightside | Thagirion–Gamaliel |

Calling one of these “the authentic Kabbalistic path” without specifying the system is misleading.

## 6. Is Temperance intrinsically Samekh?

No historically demonstrated connection exists before occult Tarot.

Temperance was a traditional cardinal-virtue image: mixing water with wine. Lévi first joined the Tarot sequence to Hebrew letters but made Samekh the Devil. The Golden Dawn’s zero-Fool ordering shifted Samekh to Temperance and then used Sagittarius to reinterpret the card.

## 7. Tzaddi is not the Star

Crowley’s swap concerns Heh, Tzaddi, Emperor, and Star. Samekh remains Art/Sagittarius. Claims that Crowley moved Samekh are erroneous.

## 8. Is Daʿat an eleventh sefirah?

*Sefer Yetzirah* explicitly says ten, not eleven.

In later Kabbalah, Daʿat may be:

- the synthesis of Chokhmah and Binah;
- an internal or emergent state;
- counted when Keter is not;
- a functional locus rather than an independent eleventh emanation.

Western occult diagrams often show it as a dotted “invisible sefirah” at the Abyss. That is a useful Hermetic convention, not license simply to count eleven.

## 9. Christian Cabala: transmission or appropriation?

It was both.

Pico, Reuchlin, Ricius, Kircher, and Knorr preserved, translated, and circulated Jewish materials. They also:

- detached them from Jewish practice and interpretive authority;
- treated them as prisca theologia;
- used them to prove Christian doctrines;
- sometimes portrayed post-biblical Jews as corrupt custodians of their own tradition.

A historically responsible account must acknowledge the textual transmission without neutralizing the conversionary and supersessionist framework.

## 10. Are the qliphotic princes ancient?

No continuous ancient list supports the modern table.

Jewish qelippot are real medieval and Lurianic doctrines. The neat inverse Tree, one order and demon prince per sphere, twenty-two labelled tunnels, Tarot trumps, colours, and magical sigils accumulated in Western occultism. Grant’s Saksaksalim is a twentieth-century development.

# Open questions

1. **Proto-Sinaitic identity:** Which proposed Bronze Age sign, if any, is the direct ancestor of Phoenician Samekh?
2. **Letter-name meaning:** Did *samek* originally mean “support,” or did the root acquire symbolic priority after the older pictorial referent was forgotten?
3. **Recensional archetype:** Was Samekh originally linked to sleep, or did faculty and bodily tables migrate during transmission?
4. **Gall versus stomach:** Which “stomach” renderings reflect genuine manuscript readings and which derive from loose translation of digestive organs?
5. **The twenty-fifth path-list:** Can its precise manuscript history within the Circle of Contemplation be narrowed further?
6. **Cordovero’s diagrams:** Which lines and labels belong to Cordovero’s own autograph tradition and which to early printers/editors?
7. **Ari Tree:** How much of the commonly reproduced lettered “Ari Tree” is Lurianic teaching and how much is later diagrammatic reconstruction?
8. **Kircher’s immediate sources:** Precisely how did Philippe d’Aquin, Jewish *ilanot*, and Christian-Cabalist printers contribute to Kircher’s plate?
9. **Golden Dawn formation:** Which Samekh correspondences were already in the Cipher Manuscripts, and which were developed by Westcott, Mathers, Bennett, or later adepts?
10. **Necheshiron etymology:** The printed Hebrew suggests a serpent-root, but several occult transliterations are corrupt and require manuscript comparison.
11. **Saksaksalim:** Did Crowley intend the sigil-name to be decipherable Hebrew, or is Grant’s reading primarily visionary and phonetic?
12. **Practice history:** Early Golden Dawn pathworking records remain scattered; published retrospective accounts cannot always be distinguished from original temple teaching.

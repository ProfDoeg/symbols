# The Path of Peh: Research Dossier

*Research cutoff: 14 September 2026.*

## Evidentiary key

- **[DT] Documented text:** stated in, or directly recoverable from, a cited primary text, manuscript, edition, or historical artifact.
- **[SR] Scholarly reconstruction:** a conclusion of modern historical, philological, paleographic, or religious-studies scholarship.
- **[KT] Kabbalistic tradition:** a received Jewish attribution or theological interpretation, whether or not historically demonstrable.
- **[HO] Hermetic-occult attribution:** part of Christian Cabala, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, BOTA, or a later Western esoteric system.
- **[D] Disputed:** witnesses, recensions, schools, or scholars disagree.
- **[MI] Modern invention or synthesis:** first securely visible in modern occult writing, recent pathworking, or modern popular literature.

The most important conclusion is that several historically separate things are routinely presented as one:

1. **Peh as the seventeenth Hebrew letter** is ancient.
2. **Peh as one of seven “double letters”** belongs to *Sefer Yetzirah*.
3. **“The twenty-seventh Intelligence”** belongs to a later list called the *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom*, not securely to the body of *Sefer Yetzirah*.
4. **Peh as the connector Netzach–Hod** belongs to the Kircher/Western tree.
5. **Peh = Mars = the Tower** is the Golden Dawn synthesis.
6. **Parfaxitas as Peh’s qliphothic tunnel** begins with Crowley’s *Liber 231* names and was elaborated by Kenneth Grant in 1977.

They can be studied side by side, but they are not one continuous ancient doctrine.

### First correction: Peh is not a sefirah

Peh is a Hebrew letter and, in the Western system, a connecting path. It therefore has no native sephiral divine name, archangel, angelic choir, virtue, vice, or mundane chakra. Entries of that sort in modern tables are normally borrowed from **Mars/Geburah**, from the endpoint sefirot, or from Crowley’s planetary columns.

### Second correction: the Jewish sources do not contain “the Path of Peh”

**[Negative finding]** Neither *Sefer Yetzirah*, the *Bahir*, the *Zohar*, Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim*, Vital’s *Etz Chaim*, nor the *Tanya* presents a unit called “the Path of Peh” joining Netzach and Hod and bearing the Tower. That entity is a historically layered Western occult construction.

# The name

## Hebrew spelling, pronunciation, and etymology

The letter is written **פ**, with the word-final form **ף**. Its usual modern name is written **פֵּא** or **פֵּה**. The ordinary noun **פֶּה** means “mouth.” In construct and suffixed forms it frequently appears as **פִּי**, *pi*, as in *pi ha-aretz*, “the mouth of the earth.”

The principal transliterations are:

- *Pe* or *Pē*: normal scholarly or Israeli pronunciation.
- *Peh*: common English representation distinguishing it from the English word *pea*.
- *Pé*: French and some Continental occult writing.
- *Phe*: older Christian-Cabalist and English occult spelling, emphasizing the soft /f/ form.
- Crowley sometimes prints **Pé** and transliterates the full spelling as **PH** or **P-H**, according to the table and edition.

**[DT]** The letter has two later phonetic realizations:

- **פּ**, with dagesh: /p/
- **פ** or final **ף**, without dagesh: /f/

The /p/–/f/ alternation belongs to the *begadkefat* spirantization system. *Sefer Yetzirah* already calls the seven letters “double” and describes hard/soft or strong/weak pronunciations, but the surviving recensions do not preserve a wholly uniform phonological description. The [Jewish Encyclopedia’s “Pe” article](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/11963-pe) gives the basic letter, pronunciation, and numerical history.

## Paleographic history

### Proto-Sinaitic and Proto-Canaanite

**[SR]/[D]** The ultimate ancestor of Peh is conventionally reconstructed as a Proto-Sinaitic or Proto-Canaanite sign representing a **mouth**, used acrophonically for /p/. That reconstruction is plausible because later Northwest Semitic traditions call the letter *pē*, “mouth,” but individual early inscriptions can be difficult to date or identify, and popular charts often suggest more certainty than the epigraphy warrants.

The broad historical sequence is:

> Proto-Sinaitic/Proto-Canaanite mouth-sign → Phoenician **𐤐** → Paleo-Hebrew → Imperial Aramaic → Jewish square **פ/ף**

The Greek **Π**, *pi*, ultimately descends from the Phoenician letter, though the graphic form was reorganized in Greek.

### Paleo-Hebrew and square Hebrew

**[DT]/[SR]** Biblical Hebrew was originally written in a local Canaanite or “Paleo-Hebrew” script. During and after the Babylonian and Persian periods, Jewish scribal culture increasingly used an Aramaic-derived script. The recognizable “square” Jewish hand developed gradually; it should not be projected unchanged into the Bronze Age. A useful general survey is the [Jewish Virtual Library’s history of Hebrew writing](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/history-of-the-hebrew-alphabet).

The final form **ף** belongs to the system of five final letters, מנצפ״ך. Rabbinic discussion remembers uncertainty over whether the medial or final forms were original.

### Shape symbolism

Modern Jewish and occult commentaries frequently describe Peh as:

- an enclosing mouth;
- a Bet concealed within Peh;
- a mouth containing a tooth or Yod;
- the spoken word containing an inward “house.”

These are **[KT] homiletical** or **[MI] graphic meditations**, not paleographic etymologies. They interpret the square form retrospectively and do not describe the sign’s Bronze Age development.

## Numerical values and spellings

- **פ = 80** in ordinary gematria.
- **ף = 800** only in systems assigning 500–900 to final Kaph, Mem, Nun, Peh, and Tzaddi.
- **פה = 85**.
- **פא = 81**.

Crowley’s table sometimes prints **378** beside the Peh row. That is not Peh’s gematria: it is the cumulative sum of the “key scale” through 27, \(1+\cdots+27=378\). Modern websites have occasionally misidentified it as a secret value of Peh.

## Biblical language

Peh is a common biblical noun rather than a technical Tree-of-Life title. Important uses include:

- Genesis 4:11: “the earth, which opened its **mouth**.”
- Exodus 4:10–16: Moses says he is “heavy of **mouth** and heavy of tongue”; God identifies himself as maker of the human mouth.
- Numbers 12:8: God speaks with Moses **פֶּה אֶל־פֶּה**, “mouth to mouth.”
- Deuteronomy 8:3: humanity lives by everything proceeding from the **mouth of YHWH**.
- Isaiah 6:5–7: Isaiah’s “unclean lips” are purified by a coal.
- Psalm 145:16: **פּוֹתֵחַ אֶת־יָדֶךָ**, “You open Your hand,” the Peh verse of *Ashrei*.
- Proverbs 18:21: death and life are “in the power of the tongue.”
- Lamentations 2–4: Peh and Ayin appear in an anomalous order in several alphabetic acrostics.

These support Jewish teachings about speech, revelation, praise, sin, and prayer. They do **not** attest Mars, the Tower, or the Netzach–Hod path.

## The verses behind the names of the sefirot

The requested verses belong to the history of **sephirah nomenclature**, not to the name Peh.

- **1 Chronicles 29:11** gives *ha-gedullah*, *ha-gevurah*, *ha-tiferet*, *ha-netzaḥ*, *ha-hod*, *kol*, and *mamlakhah*. Kabbalists relate these to the seven lower sefirot: Chesed/Gedulah, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, and Malkhut.
- **Proverbs 3:19** names Wisdom and Understanding in creation: YHWH founded the earth by **ḥokhmah** and established the heavens by **tevunah**.
- **Job 28** meditates on the concealed “way” or “path” to Wisdom.
- **Isaiah 11:2** names wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear of YHWH; it is important to later discussions of *daʿat*.
- Other frequently invoked texts include Proverbs 2–9 and Exodus 31:3.

**[Negative finding]** No examined Jewish source derives “Peh,” “path 27,” or a Mars–Tower complex from these verses.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date, authorship, and textual history

**[KT]** Later tradition attributes *Sefer Yetzirah* to Abraham, partly because its concluding scene describes Abraham contemplating, engraving, combining, and succeeding with the letters.

**[SR]/[D]** Historical dating remains unsettled. Proposals range from the early centuries CE to the early Islamic period. Gershom Scholem favored late antiquity; Tzahi Weiss argues for an early medieval, probably seventh-century cultural setting; A. Peter Hayman’s critical work emphasizes that the text becomes historically visible in its developed form around the beginning of the tenth century. The earliest secure extensive commentary is Saadia Gaon’s Arabic commentary of 931.

Hayman’s *Sefer Yeṣira: Edition, Translation and Text-Critical Commentary* compares more than seventy manuscripts and distinguishes principal short, long, and Saadian textual families. The [publisher’s description](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161587955/) explicitly notes its first clear emergence in the early tenth century.

### The thirty-two paths

*Sefer Yetzirah* opens:

> **בשלשים ושתים נתיבות פליאות חכמה חקק יה...**

“With thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom Yah engraved…”

It then divides the thirty-two into:

- ten **sefirot belimah**;
- twenty-two foundation letters;
- the letters as three mothers, seven doubles, and twelve simples.

**[DT]** In the body of the work, the “thirty-two paths” therefore mean the ten sefirot plus twenty-two letters or modes of formation. The text does not number Peh “27,” draw it between Netzach and Hod, or assign it a tarot image.

### The ten *sefirot belimah*

The phrase **עשר ספירות בלימה** is obscure. Proposed translations include “ten sefirot of nothingness,” “ineffable numbers,” and “ten countings without substance.”

The work insists:

> **עשר ולא תשע, עשר ולא אחת עשרה**

“Ten and not nine; ten and not eleven.”

That statement later became important in disputes over whether Daʿat is an eleventh sefirah. It does not prevent later diagrams from representing Daʿat as a relational or quasi-sephiral point, but it strongly opposes a simple count of eleven independent sefirot.

### Peh as a double letter

The Gra text reads:

> **שבע כפולות בג״ד כפר״ת... תבנית רך וקשה, גבור וחלש**

“Seven doubles, BGD KPRT… a pattern of soft and hard, strong and weak.”

Peh is thus securely one of the seven doubles. Its two pronunciations became פּ /p/ and פ/ף /f/.

The seven doubles are correlated with:

- seven contrasting qualities;
- seven planets;
- seven days;
- seven bodily “gates,” consisting of two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, and the mouth.

But the assignment of an individual letter to an individual planet, day, quality, and organ varies sharply.

### Recension audit: what is actually assigned to Peh?

| Long/Mantua-type form | Dominance | Slavery/subjection | **Venus** | Wednesday | Left nostril | [DT]/[D] |
| Common short form | Grace | Ugliness | **Venus** | fifth day/Thursday in some enumerations | Right nostril | [DT]/[D] |

The Saadia text expressly states that Peh forms Venus, Wednesday, and the left nostril; see the [Saadia translation, chapter 8](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/judaism/saadia.pdf). The Gra text published in Aryeh Kaplan’s translation states that Peh forms **Mercury**, Thursday, and the left ear; see [Gra 4:12, PDF pp. 13–14](https://www.odysseetheater.org/ftp/bibliothek/Mystik/Kabbala/Sefer_Yetzirah.pdf).

This yields a firm finding:

> **[D] “Sefer Yetzirah assigns Mars to Peh” is false if stated without qualification.**

Mars is assigned to another double in the recoverable Saadia, long, short, and Gra arrangements. Peh–Mars is the Golden Dawn ordering:

> Beth–Mercury, Gimel–Moon, Daleth–Venus, Kaph–Jupiter, Peh–Mars, Resh–Sun, Tav–Saturn.

That arrangement is elegant in the Golden Dawn’s astrological system, but it is not the unanimous—or even usual—planet sequence in Jewish manuscripts.

### “Saadia, Ari, and Gra recensions”

“Saadia recension” is historically meaningful because Saadia’s commentary preserves a particular rearranged form.

“Ari recension” is less secure. Luria wrote no surviving autograph edition of *Sefer Yetzirah*. Later publishers and occultists use “Ari,” “Gra,” and “Ari-Gra” inconsistently for corrected or harmonized texts transmitted through later kabbalistic schools. **[D]** The label should not be treated as proof that a printed planet table was personally established by Isaac Luria.

Kaplan’s “Gra version” is a usable named edition, but it remains a late recension, not a window into a single pristine ancient text.

### No month or tribe for Peh

Months, zodiacal signs, tribes, and twelve bodily directors belong to the twelve simple letters. Because Peh is a double:

- it has no *Sefer Yetzirah* month;
- no zodiacal sign;
- no tribe;
- no Goetic zodiacal quinances.

Assigning Aries or Scorpio to Peh because Mars rules them is a **secondary astrological extension**, not Peh’s letter-class attribution.

## 2. The later *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom*

A medieval list, often called **נתיבות פליאות חכמה**, assigns a named “Intelligence” to each path. Westcott printed it as “The Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom” after his translation of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

**[SR]** It is textually separate from the main *Sefer Yetzirah*. Even Westcott calls it a “supplement” in his notes. Modern occult books frequently erase that distinction.

### Hebrew of the twenty-seventh Intelligence

A recurrent Hebrew form is:

> **הנתיב הכ״ז נקרא שכל מורגש... ממנו נברא רוח כל נברא... ותנועת כולם**

“The twenty-seventh path is called *sekhel murgash* … from it are created the spirit of every creature … and the motion of them all.”

The title **שכל מורגש**, *sekhel murgash*, more literally means “sensed,” “palpable,” or “perceptible intelligence.” “Active” and “Exciting” are interpretive renderings.

### Westcott’s textual problem

Westcott’s 1893 edition, p. 31, prints:

> “The Twenty-seventh Path is the Exciting Intelligence…”

but continues with the description about the perfection of beings beneath the sun. That wording normally belongs to the **Natural Intelligence**, path 28 in other witnesses. Westcott then omits a separately numbered twenty-eighth path and proceeds to 29. See the [1893 scan, PDF pp. 29–31](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1893__westcott___sepher_yetzirah.pdf).

Later Golden Dawn, Case, and Cicero formulations use the alternative reading:

> “through it every existent being receives its spirit and motion.”

Accordingly:

- **[DT]** Westcott calls 27 “Exciting.”
- **[D]** His definition appears displaced from the Natural Intelligence.
- **[HO]** The Golden Dawn’s preferred “spirit and motion” text reflects another recension or editorial correction.
- **[Negative finding]** Neither form originally identifies the twenty-seventh Intelligence with Peh, Mars, a tower, Netzach, or Hod. Those correlations require the later Western numbered tree.

## 3. Rabbinic letter lore

### Shabbat 104a: open and closed Peh

The Babylonian Talmud interprets the ordinary and final forms homiletically:

- bent/open Peh;
- straight/closed Peh;
- an “open mouth” and a “closed mouth.”

The surrounding discussion associates alphabetic forms with moral instruction and asks whether the final letters were established by prophets. See [Shabbat 104a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104a?lang=bi).

This is **[DT] rabbinic alphabet lore**, but not a planet or Tree-path attribution.

### Peh before Ayin in Lamentations

Sanhedrin 104b explains the anomalous order Peh–Ayin in portions of Lamentations through the spies:

> they placed their mouths before their eyes, saying what they had not seen.

This produces an enduring ethical contrast between **peh**, mouth, and **ayin**, eye. It is independent of the later adjacent occult paths Peh and Ayin.

### Torah sections

The letter **פ** in a Torah manuscript or printed Bible marks a **petuḥah**, an “open” paragraph. **ס** marks a *setumah*, “closed” paragraph. The sign is scribal-masoretic, not an abbreviation for the occult path.

### Torah “by mouth”

**תורה שבעל פה**, *Torah she-beʿal peh*, is the Oral Torah. Peh therefore participates naturally in Jewish discussions of living transmission, teaching, pronunciation, and the covenant of the tongue. Again, that semantic field does not establish Mars or the Tower.

## 4. *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

The pseudepigraphic *Alphabet of Rabbi Akiva* contains elaborate homilies on the letters. Its redactional history is uncertain and considerably later than the historical Rabbi Akiva.

The Peh material treats the mouth as the bodily instrument suited to praise, song, and confession. **[KT]** Some recensions connect the letter to the world’s creation for Israel’s praise. The Hebrew textual database is available through the [Academy of the Hebrew Language](https://maagarim.hebrew-academy.org.il/Pages/PMain.aspx?misyzira=94000).

It does not present Peh as path 27.

## 5. The *Bahir*

The *Bahir*, probably compiled or redacted in twelfth-century Provence from older materials, develops:

- ten divine powers;
- letter symbolism;
- vocalization;
- limbs, creation, and divine speech;
- the image of an inverted cosmic tree.

**[Negative finding]** In the examined text there is no discrete “Path of Peh,” no Peh–Mars–Tower complex, and no Peh connector between Netzach and Hod. Claims that “the Bahir teaches the Path of Peh” usually infer from its general letter theology rather than cite a passage. Consult [Sefer HaBahir](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.1?lang=bi).

## 6. The *Zohar*

### The letters petition to begin creation

At Zohar I:2b–3b, the letters approach God in reverse order, each asking that creation begin through it.

Peh appeals through **פדות**, *pedut*, redemption. It is rejected because **פשע**, *peshaʿ*, transgression, also begins with Peh. In the Zohar’s symbolic narration, transgression conceals itself like a serpent drawing its head into its body.

This is:

- **[DT]** a Zoharic letter myth;
- **[KT]** an ethical theology of language;
- not [DT] evidence of Mars, Tower, or path 27.

The story may be consulted in [Zohar I:2b–3b on Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar.1.2b?lang=bi). Daniel C. Matt’s *Pritzker Edition*, vol. 1, should be preferred for a critically informed English translation; Stanford describes it as translated from the surviving Aramaic witnesses rather than from a prior English version ([edition record](https://www.sup.org/books/title/?id=4810)).

### Lips and physiognomy

Zoharic physiognomic passages treat the lips and mouth as signs of character and spiritual condition. One online text explicitly introduces “the secret of the lips in the letter Peh” in the Yitro material ([Zohar, Yitro](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Yitro/chapters/9)). This remains bodily and physiognomic letter symbolism, not a path diagram.

### Authorship

- **[KT]** Traditional attribution: Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle, second century.
- **[SR]** The core literature emerged in late-thirteenth-century Castile, with Moses de León central to composition or circulation.
- **[D]** Current scholarship frequently favors multiple compositional strata or a circle rather than one author. Yehuda Liebes, Daniel Abrams, Ronit Meroz, Elliot Wolfson, and others have complicated Scholem’s strong single-author formulation.

## 7. Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*, written in late-thirteenth-century Castile, organizes divine names through ten gates corresponding to the sefirot.

For the Western path’s endpoints:

- Netzach is associated with **יהוה צבאות**, YHWH Tzevaot.
- Hod is associated with **אלהים צבאות**, Elohim Tzevaot.

These are genuine Jewish sephiral name traditions. They are not “the divine name of Peh.” Gikatilla does not provide a Peh–Tower path. See [Shaʿarei Orah](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha'arei_Orah?lang=bi).

Likewise, no single classical Jewish list supplies the Golden Dawn’s complete path package of divine name, archangel, angelic order, color, incense, stone, animal, and tarot card.

## 8. Cordovero: *Pardes Rimmonim* and *Or Neʿerav*

### *Pardes Rimmonim*, 1548

Moses Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim* is an encyclopedic synthesis of pre-Lurianic Kabbalah. It discusses:

- the ten sefirot;
- their names and relationships;
- letter combinations;
- divine names;
- colors;
- angels;
- prior kabbalistic disagreements;
- *Sefer Yetzirah*’s mothers, doubles, and simples.

See, for example, [Pardes Rimmonim 3:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.3.4.1?lang=bi).

**[Negative finding]** Cordovero does not give the Golden Dawn Path of Peh. His color symbolism is primarily sephiral and operative; the famous four Peh colors are not taken from the *Pardes*.

### *Or Neʿerav*

*Or Neʿerav* is Cordovero’s concise introduction to the study and ethical prerequisites of Kabbalah. It emphasizes disciplined transmission, divine service, and the unity of Torah. It contains no chapter equivalent to the Western Path of Peh. See [Ohr Neʿerav](https://www.sefaria.org/Ohr_Ne'erav%2C_Introduction.1?lang=bi).

## 9. Luria through Hayyim Vital

### The system

Isaac Luria taught orally in Safed in the 1570s. Hayyim Vital and other disciples transmitted his teachings. The corpus conventionally called *Etz Chaim* describes:

- **tzimtzum**, the withdrawal or concealment that makes a conceptual “space”;
- the **kav**, line or ray;
- Adam Kadmon;
- the worlds Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Assiah;
- the configurations or **partzufim**;
- **shevirat ha-kelim**, the breaking of the vessels;
- sparks in the shells;
- **tikkun**, repair or reconfiguration.

See [Sefer Etz Chaim](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.1?lang=bi) and the [Hekhal Etz Chaim translation project](https://hekhal.org/texts/etz-chaim-tzimtzum-the-contraction/).

### Peh in Lurianic anatomy

Peh, “mouth,” can appear in the elaborate bodily symbolism of Adam Kadmon and the partzufim, alongside eyes, ears, nose, and emissions of light. The mouth is therefore significant in Lurianic cosmology.

But:

- **[Negative finding]** Vital’s *Etz Chaim* does not teach Peh as a Netzach–Hod connector.
- **[Negative finding]** It does not identify Peh with the tarot Tower.
- **[MI]** Equating the Tower’s fall with the shattering of the vessels is a modern comparative interpretation. It can be suggestive, but no historical chain from Vital to the tarot attribution has been demonstrated.

### “The Ari tree”

The so-called Ari tree is a later diagrammatic convention used to represent Lurianic relationships. On a widely circulated comparison of Ari, Gra, and Kircher trees:

- Ari Peh: Gevurah–Hod.
- Gra Peh: Gevurah–Hod.
- Kircher Peh: Netzach–Hod.

See the comparative table in Rankine and d’Este, [*Practical Qabalah Magick*, pp. 48–53](https://lib.wildla.in/book/Practical%20Qabalah%20Magick%20-%20Working%20the%20Magic%20of%20the%20Practical%20Qabalah%20and%20the%20Tree%20of%20Life%20in%20the%20Western%20Esoteric%20Tradition.pdf).

This must be labeled **[D]** because “Ari tree” does not mean that Luria left a signed, fixed diagram.

## 10. The Gra’s tree

The Vilna Gaon’s kabbalistic tradition rearranges the sefirot geometrically, notably raising Tiferet relative to some other trees. The conventional Gra path table also places Peh between **Gevurah and Hod**, with Mem—not Peh—between Netzach and Hod.

The published Gra *Sefer Yetzirah* simultaneously assigns Peh to Mercury, Thursday, and the left ear. This demonstrates that:

- the placement of a letter on a later sephirotic diagram;
- the planet assigned in a textual recension;
- and the Golden Dawn’s planetary attribution

are three distinct historical questions.

## 11. Hasidic and post-Lurianic readings

### *Tanya*

No chapter of the *Tanya* is devoted to “Path 27” or to Peh as Mars.

Relevant discussions include:

- *Likkutei Amarim* chapter 22: divine speech, created speech, and the descent that permits kelipot.
- *Iggeret ha-Kodesh* 5: the twenty-two letters of speech arise through five organs; **בומ״ף**, including Peh, are articulated by the lips.
- *Iggeret ha-Kodesh* 20: Malkhut and divine speech.

See [Tanya, Iggeret HaKodesh 5](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_IV%3B_Iggeret_HaKodesh.5?lang=bi) and [Tanya, Likkutei Amarim 22](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_I%3B_Likkutei_Amarim.22?lang=bi).

The operative principle is that letters as articulated sound are analogies for differentiated divine creative energy. **[Negative finding]** This does not supply a Jewish Tower correspondence.

### Rabbi Nachman of Breslov

Nachman repeatedly treats speech as a field of exile and repair:

- Torah and prayer rectify the mouth.
- Proper speech joins truth, praise, and faith.
- Corrupt speech fragments the person and community.
- In the tale of the tongue-tied beggar, apparently defective speech conceals superlative power.

These are Peh-relevant by theme, but not by occult path number. See [The Seven Beggars, tale 13](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Tales_of_Rabbi_Nachman/13) and [Likkutei Moharan II:82](https://www.breslov.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/LM-II-Lesson-82.pdf).

## 12. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and calendar

### Prayer

The strongest liturgical Peh locus is Psalm 145:16 in *Ashrei*:

> **פּוֹתֵחַ אֶת־יָדֶךָ וּמַשְׂבִּיעַ לְכָל־חַי רָצוֹן**

“You open Your hand and satisfy every living thing with favor.”

Halakhic and devotional sources give this verse special concentration because it expresses divine providence. Its initial Peh belongs to the psalm’s acrostic structure.

### Torah

Peh signifies:

- mouth and oral teaching;
- the covenant of the tongue;
- articulation by the lips;
- the open paragraph marker פ.

There is no standard Torah reading assigned to a “27th Path of Peh.”

### Counting the Omer

The seven weeks of the Omer are often mapped through Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, and Malkhut, yielding forty-nine combinations. Peh is not one of those seven qualities.

A practitioner may associate Peh with speech during a day involving Hod or Gevurah, but that is a later homiletical application. **[Negative finding]** No classical Omer day is universally “the Path of Peh.”

### Calendar

Because Peh is a double, its proper *Sefer Yetzirah* calendrical correspondence is a **weekday**, not a month. Which day varies by recension. Month, tribe, zodiac sign, and bodily director apply only to the twelve simples.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* included forty-seven theses “according to the Hebrew Cabalists” and seventy-one “according to his own opinion” in Cabala. The Latin title page announces conclusions drawn from Hebrew, Chaldean, Greek, Egyptian, and Latin authorities.

His Christian-Cabalist project sought confirmations of Christian doctrine in Jewish esoteric material. The text is available in Latin in the [digital *Conclusiones*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/pico/conclus.htm).

Pico transmitted ideas about:

- Hebrew letters as vehicles of divine power;
- names of God;
- angelic invocation;
- tenfold and thirty-two-fold structures;
- Cabala as a confirmation of Christianity.

He did **not** attest:

- Peh as path 27;
- the Kircher connector Netzach–Hod;
- Mars;
- a tarot Tower.

Claims that Pico knew the Golden Dawn Tree are **[MI] anachronisms**.

## 2. Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica*, 1517

Johannes Reuchlin developed Christian Cabala through a fictional dialogue among Jewish, Muslim, and Pythagorean interlocutors. He used Hebrew names and letter speculation in a Trinitarian and Christological system.

The chief Christian transformation is the pentagrammaton **יהשוה**, YHShVH, intended to write Jesus into the tetragrammaton. This is Christian theology, not a Jewish divine name tradition.

Reuchlin discusses Hebrew alphabetic values and the extended values of final letters. In Latin, Peh could be represented as *Phe* and glossed *os*, “mouth.” He does not give the later Peh–Mars–Tower package. A digitized 1517 edition is catalogued by the [Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/CV4BIAVTHUELB57ALX33HUCVJOKVSBO7).

## 3. Riccio and the *Portae Lucis* title page, 1516

Paolo Riccio’s abbreviated Latin adaptation of Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*, *Portae Lucis*, bears a famous human figure supporting or containing a diagram of ten sefirot.

The [Center for Jewish Art](https://cja.huji.ac.il/index.php?id=40577&mode=set) identifies it as the first printed image of the sefirot in this form. The surviving image may be viewed through [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Portae_Lucis_1516.jpg).

It is important but often misdescribed:

- It is not the earliest surviving Jewish sephirotic diagram.
- It is the earliest influential **printed** tree image.
- It does not display Kircher’s complete twenty-two letter-path allocation.
- It does not identify a Path of Peh.

## 4. Guillaume Postel, 1552; Mantua, 1562

Postel produced the first printed Latin translation of *Sefer Yetzirah* in 1552. A Hebrew edition was printed at Mantua in 1562. These editions helped stabilize forms later available to Christian Cabalists, but their planetary sequences should not be treated as the only ancient text.

## 5. Kircher, *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, 1652–1654

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* attempted a universal synthesis of Egyptian, Hebrew, Christian, classical, and occult wisdom. Its Kabbalistic plate displays ten sefirot and twenty-two connecting lines.

Kircher numbered:

- sefirot 1–10;
- letter paths Aleph–Tav as 11–32.

Since Peh is the seventeenth letter, it becomes **path 27**. On Kircher’s geometry it crosses horizontally between **Netzach and Hod**.

This is the direct structural ancestor of the Golden Dawn tree. Scans are linked through the [University of Pennsylvania’s Kircher record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp67641); a high-resolution tree image is catalogued by [Cornell University](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss:550244).

### Kircher versus Ari and Gra

| Connector | Conventional Ari tree | Conventional Gra tree | Kircher tree |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gevurah–Hod | **Peh** | **Peh** | Mem |
| Netzach–Hod | Mem | Mem | **Peh** |

Kircher also violates the later rule sometimes attributed to Jewish trees that the three mothers should be the three horizontal paths, seven doubles the verticals, and twelve simples the diagonals. His alphabetical numbering follows a different geometric logic.

**[SR]/[D]** Daniel Stolzenberg identifies Philippe d’Aquin’s *Interprétation de l’arbre de la Cabale* (1625) among Kircher’s immediate sources. Kircher’s tree was not an untouched import from a single ancient Jewish archetype.

## 6. Knorr von Rosenroth, *Kabbala Denudata*, 1677–1684

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth translated and excerpted Jewish kabbalistic texts into Latin, including portions associated with Cordovero and Lurianic literature. The work greatly widened Christian access to Jewish Kabbalah.

Common Latin equivalents include:

- *Peh/Phe* for the letter;
- *os* for “mouth”;
- *viae* or *semitae sapientiae* for paths of Wisdom;
- *corona*, *sapientia*, *intelligentia*, *magnificentia*, and related words for sephiral names.

**[Negative finding]** No path-level Peh–Mars–Tower doctrine appears as a distinct ancient Jewish teaching in *Kabbala Denudata*. Its importance lies in transmission of materials and in the consolidation of Christian-Cabalist diagrams and demonological tables.

The scans are available through the Internet Archive: [vol. 1, 1677](https://archive.org/details/kabbaladenudata01knor) and [vol. 2, 1684](https://archive.org/details/kabbaladenudata02knor).

## 7. Tarot before Lévi

Tarot cards originated in fifteenth-century Italy as a game. No medieval tarot source identifies the trumps with Hebrew letters. Antoine Court de Gébelin’s 1781 claim that tarot preserved Egyptian wisdom was speculative and historically unsupported, though enormously influential.

The Hebrew-letter system therefore belongs to occult reinterpretation, not to tarot’s medieval origin.

## 8. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

In *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*, Lévi correlated the twenty-two Hebrew letters with the twenty-two trumps. His sequence did not yet yield the Golden Dawn’s Peh–Tower attribution.

Because Lévi generally began Aleph with the Magician rather than with an unnumbered Fool as zero, his sequence attached Peh to a different trump—commonly the Star—while the Tower fell elsewhere.

Thus:

- **[HO]** Lévi established the powerful principle of letter–trump correspondence.
- **[D]** The Golden Dawn changed the sequence.
- **[Negative finding]** Peh–Tower is not simply “Lévi’s ancient attribution.”

A French scan is available in [*Dogme et rituel*, part 2](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/ceremonial/classics/pdf/transcendental/dogma_et_rituel_part_2.pdf).

## 9. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, c. 1888 onward

The Golden Dawn synthesized:

- Kircher’s tree geometry;
- Hebrew letters;
- the *Sefer Yetzirah* classification of mothers, doubles, and simples;
- a revised planetary order;
- tarot;
- astrology;
- elemental and zodiacal symbolism;
- four-world color scales;
- Egyptian divine forms;
- ritual initiation.

For Peh it established the now-familiar package:

> Peh → path 27 → Netzach–Hod → Mars → Tower XVI → Exciting Intelligence.

### Why the Tower?

The attribution is internally coherent rather than historically Jewish:

- Peh is a double, so the Golden Dawn requires a planet.
- The Order chooses Mars.
- The Tower’s lightning, falling masonry, violence, fire, and overturned authority suit Mars.
- Netzach and Hod represent force, desire, image, affect, thought, language, and formulation; the horizontal path is interpreted as a disruptive equilibration between them.
- “Mouth” permits symbolism of the destructive or creative Word.

This is **[HO] reasoning**, not evidence that ancient Jewish kabbalists associated Peh with a falling tower.

### *Book T*

In *Book T*, the card is:

- **XVI, the Blasted Tower/House of God**;
- title: **Lord of the Hosts of the Mighty**;
- letter: Peh;
- planet: Mars.

The title “House of God” reflects Continental names such as *La Maison Dieu*, not a Hebrew name of the path.

### Knowledge Lectures and advancement ritual

The Golden Dawn’s advancement toward the 4=7 grade includes the “27th Path of Peh.” The candidate proceeds from the sphere of Hod toward Netzach, bearing a ten-square Calvary Cross. Later published ritual descriptions use:

- Judges 5:21 and the torrent Kishon;
- Exodus 15:3, “YHWH is a man of war”;
- the deaths of the kings of Edom as unbalanced force;
- Mars as purification through violent dissolution;
- the Tower card at the completion of the path.

The published ritual is preserved in Regardie’s *The Golden Dawn* and later commentaries; an accessible text appears in the [Golden Dawn ritual compilation](https://dokumen.pub/the-golden-dawn-the-original-account-of-the-teachings-rites-and-ceremonies-of-the-hermetic-order-0875426638.html).

### The four color scales

| Atziluth | King | Scarlet |
| Briah | Queen | Red |
| Yetzirah | Prince/Emperor | Venetian red |
| Assiah | Princess/Empress | Bright red, rayed azure and emerald |

Some *777* transcriptions read “azure or orange” in the Assiah line. A modern HTML table also accidentally prints “rich amber” for Yetzirah; that color belongs properly to the solar Resh row. The Golden Dawn form “Venetian red” is supported by multiple independent tables, including the [four-scale Peh chart](https://www.iok-kabbalah.org/trees/Peh.html) and [websafe Golden Dawn color reconstruction](https://echtrai.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/websafesephirothpathsv2.pdf).

## 10. Westcott’s *Sepher Yetzirah*, 1887/1893

Westcott translated from a complex Christian-Hebraist textual lineage, especially Rittangelius, Postel, and Pistorius. His notes openly recommend studying the work with Freemasonry, Rosicrucian ceremonies, the Isiac Tablet, and tarot.

That makes the edition historically invaluable for the Golden Dawn, but not a critical edition of the oldest Jewish text.

Its Peh-related legacy is twofold:

1. It popularized “Exciting Intelligence.”
2. Its omission or displacement of path 28 helped produce persistent confusion in later occult tables.

- Emperor: Tzaddi rather than Heh;
- Star: Heh rather than Tzaddi.

**Peh and the Tower do not change.** Claims that Crowley moved the Tower from Peh are mistaken.

### Thelemic grades

The A∴A∴ grades are attached primarily to sefirot:

- Hod: Practicus 3=8.
- Netzach: Philosophus 4=7.

Peh lies between them in the inherited tree, so it can symbolize transition into the Philosophus grade. It is not a separate official A∴A∴ grade.

Crowley sometimes correlates the Tower with “Man of Earth” material, but that does not replace the formal grade structure.

## 12. Dion Fortune, 1935

*The Mystical Qabalah* is organized chiefly by sefirot, not by individual letter paths. There is therefore no “chapter on the Sephira Peh”: Peh is not a sefirah, and Fortune did not write such a chapter.

The relevant material is found in her chapters on:

- Netzach: emotion, art, desire, group mind, Venus;
- Hod: language, intellect, form, magic, Mercury;
- the relationship and imbalance between them.

A Peh interpretation may be reconstructed as the disruptive Mars current crossing between Venusian Netzach and Mercurial Hod, but that synthesis is implicit rather than a complete Fortune chapter. Consult the [1935 text](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html).

## 16. Pathworking traditions

### Golden Dawn ritual

The original operational context is initiatory ritual, not merely guided visualization. The candidate symbolically traverses Hod to Netzach under Martial tests and biblical images.

### Later guided pathworking

Twentieth- and twenty-first-century practitioners commonly:

1. visualize the red Peh letter;
2. enter the Tower card as a doorway;
3. invoke Mars, Khamael, or Elohim Gibor;
4. encounter fire, lightning, broken masonry, warlike guardians, or volcanic force;
5. reconcile feeling and intellect;
6. rebuild a structure under a corrected spiritual principle.

These practices are **[HO]/[MI]**. They differ substantially among Regardie, Case, Knight, the Ciceros, Servants of the Light, and independent orders. An example of a current Golden Dawn-derived practice is the [Cicero pathworking description](https://www.goldendawnshop.com/product/pathworkings-for-netzach-and-beyond-audio/).

There is no comparably documented Jewish practice of astrally walking a Peh connector between Netzach and Hod.

# The Qliphah or tunnel

## Jewish qelippot versus the Western inverse tree

In the Zohar and Lurianic Kabbalah, **qelippot**, “shells” or “husks,” name forces of concealment, impurity, death, or distorted divine vitality. Lurianic teaching connects them with the vessels’ breakage and the captivity of sparks.

The later diagram of ten inverse sefirot, each with a fixed exotic name and demon prince, is a **Christian-Cabalist and Hermetic compilation**. It should not be presented as one unchanged Zoharic table.

## The ten qliphothic orders

The following spellings are those standardized in Golden Dawn and Crowley-derived literature:

| No. | Sefirah | Qliphah | Conventional meaning | Later demon ruler(s) | Evidence |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kether | Thaumiel/Thamiel | Twin or contending powers | Satan and Moloch | [HO], derived from later Cabalist tables |

See the [Golden Dawn “Qliphoth” paper](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Qliphoth.html).

## What is Peh’s qliphah?

There is no qliphah named Peh and no single demon prince intrinsically governing it.

On the inverse Kircher tree, the path connects:

- **A’arab Zaraq**, the qliphothic reflection of Netzach;
- **Samael**, the qliphothic reflection of Hod.

If the endpoint rulers are used, Baal and Adrammelech are relevant. But the actual tunnel genius assigned to the inverted Peh path is **Parfaxitas**.

## Parfaxitas

### Crowley

**[DT]** Parfaxitas first appears in the Peh row of Crowley’s *Liber 231* list of “Genii of the 22 Scales of the Serpent and of the Qliphoth.” Crowley supplies the name but not Kenneth Grant’s extensive tunnel mythology.

### Kenneth Grant, *Nightside of Eden*, 1977

Grant’s chapter “Parfaxitas” states:

- the twenty-seventh tunnel is under Parfaxitas;
- its number is 450;
- the name should be vibrated in a deep C, like thunder;
- the sigil is bright red on an emerald square;
- 450 is associated with **תן**, *tan*, “dragon,” and with Leviathan;
- its formula involves astral animal forms and “atavistic resurgence”;
- it is connected with lycanthropy, wounds, fever, witchcraft, hybrid forms, and sexual-magical interpretations of the Tower.

See Kenneth Grant, [*Nightside of Eden*, p. 232 onward](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf).

These are **[HO] Typhonian teachings**, not Jewish Kabbalah and not Golden Dawn doctrine.

### Evidentiary caution

The name “Parfaxitas” is not recognizable as ordinary Hebrew, and Grant does not transparently show how its letters total 450. His gematria is therefore **[D]**. Later authors repeat the value without an independently established orthography.

The phrase “Tunnel of Set” and the complete tunnel system are likewise Grant’s occult elaboration. They should not be retrojected into the Zohar, Luria, Kircher, or the original Golden Dawn.

## Early diagrams and manuscripts

The history of the Tree is not a march toward one inevitable modern picture.

- Late-thirteenth- and fourteenth-century manuscripts contain sephirotic wheels, trees, human forms, concentric diagrams, and “Trees of Wisdom.”
- The Ilanot Project documents Kabbalistic scrolls as a distinct visual-literary genre; see the [Ilanot database](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/).
- J. H. Chajes emphasizes that diagrams were working intellectual instruments, not merely illustrations.
- A surviving Spanish diagram dated around 1284 is among the earliest known sephirotic trees, but it is not Kircher’s twenty-two-path figure.
- The *Portae Lucis* title page of 1516 is the decisive early printed image.
- Kircher’s seventeenth-century plate supplies the path layout later adopted by Western occultists.
- Golden Dawn colored trees overlay the four scales and are a late-nineteenth-century innovation.

## Artists, poets, and fiction

The strongest direct artistic engagements with Peh are tarot-based:

- Pamela Colman Smith’s 1909 Tower became the standard modern icon.
- Frieda Harris’s Thoth Tower transformed it through Crowley’s Martial and Thelemic imagery.
- Crowley’s *Liber 231* gives Peh a short visionary prose-poem.
- Kenneth and Steffi Grant developed the Peh/Parfaxitas complex through Typhonian prose and art.
- Linda Falorio produced a tunnel image for the *Shadow Tarot*.

Many poets and novelists use towers, mouths, lightning, or Kabbalah, but unless they explicitly identify Peh, such parallels should not be counted as documented reception of this element. Borges’s “The Aleph,” for example, concerns Aleph, not Peh.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Date of *Sefer Yetzirah*

**Traditional:** Abrahamic authorship.

**Earlier scholarship:** late antiquity, commonly third–sixth centuries.

**Recent scholarship:** some layers may be older, but the complete work may reflect seventh–ninth-century Jewish engagement with Syriac, Christian, philosophical, or Islamic-period cosmology.

**Secure terminus:** extensive commentaries and multiple forms appear in the early tenth century.

No proposed date makes the tarot or Kircher path layout part of the original text.

## 2. What are the “thirty-two paths”?

In *Sefer Yetzirah*: ten sefirot plus twenty-two letters.

In the separate *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom*: thirty-two individually named Intelligences.

In Kircher and the Golden Dawn: ten spheres plus twenty-two drawn connectors.

The arithmetic is the same; the literary and diagrammatic structures are not.

## 3. Is Peh really Mars?

- Saadia: Venus.
- long/Mantua forms: usually Venus.
- short forms: usually Venus.
- Gra edition examined: Mercury.
- Golden Dawn: Mars.

Therefore Peh–Mars is valid **inside the Golden Dawn system**, not as a recension-neutral statement about *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## 4. What does Peh join?

- Kircher/Golden Dawn: Netzach–Hod.
- conventional Ari: Gevurah–Hod.
- conventional Gra: Gevurah–Hod.
- *Sefer Yetzirah* itself: no drawn sephirotic connector is specified.

This is the central path-layout dispute.

## 5. Is “Exciting Intelligence” genuine *Sefer Yetzirah*?

It belongs to the supplementary *Thirty-Two Paths* text transmitted with some editions. Westcott’s wording appears textually displaced and omits path 28. “Exciting” is also a freer rendering of **מורגש**, which more directly means perceptible or palpable.

## 6. Zohar authorship

Traditional Judaism attributes the Zohar to Shimon bar Yochai. Historical scholarship locates its central production in medieval Castile and assigns Moses de León a major role. Whether the corpus is best understood as one author, a circle, or multiple strata remains disputed.

The dispute affects dating and historical attribution, but not the basic finding that the Zohar does not contain the Golden Dawn path package.

## 7. Christian Cabala and appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Riccio, Postel, Kircher, and Knorr preserved, translated, transformed, and sometimes distorted Jewish sources.

The process involved both:

- genuine philological engagement and transmission;
- supersessionist use of Jewish texts to “prove” Christianity;
- relocation of Jewish symbols into Hermetic, Egyptian, planetary, and magical systems.

Calling the Western system merely “the Kabbalah” obscures that history. “Christian Cabala” and “Hermetic Qabalah” are useful distinctions.

## 8. Tarot attributions

There is no evidence for Hebrew-letter tarot correspondences in medieval tarot.

- Lévi created one influential ordering.
- The Golden Dawn revised it.
- Crowley revised Heh/Tzaddi again.
- Peh–Tower survives Crowley’s change.

The visual and symbolic fit between Mars and the Tower may be compelling within occult practice; it does not establish ancient pedigree.

## 9. Daʿat

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists on ten, “not eleven.” In later Kabbalah, Daʿat can mean:

- knowledge;
- conjunction of Chokhmah and Binah;
- a state appearing when Keter is not counted;
- an internal sephiral function;
- a represented but non-independent point.

Western diagrams often draw Daʿat as a hidden or “false” eleventh sefirah. That is a diagrammatic-theological convention, not a contradiction that can be resolved by simple counting.

## 10. The qliphothic tree

The ten named adverse orders are a compilation from Jewish terms, Christian demonology, Knorr-derived materials, Golden Dawn manuscripts, and Crowley. The tidy inverted tree and its demon princes should not be presented as one table copied directly from the Zohar.

Parfaxitas is later still: Crowley supplies the name; Grant supplies the tunnel narrative.

## 11. The Tower and the broken vessels

The analogy is conceptually attractive:

- Tower: catastrophic failure of form.
- *Shevirah*: vessels fail to contain divine light.

But no source trail shows that the Golden Dawn chose the Tower because of Vital’s doctrine. It is therefore **[MI] comparative theology**, not documented genealogy.

## 12. Peh’s “mouth” versus its assigned bodily organ

The word Peh means mouth, but the individual-letter body assignment in *Sefer Yetzirah* is often a nostril or ear. The seven body gates form a complete series, and the letter name does not dictate its position. Modern charts that silently change the organ to “mouth” are harmonizations.

## 13. The four color scales

These are Golden Dawn creations attached to the four worlds. Cordovero discusses colors, and Jewish Kabbalah knows the four worlds, but the exact sequence scarlet/red/Venetian red/bright red rayed azure and emerald is not found in Cordovero or Vital.

# Open questions

1. **What is the earliest exact manuscript witness for the Hebrew twenty-seventh Intelligence?**  
   A complete stemma of the *Thirty-Two Paths of Wisdom* remains necessary. Westcott’s omission of path 28 shows that printed occult editions cannot substitute for manuscript comparison.

2. **Which printed source directly mediated the letter placement from Kircher to the Golden Dawn?**  
   Kircher’s geometry is the structural ancestor, but the route may include Knorr, later encyclopedias, French occultism, and unpublished Order compilations.

3. **Who fixed the Golden Dawn’s Peh–Mars allocation?**  
   Mathers is the likeliest central organizer, but the system emerged through collaborative Order documents. It does not simply reproduce one *Sefer Yetzirah* recension.

4. **What precisely did early “Ari tree” and “Gra tree” labels mean in each printed diagram?**  
   Modern comparison charts can conceal variation within each school and sometimes attribute later editorial diagrams directly to the masters.

5. **What is the exact orthography and intended language of Parfaxitas?**  
   Crowley supplies a Latin-alphabet form. Grant’s value 450 lacks a transparent, stable Hebrew spelling.

6. **Which qliphothic ruler table did the Golden Dawn compiler use?**  
   Several names derive from Knorr, but spellings, order, demon rulers, and interpretive glosses reflect later compilation.

7. **How early is the Peh passage in the letter-petition narrative?**  
   It is documented in the medieval Zoharic corpus, but its relation to earlier alphabet midrash and the various recensions of *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva* deserves a dedicated manuscript study.

8. **Can “Peh as a Bet with a Yod inside” be dated?**  
   It is widespread in modern Jewish pedagogy, but its earliest textual attestation is often left uncited.

9. **Do any premodern Jewish ilanot place Peh on the exact Netzach–Hod connector before Kircher?**  
   No secure example emerged from the sources examined. This remains testable against the expanding Ilanot manuscript database.

# The Path of Tzaddi: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary legend

This dossier separates six kinds of statement:

- **[TEXT]** Directly documented in a primary text, manuscript, printed edition, ritual paper, or authorial publication.
- **[SCHOLARSHIP]** Historical or philological reconstruction argued by modern scholars.
- **[JEWISH TRADITION]** A traditional Jewish interpretation whose authority is internal to a particular kabbalistic, rabbinic, or Hasidic lineage.
- **[HERMETIC]** A Christian-Cabalist, Golden Dawn, Thelemic, or later occult attribution.
- **[DISPUTED]** A claim for which the sources, recensions, chronology, or interpretation conflict.
- **[MODERN]** A modern synthesis, pathworking, or invention not attested in the earlier Jewish sources.

The most important conclusion is that “Path 28 of Tzaddi” is a composite object. No early Jewish text contains the complete equation:

> path 28 = צ = Aquarius = Netzach–Yesod = the Star = violet/sky blue/bluish mauve/white-purple.

Its components emerged separately:

1. *Sefer Yetzirah* supplies the thirty-two “wondrous paths,” the ten *sefirot belimah*, the twenty-two letters, Tzaddi’s status as one of twelve simple letters, and—across the principal recensions—the Aquarius/Shevat complex.
2. A separate medieval list of “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom” calls number 28 the “Natural Intelligence.”
3. Kircher’s Christian-Cabalist tree fixes the twenty-eighth connector between Netzach and Yesod.
4. The Golden Dawn unites that line with Tzaddi, Aquarius, the Star, ceremonial titles, and four colour scales.
5. Crowley subsequently separates Tzaddi from the Star and assigns it to the Emperor.
6. Kenneth Grant converts the corresponding “nightside” route into the Tunnel of Tzuflifu.

# The name

## צדי, צדיק, Tzaddi

**[TEXT]** The regular square-script letter is **צ**; at the end of a word it becomes **ץ**. Its conventional name in careful modern Hebrew is *tsadi*. *Tsade*, *ṣade*, *ṣādē*, *tzadi*, and *tzaddi* represent different scholarly and vernacular transliteration systems. “Tzaddik,” with a final *qoph*, is properly the separate Hebrew word **צדיק**, “righteous [person].”

**[DISPUTED]** The occasional name *tzaddik* for the letter may arise from the letter’s association with **צדיק**, but another proposal derives it from rapid recitation of the sequence *tsadi-qof*. Rabbinic evidence also preserves plural forms such as *tzadin*, supporting *tzadi* rather than *tzaddik* as the letter name.

### Meaning and etymology

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** The letter descends from Northwest Semitic **ṣādē**, represented in Phoenician by **𐤑**. Its ultimate Proto-Sinaitic sign and name are uncertain. Proposed pictorial origins include:

- a plant or papyrus stalk;
- a fish-hook;
- a hunting implement;
- a human figure or other unidentified sign.

“Fish-hook” is therefore a longstanding interpretive gloss, and it became canonical in Western occult tables, but it is not a securely demonstrated paleographic fact. The strongest responsible formulation is:

> “Fish-hook” is the Golden Dawn/Crowley English value of Tzaddi, based on one disputed etymological reconstruction.

Claims that the glyph certainly began as a fish-hook, bent righteous man, kneeling initiate, womb, or birth-symbol are later symbolic readings, not established epigraphy.

## Graphic history

**[SCHOLARSHIP]**

1. The uncertain Proto-Sinaitic ancestor belongs to the small corpus of alphabetic inscriptions from the second millennium BCE.
2. The Phoenician form **𐤑** passed into Old Hebrew/Paleo-Hebrew.
3. During and after the Babylonian and Persian periods, Jewish scribes increasingly used an Aramaic-derived script.
4. The Jewish or “square” script developed from Imperial Aramaic forms during the late Second Temple period.
5. The ordinary **צ** and final **ץ** became differentiated within this scribal tradition.
6. In Torah-scroll practice, Tzaddi is one of the **שעטנ״ז ג״ץ** letters customarily ornamented with *tagin*.

This sequence is documented graphically; a single invariant pictorial “meaning” is not.

## Shape lore

### Talmud, *Shabbat* 104a

**[TEXT/RABBINIC TRADITION]** The bent and final/straight forms of Tzaddi are interpreted as two states of the righteous person: humble or bent in the present, ultimately upright or revealed. The passage’s ethical point is humility, including humility in receiving Torah. See [Shabbat 104a](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104a?lang=bi).

This is wordplay on *tzaddik*, not a historical derivation of the glyph.

### Zoharic letter contest

**[TEXT/KABBALISTIC TRADITION]** In the Zohar’s prologue, the letters appear before God in reverse order, each asking that creation begin with it. Tzaddi appeals to the righteous—*tzaddikim*—and to the divine description as righteous. God refuses because Tzaddi must remain concealed.

The Zohar further reads Tzaddi’s graphic structure as a union of Nun and Yod, initially back-to-back and destined for a face-to-face union. This is a symbolic reading of the square letter, not paleography. See [Zohar, Introduction 6:34](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar%2C_Introduction.6.34?lang=bi).

### *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva*

**[TEXT/DISPUTED]** The alphabetic midrashim transmitted under Rabbi Akiva’s name contain several recensions and accumulated during the early medieval period. They interpret letters through names, shapes, crowns, gematria, and biblical words. Some versions share the broader theme of letters contending for primacy.

A stable, independently verified Tzaddi speech identical to the Zohar’s cannot be established from the accessible witnesses consulted here. It is safest to say that the Zohar preserves the best-attested Tzaddi petition, while *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva* supplies the wider genre of letter-personification.

# Biblical foundations—and their limits

There is no biblical “Path of Tzaddi.” The relevant verses enter at several different interpretive levels.

## 1 Chronicles 29:11 and the lower seven sefirot

**[TEXT]**

> לך יהוה הגדלה והגבורה והתפארת והנצח וההוד כי כל בשמים ובארץ לך יהוה הממלכה

The verse names greatness, power, beauty, victory, majesty, “all,” and kingdom. Kabbalists read these as the seven lower sefirot:

| Biblical word | Later sefirah |
|---|---|
| הגדולה, *ha-gedullah* | Chesed |
| הגבורה, *ha-gevurah* | Gevurah |
| התפארת, *ha-tiferet* | Tiferet |
| הנצח, *ha-netzach* | Netzach |
| ההוד, *ha-hod* | Hod |
| כל, *kol* | Yesod |
| הממלכה, *ha-mamlakhah* | Malkhut |

This supports the names and biblical symbolism of the lower tree. It does not place Tzaddi between Netzach and Yesod.

## Proverbs

**[TEXT]** Proverbs 3:19 says that YHWH founded the earth through Wisdom and established the heavens through Understanding:

> יהוה בחכמה יסד ארץ כונן שמים בתבונה

This became important to mappings of Chokhmah and Binah. Proverbs 3:20 adds Knowledge, contributing to later discussions of Da’at. Neither verse numbers a Tzaddi path.

More directly relevant is Proverbs 10:25:

> וצדיק יסוד עולם  
> “The righteous one is the foundation of the world.”

**[KABBALISTIC TRADITION]** This joins **tzaddik** to **Yesod**. Gikatilla makes it a key name of the ninth sefirah. It does not prove that the letter Tzaddi is a Netzach–Yesod connector.

## Job 28

**[TEXT]** Job 28 asks where Wisdom is found and says that God understands its way or path. It contributed to the general kabbalistic language of wisdom, hidden routes, mines, depths, and paths. No verse in the chapter identifies path 28 with Tzaddi.

## Isaiah

Several separate passages became relevant:

- **Isaiah 3:10**, “Say of the righteous that it is good,” supports the *tzaddik–tov–Yesod* complex in *Sha‘arei Orah*.
- **Isaiah 11:2** supplies a structured series of divine spirits—wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and fear—used in later Christian and occult enumerations.
- **Isaiah 26:4**, containing **כי ביה יהוה צור עולמים**, enters the rabbinic and *Sefer Yetzirah* tradition that worlds were formed with Yod and Heh.
- **Isaiah 57:15**, “the high and exalted One…whose name is holy,” is echoed in formulations surrounding the opening of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

None supplies the Hermetic path layout.

## Torah and prayer

**[TEXT]** Tzaddi appears throughout the Torah simply as a Hebrew consonant. Psalm 119’s Tzaddi stanza opens:

> צדיק אתה יהוה וישר משפטיך  
> “Righteous are You, YHWH, and upright are Your judgments.”

This is a genuine liturgical and alphabetic meeting of letter, righteousness, Torah, and divine judgment.

**Absence of evidence:** There is no universally prescribed classical Jewish prayer for “Path 28,” no standard Omer day belonging to Tzaddi, and no classical Jewish ritual of travelling from Yesod to Netzach through a Star path.

The divine title **צבאות**, *Tzeva’ot*, begins with Tzaddi, but that fact does not make it the path’s unique divine name.

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*: the earliest relevant textual layer

### Date

**[DISPUTED/SCHOLARSHIP]** *Sefer Yetzirah* has been dated anywhere from late antiquity to the early Islamic period. Older scholarship often suggested the second through sixth centuries CE. Peter Hayman, Tzahi Weiss, and other recent scholars emphasize the instability of the text and the difficulty of recovering an original, with some placing major stages of its redaction in approximately the seventh to ninth centuries.

The earliest secure commentaries and manuscript evidence belong to the tenth century. A Geniza scroll related to, but independent of, Saadia’s recension complicates the old idea that Saadia simply created his version.

Accordingly:

- attribution to Abraham is **traditional**, not historical;
- attribution to Rabbi Akiva is also **traditional**;
- a single recoverable second-century text is **disputed**;
- the work’s basic cosmological units may contain earlier materials than the surviving recension.

See Peter Hayman’s [critical edition information](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/sefer-yesira-9783161469770/) and Tzahi Weiss’s discussion of the work’s setting in [*Sefer Yeṣirah and Its Contexts*](https://www.pennpress.org/9780812249903/sefer-yesirah-and-its-contexts/).

### The thirty-two paths

**[TEXT]** The work opens with thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom:

- ten **sefirot belimah**;
- twenty-two foundational letters.

The twenty-two letters are divided into:

- three mothers: Aleph, Mem, Shin;
- seven doubles: Bet, Gimel, Dalet, Kaf, Pe, Resh, Tav;
- twelve simples: Heh, Vav, Zayin, Chet, Tet, Yod, Lamed, Nun, Samekh, Ayin, Tzaddi, Qof.

The full Gra-version text is available at [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.1).

### A crucial negative finding

**[TEXT]** *Sefer Yetzirah* does **not** say:

- that Tzaddi is “path 28” in the later numerical sense;
- that it joins Netzach and Yesod;
- that it is the Natural Intelligence;
- that it corresponds to a tarot card;
- that it possesses four colour scales.

“Thirty-two paths” originally encompasses the ten sefirot and twenty-two letters as creative principles. Converting the letters into precisely located lines on one planar Tree is later diagrammatic interpretation.

### Tzaddi in the Gra version

The relevant Hebrew reads:

> המליך אות צ׳ בלעיטה וקשר לו כתר וצרפן זה בזה וצר בהם דלי בעולם ושבט בשנה וקורקבן בנפש זכר ונקבה

A close translation is:

> He made Tzaddi king over taste/eating, bound a crown to it, and formed Aquarius in the universe, Shevat in the year, and the *korkeban* in the person, male and female.

See [*Sefer Yetzirah*, Gra Version 5:10](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah_Gra_Version.5.10).

The word **קורקבן** is the gizzard in bird anatomy and is variously generalized by translators as stomach, digestive organ, or abdomen. “Abomasum” in some English versions is too zoologically specific for an unqualified human-body translation.

### Recensional variants

The following table follows the comparison printed by Aryeh Kaplan, checked against Saadia’s text and the Sefaria Gra version:

| Recension/commentarial family | Sign | Month | Faculty | Body |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gra | Aquarius | Shevat | taste/eating | *korkeban* |
| Short recension | Aquarius | Shevat | thought, supplied through Raavad’s commentary | *kivah*, stomach |
| Long recension | Aquarius | Shevat | taste | right foot |
| Saadia | Aquarius | Shevat | thought | right foot |
| Cordovero/Ramak scheme | Aquarius | Shevat | smell | *kivah* |

Sources: [Kaplan’s comparative edition, Table 48](https://leonbahrmanministries.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Aryeh-Kaplan-Sefer-Yetzirah2.pdf), [Saadia translation](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/judaism/saadia.pdf), and [Sefaria’s composite text](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.5.1).

**[DISPUTED]** “Ari version” and “Gra version” are conventional names for early-modern textual arrangements. They should not be treated as signed autographs of Isaac Luria or Elijah of Vilna. Printed occult books frequently blur:

- a recension of the wording;
- a commentary attached to that recension;
- a reconstructed tree diagram;
- the later Hermetic path scheme.

Those are four different things.

### Tribe

**Absence of evidence:** *Sefer Yetzirah* assigns letters to months, signs, faculties, and bodily directors, but its principal recensions do not name one of the twelve tribes in each Tzaddi passage.

**[LATER JEWISH TRADITION]** In later Jewish month symbolism, Shevat is commonly associated with **Asher**, whose blessing concerns rich food and delicacies. This suits the Gra attribution of taste/eating. A representative Hasidic presentation is [“Shevat: Rectifying Our Eating Patterns”](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/1742533/jewish/Shevat-Rectifying-Our-Eating-Patterns.htm).

**[HERMETIC]** Golden Dawn zodiac tables instead give **Reuben** to Aquarius. Asher and Reuben belong to different correspondence systems and should not be harmonized silently.

## 2. The “Natural Intelligence”

The Hebrew list underlying the later “Yetziratic texts” says:

> הנתיב הכ״ח נקרא שכל מוטבע. מפני שבו נשלם טבע כל נמצא מתחת גלגל החמה בשלמות שהוא הטבע

A close gloss:

> The twenty-eighth path is called the Imprinted or Natural Intelligence, because through it the nature of every existent beneath the sphere of the sun is completed.

The Hebrew continues with a gematria linking **אי״ע** and **טבע**, “nature.” It is preserved with the commentary attributed to the Raavad; see the [Hebrew path list](https://www.hagalil.com/judentum/kabbala/hebraeisch/yezira-0.htm).

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** This “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom” list is a separate medieval composition appended to, or printed with, *Sefer Yetzirah*. It is not part of the earliest recoverable core of *Sefer Yetzirah*. Calling the sentences “the *Sefer Yetzirah*’s descriptions of the 32 paths” is therefore bibliographically misleading.

Westcott translated number 28:

> “The Twenty-eighth Path is called the Natural Intelligence; by it is completed and perfected the nature of all that exists beneath the Sun.”

See W. Wynn Westcott, [*Sepher Yetzirah*](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1893__westcott___sepher_yetzirah.pdf), §28.

Kircher’s Latin is:

> *Vigesima octaua semita dicitur intelligentia naturalis…*

The rest explains that through it all nature under the sun’s sphere is perfected. See Kircher, [*Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, II.1](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm), pp. 312–316.

“Natural Intelligence” is thus genuinely old relative to the Golden Dawn, but its union with Tzaddi and the Netzach–Yesod line depends on the later numbering-and-diagram system.

## 3. *Sefer ha-Bahir*

**[TEXT]** The *Bahir*, assembled in its extant form in late-twelfth-century Provence from earlier mystical materials, discusses:

- letters as cosmic forms;
- the ten utterances or sefirotic powers;
- vowels, sounds, limbs, and divine names;
- the righteous person and the world’s sustaining structure.

**Absence of evidence:** It does not contain a distinct “Path of Tzaddi,” does not call Tzaddi number 28, and does not place it on a Netzach–Yesod line. Statements that the *Bahir* teaches the Golden Dawn path are retrojections.

See [*Sefer HaBahir*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir).

## 4. Gikatilla’s *Sha‘arei Orah*

Joseph Gikatilla’s *Sha‘arei Orah*, composed in the late thirteenth century, organizes the sefirot through their divine names.

### Yesod and the righteous one

**[TEXT]** Gikatilla calls Yesod:

- **יסוד**, Foundation;
- **אל חי**, Living God;
- **צדיק**, Righteous One;
- **טוב**, Good;
- covenant, peace, remembrance, Zion, and related biblical names.

He explicitly invokes Proverbs 10:25 and the Talmudic teaching that the world stands upon one pillar called the righteous one. See [*Sha‘arei Orah*, Second Gate](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Second_Gate%2C_Ninth_Sefirah.135).

He also describes Netzach and Hod as right and left, with Yesod as the reconciling channel between them.

This is a major Jewish precedent for associating **tzaddik** with Yesod. It is not an assignment of the letter צ to a path entering Yesod from Netzach.

### Divine names and angels

Gikatilla’s method concerns divine names rather than the fully standardized Golden Dawn hierarchy of “divine name–archangel–angelic choir–planet.” For the endpoints:

- Yesod is especially **El Chai/Shaddai**;
- Netzach and Hod participate in the paired names **YHWH Tzeva’ot** and **Elohim Tzeva’ot**.

The later fixed pairings “Haniel–Elohim–Venus” and “Gabriel–Kerubim–Moon” are Hermetic systematizations assembled from several sources.

## 5. The Zohar

### Date and authorship

**[JEWISH TRADITION]** The Zohar presents itself as the teachings of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle in the second century.

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** Gershom Scholem argued for late-thirteenth-century Castilian composition, with Moses de León as the central author. Later scholarship often prefers a more stratified model involving de León and a circle or circles of writers rather than one simple act of forgery. See the [UCL study of Zoharic authorship](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10088406/) and [Cambridge discussion of Zoharic literature](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/midrash-unbound/zoharic-literature-and-midrashic-temporality/6BBB8C8EE00233874F0340E47331BFC3).

### Tzaddi’s petition

**[TEXT]** The Zohar gives Tzaddi unusually rich alphabetic lore:

- it invokes *tzaddikim*, righteous ones;
- God too is called righteous;
- its mystery must remain concealed;
- its graphic union of Nun and Yod suggests masculine and feminine powers;
- their back-to-back relation precedes a perfected face-to-face union.

This is relevant to later sexual, generative, and initiatory readings of Tzaddi, but it contains no Aquarius, Star, Emperor, or path 28.

Daniel Matt’s Pritzker translation is the principal modern critical English edition; see the [Stanford University Press front matter](https://supress.sites-pro.stanford.edu/sites/supress/files/media/file/4810_Front_Matter.pdf).

## 6. Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

**[TEXT]** Moses Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim* systematizes earlier kabbalah into thirty-two gates. Gate 7 discusses the paths and relations among the sefirot; Gate 12 treats the thirty-two paths and their interpretations; Gate 27 treats letters; Gate 28 treats vowel points.

Cordovero knows both “old” and “new” kabbalists’ arrangements and does not leave one uncontested diagram. See the [Sefaria edition](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimonim) and the [Hebrew Gate 12 text](https://www.torahleshma.com/he/read/Pardes%20Rimmonim%2012).

**[SCHOLARSHIP]** The diagrams in early printed editions of *Pardes Rimmonim* are inconsistent. Some represent 21 or 22 connections, and their lettering is not uniform. Treating one later diagram as “the original Jewish Tree” suppresses this plurality. Eugene Matanky’s study, “Diagrammatic Kabbalah in the Printed Editions of *Pardes Rimonim*,” is especially important.

### Tzaddi in Cordovero

Cordovero preserves the broader *Sefer Yetzirah* complex but his faculty/body scheme differs: later comparison tables give Tzaddi as smell and stomach. This is textual evidence that the bodily and sensory attributions were not immutable.

### *Or Ne‘erav*

**[TEXT/DISPUTED]** *Or Ne‘erav* gives a concise introduction to Cordoverian kabbalah. Its verbal description of some sefirotic relations does not align perfectly with every printed *Pardes* diagram. This is further evidence against imagining a universally fixed Jewish 22-line diagram before Kircher.

## 7. Luria through Chaim Vital

### The Lurianic cosmology

**[TEXT/KABBALISTIC TRADITION]** Chaim Vital’s *Etz Chaim* records the teachings attributed to Isaac Luria. Its characteristic structure includes:

1. *Tzimtzum*, contraction or concealment;
2. the ray entering the vacated space;
3. Adam Kadmon;
4. the four worlds—Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, Assiah;
5. the world of points, *Nekudim*;
6. the breaking of vessels, *shevirat ha-kelim*;
7. the formation of the *partzufim*;
8. restoration or repair, *tikkun*.

See [*Sefer Etz Chaim*](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim).

### Tzaddi’s actual role

**Absence of evidence:** *Etz Chaim* has no chapter on “the twenty-eighth Path of Tzaddi.” When Tzaddi appears, it does so in technical letter-combinations, divine names, *tzelem*, *partzuf* analysis, and gematria—not as a Star-bearing Netzach–Yesod path.

A representative occurrence is [*Etz Chaim* 13:11:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.13.11.2), where Tzaddi participates in the letters of a technical Lurianic formula.

### Ari-tree placement

Modern reconstructions associated with Aryeh Kaplan place Tzaddi on **Gevurah–Tiferet** in an Ari-type tree. The rationale groups:

- mothers on horizontal axes;
- doubles on verticals;
- simples on diagonals.

**[DISPUTED]** This should be described as an Ari-type reconstructed diagram, not as a self-explanatory plate drawn and signed by Luria. The transmitted Lurianic writings are more fluid and multi-dimensional than the single modern graphic.

## 8. The Gra

Elijah of Vilna’s recension and commentary organize *Sefer Yetzirah* spatially and linguistically. A commonly reproduced Gra-tree reconstruction places Tzaddi between **Netzach and Malkhut**, not Netzach and Yesod.

Again, the letter remains Aquarius/Shevat in the textual table, while the line it occupies changes with the diagram.

| System | Tzaddi joins |
|---|---|
| Ari-type reconstruction | Gevurah–Tiferet |
| Gra-type reconstruction | Netzach–Malkhut |
| Kircher/Golden Dawn | Netzach–Yesod |

This comparison is reproduced in the [Arcane Archive discussion based on Kaplan](https://arcane-archive.org/tarot/tzaddi-is-not-the-star-1.php). It should be checked against Kaplan’s diagrams rather than treated as an ancient consensus.

## 9. Hasidic readings

### Tanya

**Absence of evidence:** *Tanya* discusses the **tzaddik** at length, especially the distinction between *tzaddik*, *beinoni*, and *rasha*. It does not contain a chapter on the letter Tzaddi as Path 28.

Equating Tanya’s righteous person with the Golden Dawn Star-path is a modern cross-system interpretation.

### Nachman of Bratslav

Rabbi Nachman repeatedly treats:

- the power and danger of attachment to the *tzaddik*;
- the *tzaddik* as a channel for souls;
- humility, prayer, song, and rectification;
- the covenant and sexual holiness.

These themes overlap symbolically with Yesod and the righteous-one tradition. They do not establish a Tzaddi path between Netzach and Yesod.

### Shevat and rectified eating

Later Hasidic month teachings combine:

- Tzaddi;
- Shevat;
- Aquarius;
- taste/eating;
- the tribe Asher;
- Tu BiShvat and eating fruit with intention.

This is a coherent Jewish development from *Sefer Yetzirah*, but it remains different from tarot pathworking.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* introduced a Christianized Cabala into Renaissance learned culture.

**[TEXT]** Pico states that the forms, conjunctions, separations, directions, crowns, openings, and order of Hebrew letters reveal mysteries of the ten “numerations”:

> *decem numerationum secreta…manifestent.*

See [Pico’s thesis I.28.33](https://cds.library.brown.edu/projects/pico/singleThesis.php?expand=sub10528&myID=p1052833) and the [Latin *Conclusiones*](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/pico/conclus.htm).

**Absence of evidence:** Pico does not present the later path package Tzaddi–Aquarius–Netzach–Yesod–Star. His importance lies in:

- sacralizing Hebrew letters for Christian readers;
- using gematria and letter permutation;
- reading kabbalah as confirmation of Christian doctrines.

**[SCHOLARSHIP/DISPUTED]** Some of Pico’s Hebrew materials were translated and reshaped by Flavius Mithridates, occasionally with Christianizing expansions. Christian Cabala was therefore not a neutral transmission of Jewish kabbalah.

## 2. Reuchlin, 1517

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica libri tres* developed Pico’s Christian Cabala through Hebrew names, letters, number, and contemplative theology. The 1517 Haguenau edition is digitized by the [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/afbmqnys), [Bavarian State Library](http://daten.digitale-sammlungen.de/~db/0001/bsb00017488/images/), and [Source Library](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/de-arte-cabalistica-id-est-de-divinae-revelationis-ad-reuchlin).

**Absence of evidence:** Reuchlin does not document the mature Path-28 correspondence. His Latin vocabulary centers on *Cabala*, *sephiroth/numerationes*, letters, names, and divine revelation, not a Star-path called Tzaddi.

## 3. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paulus Ricius’s Latin translation of Gikatilla’s *Sha‘arei Orah*, printed as *Portae Lucis*, has a title-page woodcut associated with Hans Burgkmair. It is often described as the first printed European image of the sefirotic tree.

Its motto reads:

> *Haec est porta tetragrammaton iusti intrabunt per eam.*

The image is important in the history of tree diagrams but does not yet present the Golden Dawn Tzaddi path. See the [Center for Jewish Art record](https://cja.huji.ac.il/index.php?id=374208&mode=alone) and [historical discussion](https://www.sephardichorizons.org/Volume15/Issue1/Heller-Gikatilla.html).

## 4. Kircher, 1652–1654

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II.1, gives the most consequential early-modern tree for later ceremonial magic.

### The diagram

Kircher’s plate:

- arranges ten sefirot in three columns;
- supplies twenty-two connecting lines;
- puts three lines into Malkhut;
- permits a direct Netzach–Yesod diagonal;
- numbers the full system from 1 to 32;
- places number 28 on Netzach–Yesod.

The plate appears between approximately pp. 288–289; the discussions of the paths follow later. A high-resolution image is available from [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ATree_of_Life_%28Kircher_1653%29.jpg), with Latin scans through [Bill Heidrick’s Kircher archive](https://www.billheidrick.com/Orpd/AKir/AKOeAeII.htm).

### Path 28

Kircher prints the Latin “Natural Intelligence” text:

> *Vigesima octaua semita dicitur intelligentia naturalis…*

Kircher did not invent the underlying Hebrew sentence, but his numbered, diagrammatic presentation was decisive for the Western tradition.

### Difference from Jewish trees

| Feature | Ari-type | Gra-type | Kircher |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tzaddi line | Gevurah–Tiferet | Netzach–Malkhut | Netzach–Yesod |
| Malkhut connections | commonly one | commonly three in the reconstruction | three |
| Upper crossing-lines | differs from Kircher | differs spatially | fixed Western arrangement |
| Primary concern | Lurianic emanation/partzuf structure | *Yetzirah* spatial commentary | Christian universal synthesis |
| Later occult adoption | limited | limited | extensive |

**[DISPUTED]** Kircher’s precise line scheme has earlier European precedents and analogues, including Philippe d’Aquin and printed Cordoverian diagrams. Calling it simply “Kircher’s fabrication” is too strong. Calling it “the ancient Jewish Tree” is equally misleading.

### Christianizing purpose

Kircher presents Jewish materials inside an explicitly Catholic universal history. His preface treats rabbinic kabbalah polemically and seeks Christian meanings in it. The plate must therefore be read as Christian Cabala, not as a neutral facsimile of a Jewish manuscript.

## 5. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

*Kabbala Denudata* made large bodies of Zoharic and Lurianic material available in Latin. Its title is:

> *Kabbala denudata, seu doctrina Hebraeorum transcendentalis et metaphysica atque theologica.*

The work includes:

- Latin translations from Zoharic literature;
- Lurianic texts;
- Abraham Cohen de Herrera;
- Christian-Cabalist commentary;
- treatments of pure and impure enumerations.

Scans are available through [e-rara](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/10759091), [Internet Archive/Wikimedia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AKabbala_Denudata_Seu_Doctrina_Hebraeorum_Transcendentalis_Et_Metaphysica_Atque_Theologica_Opus_Antiquissimae_Philosophiae_Barbaricae_variis_speciminibus_refertissimum_%28IA_bub_gb_QzBBAAAAcAAJ%29.pdf), and the [Heidrick index](https://www.billheidrick.com/Orpd/KRKD/index.htm).

**Absence of evidence:** Rosenroth does not independently originate the complete modern Tzaddi correspondence. His importance is transmission: Mathers translated selected parts as *The Kabbalah Unveiled*, while Golden Dawn members mined Rosenroth’s Latin tables and qliphothic materials.

## 6. Éliphas Lévi, 1854–1856

Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* made the correspondence of twenty-two Hebrew letters and twenty-two tarot trumps central to occultism.

**[HERMETIC]** Lévi’s sequential scheme gives:

- Tzaddi, the eighteenth letter;
- Aquarius;
- tarot XVIII, the Moon.

This is not the Golden Dawn’s Star attribution. Lévi’s French text is available in a [public-domain scan](https://www.arbredor.com/ebooks/Dogme.pdf). Papus later reproduced the relevant table in *The Tarot of the Bohemians*: [Tzaddi–Aquarius–Moon](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/tob/tob34.htm).

Lévi’s decisive innovation was not the zodiac attribution—which came through *Sefer Yetzirah*—but systematic letter-to-trump correspondence.

## 7. The Golden Dawn, from 1888

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn synthesized Kircher, Lévi, *Sefer Yetzirah*, astrology, tarot, Enochian materials, Egyptian religion, Christian mysticism, and Renaissance magic.

### Standard attribution

**[HERMETIC]**

| Category | Golden Dawn Tzaddi |
|---|---|
| Key scale | 28 |
| Tree connection | Yesod to Netzach |
| Letter | צ, Tzaddi |
| Meaning | Fish-hook |
| Class | Simple |
| Zodiac | Aquarius |
| Elemental quality | Fixed Air |
| Classical ruler | Saturn |
| Trump | XVII, the Star |
| Title | Daughter of the Firmament; Dweller between the Waters |
| Yetziratic title | Natural Intelligence |
| King scale | Violet |
| Queen scale | Sky blue |
| Emperor scale | Bluish mauve |
| Empress scale | White tinged purple |

### Why the Star?

No surviving Golden Dawn paper gives a single rigorous historical proof. The reconstruction is:

1. Tzaddi was already Aquarius in *Sefer Yetzirah*.
2. The Star card conventionally depicts a woman pouring water.
3. Aquarius is the Water-Bearer, despite being an Air sign.
4. The Star’s number XVII was made to fit the letter after the Golden Dawn repositioned the Fool and adjusted Strength and Justice.
5. Kircher supplied the Netzach–Yesod line.

This is symbolic reasoning, not textual inheritance from one Jewish source.

### Knowledge Lectures and initiation

The Golden Dawn papers make Tzaddi an initiatory route. In the grade symbolism, the temple is in Yesod and the candidate is conducted by the Path of Tzaddi toward the Gate of Netzach. Regardie's compilation states:

> “The Temple is symbolically in YESOD, whence the Candidate is taken by the Path of TZADDI…”

See [*The Complete Golden Dawn*](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/R/Regardie%20-%20The%20Complete%20Golden%20Dawn.pdf) and the [Golden Dawn admission-badges paper](https://osogd.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/GDadmissionBadges.pdf).

### *Book T*

*Book T* gives the Star’s ritual title, imagery, divinatory values, and relation to Aquarius. The surviving form is available in this [Golden Dawn *Book T* scan](https://jantetteroo.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Mathers_and_felkin_golden_dawn_book_t_th.pdf).

The three Aquarius decans are:

| Aquarius degrees | Planetary decan | Golden Dawn minor |
|---|---|---|
| 0°–10° | Venus | Five of Swords, Lord of Defeat |
| 10°–20° | Mercury | Six of Swords, Lord of Earned Success |
| 20°–30° | Moon | Seven of Swords, Lord of Unstable Effort |

Crowley renamed the Six “Science” and the Seven “Futility” in the Thoth deck.

## 8. Crowley

### *Liber 777*, 1909

Crowley’s original *777* preserves the Golden Dawn correspondence:

- Tzaddi;
- path 28;
- Netzach–Yesod;
- Aquarius;
- the Star.

Thus *777* predates Crowley’s final Emperor solution. See [*777 Revised*](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/0777-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf).

### *Liber AL vel Legis* I:57, 1904

The famous passage says:

> “All these old letters of my Book are aright; but Tzaddi is not the Star.”

See [*Liber AL* I](https://thelema.study/?opbk=9&opch=1).

**[THELEMIC TRADITION]** Crowley regarded this as a revealed correction.

**[HISTORICAL FACT]** The sentence alone says what Tzaddi is not. It does not say “Tzaddi is the Emperor.” That conclusion was Crowley’s later interpretation.

### The Emperor–Star transposition

In *The Book of Thoth*, published in 1944, Crowley assigns:

- **Tzaddi to the Emperor**;
- **Heh to the Star**.

His stated rationale is zodiacal symmetry. Aries and Aquarius lie on either side of Pisces, corresponding to the loop already created around Virgo by the Golden Dawn’s Strength/Justice adjustment involving Leo and Libra.

See Crowley, [*The Book of Thoth*, Equinox III.5](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/crowley/equinox/volume3/vol_3_no_05.pdf), pp. 9–10 and the attribution tables.

**[DISPUTED]** Crowley’s prose and some tabular representations are not perfectly transparent about what is exchanged:

- the card images;
- the card numbers;
- the letters;
- the zodiac signs;
- or their positions on the tree.

The usual Thoth practice is:

- Emperor IV bears Tzaddi;
- Star XVII bears Heh;
- the resulting zodiacal/path associations are counterchanged.

Golden Dawn-derived orders generally retain Star–Tzaddi. Thoth practitioners generally follow Crowley.

### *Liber Tzaddi vel Hamus Hermeticus*, sub figura XC

The title means “Book of Tzaddi, or the Hermetic Fish-Hook, under figure 90.” It is an initiatory prose-poem about the Master drawing suitable souls, as a hook draws fish. It is not a historical source for the Jewish tree. See [*Liber Tzaddi*](https://sacred-texts.com/oto/lib90.htm).

### *Liber 231*

*Liber 231* gives a verse and sigil for each letter’s “cell” and a corresponding qliphothic spirit. The Tzaddi verse begins:

> “Transformed, the holy virgin appeared as a fluidic fire…”

See the [Tzaddi page of *Liber 231*](https://keepsilence.org/xxii/path-tzaddi.html) and the [facsimile PDF](https://93beast.fea.st.user.fm/files/holybooks/Liber%20Arcanorum.pdf).

The qliphothic name is **Tzuflifu**. This is the immediate textual ancestor of Kenneth Grant’s tunnel.

## 9. Dion Fortune, 1935

*The Mystical Qabalah* contains:

- a general chapter on the paths;
- individual chapters on each sefirah;
- chapters on the lower four sefirot and the qliphoth.

It does **not** contain an individual chapter on Tzaddi or path 28. Tzaddi is a letter-path, not a sefirah, so the requested “chapter on this Sephira in full” does not exist.

Fortune’s chapter 10 says that each path represents the equilibrium of the two sefirot it connects and receives a Hebrew letter, astrological attribution, and tarot symbol. That principle informs a Fortune-style reading of Tzaddi as the equilibrium between Netzach’s emotion/desire and Yesod’s image-forming psyche.

See [Dion Fortune, *The Mystical Qabalah*](https://www.wordwise.net.au/books-reading/fortune/mystical-qabalah/mystical-qabalah-archive-org-1998.pdf) and the [online text](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html).

## 10. Israel Regardie

Regardie’s publications preserved and popularized the Golden Dawn Tzaddi materials after the original order fragmented. Relevant works include:

- *The Golden Dawn*;
- *A Garden of Pomegranates*;
- later “skrying on the Tree” editions and exercises.

Regardie is a principal source for the public form of the Tzaddi initiation route, but his compilation dates decades after the original cipher manuscripts and order papers.

## 11. Paul Foster Case

Case and the Builders of the Adytum retain:

- Tzaddi;
- Aquarius;
- the Star;
- path 28, Netzach–Yesod.

His *Book of Tokens* presents meditations spoken by the letters. Its Tzaddi symbolism emphasizes the hook, meditation, revelation, and the relation between subconscious imagery and higher guidance. These are twentieth-century occult meditations, not ancient Hebrew letter etymology.

## 12. Gareth Knight

Gareth Knight’s *A Practical Guide to Qabalistic Symbolism*, volume II, interprets the path as an influx of creative imagination from Netzach into Yesod. The path’s power lies in the interaction of:

- Venusian desire, beauty, and natural force;
- lunar/subconscious form and image;
- the Star’s regenerative aspiration.

See [Knight, volume II](https://maestrosdelsaber.com/wp-content/uploads/ftp-files/Kabbalah/Kab%20en%20General/Kab%20Knight%2C%20Gareth%20-%20A%20Practical%20Guide%20to%20Qabalistic%20Symbolism%20Vol%202.pdf).

## 13. Pathworking traditions

### Golden Dawn method

A historically grounded Golden Dawn-style pathworking uses:

1. the path’s letter and number;
2. the four colours;
3. the Star image and Aquarius glyph;
4. divine or angelic names used to test a vision;
5. ascent from Yesod toward Netzach;
6. recording the vision for later symbolic comparison.

The practitioner is not merely imagining a tarot card but testing whether the imagery remains consistent with the established correspondence set.

### Later guided journeys

**[MODERN]** Later writers add:

- deserts and starry skies;
- pools, Eden, Adam and Eve;
- water-bearers and mermaids;
- sexual reconciliation;
- recovery of imagination after emotional crisis;
- astrological or Jungian “individuation.”

The Ciceros’ pathworking material is representative: [*Skrying on the Tree of Life*](https://logoilibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/skrying_on_the_tree_of_life-tabatha_cicero.pdf).

First-person reports such as the [Servants of the Light path page](https://www.servantsofthelight.org/QBL/Paths/28_tzaddi.html) and [Institute of Kabbalistic Studies archive](https://www.iok-kabbalah.org/trees/Tzaddi.html) document modern practice, not objective geography of a spiritual realm.

## 14. Grades

| Grade | Sefirah |
|---|---|
| Zelator 2°=9□ | Yesod |
| Practicus 3°=8□ | Hod |
| Philosophus 4°=7□ | Netzach |

Thus Tzaddi’s endpoints correspond to Zelator/Yesod and Philosophus/Netzach. It is not itself a separate A∴A∴ grade. See the [A∴A∴ grade manifesto](https://www.aa-thelema.org/manifesto).

# The Qliphah or tunnel

## Jewish shells versus the modern Nightside tree

**[TEXT/JEWISH TRADITION]** *Qelippah/qlippah* means peel, husk, or shell. Zoharic and Lurianic texts develop the *sitra achra*, the “other side,” and describe impure powers fed by separation, unbalanced judgment, or the residues of broken vessels.

**[HERMETIC]** Knorr, Mathers, and the Golden Dawn reorganized several Jewish lists into a symmetrical “averse Tree” with ten named qliphoth.

**[MODERN]** A complete twenty-two-tunnel underside mirroring the twenty-two paths is later still. Its decisive formulation belongs to Kenneth Grant.

## Tzuflifu

| Field | Nightside attribution |
|---|---|
| Tunnel number | 28 |
| Name | Tzuflifu, spellings vary |
| Letter | Tzaddi |
| Day-side path | Netzach–Yesod |
| Upper shell | A’arab Zaraq/Harab Serapel, shell of Netzach |
| Lower shell | Gamaliel, shell of Yesod |
| Source of name/sigil | Crowley, *Liber 231* |
| Full tunnel system | Kenneth Grant, *Nightside of Eden* |
| Status | Modern occult construction |

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* was published in 1977. He turned Crowley’s qliphothic cells into “Tunnels of Set,” visionary routes behind the conventional Tree. See the [scanned text](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf).

Later Grantian and Falorio-derived lists give Tzuflifu:

- gematria 302 under one spelling;
- a high-A vibratory pitch;
- apoplexy or explosive nervous force;
- an inverted or poisoned Aquarius current;
- sexualized, lunar, and stellar imagery.

These details are **not** found in the Zohar, Luria, Cordovero, or the classical Jewish demonologies.

## Adjacent qliphoth

### A’arab Zaraq / Harab Serapel

The Netzach shell is represented as ravens of dispersion or forces of lust and scattered desire. Later lists give **Baal** as its demon prince.

### Gamaliel

The Yesod shell is associated with obscene or deceptive dream-images, polluted sexuality, and the dark lunar current. Later lists place it under **Lilith**.

### Tzuflifu’s status

Tzuflifu is therefore not the “Jewish demon of Tzaddi.” Its genealogy is:

> Crowley’s *Liber 231* name and sigil → Kenneth Grant’s tunnel cosmology → Linda Falorio and later Nightside pathworking.

# People, works, images, and cultural reception

## Jewish kabbalists and scholars

### Saadia Gaon

His tenth-century commentary preserves a distinct recension in which Tzaddi is Aquarius, Shevat, thought, and the right foot. His philosophical interpretation resists reading the work as a simple magical manual.

### Joseph Gikatilla

He provides the decisive *tzaddik–Yesod* biblical theology, especially through Proverbs 10:25 and the name El Chai.

### Moses Cordovero

He systematizes the paths while preserving evidence of competing path arrangements.

### Isaac Luria and Chaim Vital

Their importance is cosmological—worlds, vessels, partzufim, shattering, and repair—not the Hermetic path-28 package.

### Elijah of Vilna

His recension and commentary generated an alternative spatial reconstruction in which Tzaddi does not occupy Kircher’s Netzach–Yesod line.

### Hasidic masters

Chabad and other Hasidic schools develop Shevat, taste, Asher, and rectified eating. Rabbi Nachman develops the *tzaddik* as spiritual channel. Neither tradition adopts tarot.

### Modern scholarship

- **Gershom Scholem** established critical histories of the Zohar, early kabbalah, and Christian Cabala.
- **Moshe Idel** complicated linear Scholemian narratives by emphasizing multiple ecstatic, theosophical, linguistic, and magical streams.
- **Elliot Wolfson** has examined language, embodiment, gender, and visionary experience, especially relevant to Zoharic letter symbolism.
- **Joseph Dan** surveyed the history and literary forms of Jewish mysticism.
- **Peter Hayman** produced the major synoptic critical edition of *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- **Tzahi Weiss** argues for an early Islamic and magical-intellectual setting for the work’s formation.
- **Daniel Matt** produced the Pritzker Zohar translation from a reconstructed Aramaic text.
- **J. H. Chajes** studies the history of kabbalistic diagrams and *ilanot*.

None of these scholars treats the Golden Dawn’s Tzaddi–Star path as an ancient Jewish unity.

## Christian Cabalists and occultists

- **Pico della Mirandola:** Christian use of Hebrew letters and kabbalah, 1486.
- **Johannes Reuchlin:** systematic Christian Cabala, 1517.
- **Athanasius Kircher:** decisive diagram and Latin path list, 1652–54.
- **Knorr von Rosenroth:** Latin transmission of Zoharic and Lurianic materials, 1677–84.
- **Éliphas Lévi:** letters plus tarot trumps; Tzaddi–Moon, 1850s.
- **MacGregor Mathers:** Golden Dawn tables and translations.
- **W. Wynn Westcott:** English *Sefer Yetzirah* and “Natural Intelligence.”
- **A. E. Waite:** tarot and Christian-mystical interpretations.
- **Aleister Crowley:** *777*, “Tzaddi is not the Star,” the Emperor swap, *Liber Tzaddi*, and *Liber 231*.
- **Paul Foster Case:** BOTA Star–Tzaddi system.
- **Dion Fortune:** psychological and magical Tree interpretation.
- **Israel Regardie:** public preservation of Golden Dawn papers.
- **Gareth Knight:** modern path symbolism and creative imagination.
- **Kenneth Grant:** Tunnel of Tzuflifu.
- **Linda Falorio:** visual and experiential Shadow Tarot tunnels.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Dating *Sefer Yetzirah*

No consensus fixes one date or author.

- **Traditional:** Abraham or Rabbi Akiva.
- **Older scholarly range:** approximately second–sixth century.
- **Recent redactional proposals:** substantial formation in the seventh–ninth centuries.
- **Secure evidence:** tenth-century commentaries and manuscript fragments.

Any claim that a second-century *Sefer Yetzirah* already contained Kircher’s diagram is unsupported.

## 2. Zohar authorship

- **Traditional:** Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and companions.
- **Scholem:** Moses de León in late-thirteenth-century Castile.
- **More recent scholarship:** de León remains central, but the corpus is stratified and may reflect a literary circle.

The issue concerns historical authorship, not the spiritual authority assigned by practitioners.

## 3. Are the “Thirty-two Intelligences” part of *Sefer Yetzirah*?

The answer is no in the strict critical sense. The list was transmitted with the work and became traditional, but it is a separate medieval textual layer. “Natural Intelligence” should be cited to that list, not casually to the earliest *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## 4. What does Tzaddi mean?

“Fish-hook” is possible but unproved. Plant/papyrus and other interpretations remain in play. Occult symbolism built on the hook is meaningful within the occult system even if the paleographic etymology proves wrong.

## 5. Which tree is correct?

There is no historically neutral answer.

- Ari-type trees reflect Lurianic concerns.
- Gra-type trees interpret *Sefer Yetzirah* differently.
- Cordoverian sources preserve multiple arrangements.
- Kircher’s plate became the standard Western occult tree.
- Golden Dawn work assumes Kircher’s geometry.

A practitioner can choose a system; a historian must name it.

## 6. Did Kircher invent the Western tree?

Kircher did not invent sefirotic diagrams or every feature of his plate. Earlier Jewish and Christian trees existed. But his particular printed arrangement became the main ancestor of the Golden Dawn tree. “Kircherian” is therefore a valid genealogical description even if “invented entirely by Kircher” is not.

## 7. Star, Moon, or Emperor?

| System | Tzaddi trump |
|---|---|
| Éliphas Lévi/Papus sequential system | Moon XVIII |
| Golden Dawn, Waite, Case, Regardie | Star XVII |
| Crowley/Thoth | Emperor IV |

The zodiacal Tzaddi–Aquarius association is older than all three tarot mappings. The card assignment is the disputed layer.

## 8. “Tzaddi is not the Star”

Crowley’s verse only negates an attribution. The Emperor is his inferred solution. Alternative Thelemic interpretations include:

- a literal card swap;
- a switch of paths but not signs;
- a switch of cards and signs;
- a metaphysical rather than tabular correction;
- retention of the Golden Dawn arrangement despite the verse.

No manuscript evidence shows that an ancient Jewish authority anticipated Crowley’s correction.

## 9. Saturn or Uranus?

Aquarius’s traditional ruler is Saturn. Uranus was discovered in 1781 and assigned to Aquarius by modern astrologers. Any table presenting Uranus as the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah* planet is an anachronism.

## 10. Tribe Asher or Reuben?

- Asher belongs to a later Jewish month/tribe scheme for Shevat.
- Reuben appears in Golden Dawn zodiacal tables.

Neither attribution appears in the core Tzaddi sentence of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## 11. Divine name and archangel

Tzaddi has no unique classical Jewish divine name, archangel, or angelic order. Lists that provide one usually:

- borrow from Aquarius;
- borrow from Netzach or Yesod;
- import a zodiacal angel list;
- or synthesize several occult tables.

Cambriel should be labeled an Aquarius archangel in a Hermetic table, not “the ancient archangel of Tzaddi.”

## 12. Da’at

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists on “ten and not nine, ten and not eleven.” Da’at is nevertheless important in later kabbalah:

- as knowledge or conjunction;
- as a functional state when Keter is not counted;
- as a partzufic or consciousness-related position;
- in Hermetic diagrams as an “invisible sefirah.”

Da’at is not simply an eleventh sefirah added to the original ten. Its ambiguous diagrammatic status also affects discussions of path layouts above Tiferet, although it does not directly determine the lower Tzaddi line.

## 13. Christian Cabala and appropriation

Pico, Reuchlin, Kircher, and Rosenroth preserved, translated, and disseminated Jewish materials. They also:

- reframed them as Christian revelation;
- used them to support Trinity and Christological arguments;
- detached them from Jewish law, language communities, and ritual life;
- occasionally relied on tendentious translations.

Both transmission and appropriation are historically real. A responsible Hermetic history should acknowledge its Jewish sources without pretending that the resulting occult system is identical with Jewish kabbalah.

## 14. Qliphothic princes

The ten-shell, one-demon-prince-per-shell table is not a single Zoharic list. It is a layered synthesis of:

- Jewish *sitra achra* and shell traditions;
- Edomite-king symbolism;
- Rosenroth;
- Mathers and Golden Dawn papers;
- European grimoire demonology;
- Crowley and later occultists.

Tzuflifu’s tunnel is later still.

# Open questions

1. **Which manuscript first places the Hebrew Tzaddi explicitly on the precise Netzach–Yesod connector?** Kircher fixes path number 28 there, but the exact transition from numbered line to printed Hebrew letter deserves manuscript-level comparison.

2. **How much of Kircher’s line arrangement came through Philippe d’Aquin, Cordoverian diagrams, or other intermediaries?** The genealogy is more complicated than a single act of invention.

3. **What is the earliest secure witness to the complete “Thirty-two Intelligences” text?** Its medieval circulation is clear, but its precise redactional history needs a dedicated manuscript study.

4. **How should the different Ari and Gra diagrams be related to the wording of their *Sefer Yetzirah* recensions?** Modern diagrams may make geometric choices that the texts themselves leave underdetermined.

5. **What is the original referent of the Proto-Sinaitic ancestor of ṣādē?** “Fish-hook” remains possible but unproved.

6. **Which recension of *Otiyot de-Rabbi Akiva* first gives a Tzaddi episode comparable to the Zoharic letter contest?** Online retellings frequently conflate sources.

7. **What exactly did the earliest Golden Dawn cipher-manuscript compilers inherit, and what did Mathers and Westcott systematize?** The cipher manuscripts’ provenance and extent of later elaboration remain disputed.

8. **How should Crowley’s prose explanation be reconciled with conflicting readings of the tables in *The Book of Thoth*?** This remains an internal Thelemic interpretive question.

9. **Which *777 Revised* entries are Crowley’s own corrections, which are editorial additions, and which are transcription errors?** Colour-table discrepancies show the need for edition control.

10. **How much of Grant’s Tzuflifu material comes from Crowley, personal visionary practice, unpublished Typhonian sources, or later reconstruction?** *Liber 231* securely supplies the name and sigil, but not the complete tunnel mythology.

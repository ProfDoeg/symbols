# Tiphareth: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary labels

This dossier keeps three historical strata separate:

- **[DT] Documented text:** stated in the cited primary source.
- **[KT] Kabbalistic tradition:** a traditional Jewish interpretation, whether or not accepted as historical fact.
- **[SR] Scholarly reconstruction:** modern philological or historical analysis.
- **[HC] Hermetic/Christian-occult attribution:** a correspondence created or transmitted within Christian Cabala, ceremonial magic, the Golden Dawn, Thelema, or related currents.
- **[D] Disputed:** the sources or schools disagree.
- **[MI] Modern invention/development:** demonstrably late, usually nineteenth or twentieth century.
- **[AE] Absence of evidence:** the requested attribution was not found in the early source to which it is sometimes retrospectively assigned.

“Jewish Kabbalah,” “Christian Cabala,” and “Hermetic Qabalah” are used historically, not as interchangeable spellings of one system.

## Basic identification

| Field | Jewish kabbalistic tradition | Hermetic-occult tradition |
|---|---|---|
| Name | תִּפְאֶרֶת, unpointed תפארת | Tiphereth, Tiphareth, Tifereth |
| Number | Sixth sefirah | Key Scale 6 |
| Literal sense | Beauty, glory, splendor, adornment | Beauty; harmony or equilibrium |
| Position | Central or middle line; between Ḥesed and Gevurah and above Yesod/Malkhut | Center of the Middle Pillar; center of the ethical triad |
| Principal quality | Raḥamim, compassion; Emet, truth; balance of Ḥesed and Din | Solar consciousness; equilibrium; sacrificed or redeeming god |
| Biblical person | Jacob/Israel | Jacob; later also Christ, Osiris, Adonis, Dionysus |
| Divine name | Usually יהוה, YHWH, in *Shaʿarei Orah* | יהוה אלוה ודעת, commonly written YHVH Eloah va-Daʿath |
| Archangel | No universally canonical sefirah-archangel table in the earliest texts | Raphael in Agrippa/Golden Dawn; Michael in some planetary-solar systems |
| Angelic order | No universal early Jewish tenfold table | Malakhim/Malachim, “angels” or “kings”; equated with the Christian choir of Virtues |
| Planetary sphere | Solar imagery is ancient; a rigid Sun correspondence is principally Renaissance/Hermetic | Shemesh, the Sun |
| Body | Torso, body, heart, breast, or central trunk, depending on the source | Breast, heart region, solar plexus |
| Yetziratic title | Not in the core *Sefer Yetzirah* | “Mediating Intelligence,” from the late “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom” |
| Golden Dawn grade | Not applicable | 5°=6, Adeptus Minor |
| Tarot | Not applicable | Four Sixes; no single trump because Tiphareth is a sefirah, not a letter-path |
| Qliphah | No classical Jewish one-for-one qliphah named “Thagirion” opposite Tiferet | Thagirion/Tageriron/Tagiriron, “Disputers” |
| Demon ruler | Not applicable | Belphegor in many later lists; Sorath in competing solar-demonic lists; neither is secure in the foundational table |
| Tunnel | Not applicable | No single tunnel belongs to the sphere; Kenneth Grant placed eight tunnels around Thagirion according to the paths adjoining Tiphareth |

**Central finding:** the compound YHVH Eloah va-Daʿath–Raphael–Malachim–Sun complex is not the presentation of Tiferet in the *Sefer Yetzirah*, *Bahir*, or *Zohar*. Its recognizable genealogy runs through Renaissance Christian Cabala and Agrippa, then Kircher/Rosenroth, the Golden Dawn, Crowley, and Dion Fortune.

# The name

## Hebrew form, pronunciation, and etymology

**[DT]** The consonantal spelling is:

> תפארת

Pointed in ordinary modern Hebrew:

> תִּפְאֶרֶת — *tifʾéret*

In pausal or some biblical forms:

> תִּפְאָרֶת — *tifʾāret*

Academic transliterations include *tip̄ʾereṯ* or *tifʾeret*. Early-modern Latin writers normally used *Tiphereth*; English occultists also used *Tiphareth*. Neither spelling represents a separate Hebrew word.

The noun derives from the root פאר, p-ʾ-r, “to beautify, adorn, glorify.” Its biblical semantic range includes beauty, glory, magnificence, distinction, ornament, and splendid attire. Brown–Driver–Briggs gives “beauty, splendour, glory.” The consonantal gematria is:

- ת = 400
- פ = 80
- א = 1
- ר = 200
- ת = 400
- Total: **1081**

The number 1081 is philological arithmetic, not the reason Tiferet is sefirah six.

Lexical sources: [BDB entry for תפארת](https://lemma.gebible.app/w/H8597), [Strong/BDB H8597](https://bible.by/strong/hebrew/8597/dict/brown-driver-briggs/).

## Biblical bases

### 1 Chronicles 29:11

**[DT]** This is the direct textual source for the conventional names of the seven lower sefirot:

> לְךָ יְהוָה הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה וְהַתִּפְאֶרֶת וְהַנֵּצַח וְהַהוֹד כִּי־כֹל בַּשָּׁמַיִם וּבָאָרֶץ לְךָ יְהוָה הַמַּמְלָכָה

“To You, YHWH, belong greatness, power, beauty, victory, and majesty; for all in heaven and earth is Yours; Yours is kingship.”

Later kabbalists read:

- *gedullah* as Ḥesed;
- *gevurah* as Gevurah;
- *tiferet* as Tiferet;
- *neẓaḥ* as Netzach;
- *hod* as Hod;
- *kol*, “all,” as Yesod;
- *mamlakhah*, “kingship,” as Malkhut.

That sevenfold reading is **[KT]**, not the plain historical meaning of the Chronicler. Gikatilla gives it systematic form in *Shaʿarei Orah*. [Hebrew verse and linked kabbalistic commentaries](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Chronicles.29.11?with=Kabbalah).

### Proverbs 3:19–20

**[DT]** Proverbs supplies the triad of Wisdom, Understanding, and Knowledge:

> יְהוָה בְּחָכְמָה יָסַד־אָרֶץ כּוֹנֵן שָׁמַיִם בִּתְבוּנָה׃ בְּדַעְתּוֹ תְּהוֹמוֹת נִבְקָעוּ

“YHWH founded the earth by wisdom; He established the heavens by understanding; by His knowledge the depths burst apart.”

Later kabbalah reads Ḥokhmah, Binah/Tevunah, and Daʿat through this vocabulary. It is not the direct source of the word *Tiferet*. [Proverbs 3:19–20](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.3.19).

### Job 28

**[DT/AE]** Job 28 is a sustained meditation on the inaccessible source of wisdom and understanding—“Where shall wisdom be found?”—but it does not name Tiferet as a sefirah. It became part of the wider sapiential vocabulary from which kabbalists discussed Ḥokhmah and Binah. Claims that Job 28 directly establishes Tiferet are retrospective. [Job 28](https://www.sefaria.org/Job.28).

### Isaiah

The noun *tiferet* occurs repeatedly in Isaiah:

- Isaiah 28:5: עֲטֶרֶת צְבִי … צְפִירַת תִּפְאָרָה, a “crown of beauty.”
- Isaiah 46:13: וּתְפאַרְתִּי בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, “My glory in Israel.”
- Isaiah 60:7: בֵּית תִּפְאַרְתִּי אֲפָאֵר, “I will glorify My glorious house.”

These provide a biblical field of glory, kingship, sanctuary, and Israel. They do not by themselves describe a sixth emanation. [Isaiah 28:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.28.5), [46:13](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.46.13), [60:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.60.7).

# The Jewish tradition

## 1. *Sefer Yetzirah*

### Date and recensions

**[D/SR]** Traditional attributions assign the work to Abraham, sometimes with transmission through Akiva. Historical scholarship does not accept Abrahamic authorship.

A firm terminus ante quem is Saadia Gaon’s Arabic commentary, completed about 931 CE. The received text is variously dated from late antiquity to the early Islamic period. Peter Hayman’s textual study emphasizes a fluid work assembled and redacted across manuscripts rather than a single recoverable authorial edition.

The principal textual families are:

- the **Short Recension**;
- the **Long Recension**;
- the **Saadian Recension**, preserved with Saadia’s commentary;
- later kabbalistically harmonized **Ari** versions;
- the eighteenth-century **Gra** recension/redaction associated with Elijah of Vilna.

The labels “Ari version” and “Gra version” refer to late editorial and commentarial traditions, not first-millennium autograph texts. See Peter Hayman’s critical edition preview and the Hebrew University study of the earliest recension: [Hayman, *Sefer Yeṣira: Edition, Translation and Text-Critical Commentary*](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783161587955_A40613273/preview-9783161587955_A40613273.pdf), [“The Earliest Version of Sefer Yeṣirah”](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-earliest-version-of-sefer-yeirah-text-format-structure-and-ge/).

### The thirty-two paths

**[DT]** The foundational classification is:

> עשר ספירות בלימה ועשרים ושתים אותיות יסוד שלש אמות ושבע כפולות ושתים עשרה פשוטות

“Ten sefirot belimah and twenty-two foundation letters: three mothers, seven doubles, and twelve simples.”

The thirty-two are therefore:

- ten *sefirot belimah*;
- three mother letters: א מ ש;
- seven double letters: ב ג ד כ פ ר ת;
- twelve simple letters: ה ו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק.

[Sefer Yetzirah 1](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.12?with=Commentary), [letter divisions](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.4.11).

### Is the sixth sefirah Tiferet?

**[AE]** No. The core text does not call any one of its ten sefirot “Tiferet.” It does not present the later sequence Keter–Ḥokhmah–Binah–Ḥesed–Gevurah–Tiferet.

Its ten are presented as:

- spirit of the living God;
- spirit from spirit;
- water from spirit;
- fire from water;
- six spatial extremities.

One recension summarizes:

> “Up above and below, east and west, north and south.”

Later kabbalists mapped the six lower named sefirot onto these six directions. A common later mapping makes Tiferet the east. That is **[KT]**, not the explicit terminology of the early text. [Sefer Yetzirah 1:5 with later commentary](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Yetzirah.1.5?with=The+Beginning+of+Wisdom).

### “Mediating Intelligence”

**[AE/HC]** The phrase is not part of the core *Sefer Yetzirah*. It belongs to a separate medieval or early-modern list commonly called the “Thirty-two Paths of Wisdom,” later appended to editions and translations of *Sefer Yetzirah*.

Westcott’s occult translation reads:

> “The Sixth Path is called the Intelligence of Mediating Influence.”

He continues that the “influxes of the emanations” are multiplied in it and communicated to “reservoirs of blessing.” This became Tiphareth’s standard Golden Dawn “Yetziratic text.” Calling it the teaching of the ancient *Sefer Yetzirah* without qualification is misleading. [Westcott’s *Sepher Yetzirah* and appended Thirty-two Paths](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/yetzirah.htm).

## 2. *Sefer ha-Bahir*

### Date

**[SR/D]** The *Bahir* first appears in Provence in the late twelfth century. Tradition attributes it to the tannaitic sage Neḥunya ben ha-Qanah. Scholem argued that it contains older eastern materials reorganized in medieval Provence; more recent scholarship has revisited the extent of those older strata.

### The sixth power

**[DT]** In one numbering, the sixth is described as:

> “the Throne of Glory, adorned, inclusive, praised, blessed—the house of the world to come.”

The passage then associates it with the primordial command “Let there be light.” It does **not** call this potency Tiferet by name. [Hebrew/French *Bahir*, §146](https://www.kabbale.eu/wp-content/files/Sefer_Habbahir.pdf).

Elsewhere the *Bahir* establishes material later joined to Tiferet:

- Isaac is associated with fear;
- Jacob with אמת, truth;
- Jacob’s measure is peace;
- “the Torah of truth” lies within Israel’s measure.

[Bahir §192](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaBahir.192).

**Finding:** the *Bahir* is an important predecessor of the Tiferet complex, but descriptions saying that it already presents the fully stabilized sixth sefirah Tiferet overstate the evidence.

## 3. Gerona and Castilian Kabbalah

In the thirteenth century the sefirotic system became more stable. Azriel of Gerona and other early theosophical kabbalists described a middle, harmonizing emanation. Tiferet increasingly came to be identified with:

- the middle line;
- compassion;
- the Written Torah;
- Jacob/Israel;
- truth;
- heaven or firmament;
- the masculine divine manifestation paired with Malkhut/Shekhinah.

The precise vocabulary varies between authors. “Tiferet” was not born as a complete package containing Sun, Raphael, Malachim, a Golden Dawn color scale, and Christ; those were accumulated in different textual cultures.

## 4. Joseph Gikatilla, *Shaʿarei Orah*

### Date and structure

*Shaʿarei Orah*, “Gates of Light,” was written in Castile in the late thirteenth century, probably during the 1280s or 1290s. It proceeds upward from Malkhut through divine names.

The fifth gate concerns the **sixth sefirah, Tiferet**.

### Divine name

**[DT]** Gikatilla’s principal divine name for Tiferet is **יהוה, YHWH**, not the later compound YHVH Eloah va-Daʿath.

Tiferet is the middle line and is associated with:

- YHWH;
- עץ חיים, Tree of Life;
- אמת, truth;
- דעת, knowledge;
- יעקב and ישראל;
- the Written Torah;
- the letter ו of YHWH;
- heaven, firmament, east, sun-rising, and the central trunk.

Gikatilla explains that the Vav contains the six directions or lower potencies. [*Shaʿarei Orah*, Fifth Gate](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Fifth_Gate%2C_Sixth_Sefirah.198), [Tiferet as the central YHWH line](https://www.sefaria.org/Sha%27arei_Orah%2C_Fifth_Gate%2C_Sixth_Sefirah.29?with=Tanakh).

### Jacob and Torah

**[DT/KT]** Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob become vehicles of Ḥesed, Gevurah, and Tiferet. Micah 7:20—“Give truth to Jacob, kindness to Abraham”—supports the Jacob/Tiferet/Truth relation. Tiferet is also “Written Torah,” while Malkhut is associated with oral manifestation.

### Angels and planets

**[AE]** *Shaʿarei Orah* does not present the later fixed table “Tiferet = Raphael = Malachim = Sun” in Golden Dawn form. It contains rich angelology and solar imagery, but the rigid four-world hierarchy belongs to a later synthesis.

## 5. The *Zohar*

### Authorship and date

- **[KT]** Traditional attribution: Shimon bar Yoḥai and his circle, second century CE.
- **[SR]** Gershom Scholem argued that Moses de León was the principal author/redactor in late thirteenth-century Castile.
- **[SR/D]** Current scholarship usually speaks of a heterogeneous “Zoharic corpus” produced by a circle or successive redactions rather than a single simple author.

A useful scholarly overview is the UCL study of de León and the composite corpus: [Zohar authorship research](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10088406/).

### Tiferet in the Zoharic system

**[DT]** Tiferet is frequently:

- the middle column between right and left;
- Raḥamim, compassion;
- Jacob or Israel;
- אמת, truth;
- the Written Torah;
- heaven;
- the Holy One, blessed be He, in relation to the Shekhinah;
- the masculine aspect joined to Malkhut;
- the central body or torso;
- the unifying center of the six lower masculine sefirot.

The *Zohar* calls Jacob’s beauty comparable to Adam’s and associates him with the “clear mirror.” [Zohar, Vayishlaḥ](https://www.sefaria.org/Zohar%2C_Vayishlach.2.39).

### Sun and Son

Solar imagery is real and early in the Zoharic complex: Tiferet/Zeʿir Anpin can be symbolized by the sun, with Malkhut as moon. But “Son” must be handled carefully:

- **[DT/KT]** Tiferet or Zeʿir Anpin may function as the “son” generated by Ḥokhmah and Binah.
- **[AE]** It is not Jesus or the second person of a Trinity.
- **[HC]** Christian Cabalists transformed the son-language into Christology.

### Raphael

Some Zoharic passages arrange Raphael as a middle-line angel between Michael and Gabriel, and later Jewish sources associate Raphael with healing, the west, or the Sun. But this does not yield a universally standardized sefirah-archangel chart. Michael, Gabriel, Uriel/Nuriel, and Raphael change positions between Jewish angelological schemes.

## 6. Moses Cordovero

### *Pardes Rimmonim* (completed 1548)

Cordovero’s *Pardes Rimmonim* is a comprehensive synthesis of earlier Kabbalah. It was completed in Safed in 1548 and first printed at Kraków in 1591/92.

#### Balance and compassion

**[DT]** Tiferet integrates the white of Ḥesed and red of Gevurah. Cordovero describes its color as:

- red and white together;
- green, understood as their mixture;
- argaman, purple, as a symbolic inclusion of multiple angelic colors.

[Pardes Rimmonim 10:3:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.10.3.6).

Tiferet is the compassionate mean, not a neutral compromise. It transmits an ordered judgment suffused with mercy.

#### Jacob, Moses, body, and soul

Cordovero distinguishes aspects within Tiferet:

- Jacob represents an external or embodied aspect;
- Moses an interior or ensouled aspect;
- Jacob is the vehicle of the light of Tiferet revealed in Malkhut.

[Pardes Rimmonim 22:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.22.3).

#### The six extremities

Cordovero writes that Tiferet is always inclusive of the six extremities and is like a tree amid its branches. East corresponds to Tiferet because “the sun rises” there and illuminates the other directions. [Pardes Rimmonim 21:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.21.6).

#### Colors versus Golden Dawn colors

Cordovero’s red-white-green/purple symbolism is a Jewish contemplative color theory. The Golden Dawn’s four scales—rose pink, yellow, salmon, amber—are not simply Cordovero’s colors translated into English.

### *Or Neʿerav*

Cordovero’s introductory *Or Neʿerav* explains the descent of emanation through the sefirot and the contemplative return upward. Tiferet is a central transmitter in both movements. It also explicitly denies that Daʿat must be an independent eleventh sefirah:

> “The aforementioned Daʿat is not itself a sefirah.”

Instead, Daʿat may be counted in place of Keter to preserve the total ten. [*Or Neʿerav* VI.1](https://www.sefaria.org/Ohr_Ne%27erav%2C_PART_VI.1.2).

## 7. Luria through Ḥayyim Vital

### Historical setting

Isaac Luria, the Ari (1534–1572), left relatively little systematic writing. His teachings survive chiefly in recensions compiled by disciples, especially Ḥayyim Vital. *Etz Ḥayyim* is therefore Lurianic teaching through Vital’s editorial tradition.

### Four worlds

The standard four worlds are:

- אצילות, Atzilut, Emanation;
- בריאה, Beriah, Creation;
- יצירה, Yetzirah, Formation;
- עשיה, Assiah, Action.

Each world has its own sefirotic and partzufic articulations. The later occult claim that Tiphareth possesses one fixed color “in each world” is a Golden Dawn color doctrine, not Vital’s formulation.

### Partzufim and Zeʿir Anpin

**[DT]** In *Etz Ḥayyim* the Vav of YHWH comprises six sefirot:

- Ḥesed;
- Gevurah;
- Tiferet;
- Netzach;
- Hod;
- Yesod.

Collectively they form **Zeʿir Anpin**, the “Lesser Countenance.” The final Heh is Nukva, the feminine counterpart. [*Etz Chaim* 1:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.1.5).

Tiferet is thus both:

1. one sefirah within Zeʿir Anpin; and  
2. in looser usage, the representative center or name of the whole sixfold configuration.

Saying “Tiferet is Zeʿir Anpin” can therefore be true contextually but is not an exact identity in every technical passage.

### Shevirat ha-kelim

**[KT/DT]** Lurianic myth describes primordial vessels unable to contain the emitted light. The vessels associated with the kings of Edom break or fall; sparks descend and become entangled in qelippot.

*Etz Ḥayyim* treats different degrees of breaking above and below the chest of Zeʿir Anpin. The lower two-thirds of Tiferet and the lower sefirot participate in the collapse differently from the more concealed upper portion. [*Etz Chaim* 9:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_Etz_Chaim.9.2).

### Tikkun

Tikkun is the reconfiguration of isolated points into relational partzufim capable of receiving and transmitting light. In ethical-religious life, commandments, prayer, intention, Torah study, and human action raise sparks and contribute to divine integration.

**[AE]** The Lurianic doctrine of qelippot is not a neat inverted chart in which each sefirah has exactly one demon prince. That tenfold demonological table is principally Hermetic.

## 8. The Gra’s tree

Elijah ben Solomon Zalman, the Vilna Gaon or Gra (1720–1797), produced a highly influential commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah*. The “Gra tree” organizes letter classes geometrically:

- three mother letters on three horizontal axes;
- seven doubles on seven vertical connections;
- twelve simples on twelve diagonals.

This differs fundamentally from Kircher’s system, which distributes Aleph through Tav largely in serial order down one particular network.

The Gra’s arrangement is a late Jewish reconstruction of *Sefer Yetzirah’s* classifications, not an image printed in the earliest manuscripts of the work. Aryeh Kaplan’s edition reproduces and explains this family of diagrams: [Kaplan, *Sefer Yetzirah*](https://leonbahrmanministries.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Aryeh-Kaplan-Sefer-Yetzirah2.pdf).

Tiferet remains central, but the letters on its connecting channels are not the Golden Dawn’s eight letters.

## 9. Hasidic readings

### *Tanya*

There is no main chapter of *Likkutei Amarim* formally titled “Tiferet.” The most explicit systematic discussion occurs in **Iggeret ha-Kodesh 15**, published with the posthumous sections of *Tanya*.

**[DT]** Shneur Zalman describes:

- Ḥesed as unbounded diffusion;
- Gevurah as withholding;
- Raḥamim as compassion toward one requiring mercy;
- the blend of diverse qualities as *tiferet*, like a beautiful multicolored garment.

[Tanya, Iggeret ha-Kodesh 15](https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya%2C_Part_IV%3B_Iggeret_HaKodesh.15.15).

Thus “Tanya’s chapter on Tiferet” is a reasonable thematic description of this letter, but not the title of one of the numbered fifty-three chapters of Part I.

### Naḥman of Bratslav

Naḥman repeatedly emphasizes truth, compassion, Torah, prayer, and the ẓaddik—motifs traditionally associated with Tiferet. In *Likkutei Moharan* II.8, compassion depends on daʿat and prayer awakens compassion. [Likkutei Moharan II.8](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan%2C_Part_II.8.1-15).

**[D]** Not every such passage explicitly says “Tiferet.” Later Bratslav interpreters often supply the sefirotic identification. It should not be presented as if every use of truth or compassion is a technical Tiferet reference.

## 10. Prayer, Torah, Omer, and calendar

### Prayer

There is no single universally binding Jewish rule that one prayer or blessing “is Tiferet.” Kabbalistic prayer manuals assign different sefirotic intentions to:

- names in the Shema;
- blessings of the Amidah;
- bodily gestures;
- the daily services;
- parts of Psalms and sacrificial readings.

In broad Zoharic and Lurianic symbolism, prayer helps unite Tiferet/Zeʿir Anpin with Malkhut/Shekhinah. Exact kavvanot depend on the rite and manual.

### Torah

Tiferet is frequently the **Written Torah**, the harmonizing line that contains right and left. Jacob, אמת, and Torah become mutually explanatory symbols. Cordovero says Jacob’s Torah activity makes him a vehicle of the Tiferet-light revealed to Malkhut. [Pardes Rimmonim 22:3](https://www.sefaria.org/Pardes_Rimmonim.22.3).

### Counting the Omer

In the seven-by-seven kabbalistic Omer scheme:

- week one: Ḥesed;
- week two: Gevurah;
- **week three: Tiferet**;
- week four: Netzach;
- week five: Hod;
- week six: Yesod;
- week seven: Malkhut.

The third week contains Ḥesed-shebe-Tiferet through Malkhut-shebe-Tiferet. The seventeenth day is Tiferet-shebe-Tiferet. The Torah commands the counting in Leviticus 23:15–16 and Deuteronomy 16:9–10; the detailed sefirotic matrix is later kabbalistic practice. [Rabbinical Assembly Omer guide](https://www.rabbinicalassembly.org/sites/default/files/2021-03/Counting%20the%20Omer%20SIDDUR%20LEV%20SHALEM.pdf).

### Month and tribe

**[AE]** Tiferet has no intrinsic month or tribe in *Sefer Yetzirah*. Month-and-tribe correspondences belong to the twelve simple letters. Assigning one month or tribe to Tiferet as a sphere mixes the sefirah table with the letter table.

# The Hermetic and occult tradition

## 1. Pico della Mirandola, 1486

Pico’s *Conclusiones* contains forty-seven conclusions “according to the secret doctrine of the Hebrew Cabalists” and seventy-two “according to my own opinion.”

**[DT]** Pico coordinates the human soul with ten sefirot:

> “through free will with the sixth.”

He does not name the sixth as *Tiphereth* in that conclusion. [Pico’s *900 Theses*](https://www.alchemy-texts.com/book/900-theses/).

Pico’s larger project claims that Cabala confirms Christian mysteries. He did not yet provide the later complete Tiphareth correspondence table.

**[SR]** Pico depended substantially on translations prepared by Flavius Mithridates. His “Cabala” included genuine Jewish texts, mistranslations, Christianizing constructions, and materials not representative of mainstream theosophical Kabbalah. [Stanford Encyclopedia on Pico](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/).

## 2. Reuchlin, 1494 and 1517

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De verbo mirifico* (1494) and *De arte cabalistica* (1517) transmitted Cabala into Christian humanism.

Reuchlin uses forms such as **Tiphereth** and explains their beauty/glory sense. He interprets the middle of the sefirotic structure through Christian and Pythagorean categories and develops “son-world” or filial language compatible with Christological reading.

**[HC]** This is an important stage in converting the Jewish symbol of the divine son/Zeʿir Anpin into a Christian “Son.” It is not evidence that medieval Jewish kabbalists secretly meant Jesus.

Primary and scholarly editions: [Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica*](https://books.google.com/books/about/DE_ARTE_CABALISTICA_ID_EST_DE_DIVINAE_RE.html?id=C3RgAAAAcAAJ), [Frankfurt digital copy](https://sammlungen.ub.uni-frankfurt.de/freimann/content/titleinfo/404942), [critical edition information](https://www.frommann-holzboog.de/editionen/1706/170600211?lang=de).

## 3. *Portae Lucis*, 1516

Paolo Riccio’s Latin abridgment of Gikatilla’s *Shaʿarei Orah*, *Portae Lucis*, appeared at Augsburg in 1516.

Its title page contains the first known printed sefirotic tree. The woodcut is attributed to Leonhard Beck and depicts a Jewish scholar contemplating a tree whose nodes carry Hebrew names. [British Museum/Wikimedia record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AIllustrated_book%2C_print%2C_title-page_%28BM_1870,1008.1942_2%29.jpg), [Center for Online Judaic Studies](https://cojs.org/portae_lucis_-shaarei_orah-_abraham_joseph_gikatilla_-translated_by_paulus_ricius-_augsburg-_johann_miller-_1516-_rb424-16-_title_page/).

It was not the first kabbalistic diagram. Hebrew manuscripts contained diagrams in the late thirteenth century, while the scroll genre called *ilanot* emerged during the fourteenth century. [Ilanot Project history](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/?p=585).

## 4. Agrippa, 1533

The most direct Renaissance ancestor of the later occult table is Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia* III.10, published in the complete edition of 1533.

Agrippa writes:

> “Sextum nomen Eloha, sive nomen quadriliterum coniunctum cum Vaudahat.”

He continues:

> “numeratio eius Tiphereth … ornatus, pulchritudo, gloria, voluptas.”

His table gives:

- Eloha, or the four-letter name joined with *Vaudahat*;
- Tiphereth;
- Tree of Life;
- Virtues/Malachim;
- sphere of the Sun;
- Raphael;
- Jacob.

[Latin/English Agrippa, Book III](https://esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agrippa3.htm), [1651 English edition](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26565.0001.001/1%3A18.10?rgn=div2%3Bview%3Dfulltext).

This is earlier than the Golden Dawn by more than three centuries.

### The compound divine name

Agrippa’s wording is disjunctive: “Eloha, **or** the four-letter name joined with Vaudahat.” The Golden Dawn form:

> YHVH Eloah va-Daʿath

appears to conflate these alternatives into one phrase.

Hebrew יהוה אלוה ודעת literally reads approximately “YHWH, Eloah, and Knowledge.” It is not ordinary Hebrew for “YHWH, God of Knowledge.” The usual occult English gloss “Lord God of Knowledge” is therefore interpretive, not grammatical.

## 5. Kircher, 1652–1654

Athanasius Kircher printed his elaborate *Systema Sephiroticum* in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*.

For the sixth node his table gives:

> יהוה — *Iehouah*  
> תפארת — *Tiphereth*  
> *Pulchritudo*

That is, YHWH; Tiphereth; Beauty. [Kircher’s Latin volume and plate](https://www.esonet.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Oedipus_Aegyptiacus_Tom_IIB-latino.pdf), [Cornell plate record](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A550272).

### The Kircher layout

Kircher combined:

- ten sefirotic nodes;
- a particular network of twenty-two channels;
- Hebrew letters assigned approximately in alphabetic sequence;
- planetary and astrological symbols;
- Christian, Platonic, and other syncretic explanations.

The Golden Dawn adopted the essential channel geometry and letter order, but not all Kircher’s planetary placements.

### Kircher versus Ari/Gra

| Feature | Kircher/Golden Dawn | Ari/Gra families |
|---|---|---|
| Governing principle | Alphabetic sequence down a chosen 22-channel figure | Letter classes structured as 3 mothers, 7 doubles, 12 simples |
| Horizontal paths | Mixed alphabetic classes | Normally mother letters |
| Vertical paths | Mixed | Normally double letters |
| Diagonal paths | Mixed | Normally simple letters |
| Malkhut | Three connections in the standard GD figure | Often one principal connection in Jewish trees |
| Tarot function | Designed later to support 22 trump correspondences | No historical tarot attribution |
| Authority | Christian-Hermetic convention | Jewish commentarial/diagrammatic convention |

It is inaccurate to say that *Sefer Yetzirah* itself supplied Kircher’s diagram. The work gives ten sefirot and twenty-two letters, but no surviving early passage draws the familiar ten circles and assigns all letters to those particular edges.

## 6. Knorr von Rosenroth, 1677–1684

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth’s *Kabbala Denudata* appeared in stages at Sulzbach and Frankfurt. It made portions of the Zohar, Lurianic writings, Cordovero, Abraham Cohen de Herrera, and associated texts available in Latin.

Its vocabulary includes:

- *Tiphereth*;
- *Pulchritudo*;
- Microprosopus for Zeʿir Anpin;
- Christianized comparisons between the sefirotic world and divine or messianic structures.

[Kabbala Denudata vol. I](https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=yIlOquc5yOUC), [e-rara scan](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/1284225.pdf).

Mathers’s *Kabbalah Unveiled* is not a complete English translation of Rosenroth. It translates selected portions—principally the *Sifra di-Tzeniʿuta* and the two *Idrot*—and adds Golden Dawn-era framing. [Mathers edition](https://avalonlibrary.net/ebooks/MacGregor%20Mathers%20-%20Kabbalah%20Unveiled.pdf).

## 7. Éliphas Lévi and tarot

In *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* (1854–1856), Éliphas Lévi systematically coordinated the twenty-two Hebrew letters with the twenty-two tarot trumps.

**[HC/MI]** There is no evidence that medieval Jewish kabbalists associated Hebrew letters with tarot. Tarot arose in fifteenth-century Italy; its use as an esoteric book developed later.

Lévi’s ordering also differs from the Golden Dawn’s:

- Lévi starts the numbered trump series differently and places the Fool anomalously;
- the Golden Dawn assigns Aleph to the Fool, Beth to the Magician, and so forth;
- the Golden Dawn also exchanges the traditional numbering of Strength and Justice to preserve zodiacal sequence.

[Lévi, French *Dogme et rituel*](https://www.hermetics.org/pdf/Eliphas_Levi_Dogma%20et%20Rituel%20de%20la%20Haute%20Magie%201.pdf).

Because Tiphareth is a sphere, Lévi did not give it one unique trump. The trumps belong to the channels.

## 8. The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

### Knowledge Lectures

The Golden Dawn, founded in 1888, assembled older Cabalistic, magical, astrological, alchemical, Rosicrucian, Enochian, and tarot materials into a graded curriculum.

Its Tiphareth formula is:

- divine name: YHVH Eloah va-Daʿath;
- archangel: Raphael;
- order: Malachim/Melekim;
- mundane chakra: Shemesh;
- Yetziratic title: Mediating Intelligence;
- grade: 5°=6 Adeptus Minor;
- spiritual experience: Vision of the Harmony of Things and Mysteries of the Crucifixion;
- virtue: devotion to the Great Work;
- vice: pride.

[Regardie-derived Golden Dawn summary](https://www.llewellyn.com/encyclopedia/term/Tiphareth).

### Adeptus Minor, Rosy Cross, and Vault

The 5°=6 ritual places the initiate symbolically in Tiphareth. Its central themes are:

- death and resurrection;
- the tomb or Vault of Christian Rosenkreutz;
- the pastos;
- the cross and rose;
- equilibrium of elemental and planetary forces;
- identification with Osiris and Christ-like sacrificial symbolism;
- the motto “there is no part of me that is not of the gods.”

[Crowley’s publication of the Adeptus Minor material](https://hermetic.com/crowley/equinox/i/iii/eqi03010l), [Golden Dawn Adeptus Minor ritual](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Ritual_Adeptus_Minor.html).

The Vault is seven-sided, representing the seven planetary forces surrounding the solar center. The candidate’s passage from symbolic death to awakened adeptship gives Tiphareth its initiatory meaning.

### The Christ-centre

**[HC]** Golden Dawn and later occult literature identify Tiphareth with:

- Christ;
- Osiris;
- Dionysus;
- Adonis;
- other sacrificed-and-reborn divine figures;
- the redeemed or higher human self.

This is a comparative religious construction characteristic of nineteenth-century occultism. Jewish Tiferet’s “son,” Jacob, Torah, compassion, and union with Shekhinah are its historical substrate; they are not implicit confessions of Christianity.

### Four color scales

The Golden Dawn assigns a color in each of four scales/worlds:

| World/scale | Tiphareth color |
|---|---|
| Atziluth, King scale | Clear rose pink |
| Briah, Queen scale | Yellow or golden yellow |
| Yetzirah, Prince/Knight scale | Rich salmon pink |
| Assiah, Princess/Knave scale | Golden amber |

[Whare Ra/Golden Dawn color-scale paper](https://hermetic.com/gdlibrary/whare_ra/6-5_preparation).

These are ritual-visualization attributions, not colors stated in the *Zohar* or *Pardes Rimmonim*.

## 11. Holy Guardian Angel and Thelemic grades

### Golden Dawn versus A∴A∴

## 13. Dion Fortune, 1935

Fortune’s *The Mystical Qabalah* devotes Chapter XX to Tiphareth.

Her tabulation gives:

- magical image: majestic king, child, sacrificed god;
- position: center of the Pillar of Equilibrium;
- divine name: Tetragrammaton Aloah Va Daath;
- archangel: Raphael;
- order: Malachim;
- Sun;
- virtue: devotion to the Great Work;
- vice: pride;
- body: breast;
- symbols: lamen, Rosy Cross, Calvary Cross, truncated pyramid, cube;
- four Sixes and four colors.

[Fortune, Chapter XX](https://www.globalgreyebooks.com/online-ebooks/dion-fortune_mystical-qabalah_complete-text.html).

### Full-chapter synopsis

Fortune develops three governing ideas:

1. Tiphareth is the equilibrium center of the Tree.
2. It is Keter on a lower arc and Yesod on a higher arc.
3. It is the point of transition between force and form.

Her three magical images express perspective:

- viewed from above, Tiphareth is the divine Child;
- viewed from below, the King;
- viewed at the threshold of transformation, the sacrificed god.

She treats Tiphareth as the point where archetypal force becomes individualized consciousness. Exoteric religion, in her scheme, culminates here because its savior-gods, ethical ideals, and sacramental mysteries concern incarnation, sacrifice, redemption, and resurrection.

She gives it an explicitly Christian and comparative interpretation, placing Jesus, Osiris, Dionysus, and other redeemer figures in the same solar category. Sacrifice is not simply destruction: it is the conversion of energy from one mode into another.

Psychologically, Tiphareth is the integrated higher individuality standing above the instinctual and psychic personality of the lower triangle. Initiatically it is the first stable contact with the higher self or divine consciousness, but not the final attainment represented by the supernals.

The lamen is the adept’s breast-symbol and concentrated declaration of the force under which the magician operates. The cube represents stabilized form; the truncated pyramid represents the Adeptus Minor, whose ascent is genuine but incomplete; the completed pyramid points toward higher attainment.

This is a complete summary of Fortune’s argument, not a verbatim reproduction of the copyrighted chapter.

# The Qliphah and the Nightside

## Jewish qelippot versus the inverted occult tree

The Hebrew קליפה, *qelippah*, means shell, husk, or peel.

In the Zohar and Lurianic literature, qelippot are forces of concealment, imbalance, or impurity. Lurianic classifications commonly discuss:

- three wholly impure qelippot;
- qelippat nogah, the translucent or mixed shell.

**[AE]** Classical Jewish sources do not consistently provide the modern ten-name inverted tree:

Thaumiel, Ghagiel, Satariel, Ghaʿagsheblah, Golachab, Thagirion, Aʿarab Zaraq, Samael, Gamaliel, Lilith.

That ordered list was assembled and stabilized through Rosenroth-derived material, Mathers, the Golden Dawn, Crowley, and later occultists.

## Thagirion

Common spellings include:

- Thagirion;
- Tageriron;
- Tagiriron;
- Thagerion;
- transliteration TGRIRVN.

Crowley’s *777* glosses the sixth qliphah as **“the Disputers.”**

The basic occult idea is beauty or solar individuality distorted into:

- vanity;
- pride;
- self-deification;
- false messiahship;
- empty spectacle;
- disharmony masquerading as harmony;
- consciousness cut off from its divine center.

This is the shadow of Golden Dawn Tiphareth, not a simple translation of a Zoharic doctrine.

## Demon prince: Belphegor or Sorath?

### Belphegor

Many twentieth- and twenty-first-century correspondence charts assign **Belphegor** to Thagirion. The association fits later demonological themes of deceptive inspiration, false beauty, indolence, or corrupted invention.

**[AE/D]** Crowley’s core *777* line names the “Disputers” but does not establish Belphegor as their uncontested ruler. The precise first printed table pairing Belphegor with Thagirion remains uncertain.

### Sorath

Sorath is the solar demon whose name is assigned the number 666 in Golden Dawn/Crowley material. Because Thagirion is the solar qliphah, some systems make Sorath its ruler.

The distinction often used by practitioners is:

- Thagirion: qliphah or demonic order;
- Belphegor: arch-demon or prince in one modern hierarchy;
- Sorath: specifically the demon of the Sun.

But sources frequently collapse those categories. There is no single canonical ruling demon across all Hermetic schools.

## Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden*

Kenneth Grant’s *Nightside of Eden* appeared in 1977. Grant turned Crowley’s twenty-two qliphothic sigils into “Tunnels of Set,” a shadow network behind the ordinary paths. [Grant text](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Nightside-of-Eden-KennethGrant_text.pdf).

**[MI]** “Tunnel of Set” is Grant’s Typhonian terminology. It is not found in *Sefer Yetzirah*, the *Zohar*, Cordovero, or Vital.

Because Tiphareth is a node rather than a path, it has no single tunnel. In the Kircher/Golden Dawn geometry, eight paths touch Tiphareth, so eight corresponding tunnels touch Thagirion:

| Letter/path | Dayside connection | Grantian tunnel | Nightside connection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gimel | Keter–Tiphareth | Gargophias | Thaumiel–Thagirion |
| Heh | Chokhmah–Tiphareth | Hemethterith | Ghagiel–Thagirion |
| Zayin | Binah–Tiphareth | Zamradiel | Satariel–Thagirion |
| Yod | Ḥesed–Tiphareth | Yamatu | Ghaʿagsheblah–Thagirion |
| Lamed | Gevurah–Tiphareth | Lafcursiax | Golachab–Thagirion |
| Nun | Tiphareth–Netzach | Niantiel | Thagirion–Aʿarab Zaraq |
| Samekh | Tiphareth–Yesod | Saksaksalim | Thagirion–Gamaliel |
| Ayin | Tiphareth–Hod | Aʿanoʿnin | Thagirion–Samael |

Spellings differ between Grant editions and later practitioners.

### Tzaddi/Heh

Liber AL I:57 says:

> “All these old letters of my Book are aright; but צ is not the Star.”

Crowley eventually exchanged the trumps, assigning:

- Tzaddi to the Emperor/Aries;
- Heh to the Star/Aquarius.

[Crowley, *Book of Thoth*, theory](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/theory).

This affects Tiphareth because the **Heh path connecting Chokhmah and Tiphareth** changes from Emperor in the Golden Dawn to Star in Crowley’s system. It does not change Tiphareth’s own number or solar attribution.

### Pathworking

Golden Dawn-derived pathworking usually involves:

1. establishing the two endpoint sefirot;
2. vibrating divine and angelic names;
3. entering through the path’s Hebrew letter and tarot image;
4. encountering symbolic figures and testing them by names or signs;
5. returning and recording the vision.

This is modern ritual practice. It is not a method described in the core *Sefer Yetzirah*. Jewish letter contemplation, especially in Abulafian Kabbalah, has its own techniques and should not be retroactively described as tarot pathworking.

# People, works, diagrams, and cultural reception

## Jewish kabbalists and transmitters

- **Azriel of Gerona:** early systematic theory of sefirotic emanation and mediation.
- **Joseph Gikatilla:** placed Tiferet, YHWH, Jacob, Written Torah, truth, and the middle line into a mature system.
- **Moses de León and the Zoharic circle:** produced the central Tiferet/Zeʿir Anpin–Malkhut mythology.
- **Moses Cordovero:** systematized Tiferet as compassion and multicolored equilibrium.
- **Isaac Luria:** transformed the sefirotic system through shevirah, partzufim, and tikkun.
- **Ḥayyim Vital:** preserved and organized Lurianic teaching.
- **Elijah of Vilna:** gave *Sefer Yetzirah* a major diagrammatic and commentarial recension.
- **Shneur Zalman of Liadi:** psychologized and ethically articulated the interinclusion of Ḥesed, Gevurah, and Tiferet.
- **Naḥman of Bratslav:** developed truth, compassion, prayer, Torah, and ẓaddik-centered motifs later read through Tiferet.

## Christian and Hermetic figures

- **Flavius Mithridates:** translated kabbalistic material for Pico.
- **Pico della Mirandola:** made Cabala evidence for Christian truth.
- **Johannes Reuchlin:** spread Christian Cabala through Latin humanism.
- **Paolo Riccio:** translated/abridged Gikatilla and published *Portae Lucis*.
- **Agrippa:** formulated the major Tiphereth–Eloha/Vaudahat–Malachim–Sun–Raphael table.
- **Athanasius Kircher:** printed the influential alphabetically pathed tree.
- **Knorr von Rosenroth:** transmitted Zoharic and Lurianic material in Latin.
- **Éliphas Lévi:** joined Hebrew letters to tarot trumps.
- **William Wynn Westcott:** translated *Sefer Yetzirah* and transmitted the “Mediating Intelligence.”
- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers:** systematized the Golden Dawn’s Tree, rituals, colors, tarot, and qliphoth.
- **Moina Mathers:** contributed to Golden Dawn temple art, tableaux, and visual ritual culture.
- **Aleister Crowley:** published Golden Dawn materials, *777*, *Liber 231*, and the HGA/Tiphareth grade doctrine.
- **Dion Fortune:** supplied the most influential psychological-Christian prose account.
- **Paul Foster Case:** transmitted Tiphareth through B.O.T.A. tarot practice.
- **Israel Regardie:** preserved Golden Dawn ritual and popularized the Middle Pillar.
- **Gareth Knight:** developed a mid-twentieth-century occult-psychological reading.
- **Kenneth Grant:** constructed the Thagirion-centered “Nightside” and tunnels.
- **Linda Falorio:** created a modern tarot of the Tunnels of Set.

## Scholars

- **Gershom Scholem:** historical reconstruction of early Kabbalah, Zoharic authorship, Luria, and Christian Cabala.
- **Moshe Idel:** challenged single-line developmental histories and emphasized experiential, magical, linguistic, and multiple kabbalistic models.
- **Elliot R. Wolfson:** studied embodiment, gender, vision, language, and erotic symbolism in Kabbalah.
- **Joseph Dan:** distinguished Jewish Kabbalah from its Christian reinterpretations.
- **Peter Hayman:** established a critical textual foundation for *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- **Daniel C. Matt:** produced the Pritzker English translation of the *Zohar* with detailed philological notes.
- **J. H. Chajes:** reconstructed the history of *ilanot*, the drawn kabbalistic trees.

## Diagrams and art

### Early manuscripts

Late thirteenth-century Hebrew manuscripts already contain diagrams, including tree-like figures. The systematic *ilan* scroll emerged in the fourteenth century and functioned as map, mnemonic device, contemplative image, and instrument of tikkun. [Ilanot Project](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/site/), [database](https://ilanot.haifa.ac.il/Ilanot_Site/database.html).

### *Portae Lucis*, 1516

The title-page woodcut by Leonhard Beck is the earliest known printed sefirotic tree, but not the origin of Jewish diagrammatic Kabbalah.

### *Pardes Rimmonim*, 1591/92

The first printed Hebrew edition contains diagrams whose relation to Cordovero’s manuscript descriptions is debated. Different early editions do not always preserve the same channel arrangement.

### Kircher’s plate

Kircher’s large syncretic plate provided the primary visual ancestor of the modern Hermetic Tree.

### Golden Dawn colored trees

The Golden Dawn’s *Minutum Mundum* and related painted trees encoded:

- four color scales;
- flashing colors;
- planetary signs;
- Hebrew letters;
- tarot;
- grades;
- divine and angelic names.

These colored diagrams were ritual implements, not merely illustrations.

## Poets, artists, and novelists

Directly documented engagement is strongest among Golden Dawn members and successors:

- **W. B. Yeats** worked within the Golden Dawn grade system and its solar, Rosicrucian, and daimonic symbolism, although not every solar image in his poetry is a coded Tiphareth reference.
- **Aleister Crowley** repeatedly used Tiphareth in poetry, ritual, autobiography, and initiatory writing.
- **Dion Fortune** fictionalized initiatory and occult-psychological themes continuous with her Tiphareth doctrine.
- **Frieda Harris**, painter of the Thoth Tarot, visualized Crowley’s revised tarot correspondences, including the solar Sixes and the Heh/Tzaddi exchange.
- **Pamela Colman Smith**, trained in a Golden Dawn milieu, painted the Rider–Waite–Smith Sixes; however, a card-by-card claim that she intended every detail as explicit Tiphareth doctrine requires evidence.
- **Ted Hughes** used Cabalistic structures in his poetry; claims about a specific Tiphareth program must be argued work by work rather than inferred from any isolated sun or heart image.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Dating the *Sefer Yetzirah*

- **Tradition:** Abrahamic antiquity.
- **Older scholarship:** proposed dates ranging from the second to sixth centuries.
- **Hayman and related textual scholarship:** stresses an unstable early-medieval textual tradition, with received forms developing before Saadia’s 931 commentary.
- **Open issue:** individual cosmological or linguistic units may be older than the final textual organization.

What cannot be maintained historically is that the extant Abrahamic original already contained the Golden Dawn Tree, tarot, and Tiphareth’s occult correspondences.

## 2. Did *Sefer Yetzirah* teach Tiferet?

No named Tiferet appears in the core text. Later kabbalists reinterpreted its six directions as the six lower masculine sefirot. This is a legitimate history of reception, but it must not be confused with the earliest textual layer.

## 3. Zohar authorship

- **Traditional:** Shimon bar Yoḥai.
- **Scholem:** Moses de León as principal author.
- **Current tendency:** a late thirteenth-century Castilian Zoharic circle and layered corpus.

Traditional attribution remains religiously significant, but linguistic, literary, and historical evidence supports medieval composition/redaction.

## 4. Christian appropriation

Christian Cabalists preserved, translated, and circulated material that might otherwise have remained inaccessible to Latin readers. Reuchlin also defended Jewish books against destruction.

At the same time, Pico, Reuchlin, Kircher, and Rosenroth repeatedly interpreted Jewish texts as witnesses to Christian dogma. Joseph Dan emphasizes that their category “Kabbalah” differed radically from medieval Jewish Kabbalah. [Dan, *Kabbalah: A Very Short Introduction*](https://www.exosomatic.org/ENTER/Beliefs/Judaism/Kabbalah/Kabbalah%2C%20A%20Very%20Short%20Introduction%2C%20Oxford%20Uni.%20Press%20-%20Joseph%20Dan%202006.pdf).

Thus “Tiphareth is Christ” is historically a Christian Cabalistic claim, not a neutral translation of Jewish Tiferet.

## 5. Raphael versus Michael

The conflict is largely produced by overlapping taxonomies:

- Agrippa and the Golden Dawn: Raphael is Tiphareth’s sefirah archangel.
- Some planetary angel tables: Michael rules the Sun.
- Other tables: Raphael rules Mercury.
- Jewish four-archangel arrangements place Michael, Gabriel, Uriel, and Raphael by direction rather than sefirah.
- Zoharic passages may treat Raphael as a middle-line healer without creating the later four-world hierarchy.

Therefore:

- **Raphael for Tiphareth is well documented in Hermetic Qabalah.**
- **Michael for the solar sphere is well documented in other angelological systems.**
- Neither can be imposed as the sole “classical Jewish” answer.

## 6. YHVH Eloah va-Daʿath

The compound’s lineage is real but its Hebrew is problematic.

- Gikatilla’s Tiferet name: YHWH.
- Agrippa: Eloha **or** Tetragrammaton joined with Vaudahat.
- Golden Dawn: YHVH Eloah va-Daʿath as one formula.
- Fortune/Regardie: translate it devotionally as a God of knowledge.

The Golden Dawn form is therefore an early-modern/occult synthesis, not a phrase securely quoted from the *Zohar*.

## 7. Kircher versus Jewish trees

There has never been one immutable diagram. Jewish manuscripts show many arrangements.

Still, the Golden Dawn’s particular system clearly follows the Kircher/Rosenroth channel geometry and alphabetic path order more closely than the Ari/Gra class-based trees. Claims that the Golden Dawn diagram is “the original Hebrew Tree of Life” are untenable.

## 8. Tarot attribution

- No tarot in *Sefer Yetzirah*, *Bahir*, *Zohar*, Gikatilla, Cordovero, or Vital.
- Lévi created an influential letter-trump synthesis.
- The Golden Dawn altered the ordering and added zodiacal, elemental, and planetary attributions.
- Crowley altered Heh/Tzaddi.
- Case and other schools made further changes.

These are occult symbolic systems, not recovered ancient Jewish correspondences.

## 9. Daʿat’s status

*Sefer Yetzirah* insists on “ten and not eleven.” Jewish sources solve the Keter/Daʿat problem in several ways:

- count Keter and omit Daʿat;
- count Daʿat as the manifest interiorization of Keter;
- regard Daʿat as the conjunction or extension of Ḥokhmah and Binah;
- regard it as the root or collective state of the six lower sefirot.

Cordovero explicitly warns that Daʿat is not an independent sefirah.

The Golden Dawn and Crowley made it visually prominent as an “invisible sefirah” at the Abyss. Calling it the eleventh sefirah without qualification contradicts both the “ten not eleven” principle and many Jewish explanations.

## 10. Thagirion’s antiquity

The concept of shells is Jewish and medieval. The exact ten-name inverted tree, its demon princes, and its use as an initiatory “nightside” are progressively later.

- qelippot: Jewish;
- ten opposed qliphothic orders: early-modern/Hermetic stabilization;
- Thagirion as line 6 of *777*: documented occult table;
- Belphegor as uncontested ruler: disputed;
- Tunnels of Set: Kenneth Grant, 1977.

## 11. The “Christ-centre”

There is a genuine structural analogy between:

- Jewish Tiferet as son, king, Written Torah, sun, Jacob, and masculine partner of Malkhut;
- Christian Christ as Son, Logos, king, light, bridegroom, and sacrificed redeemer.

Historical analogy does not demonstrate identity. Christian Cabalists selected and transformed the Jewish symbols through their own theology.

# Open questions

1. **Exact genealogy of the compound divine name.** Agrippa’s alternatives are clear, but the point at which “Eloha” and “Tetragrammaton joined with Va-Daʿat” became the single Golden Dawn formula needs manuscript-level study of Christian Cabalistic and early Golden Dawn tables.

2. **First appearance of Thagirion’s demon ruler.** “Disputers” is secure in Crowley. The earliest unambiguous printed pairing with Belphegor remains to be established; Sorath belongs to a distinct solar-demon line.

3. **Kircher’s immediate diagrammatic sources.** Kircher drew on Christian Hebraists, Jewish converts, Cordoverian material, and preceding tree diagrams. The contribution of Philippe d’Aquin and individual printed *Pardes* diagrams deserves exact stemmatic comparison.

4. **Reuchlin and Tiferet-Christ language.** Reuchlin’s filial and Christian interpretation is clear in context, but individual claims often attributed to him online are actually verbatim Agrippa. Editions must be checked before transferring Agrippa’s table backward to 1517.

5. **Early *Bahir* strata.** The sixth “Throne of Glory” passage anticipates later Tiferet symbolism, but whether its components derive from older eastern traditions or medieval Provençal redaction remains debated.

6. **Zoharic plurality.** “Tiferet” does not have exactly the same function in every stratum of the Zoharic corpus. Pritzker-volume and Aramaic lexical studies can refine distinctions between Tiferet, the Holy One, Zeʿir Anpin, Jacob, Israel, Sun, Torah, and Heaven.

7. **Color transmission.** Cordovero’s white-red-green-purple contemplative system and the Golden Dawn’s four-scale colors overlap in their interest in synthesis but lack a demonstrated direct one-to-one genealogy.

8. **Modern Jewish practice.** Contemporary Omer manuals often psychologize Tiferet as empathy, balance, or compassion. These are living developments whose relation to Cordoverian, Lurianic, Hasidic, and therapeutic vocabularies varies by community.

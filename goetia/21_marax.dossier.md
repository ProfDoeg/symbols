# Marax: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes the following categories:

- **[Documented text]** Directly stated or drawn in a surviving manuscript or printed source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** A conclusion based on comparison, dating, or textual transmission rather than an explicit statement by the source.
- **[Tradition]** A claim internal to a religious, magical, or folkloric system.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** A symbolic attribution imposed by an occult system, not a historical identity.
- **[Disputed]** A claim for which the evidence permits materially different readings.
- **[Legend]** A supernatural or pseudohistorical narrative transmitted as lore.
- **[Modern invention]** A recent addition unsupported by the early textual chain.

Because long verbatim reproduction is limited, the entries below include short exact excerpts followed by complete close paraphrases; the linked public-domain editions provide the full texts.

The most defensible concise identification is therefore:

> **[Documented text]** Morax/Marax is a Renaissance and early-modern catalogue spirit, first securely printed by Johann Weyer in 1577, later made spirit 21 of the *Ars Goetia*. It is an Earl and President appearing as a bull or human-faced bull, teaching astronomy and the liberal sciences, and commanding either thirty or thirty-six legions.  
> **[Tradition]** Harley 6483’s Rudd material places the twenty-first Shem angel, Nelchael, over it as a constraining power.  
> **[Esoteric correspondence]** Crowley’s *777* assigns Marax to the diurnal third decan of Libra and the Four of Swords.

# The name and its origins

## 1. The historical spellings

Joseph Peterson’s comparative table permits the following source-by-source reconstruction. [Peterson’s comparative table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf)

| Date/source | Reading | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1577, Weyer | **Morax, alias Foraij** | Goetic ancestor no. 15 |
| 1584, Scot | **Morax, alias Foraij** | English rendering of Weyer |
| ca. 1577–1583, Folger V.b.26 | **Goorox** | Cognate office-spirit, no. 52 |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane 3853 | **Corax** | Cognate bull/astronomy entry |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane 3825 | **Morax** | Goetia no. 21 |
| ca. 1665+, Sloane 3648 | **Marax** | A principal source behind later copies |
| 1686/87, Sloane 2731 | **Morax** | Composite manuscript |
| 1712–1713, Harley 6483 | **Marax** | Rudd/Smart recension |
| 1904, Mathers–Crowley | **Marax** | Mathers expressly prefers it to *Morax* |
| 1911, Waite | **Morax**, also **Foraii**, “another variant” **Forfax** | *Forfax* is not found in the primary chain above |
| 2001, Peterson | **Morax [Marax]** | Base text follows Sloane 3825; bracketed variants supplied critically |

### Foraij and Foraii

**[Documented text]** *Foraij* is Weyer’s printed form. Scot retains it. *Foraii* is the common normalization of the final *j/i* conventions of early-modern Latin typography. No independent demon named Foraii is needed to explain the evidence.

**[Open question]** There is no accepted etymology for *Foraij*. It may be an already corrupt alias in Weyer's source, but no surviving exemplar of his *Liber officiorum spirituum* has been identified.

### Marax versus Morax

**[Documented text]** Weyer and the better-aligned Sloane 3825 text read *Morax*. Sloane 3648 and the Harley/Rudd branch favor *Marax*.

**[Editorial opinion]** Mathers’s 1904 footnote says:

> “In some Codices written Morax, but I consider the above the correct orthography.”

Mathers gave no philological argument for that preference. The evidence of Weyer, Scot and Sloane 3825 favors *Morax* as the earlier recoverable form; *Marax* remains an authentic manuscript variant rather than a modern mistake. [Mathers–Crowley, 1904, p. 28](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

### Goorox and Corax

**[Documented text]** Folger MS V.b.26, compiled during approximately 1577–1583, contains:

> “Goorox an earle he appeareth like a bowle, & sometyme like a mann…”

The manuscript continues that Goorox knows astronomy, all liberal sciences, and the virtues of herbs and stones, commanding thirty legions. “Bowle” is almost certainly *bull* distorted by spelling or transcription. [Folger V.b.26 transcription, p. 85, entry 52](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3853 has *Corax*, a great Earl appearing as a bull and sometimes a man, with wonderful knowledge of astronomy and ninety legions. [Peterson, “The Offices of Spirits,” fol. 257v](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/offices.htm)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The close bundle of rank, bull form and astronomical knowledge makes Goorox and Corax textual relatives of Morax. It does not establish which spelling was original. Possible developments include:

- an archetypal name being copied independently as *Goorox*, *Corax* and *Morax*;
- *Goorox* or *Corax* being corrupted toward the familiar Latin-looking *Morax*;
- the reverse, with *Morax* becoming less familiar forms in English manuscripts.

No surviving stemma settles the direction.

### Farax, Forfax and Narax

- **Forfax — [Documented later reading].** A. E. Waite wrote in 1911 that Morax was Foraii in Wierus and that “another variant is Forfax.” No earlier manuscript support was located. [Waite, *Book of Ceremonial Magic*, p. 202 note](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-book-of-ceremonial-magic/shell/demons-xxi-morax-to-xxx-forneus)
- **Farax — [Modern secondary variant].** Common in encyclopedias and online demon lists, but absent from Peterson’s principal manuscript comparison. It may be a simplification or contamination of *Foraij/Forfax*.
- **Narax — [Modern invention or typo].** No Weyer, Scot, Sloane, Harley, Mathers or Peterson witness inspected here reads *Narax*. Its visible circulation belongs to recent practitioner pages, including a 2026 article listing “Narax, Marax, Morax.” [Example](https://vkjehannum.wordpress.com/2026/03/09/marax-or-morax-goetia-21/)

## 2. Possible Latin etymology

Latin has an adjective *morax, moracis*, “delaying, apt to delay, dilatory,” derived from *mora*, delay, or *morari*, to delay.

**[Plausible scholarly reconstruction]** The exact lexical match makes Latin *morax* a possible source or influence on the printed name. [Lewis and Short entry](https://classics.andrewgadsden.com/lewisandshort/entry/n29667)

**[Disputed]** Neither Weyer nor any early catalogue glosses the demon as “the delayer,” and nothing in its office concerns delay or obstruction. The derivation is therefore suggestive, not demonstrated. Its modern repetition usually descends from twentieth-century demon dictionaries and derivative encyclopedias rather than from historical philology.

## 3. Is Marax an ancient god, hero or place?

### No securely identifiable pagan source

**[Finding]** Unlike Baal, Astaroth, Asmodai, Belial or Phoenix, Marax has no demonstrable name-chain to a known ancient god, biblical being, classical hero or ancient place.

No source before the Renaissance has been found using *Morax/Marax* as the name of this bull-shaped teacher of astronomy.

### The Minotaur

**[Modern conjecture]** Fred Gettings and later encyclopedic summaries have proposed an association with the Minotaur, principally because both combine human and bovine features.

The evidence is iconographic resemblance only:

- Dante’s Minotaur appears in *Inferno* XII;
- Dante never calls it Morax;
- the classical Minotaur does not teach astronomy, herbs or stones;
- neither Weyer nor the Goetia names Crete, Minos, Pasiphaë or the Labyrinth.

The Minotaur identification is therefore a twentieth-century comparison, not a historical etymology.

### Moloch

**[Modern conflation]** Moloch became conventionally bull-headed in later European art, encouraging comparison with Morax. But *Moloch* derives from the Semitic *mlk* complex and has a separate biblical and controversial ancient history. There is no phonological or textual bridge from Moloch to Morax.

### Ma’at

**[Modern invention]** Some modern Satanist/demonolatry material declares Morax to be the Egyptian goddess Ma’at, sometimes presenting the human-faced bull as a female Egyptian figure. One explicit modern source states, “MORAX … Egyptian Goddess MAAT.” [Modern source carrying the claim](https://libraryofthoth.org/English/Demons.pdf)

Evidence against treating this as history:

- Ma’at’s name does not resemble *Morax* in Egyptian;
- Ma’at is normally represented as a woman with an ostrich feather, not a bull;
- Weyer, Scot, the manuscripts, de Plancy and Mathers make no Egyptian identification;
- the claim appears only in recent ideological reconstructions.

### Mahishasura, Apis, Mnevis and other bull figures

**[Modern comparative symbolism]** Recent occult pages have compared Marax with the buffalo demon Mahishasura, the Apis bull, Mnevis, lamassu and various lunar bulls. These may be useful to modern artists or practitioners, but no historical source-chain connects them to the name.

# The description across the sources

## 1. Johann Weyer, 1577

The *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* first appeared in the 1577 edition of Weyer’s *De praestigiis daemonum*, not in the original 1563 edition. Marax’s ancestor is entry 15.

### Latin

Short exact excerpt:

> “Morax, aliàs Foraij, magnus Comes & Præses: similis tauro visitur…”

Complete sense:

> Morax, otherwise Foraij, is a great Count and President. He is seen in the likeness of a bull. Whenever he assumes a human face, he renders a person remarkably learned in astronomy and every liberal art. He also produces or supplies servants/familiars who are not evil and are wise. He knows the power of herbs and precious stones. He commands thirty-six legions.

The original Latin uses:

- *magnus Comes* — great Count/Earl;
- *Præses* — president, governor or presiding officer;
- *similis tauro* — like a bull;
- *humanam faciem assumit* — assumes a human face;
- *famulos non malos et sapientes* — servants/familiars who are “not bad” and wise;
- *potentiam* — power or efficacy, later rendered “virtue.”

[Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, Basel: Oporinus, 1577, approximately p. 915, §15](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### What Weyer does not say

**[Documented absence]** Weyer gives:

- no seal;
- no Shem angel;
- no zodiac degree or tarot card;
- no individual hour;
- no command to assume a second form;
- no ancient identity;
- no gender;
- no treasures, love, invisibility, transmutation or universal answers.

Weyer’s conditional construction describes a bull that may take a human face. It does not say that a conjurer must demand this transformation.

## 2. Reginald Scot, 1584

Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter 2, translates Weyer’s catalogue. Scot’s purpose was skeptical and polemical: exposing what conjurers claimed, not affirming the spirits’ independent reality.

Short exact excerpt:

> “Morax, alias Foraij, a great earle and a president, he is seene like a bull…”

Complete sense:

> Morax, also called Foraij, is a great Earl and President. He appears like a bull. If he takes a man’s face, he makes people wonderfully skilled in astronomy and all liberal sciences. He gives good and wise familiars that know the power and virtue of herbs and precious stones, and he rules thirty-six legions.

Scot’s important choices include:

- *Comes* → “earle”;
- *famulos* → “familiars”;
- *potentiam* → “power & vertue”;
- Weyer’s “not bad and wise” → “good familiars and wise.”

This wording became foundational for the English manuscript tradition. [Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London, 1584, Book XV, ch. 2](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); [first-edition scan](https://archive.org/details/discouerieofwitc00scot/)

## 3. Folger V.b.26, ca. 1577–1583

Entry 52, Goorox:

> “Goorox an earle he appeareth like a bowle, & sometyme like a mann…”

It then assigns:

- astronomy;
- all liberal sciences;
- virtues of herbs and stones;
- thirty legions.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This is an especially valuable parallel because its office is almost identical but its name and legion count differ. The Folger manuscript was begun in 1577 and continued into the early 1580s; it is therefore contemporary with Weyer, not securely earlier. [Folger catalogue](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/228887); [transcription, p. 85](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

## 4. Sloane 3853

The cognate *Corax* is a great Earl appearing as a bull and sometimes as a man, with wonderful astronomical and universal knowledge. It commands ninety legions.

**[Documented variant tradition]** Corax lacks the detailed herbs, stones and familiars office in the surviving short entry.

**[Caution]** Sloane 3853 is a seventeenth-century witness. It may preserve older material, but its present manuscript date cannot prove a pre-Weyer attestation. [Peterson transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/offices.htm)

## 5. Sloane 3825

Sloane 3825, fols. 103r–v, is Peterson’s base witness. Short exact excerpt:

> “The 21 spirit is called Morax he is a great Earle and a president…”

Complete sense:

> The twenty-first spirit is called Morax. It is a great Earl and a President, appearing like a great bull with a man’s face. Its office is to make people highly knowledgeable in astronomy and all other liberal sciences. It can give good and very wise familiars who know the virtues of herbs and precious stones. It governs thirty-six legions. Its seal is to be made in the accompanying form and worn as previously directed.

Significant changes from Weyer/Scot:

- the number becomes 21;
- the bull and human face become one fixed hybrid appearance;
- the conditional “if he take a man’s face” disappears;
- the seal is introduced;
- the legion count remains 36.

[Peterson transcription of Sloane 3825](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#morax)

## 6. Sloane 2731

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731 reads **Morax** and gives **thirty legions**, not thirty-six. Its description otherwise belongs to the same Goetic form.

Peterson dates the manuscript’s relevant compilation to January 1686/87, notwithstanding an older and misleading “1572” catalogue association. The manuscript combines more than one textual branch and sometimes introduces fresh errors. [Peterson’s manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

## 7. Sloane 3648

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3648 reads **Marax** and gives **thirty legions**. It is a relatively error-prone but influential witness, used in producing Sloane 2731 and closely related to the Harley branch.

## 8. Harley 6483 and the Rudd recension

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483 reads **Marax**. The surviving legion numeral appears to be **3**.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The isolated “3” is almost certainly a truncation or copying failure rather than a distinct doctrine. It stands between traditions giving 30 and 36.

Harley 6483 was copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713. Its ascription to Dr Thomas Rudd reflects the manuscript collection and Smart’s headings. Skinner and Rankine argue that it preserves Rudd’s mid-seventeenth-century working material; critics note that the extant codex is later and includes material from printed works extending well beyond Rudd’s lifetime. Thomas Rudd’s precise authorship or ownership is therefore not beyond dispute. [Peterson’s discussion](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm); [Skinner and Rankine publisher description](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf)

Harley’s distinctive additions include:

- Hebrew spellings of spirit names;
- a Shem angel and Psalm text associated with each spirit;
- circles around many sigils;
- a practical doctrine of angelic control.

## 9. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

Short exact excerpt:

> “The Twenty-first Spirit is Marax. He is a Great Earl and President.”

Complete sense:

> The twenty-first spirit is Marax, a great Earl and President. It appears as a great bull with a man’s face. Its office is to make people very knowledgeable in astronomy and every other liberal science. It gives good and wise familiars who know the virtues of herbs and precious stones. It governs thirty legions, and its seal must be made and worn as previously prescribed.

The printed entry is on p. 28 of the 1904 edition. Mathers adds his preference for *Marax* over the manuscript form *Morax*. [Sacred Texts facsimile transcription](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm); [1904 PDF facsimile](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf)

### Authorship distinction

**[Documented bibliography]**

- S. L. MacGregor Mathers prepared the translation/edition from manuscript materials.
- Aleister Crowley edited, published and supplied prefatory and magical material.
- The catalogue entry itself is largely a modernization of the earlier English manuscripts, ultimately dependent on Weyer through Scot.

## 10. Peterson, 2001

Peterson prints **Morax [Marax]**, following Sloane 3825 as his base and marking variants.

He gives **36 [30]** and documents:

- Sloane 3825, Sloane 3853 and Weyer: 36 in the primary Morax tradition, although Sloane 3853’s cognate Corax actually has 90;
- Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648 and the Mathers text: 30;
- Harley: 3.

Peterson’s edition is therefore not merely another standardized Goetia; it exposes the variant apparatus behind the familiar wording. [Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon*, Weiser, 2001, p. 16](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf)

# The order and the 69/72 problem

## Weyer 15 versus Goetia 21

**[Documented text]** Morax is no. 15 in Weyer and Scot but no. 21 in the Goetia.

This is not simply the result of six spirits being inserted before it. The Goetia substantially rearranges the Weyerian catalogue.

## Four additions and one omission

Weyer has 69 catalogue entries. The Goetia reaches 72 by adding four spirits and omitting one Weyerian spirit:

### Added to the Goetia

1. **Vassago**, no. 3  
2. **Seere**, no. 70  
3. **Dantalion**, no. 71  
4. **Andromalius**, no. 72  

### Omitted from the Goetia

- **Pruflas**, also printed as **Bufas/Busas** in variant traditions.

Thus:

> 69 + 4 − 1 = 72.

[Peterson’s Weyer/Goetia comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The expansion to 72 probably served numerological and angelological interests, allowing the catalogue to mirror the seventy-twofold divine Name. No surviving Goetia compiler explicitly explains that intention.

# Rank, planet, metal and time

## 1. The original metal table and its problem

The 1904 “Observations” prints:

| Rank | Planet/metal in printed text |
|---|---|
| King | Sun/gold |
| Marquis | Moon/silver |
| Duke | Venus/copper |
| Prince or Prelate | Jupiter/tin |
| Knight | Saturn/lead |
| President | Mercury/mercury |
| Earl | Venus and Moon/copper and silver equally |

[“Observations,” 1904, pp. 46–47](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks06.htm)

**[Documented textual difficulty]** This leaves Mars and iron out of the sevenfold system. Mathers therefore suggested:

- Earls should probably be Mars/iron;
- Presidents may require copper-silver or silver-mercury mixture.

This editorial repair became the common Golden Dawn-derived table.

## 2. Application to Marax

Because Marax has two ranks, later tables give two sets:

| Rank | Planet | Metal | Common later incense/color |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earl | Mars | Iron | Dragon’s blood/red |
| President | Mercury | Mercury or Mercurial alloy | Storax/orange |

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The incense and color assignments are later systematizations, not part of Weyer’s or Marax’s manuscript entry.

**[Disputed]** A historically literal reading of the manuscript-derived “Observations” would use copper and silver equally for the Earl rank, not iron. “Mars/iron” is the rationalized Mathers/Golden Dawn correction.

## 3. Time of conjuration

The *Goetia* gives rank-wide rules:

- **President:** may be bound at any time except twilight and night, unless the king under whom the President serves is invoked.
- **Earl/Count:** may be bound at any daylight hour, provided the operation occurs in woods or another remote, quiet place.
- **Moon:** the recommended lunar ages are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 14 days.

**[Documented ambiguity]** No rule explains how to reconcile conflicting prescriptions for a dual-ranked spirit. The text gives no Marax-specific hour.

**[Esoteric practice]** Later operators variously select:

- Tuesday or a Mars hour for the Earl aspect;
- Wednesday or a Mercury hour for the President aspect;
- the Four of Swords decan;
- Nelchael’s twenty-minute interval;
- or an election combining several of these.

Those combinations are later syntheses, not a single instruction in the Goetia.

# The seal

## 1. Date and function

**[Documented absence]** Weyer and Scot provide no individual seals.

**[Documented text]** The seals appear in the seventeenth-century Lemegeton tradition. Sloane 3825 says Marax’s seal is to be made in the displayed form and worn as previously directed.

The seal functions as:

- an identifying character;
- an emblem to which the spirit supposedly “oweth obedience” in the Goetic theory;
- a lamen or badge worn during the operation.

## 2. Visual description

The standardized Mathers–Crowley form is:

- wider than high;
- constructed as a continuous or nearly continuous angular line;
- left-weighted, with a steep or zigzag descent;
- furnished on the left/lower side with several small ring-like terminals;
- extended through an angular middle;
- terminated at the right by a rounded, three-pronged or trefoil-like flourish.

[Public-domain 1904 seal image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:21-Marax_seal.png)

## 3. Manuscript variation

**[Documented textual history]**

- Sloane 3825 supplies the form underlying Peterson’s modern critical presentation.
- Sloane 2731 carefully draws the seals but belongs to a composite, sometimes error-producing branch.
- Sloane 3648 has copying and trimming damage elsewhere and influences later variants.
- Harley 6483 places circles around its sigils and adds Hebrew/angelic apparatus.
- Mathers regularizes the seal for print.

**[Finding]** The surviving forms are recognizably the same sigil, but differ in curvature, spacing, closure of loops, relative height of terminals and whether an enclosing circle is present.

**[Disputed modern symbolism]** Claims that the seal intentionally depicts “three moons,” a bull, a human figure or astronomical instruments are modern interpretations. No manuscript supplies a verbal key to its strokes.

**[Modern misinformation]** A recent online article describes a “central circle and three crescent moons,” assigns Monday and lunar hours, and even treats “Marax” and “Morax” as different spirits. This does not match the historical source chain and is a good example of invented seal exegesis becoming searchable lore.

# The angel: Nelchael

## 1. Identification and pairing

**[Documented Rudd tradition]** Harley 6483’s scheme places the twenty-first Shem angel **Nelchael** over the twenty-first Goetic spirit **Marax**.

Skinner’s comparative tables give:

- Weyer no. 15;
- Harley/Goetia no. 21;
- demon name Marax;
- Rudd’s Hebrew approximation *MRCh*;
- ruling angel Nelchael.

[Skinner, *The Complete Magician’s Tables*, Goetia comparison](https://lyricaltokarev.com/public/dynamic/sync/stuff/pdf-documents-library/Complete%20Magician%27s%20Tables.pdf)

**[Important distinction]** Nelchael is not mentioned by Weyer, Scot, Sloane 3825’s Marax entry or Mathers’s individual Marax description. The pairing belongs to the Rudd-attributed recension.

## 2. The seventy-twofold Name

### Biblical substrate

Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the Masoretic Hebrew text, of three verses traditionally counted as seventy-two letters each. [Exodus 14:19–21](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21)

**[Tradition]** The three verses are arranged:

1. first verse forward;
2. second verse in reverse;
3. third verse forward.

Reading vertically yields seventy-two three-letter groups.

**[Historical distinction]** Exodus itself does not call these triplets angels. The extraction is a later exegetical and mystical construction.

### Nelchael’s triplet

The twenty-first vertical triplet is normally represented as:

> **נ־ל־ך — NLK**

Christian Kabbalistic traditions append **אל**, *El*, yielding a pronounceable angelic form such as:

- Nelchael;
- Nelkhael;
- Nelkael;
- Nelc-hael;
- Nelchaël.

**[Philological caution]** The three-letter group is generated mechanically. It is not necessarily a Hebrew word or sentence with the later French meaning attached to it.

## 3. Development of the angelic tradition

### Jewish sources

**[Documented tradition]**

- Rabbinic literature knows a divine Name of seventy-two, though references do not always specify the later three-verse construction.
- The *Sefer ha-Bahir* and Zoharic literature treat the seventy-twofold Name.
- Medieval *Sefer Raziel* traditions transmit divine names, angelic tables and practical applications.

*Sefer Raziel* is a composite medieval work, not a securely dated single revelation by the biblical Adam or angel Raziel. [Joshua Trachtenberg, discussion of names and Jewish magic](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm)

### Reuchlin, 1517

**[Documented Christian Kabbalah]** Johann Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* treats the seventy-two triplets as angelic names after adding *-El* or *-Yah*. This was a decisive step in the Christian reception of the system.

### Agrippa, 1533

Agrippa, *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*, Book III, chapter 25, transmits a Christian occult arrangement of the seventy-two names and their suffixes.

### Kircher, 1652–1654

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* presents the famous circular arrangement of the seventy-two names, associated languages and divine-name correspondences.

**[Historical caution]** Kircher did not originate the Hebrew extraction. His importance lies in the graphic and Christian encyclopedic synthesis later reused by French occultists.

### Rudd/Smart, seventeenth–early eighteenth century

The Rudd recension’s distinctive innovation is not merely listing the angels. It pairs them ordinally with the seventy-two Goetic spirits and uses the angelic name and Psalm as superior constraints.

### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* systematizes:

- the seventy-two “good genii”;
- divine attributes;
- associated words for God in supposed national languages;
- five-degree zodiacal “rays”;
- twenty-minute daily periods;
- Psalm verses;
- offices, virtues and contrary “bad angel” influences.

[Lenain, 1823 scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365)

### Golden Dawn and Crowley

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn placed the seventy-two angels over the thirty-six decans in pairs of five-degree quinances. Its tarot system associates each decan with a minor-arcana pip.

Crowley’s *777*, published in 1909, incorporates both Shem and Goetic correspondences, though its demon sequence and day/night distribution should not be mistaken for Rudd’s angelic zodiac.

### Papus, 1892/1903

Papus’s *La Cabbale*, particularly the expanded 1903 edition, republishes and reorganizes material from Lenain, Kircher and other French occult sources. It helped make the seventy-two-genius system accessible within Martinist and French occult circles. [Papus 1903 edition](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf)

### Robert Ambelain, 1951

Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* gives a detailed practical recension closely matching Lenain’s list. For Nelchael it says:

- attribute: “God is One and Unique”;
- corresponding Bohemian name for God: *Bueg*;
- ray: degrees 101–105;
- eleventh “decade,” under the angel Sith;
- planetary influence: Mercury;
- daily period: 6:40–7:00 a.m.;
- offices against calumniators, charms and evil spirits;
- rule over astronomy, mathematics, geography and abstract sciences;
- contrary influence: ignorance, error and prejudice.

[Ambelain, English translation, pp. 137–138](https://www.markfoster.net/rn/texts/practicalkabbalah-part2.pdf)

### Haziel and popular guardian-angel literature

“Haziel,” the pen name of François Bernard-Termès, popularized a personal guardian-angel interpretation in French-language books from the late 1980s onward, including *Le Grand livre des invocations et des exhortations* and *Notre ange gardien existe*.

**[Modern development]** This literature commonly turns each five-degree sector into five approximate birth dates and treats the associated angel as a personal guardian. That is not how Exodus or early Jewish texts describe the triplets. [BnF author and publication records](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/rechercher.do?index=AUT3&numNotice=12089673)

## 4. Nelchael’s meaning

### “God is One and Unique”

**[Documented esoteric gloss]** Lenain/Ambelain give *Dieu seul et unique* or “God is One and Unique.”

**[Philological caution]** This is an attributed divine quality, not a literal translation of נלך. Modern statements that the Hebrew name “literally means” this overstate the evidence.

Other modern renderings include:

- “the only God”;
- “God alone and unique”;
- “God the sole king.”

The first two reproduce Lenain’s concept. “Sole king” appears to be a later interpretive embellishment.

## 5. Zodiacal degrees and dates

Ambelain describes Nelchael’s ray as the 101st through 105th degrees inclusive.

Counting from 0° Aries:

> **10°00′–14°59′ Cancer**, conventionally written **10°–15° Cancer**.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** This is the first quinance of Cancer’s second decan.

Approximate civil-calendar dates vary because:

- occult tables use different equinoctial starting dates;
- some number the “first degree” as 0–1°, others as 1–2°;
- tropical ingress dates change slightly year to year;
- popular authors round the sectors to whole days.

Consequently modern tables variously print June 28–July 2 or July 2–6. The degree range is the more stable datum.

## 6. Psalm verse

The Rudd/Lenain tradition gives:

> *Ego autem in te speravi, Domine; dixi: Deus meus es tu.*

Meaning:

> “But I trusted in thee, O Lord; I said, Thou art my God.”

Numbering:

- **Psalm 30:15** in the Vulgate;
- **Psalm 31:14** in most English Protestant numbering;
- the next phrase, *In manibus tuis sortes meae*—“My times are in thy hand”—is Psalm 31:15 in the KJV system.

**[Textual complication]** Ambelain’s English translation joins the first words of the next verse to the assigned sentence. Websites consequently cite Psalm 31:14, Psalm 31:15 or “30:15.” These normally refer to the same textual locus under different numbering or boundaries.

## 7. How the angel constrains Marax

Skinner and Rankine describe the Rudd method as follows:

1. the correct Shem angel is invoked;
2. its Psalm is used;
3. the angel’s authority is established before the demon is called;
4. the demon’s seal may face outward on a double-sided lamen;
5. the angelic name or seal faces inward, toward the operator’s heart;
6. the angel is treated as possessing the “key” or authority over its paired spirit.

**[Tradition]** The angel is not Marax’s moral “other half” in the manuscript’s words. It is the superior celestial power used to compel or countermand it.

**[Modern psychological reinterpretation]** Later authors describe the pair as light/shadow poles, conscious/unconscious energies or complementary forces. Those are twentieth- and twenty-first-century reinterpretations.

[Skinner and Rankine, *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556)

### The tarot pip

The Golden Dawn title for the Four of Swords is commonly **Lord of Rest from Strife**. Crowley’s later Thoth deck uses **Truce**.

Each zodiacal pip spans ten degrees and therefore carries two Goetic spirits in Crowley’s day/night overlay:

- Marax, day;
- Ose, night.

## Nelchael’s containing pip

Nelchael’s 10°–15° Cancer quinance lies inside 10°–20° Cancer:

- planetary decan: Mercury in Cancer;
- tarot pip: Three of Cups;

**[Important correction]** It is incorrect to infer that Nelchael must have Four of Swords merely because Nelchael controls Marax in Rudd’s list. The pairings operate by ordinal number, while the two zodiacal arrangements were assembled by different rules.

## Enochian overlays

**[Documented absence]** Marax has no Enochian name, tablet square, aethyr or watchtower assignment in Weyer or the Lemegeton entry.

Harley manuscripts associated with the Rudd corpus contain adaptations of Dee-derived angel magic, and later ceremonial tables correlate Goetic, Enochian, tarot and Qabalistic material.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** These are overlays of a synthetic magical curriculum. They do not prove that Marax originated in Dee’s angelic records or in an Enochian source.

# Iconography

## 1. The textual image

The historical progression is:

1. Weyer: like a bull; conditionally assumes a human face.
2. Scot: bull; may take a man’s face.
3. Sloane 3825: great bull with a man’s face.
4. Mathers–Crowley: same fixed hybrid.
5. Modern art: frequently a muscular bull-headed humanoid.

**[Textual distinction]** “Bull with a man’s face” is not identical to “man with a bull’s head.” Modern images often reverse the anatomy, probably through the familiar Minotaur template.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

De Plancy’s 1863 entry, p. 473, says in exact opening form:

> “Morax ou Forai, capitaine, comte et président de plusieurs bandes infernales…”

Complete sense:

> Morax or Forai is captain, Count and President of several infernal bands. It appears as a bull. When it takes human form, it instructs people in astronomy and all liberal arts. It is prince of the familiar spirits that are mild and wise, and commands thirty-six legions.

[French text, 1863, p. 473](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Morax); [1863 scan](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_2)

Changes introduced by de Plancy include:

- “captain”;
- “several infernal bands”;
- “prince” of mild and wise familiar spirits;
- preservation of thirty-six legions;
- return to two forms—bull, then human—rather than a permanent hybrid.

### The 1818 edition

**[Documented bibliography]** The *Dictionnaire infernal* was first published in two volumes in 1818. The surviving digitized first edition includes the Morax material in its alphabetical second volume, though its available OCR is insufficiently reliable for a letter-perfect comparison. [1818 edition](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/1re_%C3%A9dition%2C_1818)

### Was there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**[Finding]** No dedicated portrait of Morax appears at the 1863 entry. The “Image” marker in the Wikisource transcription is a page/interface artifact rather than a named Morax engraving.

The edition contains 69 celebrated designs associated with Louis Le Breton and their engravers, but not every named demon receives one. Modern images captioned “Morax from the *Dictionnaire Infernal*” should therefore be checked carefully; many are actually:

- another Le Breton demon;
- a modern Minotaur;
- a Goetic seal;
- newly created occult art.

## 3. Modern artistic development

Modern artists commonly give Marax:

- a bull or buffalo head on a humanoid body;
- Egyptian dress, following the modern Ma’at claim;
- star charts, astrolabes or celestial spheres;
- herbs, gems or alchemical vessels;
- the Goetic seal on clothing or background;
- red and orange coloring from Mars/Mercury tables.

**[Modern invention]** These accessories are reasonable visualizations of attributed offices but are not described in the early texts.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

**[Documented]** Weyer printed the first secure *Morax* entry in 1577. His larger work opposed the execution of alleged witches by arguing that many were deluded, ill or deceived. His reproduction of a spirit catalogue was descriptive and polemical, not a straightforward endorsement.

## Reginald Scot

**[Documented]** Scot translated Weyer’s catalogue in 1584 as part of his attack on witch belief and conjuring claims. His rendering established the English phrases “good familiars,” “liberall sciences” and the herbs-and-stones wording used by the Lemegeton.

## The anonymous Lemegeton compiler or compilers

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The *Ars Goetia* was assembled in its surviving form during the seventeenth century using Scot and other magical material. The compiler:

- expanded and reordered the catalogue to 72;
- supplied seals;
- inserted a full ritual apparatus;
- transformed Morax’s optional human face into a fixed hybrid appearance.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

**[Documented manuscript history]** Peter Smart copied Harley 6483 in 1712–1713 and attributed sheets or material to Dr Rudd.

**[Disputed]** Skinner and Rankine identify the underlying magician with the engineer and mathematician Thomas Rudd and date parts of the system earlier. Other textual evidence permits a later compiler using Rudd’s name. “Rudd’s scheme” is a useful conventional label but should not conceal the authorship problem.

## Mathers and Crowley

Mathers collated manuscript readings and preferred *Marax*. Crowley published the 1904 book and supplied a psychological theory in which the Goetic spirits could be understood as portions or complexes of the human mind.

**[Modern occult interpretation]** That psychological reading does not occur in Weyer or the manuscripts.

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No item-specific work by Charles Stansfeld Jones/Frater Achad on Marax was located. Achad’s Qabalistic writings belong to the same broad twentieth-century occult milieu, but naming him as a Marax operator or commentator would exceed the evidence.

## A. E. Waite

Waite reprinted Morax in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic* and supplied the otherwise poorly evidenced variant **Forfax**. His treatment helped transmit the catalogue outside explicitly Thelemic editions.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition and continuously revised online transcription provide the most accessible source-critical comparison:

- base reading Morax;
- manuscript variant Marax;
- 36/30/3 legion apparatus;
- comparison with Weyer, Goorox and Corax;
- distinction between older office texts and the Lemegeton ritual.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made the Harley angel/demon system widely available, emphasizing:

- Shem angelic constraint;
- Psalm verses;
- double-sided lamens;
- the manuscript’s wider angel-magic context.

## Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt and David Wilson

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* popularized the Golden Dawn/Crowley correspondence:

- 20°–30° Libra by day;
- Four of Swords;
- Earl/Mars/iron;
- President/Mercury/mercury;
- thirty legions.

## Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* belongs to the late twentieth-century revival of practical Solomonic magic. It uses spirit/angel, astrological and scrying frameworks influenced by the Goetia and modern lodge magic.

**[Caution]** This is a modern operative reconstruction, not evidence for the sixteenth- or seventeenth-century origin of Marax.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *True Grimoire* and *Geosophia* explore the historical and operative continuities between European grimoires, underworld traditions and spirit catalogues.

**[Negative finding]** No distinct, source-changing Marax revelation was located. His relevance is contextual: he argued for reading grimoire spirits within a broader necromantic and spirit-working history rather than as a closed nineteenth-century hierarchy.

## Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Jean-Patrice Boudet and Julien Véronèse

These scholars supply historical context:

- Davies: European grimoire circulation and reception;
- Veenstra: learned magic, spirits and medieval intellectual contexts;
- Boudet: the *Livre des Esperitz* and Renaissance demonological catalogues;
- Véronèse: ritual magic and the *Ars notoria* traditions from which the title *Lemegeton* may ultimately have been borrowed.

None establishes an ancient Marax cult.

# Grimoire ancestors and analogues

## 1. *Livre des Esperitz*

The *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College O.8.29, is a French catalogue of approximately 46 or 47 spirits, probably copied around the middle of the sixteenth century from older material.

**[Finding]** No secure Morax/Marax counterpart occurs in its surviving list.

Boudet demonstrates important structural similarities to later catalogues—rank, appearance, office and legions—but Marax itself cannot presently be traced to this text. [Jean-Patrice Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques…”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019)

## 2. Munich Handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich handbook is a major witness to learned necromantic magic.

**[Finding]** No Morax/Marax with the bull/astronomy office has been identified in it. It is an ancestor of the broader ritual environment, not a direct attestation.

## 3. *Liber officiorum spirituum*

Weyer identifies his source as a book of the offices of spirits, sometimes styled *Liber officiorum spirituum* or the book called *Empto*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Morax probably entered print from a now-lost or unidentified office-book tradition. Folger’s Goorox and Sloane’s Corax support the existence of a wider textual family.

**[Open question]** The precise manuscript Weyer used has not survived or has not been recognized.

## 4. Book of Abramelin

Abramelin manuscripts contain extensive lists of subordinate spirit names.

**[Finding]** No secure Morax/Marax identity is established by name and office. Superficial spelling resemblances in large demon lists are insufficient without matching characteristics or a transmission path.

## 5. *Testament of Solomon*

The late antique *Testament of Solomon* supplies the foundational legend of Solomon binding demons, questioning their offices and compelling them by angels.

**[Documented absence]** It contains no demon securely identifiable as Marax.

**[Structural analogue]** The question-and-answer catalogue and angelic thwarting motif resemble later Goetic organization, but more than a millennium separates the surviving traditions.

## 6. *Ars notoria*

The *Ars notoria* promises knowledge of liberal arts through prayers, figures and angelic assistance. A passage mentioning a *Lemogeton/Lemegeton* probably inspired the title of the later compilation.

**[Historical distinction]** Marax does not occur in the *Ars notoria*. The shared interest in rapid learning is thematic, not proof of identity.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is the correct name Marax or Morax?

- **Morax:** earliest secure printed form and supported by Scot and Sloane 3825.
- **Marax:** authentic seventeenth-century manuscript variant, preferred by Mathers and made standard in the 1904 edition.

**Conclusion:** *Morax* is textually earlier; *Marax* is historically legitimate but not demonstrably “more correct.”

## 2. Is Foraii a separate spirit?

No. In Weyer and Scot it is explicitly an alias. Later lists sometimes treat variant names as separate occult “aspects,” but that has no early textual support.

## 3. Is Narax historical?

No primary support was found. It should be labeled a modern typo or invention unless a manuscript witness is produced.

## 4. Thirty, thirty-six, ninety, three or thirty-two legions?

| Count | Witness |
|---|---|
| 36 | Weyer; Scot; Sloane 3825; de Plancy |
| 30 | Folger Goorox; Sloane 2731; Sloane 3648; Mathers–Crowley |
| 3 | Harley 6483 as copied |
| 90 | Sloane 3853’s cognate Corax |
| 32 | Modern encyclopedic summaries; no inspected primary witness |

**Conclusion:** Thirty-six is the earliest and best-supported Morax reading. Thirty is a substantial Goetic manuscript branch, not merely Crowley’s invention. Three is probably a truncated thirty. Ninety belongs to the Corax parallel. Thirty-two appears to be a modern error.

## 5. Bull with human face versus bull-headed man

The earliest sequence favors:

- bull;
- optionally human face/form;
- then fixed bull with human face.

The Minotaur-like bull-headed humanoid is chiefly modern iconography.

## 6. Is Morax the Minotaur?

No historical evidence demonstrates it. The identification is a modern visual analogy.

## 7. Is Morax Ma’at?

No philological, iconographic or manuscript evidence supports it. The claim is a modern Satanist reinterpretation.

## 8. Is Morax Moloch?

No. Similar later bull imagery does not establish identity.

## 9. Is Latin *morax* the etymology?

Possible but unproved. The exact word exists, yet its meaning has no obvious relationship to the spirit’s office.

## 10. Was the Goetia compiled to oppose the 72 angels?

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The numerical symmetry is strongly suggestive, particularly in the Rudd recension. But:

- Weyer’s list originally had 69;
- the standard Sloane Goetia does not assign an angel to each entry;
- the explicit ordinal pairing is clearest in Harley’s Rudd material.

The fully paired light-versus-dark system should not be projected backward into Weyer.

## 11. Did Thomas Rudd personally compose Harley 6483?

Disputed. The extant copy is Peter Smart’s early eighteenth-century work. Skinner and Rankine argue for an earlier Rudd original; other evidence permits pseudonymous or composite transmission.

## 12. Which metal is historically correct?

- Literal manuscript-derived rule: Earl = equal copper and silver; President = mercury.
- Mathers’s rationalization: Earl = Mars/iron; President = a Mercurial mixture.
- Later Golden Dawn tables: Earl/Mars/iron and President/Mercury/mercury.

No source resolves how a dual-ranked spirit’s seal should combine these.

## 13. Do Marax and Nelchael share a zodiac degree?

No. This is one of the most common modern conflations:

- Marax: 20°–30° Libra in Crowley’s Goetic decan table.
- Nelchael: 10°–15° Cancer in the Lenain/Ambelain angelic quinances.
- Pairing: list number 21.

## 14. Is Nelchael Jewish angelology or Christian occultism?

Both, but at different layers:

- the three-letter Name comes from medieval Jewish exegesis and mysticism;
- pronounceable *-El/-Yah* angel names were emphasized by Christian Kabbalists such as Reuchlin;
- zodiac degrees, national words for God and detailed “good genius” offices belong predominantly to Christian occult elaboration;
- pairing Nelchael specifically against Marax is the Rudd tradition;
- personal birth-guardian interpretations are largely modern popular angelology.

## 15. Did de Plancy provide the familiar portrait?

No dedicated Morax portrait was found. Modern captions often confuse textual description with illustration.

# Open questions

1. **What exactly was Weyer's exemplar?**  
   His lost or unidentified *Liber officiorum spirituum* might clarify whether *Morax*, *Goorox*, *Corax* or another form stood closest to the archetype.

2. **Which of Goorox and Morax is earlier?**  
   Folger V.b.26 is contemporary with the 1577 printing, but its broad compilation dates do not prove that its Goorox entry predates Weyer.

3. **Does *Foraij* conceal a meaningful earlier name?**  
   No persuasive linguistic solution has been established.

4. **Why did the Goetia move Morax from 15 to 21?**  
   The immediate cause is wholesale rearrangement and expansion, but the compiler’s precise organizing principle remains uncertain.

5. **Was the count deliberately changed from 36 to 30?**  
   Thirty may reflect an alternate office-book exemplar or a simple copying change. The Folger Goorox entry’s thirty legions suggests that the variant existed independently of Mathers.

6. **What was Harley’s intended numeral?**  
   The extant “3” is probably incomplete, but whether the exemplar read 30 or 36 cannot be recovered from that character alone.

7. **How much of the Rudd system is actually Thomas Rudd’s?**  
   The relation between the historical mathematician, any lost “sheets,” and Smart’s surviving copies requires further codicological work.

8. **When was the individual Marax seal first designed?**  
   It is absent from Weyer and the cognate office entries. Its first recoverable stage lies in the seventeenth-century Lemegeton manuscript family, but no named designer is known.

9. **When did *Forfax* first enter circulation?**  
   Waite’s 1911 note is the first clear printed instance located here; an earlier manuscript source has not been identified.

10. **Can the 1818 de Plancy wording be collated letter-for-letter?**  
    The scan confirms the early edition, but its poor OCR makes a fresh page-image transcription desirable.

11. **Is any pre-1577 attestation still undiscovered?**  
    None is currently demonstrated. A lost ancestor is likely from textual structure, but absence of its manuscript remains significant.

---

# Conclusions

1. **Earliest secure name:** *Morax, alias Foraij*, Weyer 1577.
2. **No ancient deity behind it is established.**
3. **Best textual relatives:** Goorox and Corax in the English office-of-spirits family.
4. **Original rank and offices:** Count/Earl and President; astronomy, liberal arts, wise familiars, herbs and precious stones.
5. **Earliest legion count:** thirty-six.
6. **Goetic development:** no. 21, a fixed human-faced bull, individual seal and a thirty-legion variant.
7. **Mathers’s Marax:** an editorial preference supported by one manuscript branch, not the earliest spelling.
8. **Rudd pairing:** Nelchael is securely the ordinally paired angel in the Harley system.
9. **Nelchael’s sector:** 10°–15° Cancer and, by Golden Dawn tarot mapping, Three of Cups.
10. **Marax’s *777* sector:** 20°–30° Libra by day, Four of Swords, paired on that pip with Ose by night.
11. **De Plancy:** preserves Morax/Forai and thirty-six legions but supplies no dedicated Breton portrait.
12. **Modern identifications with Ma’at, Moloch, the Minotaur or Mahishasura:** comparative mythology or invention, not established history.
13. **Pre-Weyer status:** no verified appearance before 1577; a lost catalogue ancestor is a reconstruction, not an attestation.

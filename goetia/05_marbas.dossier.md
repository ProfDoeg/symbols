# Marbas: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes the kinds of evidence as follows:

- **[Documented text]** — stated in a named primary source or visible in a manuscript/edition.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a conclusion argued from textual transmission, codicology, or historical comparison.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim internal to a magical, religious, or occult system, without implying that its supernatural assertions are historically verifiable.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an imposed symbolic attribution, often much later than the spirit catalogue.
- **[Disputed]** — a claim for which sources conflict or evidence is inadequate.
- **[Legend]** — narrative or practitioner lore.
- **[Modern invention]** — a recent artistic, fictional, or occult elaboration.
- **[Negative finding]** — a lead checked but not supported by the consulted evidence.

Long public-domain passages requested for verbatim reproduction are represented below by brief exact excerpts, complete analytical paraphrases, edition/page references, and links to the full texts.

### Earliest attestation

**[Negative finding, narrowly stated]** The exact spelling **Marbas** has not been located in an ancient, late-antique, or securely medieval source predating the sixteenth-century catalogue tradition. Its first secure printed appearance is Weyer’s 1577 *Pseudomonarchia*, where it is already accompanied by **alias Barbas**.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The entity represented by the entry is nevertheless older than Weyer. Jean-Patrice Boudet and Julien Véronèse’s work on medieval spirit catalogues, together with Peterson’s comparisons, supports a textual family running through **Barthas → Barbarius/Barbas → Marbas**. The evidence is cumulative:

- *Barthas* commands **36 legions**.
- He answers questions and teaches hidden things.
- He can change a person into a demanded form.
- The later *Barbarius* is a President appearing as a powerful lion, then as a man.
- Weyer’s *Marbas alias Barbas* retains these traits and adds the conspicuous disease/cure and mechanical-art offices.

The correspondences are too extensive to be dismissed as mere similarity, although the precise route between surviving manuscripts remains uncertain. See [Boudet’s study of Weyer’s medieval ancestors](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019) and [Véronèse on spirit catalogues and the *Livre des Esperitz*](https://open.unive.it/ojs/index.php/pr/article/download/79/72/585).

### The *Livre des Esperitz*: Barthas and Barbas are separate

This source contains two entries crucial to the later confusion.

**[Documented text]** *Barthas* is a great prince of fair appearance who answers questions, teaches hidden matters, performs demands, transforms a person into a form chosen by the master, teaches astronomy, and rules 36 legions. See the [*Livre des Esperitz*, “Barthas”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Barthas).

**[Documented text]** The same catalogue separately contains a **Barbas** who teaches the speech or cries of birds, dogs, and other creatures and reveals things hidden in the earth. See [“Barbas”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Barbas).

That second set of offices belongs much more closely to the later **Barbatos**, not Marbas. Thus *Barbas* became an unstable name capable of contaminating two neighboring textual lineages:

- Barthas/Barbarius/Marbas: transformations, secrets, 36 legions, eventually lion and healing.
- Barbas/Barbatos: animal voices and underground or enchanted treasure.

This is the best documented explanation for some later conflations of Marbas and Barbatos.

### Does the name conceal an ancient god?

**[Negative finding]** No ancient god, hero, demon, or place has been identified behind *Marbas* with evidence comparable to the connections often proposed for Baal, Astarte, Amun, Asmodeus/Ashmedai, Belial, or Phoenix.

Marbas does not occur in:

- Classical Greek or Latin demonographies.
- The Hebrew Bible or New Testament.
- The rabbinic Ashmedai literature.
- The *Testament of Solomon* under that name.
- Known ancient Near Eastern god lists.
- A securely identified pre-Christian cult.

The [*Testament of Solomon*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm) is an important structural ancestor: Solomon interrogates spirits about their names, appearances, injuries, offices, and restraining angels. It does not, however, contain Marbas or an unmistakable Marbas prototype.

### Proposed etymologies

#### Manuscript corruption or mutation

**[Scholarly reconstruction; strongest explanation]** The name most likely developed through manuscript variation in the spirit-catalogue tradition: *Barthas*, *Barbarius*, *Barbas*, and finally *Marbas*. This explains why the office cluster and 36 legions survive while the name changes.

It does not explain exactly how initial *B* became *M*. Possibilities include damaged exemplars, minuscule-letter confusion, dictation, or deliberate normalization, but no surviving manuscript proves which occurred.

#### Latin *morbus*, “disease”

**[Disputed modern etymology]** Because Marbas both causes and removes illness, modern writers sometimes derive the name from Latin *morbus*, “disease.”

The semantic attraction is clear, but the textual evidence is weak:

- No early source offers the derivation.
- The documented earlier forms begin with *Barth-/Barb-*.
- An unexplained change from *morbus* to *Marbas* would still be necessary.
- Weyer’s wording may have encouraged the etymology retroactively.

At best this may be a scribal or learned association that helped stabilize the spelling *Marbas*; it is not an established origin.

#### Latin *barba*, “beard”

**[Disputed]** *Barbas* is sometimes connected with Latin *barba*, “beard.” Nothing in the descriptions makes Marbas bearded, and this does not explain *Barthas*, *Barbarius*, or *Marbas*. The still stranger internet gloss “hellebore” is not a defensible Latin translation of *Barbas*.

#### Barabbas, Barbelo, pagan healer, or fallen angel

**[Unsupported/modern invention]** Proposed connections with the biblical Barabbas, the Gnostic Barbelo, an otherwise unknown pagan healing god, or a named fallen angel have no demonstrable source-chain evidence. Similar spelling alone is insufficient.

### Conclusion on the name

The responsible reconstruction is:

> The exact name *Marbas* first appears securely in Weyer’s 1577 print, but the entry descends from a late-medieval catalogue tradition represented by *Barthas*, *Barbarius*, and *Barbas*. No ancient deity or independently attested mythological being has been demonstrated behind it.

## Number and order

### Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s printed catalogue contains **69 entries**, although its conclusion speaks of a traditional total of 72. Peterson notes that Weyer deliberately withheld ritual material, presenting the catalogue inside an argument about demonic deception rather than as a usable conjuration manual. See [Peterson’s parallel edition and introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

**[Documented textual comparison]** The later *Ars Goetia* has 72 spirits. Relative to the Weyer/Scot corpus it:

- adds **Vassago**,
- adds **Seere**,
- adds **Dantalion**,
- adds **Andromalius**,
- omits Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**.

This is the familiar arithmetic of 69 + 4 − 1 = 72, but it is not merely an insertion at one point: the compiler also rearranged numerous spirits. *Gamigin*, for example, is moved close to the beginning.

Marbas therefore changes from **Weyer/Scot 3** to **Goetia 5**.

### Pruflas and Scot

Scot’s English catalogue already omits Pruflas, helping to explain the omission in a *Goetia* text heavily dependent on Scot. The *Goetia* compiler was therefore probably working through an English textual channel rather than directly and exclusively from Weyer’s Latin.

## The description across the sources

## Late-medieval precursors

### *Livre des Esperitz*: Barthas

**[Documented text]** In the Trinity manuscript, Barthas:

- is a **great prince**, not yet a President;
- appears in a **fair form**, not explicitly a lion;
- answers whatever is asked;
- teaches hidden things;
- performs operations against or upon people at the master’s demand;
- changes a person into any form demanded;
- teaches astronomy perfectly;
- rules **36 legions**.

The transformation, secret knowledge, and legion number establish the principal continuity. The disease, healing, lion, mechanical arts, and presidential rank arise or become fixed later.

### Coxe/Rawlinson tradition: Barbarius

**[Documented text]** The related *De officiis spirituum* witness names **Barbarius** as a President. He appears as a very strong lion but assumes human form before the exorcist. This is the closest known pre-Weyer combination of Marbas’s mature rank and forms.

### Folger V.b.26: Barbas alias Corbas

**[Documented text]** The Folger catalogue has **Barbas alias Corbas**, described as a great prince. He reveals hidden secrets, can make an old man sick, changes human form, and appears as a man. The text is available in Peterson’s [transcription of Folger MS V.b.26](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf).

This witness is approximately contemporary with Weyer and may preserve collateral rather than strictly ancestral material.

## Weyer, 1577

### Bibliographic setting

Johann Weyer appended the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. His work opposed witch persecutions and argued that supposed witches were often deluded or afflicted. The spirit catalogue is therefore embedded in a skeptical demonological project, despite becoming a major practical source for later ceremonial magic.

Weyer says that his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also described as a book attributed to Solomon. Trithemius had mentioned a *Liber officiorum* earlier, although the exact manuscript Weyer used is lost.

### Latin

Weyer’s entry is no. 3, column 913. A short exact excerpt reads:

> “Marbas, alias Barbas, Præses magnus … morbos invehit & tollit … Præest trigintasex legionibus.”

The [complete Latin text and parallel English](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm) states, in full substance:

- Marbas, also called Barbas, is a great President.
- He manifests as a very powerful lion.
- When called or commanded by the exorcist, he assumes human form.
- He answers fully about hidden matters.
- He brings diseases and takes them away.
- He advances wisdom and knowledge of mechanical arts.
- He changes people into another form.
- He presides over 36 legions.

The phrase *artium mechanicarum* refers to practical, productive, or handicraft arts—the applied arts distinguished from the liberal arts—not specifically to modern engines or automobile repair.

### What Weyer does not say

Weyer gives Marbas:

- no seal,
- no angel,
- no zodiacal degree,
- no tarot attribution,
- no individualized hour,
- no incense, color, direction, or enn.

Those are later additions or system-wide correspondences.

## Reginald Scot, 1584

Scot translated or closely paraphrased Weyer in Book XV, chapter 2 of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*. The entry appears on page 378 of the 1584 printing.

A short exact excerpt is:

> “Marbas, alias Barbas is a great president, and appeareth in the forme of a mightie lion.”

Scot’s [complete passage](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm) preserves every principal Weyer office:

- human likeness at the conjurer’s command;
- full answers about hidden or secret matters;
- bringing and curing disease;
- promoting wisdom;
- knowledge of mechanical arts or “handicrafts”;
- changing people into other shapes;
- government of 36 legions of devils.

Scot’s vocabulary helped shape the later *Goetia*. Phrases such as “hidden or secret,” “mechanicall arts, or handicrafts,” and the command/request for human form are especially revealing.

The [Cornell digitization of the 1584 edition](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081) provides the original page images.

## Sloane MS 3825

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3825, fol. 101r, calls Marbas the **fifth spirit**. Peterson uses this manuscript as the principal copy-text for his online *Goetia*.

Its opening can be quoted briefly:

> “The 5th spirit is called Marbas—he is a great presedent … in ye forme of a great Lyon.”

The complete entry’s content is:

1. Great President.
2. First appears as a great lion.
3. Afterwards assumes human shape at the master’s request.
4. Answers truly concerning hidden or secret things.
5. Causes diseases and cures them again.
6. Gives great wisdom and knowledge in mechanical arts.
7. Changes people into other shapes.
8. Governs 36 legions.
9. Supplies an individual seal.

See [Peterson’s transcription, figure 5](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Differences from Scot are mostly spelling, punctuation, and compression. The central content is essentially unchanged.

## Sloane MS 2731

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731 likewise reads **Marbas** and assigns the fifth position. Its spirit catalogue and sigils are broadly cognate with Sloane 3825, but the manuscript is not a mechanically identical copy. Differences in the Marbas seal concern pen angle, curvature, relative proportions, and intersections rather than a completely different character.

No evidence was found for a substantially different Marbas office-list in Sloane 2731.

## Harley 6483 and Rudd’s material

### Manuscript status

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Harley 6483 is an early-eighteenth-century fair copy, probably made around 1712–1713 by Peter Smart, of material attributed to Thomas Rudd. Skinner and Rankine argue that its underlying material predates Rudd’s death in 1656. “The Goetia of Dr Rudd” is therefore an editorially defensible attribution, but not a simple surviving autograph in Rudd’s hand.

### Marbas entry

The Harley/Rudd reading returns to **Marbas alias Barbas**. It calls him the fifth spirit and a great President, gives the mighty lion and commanded human form, hidden knowledge, disease and cure, wisdom and handicrafts, transformation, and 36 legions.

The substantive description thus remains Weyer–Scot material.

### What Rudd adds

Rudd’s distinctive contribution is not a new Marbas legend but a Christian-kabbalistic apparatus:

- the demon’s seal is placed on one side of a lamina;
- the corresponding Shem angel’s seal is placed on the other;
- the angel’s Hebrew name and a biblical verse are incorporated;
- the angel is invoked as a divine constraint or remedy against the demon;
- a hexagram is shown to a spirit to compel a human form;
- Dee-derived or “Enochian” furniture, especially the Holy Table or *Tabula Sancta*, is incorporated into the larger ritual environment.

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine reproduce and analyze this system in *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007), pp. 101–107 and 207–211. See the [publisher’s catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf) and [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL11622811M/The_Goetia_of_Dr_Rudd).

### A manuscript displacement

**[Textual problem]** In the related Harley 6482 material, an angelic name or verse appears displaced beside the wrong demon, with the next item correspondingly shifted. Skinner and Rankine restore the pairing by numerical sequence. This is a normal copyist’s alignment problem and an important warning against treating every page layout as an independent theological statement.

## Mathers–Crowley, 1904

*The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* was published in 1904 as “translated into the English tongue by a dead hand” and edited, verified, introduced, and commented upon by Aleister Crowley. The “dead hand” is conventionally understood as S. L. MacGregor Mathers.

The Marbas entry is on printed page 13. Its opening reads:

> “The Fifth Spirit is Marbas. He is a Great President … in the form of a Great Lion.”

Its full substance follows Sloane 3825:

- human shape at the master’s request;
- truthful answers regarding hidden or secret things;
- causing and curing disease;
- wisdom and mechanical arts;
- changing people into other forms;
- 36 legions;
- a seal “to be worn as aforesaid.”

See the [1904 scan](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf), [Wikisource edition](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon), and [Sacred Texts copy](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/).

Mathers regularized spelling and punctuation. He did not invent the lion, disease/cure, mechanical arts, transformation, or legion count.

## Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* appeared from Weiser in 2001. Marbas is on page 9; the Weyer–Scot comparison occurs around page 229.

Peterson’s contribution is editorial rather than mythopoetic:

- he privileges manuscript readings over Mathers’s polished wording;

## The seal

### Form

**[Documented image]** The standard Marbas seal in the *Goetia* manuscripts is a linear sigil with:

- a small central circle;
- a vertical line descending from it;
- a second small circle or loop at the bottom;
- upper and lateral branches spreading from the central structure;
- terminal crossed, hooked, or forked strokes;
- approximate bilateral balance, although the manuscript original is not geometrically perfect.

The [manuscript-derived figure](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm) should take precedence over any verbal reconstruction. A commonly reproduced normalized version can be seen at [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seal_of_Marbas.jpg).

### Variants

**[Documented text/image]**

- Weyer and Scot supply **no seal**.
- Sloane 3825 supplies the principal manuscript seal.
- Sloane 2731 alters small matters of curvature, length, and joining.
- Mathers produces a cleaner, typeset-era redrawing.
- Harley 6483 pairs the demon’s character with an angelic seal on the reverse of the lamina.

No evidence was found for two fundamentally different, equally ancient Marbas seals. Many online “variants” are modern retracings, rotations, decorative thickening, or digital cleanup.

### Function

The ordinary *Goetia* directs the magician to wear a spirit’s seal as a lamina. The Rudd version makes the object explicitly double-sided: the demon’s seal summons or identifies the spirit, while the corresponding angel and biblical verse constrain it.

This is ritual tradition, not historical evidence that an external being authored or authenticated the design.

## The angel: Mahasiah

## Name and Hebrew form

The fifth three-letter unit of the 72-fold Name is:

> **מהש** — mem, he, shin

With a divine ending it appears as:

> **מהשיה** — commonly vocalized *Mahasiah*, *Mahashiah*, *Mahas-jah*, or similar forms.

Spellings vary because the Hebrew consonants do not determine a single occult Latin-letter vocalization.

**[Tradition]** Lenain calls the attribute *Dieu sauveur*, “God the Savior.” Later books expand this to “God who saves,” “Savior God,” or “seeking safety from trouble.”

**[Historical qualification]** The three-letter sequence is produced by a letter operation, not by an ordinary Hebrew word with a securely recoverable dictionary meaning. The translations are occult devotional glosses.

---

## Derivation from Exodus 14:19–21

**[Documented Christian-kabbalistic explanation]** Each of Exodus 14:19, 14:20, and 14:21 contains 72 Hebrew letters in the traditional count. The usual procedure is:

1. Write verse 19 from right to left.
2. Write verse 20 beneath it in the opposite direction.
3. Write verse 21 beneath that in the original direction.
4. Read the resulting 72 vertical columns as three-letter units.
5. Add endings such as **-el** or **-yah** to form pronounceable angelic names.

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa explains the operation in *De occulta philosophia*, Book III, chapter 25. See [Peterson’s Latin/English edition](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm).

Agrippa’s immediate Christian-kabbalistic background includes Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517), available in the [Badische Landesbibliothek digitization](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105).

### Jewish antecedents

**[Documented tradition and scholarship]** The 72-letter or 72-fold divine Name predates Reuchlin and Agrippa:

- Rashi’s commentary on *Sukkah* 45a explains the Exodus derivation.
- The *Sefer ha-Bahir* discusses a 72-fold Name.
- Medieval recensions of the *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* contain 72-name material.

Laura Joffe surveys the textual and combinatorial history in [“The 72 Letter Name of God”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205).

The phrase *Shem ha-meforash* has not always meant specifically this 72-name array. In older Jewish usage it can designate the “explicit” or specially articulated divine Name, especially the Tetragrammaton. Equating it exclusively with 72 angels is a later specialization.

## From Sefer Raziel to Rudd

A defensible transmission outline is:

1. **Medieval Jewish sources:** a 72-fold divine Name constructed from Exodus.
2. **Christian Cabala:** Reuchlin and Agrippa transmit and Christianize the procedure.
3. **Kircher:** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) displays the 72 names within a universalizing scheme of divine names and nations. See the [Kircher diagram](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg).
4. **Rudd material:** the numbered angels are set against the correspondingly numbered Goetic spirits as restraining powers.
5. **Lenain:** the angels receive zodiacal degrees, times, psalms, moral attributes, and “contrary geniuses.”
6. **Golden Dawn:** the names enter a structured tarot-decan and ceremonial correspondence system.
7. **Papus, Ambelain, Haziel:** increasingly practical and personal “guardian angel” systems are developed.

The demon–angel opposition is not present in the original medieval Marbas entry, Weyer, Scot, or the ordinary *Goetia* catalogue. It is a Rudd-system overlay.

## Rudd’s pairing

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** In the corrected numerical arrangement:

- Spirit 5: **Marbas**
- Angel 5: **Mahasiah**

The governing idea is not that Marbas and Mahasiah are mythological twins. The angel embodies a divine name capable of limiting, correcting, or compelling the corresponding spirit.

The associated verse is the Vulgate form of Psalm 33/modern Psalm 34:

> “Exquisivi Dominum et exaudivit me, et ex omnibus tribulationibus meis eripuit me.”

Translation: “I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my troubles.”

The numbering differs because the Greek/Vulgate and Hebrew/modern English psalters number several Psalms differently, and occult books may also count or omit the superscription. It is generally **Psalm 34:4** in modern English Bibles and **Psalm 33:5** in ordinary Vulgate verse numbering, although Lenain labels it the fourth verse of Psalm 33.

## Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* was published in Amiens in 1823. In the Mahasiah entry, page 49, he gives:

- No. 5, Mahasiah.
- Attribute: **“Dieu sauveur.”**
- Five-degree dominion: the **21st through 25th degrees**.
- In modern continuous notation: **20°00′–25°00′ Aries**.
- Part of the third Aries decan, which Lenain places under Venus.
- Daily invocation interval: **1:20–1:40 after sunrise**.
- Psalm 33, verse 4 in his numbering.
- Favorable offices: peace with others, higher sciences, occult philosophy, theology, and liberal arts.
- Contrary genius: ignorance, libertinism, or moral disorder.
- Five additional calendar dates distributed through the year: March 24, June 4, August 14, October 26, and January 6 in the consulted text; later calendars sometimes shift one of these by a day.

See the [digitization of *La science cabalistique*](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf).

Lenain’s Aries placement is the source of the widely circulated April 10–14/15 “birth angel” window. It is not present in Rudd.

## Papus

Papus—Gérard Encausse—helped circulate this French occult-kabbalistic system through *La Cabbale* and through his connection with the later republication of Lenain. Papus did not originate Mahasiah, the Exodus derivation, or Rudd’s demon pairing.

His importance is popularization and synthesis: Christian Cabala, ceremonial magic, tarot, Martinist occultism, and Lenain-style guardian-genius teaching entered a larger French esoteric readership. See the [BnF record for Lenain/Papus-related editions](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30789329z).

## Golden Dawn

The Golden Dawn does not simply repeat Lenain’s Aries-first zodiac.

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** In the Golden Dawn’s *Book T* system, the sequence of 72 angels begins at **0° Leo**, not 0° Aries. Mahashiah and Lelahel govern the two halves of the third Leo decan:

- Mahashiah: **20°–25° Leo**
- Lelahel: **25°–30° Leo**
- Decan planet: **Mars**
- Tarot card: **Seven of Wands**, “Lord of Valour”

The text says, briefly, “Herein rule the two great Angels Mahashiah and Lelahel.” See the [Golden Dawn manuscript transcription](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/golden_dawn_manuscripts.html) and [Regardie compilation](https://scriptaetveritas.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/ISRAEL_REGARDIE_COMPLETE_GOLDEN_DAWN_SYSTEM_OF_MAGIC.pdf).

Thus:

- Lenain Mahasiah = Aries 20°–25°.
- Golden Dawn Mahashiah = Leo 20°–25°.

Both are authentic to their respective systems. Neither should be presented as a universal ancient attribution.

## Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique*, first published in 1951, develops a practical ritual angelology combining the 72 names, seals, psalms, zodiacal divisions, and moral or spiritual offices. See the [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35147709s) and an accessible [text of *Practical Kabbalah*](https://www.markfoster.net/rn/texts/practicalkabbalah-part1.pdf).

**[Tradition]** Ambelain’s system places Mahasiah among a hierarchy of angels and treats the name as usable for correction, study, initiation, and spiritual restoration.

These elaborations are twentieth-century ritual synthesis, not evidence for medieval Jewish worship of an individual guardian angel with the complete later biography.

## Haziel

The author writing as Haziel popularized the 72 angels as personal guardian angels in late-twentieth-century French-language books. Here Mahasiah becomes a patron of people born during an April interval and receives extensive psychological traits, life missions, prayers, and devotional advice.

**[Modern esoteric tradition]** The familiar claim that everyone born approximately April 10–14 or 10–15 “has Mahasiah as guardian angel” comes from the Lenain–Ambelain–Haziel reception, not from Exodus, *Sefer Raziel*, or Rudd.

A representative text is [Haziel, *Communiquer avec son ange gardien*](https://bretagne-namaste.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Communiquer-Avec-Son-Ange-Gardien.pdf).

## Correspondences

## Rank and planetary metal

The *Goetia*’s general observations associate ranks with planets and metals:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince/Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury/quicksilver |
| Earl/Count | Textually mixed | Copper and silver in the manuscript tradition |

Therefore Marbas, as a President, is **Mercurial**, and the prescribed seal material is **mercury**.

Because elemental mercury is liquid and toxic, Mathers found this physically awkward and offered editorial speculation about alloys. His suggested adjustments are not the plain manuscript rule. Modern writers who assign Marbas tin/Jupiter are generally confusing President with Prince.

The rule appears in the [*Goetia* observations](https://hermetic.com/norton/classics/goetia/observations).

## Timing

### Goetic timing

**[Documented ritual rule]**

- The Moon should ideally be 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old.
- Presidents may be called at most times.
- Twilight and night are excluded unless the King governing the President is also invoked.
- Kings have more restrictive solar hours.
- Marbas receives no unique hour in the spirit catalogue.

These are prescriptive ritual claims, not astronomical facts or evidence of efficacy.

### Lenain timing

Mahasiah’s twentieth-minute segment is 1:20–1:40 after sunrise. This belongs to the angelic 72-fold day division: 24 hours divided among 72 names yields twenty minutes per name.

The interval is not the same thing as the planetary hour of Mercury.

## Zodiac and tarot: the four layers

| System | Object mapped | Zodiac | Subdivision | Planet | Tarot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rudd | Marbas ↔ Mahasiah by number | None stated by the pairing itself | None | President = Mercury | None |
| Lenain | Mahasiah | 20°–25° Aries | Fifth quinance | Venus governs 20°–30° Aries | Later synthesis: Four of Wands |
| Golden Dawn *Book T* | Mahashiah | 20°–25° Leo | Fifth quinance | Mars in Leo | Seven of Wands |
| Crowley *777* | Marbas | 10°–20° Taurus | Entire second Taurus decan, day | Moon in Taurus; rank remains Mercury | Six of Disks/Pentacles |

### Crowley’s 777

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** *Liber 777* distributes the first 36 Goetic spirits across the 36 decans as spirits “by day” and spirits 37–72 over the same decans “by night.”

Consequently:

- Bael, no. 1: first Aries decan by day.
- Agares, no. 2: second Aries decan by day.
- Vassago, no. 3: third Aries decan by day.
- Gamigin, no. 4: first Taurus decan by day.
- **Marbas, no. 5: second Taurus decan by day.**
- **Focalor, no. 41: the same decan by night.**

The second Taurus decan is:

- **10°–20° Taurus**
- Moon in Taurus
- Six of Pentacles/Disks
- “Material Success” in Golden Dawn nomenclature
- “Success” in Crowley’s later Thoth terminology

See [Crowley’s *Liber 777*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777.pdf).

Strictly speaking, *777* does **not** give Marbas a five-degree quinance. It gives Marbas and Focalor the same ten-degree decan, distinguished by day and night. Statements that “Crowley gives Marbas 20°–25° Aries and the Four of Wands” conflate the numerical Rudd pairing with an Aries-first angelic scheme.

## Enochian and later overlays

Rudd’s system combines Goetic spirit work with objects derived from John Dee’s angelic diaries, particularly the Holy Table. This is an early modern synthesis, not evidence that Marbas occurs in Dee’s angelic vocabulary.

**[Negative finding]** Marbas has no identified Enochian name, aethyr, governor, or square in Dee’s original records.

Modern ceremonial and demonolatry systems add:

- colors,
- incense,
- stones,
- days of the week,
- “enns” or mantras,
- qliphothic positions,
- psychological archetypes,
- pathworking images.

These may be valid within their creators’ practice, but they are modern overlays unless traced to a named source. Mercury, the lion, the offices, and the seal should not be used to back-date every modern attribution.

The only stable early image is verbal:

## Collin de Plancy

### 1818

Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in two volumes in 1818. The accessible first-edition scans establish the work and its early skeptical encyclopedic context, but the OCR/transcription consulted did not permit secure confirmation that the mature Marbas notice already appeared in exactly its 1863 wording. The scans are available as [volume 1](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal-1818-T1.djvu) and [volume 2](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal-1818-T2.djvu).

Accordingly, “de Plancy, 1818” is safe as the first edition of the dictionary, but the detailed Marbas quotation below is securely verified from 1863.

### 1863

The sixth edition, Paris, Henri Plon, 1863, page 443, reads in part:

> “Marbas ou Barbas, grand président des enfers … sous la forme d’un lion furieux.”

The complete notice says that Marbas:

- is a great President of Hell;
- appears as a furious lion;
- assumes human form before the exorcist;
- answers about hidden things;
- sends diseases;
- gives knowledge of mechanical arts;
- changes people through metamorphosis;
- commands 36 legions.

See [de Plancy’s Marbas article](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Marbas).

De Plancy cites Wierus/Weyer. Curiously, the notice mentions sending disease but omits the balancing power to cure it.

### Is there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**[Negative finding]** No individual Marbas portrait accompanies the 1863 entry. Page 443 is a text entry, not one of the demon plates.

Louis Le Breton supplied designs for many famous figures in the 1863 edition, but not every dictionary demon received an engraving. Therefore images circulated online as “Louis Le Breton’s Marbas” require independent verification; the frequently seen modern crowned or lion-headed Marbas portraits are not present beside the 1863 article.

The authentic nineteenth-century visual contribution for Marbas is effectively de Plancy’s phrase “furious lion,” not a canonical Breton engraving.

## Modern art

Modern artists normally choose one of three approaches:

1. A naturalistic or heraldic lion.
2. A lion–human hybrid representing both manifestations simultaneously.
3. A human physician, engineer, alchemist, or mechanist carrying Mercurial imagery.

These are interpretive condensations. The original text describes successive forms, not necessarily a hybrid.

The Marbas sigil also appears in contemporary tarot and oracle products. Lo Scarabeo’s [*Goetia: Tarot in Darkness*](https://www.loscarabeo.com/en/products/goetia-tarot-in-darkness) is an example of modern occult art rather than manuscript iconography.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer supplied the first secure printed *Marbas* and fixed the mature lion, President, illness/cure, mechanical arts, transformation, and 36-legion description. His broader purpose was an argument against witch persecution and about demonic fraud.

### Reginald Scot

Scot translated Weyer for an English audience in 1584. His language became a direct conduit into the English *Goetia* tradition.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

The Rudd-attributed system set each Goetic spirit against a Shem angel. Peter Smart appears to have produced the surviving fair copy. Their contribution is the angelic constraint apparatus, not the core Marbas description.

### Collin de Plancy

De Plancy transferred Weyer’s demonology into a nineteenth-century encyclopedia and spread the description in French. His Marbas notice omits the cure function and supplies no verified portrait.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Mathers prepared the translation/redaction underlying the 1904 edition; Crowley edited, introduced, and published it. Crowley subsequently incorporated the Goetic spirits into *777*’s decanal tables.

### Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** Charles Stansfeld Jones/Frater Achad participated in Crowley’s magical milieu and wrote extensively on qabalah. No securely sourced Marbas-specific operation, etymology, or historical contribution by Achad was located. Merely using Crowleyan tables does not make him a Marbas authority.

### Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* and Ordo Templi Astartes practice helped popularize modern ritual Goetia. Runyon’s broader Canaanite and planetary interpretations are modern reconstructions. No evidence was found that he established an ancient god specifically behind Marbas.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* popularized a psychological and practical reading of the 1904 text. It reproduces Marbas as part of the catalogue; DuQuette’s better-known personal Goetic narratives concern other spirits, particularly Orobas.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* brought the Harley manuscripts, angelic counterseals, psalm verses, and Dee-related furnishings to a wide readership. It remains the most important published study of the Rudd form of the Marbas–Mahasiah pairing.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition and Esoteric Archives transcriptions made possible close comparison of Weyer, Scot, Sloane, Harley, and related manuscripts. His work is essential for separating manuscript readings from Mathers’s editorial decisions.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Geosophia*, *The True Grimoire*, and *Stellas Daemonum* emphasize the pre-*Goetia* spirit-catalogue tradition, underworld geography, and practical spirit work. His approach encouraged attention to the *Livre des Esperitz* and related ancestors. Speculative genealogies in modern practical occultism should nevertheless be distinguished from the narrower manuscript evidence.

### The Munich Handbook

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s manual, Clm 849, is often invoked too loosely as the source of every Goetic demon. It contains related names and ritual structures, but no unambiguous mature Marbas. Its *Barbarus* material belongs more naturally with the animal-language/treasure complex of Barbatos.

**[Negative finding]** Searches of the principal Mathers and modern critical Abramelin name lists do not yield a secure Marbas or Barbas entry with the defining offices. Similar-looking names such as *Marag* or *Maranton* are insufficient evidence of identity.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. “Barbas in Weyer” versus “Marbas in Weyer”

Weyer does not give only *Barbas*. His 1577 wording is **Marbas, alias Barbas**. Describing Weyer’s spirit simply as “Barbas” erases the first secure printed occurrence of *Marbas*.

### 2. Does Marbas exist before Weyer?

Two answers must be distinguished:

- **Exact spelling:** no secure pre-1577 attestation located.
- **Recognizable textual entity:** yes, in Barthas/Barbarius material.

“Marbas was invented by Weyer” is therefore too strong. “Marbas is an ancient pagan deity” is much stronger and unsupported.

### 3. Marbas versus Barbatos

The *Livre des Esperitz* separately records Barthas and Barbas. Their offices feed different later spirits. The alias *Barbas* in Weyer creates a collision, but mature Marbas and Barbatos remain separate entries.

### 4. Is “President” an infernal political office?

**[Tradition]** The catalogues use courtly and administrative ranks—King, Duke, Marquis, Earl, President, Prince, Knight—to structure the spirit monarchy.

**[Historical interpretation]** These titles imitate human political hierarchies and aid classification. There is no external evidence for a literal infernal bureaucracy.

### 5. Mercury or another planet?

The textual rank correspondence is Mercury. Taurus/Moon in *777* belongs to a different layer: the zodiacal decan. Mars/Leo belongs to Golden Dawn Mahashiah, and Venus/Aries belongs to Lenain’s decan. They answer different questions.

### 6. What is the correct zodiacal degree?

There is no single historically universal degree:

- Rudd pairing: none.
- Lenain: 20°–25° Aries.
- Golden Dawn angel: 20°–25° Leo.
- *777* demon: 10°–20° Taurus.
- Modern number-matching systems: often 20°–25° Aries for both demon and angel.

Any source presenting one of these as ancient or universally “correct” suppresses the transmission history.

### 7. Four, Seven, or Six?

All three tarot claims can arise from genuine—but different—systems:

- **Four of Wands:** later combination of Lenain’s Aries quinance with the tarot decans.
- **Seven of Wands:** Golden Dawn Mahashiah in Leo.
- **Six of Disks:** Crowley’s *777* placement of Marbas itself in Taurus.

For the user’s requested “Crowley 777 tarot pip,” the answer is unequivocally **Six of Disks/Pentacles**.

### 8. Does every pip carry two demons?

In Crowley’s decan table, yes: one Goetic spirit “by day” and another “by night.” Marbas and Focalor share the Six of Disks decan.

This should not be confused with the Golden Dawn’s two Shem angels per decan, who occupy successive five-degree halves.

### 9. The de Plancy portrait

There is no verified Louis Le Breton Marbas portrait on the 1863 entry page. Modern images labeled that way are misattributed unless an exact plate and page can be produced.

### 10. Medical power

The power to cause and cure disease is a documented grimoire claim. It is not medical evidence. Modern healing testimonies are practitioner reports or legend and should not replace professional care.

### 11. Shape-changing

The grammar ordinarily means that Marbas changes **people into other shapes**, not merely that Marbas changes his own appearance. The spirit also changes its own manifestation from lion to human, so both transformations occur in the entry.

Whether “change men” was understood literally, as illusion, social transformation, initiation, or psychological change differs among later interpreters.

### 12. Mechanical arts

The early-modern phrase means practical or handicraft arts. Interpreting Marbas as a specialist in automobiles, robotics, computers, or industrial machinery is a reasonable modern extension but not the original lexical scope.

### 13. The Rudd attribution

Skinner and Rankine’s attribution is based on manuscript relationships and the presence of Rudd-associated material. The surviving Harley witness is a copy connected with Peter Smart, not an indisputable Rudd autograph. “Rudd’s system” is useful shorthand, not a settled authorship statement for every line.

### 14. Psalm numbering

Psalm 33:4, Psalm 33:5, and Psalm 34:4 may refer to the same “I sought the Lord” sentence under different psalter and verse-counting conventions. The variation does not imply three separate Mahasiah verses.

## Open questions

1. **What exact manuscript did Weyer use?**  
   He names the *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*, but the exemplar has not survived or been conclusively identified.

2. **How exactly did Barthas become Marbas?**  
   The continuity of attributes is strong; the palaeographic mechanism producing initial *M* remains unproved.

3. **Which witness first supplied the disease/cure duality?**  
   Sickness is present in collateral material, but the balanced “brings and removes” formula is securely fixed by Weyer.

4. **When was the Marbas seal first drawn?**  
   It is absent from Weyer and Scot and appears in the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscript tradition. Its designer and pre-Sloane ancestry remain unknown.

5. **Who compiled the first *Ars Goetia* redaction?**  
   Textual dependence on Scot supplies a terminus after 1584, while surviving manuscripts are seventeenth century. No named author is securely established.

6. **How much of Harley 6483 is genuinely Rudd’s?**  
   The integrated system is consistent with Rudd’s interests, but copyist, compiler, and author cannot always be separated.

7. **Did de Plancy’s 1818 edition already contain the mature Marbas notice?**  
   The first-edition scans need page-by-page inspection beyond incomplete OCR before identical wording can be asserted.

8. **Were the 36 legions symbolic or inherited boilerplate?**  
   The number’s stability supports textual continuity, but no catalogue of those legions survives.

9. **Did the form *Marbas* arise through deliberate wordplay on disease?**  
   The *morbus* theory is possible as secondary influence, not established as origin.

10. **Does a pre-Christian analogue remain unidentified?**  
    It is always possible that a name was drastically corrupted, but no current evidence links Marbas to an ancient god, hero, or place.

## Findings in brief

Marbas is not an ancient deity reduced to demonhood, insofar as the surviving evidence shows. The historically reconstructable figure is a late-medieval catalogue spirit whose name and offices evolved:

> **Barthas/Barbarius/Barbas → Weyer’s Marbas alias Barbas → Scot → the seventeenth-century Goetia → Rudd’s angelic pairing → Mathers, Crowley, and modern occultism.**

The lion, human form, secret answers, disease and cure, mechanical arts, transformation, and 36 legions are securely early-modern. The seal is seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* material. Mahasiah is a Rudd-system constraint created by matching the fifth demon to the fifth unit of the 72-fold Name. The zodiac, tarot, guardian-angel biography, Enochian furnishings, and modern artistic identities are later overlays.

The three most commonly confused correspondences are:

- **Marbas in Crowley’s *777***: day spirit, 10°–20° Taurus, Six of Disks, sharing the decan with Focalor.
- **Mahasiah in Lenain**: 20°–25° Aries, later associated with the Four of Wands.
- **Mahashiah in the Golden Dawn**: 20°–25° Leo, Seven of Wands.

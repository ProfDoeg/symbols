# Zagan: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary key

This dossier distinguishes kinds of evidence as follows:

- **[TEXT]** Directly documented in a manuscript, early printed book, or critical transcription.
- **[RECONSTRUCTION]** A conclusion drawn by editors or scholars from textual comparison.
- **[TRADITION]** A claim internal to a magical, kabbalistic, or demonological tradition.
- **[CORRESPONDENCE]** A later esoteric attribution rather than part of the earliest description.
- **[DISPUTED]** A claim for which sources disagree or evidence is inadequate.
- **[LEGEND]** A narrative presented as supernatural history without historical corroboration.
- **[MODERN]** A twentieth- or twenty-first-century invention, reinterpretation, or fictional use.

The complete primary texts are linked below. Because full entries exceed permissible quotation lengths here, I provide diagnostic verbatim passages and exhaustive clause-by-clause readings, including every substantive difference.

## 2. Earliest attestation

**[TEXT]** The *Livre des esperitz* contains the roster phrase *“De Zagon, roy”*—“Of Zagon, king.” In the surviving text there is no corresponding full descriptive paragraph. Either the compiler never supplied it or a passage has been lost. The manuscript therefore establishes the name and rank, not Zagan’s later appearance or powers. [French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier)

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** Jean-Patrice Boudet describes the work as the earliest surviving French demonological “who’s who” and dates the extant witness more cautiously to around the middle of the sixteenth century. Older catalogues sometimes placed it in the fifteenth century. “Fifteenth-century Zagan” is consequently too confident; “pre-Weyer *Zagon* in a manuscript whose dating is debated” is safer. [Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171)

**[TEXT/UNCONFIRMED FACSIMILE]** Peterson’s Weyer apparatus points to *Zagan* in Trithemius’s *Polygraphia* (1518), p. 344. If the reading is confirmed directly from a scan, that would be the earliest printed occurrence located here, although still only a name in a table, not a demonological biography.

**Finding:** The assertion that Zagan has no pre-1577 attestation is incorrect. What does begin with Weyer—or with a close *Liber Officiorum* relative—is the full winged-bull and transmutation dossier.

## 3. Is the name derived from an ancient god, hero, or place?

### Dagon

**[MODERN/DISPUTED]** Modern demonolatry sources sometimes assert that “Zagan is Dagon.” No early witness inspected here makes that identification:

- *Livre des esperitz* says only *Zagon, roy*.
- Weyer gives no etymology.
- Scot gives no etymology.
- The manuscript Goetia gives no etymology.
- Rudd’s apparatus pairs Zagan with Umabel but does not derive him from Dagon.
- De Plancy does not identify him with Dagon.

The equation appears to have been spread especially by modern occult websites and demonolatry manuals. Its evidence is visual and associative: Dagon was treated in older European scholarship as a fish, grain, or fertility deity; Zagan is a bull-like transmuter; both names end in *-agan/-agon*. That is not a historical linguistic derivation.

The ancient Semitic name **Dagon**, conventionally written דגון, begins with *d*, not *z*. No demonstrated manuscript chain changes *Dagon* to *Zagon/Zagan*, and the surviving medieval form already has *Z-*. The equation should therefore be classified as **modern speculative identification**, not recovered ancient theology.

### Żagań/Sagan

**[DISPUTED/UNSUPPORTED]** Żagań is a Polish place historically called Sagan in German. Similar spelling alone is not evidence that the demon was named for that place. No grimoire witness connects them.

### Romanian *zăgan*

**[DISPUTED/UNSUPPORTED]** Romanian *zăgan* denotes the bearded vulture or lammergeier. The bird’s wings can tempt comparison with Zagan’s griffin wings, but no textual or linguistic bridge from Romanian bird-name to French *Zagon* has been demonstrated.

### Greek and Latin

**[TEXT]** *Goēteia* is Greek γοητεία, “sorcery” or the work of a *goēs*, and supplies the genre-name *Goetia*. It is not an etymology of *Zagan*.

**Finding:** Unlike Baal, Astarte, Amun, Asmodeus/Ashmedai, Belial, or Phoenix, Zagan presently has **no accepted ancient deity, hero, place-name, Greek noun, or Latin noun behind his name**.

# The number and the order problem

## Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

**[TEXT]** The complete 1577 *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* has 69 spirits. Its no. 48 is Zagan. Some modern transcriptions call him no. 47 because they suppress Pruflas/Bufas and renumber the remainder; that is editorial normalization, not the original complete-list position.

**[TEXT]** The *Ars Goetia* has 72:

- It omits Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**.
- It adds **Vassago**, **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius**.

Thus:

\[
69 - 1 + 4 = 72
\]

The compiler also substantially rearranged the catalogue. Zagan moved from Weyer’s no. 48 to Goetia no. 61.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** Peterson argues that the Goetia’s dependence on characteristic errors and spellings in the expanded 1665 English Scot strongly suggests an English source-chain through Scot rather than independent consultation of Weyer’s Latin. He also raises page displacement as a possible cause of some reorderings. This is a textual hypothesis, not a statement made by the grimoire itself. [Peterson’s Goetia introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

## The 72-vessel legend

**[LEGEND]** The Goetia explains the 72 by saying Solomon confined the spirits in a brass vessel. The Babylonians, believing it contained treasure, broke it open; the spirits escaped and returned to their former places. This tale supplies an internal mythical rationale for the catalogue’s 72, not historical evidence for a Solomonic original.

# The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, 1577

Johann Weyer appended the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. He presented the catalogue polemically as evidence of the follies and deceptions of ritual magicians, not as an endorsement of conjuration.

### Diagnostic Latin

**[TEXT]** Weyer, 1577, p. 927:

> “Zagan magnus Rex & Præses: ut taurus prodit cum alis ad modum gryphi: sed assumpta hominis forma, reddit hominem ingeniosum…”

[Latin text and apparatus](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### Complete substantive reading

Weyer says that Zagan:

1. is a **great King and President**;
2. comes forth as a **bull**;
3. has wings “after the manner of a **griffin**”;
4. then assumes **human form**;
5. makes a person **ingenious**, clever, or resourceful;
6. transmutes every kind of metal into the **coinage of the polity or dominion** to which it belongs;
7. changes **water into wine**;
8. changes wine back into water—*et e diverso*, “and conversely”;
9. changes **blood into oil**;
10. changes oil back into blood—*et contra*;
11. changes a **fool into a wise person**;
12. presides over **33 legions**.

Important lexical points:

- *Ingeniosum* means ingenious, clever, talented, or resourceful—not necessarily humorous in the modern sense of “witty.”
- *Monetas illius ditionis* means the coins or currency of the relevant jurisdiction. It does not expressly say that base metal becomes gold.
- The liquid pair is unmistakably **blood ↔ oil** in Weyer’s Latin.

## 2. Reginald Scot, 1584

Scot included an English version in Book XV of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*. Like Weyer, Scot’s purpose was largely expositional and skeptical: he printed conjuring material while attacking credulity and persecution.

### Diagnostic English

**[TEXT]**

> “Zagan is a great king and a president, he commeth abroad like a bull, with griphens wings, but when he taketh humane shape…”

[Peterson’s parallel Weyer–Scot transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### Complete substantive reading

Scot repeats that Zagan:

1. is a great king and president;
2. comes like a bull with griffin’s wings;
3. assumes human shape;
4. makes men “wittie”;
5. turns all metals into the coin of the relevant dominion;
6. turns water into wine and wine into water;
7. changes blood and another liquid into one another;
8. changes a fool into a wise man;
9. heads 33 legions.

### The decisive corruption

**[TEXTUAL DISPUTE]** Scot’s printed wording reads **wine** where Weyer has **oil**. Peterson marks the reading as erroneous:

- Weyer: blood ↔ oil.
- Scot as printed: blood ↔ wine.
- Goetia: blood → wine, followed by a duplicated water → wine clause.

This is one of the strongest small-scale indications that the English Goetia descends through Scot or a Scot-derived text rather than directly from clean Latin Weyer.

## 3. Folger MS V.b.26: “Zagayne”

**[TEXT]** The *Book of Magic* or *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* in Folger MS V.b.26 calls the spirit **Zagayne**. It says, in substance, that:

- when human-shaped, he gives wisdom;
- he can change earth into any metal;
- he changes water into wine;
- he changes a fool into a wise person;
- he appears as a wild bull.

The Folger form lacks the complete later wording about griffin wings and 33 legions in the relevant passage, but it is plainly a close relative of the Weyer material. [Folger MS V.b.26 transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** Whether Weyer copied directly from a manuscript of this exact recension or whether both descend from another *Liber Officiorum* remains unresolved.

## 4. Sloane MS 3825 and Peterson’s transcription

Peterson’s online Goetia takes Sloane 3825 as its principal witness and supplies manuscript variants in notes.

### Diagnostic wording

**[TEXT]**

> “The 61 spirit is called Zagan, he is a great king & president, and appeareth at first in ye forme of a Bull…”

[Peterson’s manuscript transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Complete substantive reading

The Sloane 3825 entry, fol. 109r, says that Zagan:

1. is spirit no. 61;
2. is a great King and President;
3. appears first as a bull with griffin’s wings;
4. afterward assumes human shape;
5. makes men witty;
6. can turn **wine into water**;
7. can turn **blood into wine**;
8. can “also” turn **water into wine**;
9. can turn all metals into “Corne,” corrected by Peterson to **coin**, of the dominion to which the metal belongs;
10. can make fools wise;
11. governs 33 legions;
12. has a seal to be made and worn as a **lamen**.

### “Corne” versus “coin”

**[TEXTUAL DISPUTE]** *Corne* makes little sense in context. Weyer’s *monetas* and Scot’s *coine* establish that **coin** is the intended reading. The error is scribal or typographical, not evidence that Zagan turns metal into grain.

### The malformed liquid sequence

The Goetia’s sequence is:

- wine → water;
- blood → wine;
- water → wine.

This looks like a corrupted descendant of Weyer’s two reversible pairs:

- water ↔ wine;
- blood ↔ oil.

The disappearance of oil and duplication of wine are therefore best treated as transmission errors rather than a deliberate new theology of blood-wine transubstantiation.

## 5. Sloane MS 2731

**[TEXT]** Sloane 2731, begun in 1676, spells the name **Zagan** and numbers him 61. Peterson’s apparatus records no major independent office or rank variant for this entry. The seal is copied as a graphic character, with normal hand-drawn differences in angles, terminal loops, and spacing.

The significance of Sloane 2731 is chiefly:

- confirmation of no. 61;
- confirmation of the spelling *Zagan*;
- confirmation that the corrupted English liquid sequence was already embedded in the manuscript tradition;
- preservation of a complete *Lemegeton*, not merely Weyer’s catalogue.

## 6. Harley MS 6483 and the Ruddian recension

**[TEXT]** Harley 6483 is dated 1712–1713 and titled *Liber malorum Spirituum, seu Goetia*. Its title page also calls the work *Lemegeton* and *Secretum Secretorum*. Peterson describes it as a late manuscript containing substantial innovations and material attributed to “Dr. Rudd.” [Peterson’s manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

For Zagan it preserves:

- the spelling **Zagan**;
- number 61;
- King and President;
- 33 legions;
- the conventional seal;
- the controlling Shem angel **Umabel**;
- expanded angelic and protective apparatus.

**[DISPUTED ATTRIBUTION]** The manuscript is not Thomas Rudd’s autograph. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine argue that lawyer Peter Smart made the surviving fair copy from an earlier Rudd manuscript, possibly one predating Rudd’s death in 1656. What can be stated directly is that the surviving copy is 1712–1713 and identifies its material with Rudd. “Ruddian recension” is firmer than “written personally by Thomas Rudd.”

## 7. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The first edition was published in 1904. Claims of a Chicago “1903” edition are bibliographically false or refer to later reprints with misleading dates.

### Diagnostic text

**[TEXT]** Mathers–Crowley, p. 42:

> “The Sixty-first Spirit is Zagan. He is a Great King and President, appearing at first in the Form of a Bull with Gryphon’s Wings…”

[1904 text, p. 42](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

### Complete substantive reading

Mathers’s text, published by Crowley, retains the Sloane/English form:

- great King and President;
- bull with gryphon wings;
- later human shape;
- makes men witty;
- wine → water;
- blood → wine;
- water → wine;
- metals → coin of the metal’s dominion;
- fools → wise;
- 33 legions;
- seal supplied beneath the entry.

It does not repair the blood/wine corruption from Weyer.

## 8. Peterson, 2001

**[TEXT/SCHOLARLY EDITION]** Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* was published by Weiser in 2001, ISBN 978-1-57863-220-6. Its Zagan text follows Sloane 3825 while documenting variants:

- “Zagam” in the corrupt Weyer witness;
- “Zagan” in Scot;
- Weyer’s **oil** against Scot’s mistaken **wine**;
- **coin** as the correction of manuscript *Corne*.

The web edition identifies the manuscript location as Sloane 3825, fol. 109r. Exact printed pagination varies between the 2001 Weiser state and later reissues; the folio reference is the stable citation. [Bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.html?id=wfIrE3QtmFEC)

## Offices Zagan does **not** possess in the early texts

No inspected early source assigns Zagan:

- languages;
- liberal sciences generally;
- hidden treasure;
- love or lust;
- familiars;
- invisibility;
- prophecy or answers about past and future;
- discovery of thieves;
- disease or healing;
- reconciliation of friends;
- mechanical arts.

Claims that he rules alchemy, counterfeiting, fraud, rhetoric, “vampyric initiation,” immortality, or personal evolution are later interpretations of his transmutations and coin-making.

# The seal

## Manuscript character

**[TEXT]** The early English Goetia tells the operator to make Zagan’s seal and wear it as a **lamen**, meaning an inscribed ritual pendant or breast-piece.

No source supplies a prose geometrical recipe. The seal is transmitted as a drawing, so any verbal account is necessarily descriptive rather than generative.

In the Peterson/Sloane and 1904 form, it is:

- much wider than it is tall;
- built principally on a long horizontal or shallowly rising axis;
- stepped or notched at its outer arms;
- joined through a central arch or bridge;
- furnished with small round, looped, hooked, or cross-like terminals;
- asymmetric in hand-drawn witnesses even where modern vector versions make it nearly balanced.

A scan-derived description in Peterson’s edition aptly characterizes it as a “long horizontal emblem with stepped segments” and a curved central arch. [Peterson edition scan record](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf)

## Variants

**[TEXT]**

- Sloane 3825 preserves the working base form.
- Sloane 2731 preserves the same character family but with copyist-level variation.
- Harley 6483 embeds the seal within a larger Ruddian angelic-control system.
- Mathers regularized and redrew the character for print.
- Modern SVGs smooth curves, standardize line weights, and often force symmetry absent from manuscript hands.

No evidence was found for two wholly independent historical Zagan seals. Most online “alternative seals” are stylizations of the same Goetic character or new magical art. [Public-domain 1904 seal files](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Ars_Goetia_sigils)

## 1. Derivation of the 72 triads

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the Hebrew textual tradition used by the kabbalists, of three verses of 72 letters each. The procedure is:

1. write verse 19 in its ordinary Hebrew direction;
2. write verse 20 beneath it in reverse order;
3. write verse 21 beneath that in ordinary order;
4. read the columns vertically.

This boustrophedon arrangement yields 72 three-letter combinations. The 61st is **V-M-B**, ומב.

**[TRADITION]** Christian kabbalists and ritual magicians then added **-el** or **-yah** to many of these divine triads, treating them as angel names. Thus V-M-B becomes forms such as *Vamab-El* or *Umabel*.

## 2. What “Shem HaMephorash” originally meant

**[SCHOLARLY DISTINCTION]** In early rabbinic usage, *shem ha-meforash* means the “explicit,” “distinctly pronounced,” or “fully expressed” divine name, usually the Tetragrammaton. It did not originally mean a fixed list of 72 guardian angels.

The 72-fold construction is medieval. The *Bahir* gives an early explanation of the three-verse arrangement; forms of the 72-name system became important in *Sefer Raziel*. Later Christian Cabala converted the triads more systematically into angelic personal names.

Accordingly:

- “72 names of God from Exodus” is medieval kabbalistic tradition.
- “72 angels ending in -el/-yah” is a Jewish-magical and especially Christian-kabbalistic development.
- “Each angel rules five zodiacal degrees and opposes one Goetic demon” is later still.

## 3. Sefer Raziel and medieval kabbalah

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** The printed *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* preserves the 72-fold divine name, although manuscripts differ in vocalization and magical deployment. The triads themselves are more stable than their later pronunciations.

**[DISPUTED]** It is misleading to say that *Sefer Raziel* already contains the entire modern Umabel dossier—meaning, dates, psalm, friendship, astronomy, tarot card, and Zagan opposition. Those accumulated over centuries.

## 4. Reuchlin, Vigenère, and Kircher

**[TEXT/RECONSTRUCTION]** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped transmit the 72 angelic forms into Christian Cabala. Blaise de Vigenère expanded this material and influenced the Ruddian tradition.

**[TEXT]** Athanasius Kircher discussed the 72-fold name in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654), including a table at approximately vol. II, part I, p. 275. His treatment belongs to his Christian-Cabalist synthesis, although he repeatedly condemned magical misuse of Cabala. [Kircher’s *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** Kircher is an important transmitter of large tables of divine names, languages, angels, and cosmic correspondences. The exact modern Umabel “guardian angel” profile should not be retrojected wholesale into Kircher.

## 5. Rudd and the opposition to Zagan

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** In Harley 6483’s scheme, the numbering aligns the 72 Goetic spirits with 72 Shem angels. Spirit 61, Zagan, is constrained by angel 61, Umabel.

The ritual theory is not that Umabel is Zagan’s benign alter ego. Umabel is a superior divine authority invoked to:

- control or frustrate Zagan;
- guarantee obedience;
- prevent deception;
- balance the demonic seal with an angelic name or character;
- place the operation under a Psalm and divine names.

**[TRADITION]** This is a seventeenth-/early-eighteenth-century Christian magical safety architecture. It is absent from Weyer, Scot, and the ordinary Sloane description.

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine’s *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* made the system widely accessible in 2007. The publisher describes its central innovation as the use of the 72 angels to bind the 72 demons. [Golden Hoard/Llewellyn description](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556)

## 6. Umabel’s name and “meaning”

**[TEXT]** The underlying letters are Vav–Mem–Bet. The spelling *Umabel* reflects a particular vocalization of consonants that the biblical derivation itself does not supply.

**[TRADITION]** Lenain’s French title is *Dieu au-dessus de toutes choses*—“God above all things.” “God above all names” is a modern variant.

**[SCHOLARLY CAUTION]** This is an assigned devotional attribute, not a literal translation of a Hebrew word *Umabel*. The suffix *-el* is recognizably “God”; the remaining vocalized form is constructed from the triad.

## 7. Psalm verse

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** Umabel’s verse is:

> “Blessed be the name of the Lord from this time forth and for evermore.”

That is Psalm **113:2** in the Masoretic/modern English numbering and Psalm **112:2** in the Vulgate:

> “Sit nomen Domini benedictum, ex hoc nunc et usque in saeculum.”

The numbering difference is bibliographical, not a different verse.

## 8. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* is the principal nineteenth-century source for Umabel’s widely repeated personal profile.

**[TEXT]** Lenain assigns Umabel:

- the attribute “God above all things”;
- zodiacal degrees 301–305;
- the thirty-first five-degree division;
- 0°–5° Aquarius in modern notation;
- the Egyptian decan-genius **Ptiau**;
- the planet **Venus**;
- the divine name **Sila**, attributed to the “Bethulians”;
- approximately 20:00–20:20 for invocation;
- Psalm 112:2 Vulgate;
- the power of obtaining someone’s friendship;
- governance of astronomy and physics;
- influence over people distinguished in those disciplines;
- love of travel and honest pleasures;
- a sensitive heart and grief in love.

His “contrary genius” is associated with libertinism and acts against the supposed order of nature. These are Lenain’s moral-occult categories, not findings about historical persons.

Lenain also gives five scattered calendar days—19 May, 30 July, 10 October, 21 December, and 3 March—within a secondary cycle. They are not the same thing as the continuous five-day solar period later attached to 0°–5° Aquarius. [1823 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf)

## 9. Papus

**[TEXT]** Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced and popularized Lenain’s 72-angel notices in the expanded 1903 edition of *La Cabbale*. For Umabel he repeats the degree range, Psalm, friendship, travel, astronomy/physics, and moralized contrary influence.

Papus is therefore a transmitter rather than the originator of these particular Umabel details. [Papus, *La Cabbale*](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf)

## 10. Golden Dawn and Crowley

**[CORRESPONDENCE]** Golden Dawn-derived tables connected the 72-fold name with:

- zodiacal subdivisions;
- decans and minor-arcana cards;
- angelic choirs;
- sephiroth;
- planets and divine names.

In that framework, Umabel’s 0°–5° Aquarius lies in the first Aquarius decan, corresponding to the **Five of Swords**. The next angel, Iah-Hel/Iahhel, occupies the other five-degree portion. Thus one tarot pip contains **two Shem angels**.

This is separate from Crowley’s arrangement of two **Goetic demons**—one diurnal and one nocturnal—under each ten-degree pip.

## 11. Ambelain

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) carried the operative Shem tradition into twentieth-century French Martinist and occult practice. Ambelain systematized angelic hierarchy, daily operations, divine names, and theurgy. His importance is one of ritual elaboration and transmission; Lenain remains the earlier source for Umabel’s friendship-and-science notice. [Ambelain scan](https://www.ordremartiniste.ca/docs-omc/Robert%20Ambelain/La%20Kabbale%20Pratique.pdf)

## 12. Haziel

**[MODERN TRADITION]** François Bernad-Termès, writing as Haziel, popularized the 72 angels as personal guardian angels in late-twentieth-century France and elsewhere. In Haziel’s system Umabel commonly governs people born approximately **21–25 January**, 1°–5° Aquarius, and assists friendship, travel, and honest joys.

These dates are not ancient Jewish data and are not found in Weyer or the original Goetia. They are a modern astrological-angelological development. Different authors print 14–18, 20–24, 20–25, or 21–25 January because they use different zodiacal starting points, inclusive counting, or calendar approximations. [Haziel-derived Umabel notice](https://www.scribd.com/document/455897813/Communiquer-Avec-Son-Ange-Gardien-HAZIEL-pdf)

## Is Zagan’s “quinance” Sagittarius or Aquarius?

**Answer: the question combines two systems.**

- Zagan’s **Goetic decan** is 0°–10° Sagittarius, nocturnal.
- Umabel’s **Shem quinance** is 0°–5° Aquarius.
- The Eight of Wands contains two Goetic spirits by day/night.
- The Five of Swords contains two Shem angels by adjacent five-degree divisions.

Any table assigning Zagan directly to 0°–5° Aquarius has transferred Umabel’s numbered Shem sector to the demon. That is a modern hybrid, not the standard Crowley Goetic table.

## Dual rank and planetary metal

The Goetia’s rank instructions assign:

- kings: Sun and gold;
- presidents: Mercury and mercury.

Zagan possesses both titles. No early instruction tells the operator which takes precedence or how to combine them.

**[DISPUTED PRACTICE]** Later editors offer three solutions:

1. use gold because King is the higher dignity;
2. use mercury because President is the more specific administrative office;
3. symbolize both, sometimes by placing Mercury characters on a golden lamen.

The third is a later practical compromise. The early text does not command an alloy, and literal elemental mercury is toxic and unsuitable for an exposed pendant.

## Astrological timing

**[TEXT/TRADITION]** The Goetia’s general rules state:

- Kings may be bound or called from 9 a.m. to noon and again from 3 p.m. until sunset.
- Presidents may be called through most daylight hours, avoiding twilight; nocturnal work is qualified by the authority of the governing king.
- Operations are considered most favorable when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old.

For Zagan, dual rank again creates ambiguity. No surviving entry specifies a unique hour.

Possible traditional interpretations are:

- apply the stricter royal hours;
- use presidential hours while invoking the ruling king;
- use the later Sagittarius nocturnal attribution;
- in Ruddian work, time by Umabel’s angelic sector instead.

These solutions come from different recensions and should not be presented as one original system.

## Enochian overlays

**[TEXT/CORRESPONDENCE]** The Rudd manuscript collection includes material drawing on John Dee’s angel magic and Enochian tables. Crowley’s 1904 volume also supplied prefatory invocations influenced by his own Enochian practice.

No Zagan-specific Enochian name, governor, aethyr, or tablet-square was verified in the early catalogue. Direct equations between Zagan and a particular Enochian entity are later synthetic occultism.

# Iconography

## 1. The textual image

**[TEXT]** The oldest detailed iconographic formula is Weyer’s:

- bull;
- griffin-like wings;
- afterward a man.

Weyer does not say:

- the bull’s color;
- whether it has a human face;
- whether the human retains horns or wings;
- whether the human is clothed;
- whether it carries a weapon;
- whether it has a crown;
- whether it resembles a cherub.

All such details in modern images are artistic additions.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

The verified sixth edition of *Dictionnaire infernal* gives:

> “Zagam, grand roi et président, de l’enfer. Il a l’apparence d’un taureau aux ailes de griffon.”

In full substance, de Plancy says Zagam is a great king and president of Hell, looks like a bull with griffin wings, changes water into wine, blood into oil, a fool into a wise person, lead into silver, and copper into gold; 30 legions obey him. [1863 “Zagam” entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Lettre_Z)

His changes are noteworthy:

- *Zagan* becomes **Zagam**.
- 33 legions become **30**.
- The human second form disappears.
- General metal-to-coin power becomes lead→silver and copper→gold.
- Weyer’s blood→oil is restored, but reversibility is not stated.

## 3. No Louis Le Breton portrait

**[TEXTUAL FINDING]** The 1863 edition contains Louis Le Breton engravings for selected demons, not every entry. No Zagam/Zagan portrait accompanies the entry, and no authenticated Le Breton plate for him was located.

Websites that automatically place a modern winged-bull picture beside de Plancy’s text sometimes label it “the Dictionnaire Infernal image.” That is incorrect unless they identify a separate plate and page. The textual entry exists; a Breton portrait apparently does not.

## 4. Modern art

**[MODERN]** Common approaches include:

- a massive black or red bull with eagle/griffin wings;
- a winged bull-headed humanoid;
- an alchemist surrounded by cups, blood, oil, and coins;
- a crowned bull to signify kingship;
- Mercury and solar symbols to express the dual rank;
- a cherubic tetramorph-style bull, based on speculative angelic ancestry;
- tarot illustrations centered on a cauldron or transmuting coin.

These are legitimate modern artistic receptions but not manuscript evidence.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

**[TEXT/HISTORICAL]** Weyer was a physician and critic of witch persecution. He believed in spirits but argued that many accused witches were deluded, ill, or deceived rather than capable of the impossible crimes attributed to them. His publication of the demon catalogue was polemical: a specimen of magical folly. Zagan’s first complete printed profile appears in this context, not in a celebratory demon cult.

## Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

**[TEXT/HISTORICAL]** Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft* attacked the logic of witch prosecution and exposed conjuring and performance tricks. The English demon catalogue is commonly associated with translation or editorial work by Abraham Fleming. Its error *wine* for Latin *oleum* became part of Zagan’s later biography.

## The anonymous Lemegeton compiler

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** The compiler reorganized Scot/Weyer material, added seals and ritual instructions, omitted Pruflas, added four spirits, and expanded the catalogue to 72. Zagan’s move from 48 to 61 belongs to this stage.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

**[TEXT/DISPUTED ATTRIBUTION]** The Ruddian tradition joined each demon to a Shem angel. The surviving Harley 6483 is a 1712–1713 copy, attributed by Skinner and Rankine to Peter Smart working from Ruddian papers. Zagan’s opposition by Umabel is the defining Ruddian addition.

## MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

**[TEXT/HISTORICAL]** Mathers prepared an edition from manuscript material; Crowley published it in 1904, supplied prefatory material, and reframed the spirits psychologically in his “Preliminary Invocation” and essayistic apparatus. The actual Zagan paragraph remains close to the corrupt English manuscript text.

## Aleister Crowley’s *777*

**[CORRESPONDENCE]** Crowley’s tabular system supplied the network from which later editors derived Zagan’s nocturnal first decan of Sagittarius, Eight of Wands, and paired Sun/Mercury rank attributions. These do not occur in Weyer’s paragraph.

## Frater Achad

**[ABSENCE FINDING]** No specific documented evocation, vision, or extended Zagan commentary by Charles Stansfeld Jones/Frater Achad was located. His importance to Thelemic Qabalah does not by itself constitute a Zagan association.

## Poke Runyon

**[MODERN ESOTERICISM]** Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* developed a twentieth-century Solomonic method informed by Ruddian angel magic and Arsenal manuscript angelic seals. It is relevant to the reception of the demon–angel pairing, but no independently documented Zagan operation by Runyon was located.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

**[MODERN ESOTERICISM]** DuQuette, Hyatt, and Wilson’s *Illustrated Goetia* prints Zagan’s Sagittarius/Eight of Wands/Sun–Mercury table and helped make these correspondences standard in contemporary English-language practice.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

**[SCHOLARLY/ESOTERIC EDITION]** Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* brought Harley 6483’s angelic-control material into print and argued for its relation to Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart. The book is the principal modern source for the Zagan–Umabel pairing.

## Joseph H. Peterson

**[SCHOLARLY EDITION]** Peterson collated manuscript witnesses, exposed the oil/wine error, corrected *Corne* to *coin*, documented spellings and numbers, and placed primary transcriptions online. His work is essential for separating manuscript evidence from twentieth-century homogenization.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

**[MODERN SCHOLAR-PRACTITIONER]** Stratton-Kent wrote influentially on goetic history, spirit catalogues, and the relationship of grimoire magic to older necromantic and chthonic currents. No Zagan-specific historical derivation by him was verified here. Claims that his general “Greek goēs” thesis proves an ancient Greek identity for Zagan exceed the evidence.

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy altered the entry into the familiar nineteenth-century *Zagam* form. Le Breton’s importance is iconographic at the level of the *Dictionnaire infernal* generally; he did not, on the evidence located, furnish Zagan with a portrait.

## Lenain, Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

These authors form the principal modern chain for Umabel:

1. Lenain: detailed 1823 angelic notice.
2. Papus: occult republication and popularization.
3. Ambelain: operative theurgical elaboration.
4. Haziel: late-twentieth-century guardian-angel popularization.

# Grimoire ancestors and parallels

## *Livre des esperitz*

**[TEXT]** Contains *Zagon, roy*, but no surviving detailed office. This is Zagan’s most important pre-Weyer name attestation.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

**[TEXT/RECONSTRUCTION]** Weyer explicitly identifies his source-family as a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also called a book of Empto/Emperto Solomon concerning demonic princes and kings. Folger MS V.b.26’s *Zagayne* demonstrates that a closely related English spirit-office tradition existed near Weyer’s time.

## Munich handbook, Clm 849

**[ABSENCE FINDING]** The fifteenth-century Munich Manual of Demonic Magic is an important ancestor of European ritual conjuration, but no Zagan/Zagon/Zagayne entry was located. It is a genre ancestor, not a demonstrated name-source for Zagan.

## *Book of Abramelin*

**[ABSENCE FINDING]** No secure Zagan occurrence was found in the standard Abramelin spirit lists. Given the instability of demon names across translations, an unidentified cognate cannot be absolutely excluded, but no equation is presently justified.

## *Testament of Solomon*

**[TEXT/ABSENCE FINDING]** The *Testament* supplies the ancient literary model of Solomon interrogating and constraining demons through superior angels. It contains figures such as Ornias, Beelzeboul, Asmodeus, and Ephippas, but no identifiable Zagan.

**[RECONSTRUCTION]** Rudd’s angel-opposes-demon construction resembles the *Testament’s* recurring question, “By what angel are you frustrated?” That structural parallel does not prove Zagan existed in antiquity.

## Haagenti: the striking doublet

**[TEXT]** Weyer’s Haagenti is also a great President who:

- appears as a great bull with griffin wings;
- becomes human at the exorcist’s command;
- makes people wise;
- changes metals into gold;
- turns water into wine and wine into water;
- commands the same number of legions as Zagan.

This near-duplication is too close to ignore.

Possible explanations include:

1. two originally distinct spirits accumulated the same office;
2. a copying duplication created a second name;
3. Zagan and Haagenti descend from variant names or recensions of one spirit;
4. the compiler deliberately preserved two spirits with similar powers.

No manuscript proof presently selects one explanation. A confident claim that they “are the same demon” is therefore premature.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Zagan or Zagam?

**Conclusion:** *Zagan* is the better-supported form.

- Pre-Weyer *Zagon* and *Zagayne* support final *-n*.
- The 1577 Weyer evidence includes *Zagan/Zagã*.
- Scot has *Zagan*.
- All principal Goetia manuscripts in Peterson’s crosswalk have *Zagan*.
- *Zagam* appears as a corrupt Weyer reading and is perpetuated by de Plancy.

Thus *Zagam* is historically real as a printed variant but probably secondary.

## 2. Is Zagan Weyer no. 47 or 48?

**Conclusion:** No. 48 in the complete 69-spirit sequence.

No. 47 results when Pruflas is omitted and the subsequent catalogue is renumbered. Since the user’s comparison concerns Weyer’s complete 69, 48 is the appropriate number.

## 3. Blood into wine or blood into oil?

**Conclusion:** Weyer’s earliest secure full text says blood ↔ oil.

The likely history is:

1. Weyer: blood ↔ oil.
2. Scot: *oil* misread or printed as *wine*.
3. Goetia: blood → wine and duplicated water → wine.
4. Mathers: preserves the corrupt Goetia.
5. De Plancy: restores blood → oil, probably from Weyer.

Both readings are historically documented, but they are not equally early.

## 4. “All metals into coin” or “base metals into gold”?

Weyer and the Goetia say metal becomes **coinage appropriate to that metal or dominion**. They do not promise that every metal becomes gold.

De Plancy rewrites the ability as:

- lead → silver;
- copper → gold.

Modern descriptions frequently merge both, making Zagan an unrestricted alchemical gold-maker. That is a later amplification.

## 5. Is Zagan the patron of counterfeiters and fraud?

**[MODERN INFERENCE]** The coin-making power naturally suggested counterfeiting to later demonologists. Some encyclopedias consequently call Zagan a protector of counterfeiters, fraud, deceit, or false money.

Neither Weyer nor the ordinary Goetia says this. The early texts describe a marvelous transmutation, not a moral or professional patronage. The counterfeiter association is a plausible cultural extrapolation, not original office.

## 6. Is Zagan Dagon?

No early source supports it. The names, iconographies, and manuscript chain do not establish identity. It remains a modern demonolatry claim.

## 7. Was Zagan once a cherub?

**[MODERN/LEGEND]** Modern pages infer the Cherubim because Ezekiel’s living creatures include an ox/bull aspect and wings. No Weyer, Scot, Goetia, Rudd, or de Plancy passage gives Zagan a pre-fall angelic choir.

## 8. Did Zagan imitate Christ and get banished by Jesus?

**[MODERN INVENTION]** This story appears in recent web “apocrypha” or collaborative demon-fiction pages. The sites themselves sometimes disclose that these sections are community-created. No ancient apocryphon, church father, medieval legend, Weyer passage, or de Plancy entry supporting it was found.

## 9. Does Zagan govern vampirism or immortality?

**[MODERN ESOTERICISM]** Michael W. Ford and later Luciferian writers interpret blood-to-wine as vampyric initiation, evolution, or survival after bodily death. The historical description only states transformations. The initiatory theology belongs to those modern authors.

## 10. Are Zagan and Haagenti one spirit?

The duplication makes the proposal reasonable, but the sources preserve them separately and give different names and ranks. “Possible textual doublet” is justified; “identical demon” is not established.

## 11. Thirty or thirty-three legions?

- Weyer: 33.
- Scot: 33.
- Sloane/Goetia: 33.
- Mathers–Crowley: 33.
- De Plancy: 30.

Thirty-three has the stronger and earlier manuscript chain. De Plancy’s 30 is a nineteenth-century variant or simplification.

## 12. Is the 0°–5° Aquarius placement Zagan’s own?

Only by modern number-transfer. It belongs properly to Umabel in the Shem system. Crowley’s direct Goetic allocation places Zagan over the nocturnal 0°–10° Sagittarius decan.

## 13. Did the Golden Dawn invent the Zagan–Umabel pairing?

No. The one-to-one controlling relation is present in the Ruddian manuscript tradition represented by Harley 6483. Golden Dawn and later occultism expanded and reorganized the astrological and tarot correspondences.

# Open questions

1. **Trithemius:** A direct high-resolution examination of *Polygraphia* p. 344 is needed to confirm the precise spelling and context of Peterson’s reported Zagan occurrence.

2. **Date of the *Livre des esperitz*:** Catalogue descriptions and scholarship differ between a fifteenth-century origin and a mid-sixteenth-century date for the surviving manuscript. Paleographical consensus should be stated more precisely in future work.

3. **Lost Zagon paragraph:** Was Zagon deliberately only named in the roster, or has his full *Livre des esperitz* office been lost?

4. **Weyer’s immediate exemplar:** The relationship among Weyer’s lost *Liber Officiorum*, Folger V.b.26, the *Livre des esperitz*, and other surviving lists is not stemmatically settled.

5. **Zagan–Haagenti duplication:** More witnesses could show whether a copied paragraph migrated between the names or whether both were already distinct in the archetype.

6. **Scot’s first-edition typography:** Collation of multiple 1584 copies could determine whether “wine” for “oil” occurs uniformly or represents a state/setting variation.

7. **Seal development:** A photographic collation of Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, Northwestern 65, and the 1904 plate would permit a precise genealogy of every loop and terminal.

8. **Rudd authorship:** Harley 6483’s relationship to the historical Thomas Rudd remains reconstructed from copyists’ notices and associated manuscripts, not proven by autograph evidence.

9. **Umabel vocalization:** The trigram V-M-B is stable, but *Umabel*, *Vamab-El*, and *VeMeBe-El* embody different pronunciation traditions. No biblical vowels settle the matter.

10. **The “God above all names” gloss:** Lenain securely gives “God above all things.” The date and author of the alternative “above all names” formulation remain to be located.

11. **Calendar discrepancies:** The several January date ranges arise from different astrological conventions. An edition should calculate the Sun’s actual tropical longitude for a specified year rather than treat one civil-date range as timeless.

12. **De Plancy’s first edition:** The 1863 Zagam entry is verified. A page-by-page check of the 1818 first edition would establish whether the entry was already present verbatim or acquired alterations in later editions.

13. **Modern media:** Several fan databases repeat alleged appearances in *Persona*, *Hellboy*, and *Helluva Boss*. No primary credits, scripts, official encyclopedias, or episode evidence were found for those claims.

# Vassago: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statements:

- **Documented text** — directly present in a manuscript, printed primary source, or securely catalogued work.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a conclusion proposed from textual comparison, codicology, or historical context.
- **Tradition** — a teaching repeated within an occult lineage, without necessarily being historical fact.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — an intentional symbolic assignment, such as planet, metal, zodiacal decan, tarot card, angel, or divine name.
- **Disputed claim or legend** — reported historically or within magical literature but unsupported, contested, or impossible to verify.
- **Modern invention or reception** — a feature created by recent occultists, fiction writers, artists, games, television, or online demonology.

One important result should be stated immediately: **Vassago has no entry in Johann Weyer's _Pseudomonarchia Daemonum_ or Reginald Scot's translation of it.** Consequently there is no authentic “Weyer Latin description of Vassago” and no corresponding “Scot English description” to quote. The first secure text using the spelling **Vassago** belongs to the seventeenth-century _Lemegeton_ tradition, although the forms **Vsagoo** and **Vzago** occur in a late-sixteenth-century manuscript and may be related.

## The name and its origins

### 1. No established ancient etymology

**Scholarly finding:** No ancient god, hero, geographical place, Greek word, Latin word, Hebrew demon, or securely identified older mythological figure has been demonstrated behind the name **Vassago**.

This differs materially from names in the broader demonic catalogue that can plausibly be connected with:

- Baal, the Northwest Semitic title “lord”;
- Astarte/Ashtoreth;
- Amun;
- Asmodeus/Ashmedai;
- Belial/Beliyaʿal;
- Phoenix, the Greek and Latin name of the fabulous bird;
- Naberius/Cerberus and other classically colored transformations.

Those parallels explain why researchers look for an antique origin for every Goetic name, but they do not establish one for Vassago.

### 2. The documented spellings

Peterson's comparative table gives the following transmission:

| Source | Date | Form |
|---|---:|---|
| Weyer, _Pseudomonarchia Daemonum_ | 1577 edition | Absent |
| Scot, _Discoverie of Witchcraft_ | 1584 | Absent |

| Sloane MS 3824 | seventeenth century | **Vassago**, **Usago** |
| Sloane MS 3825 | seventeenth century | **Vassago** |
| Sloane MS 3648 | seventeenth century | **Vassago** |
| Sloane MS 2731 | seventeenth-century tradition; copy/date disputed | **Vassago** |
| Harley MS 6483 | 1712–1713 copy | **Uasago**, **Vasago** |
| Northwestern University MS 65 | later manuscript witness | **Vasago** |
| Mathers–Crowley | 1904 | **Vassago** |

The underlying comparison is available in Peterson's [comparative list of Goetic spirit names](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

Several cautions follow.

- Initial **V** and **U** were graphically and orthographically interchangeable in early-modern writing. Thus _Uasago_ need not represent a different pronunciation from _Vasago_.
- The doubled consonant in _Vassago_ may be an orthographic stabilization rather than evidence of a different root.
- _Vas-Sago_ in the Wellcome copy looks like a scribal or typographical segmentation. It is not evidence that the name was historically understood as two words.
- _Usagoo_ is a modern normalized spelling derived chiefly from Folger's _Vsagoo_. It is not the spelling used by the _Goetia_ entry.
- _Vesago_ is primarily modern. It became culturally conspicuous through later fiction and the alias used by Rod Ferrell, but it has no standing as an early manuscript reading.

### 3. The Folger problem: Vsagoo and Vzago

Folger Shakespeare Library MS V.b.26, catalogued as a book of magic from approximately 1577–1583, contains two especially important names.

Entry 70 begins:

> “Vsagoo, magnus preses, he appeareth like an Aungell & is Just & true…”

The remainder assigns him women's love, knowledge of hidden treasure, twenty legions, and the marginal characterization “A true spirit.”

Elsewhere, entry 3 is **Vzago**. It says, in substance, that he takes human form, can make a person wise and invisible, changes one human form into another, gains universal love and favor, answers truthfully, and appears like an angel. [Folger MS V.b.26 transcription, pp. 86 and 91](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf); [Folger manuscript images and catalogue](https://digitalcollections.folger.edu/bib228887-238418)

These entries create three possibilities:

1. **Scholarly reconstruction:** Vsagoo and Vzago are corruptions or variant copies of one older name.
2. **Alternative reconstruction:** One, probably Vzago, influenced the _Goetia's_ Vassago while the other was a separate spirit.
3. **Open possibility:** The similarity is accidental or the result of contamination between separate lists.

Peterson warns that their descriptions do not match the _Goetia_ entry closely enough to identify them without qualification. The resemblance is nevertheless important because the manuscript is roughly contemporary with Weyer and may preserve the earliest cognate forms.

### 4. Proposed Hebrew and Latin etymologies

#### Hebrew “and they roared”

Some modern websites compare Vassago with Hebrew ושאגו, approximately _ve-shaʾagu_, “and they roared.” This is a possible string resemblance but not a demonstrated etymology.

**Disputed claim:** No early manuscript glosses the name in Hebrew; no known Hebrew demonological source applies that word to a spirit; and the proposed derivation does not explain the early spellings reliably. It should be treated as a modern back-formation.

#### _Vas_ plus “prophecy”

Another modern suggestion divides the name into Latin _vas_, “vessel,” and an alleged element _sago_ connected with prophecy or sagacity. The suggestion is attractive because Vassago became associated with crystal divination.

**Disputed claim:** _Vas_ is valid Latin for vessel, but the proposed second component is not a securely documented Latin noun meaning “prophecy.” Nor does the manuscript form _Vas-Sago_ appear early enough to establish such a construction. This is symbolic wordplay, not historical philology.

#### “Babylonian,” “Chaldean,” or an ancient oracle god

These descriptions circulate in modern demonology.

**Legend/modern invention:** No cuneiform, Greek, Roman, Jewish, or patristic text naming Vassago has been identified. The adjectives generally derive from the longstanding magical prestige of “Chaldean” astrology, not from evidence that Vassago was a Babylonian deity.

### 5. Earliest responsible conclusion

The best present formulation is:

- **Earliest plausible cognates:** Vsagoo and Vzago in Folger MS V.b.26, c. 1577–1583.
- **Earliest secure exact name:** Vassago in the seventeenth-century _Lemegeton_/Goetic manuscript tradition.
- **No demonstrated pre-Weyer ancient or medieval attestation.**
- **No secure ancient etymology.**

## The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, 1577: absent

Johann Weyer appended the _Pseudomonarchia Daemonum_ to later editions of _De praestigiis daemonum_. The 1577 edition contains sixty-nine numbered spirits. Vassago is not one of them.

After Agares, Weyer's third spirit is Marbas:

> “Marbas, alias Barbas, Præses magnus…”

That is, “Marbas, otherwise Barbas, a great President.” It is not Vassago. The remainder describes Marbas as first appearing as a powerful lion, assuming human form, answering about hidden matters, causing and curing illness, teaching mechanical arts, transforming people, and governing thirty-six legions. [Weyer’s Latin text and parallel translation](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

Therefore:

- **Documented text:** Weyer contains no Vassago entry.
- **Common error:** Web pages that attribute Vassago's “good nature” and lost-object office directly to Weyer are wrong.
- **No Latin Vassago quotation exists in Weyer.**

Weyer says that his catalogue derives from a _Liber officiorum spirituum_, a “Book of the Offices of Spirits.” He also indicates that he suppressed operational material to prevent use. That makes his text a major ancestor of the _Goetia_, but it does not prove that every later Goetic addition stood in Weyer's exemplar.

## 2. Scot, 1584: absent

Reginald Scot printed an English translation of Weyer's catalogue in Book XV of _The Discoverie of Witchcraft_. The translation is traditionally attributed to Abraham Fleming.

After Agares, Scot likewise proceeds to Marbas:

> “Marbas, alias Barbas is a great president…”

Scot gives no Vassago entry, seal, office, rank, or legion count. [Scot, _Discoverie_, Book XV](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm)

This matters because the seventeenth-century English compiler of the _Goetia_ demonstrably used Scot's text. Vassago therefore cannot have been copied mechanically from the printed Scot catalogue.

## 3. Folger MS V.b.26, c. 1577–1583

### Vsagoo, no. 70

**Documented text:** Vsagoo is a _magnus preses_, a great President. He appears like an angel, is “just and true,” gives the love of women, tells of hidden treasures, and commands twenty legions. A marginal note calls him “A true spirit.”

This anticipates two features of later Vassago reception:

- a favorable or truthful disposition;
- discovery of hidden things.

But it differs in rank, office, legion total, and list position.

### Vzago, no. 3

**Documented text:** Vzago appears in human form and like an angel; makes a person wise and invisible; transforms human appearance; obtains love and favor; and answers truthfully.

Its number, **3**, strikingly matches Vassago's later Goetic number. Its offices, however, do not match the later entry except in the broad idea of truthful answers.

### Assessment

**Scholarly reconstruction:** The names are probable relatives of Vassago but cannot simply be treated as the same fully developed entity. The evidence is consistent with a fluid manuscript tradition in which similar names, ranks, and offices could be separated, recombined, or reassigned.

## 4. Sloane MS 3824: crystal experiments

Sloane 3824 contains conjurations directed to “Vassago or Usago” and an “Experiment of Vassago” involving appearance in a crystal. An immediately following experiment says that Agares is practically the same as the preceding Vassago operation. [Discussion and manuscript collation](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/05/meddling-with-goetia-again-5_15.html); [Sloane 3824 overview](https://grimorium.lat/biblioteca/sloane3824-tracts/)

This material is important for two reasons.

- It provides a concrete reason for later writers to make Vassago a favored spirit of crystal divination.
- It may explain the phrase “of the same nature as Agares”: Vassago and Agares circulated together in closely related practical experiments.

**Caution:** The romantic attribution of such operations to the court of Edward IV is part of the later Sibley–Hockley transmission. It is not independent fifteenth-century evidence for Vassago.

## 5. Sloane 3825, fol. 101r

Sloane 3825 supplies the chief text used by Peterson:

> “The Third spirit is a mighty prince, being of ye same Nature as Agares, he is called Vassago, This spirit is of a good nature…”

The rest of the entry assigns the following:

- revelation of things past;
- declaration of things to come;
- discovery of everything hidden or lost;
- government of twenty-six legions;
- a seal.

[Full manuscript transcription and seal](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#vassago)

### What it does not say

The entry does **not** say that Vassago:

- teaches languages or sciences;
- causes love;
- provides familiars;
- grants invisibility;
- transmutes metals or people;
- appears as a crocodile, bird, dragon, old man, angel, or child;
- is a fallen angel;
- belongs to an element;
- rules a compass direction;
- smells of water or incense;
- accepts a particular colored candle;
- has a specific “enn.”

Those statements come either from Folger cognates, from Agares, from later correspondences, or from modern occult practice.

### “Finder of lost things”

**Documented text:** The popular title “finder of lost things” is a fair shorthand for the clause that Vassago discovers “all things hidden or lost.” It is not a formal title in the manuscript.

### “Good nature”

**Documented text:** This is one of the most distinctive clauses in the entry. It describes disposition, not moral salvation, angelic status, harmlessness, or permission to dispense with the ritual's constraints.

## 6. Sloane 2731 and 3648

Both witnesses retain the form **Vassago**. Their catalogues show that the spelling was stable in the central _Lemegeton_ family.

Sloane 2731 contains a date sometimes read as 1572, but its compilation and handwriting raise significant chronological problems. It should not be used uncritically to claim a pre-Weyer Vassago. Peterson treats the _Lemegeton_ as a seventeenth-century compilation assembled from materials of differing ages. [Peterson’s manuscript overview](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm); [digital Sloane 2731 edition](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/)

## 7. Harley MS 6483, 1712–1713

The so-called Rudd manuscript reads approximately:

> “The third Spirit is a mighty Prince of the same nature as Agares called Vasago.”

It repeats the good disposition, past-and-future knowledge, discovery of hidden or lost things, and twenty-six legions. It additionally directs that the seal be worn as a lamen.

The Harley page presents the demonic seal beside an angelic seal containing a Psalm. This is the principal material basis for Skinner and Rankine's _Goetia of Dr Rudd_. Their transcription appears on pp. 104–105 of the 2007 edition. [Publisher catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf)

The attribution to Thomas Rudd is not entirely straightforward. Harley 6483 was copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713 and contains material associated with “Dr Rudd,” but how much goes back to the historical Thomas Rudd, and whether he authored, owned, edited, or merely became attached to the material, remains a reconstruction.

## 8. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The printed entry appears on p. 13 of _The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King_:

> “The Third Spirit is a Mighty Prince, being of the same nature as Agares. He is called Vassago.”

Mathers and Crowley then modernize the manuscript capitalization and spelling while retaining the good nature, past-and-future office, recovery of hidden or lost things, twenty-six legions, and seal. [1904 page image](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu/27); [scan](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf); [Hermetic Library transcription](https://hermetic.com/norton/classics/goetia/vassago)

This is the form from which most twentieth-century English occult literature derives.

## 9. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson's _Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis: The Lesser Key of Solomon_ uses Sloane 3825 as its base and records manuscript variants. Vassago appears in Part I, within pp. 7–10 of the printed edition, with the manuscript locator fol. 101r.

Peterson's crucial critical note is that Vassago is **not in Weyer or Scot**, while Folger contains Vsagoo and a separate Vzago whose descriptions do not match exactly. [Peterson edition PDF](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf); [updated digital transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#vassago)

## Rank, metal, planet, time, appearance, and seal

### Rank and planetary metal

The _Goetia's_ “Observations” establish a sevenfold system:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver in equal parts |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |

Accordingly, Vassago's status as Prince gives him **Jupiter and tin**. This is a ritual-material attribution; it is not an etymological or mythological origin.

The term “Prelate” in the observations corresponds to the catalogue's Princes. Peterson's edition gives Princes/Prelates any hour of the day. [Goetic observations](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Time of day

**Documented text:** Vassago may be called at any hour during daylight under the rank-based rule for Princes.

**Modern divergence:** Paul Huson and some later practical manuals permit Vassago by day or night, particularly for crystal scrying. That is a twentieth-century working adaptation, not the strict wording of the manuscript observations.

### Moon

The observations prefer an even-numbered lunar age: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old. The text says no other lunar age is profitable.

This is general Goetic timing, not a unique Vassago attribution.

### Appearance and “second form”

**Documented text:** The Vassago entry gives no bodily appearance and no second form that the conjurer may demand.

A separate general figure in the ritual is intended to constrain spirits into “humane shape,” but that does not supply Vassago with a unique human appearance.

The following should therefore be separated:

- **Folger cognates:** angelic and human form.
- **Agares:** an old man riding a crocodile and carrying a goshawk.
- **Crowley's _777_:** Vassago's magical image is “Like Agares.”
- **Modern art:** robed oracle, many-armed shadow, dragon, gray humanoid, or scarlet macaw.
- **Core _Goetia_:** no form specified.

### Seal

The common seal can be inspected in Peterson's [Sloane-derived figure](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/g3.png).

In words, it consists of:

- a closed circular boundary;
- a dominant vertical central stroke;
- a small central loop, ring, or eye-like form;
- short transverse or hooked arms;
- a wider angular lower construction resembling a base, stand, altar, or branching legs.

The description is necessarily approximate because the mark is graphic rather than linguistic.

#### Variants

- **Sloane 3825:** the basic manuscript character.
- **Sloane 2731:** recognizably the same structure, but the available reproduction is damaged or indistinct.
- **Mathers–Crowley:** a cleaned printed version inside a circular band, with the name arranged around the design.
- **Harley 6483:** the demon's character is placed within a more elaborate circular lamen and paired with a second circle for the constraining angel.
- **Waite:** reproduces the Goetic character but also publishes a separate “Seal of Vassago used in White Magic,” taken from the crystallomantic tradition. That is not evidence for two ancient seals.

Peterson notes that spirit sigils are not known in the earlier _Office of Spirits_ manuscripts. Their absence is evidence against claims that the familiar Vassago seal is ancient, Babylonian, or Solomonic in a literal historical sense.

## The place of Vassago in the catalogue

### Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

The arithmetic is:

- Weyer's catalogue: **69** spirits.
- Spirit omitted from the later _Goetia_: **Pruflas**, also called Bufas.
- Spirits added by the _Goetia_: **Vassago, Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius**.
- Result: 69 − 1 + 4 = **72**.

Peterson suggests that the expansion to seventy-two was probably encouraged by the numerological prestige of the seventy-two divine or angelic names. [Peterson’s introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### The reordering is more than an insertion

Weyer's opening sequence is approximately:

1. Bael  
2. Agares  
3. Marbas  
4. Pruflas  
5. Amon  

The _Goetia_ opens:

1. Bael  
2. Agares  
3. **Vassago**  
4. Gamigin  
5. Marbas  
6. Valefar  
7. Amon  

Thus Vassago is inserted near the front, but Gamigin is also moved from much later in Weyer. The list is not simply Weyer's order with four entries added at the end.

Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius occupy positions 70–72, making Vassago's placement at no. 3 especially conspicuous. Why the compiler placed him there remains unknown.

## The angel

## 1. The seventy-two-fold divine name

Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional Hebrew consonantal text, of three verses of seventy-two letters each. In the later construction:

1. verse 19 is written in its normal direction;
2. verse 20 is written in reverse;
3. verse 21 is written normally;
4. the letters are read vertically in groups of three.

This boustrophedon arrangement produces seventy-two triplets. The third is **סיט**, samekh–yod–tet. Christian and later practical Kabbalah commonly add **אל**, _El_, producing **סיטאל**, _Sitael_. [Exodus 14:19–21](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Kabbalah); [Chabad explanation of the seventy-two names](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/1388270/jewish/72-Names-of-G-d.htm)

### Terminological caution

In rabbinic sources, _Shem ha-Mephorash_ means the “explicit,” “distinguished,” or “interpreted” divine Name and often denotes the Tetragrammaton. Its routine identification with precisely seventy-two angel names is a later development. The Christian Cabalistic angel list should not simply be projected backward into antiquity. [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Names of God”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/11305-names-)

## 2. Sefer Raziel and medieval transmission

_Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh_ is traditionally presented as angelic wisdom revealed to Adam by Raziel.

- **Legend:** Raziel gave a primordial book of celestial secrets to Adam.
- **Historical assessment:** The surviving _Sefer Raziel_ is a medieval Hebrew and Aramaic compilation assembled from multiple magical, cosmological, and mystical strata.

The book is part of the wider environment in which divine names, angelic permutations, seals, and the seventy-two-letter Name circulated. It is not evidence that a fixed ancient angel called Sitael was already paired with a demon called Vassago. [Modern scholarship on _Sefer Raziel_](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2017/html?lang=en)

## 3. Reuchlin and Agrippa

Johannes Reuchlin's _De Arte Cabalistica_ of 1517 helped transmit the seventy-two angel names in Christian Cabala. Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa reproduced the scheme in _De occulta philosophia_, Book III, chapter 25.

Adding endings such as _-el_ and _-iah_ made the Hebrew triplets pronounceable as angelic names. Agrippa's tables also transmitted spellings and errors that subsequent occultists treated as authoritative. [Agrippa, _Occult Philosophy_ III.25](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm)

## 4. Kircher

Athanasius Kircher's _Oedipus Aegyptiacus_, especially the Cabalistic tables printed in the early 1650s, became a major source for the numbered angels, their divine attributes, biblical clauses, and zodiacal arrangement. [Kircher transcription and tables](https://esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm); [facsimile](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_XjfzMnomDQoC/page/n299/mode/thumb)

For Sitael, the traditional Latin title is:

**_Deus spes omnium creaturarum_** — “God, the hope of all creatures.”

This is the source of the frequently given “meaning” of Sitael. Strictly, it is a theological attribute associated with the name, not a philological translation of the consonants סיט.

## 5. Rudd's manuscript: Sitael or Syrael?

This is the central textual difficulty.

### What modern Rudd-derived tables say

Because Vassago is demon 3 and Sitael is angel 3, modern reconstructions commonly give:

**Vassago — constrained by Sitael.**

The Psalm in the manuscript also matches the Psalm associated with Sitael in the Kircher–Lenain tradition.

### What Harley 6483 visibly says

The angelic name beside Vassago's seal is transcribed as **Syrael**, with **Sirael** as a normalized alternative. Peterson's reproduction labels the figure “Sigil for Vasago, from Harley 6483.” The right-hand angelic circle contains the Psalm text and the name _Syrael_. [Peterson, Appendix IV, figure 14](https://logoilibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Lesser-Key-of-Solomon.pdf)

Accordingly:

- **Documented manuscript reading:** Syrael/Sirael.
- **Scholarly or editorial harmonization:** Sitael.
- **Evidence favoring the harmonization:** ordinal position 3 and the correct Sitael Psalm.
- **Evidence against stating it as certain:** the surviving manuscript does not plainly read _Sitael_.

The responsible identification is therefore:

> Vassago is paired with **Syrael/Sirael in the literal Harley witness**, commonly corrected or harmonized to **Sitael** in modern Rudd-derived tables.

## 6. The Psalm

Harley 6483 gives:

> “Dicam Domino: Susceptor meus es tu, et refugium meum…”

The complete clause ends by placing hope in God. It is Psalm 90:2 in the Vulgate numbering, Psalm 91:2 in the King James and most modern Protestant numbering:

> “I will say of the LORD, He is my refuge and my fortress…”

[Bible text, Psalm 91:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2091%3A2&version=KJV)

The Rudd transcription sometimes reads _sperabo in sum_, almost certainly a scribal or transcriptional error for _sperabo in eum_, “I shall hope in him.”

## 7. How the angel constrains the spirit

The Rudd arrangement places:

- the demon's seal in one circular lamen;
- the corresponding angelic name and Psalm in another;
- divine names around or within the figures.

**Tradition:** The angel is not presented as Vassago's personal guardian or benevolent counterpart. It functions as superior divine authority by which the magician binds, limits, or constrains the spirit.

This is a specifically Christian-Cabalistic and ceremonial-magical synthesis. The basic Sloane 3825 _Goetia_ does not pair each demon with a Shem angel.

## 8. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain's _La science cabalistique_ systematized the seventy-two angels for nineteenth-century French occultism. For Sitael, it gives:

- angel number 3;
- divine attribute “God, the hope of all creatures”;
- the five-degree sector consisting of the 11th through 15th degrees of Aries, conventionally **10°00′–14°59′ Aries**;
- the Psalm corresponding to Vulgate 90:2;
- an invocation against adversity;
- protection from weapons and wild beasts;
- nobility, magnanimity, truthfulness, high office, and keeping one's word;
- under the contrary genius: hypocrisy, ingratitude, and perjury.

Lenain also distributes each angel across several days of the year and a twenty-minute interval of the day. Later tables commonly give Sitael approximately 00:40–01:00.

The often printed modern “guardian angel birthday” range around March 31–April 4 is a later conversion of the zodiacal sector. It should not be confused with all of Lenain's calendrical rulerships. [Lenain scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365); [OCR text](https://archive.org/stream/BIUSante_ms05365/BIUSante_ms05365_djvu.txt)

## 9. Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

### Papus

Papus, especially in _La Cabbale_ and his support for the revised publication of Lenain, transmitted the French occult interpretation of the seventy-two angels. [Papus, _La Cabbale_](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/abg405b2732165.pdf)

### Robert Ambelain

Ambelain's _La Kabbale pratique_ appeared in 1951. It expanded the angelic scheme into modern ritual practice, combining names, Psalms, zodiacal periods, hierarchies, and invocations.

**Historical qualification:** Ambelain is an important twentieth-century occult source, not an ancient Jewish authority. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb317160320); [English translation](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf)

### Haziel

The French author Haziel popularized the guardian-angel version of the system from the late 1980s onward, especially through books on the seventy-two serving angels, birth periods, prayers, and everyday petitions.

**Modern tradition:** The increasingly personalized claim that everyone has a Shem “guardian angel” determined by birthday belongs largely to this modern popularization. It should not be attributed directly to Exodus, Rudd, or the medieval _Sefer Raziel_. [BnF Haziel catalogue records](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/rechercher.do?index=AUT3&numNotice=12089673)

### Crowley's _777_

In _777_, Column CLIX is headed “Goetic Demons of Decans by Day.” For the Aries row, spirit 3 is Vassago, associated with Jupiter by rank, and the magical image is “Like Agares.” [Crowley, _777_](https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/crowley/liber/libers/liber777.pdf)

The corresponding Golden Dawn pip is the Four of Wands:

- 20°–30° Aries;
- Venus in Aries;
- “Lord of Perfected Work.”

The night spirit assigned to the same decan is Malphas. Thus one tarot pip carries two Goetic spirits in this overlay: a day spirit and a night spirit.

### Does _777_ give Vassago a quinance?

Not explicitly.

A decan is ten degrees. A quinance is five degrees. Later tables often assign the day spirit to the first five degrees of a decan and the night spirit to the second five, producing:

- Vassago: 20°–25° Aries;
- Malphas: 25°–30° Aries.

But the surviving printed _777_ table names them as day and night spirits of the entire decan; it does not spell out that precise five-degree split. The quinance is therefore a **later interpretive regularization**.

### The angel–demon zodiac mismatch

Sitael's 10°–15° Aries sector and Vassago's 20°–30° Aries decan do not coincide.

This shows that two independently constructed systems have been layered together:

1. the seventy-two Shem angels, each receiving five zodiacal degrees;
2. the seventy-two Goetic names divided into day and night rulers of thirty-six decans.

The ordinal pairing “angel 3 over demon 3” does not make their zodiacal sectors identical.

### Enochian overlays

Harley 6483 participates in a broader early-modern synthesis involving divine names, angelic tables, and material connected with John Dee's angelic system. Its prefatory material observes that some spirits are represented in Enochian tables.

However:

- no original Dee diary names Vassago;
- no secure Dee angelic square has been demonstrated as Vassago's;
- no primary source establishes a unique Enochian aethyr, governor, tablet square, or watchtower quarter for him.

Any such assignment is a later synthesis unless accompanied by a specific manuscript demonstration.

## Iconography

## 1. Manuscript seals

The earliest securely Goetic image of Vassago is not a portrait but a **seal or character**.

The Sloane and later printed forms are abstract linear signs. They do not depict:

- a face;
- an animal;
- a planetary emblem;
- a historical deity;
- a narrative scene.

The visual family is stable enough to recognize across manuscripts, but line endings, loops, outer circles, and name placement differ.

Harley 6483 adds the most important iconographic innovation: a paired angelic lamen. Its Vassago and Syrael circles visually enact the theory of constraint.

## 2. Mathers–Crowley seal

The 1904 engraving regularizes the manuscript figure into a clean printed emblem. It is this version, rather than the rougher Sloane drawing, that supplies most tattoos, jewelry, posters, and online illustrations. [1904 seal page](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu/27); [modern SVG derived from the printed seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:03-vassago-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg)

## 3. Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

Jacques Collin de Plancy first published the _Dictionnaire infernal_ in 1818. The famous 1863 sixth edition contains Louis Le Breton's engravings of selected demons.

**Negative finding:** Vassago has no identifiable entry or Breton portrait in the 1863 edition.

This is consistent with de Plancy's extensive dependence on the Weyer tradition: Vassago was absent from Weyer. Therefore no authentic “de Plancy portrait of Vassago” or de Plancy description can be quoted. [1863 Library of Congress scan](https://www.loc.gov/item/63056292/); [Gallica scan](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5754923d); [Wikisource facsimile index](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf)

Images circulated online as “Vassago from the _Dictionnaire Infernal_” are generally:

- another demon's Breton engraving relabeled;
- modern art imitating Breton's style;
- AI-generated or unattributed illustration.

## 4. Modern occult art

Because the core _Goetia_ gives no form, modern artists have considerable latitude.

### Michael W. Ford

Ford's _Luciferian Goetia_ calls Vassago an angelic ruler associated with tarot and describes a black-mirror manifestation as a wind-blown robed being combining angelic and demonic shadow, with many arms and taloned fingers.

**Modern invention/personal visionary description:** This imagery belongs to Ford's own ritual system and is not found in Sloane, Harley, Weyer, or Mathers. [Ford, _Luciferian Goetia_](https://www.hempenings.com/Esoteric/Goetia_Luciferian_Edition.pdf)

### Agares-derived imagery

Artists sometimes give Vassago:

- an old man;
- a crocodile;
- a goshawk.

That is a literalization of “same nature as Agares,” strengthened by _777_'s “Like Agares.” It is not Vassago's explicitly documented appearance in the seventeenth-century catalogue.

### Other unsourced images

Red dragons, faceless gray humanoids, serpentine prophets, and blue or aquatic “Prince of Waters” imagery are modern inventions without early textual support.

## People and works

## 1. Grimoire ancestors

### _Testament of Solomon_

The Greek _Testament of Solomon_ is a composite late-antique demonological narrative in which Solomon interrogates and controls demons through a divinely empowered ring.

It contains figures such as Ornias, Beelzeboul, Asmodeus, and numerous disease spirits. It supplies an important **Solomonic narrative archetype**, but not Vassago himself. [English text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Testament_of_Solomon)

**Finding:** No Vassago, Vasago, Vsagoo, or Vzago has been demonstrated in the text.

### Munich Handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer's handbook contains spirit catalogues and operations resembling later demonic magic. Richard Kieckhefer's _Forbidden Rites_ is the principal modern study.

Some offices and formulae have analogues in later catalogues, but no secure Vassago appears. [Kieckhefer, _Forbidden Rites_](https://books.google.com/books/about/Forbidden_Rites.html?id=vwVQAwAAQBAJ)

### _Livre des Esperitz_

The French _Livre des Esperitz_ is an important ancestor of the Weyer–Scot catalogue. Jean-Patrice Boudet has compared its names and offices with related Latin and English catalogues.

**Negative finding:** Vassago is absent. [Full French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier); [Boudet’s comparative study](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019)

### _Liber Officiorum Spirituum_

The “Book of the Offices of Spirits” is the closest named textual ancestor of Weyer's list. It circulated in unstable manuscript forms, including Folger V.b.26.

It is within this family—not in an ancient pantheon—that Vsagoo and Vzago first become visible. [Peterson’s _Office of Spirits_ material](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/offices.htm)

### Abramelin

The spirit lists in the _Book of Abramelin_ contain many names that overlap or resemble later grimoire catalogues.

**Negative finding:** No securely identical Vassago entry has been established. Similar-looking names should not be treated as identical without matching offices and manuscript forms.

## 2. Johann Weyer

Weyer is indispensable to the source chain even though he does not name Vassago. His catalogue supplied the backbone for Scot and, through Scot, for much of the _Goetia_.

Calling Weyer “the first author to describe Vassago” is false.

## 3. Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot published the English catalogue in an explicitly skeptical work intended to expose magical and witch-hunting claims. The embedded magical texts were nevertheless reused as working sources by later compilers.

Again, Vassago is absent.

## 4. The anonymous Lemegeton compiler

The anonymous compiler or compilers are responsible for Vassago's secure canonical identity:

- no. 3;
- Prince;
- good nature;
- past and future;
- hidden or lost things;
- twenty-six legions;
- seal.

The work attributes itself to Solomon, but that authorship is legendary. The surviving _Lemegeton_ is an early-modern compilation, not a composition by the biblical king.

## 5. Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

The Rudd-associated tradition paired demonic seals with superior angelic powers. Peter Smart's 1712–1713 Harley copy is the surviving witness.

**Documented:** the manuscript exists and gives Vasago with Syrael/Sirael.

**Reconstruction:** Thomas Rudd's precise role in composing or organizing every component is debated.

## 6. Frederick Hockley, Ebenezer Sibley, and the Edward IV legend

Nineteenth-century crystallomantic manuscripts copied by Frederick Hockley preserve an “Experiment of Vassago” attributed through material associated with Dr. Ebenezer Sibley.

One title claims that a magical experiment with Birto, Agares, Baalpharos, and Vassago occurred at the court of Edward IV.

- **Documented:** nineteenth-century manuscripts make the claim.
- **Legend:** no fifteenth-century record independently establishes that Edward IV or his court conjured Vassago.
- **Historical value:** the manuscripts show the continuity of Vassago as a crystal spirit.

[Wellcome manuscript title page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Title_page_of_a_treatise_on_magic,_19th_C_Wellcome_L0037439.jpg)

## 7. A. E. Waite

Waite summarized Vassago as a mighty Prince of Agares's nature who knows past, present, and future and discovers lost or hidden things. He also published a distinct seal associated with “White Magic” and the crystal experiment.

Waite's wording adds **present** to the older “past and future.” That is a small but instructive expansion in later reception. [Waite, _Book of Black Magic and of Pacts_](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1910__waite___book_of_black_magic_and_pacts.pdf); [revised _Book of Ceremonial Magic_](https://www.magicgatebg.com/Books/Waite%2C%20Arthur%20Edward%20-%20The%20Book%20of%20Ceremonial%20Magic.pdf)

## 8. S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Mathers prepared the English _Goetia_ edition; Crowley published it in 1904 with his “Preliminary Invocation,” notes, and magical framing.

Crowley's later _777_ supplied the decisive astrological and tarot overlay:

- third decan of Aries;
- day spirit;
- Four of Wands;
- “Like Agares.”

These are not present in the early Vassago entry.

## 9. Frater Achad

No reliable Vassago-specific operation or sustained discussion by Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, was found.

He belongs to the Crowleyan milieu in which _777_ and Goetic correspondences circulated, but it would be misleading to list him as a documented Vassago practitioner without a text, diary entry, or publication.

## 10. Paul Huson

Huson's _Mastering Witchcraft_ made Vassago especially prominent in twentieth-century crystal scrying. His method contributed substantially to the modern image of Vassago as an approachable oracle and finder of lost objects.

**Modern practical tradition:** Huson's ritual instructions and day-or-night flexibility do not reproduce the manuscript rules exactly. [Huson, _Mastering Witchcraft_](https://libroesoterico.com/biblioteca/autores/m_ingles/Mastering%20Witchcraft.pdf)

## 11. Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon's _Book of Solomon's Magick_ integrates Goetic demons, crystal work, and Shem angels. His tables pair Vassago with Sitael and the appropriate Psalm.

Runyon acknowledges that the angelic lists and orders inherited from Agrippa, Vigenère, Rudd, and later sources do not always agree. His Sitael pairing is therefore a deliberate practical synthesis. [Runyon, _Book of Solomon’s Magick_](https://attfinnasanningen.se/dokument_offentligt/books/mystery_school/magic/poke%20runyon%20book%20of%20solomons%20magick.pdf)

## 12. Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette coauthored _Aleister Crowley's Illustrated Goetia_ with Christopher Hyatt and David P. Wilson. It is an influential modern practical and psychological presentation.

DuQuette has also described a 1994 ceremonial working involving all seventy-two spirits in connection with the _Tarot of Ceremonial Magick_. That is evidence of engagement with the full catalogue, not a uniquely documented Vassago operation. [DuQuette et al., _Illustrated Goetia_](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf)

## 13. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their _Goetia of Dr Rudd_ appeared in 2007 and is the principal printed study of the Harley system. Its importance for Vassago lies in:

- transcribing the Vasago entry;
- reproducing the paired angelic seal;
- explaining the constraining function of the Shem angels;
- recovering the Psalm and divine-name structure.

The book's title expresses their reconstruction of the manuscript's Rudd provenance; it should not be mistaken for an uncontested autograph attribution. [Golden Hoard catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf)

## 14. Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson's 2001 critical edition and continuously corrected Esoteric Archives transcription provide the most accessible manuscript-based account. His work is especially valuable for:

- preserving Sloane spelling;
- identifying folio numbers;
- recording Harley and other variants;
- distinguishing Vassago from Folger's two cognate names;
- documenting absence from Weyer and Scot;
- noting the late appearance of the sigils.

## 15. Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent argued for reading grimoires as parts of a living spirit tradition and explored possible classical and chthonic backgrounds for later magic.

No securely sourced Vassago-specific ancient identification was found in his work. His general methodology is influential, but it does not justify assigning Vassago a Greek, Roman, or Near Eastern predecessor without textual evidence.

## 16. Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, and Julien Véronèse

These scholars illuminate the broader manuscript world:

- Owen Davies surveys the social history and transmission of grimoires.
- Jan Veenstra has studied the _Almadel_ tradition.
- Julien Véronèse has studied medieval ritual magic including the _Ars Notoria_ and related Solomonic works.

Their research supports a layered medieval and early-modern background for the _Lemegeton_, but none has demonstrated a pre-Weyer Vassago.

## 1. Dante and Milton

**Negative finding:** Vassago does not appear by name in Dante's _Divine Comedy_ or Milton's _Paradise Lost_.

Dante predates the earliest plausible Vassago forms by centuries. Milton's poem appeared in 1667, around the broad period of the surviving _Lemegeton_, but uses a different biblical and classical demonology.

## 2. Dean Koontz, _Hideaway_ (1992)

The antagonist Jeremy Nyebern adopts the demonic name **Vassago**. The novel places him in a supernatural struggle involving death, hell, and a psychic link with the protagonist.

This is one of the clearest mainstream literary uses of the name. Contemporary reviews explicitly describe Vassago as the character's self-adopted demonic identity. [Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Hideaway.html?id=WjceLaNtlE8C); [Kirkus review](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/dean-koontz/hideaway/)

## 3. Rod Ferrell and “Vesago”

Rod Ferrell used _Vesago_ as a vampire or demonic alias in the 1990s. Later criminological literature connected the misspelled alias with Vassago.

**Documented modern reception:** The alias and connection were reported.

**Caution:** Claims about Vassago's supposed temperament, direction, element, or mythology in retellings of the case often come from modern demonological summaries rather than the _Goetia_. The crime does not illuminate the spirit's historical origin.

## 4. _Mobile Suit Gundam X_

The NRX-0013 **Gundam Virsago**—_Gundam Versago_ in some romanizations—is generally understood as a distorted use of Vassago's name. It is an antagonist mobile suit rather than a demonological portrayal. [Japanese Gundam reference](https://gundam.wiki.cre.jp/wiki/ガンダムヴァサーゴ)

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Was Vassago in Weyer?

**Finding:** No.

Vassago is absent from the 1577 _Pseudomonarchia_. Confusion arises because the _Goetia_ is broadly Weyer-derived and modern summaries sometimes apply that ancestry to every individual entry.

### 2. Was Vassago in Scot?

**Finding:** No.

Scot translated the Weyer sequence and likewise has no Vassago. The _Goetia_ compiler added or recovered the entry from elsewhere.

### 3. Is Folger's Vsagoo the same spirit?

**Disputed.**

Supporting evidence:

- close name;
- truthful or favorable character;
- hidden things;
- late-sixteenth-century date.

Contrary evidence:

- President rather than Prince;
- twenty rather than twenty-six legions;
- women's love and treasure rather than past/future and lost things;
- no. 70 rather than no. 3.

### 4. Is Folger's Vzago the same spirit?

**Disputed, perhaps somewhat stronger numerically.**

Supporting evidence:

- no. 3;
- close name;
- truthful answers;
- favorable angelic appearance.

Contrary evidence:

- substantially different offices;
- no matching legion total;
- invisibility, transformation, wisdom, and love are absent from the Goetic Vassago entry.

### 5. Were Vsagoo and Vzago accidentally conflated?

**Plausible scholarly reconstruction, unproved.**

The _Goetia's_ Vassago may combine a name/number from one entry with the disposition or hidden-knowledge office of another. No surviving intermediate manuscript demonstrates the actual editorial act.

### 6. Why was Vassago added?

Unknown.

The compiler may have:

- drawn from another _Office of Spirits_ manuscript;
- known the Vassago crystal experiment;
- regularized Vzago;
- expanded the catalogue deliberately to seventy-two;
- combined several of these processes.

No compiler's note explains the decision.

### 7. Is Vassago “good”?

**Documented wording, disputed interpretation.**

“Of a good nature” is in the manuscript. Possible readings include:

- comparatively tractable;
- truthful;
- beneficent in office;
- less hostile than other spirits.

It does not necessarily mean ethically good in a modern religious sense. The same ritual still treats him as a spirit to be constrained.

### 8. Does Vassago look like Agares?

**Later correspondence, not a clear core description.**

“Same nature as Agares” may concern disposition, operation, or textual association. Crowley's later “Like Agares” turns it into a magical-image correspondence. Treating the crocodile-riding old man as Vassago's original documented form overstates the source.

### 9. Is Sitael definitely the Harley angel?

**No.**

- Literal manuscript: Syrael/Sirael.
- Modern ordinal correction: Sitael.
- Supporting clue: Sitael's Psalm is present.
- Final status: strong harmonization, not a transparent manuscript reading.

### 10. Do Sitael and Vassago rule the same five degrees?

**No in the source systems.**

- Sitael: 10°–15° Aries.
- Vassago: day spirit of 20°–30° Aries in _777_.
- Vassago's 20°–25° quinance: later inference.

Conflating them erases the separate historical construction of the angel and demon tables.

### 11. How many legions?

- _Goetia_: 26.
- Harley/Rudd: 26.
- Folger Vsagoo: 20.
- Folger Vzago: none given.
- A later Welsh magical tradition is sometimes reported as giving 31, but that reading requires direct manuscript verification.

The only well-established Vassago total is twenty-six.

### 12. Is the seal ancient?

**No evidence supports that claim.**

The recognizable seal belongs to early-modern manuscript magic. Earlier _Office of Spirits_ witnesses lack the Goetic seal series. Claims that it is Babylonian, antediluvian, or literally designed by Solomon are legendary.

### 13. Does Vassago have an “enn”?

The formula commonly printed as _Keyan vefa jedan tasa Vassago_ belongs to modern demonolatry.

**Modern invention:** It is absent from Weyer, Scot, Sloane, Harley, Mathers–Crowley, Rudd, and the historic Shem tradition.

### 14. Is Vassago a fallen angel?

The Vassago entry does not say so.

Some other Goetic spirits are assigned former angelic orders, but Vassago is not. Calling him a fallen angel is a generalized theological interpretation.

### 15. Is there a de Plancy portrait?

No identifiable portrait or entry exists in the 1863 illustrated _Dictionnaire infernal_. Images claiming otherwise are misattributed or modern.

### 16. Did Edward IV's court conjure Vassago?

The claim appears in much later crystallomantic manuscript titles. It is a legend of provenance, not independently established Tudor-era evidence.

### 17. Did “Prince” always mean Jupiter?

The planet and tin derive from the _Goetia's_ rank table. They are ritual correspondences, not inherent meanings of the word “prince.” Other occult systems sometimes give princely spirits different planets.

### 18. Can Vassago be conjured at night?

The core rank rule says Princes may be called at any hour of the day. Later crystal practitioners extend this to night. The difference represents practice changing over time, not a single unambiguous tradition.

## Open questions

1. **What exact source supplied Vassago to the _Lemegeton_ compiler?**  
   Folger V.b.26 and Sloane 3824 give strong leads, but no surviving text shows the precise transition.

2. **Are Vsagoo and Vzago one spirit, two spirits, or independent corruptions?**  
   Names, numbers, ranks, and offices point in different directions.

3. **How old is the “Experiment of Vassago”?**  
   Its surviving witnesses are early modern or later. Attributions to Edward IV cannot presently be confirmed.

4. **Does the name have a lost vernacular or scribal origin?**  
   Its unstable V/U forms and doubled final vowels could conceal a source word, but current Hebrew and Latin proposals are speculative.

5. **Was Syrael a copying error for Sitael?**  
   The ordinal and Psalm strongly suggest this, but an unknown angelic source or deliberate variant remains possible.

6. **Who first explicitly divided Vassago's _777_ decan into a 20°–25° quinance?**  
   The division is common in later tables but is not explicit in Crowley's printed column.

7. **What does “same nature as Agares” originally mean?**  
   Possibilities include temperament, elemental nature, office, mode of appearance, or a memory of paired crystal experiments.

8. **How much of Harley 6483's organization goes back to Thomas Rudd?**  
   The surviving witness is Peter Smart's later copy, and the stages of compilation remain debatable.

9. **Can any exact Vassago form be found in an unedited _Liber Officiorum Spirituum_ witness?**  
   Folger suggests that further comparison of unpublished manuscripts may clarify the name's development.

10. **Are there additional early sigil variants?**  
    Peterson's finding that the sigils are absent from earlier _Office_ manuscripts makes the first stage of their creation especially important and still obscure.

---

## Conclusions

The historically recoverable Vassago is not an ancient god demoted into a demon, at least on present evidence. He is an early-modern grimoire figure whose identity crystallized through several stages:

1. possible late-sixteenth-century cognates, Vsagoo and Vzago;
2. seventeenth-century crystal operations involving Vassago or Usago;
3. canonicalization as spirit 3 in the _Ars Goetia_;
4. description as a good-natured Prince governing twenty-six legions and revealing past, future, hidden, and lost things;
5. addition of a seal and rank-based Jupiter/tin correspondence;
6. pairing in Harley 6483 with an angel written Syrael/Sirael, later harmonized to Sitael;
7. nineteenth-century French expansion of Sitael's Psalm, zodiacal sector, and moral attributes;
8. Golden Dawn and Crowleyan assignment of Vassago to the daytime third decan of Aries and Four of Wands;
9. twentieth-century specialization as a crystal oracle and finder of lost objects;
10. modern reinvention in novels, television, games, music, visionary art, and demonolatry.

The most secure core remains very small: **Vassago is the third Goetic spirit, a Prince of good nature, knowing past and future, discovering hidden and lost things, and ruling twenty-six legions.** Nearly everything visual, mythological, angelological, and astrological beyond that core is a later layer whose origin can—and should—be identified separately.

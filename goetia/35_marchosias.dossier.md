# Marchosias: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Documented text]** — stated in a primary source or surviving manuscript.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — proposed from textual comparison, manuscript dating, or historical context.
- **[Tradition]** — transmitted religious, magical, or occult teaching not independently verifiable as history.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an operative attribution within a particular occult system.
- **[Disputed]** — conflicting witnesses or interpretations exist.
- **[Legend]** — a narrative presented as supernatural history by its source.
- **[Modern invention]** — an attribution first found in recent occultism, fiction, games, or fandom.
- **[Negative finding]** — specifically searched for but not found in the relevant sources.

Because online transcriptions and editions carry source-specific quotation limits, the three long catalog entries cannot be reproduced wholesale here. Short exact passages are followed by exhaustive clause-by-clause diplomatic summaries and links to the complete primary texts.

# The name and its origins

## 1. Remote background: what is not Marchosias

### The Testament of Solomon

**[Documented text; negative finding]** The late-antique or Byzantine *Testament of Solomon* supplies the broad literary archetype of Solomon interrogating, binding, and assigning work to demons. Its spirits include figures such as Ornias, Beelzeboul, Asmodeus and the thirty-six decan spirits, but no Marchosias, Marchocias, Margotias, or convincing equivalent.

It is therefore a conceptual ancestor of Solomonic demon catalogues, not a demonstrated textual source for this name.

### Michael Scot and medieval spirit catalogues

**[Documented text; negative finding]** Michael Scot’s thirteenth-century *Liber introductorius* contains a shorter catalogue of named aerial spirits. Peterson finds approximately twenty names that anticipate later “offices of spirits,” but no recognizable Marchosias name-family.

**[Documented text; negative finding]** The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook, Clm 849, likewise contains spirit names and operations but no secure Marchosias cognate.

**[Negative finding]** The extant demon lists associated with the *Book of Abramelin* do not yield Marchosias or an unambiguous Margoas/Margotias form. Treating Abramelin as the source of Marchosias is consequently unsupported.

## 2. The Florentine witness, January 1494

**[Documented text]** The earliest presently identifiable member of the name-family occurs not in Weyer but in Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 89 sup. 38, in the *Fasciculus rerum geomanticarum*, fol. 263r. The relevant section is dated January 1494.

The manuscript gives approximately:

- **Margoas**
- **Margodas**
- **Margutas**

A modern Italian transcription also reads **Mardoas**, but that fourth form is less securely supported by the published scholarly comparison and should remain bracketed.

The Florentine spirit is a great marquis, manifests as a very strong wolf with a serpent’s tail, assumes human form, is an excellent fighter or military leader, answers truthfully, remains faithful, formerly belonged to the Dominations, hopes to return after 1,200 years, and governs twenty legions.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The combination is too specific to be coincidence: wolf, serpent-tail, human fighter, truthfulness, Dominations, 1,200-year return, and name resemblance. It is the earliest reconstructable Marchosias profile.

**[Disputed reading]** Its clause about the mouth or tail is corrupt or obscure. Later stages render it first as an unidentified emission, and finally as fire.

## 3. The *Livre des esperitz*

**[Documented text]** Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, fols. 179r–182v, lists:

> “de Margotias, marquis”

The manuscript has often been called mid-fifteenth century in popular cataloguing, while Jean-Patrice Boudet’s edition dates this copy to the middle of the sixteenth century. The textual tradition may be older than the particular surviving witness.

**[Documented text]** Margotias stands in a French list containing numerous unmistakable ancestors of Weyer’s spirits: Amon, Asmoday, Furfur, Fenix, Distolas/Stolas, and others.

**[Important limitation]** In the surviving *Livre des esperitz*, Margotias is named and ranked, but does not receive the full Marchosias description found in the Florentine text and Weyer.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Boudet showed that much of Weyer’s catalogue overlaps the French *Livre*, but neither is a simple complete copy of the other. They appear to descend from a wider, unstable “offices of spirits” tradition.

## 4. Weyer: *Marchocias*, 1577

**[Documented text]** Johann Weyer published the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. He says his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also styled *Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis*.

The entry is headed **Marchocias**. Its usual modern enumeration is §31.

The exact opening is:

> “Marchocias magnus Marchio est. Se ostentat specie lupæ ferocissimæ cum alis gryphi, cauda serpentina…”

That is: Marchocias is a great marquis and appears as a very ferocious she-wolf with gryphon wings and a serpent’s tail.

Weyer then says, clause by clause:

- Something indeterminate is vomited from the mouth.
- In human form Marchocias is an excellent fighter.
- It answers questions truthfully.
- It is faithful in all commands of the exorcist.
- It formerly belonged to the angelic order of Dominations.
- Thirty legions are subject to it.
- It hopes to return to the Seventh Throne after 1,200 years.
- That hope is false.

The complete Latin is available in Peterson’s [Weyer transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Weyer’s purpose

**[Documented historical context]** Weyer was not publishing an uncomplicated devotional manual. *De praestigiis* opposed witch persecutions and argued that many supposed witches were deceived, ill, coerced, or victims of demonic illusion. He reportedly omitted parts of the conjuring source to prevent practical use.

That skeptical frame disappeared when the catalogue was absorbed into later grimoires.

## 5. Reginald Scot: *Marchosias*, 1584

**[Documented text]** Reginald Scot reproduced an English version of Weyer’s catalogue in Book XV, chapter 2 of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*.

Its exact opening is:

> “Marchosias is a great marquesse, he sheweth himselfe in the shape of a cruell shee woolfe…”

Scot continues:

- gryphon wings;
- serpent tail;
- “spetting I cannot tell what” from the mouth;
- an excellent fighter in human form;
- truthful answers;
- faithfulness in the conjurer’s business or commands;
- former membership in the Dominations;
- thirty legions;
- hope of returning to the Seventh Throne after 1,200 years;
- deception in that hope.

Scot’s printed heading uses **Marchosias**, while comparison with Weyer produces the editorial variant **Marchocias**.

The complete English text is at Peterson’s [Weyer–Scot parallel edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); a first-edition facsimile is in Cornell’s [Digital Witchcraft Collection](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081).

**[Documented historical context]** Scot, like Weyer, was not endorsing the demon catalogue at face value. His book attacked witch-hunting credulity and exposed conjuring claims, impostures and stage tricks.

## 6. The Lemegeton manuscripts

### Sloane MS 3825

**[Documented text]** British Library Sloane MS 3825, seventeenth century, gives **Marchosias** as spirit 35, fol. 105v. Peterson treats it as the principal manuscript for his edition.

Its exact opening is:

> “The 35 spirit is called Marchosias, he is a great and mighty Marquiz appering at first in the forme of a wolfe…”

The entry then states:

- wolf rather than explicitly she-wolf;
- gryphon wings and serpent tail;
- vomiting fire;
- human form commanded by the exorcist;
- a strong fighter;
- true answers to all questions;
- exceptional faithfulness in performing the exorcist’s business;
- former membership in the Dominations;
- thirty legions;
- statement to its “chief Master,” Solomon;
- hope of returning to the Seventh Throne after 1,200 years;
- a seal to be made and worn as a lamen.

Unlike Weyer and Scot, it does **not** conclude that the hope is false.

The complete diplomatic text appears in Peterson’s [online Goetia](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm) and in *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (2001), p. 23 in some digitizations and approximately p. 62 in the Weiser pagination/OCR stream, depending on front-matter counting.

### Sloane MS 2731

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731 also has **Marchosias**, number 35. Its date was once catalogued too early; Peterson argues that internal evidence points to approximately 1687 and that it contains modernization and copying errors.

### Sloane MS 3648

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3648, approximately 1665 or later, likewise gives **Marchosias**, number 35.

### Harley MS 6483

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483, copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713 and internally attributed to material from Dr Thomas Rudd, gives **Marchosias**, number 35. It is not an autograph manuscript by Rudd, who died in 1656.

## 7. Mathers and Crowley, 1904

**[Documented text]** *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, translated by S. L. MacGregor Mathers and edited by Aleister Crowley, was published in 1904. The Marchosias entry is on printed p. 22.

It begins:

> “The Thirty-fifth Spirit is Marchosias. He is a Great and Mighty Marquis, appearing at first in the form of a Wolf…”

It retains the wolf, wings, serpent-tail, fire, human form, strong fighting ability, Dominations, thirty legions and 1,200-year hope.

**[Textual omission]** The 1904 printed paragraph omits two clauses preserved in Sloane 3825: that Marchosias answers all questions truly and is faithful in doing the exorcist’s business.

### The “ox” variant

A footnote says that in one badly written seventeenth-century codex, “Wolf” might be read as “Ox.” Crowley adds that Marchosias appeared to him like a dazed ox.

**[Disputed manuscript reading]** “Ox” is not the consensus reading.

**[Documented personal report]** Crowley’s claimed ox-like manifestation is his editorial anecdote, not part of Weyer, Scot, or the main manuscript tradition.

The scanned edition is available as the [Mathers–Crowley Goetia PDF](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf).

## 8. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* restored a manuscript-based reading, primarily from Sloane 3825, while recording significant variants from Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483 and other witnesses.

**[Scholarly editorial finding]** Peterson demonstrates that the Goetia’s prose depends much more directly on Scot’s English Weyer material than on the Latin independently. It repeats English translation choices and errors.

He also cautions that many elaborate spellings and explanatory footnotes introduced by Mathers and Crowley lack manuscript support.

# Etymology

## No demonstrable ancient god, hero, or place

**[Negative finding]** No securely attested ancient deity, hero, city, ethnonym or classical demon lies behind Marchosias in the way that Astaroth relates to Astarte, Amon to Amun, Asmoday to Asmodeus/Ashmedai, Phoenix to the Greek *phoinix*, or Belial to Hebrew *bəliyyaʿal*.

Searches for a Greek, Hebrew, Mesopotamian, Roman or Egyptian Marchosias ancestor produced no philologically defensible result.

## The *marchio* hypothesis

The common derivation is from Late or Medieval Latin **marchio**, “marquis, margrave, lord of a frontier march,” ultimately from a Germanic word related to a boundary or *mark*.

**[Plausible but disputed reconstruction]** The resemblance is real, and every developed version calls the spirit a *marchio* or marquis. Nevertheless:

- the earlier forms **Margoas**, **Margodas**, **Margutas** and **Margotias** do not look like a straightforward inflection of *marchio*;
- the name and title may have contaminated one another in transmission;
- a copyist may have reshaped an opaque name to resemble its rank;
- alternatively, a title may indeed have become a proper name.

“Marchosias means the Marquis” is therefore a reasonable mnemonic etymology, not a proven origin.

## Rejected or unsupported derivations

- **[Modern invention]** Derivation from the Roman god Mars because Marchosias fights.
- **[Modern invention]** Identification with Lycaon, the wolf-transformed king of Arcadia.
- **[Modern invention]** Identification with the Capitoline Wolf.
- **[Modern invention]** Connection to the constellation Lupus.
- **[Unsupported]** A Hebrew derivation offered without a proposed Hebrew spelling and historical pathway.
- **[Unsupported]** Claims that the suffix is Greek and signifies divine or martial status.

These are symbolic associations created after the description was established.

## What Marchosias does—and does not do

**[Documented text]** Across the principal lineage Marchosias:

- fights extremely well;
- gives true answers;
- reliably performs the operator’s commands.

**[Negative finding]** The historical entries do not say that Marchosias:

- teaches languages;
- teaches all sciences;
- finds treasure;
- causes love;
- grants familiars;
- confers invisibility;
- transmutes metals;
- reveals herbs or gemstones;
- transports people instantaneously.

Those offices belong to other Goetic spirits. Lists assigning them to Marchosias are modern synthetic demonographies.

## Gender

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s Latin *lupa* is specifically a female wolf. Scot translates it as “shee woolfe.”

**[Documented textual change]** The Goetia reduces this to gender-neutral “wolf,” while using masculine English pronouns for the spirit and human form.

**[Disputed interpretation]** This establishes the sex of the animal manifestation, not necessarily a doctrinal statement about the spirit’s permanent gender.

**[Modern tradition/UPG]** Contemporary practitioners variously call Marchosias male, female, feminine, androgynous or shape-shifting. Those reports belong to modern personal revelation rather than the premodern textual chain.

## The Seventh Throne

**[Legend]** Weyer says Marchocias was of the Dominations and hopes, after 1,200 years, to return to the Seventh Throne, but is deceived.

No theological explanation is supplied. “Seventh Throne” might mean:

- a celestial throne or heaven;
- a lost position within the angelic hierarchy;
- the seventh planetary sphere;
- simply a copied formula whose original cosmology had been forgotten.

**[Textual parallel]** Phoenix/Phenex is also assigned a 1,200-year hope of returning to heaven. This suggests a reusable catalogue motif rather than an independent biography.

**[Disputed]** Later occult writers turn the statement into a story of repentance or impending redemption. The sources report only a hope; they do not say Marchosias repents, will be forgiven, or becomes an angel again.

## Legions

Thirty is stable from Weyer onward. The Florentine ancestor has twenty, possibly reflecting an earlier recension or correction.

Scot elsewhere defines a demonological legion as 6,666 spirits. If mechanically applied, thirty legions would mean 199,980 spirits.

**[Caution]** This is grimoire arithmetic, not a reliable measurement and not the size of a historical Roman legion.

# The seal

## Form

**[Documented image]** Sloane 3825 places a seal beneath the entry. In broad verbal terms it consists of:

- a long horizontal upper stroke;
- curled or hooked terminations;
- a central vertical axis;
- a small cross-like or branched feature;
- dependent loops, circles or hook-like strokes below.

The exact geometry cannot be recovered from words alone. The seal is a drawing, not a diagram accompanied by a canonical verbal construction rule.

The standardized 1904 version can be viewed in the [public-domain Marchosias seal rendering](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:35-marchosias-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg).

## Manuscript variation

**[Documented general finding]** Sloane 3825, 3648, 2731, Harley 6483 and later printed copies preserve the same recognizable graphic family, but vary in:

- number and closure of subordinate loops;
- whether strokes touch or merely approach;
- curl direction and depth;
- length of the horizontal bar;
- symmetry;
- terminal hooks;
- proportions of the central stem.

**[Open textual problem]** No accessible critical publication supplies a stroke-by-stroke facsimile plate collating all five Marchosias seals. Mathers himself complained that Harley 6483 contained errors “particularly in the Sigils,” but did not provide a complete diplomatic comparison.

**[Documented chronology]** The individualized Goetic seal is absent from Weyer, Scot, the *Livre des esperitz*, and the Florentine descriptive catalogue. It belongs to the later Lemegeton manuscript layer.

## Material

The Goetia’s general “Observations” assign seals by rank:

- kings: gold/Sun;
- marquises: silver/Moon;
- dukes: copper/Venus;
- princes or “prelates”: tin/Jupiter;
- knights: lead/Saturn;
- presidents: mercury/Mercury;
- earls: copper and silver.

Thus Marchosias’s seal is to be made in **silver**, under the **Moon**.

“Made and worn as a lamen” means carried as a ritual breast-pendant or plate, not merely copied decoratively.

# The angel: Chavakiah

## The seventy-twofold name

### Exodus 14:19–21

**[Jewish textual tradition]** The standard construction takes the three Hebrew verses Exodus 14:19, 14:20 and 14:21, each containing seventy-two letters:

1. verse 19 is written forward;
2. verse 20 is written backward;
3. verse 21 is written forward;
4. vertical columns yield seventy-two three-letter names.

This produces a boustrophedon arrangement. Rashi discusses this construction; it also appears in medieval kabbalistic material associated with *Sefer Raziel*.

**[Important distinction]** In earlier Jewish usage, *Shem ha-meforash* can mean the “explicit” or specially articulated divine name, particularly the Tetragrammaton. It does not always mean this particular list of seventy-two angels.

Ibn Ezra preserves a different interpretation in which the “name of seventy-two” arises from numerical expansions of YHWH. The Exodus triplets are therefore an important kabbalistic construction, not the only historical meaning of the expression.

## Chavakiah’s letters and names

The thirty-fifth triplet in the conventional order is:

**כוק** — kaf, vav, qof.

With the theophoric ending *-Yah*:

**כוקיה**

Transliterations include:

- Chavakiah
- Chavakhiah
- Chavaquiah
- Khavaqiah
- Kavakiah
- Chauakiah
- Chevakiah

**[Philological caution]** The vowels are supplied by later tradition. Unpointed Hebrew does not determine the modern occult pronunciation. Vav may be rendered *v*, *w* or a vowel, kaf may be *k/kh*, and qof *q/k*.

Forms replacing the triplet with **חבק** or other letters occur online but do not represent the standard Exodus-derived number 35.

## Meaning

Lenain gives:

> “Son attribut: Dieu qui donne la joie.”

“God who gives joy.”

**[Esoteric correspondence]** This is an attributed divine quality. כוק is not an ordinary Hebrew word literally meaning “joy,” nor is *Chavakiah* a normal lexical Hebrew sentence.

## Psalm verse

Lenain prescribes the Latin:

> “Dilexi quoniam exaudiet Dominus vocem orationis meæ.”

This is:

- **Psalm 114:1** in Lenain’s Vulgate/Septuagint numbering;
- **Psalm 116:1** in Hebrew, Protestant and most modern English numbering.

A direct English rendering is: “I love [the Lord], because he hears my voice and my supplications.”

The numbering difference explains why modern angel tables disagree while citing the same words.

## Degrees, days and time

Lenain gives Chavakiah:

- ray **171st through 175th degree inclusive**;
- eighteenth decan;
- decan genius **Aphoso**;
- influence of **Mercury**;
- five recurring calendar dates: **23 April, 4 July, 14 September, 25 November, 5 February**;
- operative time: **11:20–11:40 a.m.**

Beginning the 360-degree zodiac at 0° Aries makes 171°–175° equivalent to approximately **20°–25° Virgo**.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Virgo’s third decan, 20°–30°, is Mercury in Virgo and corresponds in Golden Dawn tarot to the Ten of Pentacles/Disks. Chavakiah occupies its first five-degree half; Menadel occupies the second.

**[Modern simplification]** Modern “guardian angel birthday” tables often replace Lenain’s five distributed days with five consecutive dates in September. Published versions variously give September 9–13, 13–17, 6–10 or nearby ranges. Those are later calendrical conversions, not Lenain’s actual five-date table.

## Offices in Lenain

**[Documented esoteric text]** Chavakiah is invoked:

- to regain the favor of someone one has offended;
- for reconciliation;
- concerning wills and testaments;
- concerning inheritances and successions;
- for amicable division of property;
- to maintain peace and harmony in families.

The “contrary genius” causes family discord and unjust, ruinous lawsuits.

These are Chavakiah’s offices, not Marchosias’s.

## From *Sefer Raziel* to Kircher

### *Sefer Raziel*

**[Documented tradition]** Medieval *Sefer Raziel* material transmits the seventy-twofold divine name and related angelic and talismanic procedures.

**[Critical limitation]** This does not prove that the complete modern package—Chavakiah’s spelling, “God who gives joy,” Psalm 116:1, 20°–25° Virgo and family-inheritance offices—was already present in ancient Judaism.

### Christian Kabbalah

Through writers such as Johannes Reuchlin, Guillaume Postel, Blaise de Vigenère and Athanasius Kircher, Christian Kabbalah reorganized the triplets as angel names by adding endings such as **-el** and **-iah**.

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) presents the seventy-two names as divine powers associated with seventy-two nations and languages. Kircher’s synthesis strongly influenced later ceremonial magic.

### Rudd

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** Harley 6483 incorporates the seventy-two angels as controlling powers set against the seventy-two Goetic spirits by ordinal number. Consequently:

- Marchosias = demon 35;
- Chavakiah = angel 35.

The angelic name is used as a divine constraint, not as Marchosias’s ally or “higher self.”

**[Tradition]** In the Rudd operation, the operator surrounds or backs the demonic seal with divine and angelic authority. Skinner and Rankine describe the corresponding angel as engraved on ritual equipment including the breastplate/lamen and brass vessel.

**[Attribution problem]** Harley 6483 was copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713 from papers attributed to “Dr Rudd.” The connection with Thomas Rudd is plausible but not an autograph fact.

### Skinner and Rankine, 2007

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine’s *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* reconstructs this angel-constrains-demon system from Harley 6483 and related manuscripts. Their edition made the Chavakiah–Marchosias pairing widely known in contemporary ceremonial magic.

### Lenain, Papus, Ambelain and Haziel

- **1823 — Lenain:** systematizes names, meanings, psalms, degrees, dates, times, good offices and contrary influences.
- **1903 — Papus:** reproduces and promotes Lenain’s angel material in *La Cabbale*.
- **1909 — Papus-associated reprint:** helps revive Lenain’s rare book in French occult circles.
- **1951 — Robert Ambelain:** adapts the system in *La Kabbale pratique*.
- **Late twentieth century — Haziel:** popularizes the angels as personal guardians, psychological types and sources of daily devotional aid.

**[Historical distinction]** The Rudd pairing and the Lenain personal-angel system are separate developments. Modern books often combine them.

## Resolving the Virgo/Pisces conflict

Crowley’s *777 Revised*, Tables CLV–CLXVI, distributes the Goetic spirits over the thirty-six decans in three columns:

- ascendant;
- succedent;
- cadent;

first by day and then by night.

Marchosias appears among the **day, succedent** spirits. It occupies the same decan position as Dantalion in the night table. This yields Pisces 10°–20°, the Nine of Cups.

By contrast, assigning all seventy-two Shem angels successively around the zodiac in five-degree arcs places Chavakiah at Virgo 20°–25°.

**[Conclusion]** The controlling angel shares Marchosias’s ordinal number but not Crowley’s Goetic decan.

## Astrological timing of conjuration

The older Goetia gives operative timing by rank and lunar age, not by natal birthday:

- Moon 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 14 days old;
- marquis hours 3 p.m.–9 p.m. and 9 p.m.–sunrise;
- silver lamen under the Moon.

The Pisces decan attribution comes from the Golden Dawn/Crowley correspondence tables and need not govern a seventeenth-century operation.

The Chavakiah 11:20–11:40 time is Lenain’s angelic invocation period, not Marchosias’s Goetic hour.

## Enochian overlays

**[Negative historical finding]** No Enochian material appears in the Weyer or Scot description, and Marchosias is not an entity from John Dee’s surviving angel diaries.

Harley 6483’s catalogue notice remarks that some spirits are described in “Enoch’s Tables,” but this belongs to the Rudd/Smart compilation and does not make Marchosias a Dee Enochian name.

**[Modern overlay]** Twentieth-century ceremonial magicians—including Crowley-influenced and later lodge systems—have combined:

- Goetic demons;
- Shem angels;
- Enochian tablets;
- tarot decans;
- zodiacal quinances;
- planetary hours.

These are workable synthetic systems for believers, but they should not be projected backward into Weyer’s source.

# Number and order: Weyer’s 69 versus the Goetia’s 72

Weyer’s catalogue contains sixty-nine entries. The Goetia contains seventy-two.

The arithmetic is:

- start with Weyer’s 69;
- add **Vassago**;
- add **Seere**;
- add **Dantalion**;
- add **Andromalius**;
- omit **Pruflas/Bufas**;
- result: 72.

Marchocias is approximately 31st in Weyer’s sequence but becomes Marchosias 35 in the Goetia because the compiler both added material and extensively rearranged the catalogue.

**[Textual anomaly]** Peterson’s comparison PDF labels the Weyer row “30. Marchocias,” whereas the continuous Weyer transcription and standard sequence place it at §31: Furfur is 30, Marchocias 31. This appears to be a modern table-numbering inconsistency, not a separate demon.

**[Scholarly hypothesis]** Peterson has suggested that portions of the catalogue may have been rearranged through the handling of loose leaves or copied gatherings. No surviving draft proves exactly how the new order arose.

# Iconography

## Manuscript iconography

The earliest witnesses provide verbal imagery, not portraits:

- wolf or she-wolf;
- gryphon wings;
- serpent tail;
- an unidentified mouth emission, later fire;
- human warrior form.

The Lemegeton adds the abstract seal but no drawn portrait of the beast.

## Collin de Plancy

### 1818

Collin de Plancy first published the *Dictionnaire infernal* in two volumes in 1818. Later editions were heavily revised as his religious and editorial position changed.

**[Documented reception]** The work helped remove the Goetic figures from a specialist conjuring-book context and place them in an alphabetical encyclopedia of demons, folklore and superstition.

### 1863 entry

The sixth edition gives **Marchocias**, pp. 444–445. Its opening is:

> “Marchocias, grand marquis des enfers. Il se montre sous la figure d’une louve féroce, avec des ailes de griffon…”

It then describes the serpent tail, flame-vomiting, human form as a great soldier, obedience to exorcists, membership in the Dominations, and thirty legions.

**[Textual correction]** The Wikisource transcription currently reads *vomit des femmes*—“vomits women.” Context and the printed scan support *flammes*, “flames.” “Vomits women” is an OCR/transcription accident, not a variant Marchosias legend.

De Plancy omits:

- true answers;
- detailed faithfulness;
- the 1,200-year hope;
- the Seventh Throne.

The entry is available at [Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Marchocias).

## Louis Le Breton’s portrait

**[Documented image]** The 1863 edition includes Louis Le Breton’s engraving. It depicts:

- a winged wolf-like quadruped in profile;
- the head turned toward the viewer;
- an open mouth emitting flame;
- large feathered wings;
- a long, thin serpent tail curling upward;
- one forepaw raised;
- no human form and no visible martial equipment.

This engraving, rather than a Goetia manuscript, established the modern visual canon. It can be viewed at [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ill_dict_infernal_p0461_marchocias.jpg).

## Modern art

Modern images fall into several families:

1. faithful redrawings of Le Breton’s winged wolf;
2. heraldic wolf–gryphon hybrids;
3. armored male warriors;
4. female or anthropomorphic wolf figures;
5. anime-style demon women;
6. occult sigil art combining the seal with lunar, Virgo or Pisces symbols.

The last four are modern interpretations. No early source describes armor, clothing, hair color, human sex, jewelry, weapons, gemstones or a personal color palette.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer is the first printer of **Marchocias**, but not the inventor of the underlying figure. His importance is preservation: without his 1577 appendix, the spirit might have remained in obscure manuscript catalogues.

## Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot transmitted the catalogue into English in 1584. The precise responsibility of Abraham Fleming for portions of the translation remains an editorial question, but the English wording became decisive: the Goetia often follows Scot more closely than Weyer’s Latin.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

The “Dr Rudd” material overlays the Goetic spirits with controlling Shem angels. Peter Smart’s 1712–1713 manuscript is the surviving witness. Rudd’s authorship remains attribution rather than autograph certainty.

## Mathers and Crowley

Mathers prepared the English occult edition; Crowley published and annotated it in 1904. They stabilized the spelling **Marchosias**, standardized the seal, and gave the text a prominent place in modern ceremonial magic.

Crowley’s claimed ox-like manifestation should be understood as personal testimony printed in a footnote.

## Aleister Crowley’s *777*

*777* and especially its revised tables make Marchosias a day spirit of the second decan of Pisces, with the Nine of Cups. This correspondence is separate from the 1904 narrative entry.

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No reliable source consulted documents a specific Marchosias operation, vision or doctrine by Charles Stansfeld Jones/Frater Achad. His relevance is to the wider Thelemic and Qabalistic environment, not to Marchosias specifically.

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy condensed the textual profile; Le Breton supplied its most influential portrait.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition and subsequent online work are central for:

- manuscript readings;
- folio references;
- spelling comparison;
- identification of the Weyer–Scot source relationship;
- dating of the Sloane manuscripts;
- separation of manuscript text from Mathers–Crowley alterations.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* restored the controlling-angel apparatus and popularized the Chavakiah–Marchosias pairing.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica* and related work reinterpret Goetia within a broader necromantic, classical and grimoire context.

**[Caution]** This is a historical/esoteric reinterpretation of the system; no specific personal Marchosias working by Stratton-Kent was established in the consulted evidence.

## Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* and Ordo Templi Astartes teaching helped popularize mirror-based Goetic evocation and an initiatory reconstruction of the Lemegeton.

**[Negative finding]** A distinctive published Marchosias encounter was not located. He is relevant as a modern Goetia practitioner, not as a source for the historical entry.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette has written and taught extensively on Crowley, ceremonial magic, tarot and Goetia, including *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* with Christopher Hyatt.

**[Negative finding]** The sources consulted did not establish a specific historically consequential Marchosias working by DuQuette.

## Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Jean-Patrice Boudet and Julien Véronèse

- Owen Davies supplies the broad history of grimoires and their circulation.
- Jan Veenstra’s scholarship helps contextualize medieval learned magic and spirit traditions.
- Boudet’s 2003 comparison is essential for the *Livre des esperitz* and its relationship to Renaissance catalogues.
- Véronèse’s work situates ritual magic, necromancy and textual transmission in medieval learned culture.

Boudet is the most directly relevant to the pre-Weyer name-family.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Was Weyer the first source?

No.

**[Corrected finding]** Weyer is the first known printer, but the Florentine manuscript of January 1494 already contains the full name-family and most of the profile. The *Livre des esperitz* also preserves **Margotias**.

The safest history is:

**Margoas/Margodas/Margutas → Margotias → Marchocias → Marchosias.**

The exact branching order cannot be proven.

## 2. Weyer number 30 or 31?

The normal continuous sequence makes Marchocias number 31. A Peterson variant table prints 30.

**[Most probable resolution]** The table is off by one at that point. It does not imply a second recension in which Marchocias meaningfully changed position by itself.

## 3. Wolf or ox?

- Wolf/she-wolf: all principal early witnesses.
- Ox: a doubtful reading in one poorly written codex.
- Dazed ox: Crowley’s reported personal perception.

The ox is not equal in textual weight to the wolf.

## 4. Fire or something unknown?

The sequence is revealing:

1. Florentine ancestor: obscure/corrupt mouth-tail clause.
2. Weyer: “I know not what” is vomited.
3. Scot: “spetting I cannot tell what.”
4. Goetia: vomiting fire.
5. De Plancy/Le Breton: flames.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** “Fire” is a clarifying editorial development, not an exact preservation of Weyer. Whether the compiler guessed, consulted another source, or borrowed a conventional demonic feature is unknown.

## 5. Male or female?

The animal form is grammatically and explicitly female in Weyer and Scot. Later English texts use masculine pronouns and suppress “she.”

Both “Marchosias is definitively a male demon” and “Marchosias is definitively a goddess/demoness” go beyond the early sources.

## 6. Is Marchosias a repentant demon?

The text gives hope for restoration, not repentance. Weyer expressly says the hope is mistaken.

Modern “redemption arc” readings are interpretations.

## 7. Is Marchosias of the Seventh Throne?

The source says Marchosias hopes to return *to* the Seventh Throne. It does not unambiguously say “Marchosias was formerly ruler of the Seventh Throne.”

## 8. Does Marchosias grant invisibility and treasure?

Not in the source chain. Those offices have migrated from other spirits and modern correspondence lists.

## 9. Is Chavakiah an ancient Jewish guardian angel paired with Marchosias?

**[Disputed formulation]**

- The Exodus triplet has genuine Jewish kabbalistic history.
- Angelizing the triplets with *-el/-iah* has medieval and Christian-kabbalistic development.
- Pairing angel 35 with demon 35 is Rudd’s early-modern ceremonial scheme.
- Meanings, psalms, personal birth dates and psychological profiles are later developments.

The complete pairing should not be called an unchanged teaching from ancient Israel.

## 10. Is the angel in Virgo while the demon is in Pisces?

Yes, in the respective systems:

- Chavakiah’s sequential quinance: Virgo 20°–25°.
- Marchosias’s Crowley Goetic decan: Pisces 10°–20° by day.

Modern charts that give Marchosias Virgo 20°–25° have transferred the angel’s ordinal quinance to the demon. Modern charts giving Chavakiah Pisces have done the reverse.

## 11. Misprints that became traditions

Important examples include:

- **Marchias** in Wellcome 3203.
- Weyer 30 versus 31 in a modern comparison table.
- **vomit des femmes** for *flammes* in online de Plancy text.
- “Ox” elevated from a doubtful manuscript reading.
- Hebrew character corruption in Mathers-derived fonts.
- “Marchosias teaches invisibility/languages” through copied composite websites.
- conflicting birthday ranges derived from Lenain’s nonconsecutive five-date system.

## 12. Conflation with other spirits

Marchosias is sometimes conflated with:

- **Phenex**, because both have a 1,200-year restoration hope;
- **Glasya-Labolas**, another winged canine and fighter;
- **Amon**, a wolf-serpent hybrid in some tables;
- **Stolas**, because it follows Marchosias as number 36;
- **Dantalion**, its Crowley night-decan partner;
- **Chavakiah**, its Rudd controller;
- Lycaon or the Capitoline Wolf, through modern wolf symbolism.

None of these identities is supported by the early text.

# Open questions

1. **What does the earliest name mean?**  
   The title-based *marchio* explanation does not fully account for Margoas/Margodas/Margutas.

2. **What exactly was emitted from the mouth in the earliest recension?**  
   The Florentine passage is obscure; Weyer admits uncertainty; “fire” appears later.

3. **How are the Florentine catalogue, the French *Livre des esperitz* and Weyer’s lost *Liber officiorum spirituum* stemmatically related?**  
   They overlap but cannot yet be arranged in a secure single-line genealogy.

4. **Why did the legion count change from twenty to thirty?**  
   It may be a correction, scribal numeral confusion, or evidence of different recensions.

5. **What cosmology produced the Seventh Throne and 1,200-year period?**  
   No source explains it. The repetition in the Phenex entry suggests a inherited formula.

6. **Where did the Marchosias seal originate?**  
   No matching seal is known from the pre-Lemegeton Offices, and no construction rule accompanies it.

7. **How accurately does Harley 6483 preserve Rudd?**  
   It is a later Smart copy with demonstrable errors; the hypothetical Rudd exemplar is lost.

8. **Can all surviving seal variants be stemmatically classified?**  
   This would require direct high-resolution collation of Sloane 2731, 3648, 3825, Harley 6483, Northwestern 65 and Wellcome 3203.

9. **Did Crowley actually work with Marchosias?**  
   His ox remark is evidence that he claimed an appearance, but no detailed contemporary magical record was located to establish date, ritual or circumstances.

10. **When did “Marchosias = female demon” become a stable modern identity rather than an observation about the she-wolf form?**  
    It is prominent in recent demonolatry but lacks a clearly identified founding publication.

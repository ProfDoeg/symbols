# Andras: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary method

This dossier traces Andras from the earliest presently identified manuscript witness through Johann Weyer, Reginald Scot, the *Lemegeton/Ars Goetia* manuscripts, Thomas Rudd–attributed material, Mathers and Crowley, modern editions, occult correspondences, illustration, and popular culture.

The labels used throughout are:

- **[Documented text]** — stated or depicted in a cited primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a conclusion proposed from manuscript or historical evidence.
- **[Tradition]** — a received claim transmitted within ritual or kabbalistic literature.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a deliberately constructed occult association, not a historical identity.
- **[Disputed]** — asserted but contested, weakly evidenced, or dependent on uncertain attribution.
- **[Legend]** — supernatural narrative reported as tradition rather than verified history.
- **[Modern invention]** — a feature demonstrably introduced in recent occultism or fiction.
- **[Negative finding]** — not found in the examined source or source family.

Short verbatim excerpts are followed by detailed, clause-by-clause translations or paraphrases. The complete historical texts and page images are linked.

# The name and its origins

## 1. Earliest attestation: Florence, 1494

**[Documented text]** The earliest identified occurrence is not Weyer’s 1577 printing. It appears in Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana MS Plut. 89 sup. 38, a *Fasciculus rerum geomanticarum* dated January 1494. In the spirit catalogue on fol. 263r the twentieth entry reads:

> “Andras vel Vandras”

The entry identifies him as a great marquis commanding thirty legions. Joseph Peterson reports this witness in his revised apparatus to Weyer; Jean-Patrice Boudet’s work on medieval catalogues likewise aligns the Florentine **F20** with Weyer’s **W54**. [Peterson’s Weyer edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm) and [Boudet’s catalogue study](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

This advances the secure documentary history of the name by eighty-three years before the fifth edition of Weyer’s *De praestigiis daemonum*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The Florence list belongs to the broad late-medieval family of catalogues of spirit “offices,” the family from which Weyer later drew. It does not prove that Weyer copied this particular codex. It demonstrates that both the name and the core description were circulating in manuscript before him.

**[Negative finding]** No earlier securely dated occurrence of **Andras** has yet been demonstrated in the *Livre des Esperitz*, the Munich handbook Clm 849, the *Book of Abramelin*, or the *Testament of Solomon*.

Mathers’s Hebrew letters are **not evidence that the name is ancient Hebrew**. They are a modern back-transliteration created for the 1904 edition.

## 3. Etymology

### No demonstrated ancient deity behind the name

**[Negative finding]** Andras differs from names such as Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Ammon/Amun, Asmoday/Ashmedai, Belial/Beliyaʿal, or Phenex/Phoenix. No pre-medieval deity, hero, city, ethnic name, or transparent Greek, Latin, or Hebrew term has been securely identified as its source.

The 1494 reading **Vandras** may be:

- an alternative form already present in the copyist’s exemplar;
- a scribal addition or loss of initial *V*;
- or evidence of a pronunciation no longer recoverable.

There is not enough evidence to choose among those possibilities.

### Greek *andros*

**[Disputed]** Modern internet etymologies frequently connect Andras to Greek **ἀνδρός**, *andros*, the genitive of *anēr*, “man.” The resemblance is real, but no manuscript gloss, classical citation, or documented transmission connects that word to the demon. It also does not explain the exact formation of the name or the variant *Vandras*. This is a phonetic suggestion, not an established etymology.

### Welsh *andras/andros*

**[Documented linguistic parallel; disputed historical connection]** Welsh **andras**, also **andros**, means evil, blight, demon, or devil. Welsh lexicography derives it from *anras*, commonly analyzed as *an-* plus *gras*, “without grace” or “gracelessness.” Nineteenth-century Welsh folklore records *Andras or Andros* as a North Welsh designation for the Evil One. [Geiriadur-derived lexical entry](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/andras) and Elias Owen’s [*Welsh Folk-Lore*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20096/20096-h/20096-h.htm).

This is the closest genuine lexical parallel. Nevertheless:

- the Goetic name is already attested in an Italian manuscript in 1494;
- no route from Welsh vernacular speech to that Florentine catalogue has been established;
- no medieval source equates the spirit with the Welsh word.

It therefore remains suggestive rather than proven.

### Andraste or Andrasta

**[Disputed modern identification]** Andras has sometimes been connected with Andraste/Andrasta, the British goddess invoked by Boudica in Cassius Dio. The goddess is ancient and genuinely attested, but the identification with the Goetic marquis is not.

The problems are substantial:

- the names are not identical;
- there is a gap of more than a millennium;
- no medieval grimoire makes the connection;
- no inscription or textual genealogy bridges Dio’s goddess and the Florentine spirit;
- Andraste’s iconography does not explain the raven-headed angel, wolf, or marquisate.

The proposed connection seems partly encouraged by nineteenth-century Welsh antiquarian attempts to relate Welsh *Andras* to the ancient British goddess. It should be classified as a later conjecture, not recovered pagan continuity.

### Andrew, Andreas, and Hungarian András

**[Disputed]** Greek *Andreas* and Hungarian **András**, forms of Andrew, are phonetic homonyms. No evidence makes the spirit a corruption of the apostle’s or any historical person’s name.

### Andras and Andrealphus

**[Documented distinction]** They are separate spirits, not alternate names:

- Florence places a form of Andrealphus immediately before “Andras vel Vandras.”
- Weyer has Andras 54 and Androalphus 55.
- The *Goetia* has Andras 63 and Andrealphus 65.

Their similar names and bird-headed appearances promote modern confusion, but the early catalogue already distinguishes them.

# The description across the sources

## 1. The Florentine catalogue, 1494

**[Documented text]** The Florence witness already supplies the essential identity:

- Andras or Vandras;
- great marquis;
- thirty legions.

The detailed appearance and office align with Weyer’s later catalogue according to the scholarly concordance, although Peterson’s online apparatus does not print a complete diplomatic transcription of the Florentine paragraph.

The surviving Florence collection also contains spirit sigils, but Peterson cautions that the sigils associated with that manuscript tradition do **not** match the familiar *Goetia* seals. Consequently, the 1494 name does not establish that the later Andras seal existed in the same form.

## 2. Weyer, 1577

Johann Weyer published the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Andras is spirit 54 of 69 and appears on printed page 928 in Peterson’s transcription.

A representative portion of the Latin reads:

> “Andras magnus Marchio: visitur forma angelica, capite nycticoraci nigro simili, in lupo nigro & fortissimo equitans…”

A complete clause-by-clause rendering is:

- Andras is a great marquis.
- He is seen in an angelic form.
- His head resembles a black *nycticorax*.
- He rides a black and extremely strong wolf.
- He carries in his hand a very sharp sword.
- He knows how to kill the master, servant, and assistants.
- He is the author of discords.
- He rules thirty legions.

The Latin noun **nycticorax** literally evokes a “night raven.” Depending on period zoological vocabulary it can also denote a night-heron or an owl-like nocturnal bird. Scot chooses “night raven”; de Plancy later chooses an owl.

**[Documented text]** Weyer gives no seal, Shem angel, zodiacal degree, tarot card, metal, secondary human form, or special conjuration hour in the individual Andras entry. [Weyer’s complete Latin and English apparatus](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Weyer’s stance

Weyer was not presenting the catalogue as a simple devotional endorsement. *De praestigiis* opposed witch persecution and argued that many supposed witches were deluded, ill, or deceived by demons. Nevertheless, he accepted a supernatural framework much more readily than a modern rationalist would. His inclusion of the catalogue served a polemical and documentary purpose.

Weyer says his source was a book of spirit offices associated with Solomon, elsewhere described as *Liber officiorum spirituum* or *Empto. Salomonis*. He also says he deliberately suppressed parts of the magical apparatus. This helps explain why his text contains descriptions and offices but no seals.

## 3. Reginald Scot, 1584

Scot reproduces Weyer’s catalogue in Book XV of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*. The Andras passage appears on page 224 of the 1584 edition:

> “Andras is a great marquesse, and is seene in an angels shape with a head like a blacke night raven…”

The remainder states that he:

- rides a black and very strong wolf;
- brandishes a sharp sword;
- can kill the master, servant, and all assistants;
- is the author of discords;
- rules thirty legions.

This is a close English rendering of Weyer, traditionally associated with Abraham Fleming’s translation work. [Scot, Book XV, complete text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

Scot’s broader purpose was skeptical and anti-persecution. He printed conjurations and demon catalogues largely to expose what he regarded as the fraud, credulity, and clerical absurdity surrounding witchcraft.

### Scot’s general timing for marquises

Later in the same book Scot says:

> “Marquesses may be bound from the ninth houre till compline, and from compline till the end of the daie.”

**[Documented but ambiguous]** This is a class rule, not part of the Andras paragraph. “Ninth hour” and “compline” are canonical-hour language, whose precise civil-clock equivalents vary by season and usage. The later *Goetia* turns the rule into fixed clock periods.

## 4. Sloane 2731 and 3825: the *Ars Goetia*

In the *Ars Goetia*, Andras has become number 63. Peterson’s principal transcription, based especially on Sloane 3825, gives the entry at fol. 109r.

Its decisive warning reads:

> “His office is to sow discords; if ye Exorcist hath not a care he will kill him and his fellows…”

The complete content is:

- he is the sixty-third spirit;
- his name is Andras;
- he is a great marquis;
- he appears in the form of an angel;
- he has a head like a black night raven;
- he rides a black, strong wolf;
- he flourishes a sharp, bright sword in his hand;
- his office is to sow discords;
- unless the exorcist takes care, he will kill the exorcist and companions;
- he governs thirty legions;
- his seal is shown;
- the seal is to be made and worn as a lamen on the breast.

[Peterson’s diplomatic *Goetia* text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

### The consequential textual change

| Weyer/Scot | *Goetia* |
|---|---|
| Andras knows how to kill “the master, servant, and assistants.” | Andras may kill “the Exorcist … and his fellows” if the operator is careless. |
| Statement of destructive ability or office | Direct ritual warning |
| “Author of discords” | “Sow discords” |

**[Scholarly observation]** This is more than modernization of spelling. The *Goetia* compiler converts Weyer’s reported ability into a danger internal to the ritual.

### No demanded second form

**[Negative finding]** Many Goetic entries say that a spirit initially appears as an animal or composite and must be commanded into human form. No such second form is prescribed for Andras in Weyer, Scot, Sloane 3825, or Mathers–Crowley. The angelic-bodied, bird-headed rider is the only form given.

### No positive instructional offices

**[Negative finding]** Andras is not said in these texts to:

- teach languages or sciences;
- reveal treasure;
- grant love;
- provide familiars;
- confer invisibility;
- transmute metals;
- answer questions;
- reconcile friends;
- bestow dignities.

His historically attested office is discord, accompanied by lethal capacity or danger.

## 5. Harley 6483 and the Rudd recension

Harley MS 6483 was copied in 1712–1713 by Peter Smart. Its *Goetia* recension associates each of the 72 demons with one of the 72 angels of the Shem HaMephorash.

**[Documented manuscript practice]** The demon’s sigil and the corresponding angelic name or seal are placed on opposite sides of a lamen. In the reconstruction published by Stephen Skinner and David Rankine:

- the demonic face points outward;
- the angelic face points toward the operator’s heart;
- the angel is invoked as the controlling or constraining power over the demon.

Number-for-number pairing makes spirit 63, Andras, correspond to angel 63, Anauel. This is an imposed control architecture; the angel is not part of Weyer’s description.

### The Thomas Rudd attribution

**[Disputed]** Skinner and Rankine argue that Smart copied material originating with the seventeenth-century mathematician and engineer Thomas Rudd, perhaps from an exemplar of about 1655–1656. The manuscript itself is later.

Dan Harms and Egil Asprem have emphasized evidence that complicates the attribution:

- Smart sometimes assigned texts to Rudd without secure external corroboration.
- Material attributed to “Dr Rudd” can be traced to other printed writers.
- A supposed Rudd nativity appears to be John Heydon’s.
- Names in copied passages were altered in ways suggestive of deliberate Rudd-family attribution.

Thus:

- **the Harley manuscript and its contents are real;**
- **the early-eighteenth-century Smart copy is documented;**
- **the derivation of the whole recension from the historical Thomas Rudd remains contested.**

[British Library Harley 6483 record](https://www.bl.uk/manuscripts/FullDisplay.aspx?ref=Harley_MS_6483), [Harms’s manuscript critique](https://danharms.wordpress.com/2007/11/09/on-the-shelf-the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-part-7/), and [Asprem’s study](https://dare.uva.nl/id/cc6cdcac-4c32-4152-a6d2-ab9a752c5a7d).

## 6. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The 1904 *Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, edited by Aleister Crowley from a translation by S. L. MacGregor Mathers, prints Andras on page 42:

> “The Sixty-third Spirit is Andras. He is a Great Marquis, appearing in the Form of an Angel…”

The entry then preserves the black night-raven head, strong black wolf, raised sharp and bright sword, office of sowing discord, warning that he may slay the exorcist and companions, thirty legions, and the seal.

[Mathers–Crowley, page 42](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

The wording is a normalized literary version of the Sloane manuscript tradition, not an independent ancient witness.

Crowley’s separate “Initiated Interpretation of Ceremonial Magic” proposes that magical spirits may be treated as portions of the human brain. **[Esoteric interpretation]** Under that model, Andras can be psychologized as a personification of destructive conflict, but the 1904 text does not replace the ritual entity with one exclusive psychological definition. [Crowley’s introductory interpretation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu/17).

## 7. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s 2001 *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* restores manuscript readings, identifies variants, and separates manuscript evidence from Mathers’s editorial constructions.

For Andras, Peterson confirms:

- spelling **Andras**;
- number 63;
- Great Marquis;
- thirty legions;
- the same composite appearance;
- discord;
- the lethal warning;
- and the lamen instruction.

The edition was published by Weiser in 2001, ISBN 978-1-57863-220-6. [Publisher’s edition record](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/).

# Order and number: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

Weyer prints 69 spirits. The *Goetia* has 72.

The numerical change is produced by:

- omitting Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**;
- adding **Vassago** near the beginning;
- adding **Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius** at the end.

Thus:

\[
69 - 1 + 4 = 72
\]

Scot also omits Pruflas from his English catalogue, leaving 68 individual descriptions.

Andras moves from Weyer 54 to Goetia 63 because the *Goetia* did not merely append spirits; it reorganized much of the sequence. Peterson compares the reordering to a disturbance or reshuffling of source pages, although its exact cause remains unknown.

Around Andras:

| Source | Immediate sequence |
|---|---|
| Florence 1494 | A form of Andrealphus, then **Andras/Vandras** |
| Weyer | Amduscias 53, **Andras 54**, Androalphus 55 |
| *Goetia* | Valac 62, **Andras 63**, Flauros 64, Andrealphus 65 |

**[Negative finding]** There is no evidence that the move from 54 to 63 reflects a newly discovered ancient hierarchy. It is a consequence of the *Goetia*’s reorganization.

# Rank, planet, metal, time, and seal

## Marquis: Moon and silver

The *Goetia*’s “Observations” assign metals by rank:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Earl/Count | Mixed or disputed | Copper and silver in the text; Mathers suspected iron/Mars |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |

Therefore Andras, as a Marquis, has:

- **planet:** Moon;
- **metal:** silver.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** This does not mean that Weyer regarded Andras as intrinsically lunar. It is a ritual/material assignment created by the *Goetia*’s rank system. [*Goetia* “Observations”](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks07.htm).

## Hours

For marquises, the *Goetia* specifies:

- 3 p.m. to 9 p.m.;
- and 9 p.m. until sunrise.

Its general lunar instructions favor working when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old.

**[Tradition]** These are prescriptive ritual rules internal to the grimoire. They are not historical observations about successful conjurations.

## The seal

**[Documented image]** The standard Andras seal is printed beside the manuscript and Mathers–Crowley entries. Its essential visual structure is:

- a tall central upright or spine;
- a strong upper horizontal or crosspiece;
- multiple short bars and angular branches;
- an asymmetrical hooked or stepped lower construction;
- several small circular terminals;
- a compact, scaffold-like overall appearance rather than a pictorial wolf, raven, or sword.

The source does not name the component strokes. An exact description must therefore defer to the figure itself: [Peterson’s Sloane-derived seal image](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g63.jpg).

The text requires it to be “made and worn as a lamen” on the breast.

### Seal variants

- **Weyer and Scot:** no seal.
- **Florence 1494:** spirit sigils occur in the larger manuscript, but they do not demonstrably match the *Goetia* seals.
- **Sloane 2731/3825:** the familiar Andras sigil, with small differences of pen angle, proportion, terminal circles, and line junctions.
- **Harley 6483:** the demon seal is incorporated into a double-sided protective lamen with Anauel on the reverse.
- **Mathers–Crowley:** a redrawn print version regularizing the manuscript hand.
- **Modern occult art:** usually copies the 1904 or Petersonized line image, often smoothing the geometry and making it more symmetrical than the manuscripts.

**[Open manuscript issue]** No freely accessible diplomatic study currently supplies a stroke-by-stroke overlay of every Andras seal witness. Claims that one online redraw is the uniquely “original” or “correct” seal exceed the evidence.

# The angel: Anauel

## 1. Deriving the 72 names

Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the relevant Hebrew textual tradition, of three consecutive verses of 72 letters each. The later kabbalistic technique writes:

1. verse 19 in its normal direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its normal direction.

Reading the resulting columns produces 72 three-letter groups. This alternating or boustrophedon construction is associated with the 72-fold Name, later called the Shem HaMephorash.

The sixty-third triplet is conventionally represented as:

> ענו — ʿayin, nun, vav

Christian and occult writers form an angel-name by adding a divine suffix, here **-el**, yielding **Anauel** or related spellings.

**[Historical distinction]**

- The three-letter Hebrew group belongs to Jewish traditions of the divine Name.
- Treating every group as an individualized guardian angel with a biography, zodiacal jurisdiction, Psalm, moral quality, and opposing demon is a later Christian-kabbalistic and occult development.

Sources include the *Bahir*, medieval *Sefer Raziel* traditions, and later Christian kabbalists. [Exodus 14:19–21](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Kabbalah), [*Bahir* §§109–110](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-109-110-targum/), and Joshua Trachtenberg’s discussion of [*Sefer Raziel* and the 72-fold Name](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

## 2. Name and meaning

### Spellings

| Writer or tradition | Form |
|---|---|
| Reuchlin | **Anauel** |
| Agrippa tradition | **Annauel** |
| Lenain | **Anauel** |
| Papus | **Anauel** |
| Ambelain | **Anauel** |
| Modern English/French lists | Anauel, Anavel |
| Some derivative tables | Chanavel |

The *u/v* variation reflects early-modern orthography. “Chanavel” appears to be a derivative corruption or alternate parsing, not the dominant reading. [Peterson’s Shem-name comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/csds_spirit_variants.html).

### “God infinitely good”

Lenain gives:

> “Dieu infiniment bon.”

**[Documented esoteric epithet]** This is best rendered “God infinitely good” or “God of infinite goodness.”

It is not a secure word-for-word translation of the consonants ענו. The ending **-el** clearly means “God”; the interpretation of the three-letter root is less certain. Modern translations such as “gentle God,” “humble God,” or “God who answers” draw on possible Hebrew associations but are not demonstrated by Lenain’s text.

## 3. Zodiacal five degrees

Lenain assigns Anauel to rays 311–315 of the zodiac:

- 300° begins Aquarius;
- 311°–315° corresponds to **10°–15° Aquarius**.

This is the first five-degree half of the second Aquarius decan.

Some modern guardian-angel calendars translate that sector into approximately **30 January–4 February**. Lenain instead lists five scattered annual dates:

- 21 May;
- 1 August;
- 12 October;
- 23 December;
- 5 March.

These are two different calendrical conventions. The contiguous January–February dates should not be back-projected into Lenain.

## 4. Psalm and invocation time

Lenain prescribes:

> “Servite Domino in timore: et exultate ei cum tremore.”

This is Psalm 2:11 in the Vulgate numbering: “Serve the Lord with fear, and rejoice before him with trembling.”

He gives Anauel’s daily interval as **8:40 p.m.–9:00 p.m.**

The same verse is repeated by Papus and Ambelain.

**[Disputed modern variant]** Several contemporary angel websites instead assign Psalm 100:2, “Serve the Lord with gladness.” That substitution may result from the shared opening word *Servite*, from a reformed table, or from copying error. It is not the verse in Lenain, Papus, or Ambelain and should not be labeled “Rudd’s Psalm” without direct manuscript evidence.

## 5. Offices of Anauel

Lenain’s 1823 entry attributes the following powers to Anauel:

- converting nations to Christianity and confounding enemies of that religion;
- protection from accidents;
- preservation or recovery of health;
- influence over commerce, banking, business agents, and clerks;
- subtlety, ingenuity, industry, and activity in those born under the angel.

The contrary or adverse genius produces:

- madness or folly;
- prodigality;
- bad conduct.

These are **[documented nineteenth-century occult attributions]**, not claims found in Exodus or medieval Jewish scripture. [Lenain, *La science cabalistique*, p. 89](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf).

## 6. Historical transmission of the angelic system

### Medieval Jewish sources

- **[Tradition]** The 72 triads arise from Exodus 14:19–21.
- **[Documented medieval development]** The *Bahir* associates the divine Name with 72 names or powers.
- **[Documented manuscript diversity]** *Sefer Raziel* traditions transmit the 72-fold Name, though vocalizations and angelic expansions vary. The printed *Sefer Raziel* appeared in Amsterdam in 1701, long after the traditions it compiled.

### Reuchlin, 1517

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* brought the system into Christian kabbalistic discourse and supplied angelic forms based on the triplets. Anauel appears in this transmission. [Reuchlin, *On the Art of the Kabbalah*](https://archive.org/details/onartofkabbalahd0000reuc).

### Agrippa, 1533

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa systematized the 72 names in *De occulta philosophia*, Book III, connecting divine names, angels, and celestial order. [Agrippa, *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*](https://www.alchemy-texts.com/book/three-books-of-occult-philosophy/).

### Kircher, 1652–1654

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* reproduced and elaborated Christian-kabbalistic divine-name tables. His synthesis helped transmit the names into learned European occultism, but it is not evidence that every later angelic office or Psalm was already medieval. [Kircher scan](https://www.esonet.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Oedipus_Aegyptiacus02.pdf).

### Rudd/Smart, early eighteenth century

Harley 6483 joins the 72 Goetic demons to the 72 angelic names as a ritual control system. Sequential pairing gives Andras–Anauel.

### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* supplies the mature package of:

- angel number;
- divine attribute;
- zodiacal five degrees;
- five calendar days;
- twenty-minute interval;
- Psalm;
- offices;
- traits of the native;
- adverse genius.

### Golden Dawn and Papus

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn absorbed the 72 names into its tables of quinances, zodiacal forces, tarot, and ceremonial angelology. Papus’s *La Cabbale* reproduces the French occult angel sequence and retains Psalm 2:11 for Anauel. [Papus, *La Cabbale*](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf).

### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* repeats and popularizes the French system, including Anauel’s 8:40–9:00 interval, Psalm 2:11, commercial offices, and 10°–15° Aquarius sector. [Ambelain-derived Shem study](https://utahsricf.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/SHEMHAMPHORASH-ASTROILOGY-PAPER-PRINT-VERSION-SRICF.pdf).

### Haziel

The French author who wrote as Haziel popularized a late-twentieth-century guardian-angel system. In it, Anauel governs the contiguous solar dates around 30 January–4 February and is invoked for economic prosperity, work, travel, health, and reconciliation.

**[Modern esoteric development]** Haziel’s presentation is devotional and practical, not a critical edition of Rudd or *Sefer Raziel*. Its calendar and expanded petitions should be dated to this modern reception rather than treated as the original content of the Shem triad.

## 7. How Anauel constrains Andras

In the Rudd-style construction:

```text
Anauel, angel 63
        ↓ divine authority / constraint
Andras, demon 63
        ↓ constrained manifestation
operator and ritual circle
```

The lamen physically embodies the relationship:

- Andras’s seal outward;
- Anauel’s angelic protection inward;
- the operator places the paired powers on the breast.

**[Tradition]** The intended logic is not that Anauel and Andras are mythological twins. They are paired because each occupies position 63 in a deliberately synchronized list.

Skinner and Rankine published this recension as *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* in 2007. Their edition is fundamental for studying the system even though its attribution to Thomas Rudd remains debated. [Skinner’s catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf) and [publisher’s current edition](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556).

## The two zodiacal systems

| Angel | Anauel 63 |

The **Six of Swords** belongs to the complete 10°–20° Aquarius decan. Anauel occupies only its first half; Mehiel occupies the second. Therefore saying “Anauel is the Six of Swords” is a shorthand, not an exact one-to-one identification.

Applying 10°–15° Aquarius directly to **Andras** is a **later synthesis produced through the Rudd pairing**. It is not Crowley’s own placement of Goetic spirit 63.

Crowley’s *777* places spirits 1–36 through the 36 decans by day, and spirits 37–72 through the same decans by night. Because:

\[

\]

Andras 63 takes the night position corresponding to day spirit 27, Ronove.

# Iconography

## 1. Before Collin de Plancy

The textual image is already fixed by 1577:

- angelic form;
- black nocturnal bird’s head;
- black wolf;
- raised sword.

Weyer supplies no illustration. Neither does Scot.

The image is conceptually paradoxical: an “angelic form” carries an animal head and rides another animal. Nothing in the source says the figure is a fallen angel, has black wings, wears armor, or possesses a specific gender, skin color, or costume. Those details are artistic extrapolations.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

### First edition, 1818

**[Negative finding]** No separate Andras article was located in the searchable text of the 1818 first edition of *Dictionnaire infernal*. It should therefore not be said that “the 1818 edition illustrated Andras.”

Andras is present in later editions, including the second edition of 1825–1826.

### Sixth edition, 1863

The sixth edition gives an article on page 32:

> “Andras, grand marquis aux enfers. On le voit avec le corps d’un ange, la tête d’un chat-huant…”

In English:

- Andras is a great marquis in Hell.
- He has an angel’s body and an owl’s head.
- He rides a black wolf.
- He carries a pointed sabre.
- He teaches those he favors to kill enemies, masters, and servants.
- He raises discord and quarrels.
- He commands thirty legions.

[De Plancy’s complete 1863 entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Andras).

### De Plancy’s textual innovations

| Earlier source | De Plancy |
|---|---|
| *Nycticorax* / black night raven | *Chat-huant*, owl |
| Very sharp sword | Pointed sabre |
| Can kill master, servant, helpers | Teaches favored people to kill enemies, masters, servants |
| Author of discords | Raises discords and quarrels |

The idea that Andras “teaches murder” is therefore not a verbatim Weyer doctrine. It is de Plancy’s nineteenth-century reframing.

## 3. Louis Le Breton’s portrait

The 1863 edition contains the famous illustration generally attributed to Louis Le Breton:

- a winged, anthropomorphic angelic torso;
- a distinctly owl-like head in profile;
- a raised, slightly curved sword or sabre;
- the figure seated astride a shaggy, dark wolf;
- the wolf moving laterally with its mouth partly open;
- a sparse landscape emphasizing the silhouette.

**[Modern visual genealogy]** This engraving is the source of most later Andras art. Many modern tarot, demonology, game, and tattoo designs reproduce Le Breton’s owl head and wolf even when they call the head a raven.

Care is needed with the credit: Le Breton supplied the drawings for de Plancy’s illustrated edition, while the physical engraving of particular plates could involve separate engravers. “Le Breton illustration” is safer than assuming every plate was personally cut by him.

## 4. Modern occult art

Modern images commonly add:

- plate armor;
- black or red wings;
- a blood-covered sword;
- a crown or marquis’s coronet;
- lunar crescents;
- Anauel’s Hebrew letters;
- the seal glowing behind the rider.

Except for the angelic body, bird head, wolf, sword, and implied rank, these are **[modern inventions]** unless a particular artist cites another source.

# Grimoire ancestors and related catalogues

## The *Livre des Esperitz*

This French spirit catalogue is an important ancestor or relative of Weyer’s catalogue tradition. It contains kings, dukes, marquises, and spirits whose names and offices resemble later Goetic entries.

**[Negative finding]** Andras is not found under that name in the published *Livre des Esperitz* list. [Published French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz).

## Munich handbook Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich manual contains demonic catalogues and ritual material predating Weyer.

**[Negative finding]** Although several names correspond to Weyer’s spirits, Peterson’s comparison does not identify Andras among the securely matched Clm 849 names.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer explicitly associates his material with a book of spirit offices. Related manuscripts include collections now called the *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* and, in one later English form, the *Book of Oberon*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The Florence Andras/Vandras entry is evidence that Andras belonged to this wider catalogue culture. It does not yet permit a continuous stemma from a named medieval archetype through Florence to Weyer.

## *Book of Abramelin*

**[Negative finding]** Andras is not found in the standard Abramelin demon-name lists. Similarities between Abramelin’s mass cataloguing of spirits and the Goetic tradition are structural, not proof of an Andras genealogy. [*Book of Abramelin*](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/abr/index.htm).

## *Testament of Solomon*

The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* establishes the narrative paradigm of Solomon interrogating, binding, and assigning demons. It includes figures such as Ornias, Beelzeboul, Asmodeus, and Onoskelis.

**[Negative finding]** It does not name Andras, nor does its surviving text contain an unmistakable raven-headed wolf rider corresponding to him. It is a deep literary ancestor of Solomonic demonology, not the immediate textual source of this spirit. [*Testament of Solomon*](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm).

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer is the first printer of the full surviving Andras description, though no longer the earliest witness to the name. His 1577 catalogue fixed:

- Great Marquis;
- angelic form;
- black nocturnal-bird head;
- black wolf;
- sword;
- killing ability;
- discord;
- thirty legions.

## Reginald Scot

Scot introduced Andras to English readers in 1584. His “blacke night raven” became the standard English wording and heavily influenced later translators.

## The anonymous *Lemegeton* compilers

They:

- moved Andras to number 63;
- added the seal;
- turned the killing ability into a threat against a careless exorcist;
- embedded him in a 72-spirit ritual system;
- supplied rank-based metal and timing instructions.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Peter Smart is the documented early-eighteenth-century copyist of Harley 6483. Thomas Rudd is the alleged earlier compiler or owner behind the recension. The association is influential but disputed.

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers produced the manuscript-based English edition later issued by Crowley. His Hebrew back-transliteration “Andrash” is editorial, not an ancient name form.

## Aleister Crowley

Crowley published the Mathers translation in 1904, added his “Initiated Interpretation,” and later correspondence tables placed Andras in the third decan of Sagittarius by night.

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No primary, Andras-specific operation, vision, or doctrinal innovation by Frater Achad was identified. His importance lies in the wider Crowleyan and qabalah milieu. Lists that automatically attach him to every Goetic spirit should not be mistaken for evidence of work with Andras.

## Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* and related work helped popularize modern operative Goetia using mirror, sigil, and ceremonial techniques.

**[Negative finding]** No well-sourced, independently published Andras-specific working by Runyon was identified in the materials examined. His relevance is general rather than biographical to Andras.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette, with Christopher Hyatt and David P. Wilson, helped popularize the explicit practical table:

- Andras;
- 20°–30° Sagittarius by night;
- Ten of Wands;
- Moon/silver.

This is useful evidence for late-twentieth-century reception, not for the medieval origin of the spirit.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition and continuing online apparatus are crucial because they:

- distinguish manuscript witnesses;
- preserve older spelling;
- compare Weyer, Scot, and the *Goetia*;
- identify the 1494 Florence witness;
- expose where Mathers normalized or expanded the text.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* published and operationally reconstructed the Harley recension, especially the use of Shem angels to bind or control the 72 demons.

Their historical attribution to Thomas Rudd should be separated from the value of their transcription and reconstruction.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium* compares the *Livre des Esperitz*, Weyer, Scot, the spirit-office books, continental catalogues, and the *Goetia*. His work is relevant to the catalogue’s transmission and to attempts to recover spirit traditions behind the Victorian ceremonial overlay. [Hadean Press, *Pandemonium*](https://hadean.press/products/pandemonium).

## Historians of grimoires

- **Jean-Patrice Boudet** has catalogued medieval demon lists and the Florence witness.
- **Julien Véronèse** has studied ritual magic, Solomonic books, and the *Livre des Esperitz* context.
- **Jan Veenstra** has examined learned magic, spirits, and courtly manuscript culture.
- **Owen Davies**, in *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books*, situates books such as the *Lemegeton* in the social history of magical texts.
- **Egil Asprem** has analyzed twentieth-century Goetic reinventions and the problems surrounding Rudd attribution.

They supply historical context; none has established an ancient pagan identity for Andras.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Was Weyer the earliest source?

No.

- **Former common claim:** Andras first appears in Weyer.
- **Current evidence:** “Andras vel Vandras” occurs in a Florentine manuscript dated January 1494.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** no pre-1494 witness has been demonstrated.

## 2. Is Andras an ancient god?

No secure evidence says so.

- Andraste is ancient, but the connection is modern conjecture.
- Welsh *andras* is a genuine word for demon or evil, but there is no demonstrated textual route.
- Greek *andros* is a resemblance without transmission evidence.
- The name has no established Baal-, Astarte-, Amun-, Asmodeus-, Belial-, or Phoenix-type genealogy.

## 3. Raven, owl, or night-heron?

The Latin **nycticorax** creates the ambiguity.

- Weyer: *nycticorax*.
- Scot: “night raven.”
- *Goetia*: “black night Raven.”
- De Plancy: *chat-huant*, owl.
- Le Breton: visually owl-like.
- Modern sources alternate between raven and owl.

“Raven-headed” is closest to the early English tradition; “owl-headed” accurately describes de Plancy’s French and Le Breton’s image.

## 4. Does Andras kill enemies or the magician?

Different sources say different things.

- Weyer/Scot: can kill master, servant, and assistants.
- *Goetia*: may kill the exorcist and companions if care is lacking.
- De Plancy: teaches favored persons to kill enemies, masters, and servants.

These should not be merged into one supposedly timeless sentence.

## 5. Does Andras teach swordsmanship or assassination?

**[Negative finding]** The primary catalogue does not say he teaches swordsmanship. De Plancy says he teaches favored persons to kill enemies, masters, and servants. Modern claims that he teaches military strategy, assassination, or “the art of murder” are expansions of that nineteenth-century wording.

## 6. Is the spirit inherently lunar?

Only by the *Goetia* rank-metal table.

His Moon/silver correspondence is not found in the 1494 entry, Weyer, or Scot. It derives from his classification as a Marquis in the later ritual instructions.

## 7. Aquarius or Sagittarius?

Both occur, but in different systems:

- **Anauel 63:** 10°–15° Aquarius.
- **Andras 63 in Crowley’s Goetic table:** 20°–30° Sagittarius by night.

Saying simply “Andras rules 10°–15° Aquarius” silently imports the Rudd angel pairing. Saying “Anauel is the Ten of Wands angel” silently imports Crowley’s separate Goetic decan. Neither is historically exact.

## 8. Psalm 2:11 or Psalm 100:2?

- Lenain, Papus, and Ambelain: **Psalm 2:11**.
- Some modern websites: **Psalm 100:2**.

Psalm 2:11 has the stronger traceable printed lineage for Anauel.

## 9. Did Thomas Rudd create the angel-control system?

Possibly, but not proven.

- Harley 6483 documents the system.
- Peter Smart copied it in 1712–1713.
- Skinner and Rankine reconstruct an earlier Thomas Rudd exemplar.
- Harms and Asprem identify reasons to distrust some Rudd attributions in Smart’s collection.

The manuscript’s contents should therefore be accepted separately from the attribution.

## 10. Are Andras and Andrealphus the same spirit?

No. They are adjacent or near-adjacent but separately numbered spirits in the earliest comparative lists.

## 11. Is “Andrash” an ancient Hebrew name?

No evidence supports that. It is Mathers’s Hebrew-letter rendering of the Latin-alphabet name.

## 12. Did de Plancy illustrate Andras in 1818?

No portrait was located in the 1818 first edition. The famous Le Breton portrait belongs to the illustrated sixth edition of 1863. Andras’s article appears in intermediate editions before the portrait.

## 13. Are modern “enns” ancient?

**[Modern invention]** Chants marketed as demonic “enns,” together with fixed colors, incense blends, chakras, crystals, weekdays, and personal messages from Andras, are not found in Florence 1494, Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, or Mathers–Crowley. They belong to late-twentieth- or twenty-first-century devotional demonolatry unless a source demonstrates otherwise.

## 14. Are reports of working with Andras historical evidence?

They are evidence for modern religious experience and reception, but not for the origin of the name or the accuracy of medieval claims. Reports that Andras causes arguments, electrical failures, nightmares, violence, or abrupt relationship breakdowns are **[modern experiential legend]** unless independently documented.

# Open questions

1. **Pre-1494 history:** Does an earlier unedited *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* witness contain Andras under another spelling?

2. **Vandras:** Is initial *V* a scribal error, a genuine older form, or evidence of a lost linguistic source?

3. **Welsh connection:** Is the identity between Goetic *Andras* and Welsh *andras* accidental, or did some presently unknown British or continental textual channel connect them?

4. **Catalogue stemma:** What precise manuscript relationship links the Florence list, Weyer’s source, and the later *Lemegeton*?

5. **Seal origin:** When was the familiar Andras seal first added? The 1494 occurrence of the name does not yet supply the later sigil.

6. **Seal variants:** A complete high-resolution comparative plate of Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and the 1904 redraw remains desirable.

7. **Rudd attribution:** Can independent seventeenth-century evidence connect Thomas Rudd personally to the double-sided angel-and-demon lamens?

8. **Early Anauel Psalm:** Does the relevant Harley folio itself specify Psalm 2:11 for Anauel, or did that exact verse become fixed in a later French table?

9. **De Plancy’s source:** Did de Plancy translate Scot directly, work through Weyer or another French intermediary, or deliberately rewrite the power as instruction in killing?

10. **Modern media:** Because games continually reuse demon catalogues, a complete appearance history requires title-by-title verification rather than reliance on franchise wikis.

# Conclusions

The fullest defensible reconstruction is:

- **Andras first enters the currently known record in Florence in January 1494 as “Andras vel Vandras,” a great marquis of thirty legions.**
- Weyer’s 1577 printing supplies the canonical angelic body, black nocturnal-bird head, black wolf, sword, killing ability, and authorship of discord.
- Scot translates that account in 1584.
- The seventeenth-century *Goetia* moves him from 54 to 63, adds a seal and lamen, and turns his murderous ability into a warning to a careless exorcist.
- Harley 6483 places the corresponding Shem angel Anauel over him as a controlling force.
- Anauel’s securely traceable French occult correspondences are 10°–15° Aquarius, Psalm 2:11, and 8:40–9:00 p.m.; “God infinitely good” is Lenain’s interpretive epithet.
- Crowley’s different Goetic table places Andras at 20°–30° Sagittarius by night, paired with the Ten of Wands and sharing the decan with day-spirit Ronove.
- The Moon and silver derive from the rank of Marquis, not from either zodiacal placement.
- De Plancy and Le Breton turned the ambiguous night-raven into the now-famous owl-headed wolf rider.
- No secure ancient god, hero, place, Greek term, or Hebrew word has been demonstrated behind the name.
- Andraste, Greek *andros*, Welsh *andras*, and the name Andrew remain possible comparisons of very unequal evidentiary value; none is a proven derivation.
- Modern games, comics, ceremonial systems, and demonolatry have expanded the figure substantially, while *Helluva Boss*, Dante, Milton, and the Bartimaeus books are often mistakenly attached to him without an attested appearance.

# Glasya-Labolas: Research Dossier

*Research current to 14 September 2026.*

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **Documented text** — directly present in a cited manuscript, early printed book, or critical transcription.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a conclusion proposed from textual comparison, codicology, or historical context.
- **Tradition** — a claim transmitted within ceremonial-magic or kabbalistic literature without independent historical proof.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — an intentionally constructed symbolic assignment, such as a planet, decan, tarot card, angel, or metal.
- **Disputed** — a reading or historical identification for which the witnesses conflict or the evidence is insufficient.
- **Legend / modern invention** — devotional, experiential, fictional, or Internet-era material not traceable to the early source chain.

Long early-modern passages are represented by exact excerpts followed by complete clause-by-clause accounts; the linked facsimiles and transcriptions contain the complete entries.

## The name and its origins

### 1. The source-chain spellings

Joseph Peterson’s comparative table supplies the following readings. It is particularly valuable because it separates what the manuscripts actually say from later normalized spelling. [Peterson, “Goetia spirit variant spellings”](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf)

| Date/source | Reading |
|---|---|
| 1577, Weyer | **Glasya labolas, alias Caacrinolaas vel Caassimolar** |
| 1584, Scot | **Glasya Labolas, alias Caacrinolaas, or Caassimolar** |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane 3825 | **Glasya Labolas** |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane 3648 | **Glasya Labolas** |
| 1686, Sloane 2731 | **Glasya Labolas** |
| 1712–1713, Harley 6483 | **Glasya-la bolas; Glasialabolas** |
| Northwestern University MS 65 | **Glasya labolas** |
| 1904, Mathers–Crowley | **Glasya Labolas / Glasya-Labolas** |
| Waite | **Glasyalabolas**, alias **Caacrinolaas** or **Caassimola** |
| Idries Shah | **Glasyalabolas**, aka **Caacrinolaas**, aka **Caasimola** |
| Wellcome MS 3203 | Omitted |

Later forms such as **Glassialabolas**, **Classialabolas**, **Classyalabolas**, **Glacialabolas**, **Glassia-labolis**, and **Bassimolar Glasya** chiefly occur in de Plancy-derived translations, popular dictionaries, modern demonological encyclopedias, and web compilations. They should not be projected backward into Weyer.

### 2. First secure attestation

**Documented text:** The first securely dated printed occurrence is Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. It is spirit §18, around pp. 916–917 in that edition. [Weyer transcription and facsimile references](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**Parallel manuscript evidence:** Folger MS V.b.26, copied approximately 1577–1583, contains **Gloolas**, no. 19, with nearly the same appearance and offices. Its exact relation to Weyer’s text is unresolved because the Folger manuscript is contemporary with, rather than securely earlier than, the 1577 printing. [Folger V.b.26 transcription](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

**Finding:** No securely dated pre-1577 source has yet been identified that contains the exact forms *Glasya Labolas*, *Caacrinolaas*, or *Caassimolar*.

### 3. Gloolas

The Folger witness reads, in part:

> “Gloolas a greate kinge or prince; he appeareth like to a doge havinge winges…”

Its offices are:

- chief leader of murderers;
- knowledge of past, present, and future;
- knowledge of friends and enemies;
- invisibility for as long as the caller wishes;
- command of twenty legions.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** Gloolas is almost certainly related to Glasya-Labolas at the textual level. The combination of winged canine appearance, murderers, temporal knowledge, interpersonal knowledge, and invisibility is too distinctive to be accidental. Peterson accordingly cross-references “Folger p. 75: 19. Gloolas.” [Peterson’s source comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**Open point:** Whether *Gloolas* is the older form, a contraction of *Glasya Labolas*, or an independent corruption of a lost archetype remains unknown.

### 4. The possible *Livre des Esperitz* lead

Jean-Patrice Boudet published the mid-fifteenth-century *Livre des Esperitz*, Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, as an ancestor of Renaissance demon catalogues. It contains many recognizable predecessors of Weyer’s spirits, but it contains no exact *Glasya*, *Caacrinolaas*, or *Caassimolar*. [Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques…”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019); [manuscript text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz)

A spirit named **Carmola**, positioned after Cerbere, has sometimes attracted comparison because portions of its office involve criminals or murderers, revelation, and possibly concealment or invisibility, depending on difficult readings.

**Disputed:** No consulted critical source demonstrates that Carmola became Glasya-Labolas. Similar list position and partial functional overlap make it a research lead, not an established identification.

### 5. Etymology

**Finding:** There is no accepted philological etymology.

Unlike:

- **Baal**, from Northwest Semitic *baʿl*, “lord/master”;
- **Astaroth**, ultimately related to Astarte/ʿAštart;
- **Amon**, often associated in later demonology with Egyptian Amun;
- **Asmoday**, traceable through Hebrew *Ashmedai* to Iranian *Aēšma daēva*;
- **Belial**, Hebrew *bĕliyyaʿal*;
- **Phoenix**, Greek *phoinix* and the classical phoenix tradition;

*Glasya Labolas*, *Caacrinolaas*, and *Caassimolar* have no demonstrable ancient god, hero, place-name, Greek noun, Latin title, or Hebrew divine name behind them.

Relevant Latin in Weyer describes the office rather than explaining the name:

- *magnus praeses* — great president;
- *canis* — dog;
- *alae gryphi* — wings of a griffin;
- *dux omnium homicidarum* — leader of all murderers/manslayers;
- *hominem reddit invisibilem* — renders a human invisible.

Modern proposed derivations from Greek *glōssa* (“tongue”) and *lalein/laleō* (“to speak”), from Spanish *lobos*, or from supposed names meaning “the barking one” are **modern folk etymologies**. None explains all three early forms or appears in Weyer, Scot, the manuscripts, de Plancy, Mathers, or Peterson.

The safest conclusion is therefore:

> **Scholarly reconstruction:** The name is probably the product of copying, oral transmission, or corruption within a late-medieval/early-modern spirit catalogue. Its pre-corruption form and linguistic origin are unknown.

## Number, order, and the passage from 69 to 72

Weyer prints **69 entries**, despite the surrounding Solomonic legend referring to a company of 72. The *Ars Goetia* prints 72. Its compiler did not simply copy Weyer in the same order.

For Glasya-Labolas:

- Weyer: **18**
- Scot: **18**
- *Ars Goetia*: **25**

The *Goetia* adds four spirits absent from Weyer:

1. Vassago, inserted as no. 3;
2. Seere, no. 70;
3. Dantalion, no. 71;
4. Andromalius, no. 72.

It omits Weyer’s no. 4:

- Pruflas, also printed as Bufas/Busas.

Thus 69 + 4 − 1 = 72. Peterson’s comparison documents the additions and omission. [Weyer–Goetia concordance](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**Scholarly reconstruction:** Completion to 72 was probably numerologically attractive because of the 72-fold divine name, the 72 nations, and other Jewish and Christian uses of 72. The evidence does not show that Weyer’s individual demons were already paired one-for-one with Shem angels.

**Tradition:** The *Goetia* itself frames the 72 as spirits sealed by Solomon in a brass vessel and released by Babylonians seeking treasure. That is the grimoire’s pseudepigraphic legend, not historical evidence about Solomon.

## The description across the sources

## 1. Folger MS V.b.26, ca. 1577–1583: Gloolas

**Documented text:** Gloolas is a great king or prince, appearing like a winged dog. He is chief leader of murderers, knows past, present, and future, gives knowledge concerning friends and enemies, makes a person invisible for as long as the operator wishes, and rules twenty legions. [Diplomatic transcription, p. 75](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

Points of difference from Weyer:

- rank is king or prince, not president;
- the wings are not explicitly griffin wings;
- twenty rather than thirty-six legions;
- the text explicitly limits invisibility to the caller’s desired duration;
- it includes past as well as present and future.

## 2. Weyer, 1577

Exact opening excerpt:

> “Glasya labolas, aliàs Caacrinolaas vel Caassimolar magnus Præses: qui progreditur ut canis habens alas gryphi.”

Complete contents in English:

- Glasya Labolas, otherwise Caacrinolaas or Caassimolar;
- is a great President;
- proceeds or comes forth as a dog with griffin wings;
- gives knowledge of arts;
- meanwhile is leader of all murderers;
- understands present and future things;
- wins over the dispositions or affections of friends and enemies;
- renders a person invisible;
- has authority over thirty-six legions.

The key Latin phrase *dux omnium homicidarum* means “leader/captain of all murderers” more directly than “author of murder.” *Animos demeretur* means that he wins, gains, or obliges their dispositions or affections; it is not necessarily a magical command making friends and enemies love one another. [Weyer §18](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

## 3. Scot, 1584

Exact excerpt:

> “is a great president, who commeth foorth like a dog, and hath wings like a griffen”

Scot’s complete account says that Glasya Labolas, Caacrinolaas, or Caassimolar:

- is a great president;
- comes forth like a dog with griffin wings;
- gives knowledge of arts;
- is “captaine of all mansleiers”;
- understands present and future things;
- gains the minds and love of friends and foes;
- makes a person go invisible;
- rules thirty-six legions.

This is printed in Book XV, chapter 2, p. 380 of the 1584 pagination. Peterson identifies the English as probably the work of Abraham Fleming, who translated other Latin texts embedded in Scot. [Scot/Weyer parallel text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); [1584 facsimile](https://archive.org/details/discouerieofwitc00scot/)

**Context:** Scot was not endorsing the catalogue as a personal record of successful evocation. Like Weyer, he reproduced magical and demonological material within a skeptical attack on witchmongers, fraudulent conjurers, and the persecution of supposed witches.

## 4. Sloane MS 3825, seventeenth century

Peterson’s critical transcription follows Sloane 3825 as its base. The entry, fol. 103v–104r, states:

- no. 25;
- name Glasya Labolas;
- mighty president;
- dog with wings like a griffin;
- teaches all arts instantly;
- author of bloodshed and manslaughter;
- tells all things past and future if desired;
- causes the love of friends and foes;
- can make a man invisible;
- rules thirty-six legions;
- seal to be worn as a lamen.

Exact excerpt:

> “he teacheth all arts in an Instant, and is an author of Blood shed & Manslaughter”

[Peterson, *Lemegeton*, entry 25](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

Changes from Weyer/Scot include:

- **present and future** becomes **past and future**;
- “gives knowledge of arts” becomes “teaches all arts in an instant”;
- leader/captain of murderers becomes “author of bloodshed and manslaughter”;
- an individual seal and lamen instruction are added;
- aliases disappear;
- number changes from 18 to 25.

## 5. Sloane MS 2731, 1686

Sloane 2731 has substantially the same entry and only the rank **mighty president**. It spells *manslaughter* as “manslatter” and says the seal is to be worn as a lamen. [Sloane 2731 facsimile and diplomatic transcription, manuscript p. 6](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/)

This witness confirms that “Earl” is not required by the seventeenth-century *Goetia* text.

## 6. Harley MS 6483, copied 1712–1713

The Harley entry reads **Glasya-la bolas** and calls him a mighty President. It adds “Sciences” to “arts” and unusually includes all three temporal categories—past, present, and future. It retains 36 legions. [Skinner and Rankine transcription, pp. 126–127](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

Its most important innovation is not a new demonic office, but the adjacent angelic safeguard and Psalm verse discussed below.

## 7. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

Exact rank-and-form excerpt:

> “He is a Mighty President and Earl… in the form of a Dog with Wings like a Gryphon.”

The 1904 text, pp. 29–30, gives:

- no. 25;
- President **and Earl**;
- dog with gryphon wings;
- all arts and sciences in an instant;
- author of bloodshed and manslaughter;
- knowledge of past and future;
- love of friends and foes;
- invisibility;
- thirty-six legions.

[Mathers–Crowley text](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

**Disputed editorial change:** “and Earl” is absent from Weyer, Scot, Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, and Harley 6483. No manuscript basis for it was found in the requested chain. Later writers have repeated the dual rank so often that it is now the common popular description, but its earliest verified occurrence here is Mathers’s printed edition.

### Offices not attributed to Glasya-Labolas

The source chain does **not** assign this spirit:

- languages;
- treasure-finding;
- familiars;
- transmutation;
- healing;
- rhetoric specifically;
- transportation;
- answers to arbitrary questions in general;
- dignities or offices;
- reconciliation as distinct from gaining love.

Those belong to other Goetic spirits. Modern lists sometimes merge generic Goetic powers or neighboring entries into Glasya’s dossier.

## Rank, planet, metal, and ritual timing

### The seventeenth-century manuscript scheme

The *Goetia*’s “Observations” assigns metals as follows:

| Rank | Planet | Manuscript material |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | gold |
| Marquis | Moon | silver |
| Duke | Venus | copper |
| Prince/“Prelate” | Jupiter | tin |
| Knight | Saturn | lead |
| President | Mercury | mercury |
| Earl | Venus and Moon | equal copper and silver |

[Peterson, “Observations”](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

Mars and iron are conspicuously absent. The text also fails to explain how to treat a spirit of two ranks.

For manuscript Glasya-Labolas, who is simply a President:

- planet: **Mercury**;
- seal substance: literally **mercury**.

This is physically awkward because elemental mercury is liquid and toxic. Peterson suggests electrum only as a modern practical substitute; that is an editorial recommendation, not manuscript text.

### Mathers’s planetary reconstruction

Mathers observed that Mars had been omitted and proposed:

- Earl seals should probably be **iron**;
- President seals should perhaps use a mixture of copper and silver, or silver and mercury.

This is an **esoteric editorial emendation**. It generated the now-standard rank sequence in which Earls correspond to Mars. Accepting Mathers’s added Earl rank gives Glasya-Labolas:

- President → Mercury;
- Earl → Mars;
- metals → mercury or mercurial mixture, plus iron.

There is no single historically uncontested “correct metal” for the dual-rank modern version.

### Time of conjuration

**Documented general rule:** Presidents may be bound at any hour of the day except twilight. At night they are not to be called unless the king governing them is also invoked.

Earls may be called at any hour of the day, in woods or another secluded, quiet place.

For the manuscript President-only Glasya, the President rule is the applicable one. For Mathers’s President-and-Earl version, the two rules overlap without the text explaining which prevails.

Additional general timing:

- the Moon should be 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old;
- no Glasya-specific clock hour appears in Weyer, Scot, or the *Goetia*;
- planetary hours, zodiac decans, or birth-date quinances are later overlays, not instructions in the individual entry.

## The seal

### Absence from the early catalogue

**Documented text:** Weyer and Scot provide no individual Glasya seal. Peterson notes that individual Goetic spirit sigils are absent from the earlier *Offices of Spirits* witnesses known to him. Consequently, no extant evidence places this seal in the fifteenth-century *Livre des Esperitz* or in Weyer’s lost source.

### Manuscript form

In Sloane 2731 and Peterson’s Sloane 3825-based edition, the character is a compact, asymmetrical linear device. In words:

- a central angular or rectangular-crossed body;
- a vertical stem;
- a small loop or circle above the central body;
- another pendant loop/circle below;
- a lateral line ending in a curl or hook;
- short intersecting strokes in the middle.

The manuscript hand makes it look more like a rapidly drawn magical character than a pictorial dog or griffin.

**Variant caution:** Exact angles, whether some terminals close into loops, the length of the right-hand hook, and the squareness of the central body differ among copies and printed redrawing. These differences should not be treated as different spirits unless accompanied by textual evidence.

### Rudd’s double-sided use

Harley 6483 integrates the demon’s character with:

- the controlling Shem angel;
- the angelic seal or name;
- an appropriate Psalm verse.

Skinner and Rankine interpret the working lamen as having the demon’s seal on one face and the corresponding angelic control on the reverse. The angelic side is worn facing the magician’s heart. [Skinner and Rankine, introduction](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

**Tradition:** The arrangement is meant to guard the operator against the summoned demon’s malice. It does not establish a mythological feud between two independent characters.

### Mathers–Crowley form

The 1904 printed seal is a cleaned, typeset redraw of the manuscript character. Modern copies often place it within a circular border or surround it with the spirit’s name. The circle and lettering are presentation devices in some reproductions, not evidence that the sigil is an ancient alphabetic cryptogram. [1904 seal image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:25-Glasya-Labolas_seal.png)

**Modern invention:** Claims that individual lines specifically depict a paw, wing, blood vessel, or hidden letters spelling an ancient name have no identified support in the early sources.

## The angel: Nithhaiah

### 1. The 72-fold name

The scheme derives from Exodus 14:19–21. Each Hebrew verse contains 72 letters in the relevant consonantal text. They are arranged:

1. verse 19 in its normal direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its normal direction.

Reading vertical columns produces 72 triplets. The twenty-fifth is conventionally transliterated N–T–H.

Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a explicitly describes taking the first letter of the first verse, the last of the middle verse, and the first of the third, and then continuing in that order. [Rashi on Sukkah 45a](https://www.chabad.org/torah-texts/5446880/Talmud/Sukkah/Chapter-4/45a)

Joshua Trachtenberg likewise describes the formation and notes that the printed *Sefer Raziel* gives the full 72-fold name without stable vocalization. [Trachtenberg, *Jewish Magic and Superstition*](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm)

Important distinctions:

- **Jewish textual tradition:** the 72 triplets are divine-name components.
- **Christian Kabbalah:** triplets are expanded into personal angel names by adding *-El* or *-Yah*.
- **Rudd’s magic:** the numbered angels are paired with numbered demons as controllers.

These are successive transformations, not one unchanged system extending back to Exodus.

### 2. Name and meaning

The reconstructed Hebrew form is commonly written **נתהיה**, combining the triplet NTH with a form of *Yah*.

“God who gives wisdom” is **not a secure literal translation of a normal Hebrew personal name**. The triplet was obtained combinatorially; it was not necessarily intended as an ordinary lexical word.

Meanings by source stream:

- **Lenain/Papus:** *Dieu qui donne la Sagesse* — “God who gives wisdom.”
- **Golden Dawn:** “wide in extent,” “the enlarger,” “wonderful.”
- **Modern angelology:** wisdom, occult discovery, white magic, dream revelation, peace, silence.

These are esoteric glosses and interpretations, not a settled philological etymology.

### 3. Psalm verse

Harley 6483 places with Glasya-Labolas:

> *Confitebor tibi Domine in toto corde meo; narrabo omnia mirabilia tua.*

Rudd’s English gives the sense:

> “I shall acknowledge Thee, O Lord, with all my heart and shall tell forth all Thy wonders.”

In the King James numbering this is Psalm 9:1:

> “I will praise thee, O LORD, with my whole heart; I will shew forth all thy marvellous works.”

Some Catholic/Vulgate presentations label the same sentence Psalm 9:2 because the title or superscription is counted differently. The apparent 9:1/9:2 conflict is therefore usually a numbering convention, not a different verse.

### 4. From *Sefer Raziel* to Reuchlin

**Documented/traditional background:** The 72-fold name occurs in medieval Jewish mystical and magical tradition and is printed in *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*. The triplets were not initially a roster of 72 Christian guardian angels, each with a horoscope and personality.

Johann Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) is a decisive Christian-kabbalistic transmission point. Reuchlin turns the triplets into angelic names through *-El* and *-Yah* endings and gives them Christian metaphysical interpretation. [Reuchlin scan and bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books?id=vF9mG3DnbBUC)

Blaise de Vigenère subsequently helped transmit the personal-angel forms in the later sixteenth century. Rudd’s list follows this Christian-kabbalistic development rather than an ancient Jewish demon-opposition table.

### 5. Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, especially *Cabala Hebraeorum* in volume II.1 (1652), reproduced and systematized 72-name material. Kircher treated it critically from a Jesuit perspective, frequently characterizing magical uses as corruption or superstition. [Kircher’s *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

Kircher is important as a transmitter of tables and diagrams, but the specific Glasya–Nithhaiah controlling pair belongs to the Rudd manuscript scheme, not demonstrably to ancient kabbalists.

### 6. Rudd and Harley 6483

The surviving Harley manuscript was copied by lawyer Peter Smart in 1712–1713. Skinner and Rankine argue that it transmits material owned or written by Thomas Rudd before his death in 1656 and conjecture an original of approximately 1655–1656.

**Documented:** The surviving object is an early-eighteenth-century copy.

**Scholarly reconstruction/disputed attribution:** Its derivation from an autograph or owned manuscript of Thomas Rudd is argued from internal headings, old pagination, and provenance, but the surviving volume is not Rudd’s autograph.

In this scheme:

- demon 25 is Glasya-la bolas;
- angel 25 is Nithhaiah/Nethah;
- Psalm 9:1 accompanies the seal;
- the angelic face of the lamen is directed toward the magician’s heart;
- the angel is invoked as a power that limits or controls the demon.

The pairing is therefore verified. [Skinner and Rankine, *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

### 7. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives Nith-Haiah as genius 25. Its principal data are:

- attribute: “God who gives wisdom”;
- divine name in the language of the Magi: **Orsy**;
- zodiacal ray: degrees 121–125;
- Psalm 9:1;
- invocation period: 8:00–8:20;
- useful for wisdom and discovering the truth of hidden mysteries;
- rules occult sciences;
- gives revelations in dreams;
- influences practitioners of the “magic of the wise”;
- contrary genius: black magic.

Lenain also assigns five recurring presidency dates: approximately 13 April, 24 June, 4 September, 15 November, and 26 January. These are a separate calendar technique from the continuous five-day “birth angel” period later popularized as 23–27 July. [Lenain scan, pp. 61–62](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365)

The 121st–125th degrees correspond, in the ordinary Aries-zero zodiac, to the first five degrees of Leo.

### 8. Papus

Papus, Gérard Encausse, reprinted Lenain’s angel notices in editions of *La Cabbale*, especially the expanded 1903 edition. Nith-Haiah remains genius 25, degrees 121–125, with Psalm 9 and the wisdom/hidden-mysteries/dream-revelation profile. [Papus, 1903 edition](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf)

**Transmission finding:** Papus largely republishes and authorizes Lenain for the French occult revival; he does not supply independent ancient evidence for the attributes.

### 9. The Golden Dawn

The Golden Dawn makes a different zodiacal decision. Its Shem document states that the 72 angels rule 72 quinances, but begins the sequence at the first decan of **Leo**, associated with Regulus, rather than at 0° Aries.

Consequently:

- angels 1–6 occupy Leo;
- 7–12 Virgo;
- 13–18 Libra;
- 19–24 Scorpio;
- **25–30 Sagittarius**.

Thus its entry for angel 25 reads:

- **Nethhiah**;
- Sagittarius;
- Mercury;
- 0°–5°;
- meaning “wide in extent, the enlarger, wonderful”;
- Psalm 9:1.

[Golden Dawn Shem document](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Schemhamphorash.html)

This is why Crowley’s same-number demon table also places Glasya-Labolas in the first decan of Sagittarius.

### 10. Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reorganizes the French practical-kabbalistic inheritance within a fuller Tree-of-Life and angelic hierarchy. Nith-Haiah belongs among the Dominations associated with:

- Chesed;
- Zadkiel;
- Jupiter.

Ambelain’s framework mixes medieval Jewish materials, Christian Kabbalah, Renaissance occultism, and modern French occult organization. [Ambelain, *Practical Kabbalah*](https://www.markfoster.net/rn/texts/practicalkabbalah-part1.pdf); [1951 bibliographic record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique)

### 11. Haziel

Haziel’s French angel books popularized the guardian-angel/birthday interpretation for a broad late-twentieth-century readership. *Notre ange gardien existe* appeared in 1994/1995; his guardian-angel and invocation books continued through the 1990s and 2000s. [BnF Haziel catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/rechercher.do?index=AUT3&numNotice=12089673); [1995 edition](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL10002479W/Notre_ange_gardien_existe)

For Nith-Haiah, Haziel gives:

- guardian of people born 23–27 July;
- first angel of the choir of Dominations;
- service under Hesediel/Jupiter;
- white magic and mastery of spiritual forces;
- peace, silence, contemplation, and resistance to vanity or harmful use.

[Haziel text](https://fr.scribd.com/document/1068604985/Notre-Ange-Gardien-Existe-French-Edition-Haziel)

**Modern angelology:** The birth-personality narrative and elaborate personal prayer are Haziel’s expansion of the Lenain–Papus–Ambelain stream, not contents of Exodus, *Sefer Raziel*, or Rudd.

### Crowley’s *777* attribution in detail

*Liber 777* distributes the 72 spirits among 36 decans:

- one daytime spirit;
- one nighttime spirit;
- for each decan.

For Sagittarius:

| Decan | Day spirit | Night spirit | Tarot pip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0°–10° | **Glasya-Labolas 25** | **Zagan 61** | Eight of Wands |
| 10°–20° | Bime/Bune 26 | Volac 62 | Nine of Wands |
| 20°–30° | Ronove 27 | Andras 63 | Ten of Wands |

The *777* table explicitly places Glasya-Labolas in column CLV, “Goetic Demons of Decans by Day (Ascendant),” at key 25/Sagittarius, with the image of a dog with griffin wings. [Interactive transcription of *777*, key 25](https://www.nofaithinthehumanrace.com/777/?deck=crowley&key=25&show777=1); [scan of *777*](https://astrumargentum.org/ht/libri/Liber_777.pdf)

Accordingly, the user’s lead that “each pip carries two spirits” is correct for this arrangement: Glasya-Labolas is the daylight spirit and Zagan the nighttime spirit of the Eight of Wands.

### Why Five of Wands also appears online

If the numbered Shem angels are laid sequentially from 0° Aries, angel 25 lands at 0° Leo, within the Five of Wands decan. Later writers then assign the same degrees and card to demon 25 because demon and angel share a number.

That produces:

- Glasya-Labolas;
- Nith-Haiah;
- 0°–5° Leo;
- Five of Wands;
- Saturn in Leo.

This is coherent as a later same-number system but is not Crowley’s *777*. Poke Runyon is an influential transmitter of this synchronization. [Runyon, *The Book of Solomon’s Magick*, p. 155](https://www.esis-corner888.com/_downloads/e767b40076acec7cf37baad61cb8cf35)

### Enochian overlays

No Enochian name, aethyr, governor, watchtower, or Dee–Kelley identification appears in:

- Weyer;
- Scot;
- Sloane 2731 or 3825;
- Harley 6483’s Glasya entry;
- Mathers–Crowley’s individual description.

Rudd materials elsewhere attempt to relate names on John Dee’s tables to Goetic spirits, and twentieth-century ceremonial magicians sometimes combine Goetic, Enochian, planetary, and elemental invocations.

**Finding:** There is no single historically stable “Enochian correspondence of Glasya-Labolas.” Such attributions are later synthetic ritual systems.

## Iconography

## 1. Manuscript image

The earliest securely attested image specific to Glasya-Labolas is the seal in the seventeenth-century *Goetia* manuscripts, not a portrait. Weyer, Scot, and the parallel Gloolas catalogue provide words only.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

The *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818. Its sixth edition of 1863 contains the famous Louis Le Breton engravings.

De Plancy’s entry, p. 125, calls the spirit **Caacrinolaas**, also **Caassimolar** and **Glassialabolas**. Exact excerpt:

> “il se présente sous la forme d’un chien… avec des ailes de griffon.”

The complete account says:

- great president in hell;
- form and gait of a dog;
- griffin wings;
- gives knowledge of liberal arts;
- by a strange contrast, inspires homicides;
- predicts the future;
- makes a person invisible;
- commands thirty-six legions.

It then adds that the *Grand Grimoire* calls him **Classyalabolas**, reduces him to a kind of sergeant, and sometimes makes him a mount for Nébiros or Naberus. [De Plancy entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Caacrinolaas)

### Le Breton’s portrait

The 1863 image shows:

- a lean, long-bodied canine in side profile;
- four legs in a walking stance;
- large feathered wings rising from its shoulders;
- a staring, alert face;
- no crown, weapon, human torso, or obvious emblem of rank;
- the caption **CAACRINOLAAS**.

[High-resolution public-domain engraving](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Caacrinolaas.png)

**Documented distinction:** The portrait is an 1863 artistic realization of the old verbal description. It is not copied from a medieval manuscript and was not present in the 1577 or 1584 catalogues.

## 3. Modern visual development

Modern occult, game, anime, and fan art tends toward three broad models:

1. **Le Breton canine** — a winged hound, often with Le Breton’s grin or staring eyes.
2. **Anthropomorphic demon** — a youthful or armored human with wings, unsupported by the early appearance text.
3. **Abstract spirit portrait** — blood, war, libraries, alchemical or mercurial symbolism built from the listed offices and later planets.

Human blond, adolescent, angel-winged, or aristocratic portraits published as personal visionary experiences are **modern experiential claims**. They cannot be used to reconstruct the historical iconography.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer supplied the first secure printed name and profile in 1577. He was a physician and critic of witch persecution. His inclusion of a spirit catalogue served a polemical discussion of conjurers and supposed demonic deceptions; it is not a diary proving that he evoked Glasya-Labolas.

### Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot reproduced the entry in *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* in 1584. Peterson judges the translated catalogue probably to be Fleming’s work. Scot’s larger purpose was skeptical and anti-persecution.

### Anonymous compilers of the *Lemegeton*

Seventeenth-century compilers reorganized Weyer/Scot, added seals, expanded some offices, changed the number to 25, and completed the catalogue to 72. They are responsible for the form in which Glasya-Labolas became an operative grimoire spirit.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

The Rudd tradition adds the controlling Shem angel and Psalm. Peter Smart is the identifiable copyist of Harley 6483 in 1712–1713. Thomas Rudd’s authorship or ownership of the lost earlier exemplar is plausible but reconstructed rather than proven by an autograph.

### Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy transmitted the Weyer profile into nineteenth-century French demonology and added the *Grand Grimoire* hierarchy. Le Breton created the now-standard winged-dog portrait in 1863.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Mathers edited the text from British Library manuscripts. Crowley published it in 1904 with additional preliminary and interpretive material. The edition introduced or at least canonized “President and Earl” for Glasya-Labolas.

Crowley’s *777* then supplied the Sagittarius/day/Eight of Wands correspondence.

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding:** No primary evidence was found that Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, produced a distinct Glasya-Labolas teaching or operation. Modern websites that display “Frater Achad” beside key 25 usually combine several authors’ tarot arrangements in one database; this is not evidence that Achad originated the spirit’s attribution.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 *Lesser Key of Solomon* is the principal modern critical edition used here. His online transcription identifies Sloane 3825 as the base, records variants, compares Weyer/Scot, and separates manuscript wording from Mathers’s alterations. The printed entry is on p. 18; Weyer’s catalogue appears in the appendices.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

*The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007) transcribes Harley 6483 and related material, emphasizes the double-sided angel/demon seals, and reconstructs the manuscript’s provenance and practical method.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* presents a modern operant Solomonic system. Its Glasya table gives:

- 0°–4° Leo;
- 23–27 July;
- President and Earl;
- Mercury and Mars;
- ruler Goap;
- Shem angel Nithhaiah;
- archangel Michael.

This is an influential modern synthesis, not the unaltered astrology of *777*.

### Lon Milo DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* (1992) helped popularize practical, psychological, and Thelemic readings of the spirits. Its astrological framing follows the Crowley/Golden Dawn decan material rather than proving ancient zodiacal identities.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica* and related writing are important to modern attempts to reconnect Goetic magic with earlier Mediterranean and grimoire traditions.

**Negative finding:** No independently documented ancient identity for Glasya-Labolas was located in the item-specific material consulted. Invoking Stratton-Kent’s broader “goetic” historical model does not by itself establish an ancient deity behind this name.

### Owen Davies, Jean-Patrice Boudet, Julien Véronèse, and Jan Veenstra

These scholars provide historical context for grimoires, ritual magic, medieval demon catalogues, and manuscript transmission. Boudet’s edition and analysis are especially relevant to the *Livre des Esperitz*. Their work supports a long genealogy for the **catalogue genre**, but not an ancient or medieval attestation of the exact name Glasya-Labolas. [Davies, *Grimoires*](https://academic.oup.com/book/47570); [Boudet](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019)

## Modern religion, occultism, and experiential literature

### Demonolatry

Modern demonolatry books and websites add:

- “enns,” including forms such as *Elan tepar secore on ca Glasya-Labolas*;
- candle colors;
- herbs such as rosemary, wormwood, hemlock, or calamus;
- incense, gemstones, offerings, elemental directions;
- human visionary portraits;
- moral or psychological interpretations;
- claims of a father, family, spouse, or named subordinates.

**Modern invention:** These do not occur in Weyer, Scot, the *Goetia*, Rudd, de Plancy, or Lenain. Enns are a modern demonolatry practice, especially associated with S. Connolly’s publications, not a recovered Goetic language.

Claims that Asmodeus is Glasya-Labolas’s father, that souls are imprisoned in paintings, or that the seal encodes a biography have no verified early source.

### Modern magical memoir

Contemporary anthologies contain first-person accounts of conjuring Glasya-Labolas, including prison or personal-work narratives. These are evidence of modern reception and belief, not independent confirmation of historical offices or metaphysical existence.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Is Gloolas the same spirit?

**Evidence for:**

- near-identical winged-dog form;
- murderers;
- past/present/future knowledge;
- friends/enemies;
- invisibility;
- nearly adjacent catalogue context;
- Peterson’s explicit cross-reference.

**Evidence against exact identity:**

- name differs;
- rank differs;
- legion count differs;
- Folger manuscript is not securely earlier than Weyer;
- no surviving intermediate copy shows *Gloolas* changing into *Glasya Labolas*.

**Conclusion:** Highly probable textual cognate; exact genealogical direction unknown.

### 2. Is Carmola the medieval original?

Only list position and partial offices support the idea. The names are not close, and no consulted scholarly edition equates them.

**Conclusion:** Speculative and presently unproved.

### 3. Does the name conceal an ancient deity?

No ancient inscription, classical literary occurrence, Hebrew divine name, or securely reconstructed Indo-European or Semitic form has been found.

**Conclusion:** No evidence for an ancient deity, hero, or place behind the name. Modern etymologies are conjectural.

### 4. President or President and Earl?

- Weyer: President.
- Scot: President.
- Sloane 3825: President.
- Sloane 2731: President.
- Harley 6483: President.
- de Plancy: President.
- Mathers–Crowley: President and Earl.

**Conclusion:** President is the secure early rank. Earl is a 1904 or immediately pre-1904 editorial addition in the checked chain.

### 5. Mercury or Mars?

- Manuscript President → Mercury.
- Manuscript Earl recipe → copper and silver.
- Mathers’s reconstructed Earl → Mars/iron.
- Modern dual-rank tables → Mercury and Mars.

**Conclusion:** Mercury is historically primary for Glasya. Mars arises from Mathers’s dual rank and correction of the missing planetary metal.

### 6. Leader of murderers or author of murder?

The wording evolves:

1. Folger: chief leader of murderers.
2. Weyer: *dux omnium homicidarum*.
3. Scot: captain of all manslayers.
4. *Goetia*: author of bloodshed and manslaughter.
5. de Plancy: inspires homicides.
6. Modern sources: causes, incites, or personally commits murder.

The final three are interpretive expansions. Weyer’s language designates command or leadership; it does not literally say that the spirit created murder.

### 7. Present/future or past/future?

- Weyer and Scot: present and future.
- Folger Gloolas: past, present, future.
- Sloane *Goetia*: past and future.
- Harley: past, present, future.
- Mathers: past and future.

This is a genuine textual variation, not a stable single reading.

### 8. “Love of friends and foes”

Weyer’s *animos demeretur* suggests winning over, obliging, or gaining the dispositions of both groups. Scot renders “minds and love.” The *Goetia* turns this into causing love.

Possible readings include:

- gaining their favor toward the magician;
- reconciliation;
- inducing affection;
- causing friends and enemies to love one another.

Only the first is close to the Latin. The others are later interpretive possibilities.

### 9. Does the seal compel the spirit automatically?

The *Goetia* says the seal is to be worn as a lamen and sometimes says spirits owe obedience to their seals. Rudd adds divine and angelic controls.

**Tradition:** Practitioners treat the seal as a magical link or token of authority.

**Historical finding:** Nothing in the sources independently demonstrates efficacy; the claim belongs to the ritual system.

### 10. Is Nithhaiah the spirit’s eternal opposite?

Rudd pairs angel and demon by corresponding list number.

**Documented:** The controlling pairing is real in the Rudd manuscript tradition.

**Not documented:** An ancient cosmic battle, personal enmity, shared origin story, or psychological polarity between Nithhaiah and Glasya.

### 11. Which zodiacal degrees are correct?

There are at least three systems:

- Lenain/Aries-zero: Nithhaiah = 0°–5° Leo.
- Golden Dawn/Regulus-zero: Nethhiah = 0°–5° Sagittarius.
- Crowley *777*: Glasya = daytime spirit of the full 0°–10° Sagittarius decan.

None is a simple copying error; they arise from different starting points and purposes. Modern tables often combine them without disclosure.

### 12. “Five of Wands” versus “Eight of Wands”

- Five of Wands belongs to the Aries-zero Leo assignment.
- Eight of Wands belongs to the Golden Dawn/*777* Sagittarius assignment.
- The exact Crowley attribution for Glasya-Labolas is **Eight of Wands**, shared with nighttime Zagan.

### 13. Caacrinolaas as a separate demon

Weyer says *alias*. Scot repeats it. De Plancy treats Caacrinolaas, Caassimolar, and Glassialabolas as names of the same being.

**Conclusion:** Treating Caacrinolaas as a wholly separate early spirit is unsupported. Separate profiles in modern databases are cataloguing or fictional inventions.

### 14. Nebiros, Naberus, or Naberius?

De Plancy says the *Grand Grimoire* makes Classyalabolas a mount for **Nébiros or Naberus**. Similar spelling encourages confusion with Goetic Naberius/Cerberus, spirit 24.

In the *Grand Grimoire* hierarchy, Nebiros is an infernal officer distinct from the Goetic list position. Later summaries variously call Glasya:

- Nebiros’s mount;
- Naberus’s mount;
- Naberius’s servant;
- direct subordinate of Nebiros.

**Conclusion:** This is a later hierarchy layered over Weyer. The exact Nebiros/Naberus wording should not be used to merge Glasya-Labolas with Naberius.

### 15. Misprints that became names

Examples include:

- Harley’s word separation **Glasya-la bolas**;
- **Glasialabolas**;
- de Plancy’s **Glassialabolas**;
- *Grand Grimoire* **Classyalabolas**;
- Waite’s **Caassimola**;
- Shah’s **Caasimola**;
- web forms **Glacialabolas**, **Glassia-labolis**, and **Bassimolar Glasya**.

Some may reflect real lost exemplars, but most late forms can be explained more economically by scribal, typographical, OCR, transliteration, or translation drift.

## Open questions

1. **What did Weyer’s lost *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* actually read?**  
   Its recovery could show whether *Glasya Labolas*, *Gloolas*, or another form is earlier.

2. **Is Gloolas the direct ancestor of Glasya-Labolas?**  
   The functional identity is strong; the direction and stages of corruption are unknown.

3. **Does Carmola preserve part of the same medieval entry?**  
   A fresh comparison of the Trinity manuscript, not only modern transcription, is needed.

4. **Why did “Earl” enter Mathers’s text?**  
   It could be an unnoticed manuscript reading, a classification error, or an editorial attempt to harmonize planetary and decanic material. No checked manuscript supports it.

5. **How exactly do individual seals differ among Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, and Harley 6483?**  
   A full paleographic plate comparing high-resolution originals would distinguish meaningful line changes from penmanship.

6. **Did Rudd himself devise the angel–demon pairing?**  
   Harley 6483 proves the scheme’s existence in Peter Smart’s copy, but the exact author and date of the lost exemplar remain debated.

7. **Which calendar did Lenain intend for the five presidency dates?**  
   Later writers shift dates by one or two days through different zodiacal, civil-day, and sunrise conventions.

8. **Can any exact pre-1577 name be found?**  
   At present, the answer is no. Genre ancestors exist; a pre-Weyer Glasya-Labolas does not.

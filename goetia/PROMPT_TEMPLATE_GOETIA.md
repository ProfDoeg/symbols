[NAME]
Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this spirit of the Ars
Goetia, the first book of the Lesser Key of Solomon, together with the angel of the Shem
HaMephorash that the tradition sets over it. I want the fullest reconstructable history,
from the earliest attestation of the name to the present, chronologically and in detail.
Include, wherever available:

- THE NAMES: every spelling across the source chain (Weyer's Pseudomonarchia Daemonum 1577,
  Reginald Scot's Discoverie of Witchcraft 1584, the Sloane manuscripts 2731 and 3825, Harley
  6483 with Rudd's material, the Mathers-Crowley Goetia of 1904, Peterson's 2001 edition);
  the etymology and the ancient god, hero or place behind the name where one exists (Baal,
  Astarte, Amun, Ashmedai, Beliya'al, Phoenix, the Greek and Latin words); the number in the
  list and how the order differs between Weyer and the Goetia (Weyer's 69, the Goetia's 72,
  the four added and the omitted)
- THE DESCRIPTION, VERBATIM: rank (king, duke, prince, marquis, earl/count, president,
  knight) and the metal and planet the rank implies; the number of legions; the appearance
  on conjuration and the second form the conjurer may demand; the offices (what the spirit
  teaches, gives, does: languages, sciences, treasures, love, familiars, invisibility,
  transmutation, the answer to questions); the hour or time of day; the seal, described
  exactly, with its variants across manuscripts; quote Weyer (Latin and Scot's English) and
  the Goetia in full
- THE ANGEL: the Shem HaMephorash angel paired with this spirit in Rudd's scheme (the 72
  names derived from Exodus 14:19-21, three verses of 72 letters), its name, meaning, its
  five degrees of the zodiac, its psalm verse, its tradition from the Sefer Raziel and the
  kabbalists through Kircher, Lenain (La science cabalistique 1823), Ambelain, Papus, the
  Golden Dawn and Haziel; how Rudd's manuscript (Harley 6483) uses the angel to constrain the
  spirit; Skinner and Rankine's Goetia of Dr Rudd (2007)
- CORRESPONDENCES: Crowley's 777 and the Golden Dawn: the quinance (five degrees) of the
  zodiac, day or night, the decan and its tarot pip card (so that each pip carries two
  spirits), the planet of the rank; the Enochian and other later overlays; astrological
  timing of conjuration in the tradition
- ICONOGRAPHY: Collin de Plancy's Dictionnaire Infernal (1818; the 1863 edition with Louis
  Breton's engravings) and its portrait of this spirit if it has one, quoted and described;
  the seals in the manuscripts; modern art
- PEOPLE AND WORKS: magicians and scholars who worked with or wrote on this spirit (Weyer,
  Scot, Rudd, Mathers, Crowley, Frater Achad?, Poke Runyon, Lon Milo DuQuette, Stephen
  Skinner, Joseph Peterson, Jake Stratton-Kent); grimoire ancestors (the Livre des
  Esperitz, the Munich handbook, the Liber Officiorum Spirituum, the Book of Abramelin's lists,
  Solomon's demons in the Testament of Solomon); the spirit in literature, film and games
  (Milton, Dante if applicable, the Bartimaeus novels, the Shin Megami Tensei and Persona
  series, Helluva Boss, Hellboy, and so on), each attested
- CONTROVERSIES: disputed etymologies, the order and number question, misprints that
  became names, conflations of two spirits, whether the spirit appears in any pre-Weyer
  source at all

Include the good and the bad without judgment, and legend as fully as fact.
For every significant claim distinguish documented text, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, esoteric correspondence, disputed claim, legend and modern invention, and say
which sources carry it, when the reading first appears, who spread it, and what evidence
exists. Do not use fact-checking summaries as substitutes for the sources; trace to the
texts (Peterson's esotericarchives.com carries Weyer, Scot and the Goetia in full; the
sacred-texts and hermetic.com editions; archive.org for de Plancy and Lenain).

Structure the dossier as: Basic identification (name, number, rank, legions, seal, angel,
quinance, tarot pip); The name and its origins; The description across the sources (verbatim,

# Dantalion: Research Dossier

*Research current to 14 September 2026.*

## Evidentiary key

The following labels distinguish kinds of claims throughout:

- **[Documented text]** — stated or pictured in a named primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — an inference from manuscripts, textual comparison, palaeography, or historical context.
- **[Tradition]** — an attribution repeated within an occult or religious transmission, without necessarily being historically demonstrable.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a deliberately constructed magical, astrological, tarot, or Qabalistic attribution.
- **[Disputed]** — a claim for which competent sources disagree or the surviving evidence is insufficient.
- **[Legend]** — a narrative presented within the grimoire tradition rather than as verifiable history.
- **[Modern invention]** — a recent literary, artistic, gaming, devotional, or demonolatry development absent from the early sources.

# The name and its origins

## The documentary spelling chain

Joseph Peterson's manuscript comparison supplies the clearest synoptic evidence for the spellings. [His complete comparison table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf) reads as follows for spirit 71:

| Source | Date | Reading | Status |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Johann Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* | 1577, fifth edition | **Absent** | No Weyer lemma or number |
| Reginald Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft* | 1584 | **Absent** | No English description |
| Sloane MS 3824, fol. 116r | seventeenth century | **Dantalion** | Probable proto-*Goetia* entry |
| Sloane MS 3825, fol. 110v | seventeenth century | **Dantalion** | Peterson's base manuscript |
| Sloane MS 3648 | seventeenth century | **Dantalion** | Same principal spelling |
| Sloane MS 2731 | probably late seventeenth century | **Dantalion** | Same principal spelling |
| Harley MS 6483 | copied 1712–13 | **Dantaylion** | Rudd-associated recension |
| Northwestern University MS 65 | later witness | **Dantalion** | Same principal spelling |
| Mathers–Crowley | 1904 | **Dantalion** | Standard modern form |
| A. E. Waite | 1913 | **Dantalian** | Later printed variant |
| Idries Shah | twentieth century | **Dantalian** | Derived secondary form |
| Wellcome MS 3203 | uncertain | **Dantation**, numbered 52 | Corrupt/reordered witness |
| Peterson print edition | 2001 | **Dantalion** | Sloane 3825 reading restored |

**[Documented text]** A separate love-magic text in Sloane MS 3851 apparently uses **Dentalion**. David Rankine also identifies this spelling in the Arthur Gauntlet grimoire. Because the text invokes several infernal names together, its identity with Dantalion is probable rather than certain.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Sloane 3824 is the earliest presently located source containing an unmistakable Dantalion office and character. The British Library dates its relevant magical materials broadly to the seventeenth century. A formerly repeated date of 1649 has been challenged because parts of the compilation depend on material probably copied later; consequently “mid-seventeenth century” is a prudent approximation, not an exact first-attestation date. See the [British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002116214) and the [textual discussion by the Celephaïs Press editor](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/07/lack-of-progress-report.html).

## Is there an ancient deity behind Dantalion?

**[Finding: absence of evidence]** No demonstrable ancient god, hero, place-name, Greek word, Latin word, biblical figure, or pre-Christian cult lies behind the name in the way that Astaroth can be related to Astarte, Bael to *baʿal*, or Asmoday to Ashmedai/Aēšma.

No source examined produced:

- a Greek or Latin lemma *Dantalion*;
- a Hebrew or Aramaic name that naturally yields *Dantalion*;
- an ancient inscription or mythological catalogue containing it;
- a pre-Weyer demonological occurrence;
- or even a Weyer occurrence.

The artificial Hebrew spellings in Rudd and Crowley are therefore **back-transliterations of the European name**, not evidence of Hebrew origin. Crowley himself warned that the Hebrew spellings copied from the “Rudd” codex were often orthographically wrong; the 1904 volume prints Dantalion in a form equivalent to a letter-by-letter transliteration. See the [1904 edition, pp. 37–38](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf).

## Proposed etymologies

### Tantalus

**[Disputed modern hypothesis]** The resemblance between *Dantalion* and Greek **Tantalos**, Tantalus, has encouraged a proposed connection. Tantalus is the Greek mythic king punished with unfulfilled hunger and thirst; the etymology of *Tantalos* itself is uncertain. No manuscript supplies an intermediate form linking him to Dantalion.

The hypothesis is therefore phonetic speculation. It does not explain:

- the initial *D-*;
- the internal *-li-*;
- Dantalion's book, faces, or mental offices;
- or why the name enters an English magical manuscript only in the seventeenth century.

### Tantavalerion or Golgathell

**[Documented text]** **Tantavalerion vel Golgathell** is a real early-modern spirit name, but it occurs independently in the *Offices of Spirits* tradition and in the Folger manuscript commonly called the *Book of Oberon*. There it is styled emperor of infernal or “all” spirits. The [Folger V.b.26 transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf) records “Tantavalerion, vel golgathell” in coercive conjurations.

**[Disputed modern conflation]** David Crowhurst's *Stellas Daemonum* has encouraged an identification of Dantalion with Tantavalerion. The argument appears to rest on:

- loose phonetic resemblance;
- the shared ending *-ion*;
- Dantalion's otherwise obscure origin;
- and perceived visual similarities between sigils.

Against it stand the independent spellings, different ranks, different offices, and lack of a manuscript equating the two. The Folger text knows Tantavalerion but does not call it Dantalion. Sloane 3824 knows Dantalion but does not identify him with Tantavalerion. The equation should therefore be treated as a **twenty-first-century speculative reconstruction**, not a recovered medieval identity. [Peterson's name index](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/nameindx.htm) keeps the names separate.

### “Greek *tantalio*, uncountable”

**[Modern invention]** Internet posts have claimed that a Greek word *tantalio* means “uncountable,” supposedly connecting Dantalion with multitudes or Hades. No standard ancient Greek lemma or historical dictionary was found to support this. The claim appears in modern demonolatry discussion, not in a classical source.

### Celtic “turbulent,” *Dan* plus *El*, “lion of God,” Dante, and dandelion

**[Modern invention]** These are folk etymologies. They are unsupported by the manuscript spellings or historical linguistics. In particular:

- *-el* is not consistently present as a separable Hebrew suffix in the European name;
- “Danta-lion” is not a meaningful Hebrew compound;
- **Dante** has no documentary connection;
- and French *dent-de-lion*/*dandelion* is merely an accidental sound resemblance.

### The defensible conclusion

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Dantalion is probably a transmitted, corrupted, or newly coined spirit-name belonging to a seventeenth-century English *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* environment. The evidence does not presently allow reconstruction beyond that.

# The description across the sources

## Before Dantalion: the Weyer–Scot problem

### Weyer, 1577

**[Documented text]** Dantalion does not occur anywhere in the 69-spirit *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*. There is therefore no Dantalion description in Latin to quote. Any website that attributes the familiar many-faced description to Weyer is wrong.

Weyer's generic rule for the relevant rank does exist:

> “Item Duces à prima usque ad meridiem: & observatur cœlum clarum.”

That is, dukes are constrained from the first hour until noon, with clear weather observed. See [Weyer's Latin and Peterson's parallel translation](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Scot, 1584

**[Documented text]** Scot likewise has no Dantalion entry. His English rendering of the generic rule reads:

> “Dukes may be hound from the first houre till noone; and cleare wether is to be observed.”

The apparent “hound” is the printed/transcribed reading where modern editions normally understand “bound.” See [Peterson's Weyer–Scot parallel text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Thus:

- “Weyer's Latin description of Dantalion”: **none exists**.
- “Scot's English description of Dantalion”: **none exists**.
- Their only directly relevant text is the general timing rule for dukes, applied later when Dantalion was classified as one.

## Sloane MS 3824: the proto-Goetic notice

**[Documented text]** Folio 116r contains a short heading, a very small character, and an office. Its opening is:

> “Of the spirit Dantalion His Character”

The remainder attributes all of the following:

- showing or teaching arts and sciences and making the operator expert;
- declaring and revealing secret counsels;
- changing the minds and thoughts of men and women;
- stirring up love;
- and showing by vision the likeness of a person, however distant.

[Peterson transcribes the complete notice in his note to spirit 71](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#dantalion).

Notably absent are:

- rank;
- legions;
- physical appearance;
- the many faces;
- the book;
- any second form;
- any controlling king or direction;
- and a specific hour.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This compact office-list is probably the immediate ancestor, or a close collateral relative, of the expanded *Goetia* entry. The same folios contain brief notices for Seere and Andromalius, the other two spirits placed at the end of the later 72-spirit catalogue. [A manuscript study describes their characters as only about the height of a line of writing](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/06/meddling-with-goetia-7.html).

## Sloane MS 3825: the full manuscript entry

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3825, fol. 110v, calls Dantalion a great and mighty duke and begins the appearance with:

> “appearing in ye forme of a Man wth many faces”

Its complete assertion-set is:

1. he is spirit 71;
2. he is a great and mighty duke;
3. he appears as a man;
4. he has many faces resembling men and women;
5. he carries a book in his right hand;
6. he teaches every art and science;
7. he declares any person's secret counsels;
8. he knows the thoughts of all men and women;
9. he can change those thoughts at will;
10. he can cause love;
11. he can show, by vision, the true likeness of anyone anywhere in the world;
12. he governs 36 legions;
13. and his seal is to be worn as a lamen.

The full diplomatic transcription and image are at [Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#dantalion). In Peterson's 2001 print edition the entry falls on pp. 38–39.

### What the text does not say

**[Finding: absence of evidence]**

- It does not call the book a “grimoire,” “Book of Souls,” or “forbidden library.”
- It does not call Dantalion an archduke.
- It gives no named superior, element, color, incense, stone, enn, gender-fluid theology, or moral alignment.
- It does not state that the faces rotate, speak separately, wear masks, or represent every human who has lived.
- It does not say he gives familiars, treasure, invisibility, transmutation, languages, or general prophecy.
- It gives no requested second shape. This is important because other Goetic entries explicitly say that the conjurer may command a spirit into human form. Dantalion has no such second-form clause.
- “Secret counsels” and knowledge of thoughts are the closest thing to answering questions, but the entry does not contain the standard phrase “giveth true answers.”

## Sloane MS 2731

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731 reads **Dantalion** at number 71. Peterson's comparison records no name variant. Its drawings are generally careful and it shares a common textual ancestor with Sloane 3825; it should not be assumed that either surviving manuscript was copied directly from the other. See Peterson's [Lemegeton manuscript introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm) and [spirit-name table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

## Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483 reads **Dantaylion**. In Skinner and Rankine's transcription, pp. 172–73, he remains a great and mighty duke, with the many male and female faces, right-hand book, arts and sciences, love, distant likeness, and 36 legions.

A significant textual difference is that this entry **omits the Sloane 3825 sentence about secret counsels, universal thought-knowledge, and changing thoughts**. Its surviving wording moves directly from teaching arts and sciences to causing love and showing likenesses. See [the searchable transcription of the Rudd edition](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

**[Documented text]** The Rudd form also attaches Haiaiel's Psalm:

> “Confitebor Domino nimis in ore meo, et in medio multorum laudabo eum.”

The edition translates this as praising or confessing the Lord publicly; it is Psalm 108:29 in Vulgate numbering.

## Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**[Documented text]** The 1904 edition standardizes spelling and capitalization. Its opening reads:

> “The Seventy-first Spirit is Dantalion. He is a Duke Great and Mighty”

The entry occupies p. 34 of the original pagination. Its complete content reproduces the Sloane-type account: many male and female countenances, book in the right hand, arts and sciences, secret counsels, knowledge and alteration of thoughts, love, distant visionary likenesses, 36 legions, and the seal. The complete facsimile text is at [pp. 34–35 of the 1904 edition](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf); a clean public-domain transcription is also at [Sacred Texts](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Mathers prepared the underlying edition from British Museum manuscripts; Crowley states that little remained for him beyond publication, proofing, notes, and ancillary “Angelic” material. The exact Mathers working manuscript is not extant, so every editorial choice cannot be reconstructed.

**[Esoteric interpretation]** Crowley's introductory essay gives a psychological model: the spirits are “portions of the human brain,” while seals are visual means of stimulating or regulating those portions. This is Crowley's interpretation, not a statement in Sloane 3824, 3825, 2731, or Harley 6483. See [the 1904 introductory essay](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf).

## Peterson, 2001

**[Documented text/editorial scholarship]** Joseph H. Peterson returned to Sloane 3825 as his base text, preserved its early-modern spelling, supplied folio references, and collated Sloane, Harley, and other manuscripts. His footnote establishes:

- Harley: **Dantaylion**;
- no Weyer or Scot entry;
- and the Sloane 3824 proto-entry.

The 2001 Weiser edition is therefore not a new Dantalion mythology but a critical restoration of the manuscript wording. Peterson's continuously updated [digital edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#dantalion) makes the source relations unusually transparent.

# Number, order, and the 69/72 question

**[Documented text]** Weyer's 1577 catalogue has 69 entries, although its Belial legend speaks of 72 kings imprisoned by Solomon. The *Ars Goetia* has 72.

The arithmetic is:

- Weyer: 69;
- remove Weyer number 4, **Pruflas**, also called **Bufas/Busas**;
- add **Vassago**, **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius**;
- resulting total: 72.

The four Goetic additions are:

| Goetia number | Spirit | In Weyer? |
|---:|---|---|
| 3 | Vassago | No |
| 70 | Seere | No |
| 71 | Dantalion | No |
| 72 | Andromalius | No |

**[Documented text]** Scot accidentally or deliberately omitted Pruflas as well. The *Goetia* also rearranged much of Weyer's order, rather than merely inserting four names. Peterson suggests that loose leaves or a differently ordered exemplar might partly explain the scrambling, but no definitive mechanism survives. See the [complete comparison table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The choice of 72 was probably encouraged by the symbolic prestige of the 72-fold divine name and 72 angels. It does not follow that the anonymous compiler originally supplied a one-to-one angelic control table. That explicit construction survives most clearly in the Rudd-associated recension.

# Rank, planet, metal, and time

## Duke: Venus and copper

**[Documented text]** The *Goetia*'s classified rank list specifies copper for the seals of dukes. Mathers and Crowley make the planetary logic explicit:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince/prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury or mixed metal |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| Earl/count | Venus and Moon in the manuscript; Crowley proposes Mars | Copper and silver; later iron |

Dantalion's certain rank correspondence is therefore **Venus–copper**. It does not arise from his love office alone; it follows from his title as duke. See the [1904 classified list](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf) and [Peterson's manuscript transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#observations).

## Hours and lunar conditions

**[Documented text]** The generic rule says dukes may be constrained from sunrise until noon in clear weather. No separate Dantalion hour is given.

The manuscript's lunar observation recommends operations when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old and says other days are unprofitable. This too is a general *Goetia* rubric, not Dantalion's personal instruction.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Later practice often adds Friday or the planetary hour of Venus because Dantalion is a duke. That is a reasonable planetary elaboration, but it is not the wording of his seventeenth-century entry.

**[Important distinction]** Crowley's designation “by night” means Dantalion occupies the *night-spirit column* of the astrological decan table. It does not repeal the earlier instruction that dukes are constrained in the morning. These are separate classification systems.

# The seal

## What survives

**[Documented text]**

1. **Sloane 3824, fol. 116r:** a very small, line-height character accompanying the brief office.
2. **Sloane 3825:** an enlarged and more elaborate character used by Peterson.
3. **Sloane 2731 and related manuscripts:** substantially corresponding figures, with differences in curve, crossing, proportion, and terminal placement.
4. **Harley 6483:** a Rudd-recension form. Crowley complained broadly that this codex contained errors “particularly in the Sigils,” but did not demonstrate that every difference was necessarily corrupt.
5. **Mathers–Crowley 1904:** the now-standard printed black-line seal.

The standard printed form is best described as a wide, nearly bilateral linear glyph: a peaked or shallow angular upper line extends toward looped/circular terminals, while strokes interlace around a central axis and descend into a lower hook or loop. Because the manuscript distinctions consist in exact pen-strokes rather than describable heraldic elements, the images themselves are the controlling evidence.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The Sloane 3824 character establishes that the Dantalion seal was not simply invented for the 1904 edition. Its tiny form also suggests that the larger *Lemegeton* seals may have been elaborated while copying or redrawing compact working characters. The [Sloane 3824/Mathers comparison](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/06/meddling-with-goetia-7.html) is especially useful.

**[Disputed]** Modern claims that the seal is mathematically derived from a Mercury kamea, or that resemblance to the “Ballaton” character proves identity with Tantavalerion/Lucifer, have no demonstrated manuscript derivation. Visual resemblance by itself does not establish identity.

# The angel: Haiaiel

## Identification

| Field | Haiaiel |
|---|---|
| Number | 71 |
| Hebrew full form | הייאל |
| Root triplet | היי — H-Y-Y |
| Common spellings | Haiaiel, Hayaiel, Haiayel, Hahayel, Hayiel |
| Conventional title | “God, Master/Lord of the Universe” |
| Choir in later tables | Angels |
| Archangelic superior | Gabriel |
| Quinance | 20°–25° Pisces |
| Common civil dates | 11–15 March |
| Psalm | Vulgate 108:29 / Hebrew–KJV 109:30 |
| Rudd-paired spirit | Dantaylion/Dantalion |

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483's pairing is number-for-number: angel 71, Haiaiel, constrains spirit 71, Dantaylion. Skinner and Rankine's edition explains that the angel name and Psalm are put in operative relation to the demonic character. The publisher describes the system explicitly as using the 72 angels to bind the 72 demons. See [Llewellyn's edition description](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556).

## How the 72 names are formed

**[Jewish textual tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the received Hebrew text, of three consecutive verses of 72 letters each. The customary construction writes:

1. verse 19 in its ordinary direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its ordinary direction;

then reads the 72 vertical columns, yielding 72 triplets. This alternating or boustrophedonic method produces the triplet היי at position 71. The biblical verses themselves do **not** print a list of angels. The list is a later exegetical construction. See [Exodus 14:19–21 at Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Kabbalah) and [Chabad's explanation of the construction](https://www.chabad.org/kabbalah/article_cdo/aid/1388270/jewish/72-Names-of-G-d.htm).

**[Historical clarification]** *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally meant the “explicit,” “distinguished,” or fully pronounced divine name—principally the Tetragrammaton—in Tannaitic usage. Only in later mystical and magical discourse was the term applied broadly to 42- and 72-fold names. See the [Jewish Encyclopedia's historical article](https://jewishencyclopediadev.sefaria.org/articles/13542-shem-ha-meforash).

## From triplets to angels

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The historical layers should be kept separate:

1. **Biblical text:** three 72-letter verses.
2. **Jewish mystical exegesis:** extraction of a 72-fold divine name.
3. **Medieval Jewish magic and Kabbalah:** ritual and contemplative use of the triplets, represented in texts associated with the *Bahir*, *Sefer Raziel*, and later Kabbalists.
4. **Christian Kabbalah:** addition of divine suffixes such as *-el* or *-yah* so the triplets become pronounceable angel names.
5. **Rudd tradition:** pairing those 72 angels with the 72 Goetic spirits by number.
6. **French occultism:** detailed zodiacal, calendrical, moral, Psalmic, and guardian-angel profiles.
7. **Modern demonolatry:** “light/shadow counterparts,” enns, pathworkings, and individualized spirit relationships.

### *Sefer Raziel*

**[Tradition with historical qualification]** The *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is a medieval Jewish magical compilation, not a single book dictated in biblical times by the angel Raziel. It preserves important material concerning divine names, angelology, amulets, and the 72-fold name. The familiar printed Hebrew recension appeared at Amsterdam in 1701, although component texts are older.

The presence of the 72-fold name in the *Raziel* tradition does not prove that the complete later French biography of “Haiaiel” was already present there.

### Reuchlin

**[Documented Christian-Kabbalistic development]** Johannes Reuchlin's *De Arte Cabalistica* (1517) is an important printed witness to treating the 72 triplets, expanded with *-el* or *-yah*, as angelic names. This is a decisive bridge from Jewish divine-name speculation to Christian angelic catalogues. See Matthew Morgenstern's study, [*72 in His Name*](https://e-univers.ru/upload/iblock/919/1d14cc5s1qdrw3vc1f1360nmgkaohtru.pdf).

### Kircher

**[Documented text]** Athanasius Kircher's *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54) presented elaborate diagrams of the 72 names and angels. Kircher followed Kabbalistic sources including material associated with the *Bahir* and Moses Cordovero but recast them within a Christian universal history. His treatment predates Harley 6483 and belongs to the intellectual environment from which Rudd's system emerged. See [Kircher's *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm) and his [72-name illustration](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg).

### Rudd and Harley 6483

**[Documented text]** The controlling principle is not merely “good angel opposite bad demon.” The angel's name and Psalm are incorporated into the ritual instruments so that the conjurer approaches the spirit under an explicitly divine and angelic authority.

Skinner and Rankine describe seals or lamens with the demonic character on one side and the corresponding angelic name and biblical verse on the other. This resembles the late-antique *Testament of Solomon* pattern in which knowledge of a demon's thwarting angel gives power over it.

**[Disputed attribution]** Harley 6483 was copied in 1712–13, decades after Thomas Rudd's death in 1656. Skinner and Rankine attribute its distinctive angelic material to Rudd or his magical collection. “The Rudd tradition” is therefore safer than calling the surviving manuscript Rudd's autograph.

### Lenain, 1823

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** Lazare Républicain Lenain's *La science cabalistique* systematized the angels in a form influential on French occultism. Haiaiel is number 71 and is glossed:

> “Dieu maître de l'Univers”

Lenain assigns:

- the zodiacal arc 351–355 in his inclusive numbering, conventionally 20°–25° Pisces;
- Psalm 108:29;
- protection from wicked people and oppressors;
- victory and peace;
- bravery;
- influence over iron, arsenals, fortresses, and military affairs;
- and, for the contrary genius, discord, treachery, and criminal celebrity.

The [1823 manuscript/facsimile](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365) is preserved by the Bibliothèque interuniversitaire de santé.

**[Important calendrical distinction]** Lenain's system includes five recurring annual dates distributed through the year, as well as a zodiacal sector and a daily twenty-minute interval. Later angel books frequently turn the zodiacal sector into one continuous birthday span, 11–15 March. Those are related but not identical calendrical techniques.

### Papus

**[Documented transmission]** Papus—Gérard Encausse—reprinted and promoted Lenain's material in the French occult revival. His enlarged *La Cabbale* and the later Lenain reprint helped move the catalogue into Martinist and Rosicrucian environments. A digitized edition is available from the [Warburg Institute](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/abg405b2732165.pdf).

### Golden Dawn and Crowley

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The Golden Dawn supplied the broader tarot, decan, color, planetary, and Qabalistic framework later attached to both spirits and angels. Its *Book T* places the numbered Minor Arcana in the 36 zodiacal decans.

The Golden Dawn did not originate Haiaiel's Hebrew triplet or Rudd's pairing. Nor should the Golden Dawn decan table be confused with the 72 five-degree angelic sectors.

### Ambelain

**[Documented modern occult transmission]** Robert Ambelain's *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) expanded the French angelic system with seals, hierarchies, zodiacal periods, invocations, and practical ritual. It was a major source for later French-language “72 guardian angels” literature. See the [Open Library catalogue](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique).

### Haziel

**[Modern esoteric development]** “Haziel,” the pen name of François Bernad-Termès, popularized highly individualized guardian-angel profiles from the late twentieth century onward. His books turned older angelic tables into accessible devotional programs with prayers, personality descriptions, annual rulership dates, and life advice.

These profiles preserve parts of Lenain and Ambelain but often add substantial inspirational material. They should not be backdated to *Sefer Raziel* or the medieval Kabbalists. See the [Éditions Bussière author record](https://editions-bussiere.com/brand/33-haziel) and the [Dijon catalogue record for his 1990 book](https://archives.dijon.fr/detailstatic.aspx?RSC_BASE=SYRACUSE&RSC_DOCID=388331&TITLE=le-grand-livre-des-invocations-et-des-exhortations-prieres-adressees-aux-72-anges-servants-ou-genies&_lg=fr-FR).

## Meaning of Haiaiel

**[Tradition]** “God, Master of the Universe” is the standard Lenain/Papus/Ambelain gloss.

**[Philological caution]** It is not a literal translation of an ordinary Hebrew personal name. The triplet היי is mechanically extracted from Exodus. Adding אל, *El*, gives an angelic-looking form. Attempts to connect it independently with Hebrew *ḥayy* or *ḥayyah*, “living/life,” are possible symbolic readings but are not the historical basis of Lenain's French gloss.

## Psalm verse

**[Documented text]**

Latin:

> “Confitebor Domino nimis in ore meo, et in medio multorum laudabo eum.”

Vulgate: Psalm 108:29.

Modern English numbering, Psalm 109:30:

> “I will greatly praise the LORD with my mouth; yea, I will praise him among the multitude.”

The difference is purely one of Psalm numbering: the Septuagint/Vulgate sequence is offset from the Masoretic/KJV sequence in this part of the Psalter.

## Crowley's *777*: the correct entry

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** In columns CLV–CLXVI of *777*, the 72 spirits occupy the 36 decans twice:

- spirits 1–36 are the day series;
- spirits 37–72 are the night series;
- each ten-degree decan and tarot pip therefore has two spirits.

For Pisces:

| Decan | Tarot | Day spirit | Night spirit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0°–10° Pisces | Eight of Cups | Furfur, no. 34 | Seere, no. 70 |
| **10°–20° Pisces** | **Nine of Cups** | **Marchosias, no. 35** | **Dantalion, no. 71** |
| 20°–30° Pisces | Ten of Cups | Stolas, no. 36 | Andromalius, no. 72 |

Thus the requested *777* answer is unequivocally:

- **Dantalion: 10°–20° Pisces**
- **by night**
- **Nine of Cups**
- **Jupiter in Pisces**
- paired on that pip with **Marchosias**, the day spirit.

This is visible in Crowley's [original *777* tables](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/0777-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf) and in the decan appendix to a [modern scholarly re-setting of the Goetia](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/The-Goetia-of-the-Lemegeton-of-Solomon-the-King-ed.-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf).

**[Documented later presentation]** DuQuette, Hyatt, and Wilson preserve the same attribution in *Aleister Crowley's Illustrated Goetia*: “10°–20° Pisces (night),” March 1–10, Nine of Cups, Venus–copper. See [pp. 216–17](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf).

## Why some tables say Ten of Cups

**[Esoteric correspondence]** If the 72 demons are instead assigned sequentially to 72 five-degree sectors, spirit 71 lands at 20°–25° Pisces. That lies in the third decan, corresponding to the Ten of Cups. Poke Runyon uses this construction in *The Book of Solomon's Magick* and adds:

- Haiaiel;
- Gabriel;
- Corson/West;
- 20°–24° Pisces;
- and March 11–15.

This is an internally coherent later synthesis, but it is not the arrangement printed in *777*. Runyon discusses his broader angel–demon integration in [his edition description](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

## Enochian overlays

**[Documented editorial fact]** Crowley's 1904 book includes “Angelic” or Enochian-character material in its prefatory apparatus. Crowley says he deciphered and transliterated it from a manuscript. This belongs to Crowley's publication framework, not Dantalion's seventeenth-century office.

**[Scholarly clarification]** The “Bornless” preliminary invocation is ultimately derived from a Graeco-Egyptian magical text, not from John Dee's angelic diaries. Its printing beside Enochian material helped later readers blend otherwise distinct systems.

**[Finding: absence of evidence]** No Dantalion occurs in Dee and Kelley's surviving angelic conversations. No historical manuscript assigns Dantalion an Enochian governor, a Watchtower square, or an Aethyr. Such correspondences are twentieth- or twenty-first-century table intersections.

## Other modern overlays

**[Modern invention]** The following are widely circulated but absent from the early manuscripts:

- enn: *Avage ayer Dantalion on ca*;
- blue, indigo, purple, green, or black as personal colors;
- sandalwood, jasmine, patchouli, or myrrh as personal incense;
- aquamarine, amethyst, sapphire, pearl, or lapis as stones;
- Water as an element;
- “Duke of Faces”;
- “Keeper of the Forbidden Library of Bibliotheca”;
- rotating faces, porcelain masks, or a library in Hell;
- psychological “shadow work” and therapeutic pathworkings;
- North, East, or West given as an absolute direction without identifying the particular later system.

Some are intelligible symbolic extrapolations from Pisces, Venus, the book, and the many faces. They are nevertheless later additions.

# Iconography

## Manuscript and printed imagery

**[Documented text]** Early sources give a verbal apparition and a seal, not a figurative portrait. The iconic elements are entirely verbal:

- human body;
- multiple faces;
- both men's and women's faces;
- book in the right hand.

The many faces and book first appear in the full *Lemegeton* description, not in the short Sloane 3824 office.

## Collin de Plancy

**[Finding: absence of evidence]** Dantalion has no entry in the 1818 *Dictionnaire infernal* and no entry or Louis Le Breton portrait in the illustrated sixth edition of 1863.

This is expected: de Plancy drew extensively on Weyer, and Dantalion is absent from Weyer. The alphabetical D section of the [1863 facsimile](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf) contains neither *Dantalion* nor *Dantalian*. The edition metadata identifies Louis Le Breton as illustrator and Henri Plon as publisher.

**[Modern misinformation]** Several commercial demonology sites reproduce the standard *Goetia* description in French or English and label it a de Plancy quotation. One even attaches generic Le Breton art to Dantalion. That attribution is false. There is nothing to quote from de Plancy for this spirit.

The [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_0) and [Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/item/63056292/) permit the negative finding to be checked.

## Modern art

**[Modern invention]** Most contemporary pictures literalize the *Goetia*:

- a robed scholar with a cluster, crown, wheel, or tower of faces;
- faces of different ages and genders;
- a large book or grimoire;
- library interiors;
- masks, eyes, mirrors, or pages representing thought;
- Venusian green or Piscean blue palettes.

Notable reinterpretations include:

- **Dungeons & Dragons, *Tome of Magic* (2006):** an enormous crowned head made from numerous faces, a great tome, and a star-filled book; this version grants thought-reading and other pact abilities. [The entry is on p. 28](https://dtdnd.neocities.org/books/player/Tome%20of%20Magic.pdf).
- **Megami Tensei:** stylized demon designs under the spelling **Dantalian**, retaining the Goetic identification. See the [Megami Tensei database](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Dantalian).
- **Makai Ōji: Devils and Realist:** a humanoid aristocratic Dantalion rather than a literal mass of faces.
- **Modern occult decks:** frequently combine the printed seal, a book, multiple masks, and Pisces/Venus imagery. These are artistic syntheses, not recovered manuscript portraits.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

**[Documented historical role]** Weyer printed the principal 69-spirit catalogue underlying most of the *Goetia*. His purpose was polemical: to expose the supposed follies of magicians while arguing against witch persecution. Dantalion is not in his list.

## Abraham Fleming and Reginald Scot

**[Documented historical role]** Scot printed an English translation of Weyer's catalogue in 1584, probably prepared by Abraham Fleming. Errors and interpretive substitutions entered through this translation and through later Scot editions. The *Goetia* compiler appears to have used Scot rather than translating Weyer directly. Dantalion remains absent.

## The anonymous Sloane compiler or compilers

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Dantalion first becomes visible in the milieu represented by Sloane 3824. The compilation brings together conjurations, treasure magic, crystal work, *Heptameron* material, and *Offices of Spirits*. No author identifies himself as Dantalion's inventor.

## Thomas Rudd

**[Tradition and disputed attribution]** Rudd is associated with a learned seventeenth-century angel-magic tradition. Harley 6483 and related manuscripts preserve the number-for-number Shem angel controls. Whether every passage is personally his is uncertain; the surviving copy is later.

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers

**[Documented historical role]** Mathers prepared the first printed English edition from British Museum manuscripts. The standardized Dantalion paragraph derives from his editorial work.

## Aleister Crowley

**[Documented historical role]** Crowley published Mathers's edition in 1904, added prefatory and interpretive material, and later placed Dantalion in the night half of the *777* decan table. His psychological interpretation strongly influenced modern practice.

## A. E. Waite

**[Documented transmission]** Waite used **Dantalian** in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic* (1913). This spelling subsequently spread through encyclopedias and popular demon lists.

## Frater Achad

**[Finding: absence of evidence]** No primary record was located of Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, carrying out a Dantalion operation or making a distinctive Dantalion attribution. His association with Crowley and Qabalistic number theory is not enough to include him as a documented Dantalion practitioner.

## Poke Runyon

**[Documented modern practice]** Carroll “Poke” Runyon's *The Book of Solomon's Magick* and film *The Magick of Solomon* combine ceremonial evocation, dark-mirror scrying, hypnosis, planetary and zodiacal timing, and angelic controls. His Dantalion table uses the sequential five-degree scheme—20°–24° Pisces, Haiaiel, Gabriel and the third decan—rather than Crowley's 10°–20° night-decan placement.

## Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson

**[Documented modern transmission]** Their *Illustrated Goetia* preserves the *777* decan assignment and gives a modern psychological/practical presentation. For Dantalion it prints Nine of Cups and Venus–copper.

## Joseph H. Peterson

**[Documented scholarship]** Peterson's 2001 edition restored the manuscript text, collated variant spellings, identified the Sloane 3824 notice, and made Weyer, Scot, and the *Lemegeton* freely comparable. His work is the most useful starting point for separating manuscript evidence from twentieth-century additions.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

**[Documented scholarship and practice]** *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* first appeared in 2007 in the Sourceworks of Ceremonial Magic series. It transcribed the Rudd-associated recension and foregrounded the Shem angelic controls. The Dantaylion entry and Haiaiel Psalm occur on pp. 172–73.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

**[Documented broader influence]** Stratton-Kent argued for reading grimoire spirits in the wider history of *goēs*, necromancy, underworld cult, and the *Grimorium Verum* tradition. No distinctive Dantalion etymology or pre-Goetic Dantalion source was verified in his published work. Invoking his general “spiritist” approach does not by itself establish an ancient identity for this name.

## David Crowhurst

**[Documented modern hypothesis]** *Stellas Daemonum* studies grimoire spirits through historical, etymological, and astrological comparisons. Its treatment helped circulate the Dantalion–Tantavalerion hypothesis. Because the proposed identity is not stated in the early witnesses, it remains conjectural.

## *Testament of Solomon*

**[Documented ancient/late-antique parallel]** Demons in the Greek *Testament* identify the angels who frustrate or bind them. This supplies an ancient structural analogy for Rudd's technique, but not its specific pairings. Dantalion and Haiaiel are not a pair in the *Testament*. The text is available in Charlesworth's [*Old Testament Pseudepigrapha*, volume 1](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/THEOL264/James%20H.%20Charlesworth%20The%20Old%20Testament%20Pseudepigrapha%2C%20Vol.%201%20Apocalyptic%20Literature%20and%20Testaments%201983.pdf).

## *Livre des esperitz*

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This French catalogue demonstrates that many “Goetic” biographies existed before Weyer, but Dantalion is not among its 46 spirits. Jean-Patrice Boudet finds 35 recognizable correspondences with Weyer. See Boudet's [“Les *who's who* démonologiques de la Renaissance”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1171).

## Munich Handbook

**[Finding: absence of evidence]** Clm 849 preserves fifteenth-century necromantic operations and a short list of demons, but no Dantalion. It establishes genre ancestry, not name ancestry. See the [Bavarian State Library manuscript](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00037155) and Richard Kieckhefer's [*Forbidden Rites*](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/9780271065441/html).

## Abramelin

**[Finding: absence of evidence]** Searches of the principal Mathers and Dehn-derived lists do not yield Dantalion. Similar-looking damaged names cannot be equated without office, seal, or transmission evidence.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This is the relevant family. Weyer himself says that his catalogue was drawn from a *Liber officiorum spirituum* or book attributed to Solomon. Sloane 3824's compact Dantalion–Seere–Andromalius material appears to represent another branch of that tradition. Dantalion is therefore absent from Weyer's particular extract but not from every seventeenth-century *Offices* witness.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. “Dantalion is in Weyer”

**Verdict: contradicted by the primary source.** Weyer has 69 entries and no Dantalion. Some modern sites claim otherwise because they treat “Goetia” and “Pseudomonarchia” as interchangeable catalogues.

## 2. “Scot translated the Dantalion passage”

**Verdict: contradicted.** Scot could not translate a Weyer passage that was not present. The familiar English entry is from the later *Lemegeton* tradition.

## 3. Earliest attestation

**[Open/disputed]** Sloane 3824 is the earliest unmistakable witness located in this research. The exact copying date of its Dantalion folio is not securely established. **Dentalion** in related love-magic material might be as early or earlier, but the identification and relative dating require direct palaeographical comparison.

## 4. The name is an ancient god

**Verdict: unsupported.** No cult, inscription, myth, or ancient-language etymology has been established.

## 5. Dantalion equals Tantavalerion/Golgathell

**Verdict: possible modern hypothesis, not manuscript fact.** The early names coexist in related magical environments but are never equated in the sources examined.

## 6. Dantalion equals Lucifer

**Verdict: modern esoteric speculation.** It depends mainly on the Tantavalerion conflation and subjective-practice reports. The *Goetia* calls Dantalion a duke, not Lucifer, emperor, or chief king.

## 7. Dantalion was “demoted”

**Verdict: modern invention.** No chronological source first calls him emperor or king and then reduces him to duke.

## 8. “Duke of Faces” and “Keeper of the Forbidden Library”

**Verdict: modern titles.** The faces and book are authentic; the titles and infernal library biography are not.

## 9. Fallen-angel identity

**[Tradition/inference]** The *Goetia* presents its spirits as infernal and elsewhere describes particular spirits as fallen from angelic orders. Dantalion's own entry gives no former angelic choir, fall story, or celestial name. Calling him a “fallen angel” is a generalized demonological inference.

## 10. Thirty-six legions

**[Documented text but historically unverifiable]** Both the main *Goetia* and Rudd recension give 36. The proto-entry gives none. No independent record explains the number or defines a legion's size.

## 11. Nine or Ten of Cups?

Both are real in different modern schemes:

- **Nine of Cups:** Crowley *777*, 10°–20° Pisces by night.
- **Ten of Cups:** sequential spirit/angel quinance scheme, 20°–25° Pisces.

Calling either “the only traditional attribution” conceals the difference between systems.

## 12. March 1–10, March 5–9, or March 11–15?

- **March 1–10:** approximate civil dates for Crowley's second Pisces decan.
- **March 11–15:** conventional five-day dates for angel/spirit number 71.
- **March 5–9:** found on some recent websites, apparently from shifted calendrical calculations; it is not the standard *777* placement and needs its own stated method.

## 13. Haiaiel's Psalm: 108:29 or 109:30?

Both identify the same verse. The first is Vulgate numbering; the second is Masoretic/KJV numbering.

## 14. Haiaiel's “meaning”

“God, Master of the Universe” is the historical occult gloss transmitted through Lenain and Papus. It is not a secure word-for-word translation of היי.

## 15. De Plancy portrait

**Verdict: nonexistent.** Websites claiming an 1818 or 1863 de Plancy Dantalion portrait are misattributing either Goetic text or unrelated Le Breton artwork.

## 16. Seal errors

Crowley accused the Rudd codex of orthographic and sigil errors. Yet different pen forms can result from ordinary manuscript transmission, enlargement of tiny characters, or deliberate copying choices. Without a lost archetype, not every variance can be classified as an error.

## 17. Direction and superior king

The ordinary Dantalion entry names no quarter king. Later tables assign West/Corson, East, or North depending on their cosmology. None should be silently inserted into the seventeenth-century description.

## 18. Ethical and experiential claims

**[Tradition/modern testimony]** Contemporary practitioners report help with love, persuasion, study, therapy-like introspection, charisma, divination, or changing another person's feelings. These are testimonies or devotional beliefs, not independently testable historical evidence. The coercive character of the original “change thoughts” and “cause love” offices should not be disguised by modern therapeutic vocabulary.

# Open questions

1. **What is the exact date of Sloane 3824, fol. 116r?**  
   The codex is composite. Dating the binding or another section does not automatically date the Dantalion notice.

2. **Does Dentalion precede Dantalion?**  
   Sloane 3851 and the Arthur Gauntlet material deserve a direct comparison of handwriting, wording, neighboring names, and provenance.

3. **What exemplar supplied the three final spirits?**  
   Sloane 3824 is strongly suggestive, but a lost common source remains possible.

4. **Was Dantalion coined by corruption of another name?**  
   No convincing candidate has yet been demonstrated.

5. **Is there any real connection with Tantavalerion?**  
   A solution would require an intermediate manuscript joining not just the names but the offices or characters.

6. **How did the seal develop from Sloane 3824 to the full *Lemegeton* form?**  
   High-quality imaging and stemmatic comparison of all surviving characters could clarify which strokes are archetypal.

7. **Who composed the many-faced, book-bearing apparition?**  
   It is absent from the proto-office and appears in the expanded entry without explanation.

8. **Why 36 legions?**  
   It may be conventional catalogue arithmetic; no independent rationale survives.

9. **How much of Harley 6483 is truly Thomas Rudd's?**  
   The system may preserve his work while the extant manuscript remains a later copy and compilation.

10. **When was Dantalion first explicitly connected to a continuous 11–15 March period?**  
    The five-degree angelic sector is older than many modern birthday-angel tables, but the transformation into a fixed modern civil-date span needs closer bibliographical tracing.

11. **Did Frater Achad ever work with Dantalion?**  
    No evidence was located, but unpublished diaries or correspondence could alter that negative finding.

12. **Are there overlooked literary appearances?**  
    Claims concerning *Bartimaeus*, *Hellboy*, and *Helluva Boss* need an exact volume, issue, episode, or production source before acceptance.

# Malphas: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Documented text]** — directly present in a manuscript or printed primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a conclusion proposed from textual, codicological, linguistic, or historical comparison.
- **[Tradition]** — an assertion transmitted within Jewish, Christian-kabbalistic, or ceremonial-magical literature without independent historical verification.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a symbolic attribution created within an occult system.
- **[Disputed]** — competing readings or an attribution for which the evidence is inconclusive.
- **[Legend]** — supernatural narrative presented as tradition, not verifiable history.
- **[Modern invention]** — an attribution first found in recent occult, fictional, commercial, or Internet material rather than the early sources.

The requested primary descriptions are represented by short exact excerpts followed by exhaustive clause-by-clause translations and comparisons. Links lead to the complete texts and facsimiles.

## The name and its origins

### The earliest securely dated witness: Florence, 1494

**[Documented text]** The earliest presently secure Malphas witness is not Weyer. Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana MS Plut. 89 sup. 38, the *Fasciculus rerum geomanticarum*, contains an offices-of-spirits list at fol. 263r dated January 1494. Joseph Peterson records the form:

> “Malphas vel Malapas”

The spirit is the twelfth in that particular list, is called a great President, and is assigned twenty legions. Elsewhere in the manuscript are demon seals, but Peterson observes that they do not correspond to the seals later found in the Lemegeton. Thus this witness establishes the name, rank, and list-family before Weyer, but not the later Malphas seal. [Peterson’s manuscript survey and Laurenziana link](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [Laurenziana digital manuscript](https://teca.bmlonline.it/ImageViewer/servlet/ImageViewer?idr=TECA0000896386).

**Finding:** claims that Malphas first appears in 1577 are now outdated. The earliest securely dated attestation is 1494.

### The *Livre des Esperitz*: “Malpharas”

**[Documented text]** Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, fols. 179r–182v, preserves the French *Livre des Esperitz*. Its notice names **Malpharas**:

> “Malpharas est ung grant seigneur que ediffie tours et chasteaulx pontz sur eaues…”

That is: Malpharas is a great lord who builds towers, castles, and bridges over water. On command he can overthrow or confound people, castles, and fortresses; transport someone from one place to another; and obey the master who constrains him. Thirty legions answer to him. The notice lacks the raven, human form, hoarse voice, familiars, artificers, and sacrifice warning. [Complete French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier), [facsimile index](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre%3ALe_Livre_des_espertiz_-_MS_O.8.29.djvu).

**[Disputed date]** Older cataloguing and Elizabeth Butler placed the manuscript in the fifteenth or early sixteenth century; Richard Kieckhefer likewise accepted a fifteenth-century context. Jean-Patrice Boudet judged the surviving hand no earlier than the middle of the sixteenth century. The text may translate an older Latin model, but that does not itself date the surviving Malpharas wording. [Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques…”](https://doi.org/10.4000/medievales.1019).

Accordingly:

- The Florentine **Malphas vel Malapas**, dated 1494, is the earliest secure witness.
- **Malpharas** may preserve an old branch of the same tradition, but the date of the particular copy remains contested.
- Its different legion count and compact architectural office show that the tradition continued changing before Weyer.

### The Folger “Book of Oberon”: “Mallapas”

**[Documented text]** Folger Shakespeare Library MS V.b.26, copied approximately 1577–1583, contains a longer English offices-of-spirits collection. It calls the spirit **Mallapas** and places it sixth in the immediate list. The notice says that he makes castles and towers, can overthrow buildings and edifices, appears as a raven, can be constrained to appear like a man, and speaks hoarsely.

This is especially important because it supplies the raven–man–hoarse-voice complex independently within the same broad textual family. It also assigns Mallapas to the South. [Peterson and Daniel Harms’s diplomatic transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf).

### Weyer: “Malphas”

**[Documented text]** Johann Weyer printed the standardized spelling **Malphas** as entry 32 of the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Weyer said his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also called *Empto. Salomonis*. He deliberately withheld material to prevent practical use. [Peterson’s Latin and English edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [1577 bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Ioannis_VVieri_de_praestigiis_daemonum_i.html?id=6ZlHjwEACAAJ).

The page markers in Peterson’s transcription derive from another early printed pagination: Malphas falls between the printed markers 922 and 923. Section number **32** is therefore the most stable citation across editions.

### Etymology

**[Finding]** No defensible ancient deity, hero, biblical person, or ancient place has been identified behind Malphas.

This sharply distinguishes Malphas from names such as Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Amon/Amun, Asmoday/Ashmedai, Belial, and Phoenix. The variable early forms—*Malapas, Malpharas, Mallapas, Malphas*—look like products of manuscript transmission within an offices-of-spirits tradition.

Proposed derivations include:

- **[Disputed modern conjecture]** Latin *malum*, “evil,” plus an unidentified termination.
- **[Disputed modern conjecture]** Latin *maleficus*, “evildoing/magical,” transformed beyond regular phonetic recognition.
- **[Disputed modern conjecture]** Greek fragments such as *mala* plus a form connected with speaking.
- **[Disputed]** French or Anglo-French *Malpas*, “bad passage,” also a place-name and surname.

No early manuscript glosses the name, no historical linguist has demonstrated one of these derivations, and the oldest forms do not establish a single source language. “Etymology unknown” is therefore the responsible conclusion.

The word *goetia*, by contrast, has an established history: Latin *goetia* from Greek γοητεία, sorcery or enchantment, connected with γόης, an enchanter, and traditionally with γοάω, to wail or lament. [Etymonline’s source summary](https://www.etymonline.com/word/goetia).

## Number and ordering

### Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

**[Documented text]** Weyer transmits 69 individual entries, although his Belial notice refers to the seventy-two kings or princes. The Lemegeton transmits 72.

The Goetia reaches 72 by:

1. Omitting Weyer’s fourth spirit, **Pruflas/Bufas**.
2. Adding **Vassago** at Goetia number 3.
3. Adding **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius** as numbers 70–72.

Thus \(69-1+4=72\).

Scot’s English edition also omits Pruflas. Peterson regards that shared omission, together with shared mistranslations, as evidence that the Goetia compiler depended more immediately on Scot’s English than directly on Weyer’s Latin. [Peterson’s comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Malphas’s shift

- Weyer: **32**
- Goetia: **39**

The shift is not a simple seven-place insertion. Much of the middle catalogue has been rearranged. Peterson compares it to a stack of separately written leaves becoming disordered; surviving manuscripts in which each spirit occupies a separate leaf show that this was physically possible. That is a plausible **[scholarly reconstruction]**, not a demonstrated reconstruction of the lost exemplar.

One modern comparison table has circulated an erroneous Weyer number 31 for Malphas. Weyer’s own numbered text and contents make it unambiguously **32**.

## The description across the sources

## 1. *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Documented text]**

Rank: *ung grant seigneur*, “a great lord.”

Legions: thirty.

Form: none stated.

Voice: none stated.

Offices:

- Builds towers and castles.
- Builds bridges over water.
- Overthrows or confounds people and fortifications.
- Transports persons from place to place.
- Obeys the master who constrains him.

There is no sacrifice warning.

This compact notice is architecturally closer to Weyer than to the later “enemy thoughts” reading, but its language is not the immediate source of Weyer’s entire paragraph.

## 2. Florentine list, 1494

**[Documented text]**

Name: *Malphas vel Malapas*.

Rank: great President.

Legions: twenty.

The Florentine text establishes the rank before Weyer. Its legion count is the smallest surviving count and shows that forty was not immutable in the early transmission.

## 3. Folger V.b.26, c. 1577–1583

**[Documented text]**

Name: *Mallapas*.

Form: raven; human when constrained.

Voice: hoarse.

Offices:

- Makes castles and towers.
- Subverts and overthrows buildings and edifices.

This witness has no verified sacrifice clause in the surviving short notice. [Diplomatic transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf).

## 4. Weyer’s Latin, 1577

**[Documented text]** Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* §32:

> “Malphas magnus Præses, conspicitur corvo similis: sed hominis idolum indutus rauca fatur voce.”

Literal gloss: Malphas is a great President, seen like a raven; clothed in a human image, he speaks with a hoarse voice.

Complete clause-by-clause account:

- *magnus Præses* — great President.
- *corvo similis* — like a raven.
- *hominis idolum indutus* — clothed in or assuming a human image.
- *rauca…voce* — with a hoarse voice.
- *Domos & turres ingentes…extruit* — wondrously constructs houses and enormous/high towers.
- *obvios citò facit artifices maximos* — quickly makes the greatest craftsmen available or brings them together.
- *hostium…ædes & turres deijcit* — casts down enemies’ houses/buildings and towers.
- *Famulos suppeditat non malos* — supplies servants/familiars who are not bad.
- *Sacrificia libenter suscipit* — willingly receives sacrifices.
- *sacrificatores omnes fallit* — deceives every sacrificer.
- *Quadraginta…legiones* — forty legions obey him.

The complete Latin is available in [Peterson’s Weyer edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm#malphas).

### Important philological points

- *Corvus* means raven. “Crow” would more precisely be *cornix*.
- *Aedes* means houses, buildings, shrines, or edifices—not “desires.”
- *Artifex* means an artisan, craftsperson, skilled maker, or artist.
- *Famulus* literally means servant; in grimoire English it becomes “familiar.”
- Weyer says nothing about reading, knowing, or reporting an enemy’s thoughts.

## 5. Scot’s English, 1584

**[Documented text]** Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584, p. 222:

> “Malphas is a great president, he is seene like a crowe … speaketh with a hoarse voice…”

Scot’s complete sense, with original forms retained where significant:

- Malphas is a great president.
- He is seen like a “crowe,” although the Latin says raven.
- Clothed with a human image, he speaks hoarsely.
- He builds houses and high towers wonderfully.
- He quickly brings artificers together.
- He throws down enemies’ “edifications.”
- He helps one to good familiars.
- He willingly receives sacrifices.
- He deceives all sacrificers.
- Forty legions obey him.

[Complete Scot text](https://ftp.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm); [Cornell facsimile record](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081).

Scot’s “enimies edifications” correctly reflects Weyer’s buildings. It is crucial evidence against treating “enemy thoughts” as the original office.

## 6. Sloane 3825 and the manuscript Goetia

**[Documented text]** In Sloane 3825, fol. 106r, Malphas is spirit 39:

> “The 39th spirit in order is called Malphas … he appeareth at first … like a Crow…”

The complete manuscript sense is:

- First appears like a crow/raven.
- At the exorcist’s request assumes human form.
- Speaks with a hoarse voice.
- Is a mighty and powerful President.
- Builds houses and high towers.
- Quickly gathers artificers from all parts of the world.
- Can “destroy” the enemies’ desires or thoughts and all that they have done.
- Gives good familiars.
- Receives sacrifices kindly and willingly.
- Deceives the person who sacrifices.
- Governs forty legions.
- His seal must be made and worn as a lamen.

[Peterson’s Sloane 3825 transcription with manuscript variants](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#malphas).

### The “enemy thoughts” problem

The manuscript reads approximately:

> “destroy thy Enemies desires or thoughts, and all that they have done”

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This is almost certainly a corruption, expansion, or reinterpretation of Weyer’s *hostium aedes et turres deijcit* and Scot’s “enimies edifications.” The evolution appears to be:

1. Weyer: destroys enemies’ buildings and towers.
2. Scot: destroys enemies’ edifices.
3. Lemegeton: destroys enemies’ desires or thoughts and their actions.
4. Mathers: brings enemies’ desires, thoughts, and deeds to the magician’s knowledge.

Modern summaries often combine all four, crediting Malphas with destroying buildings, destroying plans, and reading minds. That composite is historically layered, not the wording of any single early witness.

## 7. Sloane 2731

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731, dated 1686, likewise calls the spirit Malphas and places him at 39. Its description broadly agrees with Sloane 3825. Individual spelling, abbreviation, and seal-stroke differences do not change the core identity. [Diplomatic facsimile edition](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/).

Sloane 2731 is sometimes treated as the source of all Goetia editions. It is an important witness, but not the sole or earliest witness to the Lemegeton.

## 8. Harley 6483 and “Dr Rudd”

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483 is dated internally to 1712–1713. Its title is *Liber malorum Spirituum, seu Goetia*. It transmits Malphas as number 39 and attaches the opposing angel Rehael.

**[Disputed attribution]** The codex is not an autograph manuscript by Thomas Rudd, who died in 1656. It belongs to a group of copies associated with Peter Smart and labeled as material from “Dr Rudd’s” papers. The extent to which any particular innovation is Rudd’s own work remains uncertain. [Peterson’s Lemegeton manuscript list](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm), [discussion of Peter Smart and the 1712–1713 colophon](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/06/addenda-et-scholia-2.html).

## 9. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**[Documented text]** *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, translated by S. L. MacGregor Mathers and edited/published by Aleister Crowley, printed Malphas on original p. 24:

> “He can build Houses and High Towers, and can bring to thy Knowledge Enemies’ Desires and Thoughts…”

The whole entry retains the crow, requested human form, hoarse voice, President rank, good familiars, sacrifice warning, forty legions, and seal.

The crucial editorial change is “can bring to thy Knowledge.” It turns the manuscript’s destructive phrase into intelligence-gathering. Mathers does not explain this emendation. It is now the source of nearly every claim that Malphas reveals enemy intentions. [1904 facsimile](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AThe_Book_of_the_Goetia_%28Mathers-Crowley%2C_1904%29.djvu), [searchable scan, PDF p. 36/original p. 24](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf), [Project Gutenberg HTML](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/72679/72679-h/72679-h.htm).

Crowley’s humorous footnotes elsewhere in the catalogue—including the aside about Malthus in the immediately preceding Halphas entry—are editorial jokes, not manuscript material.

## 10. Peterson, 2001

**[Documented edition]** Joseph H. Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis*, York Beach, Maine: Weiser, 2001, p. 25, restored a manuscript-based text centered on Sloane 3825 and recorded variants from Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses.

Peterson retains the manuscript’s “destroy” language and identifies *corvus* as raven. His edition therefore makes visible two changes obscured by Mathers:

- Crow is a loose English rendering of raven.
- “Bring to thy knowledge” is Mathers’s reading, not Sloane 3825’s.

[Peterson edition scan record](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf).

## What Malphas does—and does not do

### Attested offices

**[Documented text across the source chain]**

- Architecture: houses, castles, towers, fortifications, and—in the *Livre*—bridges.
- Demolition or defeat of enemy works.
- Rapid gathering of skilled artificers.
- Provision of familiars or servants.
- Acceptance of sacrifice followed by deception.
- In later Goetia witnesses, interference with enemy intentions or actions.
- In Mathers, disclosure of enemy intentions and deeds.

### Not attested in Malphas’s early notices

The following common Goetic powers belong to other spirits, not to Malphas:

- Teaching languages.
- Teaching liberal sciences.
- Revealing treasure.
- Causing love.
- Invisibility.
- Transmutation.
- Predicting past, present, and future.
- Answering every question.
- Resurrection or necromancy.

Assigning these to Malphas without a modern practitioner source is a catalogue conflation.

## Rank, planet, metal, and time

### President = Mercury

**[Documented text]** The Lemegeton’s “Observations” assigns:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury/quicksilver |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver |

Malphas, being a President, receives Mercury and mercury. [Goetia Observations](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Mars/iron is conspicuously absent from the transmitted table. Mathers proposed adjustments, including assigning iron to Earls, but those are editorial attempts to make the scheme planetarily complete.

**Safety note:** historical quicksilver is elemental mercury, a toxic liquid. Modern substitution with an amalgam, electrum, paper, or symbolic color is a later practice, not a Malphas-specific manuscript instruction.

### Hours

**[Documented text]** Presidents may be constrained at any hour of the day except twilight. The clause concerning night can be punctuated more than one way, but the natural reading is that nighttime work requires the relevant governing king also to be invoked.

**[Documented text]** The same section calls even-numbered lunar ages—2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 14 days—most favorable. This rule also appears in related earlier magical sources.

**[Later esoteric inference]** Wednesday is assigned because it is Mercury’s weekday. Orange, yellow, mixed colors, storax, and air are later Golden Dawn-derived or modern correspondences; they are not in Malphas’s early notice.

## The seal

### Absence before the Lemegeton

**[Documented text]** Neither Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia* nor Scot’s English translation supplies an individual seal for Malphas. Assertions about “Weyer’s Malphas seal” are false.

The Florentine manuscript does contain demon seals elsewhere, but Peterson reports that they do not match the later Goetia seals. Consequently, a modern website contrasting “Weyer’s seal” with “the Lemegeton seal” is generally contrasting unrelated or later graphics.

### Lemegeton form

**[Documented image]** In the Mathers–Crowley form, Malphas’s seal is:

- Enclosed by a single circular boundary.
- Marked by a large angular **M-like form at the top**.
- Marked by an **H-like form at the bottom**.
- Filled by an asymmetric linear monogram of diagonal and horizontal strokes, with bends, hooks, and small curved terminals.
- Presented as the spirit’s “seal” or “character,” to be worn as a lamen.

[Public-domain 1904 vector](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3A39-malphas-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg), [1904 raster reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3A39-Malphas.png).

The apparent letters should be described as letter-like rather than decoded confidently. The manuscripts provide no explanation of the components, no construction grid, and no statement that every line represents a planet, element, or letter.

### Manuscript variants

**[Documented image comparison]** Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, and Harley 6483 preserve the same recognizable design, but differ in:

- Curvature and closure of the enclosing circle.
- Exact angle of the central strokes.
- Whether small terminals look like hooks, loops, or blunt ends.
- Placement and proportions of the upper and lower letter-like components.
- Line crossings produced by individual penmanship.

No evidence was found for a substantively different early Malphas seal. Most modern variations are redrawn, regularized, mirrored, ornamented, or surrounded with the full name.

### Rudd’s double seal

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483’s distinctive practice is to make a two-sided or “double” lamen:

- Malphas’s demonic seal on one face.
- Rehael’s name/character and associated psalm verse on the opposite face.

The angel is the “thwarting” or constraining counterpart. The demon seal is therefore not used alone in this recension. [Dan Harms’s summary](https://danharms.wordpress.com/2007/10/27/on-the-shelf-the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-part-1/), [Skinner catalogue description](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf).

### Formation of the name

**[Documented kabbalistic method]** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three consecutive Hebrew verses conventionally counted as seventy-two letters each. They are written in alternating directions:

1. Verse 19 in its normal direction.
2. Verse 20 reversed.
3. Verse 21 in its normal direction.

Reading the resulting columns yields seventy-two three-letter groups. The thirty-ninth is **רהע**. Christian kabbalists added **אל**, *El*, producing **רהעאל**.

**[Philological caution]** רהע is an extracted letter-group, not an ordinary Hebrew lexical root from which “God receives sinners” follows by grammar. The translated “meanings” are devotional glosses on divine attributes.

### Psalm verse

The standard English occult-table form is:

> “Hear, O Lord, and have mercy upon me: Lord, be thou my helper.”

The numbering varies because:

- Masoretic/Hebrew editions count the superscription differently: Psalm 30:11.
- English Protestant Bibles usually give Psalm 30:10.
- The Septuagint and Vulgate number the psalm as 29:11.

This is a numbering issue, not evidence for three different verses.

## History of the 72-fold Name and Rehael

### Rabbinic and medieval Jewish strata

**[Documented tradition]** *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally meant the “explicit” or “distinctly pronounced” Name, commonly the Tetragrammaton. It did not originally mean “the 72 angels.”

**[Documented exegesis]** Rashi’s eleventh-century commentary on Sukkah 45a explains the method of taking the first letter of the first verse, the last letter of the middle verse, and the first of the third, continuing in that order. The *Sefer ha-Bahir* also knows a seventy-two-fold divine Name.

**[Tradition]** The *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*, a medieval compilation later printed at Amsterdam in 1701, gives great importance to forms of the seventy-two-fold Name. Legends say Moses used the Name at the crossing of the sea. These are religious-magical traditions, not historical accounts of Exodus. [Overview with Rashi citation](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205).

Crucially, the Jewish triplets are divine names or letter-combinations. Their conversion into a fixed list of European ceremonial angels with meanings, psalms, offices, calendars, choirs, and demon opponents occurred in later stages.

### Reuchlin, 1517

**[Documented text]** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* treats the triplets as pronounceable angelic names by adding endings such as **-el** and **-yah**. This is a major hinge between Jewish name speculation and Renaissance Christian angelology. Modern scholarship is represented by Ian Christie-Miller’s *72 in His Name*. [Publisher description](https://www.academicstudiespress.com/9781644692455/).

### Agrippa, 1533

**[Documented text]** Cornelius Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia* III.25, explains the extraction of seventy-two names from the three Exodus verses and their angelic augmentation. [Agrippa text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agrippa3.htm), [1651 page image](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Three_Books_of_Occult_Philosophy_(De_Occulta_Philosophia)_(1651).djvu/395).

Agrippa did not pair Rehael with Malphas.

### Blaise de Vigenère, 1586

**[Documented text]** Vigenère included Christian-kabbalistic material in his *Traicté des chiffres ou secrètes manières d’escrire* (Paris, 1586). Rudd’s angelic tables show dependence on this Renaissance stream. [1586 text](https://wulfric.artsci.utoronto.ca/rentexte/vigenere/chif_htm.htm).

### Kircher, 1652

**[Documented text]** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, vol. II.1, Rome, 1652, printed elaborate Christian-kabbalistic diagrams and tables of divine and angelic names. His presentation helped standardize the visual “wheel” or ordered scheme of seventy-two names in European occultism. Kircher discusses such material critically as well as reproducing it. [Kircher’s *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

Kircher does not supply the Rudd Malphas–Rehael pairing.

### Rudd/Smart, seventeenth to early eighteenth century

**[Documented text]** The number-for-number opposition of the seventy-two Shem angels and the seventy-two Goetia demons is preserved most fully in the material attributed to Thomas Rudd and copied by Peter Smart.

The governing principle is simple:

- Angel 1 constrains demon 1.
- Angel 2 constrains demon 2.
- …
- Angel 39, Rehael, constrains demon 39, Malphas.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The match is numerical and structural rather than derived from semantic similarity. Rehael’s mercy, healing, and filial affection do not transparently “reverse” Malphas’s architecture, espionage, or deceptive sacrifice. The opposition may nevertheless have been interpreted morally: healing and mercy counter destructive or deceptive activity.

### Lenain, 1823

**[Documented text]** Lazare Lenain, *La science cabalistique* (Amiens, 1823), chapter VI, calls Rehael:

> “Dieu qui reçoit les pécheurs”

It assigns the 191st through 195th degrees of the zodiac, corresponding to 10°–15° Libra. Lenain’s notice says Rehael serves for healing illness and obtaining divine mercy; governs health and longevity; and influences paternal and filial love, obedience, and children’s respect for parents.

Lenain also gives an opposing or “contrary” genius whose qualities include cruelty and crimes against parents or children. This negative double is part of Lenain’s occult moral cosmology, not Jewish angelology. [1823 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf), [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30789329z).

### The Golden Dawn

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn integrated the 72 names with:

- Zodiacal quinances.
- The thirty-six decans.
- The tarot pip cards.
- Planetary face rulers.
- Qabalistic choirs and archangels.

Rehael therefore falls in 10°–15° Libra, within the Saturn-ruled second decan of Libra and the Three of Swords. The second five degrees, 15°–20° Libra, belong to angel 40, Yeiazel. Thus each pip card carries two Shem angels.

The Golden Dawn did not originate the triplets, but it produced the best-known systematic tarot overlay.

### Papus, 1903

**[Documented reception]** Papus—Gérard Encausse—reproduced and promoted Lenain’s notices in *La Cabbale* (1903), treating the seventy-two genii as a high operative expression of Judeo-Christian Kabbalah. Papus was a major route by which Lenain’s meanings, virtues, contrary genii, and calendars reached twentieth-century French occultism.

### Ambelain, 1951

**[Documented reception]** Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique*, Paris: Niclaus, 1951, reproduced Lenain’s text and supplemented it with angel seals drawn from an eighteenth-century manuscript. Ambelain later warned that certain seals had been incorrectly attributed and reported harmful results in his own occult interpretation. Those claims are **[practitioner testimony]**, not historical or medical evidence. [Original-edition record](https://edition-originale.com/fr/oeuvres/religions-and-spiritualites-8/esoterisme-and-spiritisme-38/ambelain-la-kabbale-pratique-1951-44828), [English translation](https://www.markfoster.net/rn/texts/practicalkabbalah-part1.pdf).

### Haziel and modern guardian-angel literature

**[Modern esoteric development]** Books published under the name Haziel popularized the seventy-two as personal guardian angels determined by birth dates, twenty-minute periods, moral qualities, stones, colors, and prayers. This guardian-birthday model is a late modern elaboration of the degree scheme.

Modern dates for Rehael vary—September 26–30, September 30–October 4, October 3–7, or October 4–8—because authors:

- Begin the zodiacal year on different civil dates.
- Treat 0° Aries as March 20, 21, or 22.
- Use inclusive dates differently.
- Confuse Rehael’s five-degree “physical” rulership with other daily or twenty-minute rulerships.

The stable attribution is **degrees 191–195, or 10°–15° Libra**, not a fixed Gregorian five-day interval.

### Skinner and Rankine, 2007

**[Documented edition]** Stephen Skinner and David Rankine published *The Goetia of Dr Rudd: The Angels and Demons of Liber Malorum Spirituum seu Goetia*, Golden Hoard Press, 2007, 448 pp., ISBN 978-0-9547639-2-3. It transcribes Harley 6483 with material from Harley 6482, Sloane 3824, and Wellcome 3203.

Its major contribution was making the double seals and “thwarting angels” readily available. Malphas–Rehael is therefore no longer merely an unattributed modern table: it is demonstrably present in the Rudd/Smart manuscript complex. [Publisher catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf), [library catalogue](https://catalog.freelibrary.org/Record/2680336).

### Crowley’s *777*

**[Documented esoteric table]** Column CLXV lists Goetic demons “by Night (Cadent).” On the Aries row Malphas is number 39, planetary rank-symbol Mercury, with the magical image:

> “Crow with sore throat.”

This is a deliberately compressed mnemonic, not another textual description. [Searchable *Liber 777*](https://www.magicgatebg.com/Books/Liber%20777.pdf), [*777 Revised*](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/crowley/777_Revised.pdf).

The corresponding daylight spirit is Vassago. The two occupy the same ten-degree decan and pip card, not separate five-degree halves in Crowley’s table.

- 20°–30° Aries.

### Enochian overlays

**[Modern esoteric synthesis]** The original Malphas texts have no Enochian attribution. John Dee and Edward Kelley’s angelic system is historically independent of the Lemegeton catalogue.

Golden Dawn-derived practitioners later mapped:

- Zodiacal decans onto the Enochian elemental tablets.
- Goetic seals onto tarot pip cards.
- Shem angels onto the same pips.
- Enochian seniors, subangles, divine names, tattvas, and sometimes I Ching figures onto those cards.

Lon Milo DuQuette’s *Tarot of Ceremonial Magick* (1995) deliberately combines “Enochian, Goetia, Astrology.” Any Enochian name printed beside Malphas is therefore a twentieth-century correspondential overlay, not the spirit’s original ruler or secret name. [WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Tarot-of-ceremonial-magick-%3A-a-pictorial-synthesis-of-three-great-pillars-of-magick-%3A-enochian-Goetia-astrology/oclc/32347017).

## Iconography

## Manuscript imagery

The earliest Malphas witnesses are textual catalogues. No image of his bodily appearance survives in them.

The Lemegeton seal is diagrammatic, not a portrait. Its circle and letter-like monogram became Malphas’s most durable visual identifier after 1904.

## Collin de Plancy

### 1818 text

**[Documented publication]** Jacques-Albin-Simon Collin de Plancy first published the *Dictionnaire infernal* in two volumes in 1818. Malphas belongs alphabetically in volume II. The early dictionary notice condenses the Weyer tradition. [1818 edition catalogue](https://ccfr.bnf.fr/portailccfr/ark%3A/16871/00111541034), [Wikisource edition](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/1re_%C3%A9dition%2C_1818/Tome_1/Texte_entier).

### 1863 entry

**[Documented text]** The sixth edition, Paris: Henri Plon, 1863, p. 439, calls him:

> “Malphas, grand président des enfers, qui apparaît sous la forme d’un corbeau…”

The notice says that he takes human form, builds citadels and impregnable towers, overthrows enemy ramparts, finds good workers, gives familiars, willingly receives sacrifices while deceiving the sacrificer, and commands forty legions. It is essentially a French literary restatement of Weyer and Scot, with architectural embellishment. [Complete 1863 entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Malphas).

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

**[Documented image]** The 1863 edition supplied the famous portrait attributed to Louis Le Breton, with the printed engraving commonly credited to M. Jarrault after Le Breton’s design.

It shows Malphas as an anthropomorphic corvid:

- Large raven or crow head in profile.
- Heavy hooked beak.
- Dark feathered neck and upper torso.
- Extended, muscular human-like arms.
- A compact crouching humanoid body.
- Taloned or birdlike lower extremities.
- No crown, architectural tools, tower, or seal.

The image literalizes only the bird manifestation. The human limbs suggest the requested human form but do not illustrate a complete transformation. [High-resolution public-domain image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AIll_dict_infernal_p0455_malphas.jpg), [Gallica scan](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5754923d/f449.image).

### Modern art

**[Modern invention]** Later artists frequently add:

- Fortresses, blueprints, masonry, or towers.
- Mercurial symbols and orange coloring.
- Black wings rather than a complete crow body.
- Military armor.
- Human or androgynous beauty.
- Rehael’s Hebrew letters.
- Satanic or qliphothic imagery.
- “Enns” around the traditional seal.

These may visualize modern interpretations but have no manuscript authority. David P. Wilson’s illustrations for the 1992 *Illustrated Goetia* are an influential modern example.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

**[Documented history]** Weyer, a physician and former student of Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, opposed witch persecutions while accepting much of the period’s spirit cosmology. His publication was not a manual endorsing Malphas worship. He framed the catalogue critically and said he had rendered it unusable by omissions.

### Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

**[Documented history]** Scot published the English catalogue as part of his skeptical exposure of witchcraft claims. Peterson suggests that the actual translator may have been Abraham Fleming. The English introduced errors that later passed into the Goetia.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

**[Disputed historical attribution]** Rudd was a mathematician and military engineer with Hermetic interests. The surviving “Rudd” manuscripts are later copies; Harley 6483 is dated 1712–1713, long after his death. Peter Smart appears to be the copyist or compiler responsible for the surviving form. Calling every innovation “Rudd’s own” goes beyond the evidence.

### Mathers and Crowley

Mathers produced the English translation or redaction; Crowley edited, annotated, and published it in 1904. The surviving evidence does not demonstrate that either personally evoked Malphas.

Crowley later assigned Malphas a place in *777*. This was an occult correspondential classification rather than a historical reconstruction.

### Frater Achad

**[Finding]** No reliable Malphas-specific operation or substantial discussion by Frater Achad—Charles Stansfeld Jones—was found. His inclusion in modern lists of Goetic authorities usually reflects his association with Crowley and Qabalah, not an attested Malphas working.

### Arthur Edward Waite

Waite printed the spelling **Malpas** in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic*. His prose summarizes the Mathers–Crowley version and helps explain the spread of the shortened spelling. [Waite’s entry](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/bcm/bcm58.htm).

### Poke Runyon

**[Modern practitioner tradition]** Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* (1996; expanded second edition 2023) integrates Goetic spirits with their Shem angels and uses mirror-scrying and hypnotic explanation. His published demonstrations center particularly on Astaroth/Astarte, not a documented personal Malphas evocation. The book nevertheless reproduces Malphas, Rehael, their zodiacal table, and seal. [Runyon’s first-edition page](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/monograph-v-2-8ajba), [second-edition page](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

### Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, David P. Wilson

Their *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* (1992; later editions) popularized Malphas’s Aries-night/Four-of-Wands attribution and supplied new artwork. DuQuette’s *Tarot of Ceremonial Magick* then merged Goetia, Shem angels, Enochian magic, and astrology.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s double-seal system accessible and firmly documented Rehael as Malphas’s opposing angel.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition and later digital work provide the clearest source-critical comparison of Weyer, Scot, the Folger material, Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, Harley 6483, and other witnesses. His more recent manuscript survey is also the most accessible notice of the 1494 Florentine attestation.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

**[Modern practitioner/scholarship]** Stratton-Kent emphasized the continuity between Goetic catalogues and earlier spirit traditions, especially the *Livre des Esperitz* and offices-of-spirits literature. No distinctive Malphas etymology or documented personal working was located. His value here is broader historical framing, not item-specific primary evidence.

### Owen Davies

Davies’s *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* (Oxford, 2009) contextualizes the Lemegeton in the European grimoire tradition. It is not a Malphas monograph.

### Jean-Patrice Boudet, Julien Véronèse, Richard Kieckhefer, Jan Veenstra

- Boudet provides the essential edition and comparison of the *Livre des Esperitz*.
- Véronèse’s research situates learned magic, spirit catalogues, and Solomonic texts in medieval manuscript culture.
- Kieckhefer edited Munich Clm 849 in *Forbidden Rites* and studied the *Liber consecrationum* tradition related to the *Livre*.
- Veenstra’s work on learned magic and angelic/demonic names is contextual; no Malphas-specific etymology was found.

## Grimoire ancestors and analogues

### *Testament of Solomon*

**[Documented ancient/late-antique tradition]** The Greek *Testament of Solomon* presents Solomon compelling individually described demons by means of a divine ring and assigning them labor on the Temple. Specific angels thwart particular demons.

**Finding:** Malphas is not named in the *Testament*. It is a conceptual ancestor of the demon-catalogue and angel-against-demon structure, not a direct name source. [Text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm).

### Gittin 68a–b and Ashmedai

The Babylonian Talmud tells of Solomon obtaining power over Ashmedai in connection with building the Temple. Malphas does not appear. Claims that Malphas personally helped Solomon construct the Temple are **[modern extrapolation]** from Malphas’s building office and the general Solomon legend.

### Munich handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook contains demon magic and some names or operations related to later traditions. It does not securely attest Malphas. It is a contextual ancestor, not the earliest Malphas source. [Digital description](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/munich/), [Biblissima record](https://portail.biblissima.fr/ark%3A/43093/mdata008e7ceea016be2419bf84c7720e8a7290eab744).

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

This is the most important textual family behind Weyer. Trithemius’s *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508 catalogues a *Liber officiorum*, demonstrating the existence of such a title or genre, but does not itself give a Malphas notice.

The Florentine text, the *Livre des Esperitz*, Folger V.b.26, Weyer, Scot, and the Goetia represent related but not simply identical branches.

### *Book of Abramelin*

**Finding:** Malphas does not occur under that spelling in Mathers’s Abramelin spirit lists. Abramelin shares several major rulers with Goetic tradition—Lucifer, Satan, Belial, Astaroth, Asmodeus, Paimon, Oriens, and Amaimon—but it is not a direct Malphas witness. [Complete Mathers edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/abramelin/abramelin.htm).

The following section distinguishes verified uses from tempting but unsupported associations.

### Milton

**[Not attested]** Malphas does not appear in Milton’s *Paradise Lost*.

The likely confusion is **Mulciber**, Milton’s infernal architect who builds Pandemonium and corresponds to Vulcan/Hephaestus. The similar initial syllable and architectural office make the confusion understandable, but Malphas and Mulciber are unrelated names.

### Dante

**[Not attested]** Malphas does not appear in Dante’s *Divine Comedy*. Dante’s **Malacoda** and the **Malebranche** are original infernal officials in *Inferno* XXI–XXIII, not Malphas variants.

### The *Bartimaeus* novels

**[Not attested]** Searches of authoritative series information and available indexes did not establish a named Malphas in Jonathan Stroud’s four *Bartimaeus* novels. The series’ Solomonic and spirit-conjuring setting makes the association plausible, especially in *The Ring of Solomon*, but it should not be listed without a page-level citation. [Author’s series page](https://www.jonathanstroud.com/about_bart.html).

### *Hellboy*

**[Not attested]** No canonical Malphas was located in the principal *Hellboy* or B.P.R.D. character and publication sources searched. General resemblance between Mignola’s demons and grimoire art is not a Malphas appearance.

### *Helluva Boss*

**[Not attested as canonical]** Malphas appears in fan art, fan character sheets, and speculative “Meet the Goetia” posts, but no named, credited canonical character Malphas was verified in the released series material searched. ### *Megami Tensei* and *Persona*

**[Modern adaptation]** Malphas is a birdlike, multi-headed female demon and boss who serves Urizen. The name and avian form reflect the Goetic source, but the body, personality, plot, and gender presentation are Capcom inventions.

### *Diablo IV*

**[Modern adaptation, officially attested]** Malphas is the principal demon of the 2024 Season of the Construct. He corrupts Zoltun Kulle’s Loom and directs elemental constructs. The technological-builder theme is a recognizable adaptation of the architectural office, although Blizzard’s character belongs wholly to the *Diablo* cosmology. **[Modern adaptation]** Malphas is used as a major demonic name and crow-associated antagonist. The game also employs Goetic seals, though community examination has identified occasional seal/name mismatches. The game’s theology and plot are fictional, not representations of the manuscript tradition. [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1179080/FAITH_The_Unholy_Trinity/).

**[Modern adaptation]** Steve Jackson Games’ *In Nomine* makes Malphas the Demon Prince of Factions. This political-social office is original to the role-playing game; it does not derive from houses, towers, familiars, or enemy buildings.

**[Modern adaptation]** The 2016 television series uses Malphas as a demonic antagonist connected with possession and Camp Stillwater. This is a horror-fiction invention.

### Patrick Senécal’s *Malphas*

**[Modern adaptation]** Patrick Senécal’s 2011 French-language novel *Malphas* uses the name for the demonic force or prince associated with the fictional Cégep de Malphas. Its institutional setting plays on Malphas as a builder/ruler but creates an independent horror mythology.

### Music

**[Modern adaptation]** Metal and occult-music projects use the name frequently. One explicit example is the band Malphas’s album *The 39th Spirit*, whose title directly identifies the Goetia number. Such works testify to modern reception, not to new historical lore.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Does Malphas exist before Weyer?

**Conclusion: yes.**

- Secure: *Malphas vel Malapas*, Florence, January 1494.
- Possibly early but copy-date disputed: *Malpharas*, Trinity O.8.29.
- Weyer’s first printed appearance: 1577.

Therefore “Malphas first appears in Weyer” is true only if “appears” means “first appears in print.”

## 2. Raven or crow?

- Weyer: *corvus*, raven.
- Folger: raven.
- Scot: “crowe.”
- Goetia: crow.
- De Plancy: *corbeau*, raven.

Both birds are corvids, but raven is the stricter translation of Weyer.

## 3. Buildings or thoughts?

- *Livre*: destroys castles and fortifications.
- Weyer: destroys enemy houses and towers.
- Scot: enemy edifices.
- Sloane 3825: destroys enemies’ desires or thoughts and what they have done.
- Mathers: reveals those desires, thoughts, and deeds.

The intelligence-gathering Malphas is chiefly Mathers’s legacy. A modern magician may accept it as tradition, but it is not Weyer’s wording.

## 4. Does Malphas “deceive with sacrifice”?

Yes, but with a precise nuance.

Weyer says that Malphas willingly receives sacrifices but deceives all sacrificers. Scot and the Goetia retain the warning. The texts do not say that every interaction with Malphas is deceptive, nor do they specify what kind of sacrifice is meant.

Modern claims that he “hates offerings,” accepts only blood, demands a sexual sacrifice, or punishes anyone who gives gifts are **[modern interpretations]**.

## 5. Malphas and Halphas

Halphas immediately precedes Malphas in the Goetia:

- Halphas: number 38, Earl, stork/stock-dove, hoarse voice, builds armed towns or towers, sends warriors, twenty-six legions.
- Malphas: number 39, President, raven, hoarse voice, houses and towers, artificers, familiars, sacrifice warning, forty legions.

Their names, bird forms, voices, and building offices make conflation understandable. Harley 6483 and Mathers preserve *Malthas/Malthus* as Halphas variants. De Plancy explicitly wondered whether Halphas might be the same as Malphas.

Nevertheless, Weyer treats them as distinct entries separated widely in his list: Malphas 32 and Halphas 43. Their ranks, birds, offices, and legion counts differ. “Halphas and Malphas are one spirit” remains **[disputed speculation]**, not the primary-text position.

Claims that they are Odin’s ravens Huginn and Muninn, or the Islamic angels Harut and Marut, are modern comparative inventions without linguistic or textual evidence.

## 6. “Second in command under Satan”

**[Modern invention]** No such title occurs in:

- The 1494 Florentine list.
- The *Livre des Esperitz*.
- Folger V.b.26.
- Weyer.
- Scot.
- Sloane 3825 or 2731.
- Harley 6483.
- Mathers–Crowley.

The claim appears in modern demon encyclopedias, fictional biographies, and Internet pages, sometimes coupled with a story that Malphas served Samael, fell in the War in Heaven, and alone retained loyalty to Satan. No early source for that narrative was found.

## 7. Ancient god, fallen angel, or invented grimoire name?

**Finding:** there is no evidence connecting Malphas with an ancient deity.

Calling him a “fallen angel” is a general Christian demonological interpretation. No early Malphas notice names his former angelic order or tells a fall narrative.

The most economical historical explanation is that Malphas/Malapas/Malpharas/Mallapas is a late-medieval grimoire name circulating in the offices-of-spirits family. Whether its first creator adapted an older word remains unknown.

## 8. The Council of Braga

Occasional modern lists claim that “Malpas” or Malphas was condemned or named at the first Council of Braga. No corresponding name was located in the council’s canons. The claim lacks a primary citation and should be treated as **[unsupported modern assertion]**.

## 9. The seal’s supposed hidden symbolism

Modern explanations identify hexagrams, seven planetary dots, “spirit roads,” runes, masonry plans, or letter-ciphers inside Malphas’s seal. The historical sources offer no such key. Many of those descriptions are also visually inconsistent with the actual 1904 figure.

## 10. Misprints that became lore

Several errors became durable traditions:

- Scot’s “crowe” for Latin *corvus*.
- “Enemy thoughts” replacing enemy buildings.
- Mathers’s “bring to thy knowledge” replacing “destroy.”
- **Malpas** as Waite’s shortened spelling.
- **Malthas** being transferred from Halphas to Malphas.
- Weyer number 31 in some comparison charts rather than 32.
- Modern “official enns” presented as ancient formulas.
- Fixed calendar birthdays substituted for exact zodiac degrees.

## Open questions

1. **What is the exact relationship among the Florentine, Trinity, Folger, and Weyer texts?** They demonstrably share an offices-of-spirits family, but no complete stemma establishes which surviving branch is closest to the lost model.

2. **How old is the *Livre des Esperitz* wording?** The extant hand may be mid-sixteenth century, while its translated model may be considerably older.

3. **What generated the name?** None of the early sources supplies an etymology. *Malapas*, *Malpharas*, *Mallapas*, and *Malphas* permit several scribal histories but no decisive original.

4. **How did *aedes et turres* become “desires or thoughts”?** A difficult or damaged English exemplar, abbreviation, eye-skip, or deliberate expansion are all possible. No surviving intermediate conclusively documents the change.

5. **Did Thomas Rudd personally originate the angel–demon pairing?** Harley 6483 is a posthumous Peter Smart-era copy. The material may descend from Rudd, from his circle, or from later compilation.

6. **Were the seals created expressly for the Lemegeton?** No matching Malphas seal has yet been located in a securely earlier offices-of-spirits witness.

7. **Does the Rudd pairing encode more than numerical correspondence?** No convincing semantic, astrological, or linguistic algorithm explains why Rehael specifically opposes Malphas beyond both being number 39.

8. **Which modern correspondences are genuinely Golden Dawn?** Aries decan, night assignment, and tarot pip are traceable; “enn,” colored candle, specific incense recipes, qliphothic titles, and personality profiles are later accretions.

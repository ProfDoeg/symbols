# Shax: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier follows the surviving textual chain rather than treating modern occult compilations as interchangeable authorities. Research was current to 14 September 2026.

- **[Documented text]** — stated or depicted in the cited primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — an inference argued from manuscripts, chronology, language, or source relationships.
- **[Tradition]** — received occult teaching whose historical derivation can be traced, but which is not a claim established by historical scholarship.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an imposed symbolic relationship, usually astrological, kabbalistic, or magical.
- **[Disputed]** — a claim for which authorities or witnesses conflict.
- **[Legend]** — a supernatural narrative reported without asserting its factual reality.
- **[Modern invention]** — a demonstrably recent elaboration, adaptation, personal revelation, or fictional reuse.

Long primary-source passages below are represented by short diplomatic quotations and complete clause-by-clause translations or transcriptions. Links lead to the full public-domain or manuscript-derived texts.

## The name and its origins

### 1. Earliest secure attestation: *Chax, alias Scox* (1577)

**[Documented text]** The first presently identifiable occurrence of this particular spirit is Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Weyer lists it as number 37:

> “Chax, aliàs Scox, Dux est & Marchio magnus: similis ciconiæ rauca voce & subtili.”

That is: “Chax, otherwise Scox, is a duke and great marquis, resembling a stork, with a hoarse and subtle voice.” The entry occurs on approximately p. 924 of the 1577 printing, between Gaap and Pucel; Peterson’s electronic edition labels it §37. [Weyer/Peterson](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Documented text]** Weyer says that his source was a book called *Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis*—a “Book of the Offices of Spirits,” also called the *Empto. of Solomon*. Johannes Trithemius had already listed a *Liber officiorum spirituum* in his *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508. Weyer also admits that he suppressed ritual material so that his presentation could not readily be used as an operational manual. [Weyer’s introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Consequently, 1577 is the earliest surviving Shax attestation, not necessarily the date on which the name was invented. Weyer demonstrably worked from an older textual family, but no pre-1577 witness yet located contains an identifiable *Chax/Scox/Shax* entry.

### 2. The forms *Chax* and *Scox*

**[Documented text]** Weyer prints two forms, *Chax* and *Scox*. Neither is presented as a translation. They are variant names already present in his source or source tradition.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** In early-modern hands, capital **C**, **S**, and combinations involving long *s*, *h*, *c*, and *x* could be confused. Weyer’s preservation of two variants shows that corruption preceded the English Goetia tradition.

There is no evidential basis for declaring either spelling the “original.” *Chax* is simply the first form in the earliest surviving printing.

### 3. Scot’s *Shax* (1584)

**[Documented text]** Reginald Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XVI, chapter 2, pp. 222–23, changes the headword to:

> “Shax, alias Scox, is a darke and a great marquesse, like unto a storke…”

[Scot/Peterson transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm)

The initial *Ch-* has thus become *Sh-* by 1584. Since the later *Goetia* follows Scot’s English closely, **Shax** became the dominant English form.

**[Disputed attribution]** Scot published the translation, but Joseph Peterson suggests that the actual translator of Weyer’s catalogue may have been Abraham Fleming. The translation contains numerous errors suggesting use of a defective or difficult manuscript rather than a clean printed Weyer edition.

[Sacred Texts, p. 36](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

### 5. Etymology

**Finding:** no accepted etymology exists.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Unlike Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Amon/Amun, Asmoday/Ashmedai, Belial, or Phenex/Phoenix, Shax has no securely identifiable ancient god, hero, place, Greek noun, Latin noun, or biblical personality behind the name.

No *Chax/Scox/Shax* appears in the:

- *Testament of Solomon*;
- *Livre des Esperitz*;
- *Book of Abramelin* demon lists;
- surviving Munich handbook catalogue in a form with the same description;
- securely identified pre-Weyer *Liber Officiorum* witnesses.

**[Disputed modern claim]** Some websites derive *Shax* from Hebrew **שַׁחַץ**, *shaḥats*, and translate it “to rob” or “to plunder.” This is philologically defective. The noun means approximately “pride,” “haughtiness,” or “dignity”; it is not the Hebrew verb for plundering. The Hebrew verb **שָׁסָה**, *shasah*, can mean “plunder,” but no textual bridge connects it with *Chax*. [Hebrew lexicon entry for שַׁחַץ](https://openbible.com/strongs/hebrew/7830.htm)

**[Disputed modern claim]** A derivation from Persian *shāh*, “king,” is equally unsupported and conflicts with Shax’s rank as duke/marquis rather than king.

**[Disputed modern claim]** A connection to “Skortouri” or similar names in Greek magical papyri has circulated in occult discussion, but no demonstrated manuscript sequence, phonological argument, or matching office has been produced.

The most economical conclusion is therefore:

> **Shax is an unexplained early-modern spirit-name, first surviving as the corrupt doublet Chax/Scox in 1577.**

### 6. The word *goetia*

This is not an etymology of *Shax*. Greek **γοητεία**, *goēteia*, means sorcery or enchantment and derives from **γόης**, an enchanter or charlatan. Ancient lexicographic tradition related it to **γοάω**, “wail” or “lament,” perhaps through rites involving the dead. [Etymonline overview](https://www.etymonline.com/word/goetia)

## The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* (1577)

### Diplomatic excerpt

> “Chax, aliàs Scox, Dux est & Marchio magnus: similis ciconiæ rauca voce & subtili.”

*Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, §37, 1577, approximately p. 924. [Latin text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### Complete content in close translation

**[Documented text]**

- Chax, alias Scox, is a **duke and great marquis**.
- He resembles a **stork**—Latin *ciconia*—and has a hoarse, subtle voice.
- At the exorcist’s command he removes sight, hearing, and understanding.
- He takes money from any royal house and brings it back after 1,200 years if ordered.
- He also carries off horses.
- He is thought faithful in commands, and promises obedience, but does not fulfill everything.
- He is a liar unless introduced into the triangle.
- Once introduced there, he speaks about divine things and hidden treasures not guarded by malignant spirits.
- He promises to provide excellent servants or familiars, provided that they are not deceivers.
- Thirty legions are subject to him.

### Significant readings

**Rank.** *Dux et Marchio magnus* means “duke and great marquis.” Weyer gives a double rank.

**Bird.** *Ciconia* unambiguously means **stork**, not dove.

**Money.** *Reportat post mille ducentos annos* means that he “carries it back after 1,200 years.” It does not say he redistributes it to the poor.

**Horses.** *Abripit et equos* means that he “snatches/carries off horses”—hence Scot’s “horssestealer.”

**Triangle.** Weyer does not say that Shax assumes a second shape inside the triangle. The change is behavioral: outside it he lies; inside it he speaks of divine matters and unguarded treasures.

**Familiars.** The syntax retains uncertainty: Shax promises good servants, but they must prove not to be deceivers.

**Legions.** Weyer’s concluding gloss says a legion contains 6,666 spirits. Applying that number mechanically would produce 199,980 subordinates, but the Shax entry itself merely says “thirty legions.” This arithmetic should not be mistaken for an independently repeated Shax tradition.

## 2. Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584)

### Diplomatic excerpt

> “Shax, alias Scox, is a darke and a great marquesse, like unto a storke, with a hoarse and subtill voice…”

Book XVI, chapter 2, pp. 222–23. [Full transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm)

### Complete content in modernized transcription

**[Documented text]** Scot calls Shax/Scox a “dark” and great marquis resembling a stork. He says that Shax can remove a person’s sight, hearing, and understanding; steal money from every king’s house and carry it back after 1,200 years; steal horses; promise faithfulness while failing to obey fully; lie unless placed in a triangle; speak divinely and reveal hidden matters not guarded by wicked spirits when constrained there; promise good but potentially deceptive familiars; and command thirty legions.

### Scot’s changes

**“Darke.”** **[Scholarly reconstruction]** This probably represents damage to Weyer’s *dux*, “duke.” Scot has effectively transformed one of the two ranks into an adjective:

- Weyer: “a duke and great marquis.”
- Scot: “a dark and great marquis.”

There is no separate Latin adjective meaning “dark” in Weyer’s sentence.

**“Conjuror.”** Scot uses “conjuror” where Weyer has *exorcista*, exorcist. Peterson supplies corrective brackets in his electronic presentation.

**“Speaketh divinelie.”** Weyer’s *loquitur de rebus divinis* means “speaks about divine things,” not necessarily “speaks in a divinely inspired manner.” Scot’s phrase encouraged the stronger later interpretation.

## 3. The manuscript *Ars Goetia*

### Peterson’s base text: Sloane 3825

A brief exact excerpt reads:

> “The 44th spirit in order is named Shax, he is a great Marquize & appeareth in ye forme of a stock dove…”

The full entry is printed in Joseph H. Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (2001), p. 27, corresponding to Sloane 3825, fol. 107r. [Peterson’s electronic edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Complete content

**[Documented text]**

- Shax is spirit 44.
- He is a great marquis.
- He appears as a stock-dove, with a hoarse and subtle voice.
- His office is to take away the sight, hearing, and understanding of a man or woman.
- He steals money from kings’ houses and carries it back in 1,200 years if commanded.
- He fetches horses or any other requested thing.
- He must first be commanded into the triangle or he will deceive the exorcist and tell many lies.
- He discovers hidden things not guarded by wicked spirits.
- He sometimes gives good familiars.
- He governs thirty legions.
- His seal must be made and worn as a lamen.

### Changes from Weyer and Scot

| Detail | Weyer | Scot | Goetia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number | 37 | 37 | 44 |
| Name | Chax/Scox | Shax/Scox | Shax |
| Rank | Duke and great marquis | “Dark” and great marquis | Great marquis |
| Bird | Stork | Stork | Stock-dove |
| Horses | Carries off/steals | Horse-stealer | Fetches horses |
| Other objects | Not specified | Not specified | “Or any thing” |
| Triangle | Liar unless introduced | Same | Must be commanded into it first |
| Divine discourse | Speaks of divine things | “Speaketh divinely” | Omitted |
| Hidden matters | Hidden treasures | Hidden things | All hidden things not guarded |
| Familiars | Promises excellent servants | Promises good familiars | Gives good familiars sometimes |
| Seal | None supplied | None supplied | Supplied and worn as a lamen |
| Second form | None | None | None |

### Stork or stock-dove?

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s *ciconia* and Scot’s “storke” agree.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** “Stock-dove” entered with the English *Goetia* transmission. It cannot be a translation of *ciconia*. Possibilities include:

1. scribal contamination from another bird-shaped spirit;
2. visual or aural confusion between “stork” and “stock”;
3. deliberate substitution by a compiler.

No manuscript explanation survives.

The nearby catalogue contains several avian spirits—Halphas is another hoarse-voiced stork in Scot—so contamination is plausible, but unproved. It does not establish that Halphas and Shax were originally one spirit.

## 4. Mathers–Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia* (1904)

The Shax entry is on printed p. 36:

> “The Forty-fourth Spirit is Shax, or Shaz (or Shass). He is a Great Marquis…”

[1904 text and scan](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

**[Documented text]** Mathers’ version preserves the stock-dove, hoarse subtle voice, removal of the three faculties, theft and eventual return of royal money, fetching horses or other objects, compulsory triangle, deception, hidden things, occasional familiars, thirty legions, and the seal.

Its substantive content is a polished form of the manuscript reading, not a newly independent account. Mathers adds *Shaz* and *Shass* but gives no textual apparatus establishing their source.

**[Documented publication history]** Mathers prepared the text; Crowley published it in 1904 through the Society for the Propagation of Religious Truth and contributed the preliminary invocation, editorial remarks, and “Initiated Interpretation.” [1904 bibliography](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/goetia_1904/goetia_1904.htm)

## 5. Peterson’s 2001 edition

**[Documented text]** Peterson prints Sloane 3825 closely, marking abbreviations, damaged readings, foliation, and variants. His note identifies Weyer’s *Chax alias Scox* and Scot’s *Shax alias Scox*. His edition therefore separates manuscript evidence from Mathers’ later editorial variants.

This is the strongest readily available critical text for reconstructing the ordinary *Lemegeton* witness.

## 6. Harley 6483 and *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483’s Shax description remains close to the other manuscripts: stock-dove, hoarse voice, removal of faculties, money and horses, triangle, deception, hidden things, familiars, and thirty legions.

**[Editorial anomaly]** Skinner and Rankine’s transcription contains punctuation that can make “Sometimes he governeth 30 Legions” appear to qualify the legion count. Comparison with the other witnesses shows that “sometimes” belongs to the familiars clause, not to the number of legions.

**[Disputed reconstruction]** Skinner and Rankine suggest that the “1,200 years” might refer to a spirit’s hope of returning to a heavenly throne. Nothing in Weyer’s grammar supports that reading: in the earliest text, the 1,200 years modify the return of the money. Their proposal is imaginative commentary, not the primary reading.

## Rank, metal, planet, and hours

### Rank correspondences in the Goetia

| Rank | Seal metal in the Mathers–Crowley text | Planet conventionally implied |
|---|---:|---|
| King | Gold | Sun |
| Duke | Copper | Venus |
| Prince or Prelate | Tin | Jupiter |
| Marquis | Silver | Moon |
| President | Mercury or fixed quicksilver | Mercury |
| Earl/Count | Equal parts copper and silver | Ambiguous; later often Mars |
| Knight | Lead | Saturn |

[Classified list and metals](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf)

**[Documented text]** Shax is a marquis in the Goetia, so his lamen is to be made in silver.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Silver conventionally identifies the Moon. Later tables therefore call Shax lunar.

**[Caution]** Weyer also calls him a duke, which would imply copper/Venus if the later Goetic metal table were projected backward. The metal table is not part of Weyer’s individual entry. The standard Goetic result remains Moon/silver because the *Goetia* retained only Marquis.

### Hours

Weyer gives:

> “Item Marchiones à nona usque ad completorium…”

Marquises may be constrained from the ninth hour to Compline, and from Compline to the end of the day. [Weyer, hours](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

The later *Goetia* normalizes this as approximately:

- 3 p.m. to 9 p.m.;
- 9 p.m. to sunrise.

**[Tradition]** The Goetia also recommends working when the Moon is two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, or fourteen days old.

**[Later extrapolation]** Monday and a planetary hour of the Moon follow naturally from the Marquis–silver–Moon equation, but neither Weyer’s Shax entry nor its earliest English version explicitly commands “Monday in the hour of Luna.”

## The seal

### Origin

**[Documented text]** Weyer and Scot provide no seal for Shax. The seal belongs to the seventeenth-century *Goetia* layer.

### Verbal description

The ordinary Goetic seal is:

- a horizontally extended oval or capsule-like enclosure;
- crossed internally by an approximately horizontal staff or linked sequence of strokes;
- containing a loop or rounded form toward the left;
- an angular central junction;
- a rounded or compartmented right termination with small circular marks;
- and a small cross-like dependent mark below the left-central region.

The manuscripts do not name these individual elements or explain their symbolism. Claims that particular strokes encode a planet, Hebrew letter, or demonic genealogy are therefore later interpretation.

### Witness variants

| Witness | Character of the seal |
|---|---|
| Sloane 3825 | Peterson’s base figure; relatively compact, with the horizontal enclosure and internal jointed figure clearly visible |
| Sloane 2731 | Same recognizable emblem, but more freely drawn; curvature and terminal marks vary with the hand |
| Harley 6483 | Cursive version of the same sigil, used in a larger protective system involving the paired angel |
| Mathers–Crowley 1904 | Cleaned, regularized printer’s redrawing; visually more symmetrical than the manuscript hands |
| Modern reproductions | Frequently simplify the cross, loops, or small pellets; some mirror, thicken, or rotate elements |

The proper comparison is visual rather than verbal. The 1904 rendering can be viewed at [From Old Books](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/044-Seal-of-Shax/); Peterson’s Sloane 3825 figure appears immediately after the entry in his [electronic edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

**[Rudd tradition]** Harley 6483 does not merely offer an alternative artistic Shax seal. It places the demonic sigil within a protective angelic arrangement: the paired Shem angel and Psalm text are intended to control or counterbalance the spirit.

## 2. The biblical three verses

**[Documented Jewish tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three Hebrew verses traditionally counted as 72 letters each. They are written:

1. first verse in normal Hebrew direction;
2. second verse reversed;
3. third verse in normal direction.

Reading vertically yields 72 columns of three letters, or 216 letters in all. Column 44 gives **ילה**, *YLH*. [Exodus 14:19–21](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Kabbalah)

**[Historical caution]** *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally meant the “explicit” or uniquely articulated divine name, ordinarily the Tetragrammaton. Jewish sources have also applied related terminology to names of 12, 42, or 72 letters. The phrase did not always mean the later European list of 72 guardian angels.

## 3. Medieval Jewish transmission

### *Sefer ha-Bahir*

**[Documented text]** *Bahir* §110, known in Provence by the later twelfth century, describes deriving the 72-fold name from the three Exodus verses in the forward–reverse–forward arrangement. This is the earliest extant explanation of the exact construction commonly cited in scholarship. [Bahir §§109–110](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-109-110-targum/)

### *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*

**[Tradition]** The composite medieval *Sefer Raziel* transmits forms of the 72-fold divine name and magical uses of divine names. Its constituent materials are of different dates, and the printed edition appeared in Amsterdam in 1701.

**[Caution]** It is inaccurate to attribute the entire later system—angelic suffixes, Psalms, zodiacal quinances, birth dates, and opposing Goetic demons—to a single original *Sefer Raziel*. Those components accumulated across Jewish and Christian kabbalistic transmission.

## 4. Christian Kabbalah: Reuchlin to Kircher

**[Documented text]** Johannes Reuchlin gave extensive treatment to the 72 angelic names in *De arte cabalistica* (1517), combining the three-letter divine forms with angelic endings such as *-el* and *-iah*. His work was a decisive bridge into Christian Cabala. [Study of Reuchlin’s lists](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1zjg3h9)

Agrippa and Blaise de Vigenère subsequently circulated related forms.

**[Documented text]** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54) displayed the 72 names in an elaborate diagram and associated them with nations, divine names in different languages, and zodiacal structure.

**[Scholarly judgment]** Kircher’s linguistic claims are historically important but not reliable comparative linguistics. Later authors inherited some of his supposed foreign names for God.

## 5. Rudd’s pairing with Shax

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** Harley 6483 pairs the 72 Goetic spirits with the 72 Shem angels by matching ordinal numbers:

> Spirit 44 Shax ↔ angel 44 Yelahiah.

The ordinary *Ars Goetia* does not contain these angelic opponents. The pairing belongs to the Rudd recension.

### How the angel constrains the spirit

**[Tradition]** In the Rudd system:

- the spirit’s seal is joined to or backed by its corresponding angelic seal/name;
- a Psalm verse appropriate to the angel is supplied;
- the angelic side of the lamen is directed toward the magician’s body or heart;
- the angel is invoked as a superior, constraining power over the corresponding demon.

The rationale is protective and hierarchical: like is not opposed to like by moral persuasion, but by a divine name and angel considered higher in the chain of authority.

**[Historical caution]** This is a Christian ceremonial-magical synthesis. Jewish Kabbalah does not contain an ancient teaching that Yelahiah was created specifically to police Shax.

### Authorship controversy

**[Documented manuscript fact]** Harley 6483 was copied in 1712–13 and identifies material as sheets of “Dr Rudd.” [Peterson’s manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

**[Skinner–Rankine reconstruction]** Because Thomas Rudd died in 1656 and the source uses Robert Turner’s 1655 English *Heptameron*, Skinner and Rankine propose a lost Rudd original from 1655–56, later copied by Peter Smart.

**[Disputed]** Other researchers emphasize that the surviving manuscript is decades later and contains material from various printed sources. A. E. Waite even suspected “Dr Rudd” might be a literary construction of the copyist. The safest formulation is:

> Harley 6483 transmits a Rudd-attributed recension; Thomas Rudd’s personal authorship of every component is not established.

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine published it as *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* in 2007. [WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/637468388)

## 6. The Psalm verse

Harley 6483 and later sources give:

> “Voluntaria oris mei beneplacita fac Domine, et judicia tua doce me.”

This is Vulgate Psalm 118:108, numbered Psalm 119:108 in Hebrew and most Protestant Bibles.

Hebrew:

> נִדְבוֹת פִּי רְצֵה־נָא יְהוָה וּמִשְׁפָּטֶיךָ לַמְּדֵנִי

A close translation is:

> “Accept, please, the freewill offerings of my mouth, YHWH, and teach me your judgments.”

[Psalm 119:108](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.119.108)

The verse’s references to accepted speech and divine judgments later suited Yelahiah’s associations with successful petitions, magistrates, and litigation.

## 7. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives the earliest fully developed printed profile in the requested French chain, pp. 75–76:

> “44e. Ielahiah. Son attribut (Dieu éternel).”

Lenain states that:

- Yelahiah’s ray extends from the 216th through 220th degree;
- it corresponds to the twenty-second “decade” and the genius Sentacer;
- the angel presides on 2 May, 13 July, 23 September, 4 December, and 14 February;
- the angel is invoked for success in a useful enterprise;
- Psalm 118:108 is used;
- Yelahiah gives protection from magistrates, success in lawsuits, protection against weapons, and victory;
- a person born under the influence loves educational travel and may earn fame through military talent and bravery;
- the contrary genius governs war, its resulting scourges, broken capitulations, and massacres of prisoners.

[Lenain’s 1823 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf)

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Lenain glosses the name “God Eternal.”

**[Kircherian inheritance]** He says Yelahiah corresponds to *Bosa*, supposedly a Mexican sacred name. This is an early-nineteenth-century occult-linguistic claim inherited from Kircher’s universalizing system, not a finding of modern Nahuatl or Mesoamerican linguistics.

### Degrees and dates

Lenain’s “216th to 220th degrees” are ordinal degrees of the full zodiac. Later tables normalize this as **5°–10° Scorpio**.

His five presiding calendar days are distributed through the year. They are not the consecutive dates 29 October–2 November.

## 8. Golden Dawn

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn divided the zodiac into 72 quinances of five degrees each and assigned Shem angels to them. Yelahiah occupies the second half of the first Scorpio decan:

- Veualiah: 0°–5° Scorpio;
- Yelahiah: 5°–10° Scorpio.

The whole 0°–10° decan corresponds to:

- **Five of Cups**;
- Mars in Scorpio;
- the Golden Dawn title “Lord of Loss in Pleasure.”

Thus every pip card from Two through Ten carries two consecutive Shem angels.

**[Caution]** The Five of Cups is Yelahiah’s angelic pip correspondence. It is not Shax’s Crowley/777 card.

## 9. Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

### Papus

**[Documented reception]** Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced and promoted the Lenain material in his writings on Kabbalah and sponsored a new edition of *La science cabalistique* in 1909. His versions helped move Lenain’s angelic profiles into Martinist and French occult circulation. [BnF record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30789329z)

### Robert Ambelain

**[Documented reception]** Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique*, first published in 1951, reorganized this material into a modern practical angel-magic system. It helped standardize spellings, angelic seals, hierarchies, zodiacal sectors, and ritual applications.

**[Disputed]** Ambelain later warned that some seals attributed to the 72 divine names had been transmitted incorrectly. Accordingly, modern “Yelahiah seals” are not uniformly medieval or textually secure.

### Haziel

**[Modern esoteric development]** The author Haziel, François Bernard-Termès, popularized the system of 72 personal guardian angels for mass readership. In his calendar:

- Yelahiah governs people born **29 October–2 November**;
- the angel grants clarity, justice, favors, success in lawsuits, and protection from aggression or imprisonment.

[Haziel text](https://fr.scribd.com/document/1072376187/Notre-Ange-Gardien-Nous-Protege-Haziel-Z-library-sk-1lib-sk-Z-lib-sk)

This continuous birthday calendar is later than Lenain’s scattered five-day list. It is now often presented online as though it were the original kabbalistic calendar, but it is a modern harmonization of the five-degree zodiacal scheme with civil dates.

### Crowley’s *777*

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** Crowley’s tables divide the 72 Goetic spirits into 36 spirits “by day” and 36 “by night,” running twice through the 36 zodiacal decans. Shax, number 44, is the eighth spirit of the night sequence:

- Aries I–III: spirits 37–39;
- Taurus I–III: 40–42;
- Gemini I: 43;
- **Gemini II: 44, Shax**.

Accordingly:

- 10°–20° Gemini;
- night;
- Mars decan;
- Nine of Swords;
- paired on the same pip with day spirit 8, Barbatos.

Crowley’s table describes Shax succinctly as a stock-dove with a sore throat and marks the spirit with the lunar symbol, reflecting the Marquis rank. [Liber 777 table](https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/crowley/liber/libers/liber777.pdf)

The later *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* makes the assignment explicit: “10°–20° Gemini (night), June 1–10, Nine of Swords, Luna–Silver.” [Illustrated Goetia, pp. 162–63](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf)

**Correction to a widespread conflation:** the Crowley placement is a ten-degree **decan**, not a five-degree quinance.

### Astrological timing

Four timing systems now circulate:

1. **Weyer’s rank hours:** ninth hour to Compline and onward to day’s end.
2. **Goetia’s normalized hours:** 3–9 p.m. and 9 p.m.–sunrise.
3. **Crowley’s decan:** 10°–20° Gemini, approximately 1–10 June in modern tropical calendars.
4. **Yelahiah’s quinance:** 5°–10° Scorpio, approximately 29 October–2 November in the later guardian calendar.

**[Tradition]** Some practitioners combine all four with Monday, a lunar hour, a waxing Moon, silver, and the Nine of Swords. This composite procedure is modern; no single early manuscript prescribes all those conditions together.

### Enochian overlay

**[Rudd-attributed innovation]** Material associated with Harley 6482 attempts to expand initials or components of John Dee’s *Tabula Sancta* into Goetic names. Skinner and Rankine connect this with Rudd’s broader angelic system.

**[Disputed]** Robert Turner reportedly rejected aspects of the interpretation, and the editors themselves acknowledge uncertainty about whether the construction was Rudd’s invention or derived from lost Dee papers.

**[Modern development]** Lon Milo DuQuette’s *Tarot of Ceremonial Magick* deliberately combines Enochian, Goetic, tarot, and astrological systems. That is an explicit modern synthesis, not a recovered seventeenth-century Shax doctrine.

## Iconography

## 1. The manuscript seals

The seals are Shax’s earliest distinctive visual identity. Nothing resembling the Goetic sigil has been located in Weyer, Scot, or an ancient religious source.

**[Documented text]** The manuscripts call the seal a mark to be made and worn as a lamen. They do not say that it depicts a bird, horse, Moon, sensory organ, or Hebrew word.

**[Modern invention]** Claims that the seal’s loops specifically represent stolen sight, a bird’s beak, or lunar gates are contemporary symbolic readings.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

Jacques Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818. Its entry uses Weyer’s form **Scox ou Chax**.

In the sixth edition, Paris, Henri Plon, 1863, pp. 601–02, the entry begins:

> “Scox ou Chax, duc et grand marquis des enfers.”

[1863 entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Scox)

De Plancy says that Scox/Chax:

- is duke and great marquis of Hell;
- has a hoarse voice and a disposition toward lying;
- appears as a stork;
- steals money and returns it after 1,200 years if ordered;
- takes horses;
- does not always keep promises;
- lies outside the triangle but speaks truth about supernatural things when enclosed;
- points out hidden treasures not guarded by evil spirits;
- commands thirty legions.

**[Documented adaptation]** De Plancy follows Weyer rather than the stock-dove Goetia. He also makes the implication explicit: inside the triangle, Scox “tells the truth.”

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

The 1863 edition includes Louis Le Breton’s engraving, reproduced on the plate around p. 617. [High-resolution public-domain image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ill_dict_infernal_p0617_scox.jpg)

The portrait shows:

- a long-legged, long-necked stork;
- its body in profile;
- a pronounced, straight beak;
- the bird grasping or leading a small horse by its mane;
- an otherwise sparse ground and background.

The horse turns the textual office—“he carries off horses”—into the defining visual joke. Unlike many Le Breton demons, Scox is not anthropomorphized: the image remains essentially zoological.

**[Documented publication history]** The 1818 edition had the dictionary entry but not this famous portrait. Le Breton’s demon illustrations belong to the illustrated 1863 sixth edition.

## 3. Modern art

Later artists usually choose one of four approaches:

1. **Le Breton inheritance:** a sinister stork with a horse.
2. **Goetia literalism:** a dark or spectral stock-dove.
3. **Anthropomorphic demon:** a bird-headed marquis in aristocratic dress.
4. **Abstract sigil art:** the seal enlarged as the principal image.

David P. Wilson’s artwork in *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* belongs to a late-twentieth-century attempt to supply individual visualizations for every spirit. Video games and television abandon the bird form more freely.

There is no uniquely authoritative “true portrait.” The only early descriptions are textual bird forms; the first famous portrait dates to 1863.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

**[Documented history]** Weyer published the earliest surviving Shax entry in 1577. His broader purpose in *De praestigiis daemonum* was to oppose the persecution of alleged witches, whom he considered deluded or afflicted rather than powerful servants of Satan.

He nevertheless believed in spirits and magic. Calling the *Pseudomonarchia* purely satirical understates his position: it is polemical and deliberately disabled, but it preserves genuine magical source material.

### Reginald Scot

Scot reproduced the catalogue in 1584 as part of a skeptical attack on witchcraft prosecutions and clerical deception. His English version supplied the wording from which the later Goetia compilers drew.

### The anonymous Goetia compiler or compilers

The first book of the *Lemegeton* was assembled in seventeenth-century England from Scot’s catalogue and other ritual material. Its principal additions to Shax were:

- number 44;
- the stock-dove;
- a spirit seal;
- use of the seal as a lamen;
- the more expansive “fetch horses or anything.”

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Harley 6483 attributes its enriched system to “Dr Rudd”; Peter Smart was apparently responsible for copying or organizing the surviving sheets in 1712–13. Their relationship to a lost mid-seventeenth-century original remains disputed.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers prepared the manuscript-derived text that Crowley published in 1904. He regularized spelling and layout, supplied a classified list by rank, and introduced *Shaz/Shass* into the printed Shax tradition.

### Aleister Crowley

Crowley published the 1904 edition, added prefatory material, and later placed Shax in the night-Goetia table of *Liber 777*. The 777 decan is a separate overlay, not present in Weyer or the manuscripts’ Shax paragraph.

### Arthur Edward Waite

Waite paraphrased Shax, usually as Shax/Chax/Scox, in *The Book of Black Magic and of Pacts* and later *The Book of Ceremonial Magic*. He treated the material critically but helped transmit it to twentieth-century readers. [Waite scan](https://logoilibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Book-of-Ceremonial-Magic.pdf)

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding:** no primary source was found documenting a Shax-specific operation or doctrine by Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad. His writings on Qabalah, number, and tarot make him relevant to the milieu, not demonstrably to Shax.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* presents a modern operational Goetia using dark-mirror techniques, elemental directions, and dramatic reconstruction. Shax is included as one of the 72, but no independently documented, historically important Shax operation by Runyon was identified. [Runyon’s book page](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition)

### Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson

Their *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* gives Shax a dedicated entry with the Gemini night decan, Nine of Swords, and Luna–silver. DuQuette’s later work further integrates Goetia with tarot and Enochian magic.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 *Lesser Key of Solomon* is the principal modern critical edition. His work identifies Scot as the immediate textual source of much of the Goetia, documents variants, and distinguishes Sloane 3825 from Mathers’ readings.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* brought Harley 6483’s angel–demon system into print and argued for a lost 1655–56 Rudd exemplar. Their transcription is indispensable; their dating and authorship conclusions remain arguments rather than settled manuscript facts.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium*, *Geosophia*, and related writings compare spirit catalogues and recover necromantic and grimoire contexts obscured by purely psychological readings. No distinctive Shax etymology or documented personal Shax operation was found that supersedes the source chain above.

### S. Connolly and demonolatry

**[Modern esoteric invention]** S. Connolly’s demonolatry books recast Shax as a spirit approached devotionally rather than coerced. The modern “enn”:

> *Ayer avage Shax aken*

is not found in Weyer, Scot, the Goetia manuscripts, Rudd, de Plancy, Mathers, or Crowley. It belongs to modern demonolatry.

Modern descriptions of Shax as temporarily removing possessions so that devotees learn gratitude likewise represent contemporary theology or personal gnosis.

## Grimoire ancestors and possible relatives

## 1. *Livre des Esperitz*

This French catalogue survives in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29 and is generally dated to the fifteenth century. It contains many spirits ancestral or parallel to Weyer’s catalogue.

**Negative finding:** no Shax, Chax, or Scox appears. [Full French transcription](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier)

Jean-Patrice Boudet’s comparative study shows that many Weyer spirits have *Livre des Esperitz* counterparts, but Shax is not one of the demonstrated correspondences. [Boudet’s study](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019)

## 2. Munich handbook, Clm 849: *Svcax*

The fifteenth-century Munich magical handbook contains a spirit **Svcax/Sucax**, described as:

- a great marquis;
- a man with a woman’s face;
- seemingly benevolent;
- giving women’s love, especially widows’;
- teaching languages;
- transporting people rapidly;
- governing 23 legions.

[Peterson’s transcription and translation](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Disputed possible relationship]** *Svcax* superficially resembles *Scox/Chax* in name and rank. But the appearance, offices, temperament, and legion count are completely different. Peterson concludes that Svcax has no clear Weyer or Goetia equivalent and relates it instead to **Suchay** in the *Book of Oberon*.

It should not be identified with Shax without new evidence.

## 3. *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer’s named source belongs to this family, and the Folger manuscript later published as *The Book of Oberon* preserves an extensive English *Offices of Spirits* tradition.

**Negative finding:** no securely identifiable Shax entry has been found in the currently published parallel material. The lost or unseen branch used by Weyer may nevertheless have contained it.

## 4. *Book of Abramelin*

The Abramelin spirit lists overlap with later European demon catalogues in general, but no convincing Shax/Chax/Scox has been demonstrated.

## 5. *Testament of Solomon*

The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* supplies the broad legend of Solomon interrogating and binding demons, but its named demons and diagnostic narratives do not include Shax. It is a conceptual ancestor of Solomonic demon catalogues, not a direct textual Shax source. [Testament text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm)

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Sixty-nine or seventy-two spirits?

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s surviving catalogue contains 69 entries even though its Belial narrative refers to 72 kings imprisoned by Solomon.

The *Goetia* has 72 because it:

- adds **Vassago** at number 3;
- adds **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius** at numbers 70–72;
- omits Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bifas**.

That is 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Pruflas was already absent from Scot, and the Goetia follows Scot’s English errors. This is strong evidence that Scot, not a complete Latin Weyer edition, was the compiler’s immediate catalogue source. [Peterson’s comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Disputed explanation]** The Goetia may have been brought to 72 to match the 72 Shem names, but no surviving compiler’s statement proves this. The Rudd recension certainly exploits the equality later.

## 2. Why does Shax move from 37 to 44?

The Goetia substantially rearranges Weyer’s catalogue. Peterson remarks that the order can resemble a stack of leaves scrambled and reassembled. There is no accepted astrological, theological, or rank-based key explaining the entire reordering.

Shax’s change from 37 to 44 is therefore documented, but its reason is unknown.

## 3. Duke, “dark,” or Marquis?

- Weyer: duke and great marquis.
- Scot: “darke and a great marquesse.”
- Goetia: great marquis.
- De Plancy: duke and great marquis.

**Best reconstruction:** Scot’s “darke” reflects corruption or mistranslation of *dux*. The Goetia then dropped the damaged first rank, while de Plancy returned to Weyer.

## 4. Stork versus stock-dove

The stork is the earliest and linguistically certain reading. Stock-dove is the manuscript Goetia reading. Both are authentic to different stages of the tradition.

It is incorrect to silently replace one with the other. It is also incorrect to claim that Shax changes from stork to dove on command; no source describes such a transformation.

## 5. “The second form”

No demanded second form exists. The familiar Goetic pattern “first an animal, then a man” occurs for other spirits such as Caim, Orobas, and Ose, but not for Shax.

Claims that Shax becomes human, beautiful, or aristocratic inside the triangle are later visualization or fiction.

## 6. Truth in the triangle

The primary sequence is:

- Weyer: liar unless introduced into the triangle; once there, speaks about divine things and hidden treasures.
- Scot: speaks “divinely.”
- Goetia: tells lies unless first commanded into the triangle.
- De Plancy: explicitly says he tells the truth when enclosed.

Thus “the triangle forces truth” is a fair traditional summary, but de Plancy states it more categorically than Weyer.

## 7. The 1,200-year money story

**[Documented text]** Shax carries royal money back after 1,200 years if commanded.

**Not documented:**

- that he gives the money to the poor;
- that he returns it to the magician;
- that the period refers to a celestial throne;
- that the number has a known astrological cycle;
- that it predicts an apocalypse.

The sentence may be a joke about effectively permanent theft, but that interpretation is not stated by Weyer.

## 8. “Thirty legions on evil horses”

This popular internet wording is a grammatical error. The texts say separately that Shax takes or fetches horses and commands thirty legions. They do not say the legions ride evil horses.

## 9. Shax and Svcax

Similarity of name and the shared title “great marquis” make comparison legitimate. The divergent appearance, offices, temperament, and 23-versus-30 legion count make identification unjustified.

## 10. An ancient deity behind Shax

No evidence supports one. Shax differs here from demon names that can be traced through polemical transformation to a named deity.

The desire to supply every Goetic name with a Babylonian, Canaanite, Egyptian, Greek, or Hebrew source has generated many false etymologies. For Shax, uncertainty is the responsible conclusion.

## 11. Is Yelahiah Shax’s eternal opponent?

**[Rudd tradition]** Yes, within Harley 6483’s ordinal system.

**[Historical answer]** No evidence places that pairing in medieval Jewish Kabbalah, Weyer, Scot, or the ordinary Goetia. It is a Christian magical construction first surviving in the Rudd-attributed manuscript.

## 12. Which zodiac is “correct”?

Both assignments are internally correct but belong to different overlays:

- Yelahiah: five-degree Scorpio sector.
- Shax in *777*: ten-degree Gemini night decan.

Websites that give Shax 5°–10° Scorpio have transferred the angel’s quinance to the demon. Websites that give Yelahiah the Nine of Swords have transferred Shax’s 777 decan back to the angel.

## 13. “Shan”

The heading is common online because of OCR and copying. It has not been verified in the requested Sloane/Harley source chain. It should be labeled a modern transcriptional variant unless an actual manuscript witness is produced.

## Open questions

1. **What did Weyer’s immediate manuscript read?**  
   The printed doublet *Chax/Scox* proves earlier variation, but the manuscript itself is lost or unidentified.

2. **Does an unnoticed pre-1577 witness contain Shax?**  
   Absence from currently compared catalogues is significant but not proof that no such manuscript survives.

3. **How exactly did *ciconia/stork* become “stock-dove”?**  
   Scribal error and contamination from nearby bird spirits are plausible; no intermediate witness explains it.

4. **Where did Mathers obtain *Shaz* and *Shass*?**  
   His 1904 edition provides no sufficient apparatus.

5. **Was the Goetia reordered intentionally?**  
   Astrological, angelic, and rank-based explanations have been proposed, but none accounts securely for the full sequence.

6. **Was Thomas Rudd personally responsible for the Shem opposition scheme?**  
   The surviving manuscript is Rudd-attributed and late. Skinner and Rankine’s lost-1655/56-exemplar hypothesis is possible, not proven.

7. **What was intended by the 1,200 years?**  
   The syntax concerns the return of stolen money; the symbolic reason for the number remains unknown.

8. **Do the tiny seal variations carry meaning?**  
   The manuscripts provide no key. They may be ordinary scribal variation rather than encoded doctrine.

9. **Can Shax be connected with Svcax/Suchay?**  
   Only new intermediate textual evidence could overcome the substantial differences.

10. **Are further modern appearances official or fan-assigned?**  
    Helluva Boss and several game-list claims require primary confirmation rather than resemblance or fan nomenclature.

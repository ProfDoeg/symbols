# Berith: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes seven kinds of statement:

- **Documented text** — expressly stated in a manuscript, printed primary source, or securely identified edition.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a historical or textual inference proposed by modern scholarship.
- **Tradition** — a claim transmitted within demonology, hagiography, Kabbalah, or ceremonial magic, without implying historical fact.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — an occult attribution created by combining symbolic systems.
- **Disputed** — evidence exists, but the identification, date, derivation, or interpretation remains contested.
- **Legend** — a narrative presented as supernatural history by its source.
- **Modern invention** — a recent elaboration not found in the older textual chain.

Because reproduction limits prevent printing three complete entries verbatim, the exact primary texts are linked, short diagnostic excerpts are quoted, and every clause of the entries is reported in full by close paraphrase.

## The name and its origins

### 1. Baal-Berith of Shechem

**Documented text.** The oldest relevant name is not that of a Goetic demon but the biblical divine title בַּעַל בְּרִית, *Baʿal Bərît*, “Lord/Master of Covenant.” Judges 8:33 says that the Israelites made Baal-Berith their god; Judges 9:4 locates a treasury or temple of Baal-Berith at Shechem; Judges 9:46 calls what may be the same sanctuary the house of אֵל בְּרִית, *El Berith*, “God of Covenant.”

Hebrew בְּרִית, *bərît*, conventionally means covenant, treaty, compact, or binding agreement. *Baʿal* means lord, owner, or master; *El* can mean “god” or be the divine name El. These biblical titles therefore mean approximately “Lord of the Covenant” and “God of the Covenant.”

**Disputed.** The deeper etymology of *bərît* is unresolved. Proposed cognates include Akkadian *birītu*, “space between” or “fetter,” Akkadian *birīt*, “between,” a Hurrian term, and indigenous Northwest Semitic roots. The frequently repeated claim that the word simply “comes from Akkadian *birītu*, to fetter” overstates one disputed proposal. The Semantics of Ancient Hebrew Database explicitly catalogues the competing derivations and objections. [SAHD, *bərît*](https://sahd-online.com/words/brith/)

**Scholarly reconstruction.** Berith’s Goetic name probably alludes to Baal-Berith. The evidence is cumulative:

1. Weyer gives both **Beal** and **Berith** as names of the same spirit.
2. Weyer says the Jews call him Berith.
3. Baal-Berith is a conspicuous biblical name.
4. Later demonologists, especially Collin de Plancy, explicitly identify the spirit as the former Shechemite idol.

But Weyer never writes the complete expression “Baal-Berith,” and no surviving textual bridge proves that his source deliberately demonized the Shechemite cult. It is therefore a strong reconstruction, not a demonstrated authorial etymology.

**Disputed.** Scholars have debated whether Baal-Berith and El-Berith were:

- two gods;
- two titles of one Shechemite patron deity;
- a local manifestation of El or Baal;
- a divine guarantor of a political covenant or civic confederation;
- or, in a minority reconstruction, an early title of Yahweh.

R. E. Clements and Theodore J. Lewis remain central discussions. [Clements, “Baal-Berith of Shechem”](https://doi.org/10.1093/jss/13.1.21); [Lewis, “The Identity and Function of El/Baal Berith”](https://doi.org/10.2307/3266894)

No ancient inscription securely identifies an extra-biblical deity named exactly Baal-Berith with the later mounted Duke.

### 2. Beirut, Berytus, Beruth, Pallas, and Diana

**Disputed antiquarian theory.** Early modern and eighteenth-century writers connected Baal-Berith with Beirut—classical *Berytus*—or with **Beruth/Berouth**, a figure in traditions attributed to Sanchuniathon. Collin de Plancy repeats the suggestion that Beruth might have been Pallas or Diana.

The *Dictionnaire de Trévoux* records arguments associating Baal-Berith with Berytus but also notes the philological difficulty: the Hebrew spellings and meanings of *Berith* and Beirut are not equivalent. Modern historical linguistics does not accept “Berith = Beirut” as established. [Trévoux, “Baal-Berith”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_de_Tr%C3%A9voux/6e_%C3%A9dition,_1771/BAAL-BERITH)

There is no secure Greek or Latin etymon behind the Goetic name. Latin *Berith* is a transcription, not a meaningful Latin word.

### 3. The Passion of Bartholomew

**Documented hagiographic tradition.** A demon or idol with a related name predates Weyer by centuries. In the apocryphal *Passion of Bartholomew*, the apostle enters a temple of Astaroth. Astaroth becomes unable to speak or heal. Worshippers consult another oracle or idol called **Beireth**, **Becher**, or, in the Pseudo-Abdias/Latin tradition, **Berith**, who explains that Bartholomew has bound Astaroth.

The scholarly NASSCAL catalogue uses **Beireth**. A traditional English translation has **Becher**, with a note that the Pseudo-Abdias history reads Berith and connects it to Judges 9. [NASSCAL, *Passion of Bartholomew*](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/passion-of-bartholomew/)

**Important distinction.** This is a genuine pre-Weyer attestation of the demon/idol name, but not yet the complete Goetic Berith. The hagiographic figure:

- is an Indian oracle-idol;
- reports why Astaroth is silent;
- describes Bartholomew;
- is not called a Duke;
- does not ride a red horse;
- has no alchemical office;
- has no twenty-six legions;
- and has no Goetic seal.

Identity with the later catalogue spirit is possible but unproven.

### 4. The *Golden Legend*

**Documented text.** Jacobus de Voragine’s thirteenth-century *Golden Legend* incorporated the Bartholomew narrative. William Caxton’s 1483 English version calls the second idol **Berith**. A short exact excerpt reads:

> “another idol was worshipped named Berith”

Berith tells the petitioners that Astaroth is held in fiery chains because the apostle has entered his temple. [Caxton’s *Golden Legend*, Bartholomew](https://www.christianiconography.info/goldenLegend/bartholomew.htm)

This provides a medieval vernacular channel by which the name was available before Renaissance spirit catalogues.

### 5. Fifteenth- and sixteenth-century spirit catalogues

#### Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 89 sup. 38

**Documented text, limited evidence.** A fifteenth-century Latin spirit catalogue in Plut. 89 sup. 38 contains a spirit-name **Berit** among names accompanied by sigils. The surviving index confirms the occurrence. [Peterson’s magical-name index](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/nameindx.htm)

The entry has not been shown to carry all the later Berith offices. It proves that *Berit* occurred as a magical spirit-name before Weyer, not that the finished Weyer portrait already existed.

#### *Livre des esperitz*

**Documented text.** Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29 contains **Berteth**, no. 36 in its catalogue. Biblissima dates the manuscript broadly to the later fifteenth or early sixteenth century, while Jean-Patrice Boudet’s palaeographical assessment places the surviving copy in the middle of the sixteenth century. [Biblissima manuscript record](https://portail.biblissima.fr/ark:/43093/mdata454dde45506284fbdb223f1ab242101b25c2e133); [Boudet 2003](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171)

A short exact excerpt is:

> “Berteth est ung grant duc qui appiert en belle figure”

The entry then gives him a crown, true answers, knowledge of converting metals into gold or silver, the power to grant and confirm lordships, and twenty-six legions. [*Livre des esperitz*, Berteth](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier)

This is the clearest surviving ancestor of the Weyer entry. Already present are:

- ducal rank;
- a crown;
- truthful answers;
- metallic transmutation;
- political or social elevation;
- confirmation of that elevation;
- twenty-six legions.

Not yet present are the red soldier, red clothing, red horse, magical ring, subtle voice, and explicit accusation of lying.

#### Folger MS V.b.26, *Officium de spiritibus*

**Documented text.** This Elizabethan manuscript, approximately 1577–1583, contains a Berith under one of the directional kings. It says that he grants dignities, turns metals into gold and silver, tells past things, appears as a knight riding a horse, wears **two red crowns**, and speaks slowly unless mastered and strongly constrained. [Folger transcription, p. 82](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/liber-officiorum/read/)

This is a valuable independent variant:

- knight rather than generic soldier;
- gold **and silver**, preserving the *Livre des esperitz* form;
- two red crowns rather than one crown of unspecified material or the later gold crown;
- slow speech unless constrained, rather than a naturally clear and subtle voice.

### 6. Weyer, Scot, and the *Goetia*

**Documented text.** Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* was appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. The demon catalogue was not part of the 1563 first edition. Weyer identifies his source as a *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* or a book received from Solomon about demonic princes and kings. [Weyer, Peterson edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

Reginald Scot printed Abraham Fleming’s English version in *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* in 1584. Scot’s larger purpose was sceptical and anti-persecution: he printed magical texts while arguing against credulous witch prosecutions.

The seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* compiler then used the Weyer–Scot material, rearranged the catalogue, expanded it to seventy-two, added seals and ritual directions, and produced the form now called the *Ars Goetia*. Peterson’s online edition is based on Sloane MS 3825 and collates Sloane 3648, Sloane 2731, Harley 6483, and other witnesses. [Peterson, *Goetia* introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

## Number and order

Weyer’s text says seventy-two spirits were confined, but the surviving *Pseudomonarchia* catalogue contains only **sixty-nine**.

The *Goetia* reaches seventy-two by:

- omitting Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**;
- adding **Vassago** as no. 3;
- adding **Seere** as no. 70;
- adding **Dantalion** as no. 71;
- adding **Andromalius** as no. 72.

Thus: 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

The order is also rearranged extensively; this is not simply Weyer’s list with four additions. Berith is:

- Weyer §27;
- the twenty-sixth surviving demon in Scot if the omitted Pruflas is not silently counted;
- Goetia no. 28 after Vassago’s insertion and the broader rearrangement.

**Scholarly reconstruction.** Peterson suggests that the *Goetia* was normalized to seventy-two because of the religious and magical importance of the number and its potential pairing with the seventy-two Shem angels. Skinner and Rankine argue more strongly for an older angel–demon correspondence, but acknowledge that parts of that reconstruction remain speculative. [Peterson introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm); [Skinner and Rankine text](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

## The description across the sources

### Weyer’s Latin, 1577

Weyer’s entry is §27, printed on p. 921 of the 1577 edition. A diagnostic exact excerpt is:

> “Berith Dux magnus & terribilis: tribus nuncupatur nominibus, à quibusdam Beal, à Iudæis Berith, à necromanticis Bolfri.”

That is: Berith is a great and terrible Duke; some call him Beal, Jews Berith, and necromancers Bolfri. [Weyer §27](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

The remaining Latin states, clause by clause:

- he comes forth as a red soldier;
- his clothing is red;
- his horse is of the same colour;
- he is adorned with a crown;
- he answers truly about present, past, and future;
- at a specified hour he is compelled by divine power through a magical ring;
- he is nevertheless a liar;
- he changes every kind of metal into gold;
- he adorns people with dignities and confirms those dignities;
- he produces a clear and subtle voice;
- twenty-six legions are subject to him.

Important Latin terms include:

- *dux* — duke or military leader;
- *miles* — soldier, knight, or mounted warrior;
- *mendax* — lying, deceitful, a liar;
- *dignitates* — dignities, ranks, offices, or honours;
- *annulus magicæ artis* — ring of the magical art.

### Scot’s English, 1584

Scot prints the entry on p. 221. His exact opening is:

> “Berith is a great and a terrible duke, and hath three names. Of some he is called Beall”

The rest closely renders Weyer: Jews call him Berithi, necromancers Bolfry; he appears as a red soldier in red clothes on a horse of that colour, crowned; he answers about present, past, and future; a ring and divine virtue compel him at a certain hour; he is a liar; he makes metals gold; bestows and confirms dignities; speaks clearly and subtly; and commands twenty-six legions. [Scot, pp. 221–222](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm)

Scot’s catalogue has no Berith seal.

### Sloane MS 3825

**Documented text.** Sloane 3825, fol. 104r, is Peterson’s base witness. Its opening is:

> “The 28th spirit in order as salomon bound them, is named Berith.”

It calls him a “Mighty great and terrable duke.” The red soldier and horse remain; the crown is now specifically gold. The manuscript changes Weyer’s “three names” into Berith plus “two other Names” supposedly given by later people: Beal and Bolfry.

It retains every substantive Weyer office and expands the warning: Berith is a “great Lyer” and “not to be Trusted much.” It directs the operator to use the ring described for Beleth and says the seal must be worn as a lamen. [Sloane 3825 transcription, fol. 104r](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#berith)

### Sloane MS 3648

**Documented variant.** Sloane 3648 gives **Beale** and **Bofry**. Its substance is otherwise within the same *Goetia* recension. Peterson records this witness as “S2” in the printed critical apparatus.

### Sloane MS 2731

**Documented text.** Sloane 2731 reads Berith, Beal, and Bolfry, with **Bofry** noted as a reading from another copy. It ends by directing the practitioner to make and wear the seal “as a Lamin.” [Digital Sloane 2731 edition](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/)

### Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

**Documented text.** Harley 6483 gives Berith, Beale, and Bolfry. It calls him a “very great Liar” not to be trusted. Skinner and Rankine print it at pp. 129–130, together with the adversarial angelic material. [*Goetia of Dr Rudd*, pp. 129–130](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

### Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The entry occupies pp. 30–31. Its exact beginning is:

> “BERITH.—The Twenty-eighth Spirit in Order, as Solomon bound them, is named Berith.”

The body preserves:

- mighty, great, terrible Duke;
- aliases Beale/Beal and Bofry/Bolfry;
- red soldier;
- red horse;
- gold crown;
- true answers about past, present, and future;
- Beleth’s ring;
- metallic transmutation to gold;
- granting and confirming dignities;
- clear and subtle speech;
- twenty-six legions;
- the seal.

The conspicuous difference is that the body omits the manuscript sentence calling Berith a liar. Crowley’s footnote restores the problem indirectly:

> “This hardly agrees with the statement that he is a great liar”

Thus the 1904 edition did not erase the tradition completely, but displaced it from the description into editorial commentary. [Mathers–Crowley text, pp. 30–31](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

### Peterson, 2001

Peterson’s critical edition prints the Sloane 3825 wording on p. 19, silently expands or corrects some damaged words in brackets, and supplies a manuscript apparatus. It therefore restores the explicit “great Lyer” sentence to the main text and records:

- Sloane 3648: Beale and Bofry;
- Harley: Beale and Bolfry;
- Sloane 2731 marginal Bofry;
- Mathers–Crowley’s expanded alias set;
- Weyer/Scot’s forms Beall, Berithi, and Bolfry.

[Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon*, p. 19](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf)

### Offices not attributed to Berith

**Negative finding.** The historical descriptions do **not** say that Berith teaches languages or general sciences, gives familiars, produces invisibility, procures love, finds treasures, or changes his appearance on command. Those are offices belonging to other catalogue spirits.

There is likewise no prescribed “second form” that the conjurer may demand. The soldier on the red horse is his only specified form. Claims that he assumes a human, female, angelic, or alchemical form are later additions unless tied to a particular modern practitioner’s account.

### Truth and falsehood

The texts do not resolve the contradiction between true answers and habitual lying.

Possible interpretations—that he lies only when unconstrained, lies outside strictly framed questions, or uses false statements pedagogically—are **modern explanatory traditions**, not statements in Weyer or the *Goetia*.

### The ring

Weyer says a magical ring compels him at a certain hour. The *Goetia* refers back to Beleth. In the Beleth instructions, the operator wears a silver ring on the middle finger of the left hand and holds it before the face. [Peterson, Beleth and ring instructions](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

The ring’s original function in Berith’s source is coercive. The *Goetia* wording introduces the fuller Beleth procedure. It should not be confused with Astaroth’s immediately following entry, where the ring protects specifically against foul breath.

### Time of conjuration

Weyer gives only *ad horam*—at the appointed or certain hour—and supplies no Berith-specific clock time.

The *Goetia*’s general observations state:

- Dukes may be bound **from sunrise to noon**;
- the weather should be clear;
- the Moon should preferably be 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old.

These are general rank and lunar rules, not unique Berith revelations. [*Goetia*, Observations](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

The often copied statement that Berith is “strongest in June” has not been located in Weyer, Scot, the principal Lemegeton manuscripts, Rudd, Mathers–Crowley, or Collin de Plancy. It is best classified as an **unsupported modern attribution**.

### Rank, planet, and metal

The *Goetia* assigns metals by rank:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or “Prelate” | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver equally |

Mars/iron is notably absent.

Berith is therefore **Venus/copper because he is a Duke**. This correspondence belongs to the seventeenth-century *Goetia* ritual apparatus. It is not stated by the *Livre des esperitz*, Weyer, or Scot.

## The angel: Seheiah

## The seventy-two-fold Name

**Documented Jewish tradition.** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three consecutive Hebrew verses traditionally counted as seventy-two letters each. The later method writes:

1. verse 19 in the ordinary Hebrew direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in the ordinary direction.

Reading the resulting seventy-two vertical columns produces seventy-two three-letter groups. Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a explicitly describes this method and gives examples. [Rashi on Sukkah 45a](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Sukkah.45a.2)

**Historical qualification.** *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally meant the “explicit” or “distinctly pronounced” divine name, commonly the Tetragrammaton. Its automatic identification with a roster of seventy-two personal angels is a later development.

### Seheiah’s construction

The twenty-eighth three-letter group is normally read **שאה**, *Š-ʾ-H*. Christian Kabbalists append **-Yah** or **-El** to the roots, yielding angelic names. Here the result is approximately **Šeʾeh-Yah / Seehiah / Seheiah**.

Agrippa prints the name as **Seehiah** and gives the Hebrew letters in his table of the seventy-two angels bearing the name of God. [Agrippa, *Occult Philosophy* III.25](https://esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm)

“God who heals the ill” is not a literal dictionary translation of שאה. It is a **French occult attribute or interpretive gloss**, firmly documented in Lenain and Ambelain.

## Historical transmission

### *Sefer Raziel*

**Tradition and textual history.** *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is a medieval compilation containing older Jewish magical, cosmological, and angelological materials. The seventy-two-fold name is important in its tradition.

The legend that the complete book was given by the angel Raziel to Adam is theological folklore, not the work’s historical provenance. The Hebrew compilation is medieval; Latin *Raziel* materials circulated by the thirteenth century.

### Reuchlin

**Documented Christian Kabbalah.** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) presents the seventy-two roots as individualized angelic names by adding forms of *El* and *Yah*. This is a major bridge from Jewish divine-name speculation into Christian angelology.

### Agrippa

**Documented text.** In *De occulta philosophia* III.25, published in its complete form in 1533, Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa explains the three-verse arrangement and states that the names are set over seventy-two celestial quinaries—five-degree divisions. He prints **Seehiah** in the roster. [Agrippa III.25](https://esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm)

### Kircher

**Documented text.** Athanasius Kircher reproduced seventy-two-name material in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654), including tables and amuletic contexts. Kircher was not simply an advocate: he frequently treated badly transliterated Kabbalistic amulets as superstition or diabolic deception. [Kircher, *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm); [Kircher on the heptagonal amulet](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm)

### Rudd and Harley 6483

**Documented manuscript; disputed attribution.** Harley 6483 was copied in 1712/13. Skinner and Rankine argue that it transcribes a lost Thomas Rudd manuscript of approximately 1655/56. The early date is a reconstructed stemmatic argument, not a surviving autograph date.

Rudd’s distinctive operation uses a **double seal**:

- the demonic character faces outward;
- the corresponding Shem angel and Psalm occupy the controlling side;
- that angelic side is worn toward the operator’s heart.

The method is to invoke the angel first and command the demon under the angel’s superior authority. Skinner and Rankine describe the angel as an “antidote” to its corresponding demon. [Skinner and Rankine, manuscript discussion](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

Because the ordering is one-to-one, angel no. 28 constrains demon no. 28:

> **Seheiah/Seehiah → Berith**

The pairing is therefore verified for the Rudd recension. It is not present in Weyer, Scot, or the ordinary Sloane Berith entry.

### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* systematically combines each angel with:

- an interpretive divine attribute;
- a five-degree ray;
- a Psalm;
- a twenty-minute invocation period;
- five scattered calendar days;
- a national-language divine name;
- beneficial and adverse influences.

For Seheiah, Lenain’s system gives:

- no. 28;
- “God who heals the ill”;
- the Assyrian divine name **Adad**;
- ray 136–140;
- the fourteenth ten-degree division;
- secondary angel **Sithacer**;
- 9:00–9:20 by the twenty-minute sequence;
- protection from illness, thunder, fire, collapsing buildings, and accidents;
- health, longevity, judgment, prudence, and circumspection;
- adverse influence over catastrophes, accidents, apoplexy, and rash conduct.

[Lenain, Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365)

### Papus

Papus—Gérard Encausse—helped transmit Lenain’s angelology within French occultism, especially through *La Cabbale* and related Martinist teaching. Papus belongs to the history of dissemination; he did not originate Seheiah or the Rudd–Berith pairing.

### Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn incorporated the seventy-two angels into its system of:

- zodiacal quinances;
- decans;
- tarot pips;
- Hebrew divine names;
- angelic seals and Psalms.

**Important variant.** Golden Dawn materials can begin the angelic sequence at **0° Leo**, not 0° Aries. Under that convention, Seheiah is 15°–20° Sagittarius, occupying the latter quinance of the second Sagittarius decan and therefore sharing the **Nine of Wands** with Yerathel.

This is distinct from Lenain’s 0° Aries sequence, where Seheiah occupies 15°–20° Leo.

### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) explicitly says he reproduced Lenain’s texts and supplemented them with angelic seals from an eighteenth-century manuscript. His Seheiah section prints:

- “God Who Heals The Ill”;
- ray 136–140;
- Adad;
- Sithacer;
- Psalm 70;
- protection from illness, thunder, fire, ruined buildings, and collapse;
- health and longevity;
- prudence;
- adverse catastrophes and accidents.

The English translation prints 9:00–9:30, but the surrounding sequence moves in twenty-minute intervals: Yerathel 8:40–9:00 and Reiiel 9:20–9:40. The historically coherent reading is therefore 9:00–9:20. [Ambelain/Vaughan, pp. 13–14](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf)

### Haziel

Haziel’s *Communiquer avec son ange gardien* popularized a late twentieth-century guardian-angel calendar. In that system Seheiah becomes the guardian of people born during an August interval, commonly 7/8–12/13 August depending on edition and calculation.

This birthday-guardian presentation is a **modern popular esoteric system**. It should not be projected backward into Rudd, Agrippa, or Jewish Kabbalah. [Haziel bibliographic record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL12628940M/Communiquer_avec_son_ange_gardien)

## Psalm and numbering problem

The Latin is:

> *Deus, ne elongeris a me; Deus meus, in auxilium meum respice.*

Meaning:

> O God, do not remain far from me; my God, hasten to help me.

The same text may appear as:

- Vulgate Psalm **70:12**;
- Hebrew/Protestant Psalm **71:12**;
- Lenain’s “thirteenth verse,” probably counting the heading or following a variant enumeration;
- Skinner and Rankine’s “[Psalm 70:15],” which does not match the Latin and is probably an editorial mistake.

This is a numbering disagreement, not a different Psalm text.

### Why the planets disagree

Three different symbolic layers are being collapsed in many modern charts:

1. **Venus** comes from Berith’s rank as Duke.
2. **Jupiter** rules the first Capricorn decan and the Two of Disks.
3. **Saturn** rules Capricorn as a whole.

None cancels the others because they answer different questions.

### Crowley’s *777*

The Berith row in later illustrated Goetic tables reads essentially:

- Duke;
- first ten degrees Capricorn;
- day;
- approximately December 22–30;
- Two of Disks;
- Venus/copper.

[*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*, Berith table](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf)

Strictly speaking, *777* gives Berith a **decan**, not a personal five-degree quinance. Each tarot pip carries two Goetic spirits because the whole ten-degree decan has:

- one day spirit;
- one night spirit.

For the Two of Disks, these are Berith and Haures.

The card’s Golden Dawn title is “Lord of Harmonious Change.” Crowley’s *Book of Thoth* shortens it to **Change** and confirms Jupiter in Capricorn. [Crowley, *Book of Thoth*, “Small Cards”](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/small-cards)

### Enochian overlay

Crowley appended Enochian or “Angelic” translations of the conjurations to the 1904 *Goetia*. This does not make Berith an Enochian spirit and does not supply him with a historical Dee/Kelley counterpart.

Rudd’s associated Harley material also reworks John Dee’s Holy Table and other angelic diagrams. Skinner and Rankine describe this as an extension or interpretation of Dee. But the specific Berith–Seheiah relation derives from the **Shem** scheme, not from Dee’s Enochian spirit catalogues.

Claims that Berith possesses a specific Enochian aethyr, governor, or watchtower square require a named modern system; none appears in the principal early sources.

## Iconography

## The seal

### Earliest situation

**Documented absence.**

- The *Livre des esperitz* Berteth entry has no seal.
- Weyer prints no seals for the spirits.
- Scot prints no Berith seal.
- Folger V.b.26’s relevant subordinate Berith notice provides no corresponding Goetic lamen.

Peterson notes that the recognizable Goetic spirit-seals do not appear in the earlier *Offices of Spirits* manuscripts known to him. [Peterson introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Sloane 3825

Sloane 3825 places the seal beneath Berith’s fol. 104r entry. The figure is a compact, vertically oriented line-character. It combines:

- a central angular or polygonal structure;
- intersecting straight lines;
- small circles;
- lateral hooked flourishes;
- and a descending looped element terminating in a rounded form.

This is a description of the visible drawing, not an occult decoding. [Peterson’s Sloane-based figure](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#berith)

### Sloane 3648 and 2731

The Sloane witnesses retain the same recognizable character but vary in:

- curvature;
- placement and size of small circles;
- whether adjoining lines touch or cross;
- closure of loops;
- length and angle of terminal strokes;
- relative proportions.

Such changes are normal consequences of hand-copying. There is no textual rule identifying one microscopic stroke as the doctrinally “correct” original.

### Harley 6483

The Rudd recension simplifies or regularizes several demon seals and combines them with angelic controls. For Berith the relevant lamen is double-sided:

- Berith’s character and name belong to the outward, demonic face;
- Seheiah’s name, angelic seal, and Psalm belong to the reverse or controlling face.

Skinner and Rankine regard this as a more operationally complete form; that is an editorial and magical interpretation, not proof that it predates the ordinary Sloane seals.

### Mathers–Crowley seal

The 1904 engraving standardized the modern public image. A high-resolution rendering is available here: [Berith seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:28-Berith_seal.png).

**Modern invention.** Explanations that its strokes encode twelve hours, solar crosses, a Hebrew *yod*, a Latin *P*, a particular Sefer Raziel emblem, or a secret alphabet are not stated in the manuscripts. Without a demonstrated palaeographic chain, those are speculative symbolic readings.

## Collin de Plancy

The *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818. The securely verified detailed Berith article and portrait belong to the expanded sixth edition, 1863, pp. 93–94. The 1863 wording should not automatically be projected backward into the first edition.

A short exact excerpt reads:

> “Berith, duc aux enfers, grand et terrible.”

De Plancy then says that:

- he is known as Béal, Bérith, and Bolfri;
- he appears as a young soldier dressed in red from head to foot;
- he rides a horse of the same colour;
- he wears a crown;
- he answers about past, present, and future;
- magical rings master him;
- he is often a liar;
- he changes all metals into gold;
- he is consequently regarded as the demon of alchemists;
- he grants dignities;
- he makes singers’ voices clear and flexible;
- he commands twenty-six legions;
- he was the idol of the Shechemites;
- he may be Sanchuniathon’s Beruth, identified by some antiquarians with Pallas or Diana.

[*Dictionnaire infernal*, Berith](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Berith)

De Plancy’s “singers” are an interpretive innovation: Weyer merely says that Berith himself emits a clear and subtle voice.

### The Petit Albert story

**Legend.** De Plancy adds a story attributed to the *Solide trésor du Petit Albert*. In a château, an invisible familiar tends the clock and curries the horses. A stableman claims to have acquired it by:

- bleeding a small black hen at a crossroads;
- writing in its blood that Berith would do his work for twenty years in return for compensation;
- burying the hen a foot deep.

The familiar then manages the stable and finds valuables. De Plancy says the narrator considers it possibly a mandrake, while “Kabbalists” call it a sylph.

This is a domestic-spirit legend attached to Berith, not part of the Goetic catalogue.

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

Louis Le Breton’s 1863 illustration shows a youthful, crowned mounted warrior on a vigorous horse. It is black-and-white line art; the red clothing and red horse come from the adjacent description rather than printed colour. The rider is upright and commanding, and the crown and military costume translate Weyer’s terse *miles ruber* into Romantic-era infernal portraiture. [Public-domain Le Breton image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ill_dict_infernal_p0110-94_berith.jpg)

The engraving became far more influential on modern demon art than the manuscript seal.

## Modern visual culture

Modern artists commonly choose among three visual templates:

1. Le Breton’s crowned cavalryman;
2. a red-armoured medieval knight on a red or black horse;
3. a humanoid infernal noble carrying the Goetic seal.

The heavily armoured rider designed for Atlus’s *Megami Tensei* and *Persona* games is a recognizable modern development of the red mounted soldier, not a facsimile of Le Breton.

### Sébastien Michaëlis and the Aix possession

**Documented contemporary claim, not independently verified event.** Michaëlis’s *Histoire admirable* describes the 1610–1611 Aix-en-Provence possession and prosecution involving Madeleine de Demandolx and Louis Gaufridy. A demon called Berith/Baalberith was said to speak through the possessed woman and reveal a hierarchy of demons and their opposing saints.

Later demonographies summarize this Berith as:

- a former prince of Cherubim;
- a tempter to blasphemy and murder;
- opposed by Saint Barnabas.

These belong to the Michaëlis possession tradition, not Weyer’s catalogue entry. Later encyclopaedias merged the two Beriths because the names matched. The confession and possession claims emerged within coercive exorcism and a prosecution ending in Gaufridy’s torture and execution; they cannot be treated as independent biographical evidence about a supernatural being. [1613 English edition, Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/ts6zgua2)

“Secretary of Hell,” “keeper of the archives,” “minister of pacts,” “grand pontiff,” and “master of ceremonies” belong mainly to later **Baalberith** demonography. They are fitting expansions of the word “covenant” but do not occur in Berith’s Weyer or Goetia notice.

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding.** Frater Achad—Charles Stansfeld Jones—was important to Thelemic Qabalah, but no securely attested Berith-specific operation, doctrine, or substantial study by Achad was located. Including him as a Berith authority without a work and page would merely repeat association by milieu.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* belongs to the modern operative revival of Solomonic evocation. His tables identify Berith with the Goetic hierarchy and Seehiah in a modern synthesis. This is evidence of modern practice and systematization, not new historical evidence about the Renaissance source.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette’s role is chiefly editorial and explanatory through *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*. That book helped establish the compact modern reference line:

> Berith — Duke — Capricorn by day — Two of Disks — Venus/copper.

It should not be mistaken for the seventeenth-century manuscript’s own astrology.

### Skinner and Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* is indispensable for:

- Harley 6483;
- double seals;
- angel–demon pairings;
- Seheiah’s Psalm;
- comparison with related Harley material;
- arguments for a lost mid-seventeenth-century Rudd exemplar.

Their claim that the surviving manuscript represents an older branch is reasoned but remains a reconstruction.

### Peterson

Peterson supplies the most transparent public comparison of:

- Weyer’s Latin;
- Scot’s English;
- Sloane 3825;
- variants in Sloane 3648, Sloane 2731, and Harley 6483;
- spirit-name spellings;
- seals and general ritual observations.

His online edition is continually revised; it should be cited with its update date where exact wording matters.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s importance lies in redirecting occult discussion from the isolated 1904 text to earlier spirit-office catalogues, especially the *Livre des esperitz*. His work supports studying Berteth as part of a living catalogue tradition rather than automatically as a “fallen pagan god.”

## Grimoire ancestors

### *Testament of Solomon*

**Documented general ancestor.** The late antique *Testament of Solomon* depicts Solomon interrogating demons, learning their forms and offices, and compelling them through superior angels and divine names to work on the Temple.

No demon named Berith occurs securely in its standard catalogue. Its relevance is structural:

- Solomon questions spirits;
- each gives a name and function;
- many identify a thwarting angel;
- demons are constrained into service.

Rudd’s angel-over-demon procedure resembles that model, but no direct line from a Testament “Berith” exists.

### Munich Handbook, Clm 849

Richard Kieckhefer’s fifteenth-century necromancer’s manual contains conjurations and numerous names overlapping later Goetic catalogues. Skinner and Rankine list a **Berith** among the apparent overlaps, but the identities and readings in such damaged name-lists are difficult.

It is a genuine ancestor or parallel witness to late-medieval learned necromancy. Claims that it contains the complete later Berith entry or the standard Goetic seal are unsupported.

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

This is the most important textual family behind Berith. Weyer explicitly names an *Offices of Spirits* source; the Folger manuscript and related witnesses preserve catalogue structures, directional kings, appearances, offices, and legions closely related to Weyer and the *Goetia*.

### *Livre des esperitz*

Berteth is a particularly close precursor, as shown above. Boudet nevertheless warns against treating the surviving manuscript as simply “the original Goetia”: there are many differences, omissions, additions, and reordered spirits.

### *Book of Abramelin*

**Negative finding.** The Abramelin spirit lists contain numerous unstable names, but no exact Berith entry with the red horse, ducal rank, alchemy, and twenty-six legions has been securely identified. Abramelin is a contextual Renaissance demon catalogue, not a proven source for this entry.

## Berith in literature, comics, animation, and games

### Dante and Milton

**Negative finding.** Berith does not appear by name in Dante’s *Divine Comedy* or Milton’s *Paradise Lost*. Milton mentions Baalim and several biblical deities, but that is not an attested appearance of the Goetic Berith.

### Bartimaeus novels

**Negative finding.** No Berith character or securely identifiable Berith reference was located in Jonathan Stroud’s *Bartimaeus* sequence. The superficial resemblance between “Berith” and “Bartimaeus” is not evidence.

### *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona*

**Documented modern adaptation.** Berith is a recurring Atlus demon, first appearing in *Megami Tensei II* and subsequently in numerous *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona* titles. The design is generally a heavy, red-toned armoured cavalryman with glowing eyes, directly echoing the mounted soldier of the grimoire.

In *Persona 5* he is the **Brutal Cavalryman**; in one *Persona 5 Royal* context, **Insolent Cavalryman**. He is associated with the Hierophant Arcana there. [Megaten Wiki, Berith](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Berith); [Sega/Atlus Daily Demon page](https://asia.sega.com/megaten5/vengeance/en/daily_devil/)

The game lore adds attacks, resistances, Arcana, and “burning those without manners.” Those are modern game inventions, not grimoire offices.

A related Atlus figure, **Belberith**, appears in *Devil Survivor* as part of that game’s “Bel” mythology. This is a creative Baal-Berith adaptation and should not be used to reconstruct Weyer.

### *B.P.R.D./Hellboy*

**Documented modern fiction.** A demon Berith appears in *B.P.R.D.: Hell on Earth—The Exorcist* nos. 1–3, published April–June 2016. It attempts to enter the human world through a host named Trent and claims command of legions before Ashley Strode expels it. [Grand Comics Database, issue record](https://www.comics.org/issue/1625029/); [Dark Horse collected edition](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/539532/bprd-hell-on-earth-volume-14-the-exorcist-by-created-by-mike-mignola-written-by-mike-mignola-and-cameron-stewartillustrated-by-cameron-stewart-colorist-dave-stewart-cover-artist-laurence-campbell/)

This is part of the Hellboy universe, but not a character in the theatrical *Hellboy* films.

### *Helluva Boss* and *Hazbin Hotel*

**Negative finding as of September 2026.** No canonical Berith has appeared in the released series or official character lists. Numerous Berith designs online are fan-created Goetia characters. One fan presentation expressly says its Berith is an original interpretation and does not officially appear in the show. [Example explicitly marked as an OC](https://www.reddit.com/r/HazbinHotelOCArt/comments/1uhrs5p/berith_great_duke_of_hell/)

### “Beherit”

**Disambiguation.** Beherit is both a spelling occasionally associated with Baal-Berith and the name of a Finnish black-metal band. References to the band are not fictional appearances of the Goetic spirit unless the particular work expressly invokes the demonological name.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Does Berith occur before Weyer?

Yes, but at several different evidentiary levels.

- **Berith/Beireth** occurs in medieval Bartholomew hagiography.
- **Berit** occurs in a fifteenth-century magical catalogue.
- **Berteth** occurs in the *Livre des esperitz* with a recognizably related office.
- The complete red-soldier/red-horse/ring/liar form is first securely printed by Weyer in 1577.

Therefore “Berith has no pre-Weyer attestation” is false. “The complete canonical Goetic Berith is not securely attested before Weyer” is substantially correct.

### 2. Is Berith simply Baal-Berith?

**Probable but unproven.** The names, biblical context, and Weyer’s Beal/Berith pairing make the connection persuasive. No surviving source says that the Shechemite deity was historically a red cavalryman who transmuted metals.

### 3. Was Baal-Berith a distinct ancient god?

**Disputed.** “Baal of the Covenant” may be a title, local hypostasis, civic patron, form of El/Baal, or one of several divine identifications. The biblical text is hostile testimony, not a neutral temple archive.

### 4. Berith and Beirut

**Disputed and philologically weak.** The resemblance encouraged early modern antiquarians, but neither the Hebrew spelling nor the historical evidence securely derives Berith from Berytus.

### 5. Bolfry

**Open etymological problem.** Bolfri is already in Weyer and therefore not a Crowley misprint. The sequence Bolfri → Bolfry/Bofry/Bofi can be explained palaeographically; the underlying name cannot.

### 6. Berith and Bael

They are separate catalogue spirits. Treating Beal as proof that Berith is merely Bael no. 1 ignores the catalogues’ explicit separation. A remote common Baal-name background remains possible.

### 7. Gold versus gold and silver

The earlier *Livre des esperitz* and Folger V.b.26 allow gold **or silver**. Weyer narrows the power to gold, and the *Goetia* follows him. This is evidence of textual evolution, not necessarily two spirits.

### 8. One crown, two crowns, gold crown

- *Livre*: a crown;
- Folger: two red crowns;
- Weyer/Scot: a crown;
- Sloane/*Goetia*: a gold crown;
- de Plancy: crown on the brow.

The two-crown Folger image is a genuine witness but not the dominant Goetic form.

### 9. Truthful prophet and liar

This contradiction is present already in Weyer. It is not created by Crowley. The manuscript *Goetia* intensifies it; the 1904 body suppresses the liar sentence, while its footnote acknowledges it.

### 10. “Bad breath”

**Probable copying or layout contamination.** A “bad breath” attribution occasionally attached to Berith in later tables belongs to the immediately following spirit, Astaroth. Weyer, Scot, and the principal Berith entries do not give Berith dangerous breath.

### 11. “Demon of alchemists”

The office of transmuting metals is old. The title “demon of alchemists” is securely documented in Collin de Plancy’s 1863 article. It is not Weyer’s title.

The stronger claim that “Berith was an alchemical element used to turn metals into gold” is unsourced in the older texts. No historical alchemical substance called Berith was verified. It appears to be a modern reification of the spirit’s office.

### 12. Minister of pacts and infernal secretary

These are later Baalberith elaborations, encouraged by the meaning “covenant.” They should be cited to the demonographies that use them, not assigned retroactively to Weyer.

### 13. Rudd’s authorship

Harley 6483 is a late copy. Its descent from a Thomas Rudd original of 1655/56 is Skinner and Rankine’s reconstruction from textual relationships, circles, printed sources, and associated manuscripts. “The Harley manuscript is Thomas Rudd’s autograph” is false.

### 14. Rudd’s Hebrew spellings

Skinner and Rankine warn that reconstructing corrupt demon names into Hebrew is often guesswork. Berith is one of the few names with a plausible Hebrew background, but the precise Hebrew forms written on Rudd’s demon seals remain early-modern learned reconstructions, not ancient spellings preserved continuously from Shechem.

### 15. Seheiah’s “meaning”

“God who heals the ill” is Lenain’s/Ambelain’s attributed divine quality. The three-letter root does not straightforwardly translate as that phrase.

### 16. Which quinance is correct?

There is no single historically universal answer:

- Lenain/Ambelain, beginning at Aries: 15°–20° Leo.
- Golden Dawn sequence beginning at Leo: 15°–20° Sagittarius.
- Rudd itself preserves the angel pairing more securely than a universal zodiac starting point.
- Crowley’s demon-decan table: Berith occupies all 0°–10° Capricorn by day.

Modern tables that print “15° Leo (1° Capricorn)” on one line are combining systems without explanation.

### 17. The Psalm number

The Latin establishes the verse. Psalm numbering varies, and at least one modern edition contains a demonstrable erroneous number. The safest citation is:

> Vulgate Psalm 70:12 / modern Psalm 71:12.

### 18. Enochian Berith

No historical Dee/Kelley spirit named Berith has been demonstrated. Crowley’s Enochian conjurations are translations or overlays applied to the Goetia ritual.

### 19. Seal interpretations

The manuscripts transmit drawings, not explanations. Assertions about Hebrew monograms, planetary hours, crosses, stars, alphabets, or secret theological sentences are modern unless a specific historical source is produced.

## Open questions

1. **Hagiography to grimoire:** Did the Berith of the Bartholomew legend directly influence the *Livre des esperitz*, or did both independently draw on biblical Baal-Berith?

2. **Lost *Offices* archetype:** What exactly did Weyer’s lost *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* say? Did it already contain the red soldier and liar warning, or were those introduced in Weyer’s immediate exemplar?

3. **Bolfri:** Is it a damaged personal name, a descriptive term, or a wholly conventional magical word?

4. **The *Livre* date:** The codex has received dates ranging from the later fifteenth century to the middle sixteenth. A full codicological reassessment could refine when the Berteth entry was copied.

5. **Plut. 89 sup. 38:** How closely does its Berit office, if recoverable, correspond to Berteth and Weyer rather than merely sharing the name?

6. **Folger’s two crowns:** Does this preserve an older iconographic detail lost by Weyer, or is it a local elaboration?

7. **Gold and silver:** Was Weyer’s gold-only reading deliberate normalization, an abridgment, or dependent on a different *Offices* branch?

8. **Rudd stemma:** Do the double seals preserve an early structural principle removed by Sloane copyists, or are they Rudd’s sophisticated seventeenth-century synthesis?

9. **Angel pairing before Rudd:** The numerological opportunity existed as soon as a seventy-two-demon list and seventy-two-angel list were juxtaposed, but no presently cited pre-Rudd witness proves the specific sequence Berith–Seheiah.

10. **Seal archetype:** No demonstrated pre-Lemegeton exemplar of Berith’s standard seal has been identified.

11. **Michaëlis and Weyer:** Did Michaëlis or his possessed informants draw the name Berith from the already printed *Pseudomonarchia*, from biblical Baal-Berith, or from an independent demonological channel?

12. **First-edition de Plancy wording:** The 1863 article is verified in full, but a diplomatic comparison with the 1818 first-edition entry would establish exactly when “demon of alchemists,” the singers, and the antiquarian identifications entered the work.

# Halphas: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes the following categories:

- **[Documented text]** — stated in a named primary source or directly visible in a manuscript/edition.
- **[Textual reconstruction]** — an inference from variant readings or source relationships.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — an argument made by an editor or historian.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim transmitted within Jewish, Christian-kabbalistic, grimoire, or occult tradition.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an occult attribution imposed on the older catalogue.
- **[Disputed]** — a claim for which the sources conflict or the evidence is insufficient.
- **[Legend]** — a narrative presented as traditional history without historical corroboration.
- **[Modern invention]** — a recent creative, practitioner, commercial, fictional, or game-specific development.
- **[Negative finding]** — an expected connection for which no supporting attestation was found.

Because of source-reproduction limits, the three long requested entries cannot be reproduced in their entirety here. Decisive clauses are quoted within those limits; complete diplomatic transcriptions and facsimiles are linked immediately beside them.

## The name and its origins

### The source-chain spellings

| Date/source | Spelling | Number | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1577, Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* | **Halphas** | 43 by modern sequential count | No alternate name supplied |
| 1584, Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft* | **Halphas** | 43 in the inherited sequence | English rendering of Weyer |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane 3825 | **Halphas** | 38 | “Stock \<dove\>”; “towers” |
| 1680s, Sloane 2731 | **Halphas** | 38 | “Stockdove”; uniquely preserves “towns” |
| 1712–13, Harley 6483 | **Malthas** | 38 | Rudd-family text; paired with Hamiah |
| 1904, Mathers–Crowley | **Halphas or Malthus**; body also **Malthous (or Malthas)** | 38 | Some scans transcribe the heading as “Halphus” |

| 1911, Waite and later derivative lists | **Halpas** | 38 | Secondary modernization or error |

The comparison is documented in Peterson’s [comparative table of Goetic names](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

### Earliest secure attestation

**[Documented text]** The earliest presently secure appearance of the exact name **Halphas** is Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, added to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in **1577**. The original *De praestigiis* appeared in 1563, but the catalogue did not: citations that date “Halphas in Weyer” to 1563 are therefore misleading. Peterson’s digital text explains the edition history and identifies the 1577 appendix. [Weyer/Peterson introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

Weyer says he copied and abbreviated a manuscript called:

*Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis, de principibus et regibus daemoniorum.*

That title may be translated as “Book of the Offices of Spirits, or the book called *Empto of Solomon*, concerning the princes and kings of demons.”

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The catalogue behind Weyer was older than 1577. Johannes Trithemius had already listed a *Liber officiorum* or related work in his *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508. Weyer also says that he suppressed portions so that the work could not readily be used. This establishes an older *type of text*, but it does not prove that the name **Halphas** was present in Trithemius’s copy.

**[Negative finding]** No surviving pre-1577 manuscript presently examined supplies the exact name *Halphas*, *Malthas*, or *Malthus* with this description. The proper historical formulation is therefore:

> The catalogue family is older than Weyer, but the first recoverable attestation of this particular name and entry is Weyer’s 1577 print.

### Does the name descend from an ancient god, hero, or place?

**[Negative finding]** No defensible ancient deity, hero, place-name, Hebrew noun, Greek divine name, or Latin mythological figure has been identified behind **Halphas**. It is unlike transparent or partly transparent Goetic names such as Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Amon, Belial, Asmoday/Asmodeus, or Phoenix.

No ancient inscription, classical author, biblical text, rabbinic source, *Testament of Solomon* recension, or medieval demon catalogue located in this research supplies *Halphas*.

### Proposed etymologies

#### “Caliph” or *khalīfa*

**[Modern disputed speculation]** A modern occult essay derives *Halphas* from “khalif,” “ruler,” and supplies the Hebrew form קלאב. No philological path from Arabic خليفة (*khalīfa*) to *Halphas* is demonstrated; the proposed Hebrew letters do not transparently encode either word. There is no support in Weyer, Scot, the manuscripts, Rudd, Mathers, or the historical scholarship. [Modern claim examined](https://www.enmerkar.com/en/magic/names-of-demons)

#### Greek *Melitē*, “honey”

**[Modern disputed speculation]** The same modern source connects *Maltus* with Greek Μελίτη, “honey.” The phonetic and semantic argument is undeveloped, and there is no early textual evidence for it.

#### “Hal” plus Greek *phaos/phōs*, “shining light”

**[Modern invention]** A still newer practitioner etymology divides *Halphas* into a supposed Semitic *hal-* and Greek *phas/phōs*, yielding “shining light.” This is a cross-language folk etymology with no historical evidence. Its first recoverable appearances are twenty-first-century online ritual writings, not philological studies.

#### Malthus the economist

**[Disputed but readily resolved]** The alternate *Malthas* predates the 1904 book in Harley 6483, but **Malthus** appears to be Mathers’s or Crowley’s normalization of that manuscript form. It cannot originate with Thomas Robert Malthus, because the *Malthas* reading is already present in a manuscript of 1712–13, decades before the economist’s birth in 1766.

Crowley nevertheless appended a joke that Malthus was “certainly in heaven,” with a reference to Shelley. **[Documented editorial joke]** That footnote is wordplay, not an etymological claim. [1904 scan, p. 34](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf)

### Halphas and Malphas

**[Documented text]** They are separate entries in Weyer and remain separate in the *Goetia*:

- **Halphas**: Weyer 43; Goetia 38; Earl; stork/stock-dove; war logistics; 26 legions.
- **Malphas**: Weyer 32; Goetia 39; President; raven/crow, then human; constructs houses or towers, gathers artificers, discovers or destroys enemies’ plans, supplies familiars; 40 legions.

Their names, bird forms, hoarse voices, and building offices are sufficiently similar to suggest textual attraction or common ancestry. But the continuous source chain treats them separately.

**[Disputed]** Collin de Plancy proposed that Halphas “may be the same as Malphas.” Skinner and Rankine similarly observe that an earlier recension might once have contained a single figure. This is possible, but no surviving common ancestor proves it.

## Number and order: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

### Weyer

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s catalogue contains **69** entries. Halphas is the **43rd by sequential count**. Early editions were not necessarily numbered in the modern table-like fashion; later numbering is complicated by an edition that repeats a paragraph number, creating occasional references to Halphas as Weyer 42.

### The Goetia

**[Documented text]** The *Ars Goetia* contains **72** spirits and places Halphas at **38**.

The arithmetic is:

- Weyer’s 69
- minus **Pruflas/Bufas**, absent from Scot’s English list and then from the *Goetia*
- plus four spirits absent from Weyer
- equals 72.

The four additions are:

1. **Vassago**, inserted as Goetia 3.
2. **Seere**, Goetia 70.
3. **Dantalion**, Goetia 71.
4. **Andromalius**, Goetia 72.

Peterson notes that Scot’s omission of Pruflas may be accidental or may reflect a defective exemplar. Because the *Goetia* follows Scot’s English mistakes more often than it returns independently to Weyer’s Latin, the missing Pruflas is evidence for dependence on the Scot branch. [Peterson’s source comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The rearrangement has no demonstrated occult key. Peterson compares it to shuffled leaves or pages. The anonymous compiler also appears to have wanted the symbolically significant total of 72, corresponding to the 72-fold divine Name and the traditional story that Solomon sealed 72 chief spirits in a vessel.

**[Legend]** The *Goetia* says Solomon shut these 72 spirits and their legions in a brass vessel and cast it into a Babylonian lake; Babylonians opened it while searching for treasure. Weyer has a related story but Latin *vase vitreo*, a glass vessel. “Brass” is likely an error or elaboration transmitted through Scot.

## The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, 1577

### Text

Weyer’s Latin begins:

> “Halphas Comes magnus, prodit similis ciconiæ rauca voce…”

and ends:

> “…Subsunt huic viginti sex legiones.”

The complete entry is available in Peterson’s [Latin transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm#Halphas). Peterson’s online pagination locates it at p. 926 of the 1660 *Opera omnia* witness; the catalogue itself first appeared in the 1577 fifth edition.

### Translation and content

**[Documented text]**

- *Comes magnus*: a great Count or Earl.
- Appears like a *ciconia*: unequivocally a **stork**.
- Has a hoarse voice.
- Builds impressive or extensive fortified towns full of arms.
- *Bellum movet*: moves, raises, or sets war in motion.
- When ordered, sends fighting men to a designated place “to meet” or oppose others.
- Commands 26 legions.

There is:

- no seal;
- no human or second appearance;
- no angel;
- no zodiacal degree;
- no tarot card;
- no claim that he teaches languages or sciences;
- no treasures, love, invisibility, transmutation, familiars, or universal answers.

The last absences matter because later encyclopedias often copy capabilities from neighboring Goetic spirits.

## 2. Scot, 1584

Reginald Scot places the catalogue in *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter II, printed p. 223 in the 1584 pagination. His entry begins:

> “Halphas is a great earle, and commeth abroad like a storke, with a hoarse voice…”

The complete entry is in Peterson’s [transcription of Scot](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

**[Documented text]** Scot preserves:

- Halphas;
- great Earl;
- stork;
- hoarse voice;
- towns filled with munitions and weapons;
- men of war sent to appointed places;
- 26 legions.

Scot does not explicitly reproduce Weyer’s separate *bellum movet*, “he raises war.” The military dispatch remains, but one office has dropped.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Peterson argues that the translator was probably Abraham Fleming and may have worked from a faulty or difficult manuscript copy rather than directly from a clean printed Weyer. Scot’s book is hostile to witch prosecutions and exposes conjuring literature partly to demonstrate its contradictions and impostures; publication does not mean Scot endorsed the catalogue’s supernatural claims.

## 3. Sloane 3825

Sloane 3825 is a principal seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscript and Peterson’s base text.

Its critical reading is:

> “…forme of a stock [*stork] \<dove\>… build up Towers [*towns]…”

**[Documented text]**

- Name: Halphas.
- Number: 38.
- Rank: great Earl.
- Appearance: the manuscript tradition reads “stock” with “dove” added or understood.
- Voice: hoarse.
- Office: build towers, furnish them with ammunition and weapons, send men of war to appointed places.
- Legions: 26.
- Seal: supplied immediately after the entry.
- Use of seal: it is to be made and worn as a lamen.

Peterson’s text and apparatus are available at [Goetia §38](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#halphas); the 2001 Weiser edition prints the entry on p. 24.

## 4. Sloane 2731

Sloane 2731, catalogued around 1687 and carrying a note that copying began in January 1686, was compiled from more than one predecessor. Its diplomatic text reads **“Stockdove”** but, importantly, **“towns.”** [Sloane 2731 diplomatic edition](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/)

**[Textual reconstruction]**

- “Towns” agrees with Weyer’s *oppida* and Scot’s “townes.”
- “Towers” occurs in Sloane 3825, Sloane 3648, and Harley 6483.
- Therefore “towns” is almost certainly the earlier reading.
- The change could arise paleographically because the words are similar and because several neighboring demons actually do build towers.
- Sloane 2731’s agreement with the older sources shows that it sometimes preserves readings from a second, better prototype.

“Stock-dove,” however, does not have equivalent support in the Latin. The strongest reconstruction remains **stork**.

## 5. Harley 6483 and the Rudd material

Harley 6483, copied in 1712–13, titles the work *Liber malorum spirituum, seu Goetia*. It reads **Malthas**, not Halphas, and retains the stock-dove/towers form of the description.

**[Documented text]**

- Malthas.
- Spirit 38.
- Great Earl.
- Stock-dove.
- Hoarse voice.
- Builds towers and supplies arms.
- Sends men of war.
- 26 legions.
- Paired with **Hamiah**.
- Accompanied by the angel’s psalm text and controlling apparatus.

**[Disputed attribution]** The manuscript belongs to a “Dr Rudd” textual complex and contains material associated with Thomas Rudd, but it was copied more than fifty years after Rudd’s death in 1656. It is therefore not an autograph. “Rudd’s Goetia” is a conventional attribution to a textual system, not proof that Rudd personally penned Harley 6483.

Skinner and Rankine’s critical edition gives Halphas/Malthas on p. 140. [Bibliographic record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/The-Goetia-of-Dr-Rudd-%3A-the-angels-and-demons-of-Liber-malorum-spirituum-seu-Goetia-Lemegeton-clavicula-Salomonis-%3A-with-a-study-of-the-techniques-of-evocation-in-the-context-of-the-angel-magic-tradition-of-the-seventeenth-century/oclc/665072905)

## 6. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The 1904 text calls the spirit **Halphas, or Malthus**, and in the body **Malthous (or Malthas)**:

> “The Thirty-eighth Spirit is Halphas, or Malthous (or Malthas). He is a Great Earl… in the Form of a Stock-Dove.”

The full public-domain entry and plate are on printed p. 34 of the [1904 facsimile](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf) and in the [Sacred Texts edition](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

**[Documented text]** Mathers–Crowley standardizes the now-familiar form:

- stock-dove;
- hoarse voice;
- builds towers;
- furnishes ammunition and weapons;
- sends men-of-war;
- 26 legions;
- printed seal.

The footnotes correctly note that Weyer has a stork and gloss “men-of-war” as warriors or men-at-arms. Crowley’s Malthus joke follows.

**[Negative finding]** The entry still gives no human form. The general *Goetia* hexagram is said to compel spirits to take human shape, but that is a ritual-wide claim, not a second form specifically attributed to Halphas.

## 7. Peterson, 2001 and the updated online edition

Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon* was published by Weiser in 2001; Halphas appears on p. 24. His online edition has subsequently been revised, most recently in 2026. [Publisher record](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/)

Peterson’s chief contributions are textual rather than new mythology:

- identifying the Weyer/Scot source chain;
- restoring “stork” and “towns” in brackets;
- identifying Sloane 2731’s independent good reading;
- recording the manuscript sigils;
- distinguishing Halphas from Malphas;
- explaining the 69-to-72 problem.

## The seal

### What the sources actually say

**[Documented text]** Weyer and Scot supply no individual seal for Halphas. Individual Goetic seals enter the surviving record with the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscript tradition. Peterson notes that he knows no such seals in the earlier *Offices of Spirits* witnesses, although other medieval and Renaissance demon catalogues have different characters. [Peterson on the sigils](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

The manuscripts do not describe Halphas’s seal verbally. They draw it. Consequently, any supposed prose “decoding” of its geometry is later interpretation.

### Controlled visual description

The standardized 1904 form is:

- a circular lamen or cartouche;
- containing a compact, predominantly rectilinear and angular central character;
- with repeated horizontal and vertical strokes enclosing or partitioning small internal spaces;
- drawn more like a monogram or magical character than a representational picture.

The exact crossings, closures, proportions, and stroke terminals differ in manuscript copies and modern redrawings. The safest exact identification is the facsimile itself: [1904 Halphas seal](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/038-Seal-of-Halphas/) and [public-domain vector rendering](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:38-halphas-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg).

### Variants

**[Documented text]**

- **Sloane 2731:** carefully drawn but affected in places by ink spread; represents the independent Sloane branch.
- **Sloane 3825:** Peterson’s main critical figure.
- **Harley 6483:** associated with the spelling *Malthas* and the angel Hamiah; its line-work is not necessarily identical to the Sloane seals.
- **Mathers–Crowley:** normalized for print and placed in a regular circular frame.

Mathers himself warns that the Rudd codex contains errors, “particularly in the Sigils.” That admission makes it unsafe to proclaim one late redraw the single original form. [Mathers’s rank and manuscript note, pp. 48–49](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks07.htm)

**[Modern invention]** Online claims that every line represents a direction, element, legion-count, weapons system, or the gematria of YHWH are not stated in any early source. Some recent pages even describe a cross ending in four circles—a design not established by the cited Halphas facsimile. Such explanations should be treated as recent symbolic meditation.

## Rank, metal, planet, and time

### Earl or Count

Latin *comes* can be translated “count”; early modern English convention gives “earl.” The offices are the same rank, not two different titles.

### Metal

The manuscript observation says:

- kings — gold;
- marquises — silver;
- dukes — copper;
- princes/prelates — tin;
- knights — lead;
- presidents — mercury;
- **earls — copper and silver equally alike**.

Thus the primary *Goetia* instruction for Halphas is **copper and silver**, whether as combined materials, a mixture, or alternative equal correspondences. The text is not explicit about metallurgy.

### Planet

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Mathers observes that the above scheme omits Mars and proposes that Earls’ seals “should be made of Iron.” In the Golden Dawn/Crowley correspondence, Earl therefore becomes **Mars**, with iron as its planetary metal. *777* explicitly places the Mars symbol beside Halphas. [*777 Revised*, columns CLXI–CLXIV](https://www.hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf)

Accordingly:

- **Primary manuscript metal:** copper plus silver.
- **Later planetary rank:** Mars.
- **Mathers’s conjectural corrected metal:** iron.

Those are three different statements and should not be collapsed.

### Hour and lunar timing

**[Documented text]** Halphas has no personal hour. The general rank rule says Counts or Earls may be bound at **any hour of the day**, provided the operation is in woods or another unfrequented, quiet place.

The same observation recommends working when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old. [Goetia observations](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#observations)

**[Later esoteric practice]** Once Earl is made Martial, later tables add Tuesday and the planetary hours of Mars. Red, iron, and dragon’s blood incense are frequent modern correspondences. These are not found in Halphas’s early entry.

**[Internal conflict]** Crowley’s *777* labels Halphas a night demon, whereas the old Earl rule permits binding by day. These belong to different systems:

- “any hour of the day in a secluded place” is the grimoire rank rule;
- “night/succedent” is the Golden Dawn/Crowley decan classification.

## Construction of the name

**[Documented Christian-kabbalistic text]** The three Hebrew verses Exodus 14:19–21 each contain 72 letters in the consonantal text. They are arranged:

1. verse 19 forward;
2. verse 20 backward;
3. verse 21 forward.

Reading the columns produces 72 triplets. The thirty-eighth is **העם**. Adding **יה** produces **העמיה**, Haamiah.

Agrippa explains the procedure and prints Haamiah among the 72 angel names in *Three Books of Occult Philosophy* III.25. [Agrippa III.25](https://esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp3b.htm)

### Gematria

**[Esoteric correspondence]**

- Triplet העם: 5 + 70 + 40 = **115**.
- Full העמיה: 5 + 70 + 40 + 10 + 5 = **130**.

Gematria is a traditional interpretive technique; it does not establish historical etymology.

### Meaning

Lenain calls Haamiah’s attribute:

> “Dieu, l’espérance de tous les enfants de la terre.”

**[Documented esoteric attribute]** “God, the hope of all the children of the earth.”

This should not be presented as a literal translation of העמיה. The root העם can look like Hebrew *ha-ʿam*, “the people,” inviting later renderings such as “people of Yah” or “nation of God.” But the angelic word was mechanically constructed from an Exodus triplet plus a divine suffix. Its “meaning” in the Western lists is an assigned theological attribute, not demonstrable ordinary Hebrew morphology.

## Psalm

Lenain prescribes the Vulgate Psalm 90:9:

> “Quoniam tu es Domine spes mea; altissimum posuisti refugium tuum.”

In the Masoretic/Hebrew and most Protestant English numbering, this is **Psalm 91:9**: because the Lord is one’s refuge and the Most High one’s habitation. [Hebrew/English Psalm 91](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.91.9)

Skinner and Rankine reproduce the Latin beside the Rudd Halphas/Malthas material on p. 140. Some modern sites incorrectly call it Psalm 90:9 and then cite the English Psalm 91 text without explaining the numbering difference.

## Zodiacal degrees and dates

Lenain says Haamiah’s ray extends from the **186th through 190th degrees inclusive**. In a zodiac beginning with Aries:

- 181st degree begins Libra;
- 186th–190th corresponds to **5°–10° Libra**.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** In a modern tropical solar calendar this is approximately **29 September–3 October**, though the entry and exit can vary with year, timezone, and the convention for inclusive civil dates.

Lenain separately lists five scattered days—26 April, 7 July, 17 September, 28 November, and 8 February—arising from another cyclic distribution. These are not the same as the continuous 5° solar birth period.

Later authors give a 20-minute daily interval, normally **12:20–12:40 solar time**, since 24 hours divided by 72 equals 20 minutes. Haziel popularized both the five-day “guardian angel” period and the daily interval. [Haziel’s Haamiah entry](https://www.scribd.com/document/569113313/Le-Grand-Livre-des-Invocations-HAZIEL-en-GB)

## Offices in Lenain

**[Documented esoteric tradition]** Lenain, pp. 69–71, says the divine names are used:

- to acquire all treasures of heaven and earth;
- against lightning, weapons, ferocious animals, and infernal spirits;
- in connection with religious worship and everything pertaining to God;
- to protect those who seek truth.

The “contrary genius” rules error, falsehood, and persons without religious principle. [Lenain primary scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365)

These are Haamiah’s offices, not Halphas’s. Modern compilations sometimes transfer the angel’s truth, treasure, and ritual functions to the demon; the early Halphas entry does not contain them.

## History of the Shem tradition

### Rabbinic and medieval Jewish foundations

**[Tradition]** *Shem ha-meforash* means the explicit, distinguished, or fully expressed divine Name. In rabbinic literature it can denote the Tetragrammaton or other extended Names; it did not originally mean a fixed modern catalogue of 72 guardian angels.

**[Documented medieval text]** The construction from Exodus 14:19–21 is described in *Sefer ha-Bahir* §110: first line forward, second reversed, third forward, yielding 72 three-letter groups. [*Bahir* §§109–110](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-109-110-targum/)

Rashi and other medieval commentators knew a 72-letter divine Name associated with these verses.

### *Sefer Raziel*

**[Tradition/Textual history]** The composite medieval *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* tradition preserves extended divine Names, angelic names, seals, and material related to the 72-fold Name. Its legendary frame says the angel Raziel revealed secret wisdom to Adam.

**[Legend]** The book’s attribution to Raziel, Adam, Noah, or Solomon is not historical authorship.

**[Negative finding]** The complete modern Haamiah dossier—Libra degrees, Psalm, offices, birthday period, and opposition to Halphas—is not demonstrably present as a unit in the earliest *Sefer Raziel* material. Those elements accumulated later.

### Reuchlin

**[Documented Christian Kabbalah]** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) prints the 72 angelic forms and explains adding **-Yah** or **-El**. Reuchlin says *Yah* expresses divine beneficence and *El* divine power or strength. [Scholarly study of Reuchlin’s lists](https://dokumen.pub/72-in-his-name-reuchlin-luther-thenaud-wolff-and-the-names-of-seventy-two-angels-9781644692455.html)

### Agrippa

Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia* III.25, published in its final form in 1533, gives the extraction method and names Haamiah. Agrippa also states that 72 angels govern the 72 five-degree divisions of the zodiac. This is an important pre-Lenain basis for the quinance scheme, though the later full set of degrees, dates, psalms, and offices is not all supplied there.

### Vigenère and Rudd

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Blaise de Vigenère transmitted the Reuchlin/Agrippa angelic material. Skinner and Rankine argue that Rudd copied and expanded Vigenère’s treatment and then aligned the 72 angels with the 72 Goetic spirits by ordinal number.

This is where **Hamiah 38 against Malthas 38** becomes historically relevant.

### Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, especially the *Cabala Hebraeorum* in volume II, part I (1652), prints elaborate 72-name tables and divine attributes. Lenain explicitly cites Kircher as his principal authority for the names and their order. [Kircher’s *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

Kircher’s work is Christian-kabbalistic synthesis, not a neutral record of ancient Egyptian or Jewish doctrine.

### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* was self-published at Amiens in 1823. It is the decisive ancestor of most modern French-language “72 guardian angel” lists.

Lenain candidly says that Kircher supplies the names and attributes but that **three of his own tables are his invention**. He systematizes:

- numbered angels;
- divine attributes;
- five-degree rays;
- five scattered governing dates;
- daily 20-minute periods;
- psalm verses;
- positive offices;
- contrary genii.

Thus many things now advertised as “ancient Kabbalah” are better dated to Lenain’s nineteenth-century synthesis.

### Papus

Papus—Gérard Encausse—reproduced and praised Lenain’s angelic system in *La Cabbale*, particularly the expanded 1903 treatment. Occultists around Papus arranged a new edition of Lenain in 1909.

**[Negative finding]** No Halphas-specific contribution by Papus was identified. His role is transmission and popularization of the angelic tables.

### Golden Dawn and Crowley

The Golden Dawn aligned angelic quinances and Goetic decans with its tarot and astrological system. Crowley’s *777*, first published anonymously in 1909 and later expanded as *777 Revised*, made the Goetic day/night decan table widely accessible.

The Golden Dawn/Crowley table is a new overlay on the older spirit catalogue, not part of Weyer, Scot, or the seventeenth-century Halphas entry.

### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* appeared in 1951. It reproduced much of Lenain and added angelic seals said to derive from eighteenth-century Arsenal Library/Boulainvilliers manuscripts. [Bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/La_Kabbale_pratique.html?id=jF8ntAEACAAJ)

In a 1989 note Ambelain claimed that experiments performed between 1955 and 1960 had shown those seals to be dangerous and actually “opposite” or malefic. **[Practitioner claim]** He associated them with illness, obsession, and possession. No controlled or independently corroborated evidence was supplied; the statement belongs to Ambelain’s personal occult testimony, not historical proof.

### Haziel

Haziel, the pseudonym of a Spanish-born popular angelology author, transformed the system into a late-twentieth-century guardian-angel program. *Le grand livre des invocations et des exhortations* is catalogued in an edition from 1987, with later Bussière editions in 2001 and 2007. [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL15773179W/Le_Grand_livre_des_invocations_et_des_exhortations)

Haziel popularized:

- three personal angels;
- birth dates;
- five recurring dates;
- daily 20-minute periods;
- devotional invocations;
- psychological “gifts.”

**[Modern tradition]** This is a devotional-occult reinterpretation of Lenain, not evidence about seventeenth-century Rudd practice. The author Haziel must also not be confused with Shem angel **Haziel**, number 9.

## How Rudd uses Haamiah to constrain Halphas

**[Documented Rudd tradition]** Rudd’s system treats the identically numbered Shem angel as the celestial authority capable of checking or binding each Goetic spirit. For number 38:

- demon: **Malthas/Halphas**;
- angel: **Hamiah/Haamiah**;
- psalm: Vulgate Psalm 90:9.

The demon’s character and the angelic name/seal are placed in relation to one another. In Skinner and Rankine’s reconstruction, the operator wears the spirit’s seal together with the corresponding angelic protection, effectively placing the demon under a superior divine name.

**[Scholarly/practical reconstruction]** Skinner and Rankine describe a double-sided or paired lamen in which the Goetic seal and thwarting angel work together, worn at the breast or heart. Their interpretation helps explain why Rudd’s version supplies angelic counterparts absent from the ordinary Sloane *Goetia*. It is still their reconstruction of the working method from several manuscripts, not a sentence in the Halphas catalogue entry itself.

**[Tradition]** The angel is not simply a benevolent “personality opposite” to the demon. Its operative role is hierarchical and juridical: divine authority, psalm, angelic name, and seal constrain the lower spirit.

## B. Crowley’s *777*: the correct Halphas placement

In *777 Revised*, columns CLXI–CLXIV divide the 36 night demons among the 36 zodiacal decans:

- #37 Phenex: first Aries decan;
- **#38 Halphas: second Aries decan**;
- #39 Malphas: third Aries decan.

Thus Halphas is:

- **night**;
- **succedent** within the sign;
- **10°–20° Aries**;
- **Three of Wands**;
- rank-planet symbol **Mars**;
- magical image summarized as a stock-dove with a sore or hoarse throat.

The Three of Wands is “Lord of Established Strength” in Golden Dawn nomenclature and “Virtue” in Crowley’s Thoth deck.

### The other spirit on the pip

The 36 day spirits and 36 night spirits share the 36 decans. The day counterpart occupying the same second Aries decan is **Agares, spirit 2**. Therefore the Three of Wands carries:

- day: Agares;
- night: Halphas.

It does **not** carry Phenex and Halphas as two adjacent 5° spirits.

## C. Haamiah’s separate Libra placement

The 72 angels occupy 72 five-degree arcs. Haamiah 38 is:

- 5°–10° Libra;
- second quinance of the first Libra decan;
- “night” half of that decan;
- Two of Swords;
- lunar decan ruler in the Chaldean sequence.

The preceding first quinance, 0°–5° Libra, belongs to **Aniel 37**. Thus the Two of Swords carries two angelic quinances:

- Aniel, 0°–5°;
- Haamiah, 5°–10°.

This is the origin of the misleading same-number shortcut. Rudd pairs demon and angel by ordinal number, but their independent astrological wheels do not place them at the same zodiacal degree.

## D. Later alternative systems

Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* (1992) adopts and illustrates the *777*-style Aries assignment. Other contemporary occult-tarot decks instead match demon 38 directly with angel 38 and therefore print Halphas on the **Two of Swords**. Both systems circulate, but only the Aries/Three of Wands attribution reflects Crowley’s Goetic decan table.

## Enochian and other later overlays

**[Negative finding]** Neither Halphas’s Weyer entry nor the Sloane/Harley catalogue description is Enochian. John Dee’s angelic language, Watchtowers, Aethyrs, Calls, and tablets form a different late-sixteenth-century system.

**[Modern synthesis]** *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* incorporates Enochian Calls into a revised Goetic working and reports a Crowley-derived method using the First and Second Calls. DuQuette’s *Tarot of Ceremonial Magick* later advertised an explicit synthesis of Enochian magic, Goetia, and astrology. [WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Tarot-of-ceremonial-magick-%3A-a-pictorial-synthesis-of-three-great-pillars-of-magick-%3A-enochian-Goetia-astrology/oclc/32347017)

These are twentieth-century ceremonial syntheses. They do not supply evidence for Halphas’s historical origin.

Other recent overlays include:

- qliphothic paths;
- “enns” or short demonolatry chants;
- personal patron or birth-demon systems;
- elemental Fire because of Aries;
- elemental Earth because of fortification;
- military strategy, cybersecurity, or “psychic defense” reinterpretations;
- pairings with crystals, herbs, or modern colors.

**[Modern invention]** None of these appears in Weyer, Scot, Sloane 2731/3825, or the bare Harley description.

## People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer, a physician and former student of Agrippa, printed the first recoverable Halphas entry in 1577. His larger purpose was to attack demonological reasoning used in witch prosecutions. He believed in spirits but argued that accused witches were often deceived, ill, or powerless rather than commanders of demons.

His decision to abbreviate ritual material is important: *Pseudomonarchia* is simultaneously the founding printed source for Halphas and a deliberately impaired exposure of a conjuring book.

## Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot’s *Discoverie* of 1584 opposed witch-hunting and credulity. The demon catalogue was printed in that polemical setting. Peterson attributes its translation probably to Abraham Fleming.

The English wording—especially “townes,” “munition,” “men of warre”—became more influential on the *Goetia* than Weyer’s Latin.

## Anonymous Lemegeton compiler(s)

The *Lemegeton* is a composite seventeenth-century work, not a book written by Solomon. Its first book reorganized the Weyer/Scot list, added seals, restored ritual materials from other sources, omitted Pruflas, and expanded the catalogue to 72.

## Thomas Rudd and the Rudd copyists

Rudd is associated with a systematic attempt to place angelic authority over demonic evocation. Harley 6483’s late copying date prevents simple autographic attribution, but the Rudd textual complex is the first major witness to Hamiah as Halphas/Malthas’s constraining angel.

## Mathers and Crowley

S. L. MacGregor Mathers transcribed and edited manuscript material; Aleister Crowley published it in 1904 and added editorial notes plus “The Initiated Interpretation of Ceremonial Magic.”

Crowley’s later *777* supplied a distinct decanic astrology. The 1904 entry itself does not give Halphas a zodiac sign or tarot card.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition compares the principal manuscripts and identifiable sources. For Halphas, his most important results are the restoration of *stork* and *towns*, the source relation to Scot, and the distinction between manuscript and editorial readings.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their *Goetia of Dr Rudd* appeared from Golden Hoard in 2007, with a 2010 distribution/printing. It transcribes Harley 6483 with related material from Harley 6482, Sloane 3824, and Wellcome 3203. Halphas/Malthas and Hamiah appear on p. 140.

Their “thwarting angel” analysis made Rudd’s paired system central to twenty-first-century Goetic practice.

## Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* incorporates the Goetic spirits into his modern ceremonial system and reproduces Halphas/Chaamiah-type correspondences. **[Modern practitioner tradition]** No independently documented Halphas operation by Runyon was found; his relevance is system-building and republication rather than a historical claim about the name.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette co-authored *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* and later created a tarot system integrating Goetia, astrology, and Enochian material. He helped popularize Halphas as a night demon of the second Aries decan.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica* and related writings argue for reading Goetia within a larger necromantic and spirit-catalogue tradition. His work is relevant to the *Livre des Esperitz*, *Offices of Spirits*, and early grimoire contexts.

**[Negative finding]** No well-supported Halphas-specific ancient etymology attributable to Stratton-Kent was found.

## Owen Davies, Jean-Patrice Boudet, Julien Véronèse, and Jan Veenstra

These scholars illuminate the grimoire context:

- Davies surveys the social history and transmission of grimoires.
- Boudet compares Renaissance demonological catalogues with medieval predecessors.
- Véronèse studies medieval ritual magic, Solomonic texts, and the *Livre des Esperitz* milieu.
- Veenstra studies medieval and early modern magic, angelology, and necromantic textual traditions.

Their relevance is genealogical and methodological. None establishes a pre-Weyer Halphas.

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No Halphas-specific operation, essay, or correspondence by Frater Achad was verified. His general Qabalistic writings do not justify listing him as someone known to have worked with Halphas.

## Grimoire ancestors and analogues

## *Testament of Solomon*

**[Legendary background]** This late-antique Greek pseudepigraphon tells how Solomon received a ring and compelled demons to identify themselves and work on the Temple.

**[Negative finding]** Halphas is not named. The *Testament* supplies the broad Solomonic demon-commanding legend, not this entry’s name or description.

## Munich Handbook, Clm 849

Richard Kieckhefer’s *Forbidden Rites* presents the fifteenth-century Munich handbook. It contains spirits with later Goetic relatives—Barbarus, Cason, Otius, Curson, Alugor, Taob, Volach, Gaeneron, Tuveries, Hanni, and Sucax.

**[Negative finding]** Searches of the Latin text found no *Halphas*, *Malthas*, *Malthus*, or *Mathus*.

A 2026 webpage claiming that “Mathus” appears in Clm 849 also misstates Halphas’s rank and Weyer number. That assertion is contradicted by the manuscript’s published spirit list and should be rejected.

## *Livre des Esperitz*

The French *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, contains a medieval/early-Renaissance catalogue closely related to Weyer’s family.

It has **Malpharas**, who builds towers, castles, and bridges, destroys fortifications, transports them, and commands 30 legions. [Complete French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier)

**[Textual reconstruction]** Malpharas is a strong ancestor of **Malphas**, not proof of Halphas. The exact Halphas name and its stork, arms, and 26 legions are absent.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer explicitly names an *Offices of Spirits* manuscript as his source. Surviving related witnesses include material in the Folger *Book of Oberon*, Sloane manuscripts, and later Hockley copies. [Digital *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/liber-officiorum/read/)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This family is Halphas’s most likely immediate prehistory. Yet because Weyer's exact exemplar is lost, we cannot determine when the name first entered it or whether Weyer altered its spelling.

## *Book of Abramelin*

**[Negative finding]** The standard Abramelin spirit lists do not provide an exact Halphas with this office. General overlap among demonic names is insufficient to claim descent.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Stork or stock-dove?

**Finding:** **Stork is textually prior.**

Evidence:

- Weyer: Latin *ciconia*, stork.
- Scot: stork.
- Northwest University witness cited by Peterson: stork.
- Sloane/Harley/Mathers branch: stock or stock-dove.

**[Textual reconstruction]** A scribe likely misread or altered *stork* to *stock*, after which “dove” was added to make “stock-dove” meaningful. A stock dove is a real species, *Columba oenas*, but that zoological fact does not make it the original reading.

## 2. Towns or towers?

**Finding:** **Towns is textually prior.**

- Weyer: *oppida*.
- Scot: townes.
- Sloane 2731: towns.
- Sloane 3825, 3648, Harley 6483: towers/towres.
- Mathers–Crowley: towers.

“Towers” became culturally dominant because of the 1904 edition.

## 3. Does Halphas cause war?

Weyer explicitly has *bellum movet*. Scot omits the independent clause but retains sending men of war. The *Goetia* inherits Scot’s reduced form. De Plancy restores a generalized “orders wars.”

Thus “causes war” is securely Weyerian but not literally present in every later witness.

## 4. Is Halphas Malthus?

*Malthas* is the Harley/Rudd reading. *Malthous* and *Malthus* are 1904 variants. These may represent:

- a corrupt exemplar;
- misreading of initial letters;
- deliberate accommodation to a more pronounceable name;
- or an alternate oral/manuscript form.

There is no evidence that the name originally referred to the economist.

## 5. Is Halphas Malphas?

The similarities justify the question but not an identification. Weyer already knows both separately. De Plancy’s “perhaps the same” is speculation; modern lists should not use **Malphas** as an unqualified Halphas spelling.

## 6. Does Halphas appear in Clm 849 as Mathus?

**No.** The published Munich list has no such name. This claim appears to be a recent web fabrication or confusion with another spirit.

## 7. Does Halphas descend from Malpharas?

**Unproved and unlikely as a direct name descent.** Malpharas aligns more closely with Malphas’s construction/destruction office and appears in a catalogue that does not separately attest Halphas.

A more cautious possibility is that duplication or division of a single “builder” complex somewhere between the *Livre des Esperitz* family and Weyer produced both Malphas and Halphas. No manuscript currently demonstrates the step.

## 8. Is the seal ancient?

No. There is no Halphas seal in Weyer or Scot, much less in antiquity. The known seal belongs to the seventeenth-century *Goetia* tradition.

## 9. Is Haamiah an ancient personal guardian angel?

The three-letter Exodus extraction is medieval. The vocalized angel name is Renaissance Christian Kabbalah. The complete guardian-angel personality, birthday window, 20-minute period, offices, and modern devotional psychology are mainly Lenain and later popularizers.

## 10. Does Rudd’s pairing prove an inherent cosmic opposition?

No historical proof can establish that. What can be established is that the Rudd textual system pairs Hamiah 38 with Malthas 38 as a controlling angel.

## 11. Halphas: Libra or Aries?

Both appear in later occult literature, but they answer different questions:

- **Halphas in Crowley’s Goetic decan table:** 10°–20° Aries, Three of Wands, night.
- **Haamiah’s angelic quinance:** 5°–10° Libra, Two of Swords.
- **Rudd pairing:** demon 38 opposed by angel 38, without making their separate zodiacal positions identical.

A table that simply assigns Halphas to Haamiah’s Libra degrees is a modern hybrid.

## 12. Iron or copper-and-silver?

- Copper and silver: manuscript instruction.
- Iron: Mathers’s logical correction based on Mars.
- Mars: *777* rank planet.

All three are historically attested statements, but only the first is the original *Goetia* metal rule.

## 13. Does de Plancy portray Halphas?

No dedicated Breton portrait was found. The commonly substituted engraving is Malphas.

## 14. Are modern “enns” ancient?

No evidence was found in the historical source chain. They belong to modern demonolatry.

## 15. Did named twentieth-century magicians personally evoke Halphas?

Claims that Crowley, Mathers, Achad, Runyon, DuQuette, Peterson, Skinner, Rankine, or Stratton-Kent personally performed a Halphas operation require direct diaries or testimony. This research found editorial, historical, or system-building engagement, but no secure item-specific working record for most of those individuals.

## Open questions

1. **What did Weyer's lost source actually read?**  
   Weyer’s manuscript almost certainly preceded 1577, but without it we cannot know whether *Halphas* was its spelling or Weyer’s normalization.

2. **When did Halphas first enter the *Offices of Spirits* family?**  
   No surviving pre-Weyer witness answers this.

3. **Was Halphas produced by duplicating an earlier Malpharas/Malphas complex?**  
   The parallel bird, voice, and building motifs make this plausible, but the evidence is circumstantial.

4. **How did *stork* become *stock-dove*?**  
   The likely stages are *stork* → *stock* → *stock dove*, but the intermediate exemplar has not survived.

5. **How did *towns* become *towers*?**  
   The paleographic similarity is suggestive, but the precise copying event is unknown.

6. **What is the stemma of the Halphas seals?**  
   Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and later redrawings require a dedicated graphic collation.

7. **Is *Malthas* a mere error or evidence of a lost alternate source?**  
   Harley 6483 proves that it is not solely a 1904 invention, but not how much older it is.

8. **How much of Harley 6483 is Thomas Rudd’s?**  
   The late copy contains Rudd-associated methods, but authorship of every component remains uncertain.

9. **Who first fixed Haamiah’s specific psalm and attribute?**  
   Kircher is a major source for the names and divine attributes; Lenain is the secure source for the complete nineteenth-century operational notice. Intermediate manuscripts may clarify the transition.

10. **Was there an eighteenth-century angelic seal for Haamiah that Rudd himself used?**  
    Ambelain’s Arsenal/Boulainvilliers seals and Rudd’s angelic devices must be kept distinct until their graphic and manuscript relationships are demonstrated.

11. **Does the 1818 *Dictionnaire infernal* already contain the 1863 Halphas wording?**  
    This requires direct page-image collation of the H section; searchable OCR was inconclusive.

12. **Is the Annabelle demon officially Halphas/Malthus?**  
    The name appears in a film prop and later reference tradition, but the explicit equation with Goetia 38 is not developed onscreen.

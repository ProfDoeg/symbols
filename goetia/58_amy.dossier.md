# Amy: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier separates the layers that modern occult summaries frequently collapse:

- **Documented text** — stated in a cited manuscript or printed primary source.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a relationship argued from textual comparison, codicology, or historical context.
- **Tradition** — a claim transmitted within Jewish, Christian-kabbalistic, or ceremonial-magic literature.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — a deliberately constructed symbolic attribution, not a historical fact about the spirit.
- **Disputed** — supported by conflicting readings or insufficient evidence.
- **Legend** — supernatural narrative presented by a source without independent historical verification.
- **Modern invention/UPG** — recent ritual language, artwork, fiction, or practitioners’ reported personal experience.

The core findings are:

1. The profile later called **Amy** is attested before Weyer, in the fifteenth-century Munich manuscript Clm 849 under the name **Hanni**.
2. The exact spelling **Amy** is first securely attested in Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* of 1577.
3. No ancient god, hero, place, or securely established language-derived etymology lies behind the name.
4. The Shem angel **Ieialel/Yeialel** was not originally part of Amy’s description. The one-to-one angel–demon pairing appears in the manuscript tradition represented by Harley MS 6483 and attributed, controversially, to Thomas Rudd.
5. Amy’s Golden Dawn/Crowley assignment to the **Five of Cups and 0°–10° Scorpio** is distinct from Yeialel’s **five-degree Capricorn sector**. They are later, partly incompatible overlays.

### Hanni in the Munich handbook

**Documented text.** Clm 849, the so-called Munich handbook of demonic magic, is a fifteenth-century Latin necromantic compilation. Its **Hanni** is a “great president” who appears in a fiery flame and then assumes human form. Hanni makes a person skilled in astronomy and the liberal arts, gives excellent familiars and the favor of magnates and princes, and reveals places where spirits guard treasure. Hanni commands **thirty legions**.

The beginning reads:

> “Hanni preses magnus … apparet in flamma ignea…”

That is, “Hanni is a great president … and appears in a fiery flame.” See Richard Kieckhefer’s transcription in *Forbidden Rites*, p. 330, corresponding to manuscript fol. 65r bis; Kieckhefer discusses the figure at pp. 190 and 291–293. The digitized manuscript is available from the [Bayerische Staatsbibliothek](https://daten.digitale-sammlungen.de/0003/bsb00037155/images/index.html?fip=eayaxdsydyztssdasxdsydeayafsdreayaewqw&groesser=&id=00037155&no=131&seite=133).

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The identity of Hanni with Weyer’s Amy is unusually strong because name, rank, fiery manifestation, human form, educational offices, familiars, and spirit-guarded treasure all coincide. The legion count differs, but numbers are among the least stable features of these lists. Peterson accordingly aligns “Clm 849: Hanni” with Weyer’s Amy in his comparative table.

**Important correction.** It is therefore inaccurate to say that the spirit itself first appears in 1577. The **profile** is fifteenth-century; the spelling **Amy** is first securely printed in 1577.

### Hanar in Folger MS V.b.26

**Documented text.** Folger Shakespeare Library MS V.b.26, dated approximately 1577–1583 and published as *The Book of Oberon*, contains:

> “Hanar, a great prince, he appeareth in a flame of fier…”

Hanar takes the form of a man, is skilled in astronomy, describes treasures guarded by spirits—including what and how many they are—and grants favor to the master. He has **eight legions**. The diplomatic transcription is available from [Esoteric Archives](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf); the official manuscript record is at the [Folger catalog](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/228887).

**Scholarly reconstruction.** Hanar is another probable member of the Hanni–Amy textual family, although its compiler calls him a **prince**, reduces the legions to eight, and omits liberal sciences and familiars. These differences show that the surviving lists were not copied mechanically from one immutable archetype.

### Weyer’s Amy

**Documented text.** Johann Weyer printed *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Amy occurs on the page sequence around original p. 929. Weyer calls the entity **Amy Præses magnus**, “Amy, a great President.”

Weyer’s skeptical setting matters. He supplied the catalog to expose the impostures and “manifest lies” of supposed magicians, and stated that he omitted material so the ritual could not be used. The catalog nevertheless became one of the principal textual sources of the later *Goetia*. Peterson provides the Latin and Scot’s translation in parallel in his [critical web edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Is *Amy* an ancient divine name?

**Finding: no documented ancient derivation.** Unlike Bael/Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Phoenix, Asmoday/Asmodeus, or Belial, Amy has no demonstrated relationship to:

- a Mesopotamian, Canaanite, Egyptian, Greek, or Roman god;
- a classical hero;
- a historical place-name;
- a named demon in the *Testament of Solomon*;
- a recoverable Hebrew common noun or divine epithet.

The ordinary European personal name *Amy* comes through Old French *Amée*, “beloved,” related to Latin *amata*. That genealogy belongs to the human name and is not evidence for the demon’s name. No manuscript in the source-chain links the spirit to *amare*, *amicus*, *amata*, or “beloved.”

### Hanni and Haniel

**Disputed scholarly suggestion.** Kieckhefer cautiously observes that Hanni might conceivably be compared with **Haniel**, an angelic name. He does not establish an identification. The suggestion rests principally on resemblance of spelling; the descriptions and functions do not prove it.

It is particularly important not to turn this suggestion into a claim that Amy “was originally the angel Haniel.” That conclusion does not appear in the manuscript and would create further difficulties: Haniel is also independently present in angelological traditions, while Amy later receives the different counter-angel Ieialel.

### Auns and Avnas

**Documented text.** Harley 6483 reads **Auns**. Mathers and Crowley print **“Amy, or Avnas”** in 1904. Peterson’s footnote to the Amy entry records:

- Harley 6483: **Auns**
- Mathers–Crowley: **Amy, or Avnas**
- Sloane 2731, 3648, and 3825: no reported departure from **Amy**

**Scholarly inference, not proven fact.** *Avnas* probably developed from a difficult manuscript reading such as *Auns*, perhaps expanded or regularized by Mathers. No securely dated pre-1904 source located for this dossier independently writes *Avnas*. It should therefore not be projected backward as an ancient alternate name.

### Modern folk etymologies

Recent websites have proposed:

- Latin *amare* or *amicus*;
- the human name Amy, “beloved”;
- Lithuanian *avinas*, “ram”;
- invented Hebrew spellings with occult gematria.

**Modern invention/disputed.** None is supported by the documented chain Hanni → Hanar/Amy → Auns → Avnas. The Lithuanian comparison is especially weak: phonetic resemblance alone does not establish transmission, and no ram appears in Amy’s early iconography. One modern overview itself concedes that there is no accepted etymology while entertaining such possibilities; see [The Horror Collection](https://thehorrorcollection.com/who-is-the-demon-amy-avnas-president-of-hell/).

### Amy and Aym are different spirits

**Documented text.** Weyer’s **Aym or Haborym** is a separate duke, appearing shortly before Amy in the list. Aym has three heads, rides a viper, carries a burning brand, sets cities or fortresses ablaze, answers about hidden matters, and commands twenty-six legions. In the *Goetia*, Aim is no. 23.

Amy is a President associated with a fiery manifestation, learning, familiars, and treasure. Similar spelling and fire imagery have encouraged modern conflation, but the source lists distinguish them consistently.

## Number and order: Weyer’s 69 versus the Goetia’s 72

### The apparent Weyer numbering problem

**Documented bibliography.** Weyer’s catalog contains **69 spirit entries**, but an early duplicate number at Pucel/Furcas causes later printed numerals to lag behind the actual count. Consequently Amy may be described as:

- the **61st actual entry**, following Peterson’s normalized enumeration;
- “no. 60” in references that reproduce the defective printed sequence;
- *Goetia* no. **58** after the later catalog was rearranged.

These are cataloging differences, not three distinct Amys.

### What the Goetia added and omitted

The *Goetia* contains 72 spirits. Relative to Weyer:

- **Added:** Vassago, Seere, Dantalion, Andromalius.
- **Omitted:** Weyer’s Pruflas/Bufas.
- Net result: 69 − 1 + 4 = **72**.

Scot had already omitted Pruflas in his English presentation. The *Goetia* did considerably more than append three names: it reordered almost the entire catalog, adopted Scot-derived English errors, supplied seals, and embedded the catalog within a larger ritual apparatus. Peterson’s side-by-side order table documents Amy’s movement from Weyer’s 61st actual entry to *Goetia* no. 58.

### The fifteenth-century description

The Clm 849 entry can be translated closely as follows:

**Documented text, translation.** Hanni is a great President. He appears in a fiery flame, but when he takes human form he renders a person marvelously capable in astronomy and the other liberal arts. He gives excellent familiars and the favor of magnates and princes, and marvelously shows places of treasures guarded by spirits. Thirty legions are beneath him.

This is the fullest early form. Two details later disappear:

- **astronomy** becomes **astrology** in Weyer;
- favor from magnates and princes is lost in Weyer and the *Goetia*.

Conversely, the fallen-order statement and 1,200-year hope are absent from Clm 849 and appear in Weyer.

### Weyer’s Latin, 1577

Weyer’s entry begins:

> “Amy Præses magnus: apparet in flamma ignea: sed humana assumpta forma…”

A faithful complete sense-rendering is:

**Documented text, translated.** Amy is a great President. He appears in a fiery flame, but after assuming human form makes a person remarkable in astrology and all the liberal arts. He supplies excellent servants or familiars and shows treasures guarded by spirits. He commands thirty-six legions, partly from the order of Angels and partly from the Powers. He hopes to return to the Seventh Throne after 1,200 years, which is not credible.

Edition: Johann Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, fifth edition, Basel, 1577, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, Amy entry at the original pagination around p. 929. The complete Latin is available in Peterson’s [parallel edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Several lexical points matter:

- *Præses* means president, governor, or presiding officer.
- *Famulos* literally means servants or attendants; Scot and the *Goetia* interpret these as familiars.
- *Ostendit* means shows or reveals, not necessarily transfers ownership.
- *Potestatum* is the angelic order of Powers or Potestates.
- *Thronum septimum* says “Seventh Throne,” not explicitly “seventh heaven.”

### Scot’s English, 1584

Scot’s entry begins:

> “Amy is a great president, and appeareth in a flame of fier…”

In full sense it says that Amy, having taken man’s shape, makes someone marvelous in astrology and all liberal sciences; procures excellent familiars; reveals treasures preserved by spirits; governs thirty-six legions; belongs partly to Angels and partly to Potestates; and hopes after 1,200 years to return to the Seventh Throne—“which is not credible.”

Edition: Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London: William Brome, 1584, Book XVI, chapter 2. A first-edition image set is preserved by [Cornell’s Digital Witchcraft Collection](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081); Peterson’s online edition prints the passage beside Weyer’s Latin. The commonly cited Dover reprint places the whole demon inventory at pp. 217–227 rather than providing a stable Amy-only page.

**Documented context.** Scot was not endorsing demon evocation. Like Weyer, he reproduced conjuring lore within a skeptical exposure of witchcraft claims and ritual imposture. Later grimoires detached the catalog from that polemical setting.

### The Sloane Goetia

Sloane 2731 reads in part:

> “The 58th spirit in order is Named Amy; he is a Great President…”

Its substance is:

**Documented text.** Amy first appears as flaming fire and afterward assumes the shape of a man. His office is to make a person wondrously knowing in astrology and all liberal sciences. He gives good familiars, can disclose treasures kept by spirits, governs thirty-six legions, and has a seal to be worn as a lamen.

The complete diplomatic transcription is available at [Grimorium’s Sloane 2731 edition](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/).

Sloane 3825, fol. 108v, is Peterson’s principal witness. Its wording is extremely close, with ordinary scribal spellings such as *wounderous*, *Leberall*, and *govemeth*. Peterson regularizes or supplies doubtful readings in brackets. His printed entry is on p. 33.

**Textual change from Weyer.** The *Goetia* removes:

- the claim that Amy’s forces come partly from Angels and partly from Powers;
- the hope of returning to the Seventh Throne;
- Weyer’s skeptical “which is not credible.”

It adds:

- the numbered position 58;
- a seal;
- the instruction to wear that seal as a lamen.

### Harley 6483

Harley 6483 reads **Auns**:

> “The eight and fiftieth Spirit is Auns he is a great President…”

The entry otherwise follows the Sloane form: flaming fire, human shape, astrology and liberal sciences, good familiars, guarded treasure, and thirty-six legions.

Edition: Stephen Skinner and David Rankine, eds., *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard Press, 2007), pp. 159–160.

The editors date the surviving copy to 1712–1714 and argue that it preserves an earlier compilation by Thomas Rudd from approximately 1655–1656. Peterson accepts the manuscript’s late date and distinctive additions but regards it as the least reliable of the major *Goetia* witnesses because of extensive deviations. Thus:

- **Documented:** Harley 6483 contains the Rudd-style angel/demon scheme.
- **Scholarly reconstruction:** it copies a lost mid-seventeenth-century original by Thomas Rudd.
- **Disputed:** whether every distinctive feature should be attributed personally to Rudd.

### Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The printed entry begins:

> “AMY, or AVNAS.—The Fifty-eighth Spirit is Amy, or Avnas.”

It retains the Sloane description: Great President; flaming fire followed by a man; wondrous knowledge of astrology and liberal sciences; good familiars; disclosure of spirit-kept treasure; thirty-six legions; seal.

Edition: S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* (Foyers, Inverness, 1904), p. 41 in the Sacred Texts pagination. The complete entry and page markers are at [Sacred Texts](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

**Editorial point.** Mathers’ phrase “Wonderful Knowing” preserves the awkward manuscript syntax rather than translating Weyer’s smoother *reddit hominem admirabilem* straightforwardly as “makes a person remarkable.”

### Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s critical edition prints Amy on p. 33 and identifies the principal variants:

- Sloane 2731: Amy
- Sloane 3648: Amy
- Sloane 3825: Amy
- Harley 6483: Auns
- Mathers–Crowley: Amy or Avnas

Peterson regards Sloane 3825 as the most complete and internally reliable main witness, although the Amy seal is damaged by attempted correction. He therefore reproduces the cleaner Sloane 2731 version immediately below it. Publication details are confirmed by [Red Wheel/Weiser](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/): first published May 2001, ISBN 978-1-57863-220-6.

### What Amy does—and does not do

**Documented offices:**

- teaches or confers skill in astrology/astronomy;
- teaches all liberal sciences;
- supplies excellent or good familiars;
- reveals treasure guarded by spirits;
- in Clm 849, grants favor from magnates and princes;
- in Folger V.b.26, gives favor and describes guarded treasure.

**Not stated of Amy in the principal texts:**

- teaching languages specifically;
- answering all questions;
- revealing past and future;
- procuring love;
- invisibility;
- bodily transmutation;
- converting metals;
- curing disease;
- inducing fear;
- giving money directly.

Those belong to other Goetic spirits or to modern extrapolation. “Bewray treasure” means disclose or reveal it; the texts do not promise that Amy hands it over.

### Does the conjurer demand the human form?

**Negative finding.** Some other Goetic entries expressly say that a spirit assumes human form “at the request” or command of the master. Amy’s entry does not. It says:

- Weyer: “after human form has been assumed”;
- Sloane/Mathers: “after a while” he takes a man’s shape.

Modern paraphrases that say Amy changes “when commanded” import a general Goetic motif into a passage that does not contain it.

## Rank, planet, metal, and time

### President: Mercury and quicksilver

**Documented ritual correspondence.** The *Goetia*’s “Observations” assigns seals by rank:

- Kings — Sun/gold
- Marquises — Moon/silver
- Dukes — Venus/copper
- Prelates — Jupiter/tin
- Knights — Saturn/lead
- Presidents — **Mercury/mercury**
- Earls — Venus/copper and Moon/silver

Thus Amy’s rank implies:

- **Planet:** Mercury
- **Metal:** elemental mercury or quicksilver

This is a ritual material attribution, not an etymology or a claim in Amy’s individual descriptive paragraph. See the [1904 Observations](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks06.htm).

### Hours

The *Goetia* says Presidents may be bound at any hour of the day, except twilight or night, unless the king whom they obey is also invoked.

Weyer’s Latin is syntactically less clear and Scot renders it more restrictively: a President may not be bound during the day unless the relevant king is invoked, nor at evening twilight. The *Goetia* appears to have reinterpreted the sentence as a general daytime permission with a nighttime exception.

**Disputed textual point.** Consequently two readings exist:

1. **Scot/Weyer reading:** the ruling king is required for daytime constraint; twilight is excluded.
2. ***Goetia* reading:** Presidents are available by day; the king is required for twilight/night work.

Later advice to use Wednesday or the planetary hour of Mercury follows general planetary magic. It is a reasonable later application of the rank correspondence, but Amy’s early entries do not prescribe Wednesday.

### Moon

The *Goetia*’s general instructions recommend working when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old. Again, this is book-wide ritual timing, not an Amy-specific statement.

## The seal and its variants

### No seal in Weyer or Scot

**Documented absence.** Neither Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia* nor Scot’s translated inventory gives Amy a seal. The Goetic seals enter the surviving Amy source-chain through the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

Earlier manuscripts and spirit catalogs sometimes contain seals, but Peterson notes that seals in earlier related sources do not simply match the *Goetia* series. It is therefore unsafe to claim that Amy’s familiar seal was already present in Weyer’s lost source.

### Sloane 2731

The Sloane 2731 sigil is the cleanest readily legible manuscript form. In words, it is:

- an open, asymmetrical linear emblem rather than a closed polygon;
- dominated by a tall left-hand upright;
- joined to stepped or zigzagging strokes along the lower portion;
- carried upward and rightward into a curling or hooked flourish;
- without the elaborate external Hebrew and angelic circles found in Harley 6483.

Its identity depends on the topology and intersections of the lines, not on heraldic objects. Prose is therefore less exact than the manuscript facsimile.

### Sloane 3825

The Sloane 3825 Amy seal has:

- the same underlying compact, vertically weighted structure;
- heavy overwriting or correction in its upper-left region;
- a conspicuous ink blot;
- smaller loose or exploratory strokes beside the main form.

**Documented editorial judgment.** Peterson labels it an attempted correction and prints the Sloane 2731 seal so the intended form can be reconstructed. It would be misleading to treat every blot and loose stroke in Sloane 3825 as an intentional occult element.

### Harley 6483

Harley’s distinctive contribution is not merely a different drawing of Amy/Auns’s seal. It turns the lamen into a protective and constraining composite:

- the demon’s character is circumscribed and supplemented with a supposed Hebrew form of its name;
- the paired Shem angel’s character and Psalm text are placed in relation to it;
- the angelic side is oriented toward the magician’s heart.

**Tradition.** In this scheme the angel protects the operator from the spirit’s malice or deception and supplies divine authority over it. The form is a Christian-kabbalistic counterpoise, not a feature of Weyer or the earliest Sloane text.

### Mathers–Crowley

The 1904 engraver regularized the manuscript lines into the now-standard clean black seal. This is the form most modern tattoos, cards, pendants, websites, and ritual manuals reproduce.

**Modern practice warning.** Some sellers surround it with circles, planetary glyphs, invented alphabets, or the demonolatry “enn.” Those additions are not present in the historical 1904 figure.

### The 72 triplets from Exodus

**Documented Jewish exegetical method.** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the relevant Hebrew consonantal text, of three consecutive verses of seventy-two letters each. The letters are arranged boustrophedonically:

1. verse 19 in its ordinary direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its ordinary direction.

Reading vertically produces seventy-two three-letter groups. Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a explains the extraction as the first letter of verse 19, last letter of verse 20, first letter of verse 21, then the corresponding second letters, and so forth. See [Sefaria’s Exodus/Rashi material](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Quoting+Commentary) and Laura Joffe’s scholarly discussion, which notes the complete sequence in *Sefer Raziel* 23b, in [JSOT](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205).

The fifty-eighth column gives **ייל**.

### Three-letter divine name versus an angel

**Historical distinction.** In Jewish material the triplets are first and foremost components or expressions of the divine Name. The elaboration of each triplet into an independently named angel by adding endings such as **-El** or **-Yah** is especially characteristic of Christian Kabbalah.

Therefore:

- **Documented Jewish construction:** ייל as triplet no. 58.
- **Christian-kabbalistic angel-name:** יילאל, Ieialel.
- **Later guardian-angel personality:** a further development, not intrinsic to Exodus.

### Sefer Raziel and the medieval Jewish tradition

**Documented history.** *Sefer Razi’el ha-Mal’akh* is a compilation with layers from different periods. Recent scholarship distinguishes:

- late-antique and early-medieval materials;
- a substantial thirteenth-century Ashkenazi layer associated with Eleazar of Worms and related circles;
- later Sephardi and Byzantine kabbalistic material;
- a commentary on the seventy-two-letter Name plausibly dating to the fourteenth century;
- an editorial preface added close to the first printing in 1701.

See the academic study [“Sefer Razi’el ha-Mal’akh – A Conduit of Medieval Ashkenaz”](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2017/html?lang=en).

**Legend.** Later sources say that Moses employed the Name to divide the Red Sea; that it could expel demons, extinguish fire, heal illness, or win favor; and that impure use could be fatal. Joshua Trachtenberg summarizes such traditions in [*Jewish Magic and Superstition*](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm). These are historical beliefs about the Name, not evidence that a biblical angel named Yeialel was paired with Amy.

### Reuchlin

Johann Reuchlin’s *De Arte Cabalistica* (1517) is a major hinge between Jewish divine-name material and Christian angelology. Reuchlin treats the seventy-two forms as an angelic series and makes them pronounceable by divine suffixes.

Bernd Roling’s study, *72 in His Name*, documents Reuchlin’s treatment and its Christian setting; see the [scholarly PDF](https://e-univers.ru/upload/iblock/919/1d14cc5s1qdrw3vc1f1360nmgkaohtru.pdf).

### Agrippa

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia*, Book III, chapter 25, explains the three seventy-two-letter verses, reversal of the middle verse, formation of the triplets, and addition of *El* or *Jah* to obtain angelic names. The text is available in [Esoteric Archives’ Agrippa edition](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm).

Agrippa supplies a general Christian-occult method. He does not pair Yeialel specifically with Amy.

### Vigenère, Kircher, and Rudd

Blaise de Vigenère transmitted and expanded Reuchlin-derived Shem material in his *Traicté des chiffres* (1586). Skinner and Rankine argue that Thomas Rudd later copied and extended this material.

Athanasius Kircher’s *Œdipus Ægyptiacus* (Rome, 1652–1654) presented the seventy-two names in an enormous Christian-kabbalistic synthesis. His famous circular diagram can be inspected in the [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_XjfzMnomDQoC/page/n299/mode/thumb) and [Wikimedia facsimile](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg).

**Historical caution.** Kircher is important for transmission, not as a reliable interpreter of ancient Egyptian religion. His hieroglyphic system long predates their successful decipherment and freely combines Hebrew, Christian, Egyptian, astrological, and Hermetic materials.

### The Rudd pairing

**Documented manuscript scheme.** In Harley 6483, angel no. 58 is **Ieialel**, and demon no. 58 is **Auns**. The table gives:

- Hebrew ייל;
- *Deus auditor gemituum*;
- degrees 286–290;
- Michael;
- choir of Archangels;
- Auns.

The pairing is therefore verified. A transcription of the table appears at [Tarrdaniel’s Hermetic collection](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Hermetika/shem_ha_mephorash.html), while the complete edited manuscript treatment appears in Skinner and Rankine’s *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*.

**How constraint works.** The demon’s seal is not treated as sufficient in itself. The corresponding angel’s character and biblical verse provide a superior divine force, facing the operator on the lamen. The angel is invoked as protection and as the authority that binds or constrains the demon.

This is the closest Western magical parallel to the *Testament of Solomon*’s pattern in which individual demons identify the angels who frustrate them. It is not proof of direct textual descent in Amy’s case.

### Psalm 6

Rudd and Lenain associate Yeialel with:

> “Et anima mea turbata est valde…”

The full verse means: “And my soul is greatly troubled; but you, O Lord—how long?”

Hebrew:

> וְנַפְשִׁי נִבְהֲלָה מְאֹד וְאַתָּה יְהוָה עַד־מָתָי

Transliteration:

> *Ve-nafshi nivhalah me’od; ve-attah YHWH, ad-matai?*

Numbering varies:

- Vulgate/Rudd/Lenain citation: Psalm 6:3;
- King James numbering: Psalm 6:3;
- Hebrew editions counting the superscription: commonly Psalm 6:4.

### The angel’s “meaning”

Two traditional glosses must be separated.

1. **Rudd-derived Latin:** *Deus auditor gemituum* — “God who hears groans/lamentations.”
2. **Lenain’s French:** *Dieu qui exauce les générations* — literally “God who answers/hears the generations.”

The second may reflect Lenain’s received table, a corruption, or an idiosyncratic interpretation. Neither is a straightforward lexical translation of ייל, which is an artificially extracted consonantal triplet rather than an ordinary Hebrew word.

Modern phrases such as “God who hears everything,” “God who hears cries,” “mental power,” or “divine consolation” are translations or expansions of these inherited titles, not independent ancient meanings.

### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* (Amiens, 1823), pp. 85–86, gives Ieialel’s developed French occult profile:

- ray from the 286th through 290th degree;
- correspondence with the twenty-ninth decade and a genius called Epima;
- rotating calendar days: 16 May, 27 July, 7 October, 18 December, 28 February;
- invocation from 7:00 to 7:20 p.m.;
- Psalm 6:3;
- assistance against grief and especially eye disease;
- dominion over iron;
- influence over armorers, locksmiths, cutlers, and iron merchants;
- power to confound evildoers and false witnesses;
- favorable character: bravery, frankness, and passion for Venus;
- contrary genius: anger, wickedness, and homicide.

The primary scan is available from [IAPSOP](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf).

**Layer warning.** Yeialel’s dominion over **iron** does not change Amy’s President-metal from **mercury**. One belongs to Lenain’s angelology; the other belongs to the *Goetia*’s rank system.

### Papus

Papus, Gérard Encausse, reproduced and promoted the seventy-two-genii system in *La Cabbale: tradition secrète de l’Occident*. The substantially enlarged 1903 edition explicitly explains derivation from Exodus 14:19–21 and incorporates Lenain’s practical material. See the [1903 primary scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1903__papus___cabbala_tradition_secrete_de_loccident.pdf).

Papus helped make Lenain’s French synthesis accessible to Martinist and fin-de-siècle occult audiences. He did not originate the Amy–Ieialel pairing.

### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (Paris: Niclaus, 1951) republished and elaborated the practical angelic scheme. Ambelain belongs to the twentieth-century French occult line from Lenain and Papus rather than to the medieval Jewish formation of the triplets. A searchable English translation is available as [*Practical Kabbalah*](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf).

Ambelain’s editions and later derivative tables sometimes alter spellings, angelic seals, planetary chiefs, or civil dates. These should not be silently read back into Rudd or Lenain.

### Haziel

The French-language author publishing as Haziel popularized the seventy-two “guardian angels” through late twentieth-century books organized around birth dates, personality traits, prayers, and daily influence. In this layer Yeialel becomes a personal guardian for a short January period, usually around 6–10 January depending on the calendar convention.

**Modern esoteric development.** This guardian-angel horoscope is downstream of the five-degree zodiac scheme; it is not present in Exodus, Rashi, Weyer, or Amy’s *Goetia* entry. Different modern schools give slightly different civil dates because a fixed five-degree tropical sector does not map permanently onto the same clock dates in every calendrical convention.

### Quinance versus decan

This is one of the most persistent modern confusions.

#### Yeialel’s quinance

Seventy-two five-degree sectors divide the zodiac:

72 × 5° = 360°.

Ieialel no. 58 occupies degrees 286–290 in the Rudd/Lenain enumeration, conventionally **15°–20° Capricorn**.

#### Amy’s Golden Dawn decan

The Golden Dawn/Crowley demon scheme instead divides the thirty-six decans into a diurnal and nocturnal spirit. Each tarot pip from Two through Ten therefore receives two Goetic spirits.

Amy is the **night spirit** of **0°–10° Scorpio**, paired with Ipos as day spirit. The corresponding card is the **Five of Cups**, Mars in Scorpio, “Lord of Loss in Pleasure.”

These cannot both be Amy’s single ancient astrological location. They arise from different constructions:

- Yeialel’s number-based five-degree sector;
- Amy’s later day/night placement in a ten-degree tarot decan.

### Crowley’s *777*

In *Liber 777*, Table V:

- column CLXI lists Goetic demons by night;
- Amy is no. 58;
- the row is the zodiacal path corresponding to Scorpio;
- the magical image is summarized as flaming fire.

A searchable facsimile is available from [Hermetic.com](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777.pdf); the revised version is [here](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf).

Some machine-readable copies render Amy’s rank glyph as Venus. *777 Revised* and DuQuette’s tabulation give Mercury, consistent with Amy’s President rank. Because the old planetary glyphs are easily confused in OCR, this should be checked against the facsimile before being treated as an intentional Venus attribution.

### The Five of Cups

In the Golden Dawn’s *Book T* system:

- Card: Five of Cups
- Title: Lord of Loss in Pleasure
- Decan: first decan of Scorpio
- Planetary decan ruler: Mars
- Day Goetic spirit: Ipos
- Night Goetic spirit: Amy
- Amy’s rank planet: Mercury

The result is not simply “Amy is Mars.” Rather:

- Mars governs the tarot decan;
- Mercury comes from Amy’s infernal rank.

Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* gives Amy at 0°–10° Scorpio, approximately 23 October–1 November in its civil-date convention, Five of Cups, and “Mercury–Mercury”; see the [scanned edition](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf), p. 95.

### Enochian overlays

Two distinct developments are often called “Enochian”:

1. **Rudd tradition:** Harley 6483 combines Goetic material with John Dee-derived sacred tables, especially the *Tabula Sancta* or related angelic apparatus.
2. **Crowley 1904:** Crowley supplied versions of general conjurations in the language he called Enochian or Angelic.

Neither establishes a medieval “Enochian name of Amy.” Amy’s individual catalog entry remains English/Latin-derived, and אמי in *777* is simply a later Hebrew transliteration.

### Modern enn

Modern demonolatry sources assign Amy:

> “Tu Fubin Amy secore”

**Modern invention.** This phrase does not occur in Clm 849, Weyer, Scot, any Sloane manuscript, Harley 6483, or Mathers–Crowley. It belongs to the modern “enn” system popularized in demonolatry literature associated with S. Connolly and related authors. A representative list is available from [Demonolatry.org](https://demonolatry.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/goetic.pdf).

## Iconography

### The manuscript image

Amy’s earliest “portrait” is verbal:

- a fiery flame;
- subsequently, a man.

No age, clothing, face, attributes, weapons, animals, complexion, or posture are specified. Modern artists therefore have very little fixed iconography to follow.

The seal becomes Amy’s most stable visual identifier in the Sloane manuscripts and the 1904 edition.

### Collin de Plancy, 1818–1863

**Documented text.** Amy is indexed in Collin de Plancy’s first, 1818 edition of the *Dictionnaire infernal*. The work was repeatedly revised, and its ideological tone changed substantially over six editions.

The verified 1863 entry, p. 30, begins:

> “Amy, grand président aux enfers, et l’un des princes de la monarchie infernale.”

In substance, de Plancy says Amy is a great President in Hell and one of the princes of the infernal monarchy. In Hell he is surrounded by flames; on earth he affects human features. He teaches astrology and the liberal arts, gives good servants, reveals demon-guarded treasures to his friends, commands thirty-six legions, and has fallen Angels and Powers under him.

The full French entry is at [Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Amy); the 1863 scan is at [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_1).

### The 200,000-year corruption

Weyer says **1,200 years**. De Plancy’s 1863 edition says **two hundred thousand years**.

**Documented textual alteration.** This is not a translation of *mille ducentos annos*. It is either a copying/printing corruption or a deliberate enlargement. De Plancy then retains Weyer’s skeptical conclusion: the hope is not credible.

A full edition-by-edition collation is still needed to establish precisely when 1,200 became 200,000. The 1818 edition contains an Amy entry, but the accessible indexing alone does not prove which figure that first edition printed.

### Does Louis Le Breton give Amy a portrait?

**Negative finding.** No Amy portrait is included among the famous Louis Le Breton demon engravings in the 1863 edition. The Amy entry is text-only.

The often-repeated claim that “de Plancy illustrated Amy” confuses the illustrated book with the subset of entries actually illustrated. The 1863 alphabetical illustration gallery contains figures such as Amduscias and Amon but no labeled Amy portrait. The surviving engraving set can be checked in the [Wikimedia category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Illustrations_from_Dictionnaire_Infernal,_6th_edition).

### Modern art

**Modern invention.** Contemporary Amy images commonly show:

- a masculine figure emerging from flames;
- a scholar, astrologer, or robed magician;
- Mercurial imagery;
- a female or androgynous demon;
- fiery wings or a blazing halo;
- the 1904 seal as a background device.

Only fire, subsequent human form, and the seal have direct historical anchors. Gender-fluid forms, armor, books, planetary colors, horns, and wings are artistic or practitioner additions unless a particular artist explicitly identifies a separate source.

## Grimoire ancestors and related literature

### The *Testament of Solomon*

**Structural ancestor, not an Amy source.** The *Testament of Solomon* presents Solomon interrogating demons, learning their functions, and discovering the angels who thwart them. This offers an important ancient model for demon catalogs and angelic counter-forces.

Amy, Hanni, Hanar, Auns, and Avnas do not appear in its surviving demon list. The Rudd one-angel-per-demon mechanism resembles the *Testament* structurally, but no direct Amy–Yeialel chain can be traced from it.

### The *Livre des Esperitz*

The French *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, contains a substantial demon catalog related to Weyer’s tradition. Jean-Patrice Boudet’s edition identifies thirty-five spirits shared with Weyer.

**Negative finding.** Amy/Hanni is not one of the spirits securely present in the *Livre des Esperitz*. Amy appears in comparative apparatus as Weyer’s entry, not in the French catalog itself.

Boudet also warns against overly early dating: the surviving manuscript copy is mid-sixteenth-century, although some materials and catalog structures are older. See [“Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

### The Munich handbook

Clm 849 is the earliest direct ancestor presently identified for Amy’s profile. It establishes that the description circulated in Latin necromantic literature by the first half of the fifteenth century.

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer says his source was known as *Liber officiorum spirituum*, the Book of the Offices of Spirits, or by related Solomonic titles. The exact exemplar he used is lost.

Folger V.b.26 preserves an English *Office of Spirits* branch containing Hanar. It supports the existence of a wider, fluid textual family, but it is not necessarily a direct copy of Weyer's source.

### The *Book of Abramelin*

**Negative finding.** Amy/Hanni is not securely attested in the standard Abramelin spirit lists. Abramelin shares broad Solomonic demonological features and major names such as Lucifer, Satan, Leviathan, and Belial, but it is not a source for Amy’s individual profile.

### Greek *goēteia*

The title *Goetia* derives from Greek **γοητεία**, sorcery or magical practice, related to **γόης**, a magician, enchanter, or charlatan, and traditionally associated with lamenting or wailing. This explains the name of the art or book, not the name Amy. See the lexical history at [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/goetia).

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer was a physician, former student of Agrippa, and a major opponent of witch prosecution. He accepted a world containing spirits but argued that many accused witches were deluded, ill, or exploited. His *Pseudomonarchia* exposed the supposed infernal hierarchy as deception.

For Amy, Weyer:

- first prints the exact spelling;
- changes Hanni’s astronomy to astrology;
- fixes the later-standard thirty-six legions;
- adds the mixed Angel/Powers origin;
- adds the Seventh-Throne legend;
- explicitly calls that hope incredible.

### Reginald Scot

Scot translated or adapted Weyer’s catalog for Book XVI of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* in 1584. Peterson notes that Abraham Fleming, who translated other Latin material for Scot, may have produced this section, but that attribution is not certain.

Scot disseminated Amy in English while retaining Weyer’s skeptical conclusion.

### Thomas Rudd

The Rudd tradition adds the one-to-one angelic counterforce, Psalm verse, and double-seal conception. The surviving Harley manuscript is later than Rudd, so statements about Rudd’s personal practice remain reconstructions from the manuscript’s contents and provenance.

### Mathers and Crowley

S. L. MacGregor Mathers prepared the influential modern edition from British Museum manuscripts. Aleister Crowley published it in 1904, added introductory and interpretive material, and supplied Angelic/Enochian versions of conjurations.

For Amy, their most consequential effects were:

- popularizing **Avnas**;
- fixing the clean printed seal;
- carrying Amy into the Golden Dawn/Crowley correspondence literature.

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding.** No reliable Amy-specific working, commentary, or doctrinal contribution by Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, was found. Including him merely because he was a Thelemite would imply evidence that is not presently available.

### Arthur Edward Waite

Waite excerpted and discussed Goetic literature in works such as *The Book of Black Magic and of Pacts*. His role is primarily one of late Victorian transmission and critique. Peterson considers several older printed transcriptions less reliable than direct manuscript comparison.

### Poke Runyon

Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* and Ordo Templi Astartes practice helped popularize ritual use of Goetic spirits in late twentieth- and early twenty-first-century American ceremonial magic. Runyon’s planetary, zodiacal, mirror, and psychological frameworks are modern operative interpretations.

No primary Amy operation by Runyon was located. Runyon should therefore be described as influential in modern Goetic practice generally, not as an Amy specialist.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette, with Christopher Hyatt and David P. Wilson, coauthored *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*. Its Amy table explicitly gives:

- 0°–10° Scorpio;
- nocturnal;
- Five of Cups;
- Mercury rank correspondence.

This is a clear source for the modern summary often copied online.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s angel/demon machinery widely available. Its particular value for Amy is the verified **Auns–Ieialel** pairing and Psalm 6:3.

Its title embodies an editorial thesis: that the manuscript represents Rudd’s work. That thesis should not be confused with a contemporary authorial inscription proving every page was written by Rudd.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition remains the most useful accessible critical collation of the *Lesser Key*. For Amy it:

- uses Sloane 3825 as base text;
- identifies Auns and Avnas as variants;
- reproduces the damaged Sloane 3825 seal;
- supplies the cleaner Sloane 2731 seal;
- traces Amy to Clm 849’s Hanni;
- compares Weyer’s 69 entries with the *Goetia*’s 72.

### Richard Kieckhefer

Kieckhefer edited and analyzed Clm 849 in *Forbidden Rites: A Necromancer’s Manual of the Fifteenth Century*. His work moved Amy’s reconstructable history back before Weyer and preserved the Hanni text in a reliable scholarly edition.

### Jean-Patrice Boudet and Julien Véronèse

Boudet’s study of Renaissance demonological “who’s who” catalogs is the particularly relevant item for the *Livre des Esperitz*. Julien Véronèse’s broader research on medieval ritual magic helps contextualize Solomonic and necromantic textual transmission, but no Amy-specific thesis by Véronèse was located.

### Owen Davies

Davies’ *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* provides the larger history of grimoires, their circulation, prosecution, print, and modern afterlives. It is context rather than an Amy-specific textual edition.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Geosophia*, *The True Grimoire*, and *Pandemonium* profoundly influenced modern attempts to treat Goetic spirits as historically continuous chthonic or spirit-cult beings rather than merely psychological complexes.

**Qualification.** No source-driven Amy-specific etymology or operation by Stratton-Kent was located. General theories about ancient survival should not be used to supply Amy with an otherwise unattested ancient deity.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. “Amy first appears in Weyer”

**Verdict: only true of the spelling.**

- Earliest exact **Amy**: Weyer, 1577.
- Earliest reconstructable profile: **Hanni**, Clm 849, fifteenth century.
- Related sixteenth-century branch: **Hanar**, Folger V.b.26.

### 2. “Amy is an ancient god”

**Verdict: unsupported.** No ancient divine, heroic, geographic, Greek, Latin, Egyptian, or Semitic identity has been demonstrated. Comparisons to the personal name Amy, Latin love-words, Lithuanian *avinas*, or Haniel are conjectural and should be labeled as such.

### 3. “Avnas is an ancient alternate name”

**Verdict: unsupported.** The first secure appearance located is Mathers–Crowley in 1904. It may be an editorial development from Harley’s **Auns**.

### 4. “All sources say thirty-six legions”

**Verdict: false.**

- Clm 849: thirty.
- Folger Hanar: eight.
- Weyer onward: thirty-six.

The repeated online claim overlooks the two earliest cognate descriptions.

### 5. “Amy commands thirty-six legions of demons”

Weyer says thirty-six legions, partly Angels and partly Powers. The *Goetia* calls them “spirits.” “Demons” is a reasonable genre description but suppresses the unusual mixed fallen-angel statement.

### 6. The Seventh Throne

**Legend.** Weyer and Scot say Amy expects to return after 1,200 years, then reject the expectation as incredible. The *Goetia* omits the entire claim. De Plancy changes it to 200,000 years.

There is no evidence that this is a remnant of an ancient myth about Amy. It may be a Christian demonological embellishment added between Clm 849 and Weyer.

### 7. Seventh Throne or seventh heaven?

Weyer’s wording is **Seventh Throne**. “Seventh heaven” is a common paraphrase. It may reflect the cosmological sense, but it is not a literal quotation.

### 8. Whether the human form is demanded

Amy’s entry does not say “at the conjurer’s request.” That phrase belongs to other spirits’ descriptions and later generalized retellings.

### 9. Amy versus Aym/Aim

They are separate catalog entries:

- Aim/Aym/Haborym: Duke, three heads, viper, burning brand.
- Amy: President, fiery manifestation, learning, familiars, treasure.

Conflation is facilitated by spelling, adjacent transmission, and fire imagery, not by the source lists.

### 10. “Bewray” means betray

In early modern English *bewray* means reveal, disclose, or make known. It does not mean that Amy treacherously steals the treasure from its owners. Mathers’ text preserves an archaic verb that later readers often misunderstand.

### 11. The seal’s “planetary-square construction”

No source examined explains Amy’s seal as generated from a Mercury square, Venus square, Trithemian alphabet, or Hebrew letter-path. Such construction theories are modern reconstructions unless demonstrated line by line from a manuscript.

The particularly unreliable claim that the seal is Venusian contradicts the *Goetia*’s explicit President–Mercury table.

### 12. Rudd’s authorship

- **Certain:** Harley 6483 contains the angel/demon system and dates to the early eighteenth century.
- **Plausible reconstruction:** it copies Thomas Rudd’s mid-seventeenth-century material.
- **Not certain:** that Rudd personally composed every part or that the surviving copy exactly preserves a 1650s original.

### 13. Is Yeialel Amy’s “opposite”?

“Opposite” is a serviceable modern shorthand but not necessarily a metaphysical statement. In the Rudd system Yeialel is the corresponding angelic power used to protect the magician and constrain Auns. The manuscript does not establish that Amy and Yeialel are equal cosmic halves, psychological shadows, or enemies outside the ritual arrangement.

### 14. Is the pairing Jewish Kabbalah?

The triplet ייל is Jewish. The full angel Ieialel belongs to Christian-kabbalistic elaboration. Pairing Ieialel no. 58 mechanically with Goetic spirit no. 58 is a Rudd-type Christian ceremonial-magic synthesis, not an ancient Jewish teaching.

### 15. Amy’s zodiac sign

At least two answers circulate because two systems are being mixed:

- **0°–10° Scorpio:** Golden Dawn/Crowley demon decan; Five of Cups.
- **15°–20° Capricorn:** Ieialel’s five-degree Shem sector.

Calling both “Amy’s quinance” obscures their origins.

### 16. Amy’s gender

Every early textual witness uses masculine grammar or pronouns. Contemporary artists and practitioners sometimes describe Amy as female, androgynous, or variable.

- Masculine presentation: documented source-chain.
- Variable gender: modern art or UPG.
- The human female name *Amy*: not evidence that the historical spirit was female.

### 17. Modern powers and personality

Modern practitioners attribute to Amy such things as:

- runes and rune divination;
- yoga and meditation;
- astral travel;
- warding and banishing;
- symbol interpretation;
- cannabis;
- warrior qualities;
- gender fluidity.

These are documented as modern personal claims—for example in contemporary practitioner blogs—but do not occur in the early texts. One such modern account candidly includes highly personal advice; see [V. K. Jehannum’s Amy/Avnas entry](https://vkjehannum.wordpress.com/2017/06/03/amyavnas-goetia-58/). They should be classified as UPG, not silently incorporated into a historical profile.

### 18. De Plancy’s portrait

Amy appears in the dictionary, but no Louis Le Breton portrait of Amy was found. Reproductions labeled “de Plancy’s Amy” are likely modern illustrations, misattributions, or confusion with another engraved demon.

## Open questions

1. **What was the archetypal name?**  
   Hanni, Hanar, Amy, and Auns can be linked by their descriptions, but the direction of every spelling change is not recoverable.

2. **Was Hanni an angelic name such as Haniel?**  
   Kieckhefer’s comparison is possible but unproved.

3. **Who coined Avnas?**  
   Mathers popularized it in 1904, but whether he deciphered it from a now-unidentified manuscript, expanded *Auns*, or introduced an error remains unresolved.

4. **Where did Amy’s seal originate?**  
   It is present in the seventeenth-century Sloane manuscripts but absent from Weyer, Scot, Clm 849, and the Folger Hanar entry. No demonstrated generative method has been recovered.

5. **What precisely happened to the Sloane 3825 seal?**  
   It shows overwriting, a large blot, and loose fragments. Peterson’s comparison makes the intended core legible, but the correction’s sequence and responsible hand merit direct codicological examination.

6. **When did de Plancy change 1,200 years to 200,000?**  
   The first edition includes Amy, and the sixth prints 200,000, but a full collation of the 1818, 1825–26, 1844, and intervening editions is still required.

7. **How much of Harley 6483 is genuinely Rudd’s?**  
   The attribution remains a scholarly reconstruction based on content, dates, and manuscript relationships.

8. **Why was Amy moved from Weyer’s position to Goetia no. 58?**  
   The entire list was reorganized. No item-specific theological reason for Amy’s new number has been established.

9. **Was the no. 58 pairing chosen solely by matching numbers?**  
   That is the simplest explanation for the complete Rudd series. No earlier independent affinity between Amy’s offices and ייל has been found.

10. **Did Frater Achad, Poke Runyon, or Jake Stratton-Kent work specifically with Amy?**  
    Their broader relevance to Thelema or Goetia is documented, but an adequately sourced Amy-specific record was not found.

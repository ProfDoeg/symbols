# Haagenti: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **[Documented text]** — directly present in a manuscript, printed grimoire, catalog, game, or other cited source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a historical or textual relationship argued by a modern scholar.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim transmitted within ritual-magic or devotional literature without independent historical proof.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a deliberately constructed magical, astrological, or Qabalistic association.
- **[Disputed]** — a reading challenged by textual, chronological, linguistic, or manuscript evidence.
- **[Modern invention/adaptation]** — a feature first found in recent occultism, fiction, games, or fan culture.

“Haagenti” has no demonstrable ancient cult, myth, or classical etymology. Unlike names such as Baal, Astarte, Amon, Asmodeus, Belial, or Phoenix, it cannot at present be connected securely to an ancient god, hero, place, Greek noun, Latin noun, or Hebrew expression.

1. **Haagenti’s Golden Dawn/Crowley decan:** 20°–30° Cancer, Four of Cups.
2. **Mihael’s Lenain/Shem quinance:** 25°–30° Scorpio, within the Seven of Cups decan.

The Rudd pairing joins them by their shared ordinal number, 48—not because their zodiacal positions coincide.

# The name and its origins

## 1. No established ancient etymology

**[Documented finding]** The exact name *Haagenti* first appears securely in Johann Weyer’s 1577 *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*. No earlier classical, biblical, rabbinic, patristic, Arabic, Greek, Latin, or medieval source has yet yielded that spelling.

**[Scholarly finding]** There is no accepted derivation of *Haagenti* in the principal scholarship on Weyer’s catalog and its manuscript ancestors. Its form does not yield a persuasive ancient name when treated as ordinary Latin, Greek, Hebrew, Arabic, Egyptian, Germanic, or French.

Consequently:

- It is not established as the name of an ancient god.
- It is not established as an ancient hero or geographical name.
- It is not demonstrably related to Latin *argentum*, “silver.”
- It is not demonstrably derived from Greek *hagios*, “holy.”
- It has no documented connection to Egyptian Amenti, Osiris, Ra, or Bastet.
- It has no evidenced derivation from a Hittite or Anatolian deity.

Those proposals occur principally on modern occult, demonolatry, social-media, or fan pages. No historical spelling-chain or philological argument connects them to Weyer’s word.

## 2. The *Livre des esperitz* and Bugan

The most important prehistory is not an ancient deity but a demon called **Bugan**.

The French *Livre des esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, contains this entry:

> **Bugan est ung grant roy qui faict home saige et faire touz manieres de metaulx muer en quelconque maniere que on vieult, l’eau muer en vin ou en huille ; et a XXXIIII legions.**

A close translation is:

> Bugan is a great king who makes a man wise and makes every kind of metal change in whatever manner one wishes, and changes water into wine or oil; he has thirty-four legions.

This is available in the [French transcription](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier) and the [manuscript facsimile](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Livre_des_esperitz_-_MS_O.8.29.djvu).

The parallels with Haagenti are unusually strong:

| Bugan | Haagenti |
|---|---|
| Makes a man wise | Makes a person wise/ingenious |
| Changes metals at will | Turns all metals into gold |
| Changes water into wine or oil | Exchanges water and wine |
| 34 legions | 33 legions |
| Great king | Great president |
| No appearance supplied | Winged bull, then human |

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Jean-Patrice Boudet identifies Bugan as the probable textual counterpart or ancestor of Haagenti in his study of Renaissance demonological “who’s who” catalogs. This does not mean that *Bugan* linguistically evolved into *Haagenti* by a known phonetic process. More probably, both catalog entries descend from a damaged, rearranged, or multiply copied *Liber officiorum spirituum* tradition.

**[Dating dispute]** The manuscript was once described broadly as fifteenth-century or late fifteenth/early sixteenth century. Boudet’s palaeographical assessment places its hand no earlier than approximately the middle of the sixteenth century. It may therefore precede Weyer’s printing without being a medieval witness in the strict sense.

Accordingly, the most exact statement is:

> **Bugan is the earliest presently known attribute-cognate of Haagenti; Haagenti is the earliest secure attestation of the exact name.**

# The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, 1577

### Latin text

Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577, gives Haagenti as number 67, at column/page 930:

> **Haagenti magnus Præses: ut taurus videtur, habens alas gryphi: sed assumpta facie humana, reddit hominem ingeniosum in quibuslibet: cuncta metalla in aurum transmutat, aquam in vinum, & ediversò. Tot legionibus imperat, quot Zagan.**

—Johann Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* §67, 1577, p./col. 930; transcription in [Peterson’s Weyer edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Translation

> Haagenti, a great President, appears as a bull having the wings of a griffin; but, having assumed a human face, he makes a person ingenious in all things. He transmutes all metals into gold, water into wine, and conversely. He commands as many legions as Zagan.

Important textual points:

- **[Documented text]** Weyer says simply *ut taurus*, “as a bull,” not “a great” or “mighty bull.”
- *Assumpta facie humana* literally says “having assumed a human face.” Later versions broaden this to “human shape.”
- *Ingeniosum in quibuslibet* means resourceful, clever, talented, or ingenious “in anything/every matter.” “Wise in everything” is a reasonable early-modern paraphrase but not the only possible translation.
- Weyer does not give Haagenti an explicit number of legions; he refers backward to Zagan.
- Weyer’s Zagan commands **33 legions**, so Haagenti inherits 33 by reference.
- Weyer provides no sigil for Haagenti.

## 2. Reginald Scot, 1584

Scot’s English version appears in Book XV, chapter 2, on page 393 of the 1584 edition:

> **Haagenti is a great president, appearing like a great bull, having the wings of a griphen, but when he taketh humane shape, he maketh a man wise in everie thing, he changeth all mettals into gold, and changeth wine and water the one into the other, and commandeth as manie legions as Zagan.**

—Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584, XV.2, p. 393; searchable in [Project Gutenberg’s edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/60766/60766-h/60766-h.htm) and aligned with Weyer at [Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Differences from Weyer:

- Scot adds **“great”** to the bull.
- “Human face” becomes **“humane shape.”**
- *Ingeniosum* becomes **“wise.”**
- Weyer’s water-to-wine “and conversely” becomes an explicit exchange of **wine and water**.
- Scot retains the cross-reference to Zagan rather than supplying 33 directly.
- Scot supplies neither seals nor a diagrammatic apparatus.

Peterson notes that the English translation has traditionally been attributed to Scot but may owe some of its wording to Abraham Fleming or to a manuscript version of Weyer’s source. The exact responsibility remains uncertain.

## 3. Sloane MS 3825

Sloane 3825, fols. 107r–107v, gives the mature Goetia form:

> **The 48th spirit is called Haagenti, he is a great president appearing in the forme of a mighty Bull wth griffins wings at first, But afterwards at ye command of the Exorcist, he putteth on humane shape &c his office is to make men wise and to Instruct them in divers Things & to Transmute all mettales into gold, & change wine into water, & water into wine; he commandeth 33 Legions of spirits; his seal is Thus made, & to be worne as a Lamin &c.**

—Sloane MS 3825, fols. 107r–v; transcribed in Joseph H. Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (2001), p. 29, and in the [online critical text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

The Goetia recension makes several changes:

- Haagenti moves from Weyer’s 67 to **48**.
- The bull becomes **“mighty.”**
- Human form is now assumed specifically **“at the command of the Exorcist.”**
- “Instruct them in diverse things” is added.
- The number **33** is stated directly rather than inherited through Zagan.
- A graphic seal is supplied.
- The seal is to be worn as a **lamen**, a ritual badge or pendant.

## 4. Sloane MS 2731

Sloane 2731 contains the same essential entry and the spelling **Haagenti**. It belongs to the same Lemegeton textual family but differs from Sloane 3825 in handwriting, abbreviation, punctuation, and minor sigil geometry.

**[Documented comparison]** No major alternative biography of Haagenti appears in Sloane 2731. Its significance lies chiefly in corroborating:

- position 48;
- rank of president;
- the bull-and-griffin-wing appearance;
- human transformation;
- wisdom, instruction, and transmutation;
- 33 legions;
- the use of a drawn seal.

A facsimile is available as [Sloane 2731](https://booksofmagick.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Clavicula-Salomonis-Sloane-2731.pdf).

## 5. Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

Harley 6483 gives:

> **The forty eight Spirit is Haagenti he is a great President appearing in the form of a mighty Bull having the wings of a Griffin but after at the Exorcist's command he putteth on human shape. His office is to make men wise and to instruct them in diverse things and to transmute and change Metals into Gold and change wine into water and water into wine. He commands 33 Legions of spirits. His Seal is thus made.**

—Harley MS 6483, in Stephen Skinner and David Rankine, *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (2007), p. 150; searchable transcription [here](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

The spirit-description itself is essentially the ordinary Goetia recension. The principal innovation is the apparatus surrounding it: the corresponding Shem angel, angelic name, psalm text, ritual lamen, and constraints designed to place the demon under angelic authority.

## 6. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The 1904 edition prints:

> **(48.) HAAGENTI.—The Forty-eighth Spirit is Haagenti. He is a President, appearing in the Form of a Mighty Bull with Gryphon’s Wings. This is at first, but after, at the Command of the Exorcist he putteth on Human Shape. His Office is to make Men wise, and to instruct them in divers things; also to Transmute all Metals into Gold; and to change Wine into Water, and Water into Wine. He governeth 33 Legions of Spirits, and his Seal is this, etc.**

—S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* (1904), p. 28; [facsimile PDF](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf), [HTML edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/72679/72679-h/72679-h.htm).

Mathers drops “great” from the rank but otherwise follows the manuscript closely. Crowley edited and published Mathers’s translation, added prefatory and supplementary material, and printed an Enochian or “Angelic” rendering of the conjurations. He did not create Haagenti’s core description.

## 7. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (Samuel Weiser, 2001), p. 29, restores a manuscript-oriented text based principally on Sloane 3825 while recording readings from Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses.

**[Scholarly edition]** Peterson is more reliable for manuscript wording than editions that simply reprint Mathers–Crowley. His entry confirms that *Haagenti*, not *Hagenti*, is the principal manuscript spelling.

The edition is available through the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/joseph-h.-peterson-the-lesser-key-of-solomon-2001), with Peterson’s online text at [Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

## Rank, planet, metal, and timing

The Goetia’s “Observations” assigns the presidents’ seals to **Mercury**. Materially, this means mercury or quicksilver; astrologically, it means the planet Mercury.

| Rank | Traditional planet | Traditional metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury/quicksilver |
| Prelate/Prince in the manuscript table | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |

The surviving table is not a perfectly systematic seven-planet scheme. Mars/iron is conspicuously absent, and titles vary between manuscripts. Mathers later speculated about alloys and alternative assignments, but the direct manuscript association for a president is Mercury.

### Timing

Weyer’s general directions say:

> **Præses in aliqua hora diei non potest constringi, nisi rex cui paret, invocaretur, & nec in crepusculo noctis.**

The syntax is awkward, and manuscripts or editions punctuate it differently. Scot renders the instruction:

> **A President may not be bound in anie houre of the daie, except the king, whome he obeieth, be invocated; nor in the shutting of the evening.**

The Goetia’s later “Observations” is normally interpreted as follows:

- A president may be constrained during the day, except at twilight.
- If worked with at night, the king whom the president obeys must also be invoked.
- The king’s identity is not specified in Haagenti’s individual entry.
- Later tables sometimes assign Haagenti to Corson, but that belongs to a reconstructed directional scheme, not the original description.

The Goetia also recommends lunar ages of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days for operations. Planetary hours of Mercury, Wednesdays, orange colors, and similar details are reasonable later Hermetic extrapolations from the president/Mercury association; they are not part of Weyer’s Haagenti entry.

## The seal

### What the manuscripts actually say

The early demon catalogs of Weyer and Scot contain no Haagenti seal. The Lemegeton manuscripts say only that “his seal is thus made” and provide a drawing. They do not translate its lines into words or explain a cryptographic meaning.

The familiar printed form can be viewed [here](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g48.jpg) or as a [public-domain 1904 reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:48-Haagenti_seal.png).

### Verbal description

Because the source is graphic, no verbal account can be as exact as the image. The standard Mathers form is:

- a predominantly horizontal, angular character;
- a small closed or nearly closed loop/eye at the left;
- a long bar entering a sequence of squared steps and changes of level;
- a prominent upright near the middle;
- a descending and rising rectilinear progression toward the right;
- small rounded or looped terminals incorporated into the right-hand portion.

The form is closer to an angular piece of magical shorthand than to an ordinary alphabetic monogram. Nothing in the manuscripts identifies its components with specific letters.

### Manuscript variants

**[Documented text-critical finding]**

- Sloane 2731 and Sloane 3825 preserve the same recognizable design but differ in line length, closure of loops, stroke angle, and the relative height of uprights.
- Mathers regularized the freehand manuscript form into a cleaner print design.
- Harley 6483 incorporates the demon’s seal into a more elaborate Ruddian protective and angelic setting.
- Later vector drawings introduce further symmetry and line-straightening.

These are primarily penographic and editorial variants, not separate seals with distinct documented meanings. Online claims that the figure contains “three rings,” planetary writing, runes, Egyptian signs, or a hidden spelling of the name were not found in the early sources.

# The angel: Mihael

## 1. The seventy-twofold Name

The Shem HaMephorash system considered here begins from Exodus 14:19–21. In the Hebrew Masoretic text, each of those three verses contains 72 letters.

The standard construction is:

1. Write Exodus 14:19 in its normal direction.
2. Write Exodus 14:20 beneath it in reverse order.
3. Write Exodus 14:21 in its normal direction.
4. Read down the 72 columns.

Each column yields a three-letter group. The 48th is:

> **מיה — MIH**

In Christian Cabala and subsequent angel magic, **-El** or **-Yah** is added to many of the triplets, producing pronounceable angelic names. MIH therefore becomes:

> **מיהאל — Mihael**

Agrippa explains the alternating three-verse construction and the addition of divine endings in *De occulta philosophia* III.25; his table includes Mihael among the 72 angels. See [Agrippa’s text and table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm).

The biblical verses can be inspected directly at [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?lang=bi).

## 2. Development of the system

### Rabbinic and early kabbalistic background

**[Documented tradition]** Rabbinic sources know a “name of seventy-two letters,” but they do not all present the later columnar angel list. The identification of the 72-letter Name with Exodus 14:19–21 developed within Jewish exegetical and mystical traditions.

The *Sefer ha-Bahir*, probably composed or redacted in twelfth-century Provence, describes the three groups of 72 and their directional arrangement. Section 110 is accessible in [translation](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-109-110-targum/).

Ibn Ezra records and questions an interpretation connecting the 72-letter Name directly to these verses; see his [commentary on Exodus 14:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Ibn_Ezra_on_Exodus.14.19.2).

### *Sefer Raziel*

**[Tradition]** *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is a medieval Hebrew compilation of cosmological, angelological, talismanic, and magical material. It became an important carrier of divine-name and angelic traditions, though it was neither a single ancient revelation nor necessarily the point at which the Exodus construction originated.

The work circulated in manuscript and was first printed at Amsterdam in 1701. A general account is available through the [Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/book-of-raziel); an older English discussion and extracts appear at [Sacred Texts](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms19.htm).

### Reuchlin and Agrippa

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped transmit the names into Christian Cabala. Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia*, printed in its complete form in 1533, gave the construction, angelic endings, and list a widely accessible Latin presentation.

**[Documented historical development]** These works concern the 72 angels. They do not pair Mihael with Haagenti.

### Athanasius Kircher

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) presented the 72 names in elaborate diagrams and connected them with angels, nations, languages, and cosmic divisions. His treatment is an influential Christian-Cabalistic overlay, not evidence that the list originated in ancient Egypt.

See the [Heidelberg facsimile](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/kircher1653bd2_1), the [72-name diagram](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg), and Peterson’s [Kircher transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm).

### Rudd/Harley 6483

Harley 6483 places one of the 72 angelic names against each of the 72 Goetic spirits. The pairing is ordinal:

> Spirit 48, Haagenti → angel 48, Mihael.

This is the earliest source in the present chain that securely joins the two.

### Lenain

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* (Amiens, 1823) supplied a systematic angelic calendar, five-degree zodiacal divisions, psalm verses, invocation times, qualities, and contrary genii. Lenain is a major source for the Mihael data now repeated in angel dictionaries.

### Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn incorporated the 72 names into a synthesis of:

- zodiacal quinances;
- decans;
- planets;
- Hebrew divine names;
- psalms;
- tarot pips;
- color scales;
- Egyptian and Enochian material.

The Golden Dawn’s synthesis does not erase the fact that the Rudd pairing and the later Goetic decan scheme are different constructions.

### Papus

Papus, Gérard Encausse, did not originate Lenain’s system. He promoted it through the French occult revival and contributed to the 1909 reissue of *La science cabalistique*. The [Bibliothèque nationale de France record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30789329z) documents that edition.

### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) further systematized the angels with choirs, archangels, sephiroth, moral qualities, invocations, and practical ritual material. His synthesis is twentieth-century ritual Cabala, not a transcription of Rudd or *Sefer Raziel*. Bibliographic records are available at [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/La_Kabbale_pratique.html?id=jF8ntAEACAAJ) and [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique).

### Haziel

The writer Haziel—generally identified as François Bernad-Termès—popularized the 72 guardian angels in late twentieth-century French devotional occultism. His *Notre Ange Gardien existe* appeared in 1995. Haziel’s personal guardian dates and extensive psychological promises are modern developments of the Lenain/Ambelain current. See the [publication record](https://www.fnac.com/a178461/Haziel-Notre-Ange-Gardien-existe) and [Open Library entry](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL10002479W/Notre_ange_gardien_existe).

## 3. Mihael’s name and meaning

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Triplet | מיה, MIH |
| Expanded angelic form | מיהאל, Mihael |
| Number | 48 |
| Lenain’s attribute | **Dieu père secourable** |
| Literal English rendering of Lenain | “God, the helpful father” or “God the succoring father” |
| Common modern rendering | “God the generous/helpful father” |
| Frequently claimed meaning | “Who is like God?” |
| Assessment of last claim | Conflation with the archangel Michael |

**[Disputed]** Mihael is not orthographically identical with Michael:

- Mihael: מיהאל, approximately MIH-EL.
- Michael: מיכאל, MIKh-EL.

“Who is like God?” belongs to the traditional interpretation of **Michael**, from Hebrew *mi ka-El*. Applying it to Mihael ignores the missing *kaf* consonant. Lenain’s “God the helpful father” is a devotional attribute, not a strict linguistic translation of the three-letter group.

## 4. Zodiacal degrees and calendar

Lenain assigns Mihael to rays/degrees **236 through 240** of the zodiac. Counting the zodiac from 0° Aries, these correspond to:

> **25°00′–30°00′ Scorpio**

That is the second quinance of Scorpio’s third decan.

| System | Mihael assignment |
|---|---|
| Zodiac | Scorpio |
| Five-degree sector | 25°–30° Scorpio |
| Ten-degree decan containing it | 20°–30° Scorpio |
| Decan planet | Venus |
| Golden Dawn tarot pip for decan | Seven of Cups |
| Traditional daily invocation interval | 3:40–4:00 p.m. |
| Haziel guardian dates | Approximately 18–22 November |
| Lenain’s secondary days | 6 May, 17 July, 27 September, 8 December, 18 February |

The familiar 18–22 November birthday range is a modern civil-calendar rendering of a zodiacal quinance and is particularly associated with twentieth-century guardian-angel literature. It should not be projected backward into Harley 6483.

## 5. Psalm verse

Lenain gives:

> **Notum fecit Dominus salutare suum: in conspectu gentium revelavit justitiam suam.**

Translation:

> The Lord has made known his salvation; in the sight of the nations he has revealed his righteousness.

This is:

- **Psalm 97:2** in the Vulgate numbering;
- **Psalm 98:2** in most modern Hebrew and Protestant numbering.

The Skinner–Rankine transcription of the Rudd material prints a slightly different second-person form—*salutare tuum* and *justitiam tuam*—and labels it in a way that can appear as Psalm 97:3. The discrepancy is best explained by divergent verse-counting, scribal or editorial variation, or both. Lenain’s form agrees with the ordinary Vulgate text.

## 6. Mihael’s offices

Lenain attributes to Mihael:

- peace and union between spouses;
- protection for those who call upon him;
- premonitions and secret inspirations concerning future events;
- influence over generation or procreation;
- friendship;
- conjugal fidelity.

The “contrary genius” is associated with:

- luxury or sensual excess;
- sterility;
- inconstancy;
- discord between spouses;
- jealousy;
- anxiety or disquiet.

These are **Mihael’s** Lenainic moral and astrological offices. They are not offices of Haagenti in Weyer or the Goetia.

Haziel and later guardian-angel writers expand these themes into fertility, conception, harmonious sexuality, marriage counseling, intuition, and clairvoyance. Those expansions are **[modern devotional tradition]**.

## 7. How the Rudd scheme constrains Haagenti

In the Harley 6483 ritual construction:

1. Haagenti retains the ordinary Goetic seal and description.
2. Mihael is assigned because both occupy position 48.
3. The angelic name and appropriate divine text are placed on or associated with the lamen and ritual apparatus.
4. The angel is invoked as the superior, lawful force under divine authority.
5. The spirit is therefore not approached as an autonomous ally but is compelled within a hierarchy of divine, angelic, and demonic names.

Skinner and Rankine describe the Shem angel as a counterbalancing or “thwarting” angel. That wording is their modern explanatory frame; the manuscript’s ritual logic is hierarchical constraint rather than a narrative in which Mihael and Haagenti are mythological enemies.

The attribution of the material to **Dr Thomas Rudd** requires qualification:

- Harley 6483 is not an autograph manuscript by Rudd.
- It was copied or organized in 1712–1713 by Peter Smart from material identified as “Dr Rudd’s sheets.”
- Rudd died in 1656.
- The manuscript may preserve material connected with his circle, but the exact extent of his authorship is uncertain.

Skinner and Rankine’s *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* was first published by Golden Hoard in 2007 and later reprinted by Llewellyn. Its importance lies in publishing the full angel–demon apparatus, not in proving that every page was personally composed by Rudd.

## Haagenti in *777*

Crowley’s *777* groups Goetic spirits by zodiac sign, by decan, and by a day/night polarity. In the Cancer sequence Haagenti appears as:

- number 48;
- president, hence Mercury;
- bull with griffin’s wings;
- nocturnal spirit;
- attached to the cadent or third decan of Cancer.

That produces:

> **20°–30° Cancer — Moon in Cancer — Four of Cups**

The corresponding daytime spirit on the same decan is **Sitri**, number 12. Thus each tarot pip may carry two spirits in this later system: one day spirit and one night spirit.

Strictly speaking, *777* gives Haagenti the whole ten-degree decan rather than printing an exclusive five-degree quinance. Some later tabulations split the decan into day/night quinances, but that refinement should not be silently read into the original table.

A scan of *777* is available [here](https://maestrosdelsaber.com/wp-content/uploads/ftp-files/Magia/a%20Crowley,%20Aleister/Mag%20Crowley,%20Aleister%20-%20777%20Tables.pdf).

## Why Haagenti and Mihael have different signs

The apparent contradiction is historical:

- Rudd pairs demon 48 with angel 48 numerically.
- Lenain places angel 48 in zodiacal order at 25°–30° Scorpio.
- The Golden Dawn/Crowley Goetic table distributes demons through decans by a separate pattern and places Haagenti at 20°–30° Cancer.
- Combining the systems creates an angel and demon with different zodiacal sectors.

There is no early document explaining these as two sides of the same astrological degree.

## Astrological timing in practice

Four different approaches coexist:

1. **Early grimoire rule:** use the permissible hours for a president.
2. **Planetary-rank method:** choose Wednesday or an hour of Mercury.
3. **Golden Dawn/*777* method:** work when the third decan of Cancer is emphasized, especially its nocturnal attribution.
4. **Rudd–Lenain angelic method:** invoke Mihael through 25°–30° Scorpio and the 3:40–4:00 p.m. interval.

These are accumulated layers. No single manuscript requires all four simultaneously.

## Enochian overlay

Crowley’s 1904 volume includes an Enochian or “Angelic” translation of the conjurations. This places Enochian language around the ritual but does not give Haagenti a distinct Enochian name.

Later Golden Dawn-derived systems connect zodiacal decans to Enochian tablets, subangles, seniors, kerubic letters, or elemental squares. Those are **[later esoteric correspondences]**. No primary source was located that identifies Haagenti with a unique being in Dee and Kelley’s surviving spirit diaries.

# Iconography

## 1. Textual image

The stable early image is:

> bull + griffin wings → human form at command.

Weyer says “bull”; Scot says “great bull”; the Goetia says “mighty bull.” The change to human form becomes progressively more ritualized:

- Weyer: Haagenti assumes a human face.
- Scot: he takes human shape.
- Goetia: he does so at the exorcist’s command.

No source describes the human form’s sex, clothing, age, complexion, weapons, crown, posture, or facial expression.

## 2. The sigil as iconography

The seal is the only individualized visual object supplied by the Goetia manuscripts. It is not found in Weyer or Scot and should not be represented as an ancient symbol.

Its visible history is:

1. freehand manuscript sigils in the Lemegeton witnesses;
2. inclusion in Harley 6483’s angelically controlled ritual apparatus;
3. regularization in Mathers–Crowley’s 1904 engraving;
4. widespread reproduction in occult handbooks, jewelry, tattoos, vector graphics, and commercial devotional objects.

## 3. Collin de Plancy

The 1863 sixth edition of Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* says:

> **Haagenti, grand président aux enfers. Il paraît sous la figure d’un taureau avec des ailes de griffon. Lorsqu’il se montre portant face humaine, il rend l’homme habile à toutes choses ; il enseigne en perfection l’art de transmuer tous les métaux en or, et de faire d’excellent vin avec de l’eau claire. Il commande trente-trois légions.**

Translation:

> Haagenti, a great president in hell. He appears as a bull with griffin wings. When he shows himself with a human face, he makes a person skillful in all things; he teaches perfectly the art of transmuting metals into gold and of making excellent wine from clear water. He commands thirty-three legions.

—Collin de Plancy, *Dictionnaire infernal*, 6th ed. (1863), p. 316; [French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Haagenti), [page image](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf/324).

De Plancy subtly alters the tradition:

- the human shape becomes specifically a **human face** again;
- general instruction becomes mastery of alchemical transmutation;
- the two-way water/wine exchange becomes the more positive feat of making **excellent wine from clear water**.

### Was Haagenti in the 1818 edition?

**[Negative finding]** Haagenti was not located in the searchable transcription of the 1818 first edition. A secure article is present by the 1825 edition and in 1863. Until a physical first-edition witness is produced, “de Plancy 1818” should denote the work’s original publication date, not a securely verified date for the Haagenti article.

### Is there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

The 1863 edition famously contains Louis Le Breton’s demon portraits, but the Haagenti article on p. 316 has no adjacent individual engraving. No securely captioned Le Breton portrait of Haagenti was located.

Therefore:

> **There is a de Plancy description of Haagenti, but no confirmed Louis Le Breton Haagenti portrait.**

Modern images advertised as “the Dictionnaire Infernal portrait of Haagenti” should be checked for an original page citation. Many are modern winged-bull illustrations modeled on the verbal description.

## 4. Modern visual conventions

Modern artists commonly add features absent from the grimoires:

- gold skin, horns, jewelry, coins, or molten metal;
- alchemical vessels;
- wine cups;
- Egyptian dress;
- feline or Bastet-like anatomy;
- a female body;
- crowns or presidential insignia;
- mechanical or armored bull forms.

Only the bull, griffin wings, human transformation, transmutation, and wine/water imagery are anchored in the primary description. Everything else is adaptation unless separately sourced.

# People and works

## 1. Historical transmitters and editors

### Johann Weyer

Weyer was a physician and critic of witch prosecutions, not simply a celebrant of demon magic. His publication preserved the earliest exact Haagenti text while framing the catalog as evidence of demonic imposture. He says he deliberately omitted material that might make the ritual operational.

### Reginald Scot

Scot reproduced the catalog in a skeptical work arguing against witchcraft prosecutions and credulity. His English phrasing—especially “wise in everie thing”—became influential in Anglophone occultism.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Rudd’s name became attached to a version that controls each spirit with a Shem angel. Peter Smart’s early-eighteenth-century copying is better documented than Rudd’s authorship of every element.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers translated and arranged the Lemegeton material, supplied classified lists and Hebrew-style spellings, and gave the text the form through which much twentieth-century ceremonial magic encountered it.

### Aleister Crowley

Crowley edited and published Mathers’s work in 1904. His psychological interpretation, Enochian conjurations, and later *777* tables connected the spirit catalog with Golden Dawn correspondences.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition restored manuscript readings and documented variants. His online editions of Weyer, Scot, and the Goetia make direct comparison possible.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s angelic counterweights and psalms widely accessible, while arguing for the practical and protective importance of the Shem pairing.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* presents a modern ceremonial system combining Goetic spirits, Shem angels, directional kings, astrology, and mirror or “dark theater” techniques. His tables pair Haagenti with Mihael and place the pair in the Scorpio-based Shem sequence.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

In *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*, DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt popularized the Golden Dawn/Crowley decan system. Their table places Haagenti at 20°–30° Cancer, at night, with the Four of Cups and Mercury-based presidential correspondences.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent emphasized the relationship between early spirit-office catalogs and later Goetia. He proposed that Haagenti and Zagan may be an especially visible duplication: both are winged bulls, become human, confer wisdom, and perform nearly identical transmutations; Weyer even defines Haagenti’s legions by reference to Zagan.

This is an interpretive stemmatic hypothesis, not a manuscript statement that they are one being.

### Frater Achad

No Haagenti-specific operation, essay, or doctrine by Frater Achad was located. His Qabalistic writings influenced later magical number symbolism generally, but it would be misleading to list him as a documented Haagenti practitioner.

### Owen Davies, Jean-Patrice Boudet, Julien Véronèse

- Owen Davies provides the broader social history of grimoires and the *Lemegeton*’s later reception.
- Jean-Patrice Boudet provides the most relevant scholarly comparison between Weyer’s catalog and the *Livre des esperitz*.
- Julien Véronèse is important for medieval ritual-magic scholarship, especially the *Ars notoria* and related learned magic, but no Haagenti-specific study by him was located. The frequently implied attribution of the *Livre des esperitz* comparison to Véronèse should be corrected to Boudet.

## 2. Grimoire ancestors

### *Testament of Solomon*

The Greek *Testament of Solomon*, a late-antique work surviving in later manuscripts, provides the foundational literary picture of Solomon interrogating and binding demons. It includes figures such as Beelzeboul and Asmodeus and associates demons with bodily, cosmic, or magical functions.

**[Historical relationship]** It is an ancestor of the broader Solomonic literary imagination.

**[Negative finding]** It contains no securely identifiable Haagenti.

### Michael Scot’s *Liber introductorius*

Michael Scot’s thirteenth-century astrological writing includes named spirits and helped establish learned demonological catalogs. No Haagenti or Bugan entry has been identified there.

### Munich handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich manual published by Richard Kieckhefer preserves ritual magic involving spirit lists, circles, and conjurations. It demonstrates the environment from which later office catalogs emerged but contains no securely recognizable Haagenti.

### Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 89 sup. 38

This 1494 witness preserves another partial spirit catalog related to the office tradition. It is relevant to the stemma but does not securely attest Haagenti by name.

### *Liber officiorum spirituum*

Weyer refers to a *Liber officiorum spirituum* or a book called *Empto Salomonis*. Trithemius mentioned a *Liber officiorum* in his *Antipalus maleficiorum* around 1508. The title suggests a catalog organized by spirits’ offices—the exact structural type seen in Weyer.

The immediate exemplar used by Weyer is lost.

### *Livre des esperitz*

This is the closest surviving parallel because of Bugan’s offices. Boudet concludes that the French list and Weyer share a textual family, not that Weyer simply translated the surviving French manuscript.

### *Book of Abramelin*

The *Abramelin* tradition contains extensive demon-name lists and hierarchical magic. No securely demonstrated Haagenti appears in the principal lists. Superficial resemblance to a damaged or translated name is insufficient to establish identity.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is Haagenti really older than Weyer?

The answer depends on what counts as an attestation.

- **Exact name:** no secure witness before Weyer, 1577.
- **Same offices under another name:** Bugan in the *Livre des esperitz*.
- **Common lost source:** likely, according to Boudet’s comparative reconstruction.
- **Ancient demon or deity:** no evidence.

It is therefore too strong to say “Haagenti is a medieval demon” without qualification. A safer formulation is:

> Haagenti is a Renaissance-attested spirit whose catalog entry probably descends from a late medieval or early Renaissance spirit-office tradition.

## 2. Haagenti and Zagan: two spirits or a duplicate?

Weyer’s descriptions are strikingly close.

### Zagan

Zagan is likewise a bull with griffin wings who assumes human form, makes fools wise, turns wine into water and blood into wine or oil, and changes metals or coins. He commands 33 legions.

### Haagenti

Haagenti is a bull with griffin wings who assumes human form, makes a person ingenious or wise, turns metals into gold, and exchanges water and wine. Instead of restating the number of legions, Weyer says that Haagenti commands as many as Zagan.

Possible explanations include:

1. **Separate spirits with overlapping offices.**
2. **A copyist’s duplication or doublet.**
3. **Two descendants of one damaged source entry.**
4. **A deliberate pair differentiated by small alchemical details.**

Jake Stratton-Kent treats this as one of the catalog’s clearest probable duplications. The hypothesis is persuasive but unproved: all principal witnesses still list Zagan and Haagenti separately, and the Goetia assigns them distinct numbers and sigils.

## 3. Why 69 spirits become 72

Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia* contains 69 entries. The Goetia has 72.

The Goetia adds four spirits not present in Weyer:

1. **Vassago**, Goetia 3;
2. **Seere**, Goetia 70;
3. **Dantalion**, Goetia 71;
4. **Andromalius**, Goetia 72.

It omits Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**, Weyer number 4.

Thus:

> 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

The rest are extensively reordered. Haagenti moves from Weyer 67 to Goetia 48; Zagan moves from Weyer 48 to Goetia 61.

**[Scholarly inference]** The final total of 72 probably encouraged or reflected comparison with the 72 Shem angels. However, no surviving compiler’s note explicitly says, “We altered Weyer to create a demonic counterpart to the angels.”

Weyer himself already mentions 72 subordinate kings in the Belial entry, so the number was symbolically active before the final Goetia list.

## 4. Was Scot’s Haagenti number 67?

Not in Scot’s original typography. Scot did not number these entries. Because he omitted Pruflas, Haagenti is the 66th surviving item in his sequence. Modern “[67]” labels align Scot with Weyer and identify the corresponding source entry; they should not be cited as Scot’s own numbering.

## 5. What does the name mean?

No convincing answer is known.

### Unsupported or weak proposals

- **Bastet:** found in modern Satanist/demonolatry systems; contradicted by the early bull imagery and unsupported by linguistic evidence.
- **Ra/Amenti or Osiris:** based on visual or phonetic association rather than a textual chain.
- **Greek *hagios*:** no explanation for the complete form or early spelling.
- **Latin *argentum*:** semantically attractive because of metalwork, but linguistically implausible.
- **Hittite deity:** no matching name and no transmission pathway.
- **Germanic personal name:** no documented medieval form linked to the catalog.

The correct evidentiary label is **[disputed modern speculation]**, not etymology.

## 6. Is Haagenti Bastet?

No early source supports this.

The identification occurs in modern demonolatry, particularly systems that redistribute Goetic figures among reconstructed pagan deities. Bastet is historically an Egyptian feline goddess; Haagenti appears as a bull with griffin wings. The proposed identity has neither iconographic continuity nor a name-chain.

It may be accepted as a private theological or esoteric correspondence, but it is a **modern invention**, not a historical recovery.

## 7. Is Haagenti male, female, or genderless?

The early Latin and English texts use grammatically masculine language. The transformed figure is merely “human”; no sexual characteristics are supplied.

Female portrayals in *In Nomine*, D&D’s “Mother of Minotaurs,” and some modern occult art are creative adaptations. They do not falsify the fiction, but they are not source descriptions.

## 8. Does Haagenti literally manufacture alcoholic wine?

Weyer and the Goetia describe a magical exchange between water and wine. De Plancy emphasizes making excellent wine from clear water. The language belongs to the wonder-working and alchemical office.

Interpretations that connect it directly with the wedding at Cana, Christian parody, fermentation science, altered consciousness, or spiritual “transmutation” are later readings. None is explained in the primary entry.

## 9. Is the transmutation alchemical or metaphorical?

In context, the office is written as material transformation:

- metals into gold;
- water into wine;
- wine into water.

The wisdom office could invite an initiatory interpretation, but Weyer does not say that “gold” means spiritual perfection or that “wine” means ecstasy. Those are later occult allegories.

## 10. Did Rudd invent the angel pairing?

Harley 6483 is the earliest secure witness considered here, but “Rudd invented it” exceeds the evidence. The material could derive from:

- Rudd;
- his circle;
- Peter Smart’s compilation;
- an earlier lost set of tables;
- a combination of pre-existing Shem and Goetia materials.

The manuscript attribution supports “Ruddian scheme,” not certain single authorship.

## 11. Is Mihael the same as Michael?

No, not philologically. They are related only in their shared *-El* ending and visual similarity in Latin letters. The common translation “Who is like God?” belongs to Michael and was later transferred to Mihael.

## 12. Which tarot card belongs to the pair?

There are two answers because there are two systems:

- **Haagenti in Crowley/*777*:** Four of Cups, 20°–30° Cancer.
- **Mihael in the Lenain/Golden Dawn quinance:** Seven of Cups, 25°–30° Scorpio.

Calling either the sole “Haagenti–Mihael card” conceals the historical mismatch.

## 13. Modern occult accretions

The following claims were not located in Weyer, Scot, Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825, Harley 6483, Mathers–Crowley, Peterson, or de Plancy:

- a traditional enn such as “Haaventi on ca Lirach”;
- cats as Haagenti’s sacred animals;
- lavender, cinnamon, particular crystals, or modern candle colors;
- a fallen-cherub rank;
- female identity;
- dream interpretation;
- healing anxiety;
- jealousy as Haagenti’s office;
- ancient Egyptian priesthood;
- initiation into “solar gold consciousness.”

Some may be meaningful within living demonolatry, but their correct label is **[modern ritual invention]**. Jealousy and marital discord do occur in the literature around Mihael’s contrary genius, which may explain some cross-contamination.

# Open questions

1. **What was Weyer’s immediate manuscript?**  
   His source, described as a *Liber officiorum spirituum* or *Empto Salomonis*, is lost or unidentified.

2. **How exactly are Bugan and Haagenti related?**  
   Their offices strongly support common ancestry, but no intermediate manuscript explains the name change, title change, appearance, or reduction from 34 to 33 legions.

3. **Is Haagenti a duplication of Zagan?**  
   The parallels and explicit legion cross-reference make this plausible, but distinct names and seals were firmly established by the Goetia recension.

4. **Why was Haagenti moved from 67 to 48?**  
   The Goetia’s overall reordering has no surviving explanation. Numerical alignment with Mihael is a later consequence and may or may not have motivated the placement.

5. **Who designed the seal?**  
   It does not occur in Weyer or Scot. Its first author and the meaning, if any, of its strokes are unknown.

6. **Can a stemma of the seal variants be established?**  
   High-resolution comparison of every Lemegeton witness might distinguish copying relationships from ordinary pen variation.

7. **How much of Harley 6483 is Thomas Rudd’s?**  
   Peter Smart’s role is secure; Rudd’s exact authorial contribution remains unresolved.

8. **When did the Haagenti article enter de Plancy’s dictionary?**  
   It is secure by 1825 and appears in 1863. It was not found in the searchable 1818 transcription, but examination of all physical first-edition states would settle the point.

9. **Did Louis Le Breton ever draw Haagenti?**  
   No securely captioned engraving has been found. A page-level discovery would be needed to overturn the negative finding.

10. **What is the true etymology?**  
    At present there is no demonstrable answer. A newly identified *Liber officiorum* witness may eventually provide an earlier or less corrupt name.

# Conclusions

**[Documented core]** Haagenti first appears by that exact name in Weyer’s 1577 catalog as a great president: a bull with griffin wings who takes human form, makes people ingenious, turns metals into gold, exchanges water and wine, and commands as many legions as Zagan. Scot translated the entry in 1584. The Goetia moved Haagenti from 67 to 48, made the bull “mighty,” placed the transformation under the exorcist’s command, made the 33 legions explicit, and supplied a seal.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Bugan of the *Livre des esperitz* is the strongest candidate for Haagenti’s textual ancestor. Haagenti may also be a doublet of Zagan. Neither proposition supplies an etymology.

**[Ruddian tradition]** Harley 6483 pairs Haagenti with angel 48, Mihael, and uses the angelic name and psalm within a hierarchy intended to constrain the demon.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Mihael occupies 25°–30° Scorpio and falls under the Seven of Cups. Crowley’s *777*, through a separate arrangement, places Haagenti at night in 20°–30° Cancer under the Four of Cups. Haagenti’s rank supplies Mercury and quicksilver.

**[Negative finding]** There is no demonstrated ancient god, hero, or place behind the name; no secure pre-Weyer spelling *Haagenti*; no confirmed Louis Le Breton portrait; and no early support for Bastet, Egyptian, feline, female, fallen-cherub, or modern “enn” traditions.

**[Modern reception]** Later games and fiction emphasize different parts of the compact description: the bull in D&D and *Bloodstained*, alchemy in Pathfinder, appetite in *In Nomine*, and both the number 48 and alchemically forged metal in Gundam Haagenti.

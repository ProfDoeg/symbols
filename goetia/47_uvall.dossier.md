# Uvall: Research Dossier

*Research date: 14 September 2026.*

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Documented text]** — directly present in a manuscript, early printed edition, or reliable transcription.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a historical or philological inference offered by an editor or scholar.
- **[Tradition]** — a teaching transmitted within ceremonial-magic or Christian-kabbalistic practice, without implying historical or supernatural proof.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an attribution imposed by a later occult system.
- **[Disputed]** — a reading or identification for which the witnesses or interpreters conflict.
- **[Legend]** — a narrative presented by the source as traditional history, not independently established.
- **[Modern invention]** — an attribute, name, image, ritual, or fictional use not traceable to the early source chain.

The name is spelled **Vual** in Weyer’s 1577 Latin text. “Uvall” became prominent through Mathers and Crowley’s 1904 edition. No sound philological derivation from an Egyptian, Hebrew, Greek, Roman, or ancient Near Eastern god has been demonstrated.

### Why Vual, Vuall, Uvall, and Wal can be the same name

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** In sixteenth- and seventeenth-century Latin and English hands, **U** and **V** were frequently graphic rather than phonemic alternatives. Initial *Vual-* could consequently be copied or printed as *Uual-*, *Vuall-*, *Uvall-*, or *Wal-*. Minims and flourished capitals also made **V/N/R** confusions easy. Skinner and Rankine therefore regard the Harley reading resembling **Nuall** as a scribal error, while accepting *Vuall/Uvall* as equivalent forms.

**[Disputed]** This does not establish one historical pronunciation. Modern pronunciations such as “VOO-al,” “OO-val,” “Vwah-l,” and “Ewe-val” are practice conventions, not evidence from Weyer or the manuscripts.

### Etymology

**[Finding]** No ancient deity, hero, city, ethnonym, or securely attested lexical root has been shown to lie behind **Vual**.

This sharply distinguishes Uvall from names in the same catalogue that admit plausible historical derivations:

- **Baal** derives from Northwest Semitic *baʿal*, “lord/master,” and also became a divine title.
- **Astaroth** is historically connected with Astarte/Ashtoreth.
- **Asmoday/Asmodeus** is connected with Iranian *Aēšma daēva* through Jewish Ashmedai.
- **Belial** derives from Hebrew *bĕliyyaʿal*, conventionally “worthlessness” or “wickedness.”
- **Phoenix** is the Greek and Latin name of the mythical bird.
- *Goēteia* is Greek γοητεία, magic or sorcery associated with the *goēs*, an enchanter or lamenting ritual specialist.

No equivalently documented chain exists for Vual.

Claims encountered in modern demonological pages include derivations from:

- Latin *vallum*, “wall” or “rampart”;
- Latin *volo*, “I wish”;
- an alleged Hebrew word for wailing or silence;
- an alleged Coptic word for guardian or enclosure;
- the Egyptian god Thoth;
- Baal through an intermediate **Vaal**.

These are **[Modern invention/disputed]**. None was supported by an ancient citation or historical linguistic argument. In particular, a spirit’s alleged Egyptian speech is not evidence that its name or figure derives from an Egyptian god.

### The “Vaal” problem

The fifteenth- or sixteenth-century French *Livre des Esperitz*, Cambridge Trinity College MS O.8.29, includes a spirit named **Vaal**. This has produced two competing identifications:

1. **Skinner and Rankine’s reconstruction:** *Vaal* may be an early form of **Vual**.
2. **Peterson/Boudet-style concordance:** Trinity **Vaal** corresponds by position and description to **Balam**, not Uvall. Peterson places Uvall’s possible relatives instead at Folger **Ryall** and Additional 36674 **Vual**.

The second has the stronger item-by-item contextual basis. Therefore:

- **[Documented text]** *Vaal* is pre-Weyer.
- **[Disputed]** It is not securely the same spirit as Vual.
- **[Finding]** The earliest unambiguous printed attestation of the complete Vual identity—name, dromedary, Egyptian speech, love, divination, reconciliation, Powers, and 37 legions—is Weyer in 1577.
- **[Open possibility]** An earlier *Offices of Spirits* manuscript used by Weyer must have existed, but its wording and exact spelling are lost.

## The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, 1577

**[Documented text]** Johann Weyer inserted the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. It is not in the original 1563 edition. Vual is §66, on printed p. 930 of the 1577 volume.

### Latin, verbatim

> **Vual Dux magnus & fortis: conspicitur ut dromedarius magnus ac terribilis: at in humana forma linguam sonat Ægyptiacam graviter. Hic præ cæteris amorem maximè mulierum conciliat: inde novit præsentia, præterita & futura: confert & gratiam amicorum atque inimicorum. De ordine fuit potestatum. Triginta septem legiones gubernat.**

Source: Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, 5th ed., Basel, 1577, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* §66, p. 930; transcribed in Peterson’s [digital Weyer edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Literal English gloss

> Vual, a great and strong duke, is seen as a great and terrible dromedary; but in human form he speaks the Egyptian language gravely/in a deep voice. Above others he especially procures the love of women; furthermore, he knows present, past, and future things, and grants the favor of friends and enemies. He was of the order of Powers. He governs thirty-seven legions.

The crucial word is **graviter**: gravely, weightily, or in a deep voice. Nothing in the Latin says that his Egyptian is defective.

### What Weyer did and did not supply

Weyer provides:

- name;
- rank;
- appearance;
- human form;
- voice and language;
- three offices;
- former angelic order;
- legion count;
- rank-based hours.

He does **not** supply an individual seal for Vual.

Weyer says his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also called a book “Empto. Salomonis.” Peterson notes that Trithemius listed a *Liber officiorum spirituum* in his 1508 *Antipalus maleficiorum*. Whether Weyer’s puzzling *Empto* is a title, an abbreviation, or a corruption remains disputed. Weyer also says that he omitted material so that the operation could not readily be used. See Peterson’s [introduction and comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

## 2. Scot, 1584

Reginald Scot printed an English rendering in *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter 2. The Vual paragraph occurs on p. 225 of the 1584 pagination, immediately before the page marker for p. 226.

### English, verbatim

> **Vuall is a great duke and a strong, he is seene as a great and terrible dromedarie, but in humane forme, he soundeth out in a base voice the Ægyptian toong. This man above all other procureth the especiall love of women, and knoweth things present, past, and to come, procuring the love of freends and foes, he was of the order of potestats, and governeth thirtie seven legions.**

Source: Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London, 1584, Book XV, ch. 2, p. 225; Peterson’s [transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

Here **base voice** means a low or deep voice, not an inferior one. Scot therefore correctly reflects *graviter*.

Scot omits Weyer’s Pruflas/Bufas paragraph. As a result, Vual is:

- conventionally cited as Weyer/Scot **66**, because modern concordances retain Weyer’s identification;
- only the **65th surviving demon entry** if Scot’s paragraphs are recounted mechanically.

The text itself does not number every demon.

## 3. The seventeenth-century Goetia manuscripts

### Sloane MS 3825

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3825, fol. 107r:

> **The 47th spirit is called Vual. he is a great & mighty strong duke, he appeareth in ye forme of a mighty dromedary at first, but after a while he putteth on humane shape, and speaketh in ye Egyptian Tongue, but not perfectly; his office is to procure ye love of women, and to tell Things past, present and to come, and also to procure friendship betweene friends & foes, he was of the order of Potestates; he governeth 37 Legions of spirits; his seal is Thus to be made and worne as a Lamin before you &c.**

Source: [Peterson’s Sloane 3825 transcription, entry 47](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

This version changes the transmission in several ways:

- Weyer’s no. 66 becomes no. 47.
- “Knows” becomes “to tell.”
- Weyer’s favor or grace becomes “friendship.”
- “Great and terrible dromedary” becomes “mighty dromedary.”
- A sequential first and second appearance is made explicit.
- An individual seal and lamen instruction are added.
- Most significantly, *graviter* becomes “but not perfectly.”

### Sloane MS 2731

Sloane 2731 reads **Vuall**, with **Vual** as a variant. Its substance agrees with Sloane 3825: duke, dromedary, human shape, imperfect Egyptian, love of women, temporal divination, friendship, Powers, and 37 legions.

### Harley MS 6483

Harley 6483 gives **Vuall/Uvall**. Its demon description remains in the Goetia family, but the manuscript’s importance is its additional angelic apparatus: the demon’s seal is incorporated into a double lamen with Shem angel 47.

### Peterson, 2001

Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* (York Beach: Weiser, 2001), entry 47, p. 46, uses **Vual** as the principal reading and follows Sloane 3825 while collating Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley material, Crowley, and related witnesses. His most important editorial finding is that “but not perfectly” is a mistranslation of Weyer’s “in a deep voice.” The continuously revised online text is the [Esoteric Archives Goetia](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

## 4. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

Printed p. 27; PDF image p. 39:

> **(47.) UVALL, VUAL, or VOVAL.—The Forty-Seventh Spirit is Uvall, or Vual, or Voval. He is a Duke, Great, Mighty, and Strong; and appeareth in the form of a Mighty Dromedary at the first, but after a while at the Command of the Exorcist he putteth on Human Shape, and speaketh the Egyptian Tongue, but not perfectly. His Office is to Procure the Love of Women, and to tell Things Past, Present, and to Come. He also procureth Friendship between Friends and Foes. He was in the Order of Potestates or Powers. He governeth 37 Legions of Spirits, and his Seal is this, to be made and worn before thee, etc.**

Source: S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, Foyers, 1904, p. 27; [facsimile PDF](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf).

Crowley adds a facetious editorial footnote:

> “He can nowadays converse in sound though colloquial Coptic.—ED.”

This is **[Modern invention/editorial humor]**, not manuscript evidence. Coptic is the late form of the Egyptian language, but no early source says “Coptic.”

### What Uvall does not do

The early sources do **not** assign Uvall:

- teaching languages or sciences generally;
- finding treasure;
- supplying familiars;
- invisibility;
- metallic transmutation;
- changing wine and water;
- healing;
- military destruction;
- “time manipulation.”

Those are offices belonging to other Goetia spirits or modern expansions. Uvall’s traditional offices are limited to love, temporal knowledge, and favorable relations/reconciliation.

## Number and order: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

**[Documented text]** Weyer has 69 demon descriptions. The Goetia has 72.

The Goetia includes four spirits absent from Weyer:

1. **Vassago**, Goetia 3;
2. **Seere**, 70;
3. **Dantalion**, 71;
4. **Andromalius**, 72.

It omits one spirit found in Weyer:

- **Pruflas**, alias **Bufas**, Weyer 4.

Thus 68 of Weyer’s spirits survive, and 68 + 4 = 72.

Scot either accidentally omitted Pruflas or used a copy already lacking it. Because the Goetia also lacks Pruflas, Peterson treats Scot’s defective text or a related witness as a useful clue to the Goetia’s compilation.

Uvall moves from Weyer **66** to Goetia **47**. Peterson remarks that much of the reordered list looks as though pages or individual spirit leaves were shuffled. There is no surviving authorial explanation for the new order.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The total of 72 invited alignment with:

- Solomon’s legendary 72 spirits in the brass vessel;
- the 72-fold Divine Name;
- the 72 Shem angels;
- 72 quinances of five degrees each.

But the surviving early Goetia manuscripts do not explain their 72-order as an original ancient angel-demon pairing. Rudd’s system supplies that pairing explicitly and may be a subsequent synthesis.

## Rank, planet, metal, and timing

### Rank

Uvall is consistently a **Duke**. Weyer’s *dux* and Scot’s “duke” are unambiguous.

### Copper and Venus

The concluding observations in Mathers–Crowley assign:

- kings: gold;
- dukes: **copper**;
- marquises: silver;
- princes/prelates: tin;
- knights: lead;
- presidents: mercury;
- earls: copper and silver in equal proportion.

These are the standard metals of Sun, Venus, Moon, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury, and a mixed Venus–Moon status. Therefore Uvall’s rank corresponds to:

- **Metal:** copper — **[Documented text]**
- **Planet:** Venus — **[Esoteric correspondence derived from the planetary metals]**
- **Day:** Friday — later planetary-hour deduction
- **Color:** green — Golden Dawn and later Venus symbolism, not the early Goetia entry

### Traditional hours

Weyer:

> **Item Duces à prima usque ad meridiem: & observatur cœlum clarum.**

Scot:

> **Dukes may be hound [bound] from the first houre till noone; and cleare wether is to be observed.**

Sources: [Weyer and Scot parallel text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm) and [Scot, Book XV, ch. 3](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

“First hour” belongs to unequal or seasonal hours beginning near sunrise, not necessarily 1:00 a.m. The operational window is therefore sunrise/first daylight hour to noon, under a clear sky.

Crowley’s “night demon” classification is a separate 777 overlay. It does not cancel or replace the older ducal timing; it conflicts with it because it was generated from list position, not from rank.

## The seal

### Earliest state

**[Documented absence]** Weyer and Scot do not give Vual an individual seal.

The individual Goetic seals appear in the seventeenth-century manuscript family. Sloane 3825 says the seal must be “made and worne as a Lamin before you.” A lamen is a pendant or breast-worn magical plate.

The Sloane 3825 image is available as [Peterson’s figure 47](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g47.jpg). The two 1904 renderings are preserved as [Vual seal 1](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:47-Vual_seal01.png) and [Vual seal 2](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:47-Vual_seal02.png).

### Verbal description

Because the sources transmit a drawing rather than a verbal recipe, the image is the authoritative description. In words, its stable topology is approximately:

- a long transverse or nearly horizontal spine;
- hooked, forked, or looped terminals;
- a central descending construction;
- an angular loop or enclosed eye near the central axis;
- lower strokes terminating in sharply bent or crossed feet.

**[Manuscript variation]** The witnesses differ in:

- whether a terminal is closed as a loop or remains a hook;
- the angle and length of the cross-stroke;
- the proportions of the central eye;
- the number and direction of small ticks;
- whether lower strokes cross, fork, or merely meet;
- the calligraphic direction in which curves appear to have been drawn.

These are graphic variants of one basic character, not separately described “first” and “second” seals in the early text. Some modern websites promote cleaned digital redrawings as “seal I” and “seal II,” but the numbering is modern editorial packaging.

### Rudd’s double lamen

**[Tradition/documented manuscript practice]** Harley 6483 places the demon’s seal and the counterbalancing Shem angelic material on opposite sides of a lamen. Skinner and Rankine explain that the angelic side is worn toward the operator’s heart. Its function is protective and coercive: the divine name, angel, and psalm constrain the demon corresponding to the same number.

This is not an instruction found in Weyer or Scot. It is the distinctive Rudd synthesis.

## Formation of the 72 names

### Biblical text

**[Documented text/tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional Hebrew consonantal text, of three verses counted as 72 letters each. The later method writes:

1. verse 19 in its normal direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its normal direction.

The resulting 72 vertical columns yield 72 three-letter combinations. The 47th is **עשל**. The verses can be consulted in Hebrew at [Sefaria, Exodus 14:19–21](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21).

This arrangement is usually called boustrophedonic, “as an ox turns in ploughing.”

### Jewish history

**[Historical distinction]** *Shem ha-meforash* originally means the “explicit,” “distinctly pronounced,” or “fully articulated” Divine Name; it does not always mean this particular list of 72 angels. Jewish sources variously use the expression for the Tetragrammaton and for longer divine-name constructions.

A 72-letter divine name was known in late-antique Jewish tradition, but the detailed Exodus extraction is not explained in the earliest references. Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a gives the now-familiar rule: first letter of the first verse, last letter of the middle verse, first letter of the last verse, and so on. Laura Joffe cites the passage in [“The Answer to the Meaning of Life, the Universe and the Elohistic Psalter”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205).

### *Sefer Raziel*

**[Documented tradition]** The medieval composite *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*, assembled from several earlier Jewish magical and cosmological materials, gives great importance to the 72-fold name. It should not be dated wholesale to antiquity merely because its legendary frame attributes revelation to the angel Raziel and transmission through Adam, Noah, and Solomon.

The triplets in Jewish sources are divine-name components. Their transformation into a regiment of individually named angels, each with a personality, office, zodiacal sector, calendar dates, and contrary demon, is a later Christian-kabbalistic and occult development.

### Reuchlin and Agrippa

Johann Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) treated the triplets as angelic names made pronounceable through endings such as **-el** and **-yah**. Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa reproduced the 72-name table in *De occulta philosophia*, Book III (1533). See [Agrippa’s table](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm).

For Asaliah:

- root: **עשל**;
- suffix: **יה**;
- expanded name: **עשליה**.

**[Important caution]** “Just God who points to truth” is not a straightforward dictionary translation of the consonants עשל. It is an occult divine epithet assigned to the constructed angel name.

### Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) printed the 72 names in an elaborate Christian-kabbalistic cosmology and attempted to associate them with divine names in 72 languages. His famous diagram survives as [The 72 Names of God](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_%28Oedipus_Aegyptiacus%2C_1652%E2%80%9354%29.jpg).

Kircher is a major conduit to Lenain. His universal-language claims are **[early-modern esoteric speculation]**, not comparative linguistics in the modern sense.

## Rudd’s pairing

In Harley 6483’s scheme, number determines the opposition:

> **47 Asaliah — Vual/Uvall**

This is confirmed in Skinner and Rankine, *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (2007), pp. 148–149 and its tables.

The surviving Harley manuscript was copied in **1712–1713**. It claims descent from papers of Dr Thomas Rudd through a copyist named Peter Smart. Skinner and Rankine argue that the underlying material dates to about 1655–1656, shortly before Rudd’s death.

That distinction matters:

- **[Documented text]** Harley 6483 is an early-eighteenth-century witness.
- **[Traditional attribution]** Its system is attributed to Thomas Rudd.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction/disputed]** A complete 1655–1656 Rudd archetype has not survived.

### How the angel constrains the demon

The Rudd lamen combines:

- the demon’s seal;
- the 47th Shem triplet/name;
- Asaliah’s name;
- a psalm verse;
- divine or angelic authority.

The angelic side faces the heart. The operative theory is that the demon’s exact celestial contrary prevents deceit or injury and forces obedience. This is a Christian-theurgical containment system, not a doctrine in the Hebrew Bible or Weyer.

## The two Asaliah psalms

### Rudd/Harley line

The line associated with Asaliah in the Rudd tradition is:

> **Quam magnificata sunt opera tua Domine, nimis profundae factae sunt cogitationes tuae.**

“Great are thy works, O LORD; thy thoughts are very deep.”

This is:

- Vulgate Psalm **91:6**;
- KJV Psalm **92:5**.

It is the verse reproduced in Rudd-derived angel-demon tables.

### Lenain line

Lenain instead assigns:

> **Quam magnificata sunt opera tua Domine! Omnia in sapientia fecisti: impleta est terra possessione tua.**

> “O Lord, how manifold are thy works! in wisdom hast thou made them all: the earth is full of thy riches.”

This is:

- Vulgate Psalm **103:24**;
- KJV Psalm **104:24**.

The verses begin similarly but are not the same. Modern angel sites frequently collapse them.

**Finding:** For “the psalm verse of the angel,” the answer depends upon the lineage:

- **Rudd’s counter-angel for Vual:** Psalm 92:5;
- **Lenain’s zodiacal guardian angel Asaliah:** Psalm 104:24.

## Lenain, 1823

In *La science cabalistique*, p. 77, Lenain writes:

> **47e. Asaliah. Son attribut (Dieu juste, qui indique la vérité). Il correspond au nom Hana, d’après la langue des peuples du Chili. Son rayon commence depuis le 231e degré jusqu’au 235e inclusivement, correspondant à la 24e décade et au génie nommé Sieme, sous l’influence de Vénus.**

He assigns the five days 5 May, 16 July, 26 September, 7 December, and 17 February; the daily time 3:20–3:40 p.m.; and Psalm 104:24. He continues that Asaliah serves to praise God and rise toward God when light is sent, governs justice and upright people, and favors contemplation of divine things. The contrary genius rules immoral or scandalous acts and dangerous, chimerical projects.

This material is in the [1823 Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365) and [Wikimedia facsimile](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_science_cabalistique_ou_lart_de_conna%C3%AEtre_les_bons_g%C3%A9nies_qui_influent_sur_la_destin%C3%A9e_des_hommes_%28IA_BIUSante_ms05365%29.pdf).

“Hana in the language of the people of Chile” is inherited from Kircher’s speculative 72-language table. It is **not accepted modern linguistics**.

## Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

### Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” devoted a chapter of *La Cabbale* (1903) to Lenain’s 72-genius notices. Papus helped transfer Lenain’s practical angelology into the French occult revival. The 1909 reprint of Lenain carried a preface by Papus.

### Robert Ambelain

Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* was first published by Niclaus in 1951. It substantially repeats Lenain’s Asaliah entry: “Just God, Who Points To Truth,” degrees 231–235, Venus, the five dates, the 3:20–3:40 period, Psalm 104:24, justice, contemplation, and the contrary angel’s immorality and chimerical schemes. See the [BnF catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb317160320) and the [English translation, pp. 20–21](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf).

A 1989 note appended to later Ambelain editions warns that seals previously attributed to the 72 good names were actually their dangerous opposites. That warning is **Ambelain’s reported practical conclusion**, not a demonstrable historical property of the seals.

### Haziel

The French occult author using the name Haziel popularized a guardian-angel model in which every person has three angels determined by date and time. *Le Grand livre des invocations et des exhortations* appeared from Bussière in 1990 and offered prayers to the 72 angels. Its [bibliographic record](https://archives.dijon.fr/detailstatic.aspx?RSC_BASE=SYRACUSE&RSC_DOCID=388331) describes it as prayers addressed to the 72 serving angels or genii.

Haziel’s presentation is **[Modern esoteric development]**. Its individualized “guardian angel” psychology and vibrational vocabulary should not be projected backward into *Sefer Raziel*, Reuchlin, or Rudd.

### Why each pip has two spirits

Crowley’s *777*, columns CLV–CLXVI, assigns:

- spirits 1–36 successively to the 36 zodiacal decans **by day**;
- spirits 37–72 to the same 36 decans **by night**.

For no. 47:

\[
47-36=11
\]

Therefore Uvall occupies the same decan as day spirit 11, Gusion: the second decan of Cancer. The relevant Golden Dawn card is the Three of Cups. The mapping is explained in the notes to the [annotated Mathers–Crowley Goetia](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf) and is printed explicitly for Uvall in *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*: “10°–20° Cancer (night) … Three of Cups … Venus–Copper.”

### The common quinance error

A widespread modern chart gives Uvall:

- 20°–25° Scorpio;
- 13–17 November;
- Seven of Cups.

That is Asaliah’s Lenain quinance applied to the demon by numerical pairing. It is not Crowley’s 777 correspondence.

Both systems are real occult constructions, but they are historically distinct:

- **Rudd/Lenain-style pairing:** one angel and, retrospectively, one demon per five degrees.
- **777:** one day demon and one night demon per ten-degree decan.

### Enochian overlays

John Dee and Edward Kelley’s sixteenth-century Angelical or Enochian system is historically separate from the Goetia catalogue. Neither Weyer nor the seventeenth-century Goetia assigns Uvall an Enochian governor, Call, aethyr, or tablet square.

Later developments include:

- Crowley’s use of Enochian elsewhere in his magical work;
- modern editions translating or adapting Goetic conjurations into “Enochian”;
- DuQuette, Hyatt, and David P. Wilson’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* (1992), which integrates modern psychological, sexual-magical, and correspondence material;
- Aurum Solis Hebrew and astrological spellings noted in Denning and Phillips’s *The Sword and the Serpent*;
- demonolatry enns sometimes incorrectly called Enochian.

These are **[Later overlays]**, not readings from Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825, or Harley 6483.

## Iconography

## The manuscript seal

The earliest distinctive image belonging specifically to Vual is the Goetic seal. Its authority is scribal and ritual rather than pictorial: it identifies and constrains the spirit but does not portray its body.

Weyer and Scot supply no portrait and no seal. Sloane 3825 supplies the seal. Harley 6483 incorporates it into a double lamen. Mathers and Crowley standardize the image for twentieth-century print culture.

## Collin de Plancy

### Text

The sixth edition of *Dictionnaire infernal* gives the entry under **Wall**, pp. 699–700:

> **Wall, grand et puissant duc du sombre empire ; il a la forme d’un dromadaire haut et terrible ; s’il prend figure humaine, il parle Égyptien ; il connaît le présent, le passé et l’avenir ; il était de l’ordre des puissances. Trente-six légions sont sous ses ordres.**

Translation:

> Wall, a great and powerful duke of the gloomy empire; he has the form of a tall and terrible dromedary; if he assumes human form, he speaks Egyptian; he knows the present, past, and future; he was of the order of Powers. Thirty-six legions are under his command.

Source: Collin de Plancy, *Dictionnaire infernal*, 6th ed., Paris, 1863, pp. 699–700, [Wikisource entry “Wall”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Wall).

Differences from Weyer:

- **Wall** rather than Vual;
- “tall” dromedary;
- no love office;
- no friendship/reconciliation;
- no deep voice;
- **36** rather than 37 legions.

The legion count is best treated as a de Plancy error or variant, because the otherwise independent early chain agrees on 37.

### Portrait

The 1863 edition includes Louis Le Breton’s engraving. It turns the textual transformation into a hybrid rather than showing two sequential forms:

- erect anthropomorphic body;
- pronounced camel/dromedary head and long neck;
- humped, stooped silhouette;
- humanlike arms and hands;
- draped or robe-like body covering;
- two-legged stance;
- grim, almost caricatural expression.

This hybrid is **Le Breton’s artistic interpretation**. Weyer does not say that Vual appears as a camel-headed humanoid; he says Vual first appears as a dromedary and, in human form, speaks Egyptian.

The first *Dictionnaire infernal* appeared in 1818. The celebrated Le Breton portrait belongs to the illustrated 1863 sixth edition, not the 1818 first edition. Scans are available through [Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf) and the [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/63056292/).

## Modern art

Modern Uvall imagery principally follows one of four patterns:

1. direct copies or polished vectors of the 1904 seal;
2. recolorings of Le Breton’s camel-human engraving;
3. conventionally attractive winged human figures used by demonolatry artists;
4. camel-themed fantasy characters bearing the name but little else from the source.

Descriptions of Uvall as a blond, green-eyed, white-winged, armored, androgynous or “unisex” being are **[Modern invention]**. They do not occur in Weyer, Scot, the Goetia, de Plancy, or Rudd.

## People and works

## Precursors and textual ancestors

### *Testament of Solomon*

The Greek *Testament of Solomon* supplies the legendary pattern of Solomon controlling and interrogating demons with a divinely empowered ring. Its surviving form is late antique or Byzantine and textually composite.

**Finding:** It contains no securely identifiable Vual/Uvall. It is a remote narrative ancestor of Solomonic demon magic, not a direct source for this name.

### Munich handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century necromancer’s handbook Clm 849, edited by Richard Kieckhefer as *Forbidden Rites*, contains spirits overlapping the later *Offices of Spirits* tradition. Peterson compares Barbarus/Barbatos, Cason/Gusoin, Otius/Botis, Curson/Purson, Alugor/Eligor, Taob/Gaap, Volach/Valac, and others.

**Finding:** Its **Volach** corresponds to **Volac/Valac**, Goetia 62, not Vual. It is not evidence for Uvall.

### *Livre des Esperitz*

This French list in Trinity O.8.29 is a major ancestor or close collateral relative of Weyer’s catalogue. Jean-Patrice Boudet found approximately 35 names corresponding to Weyer.

Its **Vaal** is the contested case discussed above. Peterson’s description-and-position comparison identifies it with Balam. Accordingly it cannot be cited as an uncontested pre-Weyer Uvall.

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

This is the direct textual family behind Weyer. Its title was known to Trithemius by 1508. Surviving descendants include Folger manuscripts and later *Offices of Spirits* copies.

Folger MS V.b.26 has **Ryall** on p. 83 under Paymon. BL Additional 36674, fol. 65r, lists **Vual** sixth in a short Latin group. These show the name travelling outside the final 72-spirit Goetia order.

### *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin’s spirit lists share some common demonological vocabulary with later grimoires, but no complete Uvall identity—dromedary, Egyptian speech, love, reconciliation, 37 legions—has been demonstrated there.

### The Goetia compiler

The anonymous seventeenth-century compiler or compilers incorporated a Scot-like version of Weyer, added seals and ritual directions, reordered the catalogue, omitted Pruflas, and added four spirits. No evidence identifies an individual author.

## Principal transmitters and editors

### Johann Weyer

Weyer was an opponent of witch persecutions but not a modern materialist. He printed the catalogue to expose what he regarded as the fraud, delusion, and blasphemy of magicians and demons. His hostile purpose nevertheless preserved the earliest secure Vual text.

### Reginald Scot

Scot printed the English catalogue within a skeptical attack on witchcraft beliefs. His translation preserved “base voice,” the correct meaning later lost in the Goetia.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Rudd is traditionally credited with supplying the 72-fold angelic counter-system. Peter Smart is named as the copyist/transmitter behind Harley 6483. The manuscript’s actual early-eighteenth-century date must be distinguished from its asserted mid-seventeenth-century archetype.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers prepared a Goetia translation/transcription, reportedly substantially complete by 1898. His wording, titles, variant names, and rank tables shaped the 1904 publication.

### Aleister Crowley

Crowley published the Mathers material in 1904, added editorial notes and his “Initiated Interpretation.” In that essay, he proposed that the spirits may be understood as “portions of the human brain.” That is Crowley’s psychological theory, not the view of Weyer or the manuscripts.

His *777* imposed the day/night decan system that makes Uvall the nocturnal second-decan Cancer spirit.

### Frater Achad

No documentary evidence was found that Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, performed a working specifically with Uvall or contributed an Uvall correspondence. His importance belongs to the wider Crowleyan and qabalistic environment. Claims of a particular Uvall link need a diary, ritual record, or citation that has not been located.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* appeared in 1996. He integrates the Goetia’s 72 spirits with the 72 Shem angels in a full zodiacal quinance mandala and employs a dark mirror in the triangle. His own account describes this as a spiritually balanced and psychologically controlled method. See the author’s [second-edition history](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

Runyon’s quinance model helps explain why modern lists assign Uvall Asaliah’s Scorpio degrees even though Crowley’s 777 gives Uvall Cancer.

### Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson

Their *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia: Sexual Evocation* first appeared in 1992. Its Uvall page explicitly gives:

- 10°–20° Cancer, night;
- 2–11 July;
- Three of Cups;
- Venus/copper.

The [Museum of Witchcraft and Magic catalogue](https://museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk/library/aleister-crowleys-illustrated-goetia/) confirms the 1992 edition and Wilson’s illustrations.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition placed the Goetia on a manuscript-critical basis and his online editions continue to collate additional witnesses. For Uvall, his key contributions are:

- preferring Vual;
- mapping Weyer 66 to Goetia 47;
- identifying Folger Ryall and Additional 36674 Vual;
- documenting manuscript spellings;
- correcting the *graviter* mistranslation;
- distinguishing the Goetia’s text from its later printed overlays.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

*The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007) published the Harley 6483 system with paired angelic and demonic lamens. It is the principal modern source for Asaliah’s formal opposition to Vual.

Their claims about a 1655–1656 archetype and certain medieval name identifications are reconstructions rather than facts guaranteed by the surviving manuscript.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica*—*The True Grimoire*, *Geosophia*, and *The Testament of Cyprian the Mage*—argued for understanding goetia through broader ancient, chthonic, necromantic, and grimoire continuities. He did not establish an ancient Egyptian or classical identity for Uvall. His relevance is methodological and comparative, not evidence for a pre-Weyer Vual.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Was the original name Vual, Wal, Wall, or Uvall?

- **Vual** is the reading in the 1577 transcription.
- **Wal** appears in Weyer’s 1660 *Opera omnia*.
- **Vuall** is Scot.
- **Uvall** is present in Harley and made famous in 1904.
- **Wall** is de Plancy.
- **Voval** appears prominently in Mathers–Crowley.
- **Vreal** was not located in a primary witness.

The safest critical headword is **Vual**, while **Uvall** remains the best-known English occult spelling.

### 2. Does Vual speak bad Egyptian?

No, not in Weyer.

- Weyer: Egyptian **graviter**, “gravely/in a deep voice.”
- Scot: Egyptian in a “base,” meaning deep, voice.
- Goetia: Egyptian “but not perfectly.”

The latter is a documented transmission error. Modern summaries that combine both—“imperfect Egyptian in a deep voice”—conflate the earlier and later readings.

### 3. Does Vual speak Coptic?

Only in Crowley’s humorous 1904 footnote. It is not a grimoire tradition preceding him.

### 4. Is Vual an Egyptian deity or connected with Thoth?

No evidence was found. The dromedary and Egyptian language contribute an early-modern exoticizing image, but neither identifies an ancient god.

### 5. Is Vual derived from Baal/Vaal?

The resemblance is insufficient. Trinity MS **Vaal** is more convincingly matched to **Balam** by its context. A Vual–Baal derivation remains speculative.

### 6. Thirty-six or thirty-seven legions?

- Weyer: 37.
- Scot: 37.
- Sloane 3825: 37.
- Mathers–Crowley: 37.
- Rudd: 37.
- de Plancy: 36.

The weight of evidence favors **37**.

Scot also appends the statement that a legion numbers 6,666, but this is part of the source’s magical arithmetic, not a historically standardized military count. Thirty-seven such legions would equal 246,642 spirits in that scheme.

### 7. Is the human form commanded?

- Weyer: merely says “in human form.”
- Scot: same.
- Sloane 3825: after a while he assumes human shape.
- Mathers: explicitly “at the Command of the Exorcist.”

The command is an elaboration in the later transmission.

### 8. Friends, enemies, or “friendship of foes”?

Weyer’s *gratiam amicorum atque inimicorum* is closer to gaining the favor of friends and enemies. Scot says “love of freends and foes.” The Goetia turns this into “friendship betweene friends & foes,” emphasizing reconciliation. Both later interpretations are possible developments of the Latin, but “reconciles enemies” is not Weyer’s exact wording.

### 9. What is Uvall’s zodiac sign?

There is no single answer because the attributions belong to different systems:

- no personal zodiac sector in Weyer or the base Goetia;
- Asaliah: 20°–25° Scorpio in Lenain;
- Uvall: 10°–20° Cancer by night in Crowley’s 777;
- Uvall: Scorpio in Runyon-style one-to-one angel/demon quinance systems;
- Uvall: still other signs in modern decks.

Any claim that “Uvall is definitively a Scorpio demon” or “definitively Cancer” suppresses the source history.

### 10. Three of Cups, Seven of Cups, or Three of Wands?

- **Three of Cups:** Crowley/777 demon-decan system.
- **Seven of Cups:** Asaliah’s third Scorpio decan transferred to Uvall.
- **Three of Wands:** a modern tarot-deck attribution.

Only the first answers “Crowley’s 777 tarot pip for spirit 47.”

### 11. Which psalm belongs to Asaliah?

- Rudd’s demon-constraining series: Psalm 92:5.
- Lenain and Ambelain: Psalm 104:24.

The similarity of their Latin openings created a persistent conflation.

### 12. Was Rudd’s complete Goetia written in the 1650s?

The surviving Harley witness is dated 1712–1713. A Thomas Rudd archetype from the 1650s is plausible within the manuscript’s claimed provenance but is not extant. “Rudd manuscript, 1655” is therefore too categorical.

### 13. Is the demon-angel pairing ancient Kabbalah?

No.

- Exodus supplies the letters.
- Jewish and medieval traditions supply the 72-fold Divine Name.
- Christian Kabbalists developed the triplets as named angels.
- Rudd pairs the numbered angels with the numbered Goetia demons.

The one-to-one demon opposition is a Western ceremonial-magic construction.

### 14. Are modern enns Enochian?

No primary evidence supports that classification. The Uvall enn is not in Dee’s diaries, Weyer, Scot, the Goetia manuscripts, Rudd, or Crowley’s 1904 edition.

### 15. Is Uvall morally associated with scandal and immorality?

Lenain assigns immoral and scandalous acts to **Asaliah’s contrary genius**, not specifically to Vual. Some modern French summaries transfer that statement to Vual. That transfer may be motivated by the Rudd pairing, but it is not in the demon’s early description.

### 16. Can Uvall manipulate time?

The source says Uvall knows or tells past, present, and future. “Manipulates time” is a modern inflation of divinatory knowledge into causal power.

## Open questions

1. **What exactly was Weyer’s exemplar?**  
   He names a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, but the manuscript he used has not been identified.

2. **Was its spelling Vual, Vuall, or Wal?**  
   The printed editions vary, and a lost handwritten exemplar could have supported more than one reading.

3. **Is Folger “Ryall” truly Vual?**  
   Its placement under Paymon and Peterson’s comparison make the identification plausible, but the name is materially different.

4. **Is Trinity “Vaal” Vual or Balam?**  
   Description and position presently favor Balam; the Vual identification survives in occult scholarship.

5. **When were Vual’s seals first drawn?**  
   They are absent from Weyer and Scot. Establishing their first exact occurrence requires a full palaeographic comparison of all seventeenth-century Goetia witnesses.

6. **Why was Vual moved from 66 to 47?**  
   No compiler explains the reordering. Scrambled exemplars or separate leaves are plausible but unproven.

7. **Why did *graviter* become “not perfectly”?**  
   A difficult or damaged intermediate English copy is likely. The precise misreading has not been located.

8. **Which manuscript first added “at the command of the exorcist”?**  
   The idea is implicit in Goetic transformation scenes but is more explicit in Mathers than in Sloane 3825.

9. **Did Thomas Rudd personally construct the 72 pairings?**  
   Harley 6483 attributes the material to his papers, but the lost exemplar prevents complete verification.

10. **Why do the Rudd and Lenain psalms differ?**  
    Both are verses beginning *Quam magnificata sunt opera tua Domine*. Accidental substitution through similar incipits is possible.

11. **What did “Egyptian tongue” mean to the original author?**  
    It might mean actual Egyptian/Coptic, an imagined magical Egyptian language, or simply an exotic language associated with ancient magic. The source does not say.

12. **How was the seal intended to be drawn?**  
    The manuscripts give a finished glyph, not stroke order, orientation instructions, or a geometric construction.

13. **Are there earlier, still-uncatalogued Office-of-Spirits witnesses?**  
    The history of related manuscripts has expanded considerably through Peterson, Boudet, Véronèse, Skinner, Rankine, and the publication of the *Book of Oberon*. A secure pre-1577 Vual remains possible but has not yet been demonstrated.

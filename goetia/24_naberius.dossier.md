# Naberius: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Documented text]** — directly stated in a cited primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a historical or textual conclusion argued from manuscripts.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim transmitted within magical, religious, or mythological literature.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an occult-system attribution, not a historical identity.
- **[Disputed]** — evidence permits competing readings.
- **[Legend]** — narrative transmitted without historical verification.
- **[Modern invention]** — a demonstrably late addition or adaptation.
- **[Negative finding]** — not found in the specified sources after targeted searches.

Because quotation limits prevent reproducing several complete source paragraphs, exact short excerpts are followed by comprehensive clause-by-clause paraphrases. The linked diplomatic texts and facsimiles contain the full passages.

### The seal in words

**[Documented image; descriptive transcription]** The familiar Naberius seal is an asymmetric linear figure organized around a central upright. Several short, undulating or hooked strokes project toward the left. To the right, the figure branches and terminates in a cluster of small closed loops or circles. The manuscript copies agree on this overall structure but differ in curvature, intersections, stroke length, and the precise placement or closure of the small loops.

The exact visual evidence is preferable to any verbal account:

- [Peterson’s comparative seals and names](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf)
- [Mathers–Crowley seal engraving](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/024-Seal-of-Naberius/)
- [Modern vectors derived from manuscript seals](https://grimorium.lat/en/seals/)

**[Negative finding]** No source in the early chain explains what individual lines or circles “mean.” Stroke-by-stroke symbolic explanations are modern interpretations.

## The name and its origins

### 1. Cerbere before Naberus

**[Documented text]** The earliest identifiable ancestor is not spelled Naberius. In the French *Livre des Esperitz*, the spirit appears as **Cerbere**, a Marquis commanding nineteen legions:

> “Cerbere est un grant marquis qui donne parfaict entendement…”

The passage continues that Cerbere gives understanding in all sciences and makes a person great in honors and riches. It assigns nineteen legions. There is no bird, hoarse voice, rhetoric specialization, or seal. See the [manuscript transcription and facsimile](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Livre_des_esperitz_-_MS_O.8.29.djvu/5).

The surviving witness is Trinity College, Cambridge, MS O.8.29, fols. 179r–182v. Its date is unsettled:

- **[Disputed]** Some catalogues place it in the later fifteenth or early sixteenth century.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** Jean-Patrice Boudet argues on palaeographic grounds that its hand is not earlier than approximately the middle of the sixteenth century.
- Either dating makes the surviving *Cerbere* text earlier than Weyer’s 1577 edition—or, at minimum, an independent witness to the immediate pre-Weyer catalogue tradition.

Boudet’s comparison suggests that the *Livre des Esperitz* and Weyer preserve cognate redactions of an older “offices of spirits” tradition. It does not establish that Weyer copied the surviving French manuscript directly. [Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1171).

### 2. Weyer’s “Naberus, alias Cerberus”

**[Documented text]** Johann Weyer’s 1577 *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* gives:

> “Naberus, aliàs Cerberus, Marchio est fortis, forma corvi se ostentans…”

That is: “Naberus, otherwise Cerberus, is a strong Marquis, showing himself in the form of a raven.” This is entry 17 in Weyer’s catalogue, printed at col. 916 of the 1577 fifth edition. [Latin and English source text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

**[Negative finding]** This investigation located no securely dated pre-1577 text using the exact form **Naberus** or **Naberius**. What predates Weyer is **Cerbere**, apparently the same catalogue figure.

### 3. From Naberus to Naberius

**[Documented text]** Reginald Scot’s 1584 English version prints **Naberius, alias Cerberus**. The extra *-i-* may reflect Latinization, editorial intervention, or transmission through another copy. It becomes the dominant English spelling.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The simplest stemma is:

```text
Cerbere
   │
   ├── lost or intermediate Offices-of-Spirits readings
   │
   └── Naberus, alias Cerberus — Weyer, 1577
                 │
                 └── Naberius, alias Cerberus — Scot, 1584
                              │
                              └── Naberius — Lemegeton manuscripts and 1904 Goetia
```

The exact mechanism that produced *Naberus* from *Cerberus* is unknown. Possibilities include scribal corruption, a contaminated exemplar containing two spellings, or the preservation of an otherwise-lost doublet.

### 4. Is Naberius literally Cerberus?

**[Documented text]** Weyer explicitly says *alias Cerberus*. De Plancy later treats the names as identifying one demon.

**[Disputed]** That establishes an early-modern textual identification, but not necessarily an ancient religious identity. The medieval *Cerbere* is already unlike classical Cerberus: he is a Marquis who teaches sciences and grants honors, not simply the guardian dog of Hades.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Naberius is best treated as an early-modern demon-catalogue transformation of the name Cerberus. No evidence presently establishes an independently worshipped ancient god, hero, or locality called Naberius.

### 5. Classical Cerberus

**[Documented ancient literature]**

- Homer mentions the “hound of Hades” but does not give the familiar name or a detailed multiheaded appearance.
- Hesiod’s *Theogony* describes Cerberus as brazen-voiced, flesh-eating, and fifty-headed.
- Later Greek and Roman art and literature normally reduce him to three heads.
- Virgil’s *Aeneid* describes triple-throated Cerberus with snakes and has the Sibyl subdue him with a drugged cake.
- Dante places Cerberus among the gluttonous in *Inferno* VI.
- Milton uses Cerberean imagery but does not present an Ars Goetia spirit named Naberius.

See [Hesiod’s *Theogony*](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HesiodTheogony.html), [Perseus on Cerberus and Heracles](https://static.perseus.tufts.edu/Herakles/cerberus.html), and [Virgil, *Aeneid* VI](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0054:book=6).

### 6. Etymology of Cerberus

The Greek name is **Κέρβερος**, Latin **Cerberus**.

**[Disputed]** No universally accepted etymology exists.

- An old comparison with Sanskrit *śarvara/karbara*, “spotted” or “variegated,” led to the romantic reconstruction “Spot,” but major historical linguists have disputed the phonology.
- The ancient/late-antique derivation from Greek *kreoboros*, “flesh-devouring,” is generally treated as folk etymology.
- A pre-Greek or otherwise unidentified origin remains possible.

Accordingly, “Naberius means valor,” “Naberius means cloud,” and similar confident internet etymologies are **[Modern invention/unsupported]** unless a particular author explicitly defines the name that way.

### 7. Naberius and Nebiros

**[Documented later conflation]** De Plancy’s separate article for *Nabérus* says he is “also called Nébiros.” Later occult encyclopedias repeat this.

**[Disputed]** In the *Grand Grimoire*/*Grimorium Verum* family, **Nebiros** has an independent textual life, commonly as an infernal officer or field marshal associated with necromancy, prediction, and occult properties. Sound resemblance and de Plancy’s conflation do not prove that every Nebiros is textually identical to Weyer’s Naberus.

Jake Stratton-Kent discusses the resemblance and possible connections with Hellenistic or Gnostic names, including a speculative comparison with “Nibareus.” That proposal is an exploratory reconstruction, not a demonstrated etymology. [Stratton-Kent, *The True Grimoire*](https://lib.wildla.in/book/Kent%2C%20Jake%20Stratton%20-%20The%20Trve%20Grimoire.pdf).

### *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Documented text]** Cerbere:

- is a great Marquis;
- gives perfect understanding in all sciences;
- makes a person very great in honors and riches;
- commands nineteen legions.

This is probably the nearest extant ancestor of the Naberius notice. [French diplomatic text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier).

### Weyer, 1577

Bibliographic location: Johann Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, fifth edition, Basel, 1577, appendix *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, col. 916, entry 17.

The complete sense of the Latin is:

- Naberus, also called Cerberus, is a strong Marquis.
- He shows himself in raven form.
- When he speaks, he produces a hoarse voice.
- He makes a person amiable and understanding in the arts.
- He makes that person especially excellent in rhetoric.
- He brings about the loss of prelacies and dignities.
- Nineteen legions obey him.

The significant Latin phrases are:

- *forma corvi* — “in the form of a raven/crow.”
- *raucam edit vocem* — “utters a hoarse voice.”
- *in Rhetoricis eximium* — “outstanding in rhetoric.”
- *dignitatum iacturam parit* — “causes the loss of dignities.”

Latin *corvus* normally indicates a raven, although “crow” is a defensible broader rendering.

**Context:** Weyer was a physician and critic of witch prosecutions. His printing of a spirit catalogue is not proof that he endorsed its magical claims. He says his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also called *Empto. Salomonis*, and indicates that he omitted material that would make the operation usable. [Peterson’s Weyer edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Scot, 1584

Bibliographic location: Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London: William Brome, 1584, Book XVI, p. 380 in the first edition’s pagination.

Scot begins:

> “Naberius, alias Cerberus, is a valiant marquesse…”

The remainder renders Weyer closely: a crow, hoarse speech, amiability, skill in all arts and especially rhetoric, loss of prelacies and dignities, and nineteen obedient legions. [1584 facsimile record](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081); [searchable public-domain edition](https://gutenberg.org/ebooks/60766).

Scot’s broader purpose, like Weyer’s, was polemical and skeptical. The catalogue appears within a work exposing what Scot regarded as the fraud, error, or superstition of conjurers and witchmongers.

### Sloane 3825 and Sloane 2731

The *Lemegeton* manuscripts move Naberius to position 24 and introduce three important changes:

1. The bird is specified as **black**.
2. It flutters “about the Circle.”
3. The loss of dignity becomes its opposite: restoration of lost dignity and honors.

The critical opening in Peterson’s transcription is:

> “The 24th spirit is called Naberius, he is a most valliant Marquiz…”

The rest specifies the black crow, fluttering around the circle, hoarse speech, arts and sciences—especially rhetoric—restoration of dignity and honors, nineteen legions, and the seal to be worn. [Peterson’s manuscript-based *Goetia*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Sloane 2731 gives the same essential reading. It is dated internally January 18, 1686/87 and preserves carefully drawn figures. [Sloane 2731 transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/).

### Harley 6483 and Rudd’s material

British Library Harley 6483, usually dated 1712–13, preserves *Liber malorum Spirituum, seu Goetia*. It is especially important because it pairs each Goetic spirit with an ordinally corresponding Shem angel.

**[Tradition/editorial attribution]** This system is conventionally attributed to Thomas Rudd, but the surviving manuscript postdates Rudd’s lifetime. It may transmit earlier Rudd material, later revisions, or both. The cautious designation is therefore “the Rudd material preserved in Harley 6483.”

### Mathers–Crowley, 1904

Bibliographic location: S. L. MacGregor Mathers, trans.; Aleister Crowley, ed., *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, Foyers, 1904, p. 18.

The entry begins:

> “The Twenty-fourth Spirit is Naberius. He is a most valiant Marquis…”

Its substantive wording follows the manuscript *Goetia*, except that it says **Black Crane**, not black crow. It retains the hoarse voice, arts and sciences, rhetoric, restored dignities and honors, nineteen legions, and wearable seal. [1904 text](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm); [facsimile PDF](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/crowley_-_goetia.pdf).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** “Crane” is most likely an editorial alteration or misreading. No earlier witness located in this investigation uses crane. Crowley’s *777* later repeats the image and adds the memorable “sore throat.”

### Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* restores **Black Crow** in its critical text, p. 17. His online transcription collates Sloane 3825 with Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses.

### Offices not actually assigned to Naberius

**[Negative finding]** The core *Livre–Weyer–Scot–Goetia* chain does **not** assign Naberius:

- languages;
- hidden treasures;
- romantic or erotic love;
- familiars;
- invisibility;
- transmutation;
- universal truthful answers;
- reconciliation of friends;
- logic as a separately named subject.

“Amiable” means socially pleasing or lovable; it should not automatically be rewritten as coercive love magic.

“Logic and rhetoric” is a frequent modern formula, but the early witnesses specify **rhetoric**, not logic. Logic may have entered through association with the trivium or contamination from other knowledge-teaching spirits.

### No second form

**[Negative finding]** Naberius’s individual entry gives no second, human, or “fair” form that the magician may demand. The broader *Goetia* ritual contains general commands that spirits assume a fair and comely form, but that is not a Naberius-specific transformation.

## Number and order: 17 versus 24

Weyer’s catalogue has **69 numbered entries**, although its Belial notice invokes the traditional total of seventy-two. The *Ars Goetia* reaches seventy-two by:

- adding **Vassago** as #3;
- adding **Seere** as #70;
- adding **Dantalion** as #71;
- adding **Andromalius** as #72;
- omitting Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**.

Thus:

```text
69 Weyer entries − Pruflas + four additions = 72 Goetic entries
```

The insertion of Vassago near the beginning and other differences shift Naberius from Weyer #17 to *Goetia* #24. Peterson has suggested that displaced or rearranged manuscript leaves may explain some ordering changes, but no complete stemma has been proved. [Peterson’s comparative table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

**[Negative finding]** “Naberius was always demon 24” is false. Twenty-four is the *Lemegeton* order, not Weyer’s.

## The angel: Hahuiah / Haheuiah

### Identification

| Field | Rudd/Golden Dawn form |
|---|---|
| Number | 24 |
| Common spellings | **Hahuiah, Hahauiah, Haheuiah, Chahuiah** |
| Hebrew triplet | חהו |
| Expanded angelic form | חהויה |
| Conventional meaning | “God good in himself”; “Goodness in Himself” |
| Zodiac in Golden Dawn/Rudd-derived table | Scorpio 25°–30° |
| Planetary ruler of quinance | Venus |
| Psalm | Vulgate Psalm 32:18; modern/Hebrew numbering Psalm 33:18 |
| Psalm incipit | *Ecce oculi Domini super metuentes eum…* |
| Function in Rudd | Angelic name and seal placed against Naberius’s seal as its constraint and safeguard |

### How the seventy-two names are derived

**[Tradition with textual documentation]** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three consecutive Hebrew verses of seventy-two letters each under traditional counting. The later method writes:

1. verse 19 forward;
2. verse 20 backward;
3. verse 21 forward.

Reading vertically produces seventy-two three-letter groups. The twenty-fourth is **חהו**, conventionally transliterated ḤHV, ChHV, HHV, or similar. Appending a divine suffix such as *-iah* produces **Hahuiah/Haheuiah**.

Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a documents the forward–reverse–forward construction. [Rashi on Sukkah 45a](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Sukkah.45a.2.1). For scholarly analysis of the letter arrangement, see Laura Joffe’s [“The Answer to the Meaning of the Seventy-Two Names of God”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205).

**[Historical caution]** *Shem ha-mephorash* originally means the “explicit,” “distinguished,” or fully articulated divine Name. Rabbinic uses often concern the Tetragrammaton, not necessarily this later list of seventy-two personified angels.

### From Jewish name tradition to angel list

A defensible historical outline is:

1. **Late antique and medieval Jewish traditions:** combinations of the divine Name, including a seventy-twofold Name, circulate in mystical and magical texts.
2. **Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh:** the medieval compilation preserves seventy-two-name material, although its layered textual history prevents attributing the complete later angel calendar to one early author.
3. **Christian Cabala:** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) and related authors interpret Hebrew letter groups within a Christian angelological framework.
4. **Kircher:** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54) prints a highly influential seventy-two-name table, angelic expansions, biblical associations, and cosmological arrangement. [Kircher transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm); [1653 scan](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/kircher1653bd2_1?ui_lang=eng).
5. **Rudd material:** Harley 6483 pairs those seventy-two angels ordinally with the seventy-two *Goetia* spirits.
6. **Lenain:** *La science cabalistique* (1823) turns the angels into an elaborate five-degree and daily-time system with moral attributes and “contrary genii.”
7. **Golden Dawn:** reorganizes the zodiacal starting point so angel 1 begins at Leo 0°, placing angel 24 in Scorpio rather than Cancer.
8. **Papus, Ambelain, Haziel:** reproduce and progressively elaborate the French angel-calendar tradition.

### Meaning

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Lenain calls Hahuiah **“Dieu bon par lui-même”**, “God good in himself.” The Golden Dawn table gives “Goodness in Himself.”

This is an occult title or divine attribute, not a literal lexical translation of the three Hebrew consonants חהו.

### Psalm verse and the Rudd anomaly

The regular Kircher–Lenain–Golden Dawn association is:

> *Ecce oculi Domini super metuentes eum…*

This is Vulgate Psalm **32:18**, corresponding to Psalm **33:18** in most modern English/Hebrew numbering: the Lord’s eye is upon those who fear him and hope in his mercy.

Skinner and Rankine’s transcription of the Naberius lamen instead gives wording beginning *Beneplacitum est Domino super timentes eum* while labeling it Psalm 32:18. That text is actually Vulgate Psalm **146:11**, corresponding to modern Psalm **147:11**. Compare the [Vulgate passages](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalmi+146%3A11&version=VULGATE%3BNIV).

**[Documented textual problem]** Harley 6483’s Naberius material therefore appears to contain either:

- the wrong verse under the right citation;
- a displaced verse copied from another entry;
- or an editorial/copying substitution.

It should not be silently “corrected” when describing the manuscript.

### Hahuiah in Lenain

Lenain’s 1823 entry assigns Hahuiah:

- degrees 116–120 of the zodiac;
- the title “God good in himself”;
- divine grace and mercy;
- aid to exiles, fugitives, escaped prisoners, and persons condemned in absentia;
- protection against harmful animals, thieves, and murderers;
- love of truth and exact sciences;
- sincerity in word and action.

His “contrary genius” governs harmful beings and inclines toward crime or destructive conduct. These are **[Esoteric correspondences]**, not attributes present in Exodus or in Rudd’s demon description. [Lenain scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_science_cabalistique_ou_lart_de_conna%C3%AEtre_les_bons_g%C3%A9nies_qui_influent_sur_la_destin%C3%A9e_des_hommes_%28IA_BIUSante_ms05365%29.pdf).

### Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

- **Papus**, *La Cabbale* (notably the 1903 edition), reproduced much of Lenain’s list and thereby widened its French occult readership.
- **Robert Ambelain**, *La Kabbale pratique* (1951), adapted the practical angelic and talismanic framework for modern ceremonial use.
- **Haziel**—the pen name of François Bernad-Termés—popularized guardian-angel calendars in the late twentieth century. His Hahuiah commonly receives July 17–22 and 7:40–8:00 a.m., plus extensive personality and devotional material.

These birth dates and personality profiles are **[Modern esoteric elaboration]**. They are not found in Weyer, Scot, the early *Goetia*, Exodus, or the earliest recoverable Shem tradition.

### How the angel constrains Naberius

**[Documented Rudd practice]** The Rudd lamen combines two authorities:

- the demon’s seal;
- the corresponding Shem angel’s name/seal and scriptural verse.

The lamen is worn with the angelic side toward the operator’s heart. The angel is not described as Naberius’s moral “twin.” It functions as superior divine authority used to constrain, regulate, or protect against the spirit.

**[Historical conclusion]** The angel–demon pairing is a late ordinal overlay. Nothing establishes that Hahuiah and Naberius formed an ancient pair before the Rudd tradition.

## Correspondences

### The two incompatible five-degree calendars

| System | Starting point | Angel 24 | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lenain/French angel calendar | Angel 1 at Aries 0° | **Cancer 25°–30°** | Degrees 116–120 counted from Aries |
| Golden Dawn/Rudd-derived table | Angel 1 at Leo 0° | **Scorpio 25°–30°** | Sequence rotated to begin with Leo |
| Crowley *777* Goetic demon table | Ten-degree decans, day/night | **Scorpio 20°–30°, day** | Naberius occupies the whole third decan, not one quinance |

This distinction resolves a common contradiction: Cancer and Scorpio derive from two different starting conventions.

### Golden Dawn and Crowley synthesis

| Layer | Attribution to Naberius |
|---|---|
| Goetic number | 24 |
| Rank | Marquis |
| Rank planet | Moon |
| Rank metal | Silver |
| *777* zodiacal decan | Scorpio 20°–30° |
| Day/night | Day |
| Decan ruler | Venus |
| Tarot pip | Seven of Cups |
| Golden Dawn card title | Lord of Illusionary Success |
| First quinance of decan | Scorpio 20°–25° — Melahel in the Golden Dawn angel sequence |
| Second quinance | Scorpio 25°–30° — Hahauiah |
| Night Goetic spirit of same decan | Vapula, printed “Napula” in some *777* material |
| *777* image | Black crane with an afflicted/hoarse throat, fluttering |

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** In *Liber 777*, tables CLIX–CLX place Naberius under the third decan of Scorpio by day. Tables CLXV–CLXVI place Vapula/Napula there by night. [Crowley, *Liber 777*](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06numerologia_cabala/Crowley_%20Liber_777.pdf).

**[Correction]** Naberius therefore does not possess a unique Crowleyan five-degree quinance. Its direct *777* allocation is the full ten-degree decan. The five-degree Hahauiah placement comes from combining the Golden Dawn Shem table with the decan table.

### Why each pip has two demons

Crowley’s table divides each zodiacal decan into a day and a night expression. Consequently, each tarot pip associated with a decan carries two Goetic spirits:

```text
Seven of Cups / Scorpio III
├── Day: Naberius
└── Night: Vapula/Napula
```

The seventy-two spirits map neatly onto thirty-six decans only by assigning two to each.

### Planetary layers

The apparent planetary conflict is a consequence of multiple systems:

- **Moon** — Naberius’s rank planet because he is a Marquis.
- **Venus** — planetary ruler of the third decan of Scorpio in the Golden Dawn tarot scheme.
- **Venus again** — ruler printed for Hahauiah’s Scorpio quinance in the Golden Dawn table.

These are correspondences of different kinds, not competing statements about a physical entity.

### Timing of conjuration

**[Documented Goetic instruction]**

- Marquis seals are made in silver.
- Marquises may be bound from 3 p.m. to 9 p.m. and again from 9 p.m. until sunrise.
- The *Goetia* recommends operations when the Moon is two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, or fourteen days old.

**[Later esoteric synthesis]**

- *777* calls Naberius the **day** spirit of Scorpio III.
- Golden Dawn astrologers may favor Scorpio III, approximately November 13–22 in fixed-date tables.
- Lenain/Haziel practice may use July 17–22 and 7:40–8:00 a.m. for Hahuiah.

The dates are approximate modern calendrical conversions; actual solar ingress varies by year and timezone.

### Enochian overlays

**[Documented context]** The larger Rudd manuscript complex includes Enochian material, angelic tables, and the *Tabula Sancta*. Crowley likewise combined Goetia with other systems, including the “Bornless” ritual and Golden Dawn techniques.

**[Negative finding]** No early manuscript evidence was located for a unique Enochian governor, aethyr, watchtower square, or Enochian name belonging specifically to Naberius. Modern charts that supply one are synthetic overlays.

### Modern demonolatry correspondences

Modern sites sometimes assign Naberius:

- an “enn” such as *Eyan tasa volocur Naberius*;
- jasmine incense;
- purple;
- ash wood;
- Air;
- particular calendar dates;
- psychological powers of eloquence, rehabilitation, and reputation management.

These are **[Modern invention/esoteric practice]**. They are absent from the *Livre des Esperitz*, Weyer, Scot, the *Lemegeton* manuscripts, and Mathers–Crowley.

## Iconography

### The manuscript seal

**[Documented]** Weyer and Scot supply no seals. Naberius’s distinctive sigil enters the securely recoverable record with the *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

- Sloane 3825 and Sloane 2731 present recognizably the same underlying glyph.
- Differences include the angle and length of its central stroke, the number and curvature of leftward hooks, and the rendering of the little circles at the right.
- Harley 6483 incorporates it into a double-sided angel–demon lamen.
- The 1904 edition regularizes the figure into a clean print engraving.

Because seals were copied by hand, small differences do not necessarily denote different spirits.

### Crow, raven, crane, and dog

Four iconographic strands must be separated:

1. **Cerbere:** no physical description in the *Livre des Esperitz*.
2. **Weyer:** *corvus*, most naturally a raven, though often rendered crow.
3. **Scot and manuscripts:** crow; the *Goetia* adds black and fluttering around the circle.
4. **Mathers–Crowley:** black crane.
5. **De Plancy/Le Breton:** the classical three-headed dog.

**[Negative finding]** None of the early catalogue texts says Naberius first appears as a three-headed dog and then changes into a bird. That familiar composite description merges his alias with classical and de Plancy iconography.

### Collin de Plancy

The first edition of Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* appeared in 1818. The celebrated sixth edition appeared in 1863 with engravings attributed to Louis Le Breton.

#### “Cerbère”

De Plancy’s 1863 article begins by identifying Cerberus or Naberus as a demon and repeats the Weyer tradition: strong Marquis, crow form when not appearing with three dog heads, hoarse voice, eloquence, amiability, fine arts, and nineteen legions. [“Cerbère,” 1863](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Cerb%C3%A8re).

It then accumulates classical and antiquarian material:

- fifty, one hundred, or three heads depending author;
- serpentine appendages;
- black teeth and a fatal bite;
- the guardian of the infernal gate;
- an Egyptian tomb-dog/Anubis interpretation.

**[Disputed antiquarian reconstruction]** The Egyptian proposal reflects nineteenth-century comparative mythology. Hellenistic Cerberus–Anubis syncretism is real, but that does not prove Naberius originated as Anubis.

The entry also recounts **[Legend]** that Marie Martin made a pact with Cerberus in 1586. No independent evidence was located establishing that episode as historical.

#### The Le Breton portrait

The engraving depicts a classical three-headed hound, not Weyer’s crow or Crowley’s crane. The animal is shown as a powerful dark dog with three alert or snarling heads. [Le Breton image](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Fichier:Ill_dict_infernal_p0165-149_cerbere.jpg).

This image had enormous downstream influence: modern occult art often assumes the three-headed hound is Naberius’s original summoned form, although that claim comes from identifying him with Cerberus rather than from his early physical description.

#### “Nabérus”

De Plancy also provides a separate *Nabérus* article, calling him Nébiros and adding offices absent from Weyer:

- field marshal and inspector-general of infernal armies;
- liberal arts;
- finding the Hand of Glory;
- properties of metals, plants, and animals;
- knowledge of the future;
- chief of necromancers.

[“Nabérus,” 1863](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Nab%C3%A9rus).

**[Documented conflation]** This is the clearest influential source for merging Naberius with the Nebiros/Grand-Grimoire complex.

### Modern art

Modern images generally choose one of four approaches:

- a black corvid;
- a black crane;
- a three-headed hound;
- a hybrid bird–hound or anthropomorphic Marquis accompanied by the seal.

Only the corvid has direct support in Weyer, Scot, and the manuscripts. The crane begins with the 1904 edition, while the three-headed dog comes from the Cerberus identification and de Plancy tradition.

## People and works

### Chronological source history

#### Anonymous *Livre des Esperitz* compiler/copyist

The *Livre* supplies the first surviving Cerbere notice: Marquis, sciences, honors/riches, nineteen legions. Its absence of bird and rhetoric imagery shows how much the description expanded before Weyer.

#### Johann Weyer, 1577

Weyer gives the earliest located **Naberus**, adds *alias Cerberus*, bird form, hoarse voice, amiability, rhetoric, and the destructive office of causing loss of dignities.

#### Reginald Scot, 1584

Scot Anglicizes the name as **Naberius**, renders *corvus* as crow, and transmits the notice to English readers. His anti-witch-hunting context remains essential.

#### Anonymous *Lemegeton* compilers, seventeenth century

They expand the catalogue to seventy-two, assign Naberius number 24 and a seal, make the crow black and mobile, and reverse lost dignity into restored dignity.

#### Thomas Rudd tradition, preserved 1712–13

The Rudd layer pairs Naberius with Hahuiah, puts angelic authority onto the protective lamen, and connects the demon catalogue with the seventy-twofold Name.

#### Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy merges grimoire demonology, classical mythology, folklore, and Grand-Grimoire material. Le Breton’s three-headed dog becomes the best-known visual representation associated with the entry.

#### Lenain, 1823

Lenain is crucial to Hahuiah’s French occult biography, degree range, daily period, protective offices, moral characteristics, and contrary genius.

#### Golden Dawn, Mathers, and Crowley

- Golden Dawn tables place Hahauiah in Scorpio 25°–30°.
- Mathers–Crowley change the bird from crow to crane in 1904.
- Crowley’s *777* assigns Naberius to Scorpio’s third decan by day and connects that decan with the Seven of Cups.

#### Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

They successively reproduce and expand the French angelic calendar. Their material belongs primarily to Hahuiah’s modern devotional reception, not to Naberius’s early textual biography.

#### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition compares the principal manuscripts and restores the black-crow reading. His online transcriptions make Weyer, Scot, the *Goetia*, and their variants directly comparable.

#### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

*The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (2007) publishes and reconstructs the Harley 6483 system, including the double seals and Shem constraints. It is indispensable but should be read with awareness that “Dr Rudd” is an attribution to a later manuscript tradition.

#### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent situates the catalogues within a broader necromantic and spirit-office tradition. His discussion of Naberius/Nebiros, Anubis, and possible Gnostic parallels is stimulating but partly speculative.

#### Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, and Julien Véronèse

Their scholarship supplies the broader history of grimoires, learned magic, necromancy, and medieval spirit conjuration. Boudet remains the most directly useful published comparison for Cerbere and Weyer.

#### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* and related work popularized a modern Solomonic system using dark-mirror scrying, the Shem angels, and a master mandala.

**[Negative finding]** No securely documented, published Naberius-specific operation by Runyon was located. His relevance is systemic rather than biographical.

#### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette co-edited or contributed to *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*, which helped popularize a psychological and ceremonial reading of the spirits.

**[Negative finding]** No distinctive Naberius operation or revelation attributable to DuQuette was located.

#### Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No Naberius-specific writing was found in the readily searchable Frater Achad corpus or bibliography. His importance to Crowleyan Qabalah does not by itself make him a Naberius source.

## Grimoire ancestors and analogues

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer identifies an “Offices of Spirits” book as his source. Several surviving texts use related titles, but no single extant manuscript has been proved to be Weyer’s exact exemplar.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The *Livre des Esperitz*, Weyer’s catalogue, and the later *Goetia* belong to a family of ranked spirit catalogues describing names, offices, appearances, and legions. Their relationships are branching rather than a simple line of verbatim copying.

### Munich Handbook, CLM 849

The fifteenth-century Munich manual contains necromantic operations and names related to later catalogue spirits.

**[Negative finding]** No direct Cerbere, Naberus, or Naberius entry was identified there. It is a contextual ancestor of ritual practice, not proof of Naberius’s individual existence before the *Livre*.

### Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 89 sup. 38

This 1494 manuscript contains conjuration experiments and several names comparable to later Goetic lists.

**[Negative finding]** It does not provide a securely identified Naberius/Cerbere entry, and its seals are not the later standardized Goetic seals.

### *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin’s demon lists share names and hierarchy concepts with broader early-modern magic.

**[Negative finding]** No direct Naberius spelling or description was identified. It should not be cited as a primary Naberius attestation.

### *Testament of Solomon*

The *Testament* supplies the ancient literary model of Solomon interrogating demons, recording their functions and thwarting them through angels or divine names.

**[Negative finding]** It contains neither Naberius nor Cerberus as this Marquis. Its relation is structural and legendary, not genealogical at the level of the name. [*Testament of Solomon*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Testament_of_Solomon).

### Solomon and the brass vessel

**[Grimoire legend]** The *Goetia* relates that Solomon confined spirits in a brass vessel and that Babylonians, expecting treasure, opened it. This story is part of the text’s pseudepigraphic authority. It is not a historical report about an Israelite king maintaining the later list of seventy-two demons.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. “First attested in Weyer”

**Verdict:** true only for the spelling *Naberus*.

- **Documented:** Weyer prints *Naberus alias Cerberus* in 1577.
- **Documented:** the earlier or independent *Livre des Esperitz* has *Cerbere* with the same rank, sciences, honors, and nineteen legions.
- **Best formulation:** the entity’s recoverable Cerbere strand predates Weyer; the exact Naberius/Naberus form has not been found before Weyer.

### 2. “Weyer first published it in 1583”

**Verdict:** erroneous.

The *Pseudomonarchia* first appeared as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in **1577**. The 1583 edition is later and often more accessible, which generated the incorrect date.

### 3. Is Naberius a corruption of Cerberus?

**Verdict:** plausible but unproved.

The sequence *Cerbere → Naberus alias Cerberus* strongly suggests textual deformation. No manuscript has yet exposed the intermediate change.

### 4. Crow, raven, or crane?

- *Livre*: no appearance.
- Weyer: Latin *corvus*, normally raven.
- Scot: crow.
- Sloane manuscripts/Peterson: black crow.
- Mathers–Crowley: black crane.
- *777*: crane with a sore throat.

**Conclusion:** crow/raven belongs to the early source chain; crane is a 1904 innovation or error.

### 5. Does Naberius appear as Cerberus, the three-headed dog?

**Verdict:** source-dependent.

- Weyer identifies the name with Cerberus but describes only a bird manifestation.
- De Plancy explicitly combines dog and crow forms.
- Le Breton depicts the dog.
- Modern writers often project the dog form backward into the *Goetia*.

The three-headed dog is traditional iconography by identification, not the summoned form given in the early Goetic entry.

### 6. Loss or restoration of dignity?

This is the clearest substantive contradiction:

- *Livre*: grants honors and riches.
- Weyer/Scot: causes loss of prelacies and dignities.
- *Goetia*: restores lost dignity and honors.

Possible explanations include:

- Weyer preserved a hostile reversal;
- a *Goetia* copyist dropped or reversed a negative;
- different catalogue branches transmitted contrary offices;
- the *Goetia* harmonized Weyer with the older honor-granting Cerbere.

No manuscript currently proves which transformation happened first.

### 7. Does he teach logic?

**Verdict:** not in the core texts.

Rhetoric is explicit. “Logic” is a later extrapolation from the liberal arts or the trivium and possibly contamination from other Goetic knowledge spirits.

### 8. Naberius versus Nebiros

**Verdict:** conflated, not securely identical in every source.

De Plancy identifies them, while several grimoire redactions give Nebiros different rank and functions. Each occurrence must be evaluated in its own textual family.

### 9. Ancient god behind Naberius

**Verdict:** no separate ancient Naberius located.

Cerberus is the ancient mythological referent. Anubis is a later comparative or syncretic association. No evidence supports an ancient cult of Naberius as such.

### 10. The angel as an eternal opposite

**Verdict:** late systematization.

The seventy-two divine letter groups and the demon catalogue developed independently. The ordinal angel–demon pairing becomes explicit in the Rudd manuscript tradition.

### 11. Hahuiah in Cancer or Scorpio?

**Verdict:** both readings are internally correct in different systems.

- Lenain counts from Aries: Cancer 25°–30°.
- The Golden Dawn begins the angel sequence at Leo: Scorpio 25°–30°.
- Crowley’s demon table places Naberius across Scorpio 20°–30° by day.

Writers often combine these tables without stating their different zero points.

### 12. The psalm discrepancy

**Verdict:** genuine textual error or displacement.

The standard Hahuiah verse is Psalm 32:18 Vulgate/33:18 modern. The Rudd transcription gives the wording of Psalm 146:11 Vulgate/147:11 modern while retaining the former citation.

### 13. Authorship by Thomas Rudd

**Verdict:** traditional and editorially plausible, but not certain.

Harley 6483 is later than Rudd. “Rudd’s system” is useful shorthand, not a verified autograph attribution.

### 14. Fixed personality, incense, colors, and enn

**Verdict:** modern occult practice.

Such attributions may be meaningful within living demonolatry, but they should not be projected into Weyer or the manuscript *Goetia*.

## Open questions

1. What is the precise date of Trinity College MS O.8.29? Catalogue descriptions and palaeographic analysis still differ.

2. Did Weyer’s lost *Liber officiorum spirituum* already read “Naberus alias Cerberus,” or did Weyer create or reconcile the double name?

3. What scribal process converted *Cerbere* into *Naberus*? No satisfactory linguistic explanation has been demonstrated.

4. Why did Weyer’s loss of dignities become restoration in the *Goetia*? Was this correction, euphemism, accidental negation loss, or reliance on an independent honor-granting branch?

5. Why did Mathers or Crowley print “crane”? A difficult manuscript hand, editorial imagination, or an unavailable intermediate copy are all possible.

6. Which stage of the Rudd manuscript tradition introduced the mismatched Hahuiah psalm?

7. How much of Harley 6483 genuinely descends from Thomas Rudd, and how much belongs to the early-eighteenth-century copyist or compiler?

8. Can a complete stemma of the Naberius seal be constructed from Sloane 2731, 3825, 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses?

9. When precisely did Naberius and Nebiros first become interchangeable outside de Plancy’s dictionary?

10. Is Stratton-Kent’s comparison with “Nibareus” anything more than phonetic resemblance? At present, no continuous textual chain has been demonstrated.

11. No evidence currently establishes a pre-medieval cult, god, hero, or place named Naberius. Discovery of a genuinely earlier occurrence would materially change the reconstruction.

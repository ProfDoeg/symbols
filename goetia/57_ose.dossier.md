# Ose: Research Dossier

*Research date: 14 September 2026.*

## Evidentiary key

- **Documented text** — directly stated in a manuscript, early printed edition, or identifiable modern work.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a historical or textual conclusion argued from surviving evidence.
- **Tradition** — a claim transmitted within occult, kabbalistic, or magical practice but not demonstrable as ordinary history.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — an imposed symbolic relationship, such as a planet, decan, tarot card, or angel–demon pairing.
- **Disputed** — supported by conflicting readings, dating, attribution, or interpretation.
- **Legend** — presented within the sources as sacred, mythic, or supernatural history.
- **Modern invention** — first found in recent occultism, fiction, games, or fan interpretation rather than the early source chain.

A quotation limitation should be noted at the outset: the linked editions contain the complete entries. Short verbatim passages are reproduced below, followed by complete, clause-by-clause synopses and comparisons.

## The name and its origins

### 1. The earliest surviving witness: Azo or Oze, circa 1494

**Documented text.** The earliest secure close ancestor presently identifiable is not Weyer’s 1577 printing. It occurs in Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana MS Plut. 89 sup. 38, fol. 306v, edited and translated by Brian Johnson in *Necromancy in the Medici Library*.

Its opening reads:

> “Azo, ut oze magnus preses apparet in similitudinem Leopardi…”

That is, “Azo, or Oze, a great president, appears in the likeness of a leopard.” The complete entry then gives:

- assumption of human form;
- wisdom in all liberal arts;
- true answers about divine and hidden matters;
- transformation into another form;
- insanity or false self-identification at the exorcist’s command;
- belief that one is a king because of a crown and sceptre;
- possession of the sceptre for one hour;
- twenty legions.

The manuscript’s only explicit colophon is dated at Rome on 4 January 1494. Johnson observes that the principal scribe’s contributions are non-consecutive and were produced over time; the hand is compatible with the late fifteenth or early sixteenth century. Accordingly:

- **Documented:** the entry is in a manuscript belonging to that scribal and codicological environment.
- **Scholarly reconstruction:** it is reasonable to call it a circa-1494 witness.
- **Caution:** the date 4 January 1494 does not individually date fol. 306v.

This nevertheless disproves the common claim that Ose has no pre-Weyer attestation. The Florentine **Azo/Oze** entry is already recognizably the same spirit. [Johnson’s edition and manuscript study](https://dokumen.pub/necromancy-in-the-medici-library-an-edition-and-translation-of-excerpts-from-biblioteca-medicea-laurenziana-ms-plut-89-sup-38-9781907881985-9781907881992.html).

### 2. The *Livre des esperitz*

**Documented text; disputed dating.** The French *Livre des esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, fol. 182v, calls the spirit **Oze**. Its entry begins:

> “Oze est ung grant marcquis qui donne bone responce…”

Here Oze is a **great marquis**, not president. He:

- answers questions well;
- changes a person from one figure to another;
- makes a thing appear other than it is;
- makes a handful of straw seem a great horse;
- makes a straw seem a gold or silver belt;
- makes people mad when commanded;
- commands twenty-five legions.

The manuscript page supplied by Wikisource labels it “mid-fifteenth century,” but modern codicological discussion is less settled. Jean-Patrice Boudet’s analysis allows for a substantially later hand, perhaps around the middle of the sixteenth century, whereas earlier scholars placed it in the later fifteenth or early sixteenth century. The text may preserve older material even if this particular copy is later.

Oze’s illusion and madness offices are therefore early, but this witness does not have the leopard or liberal sciences. [French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Oze); [Boudet’s study and edition](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

### 3. The *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* family

**Documented text and scholarly reconstruction.** Weyer said that his catalogue came from a book of the offices of spirits, which he styled *Liber officiorum spirituum* or *Empto. Salomonis*. Johannes Trithemius had already catalogued works called *Liber officiorum* and *De officiis spirituum* in his *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508.

An English witness in Folger Shakespeare Library MS V.b.26, the so-called *Book of Oberon*, has:

> “The 9 is called Oze…”

It assigns Oze the power, after taking human form, to teach the seven liberal arts, tell secrets, change a person’s shape, and appear as a leopard. The different number—nine within that subsection—shows that these catalogues circulated in distinct arrangements before the fixed 72-spirit order. [Folger transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/liber-officiorum/read/).

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The Florence, French, Folger, and Weyer witnesses are best understood as branches of an evolving *Offices of Spirits* tradition, not as independent revelations of a stable 72-member infernal hierarchy.

### 4. Weyer, 1577

Johann Weyer first added the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Oze is §56 of Weyer’s 69 entries.

**Documented name:** **Oze**.

Weyer’s text is not an invocation manual in the same form as the later *Goetia*. Weyer, a physician and opponent of witch executions, published the catalogue in a polemical work about demonic deception. Joseph Peterson notes Weyer’s assertion that he intentionally omitted or disrupted ritual material. [Weyer’s Latin with source notes](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### 5. Scot, 1584

Reginald Scot printed an English version in Book XV of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584), chapter 2, p. 224.

**Documented names:** the heading reads **Ose**, while Peterson’s critical annotation restores Weyer’s **Oze**.

Scot’s purpose, like Weyer’s, was substantially skeptical and expositional. Printing a conjuration or catalogue did not mean Scot endorsed its claims. The translation probably came through an intermediary associated with “T. R.” or “R. W.,” traditionally connected with a translator named Fleming, although the precise division of work remains uncertain. [Scot, Book XV](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

### 6. Sloane manuscripts and Harley 6483

Joseph Peterson’s manuscript comparison records:

| Witness | Reading |

| Sloane MS 3825 | **Ose** |
| Sloane MS 3648 | **Ose** |
| Sloane MS 2731 | **Ose** |

Sloane 3825 is Peterson’s principal text for the first book of the *Lemegeton*. Sloane 2731 supplied important variants and was Mathers’s principal manuscript source. Harley 6483 represents the Rudd recension copied by Peter Smart in the early eighteenth century. [Peterson’s name table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

### 7. Mathers and Crowley, 1904

The 1904 edition’s heading is **“OSE, or VOSO.”** Its body begins by listing **Oso, Ose/Osé, or Voso**.

That makes **Voso** first securely visible in this published source chain in 1904. It is absent from Weyer, Scot, and the principal Sloane readings.

- **Documented:** Mathers–Crowley printed Voso.
- **Disputed reconstruction:** Voso may reflect a scribal or editorial expansion of Oso, possibly involving early-modern *u/v* ambiguity.
- **No evidence:** it cannot presently be derived from an ancient language or deity.

[1904 facsimile, p. 29](https://www.thelemistas.org/PDF/Goetia.pdf).

### 8. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* (Weiser, 2001), p. 33, prints **Ose** as the principal reading and reports **Oso** and the Mathers–Crowley alternatives in the notes. His online transcription follows Sloane 3825 while recording significant variants. [Peterson’s *Goetia*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm); [2001 edition PDF](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf).

### 9. Is there an ancient god, hero, or place behind the name?

**Finding: none has been demonstrated.**

Unlike Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Amon/Amun, Asmoday/Ashmedai, Belial/*Bəliyyaʿal*, or Phoenix/*phoinix*, Ose has no securely identified ancient divine, heroic, geographic, Greek, Latin, or Hebrew antecedent.

The oldest spellings, **Azo** and **Oze**, must be given more weight than the later form **Ose** when evaluating etymologies.

#### Latin *os*

Modern reference pages sometimes derive Ose from Latin *os*:

- *ōs, oris* — “mouth,” potentially connected to speech or answers;
- *os, ossis* — “bone.”

**Disputed/fanciful.** These are formal resemblances, not demonstrated historical derivations. They do not explain *Azo*, *Oze*, or *Voso*, and no early source makes the connection.

#### Latin *osor*

Another modern suggestion is Latin *ōsor*, “hater” or “one who detests.”

**Disputed.** The resemblance to *Ose* is weak; no textual bridge or medieval gloss supports it.

#### Anzu, Enzu, Dionysus, Oshosi, or leopard gods

Modern occult websites have connected Ose with the Mesopotamian Anzu bird, the moon god sometimes styled Enzu, Dionysus because of leopard imagery, or the Yoruba orisha Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì/Oshosi.

**Modern syncretism.** No manuscript in the Ose chain gives these identifications; neither the phonology nor the documented offices establishes them. The leopard alone is not enough to prove descent from a particular leopard-associated deity.

### Name conclusion

The most economical reconstruction is:

> **Azo/Oze → Oze → Ose/Oso → Osé/Voso**

The shift probably reflects ordinary transmission among Latin, French, and English scribes rather than a recoverable ancient theonym. The name’s ultimate origin remains unknown.

## The number: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

### Weyer’s order

Oze is **§56** in Weyer’s list of 69 spirits. The local sequence is broadly:

- §55 Gomory
- §56 Oze
- §57 Amy

### Goetic order

Ose is **57** in the *Ars Goetia*:

- 55 Orobas
- 56 Gremory
- 57 Ose
- 58 Amy

The movement is not caused by a single simple insertion immediately before Ose. The compiler reorganized parts of the catalogue.

### The four added and one omitted

Relative to Weyer, the *Goetia* adds:

1. **Vassago** — Goetia 3
2. **Seere** — 70
3. **Dantalion** — 71
4. **Andromalius** — 72

It omits Weyer’s:

- **Pruflas**, also printed as Bufas, Weyer §4.

Four additions minus one omission produce the net change from 69 to 72.

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The number 72 was probably attractive because of the 72-fold divine Name and its later angelic names. However, no surviving early *Goetia* preface explicitly narrates a compiler deciding to manufacture an exact demon–angel opposition. The completed numerical symmetry made Rudd’s later one-to-one pairing possible.

**Textual curiosity.** Weyer’s Belial entry refers to Solomon’s confinement of 72 kings, even though Weyer’s printed catalogue contains only 69 entries. That may preserve a memory of a 72-fold conception, but it does not prove that Weyer possessed the exact later list.

## The description across the sources

## 1. Florence MS Plut. 89 sup. 38

**Date:** circa 1494, with the qualification described above.  
**Name:** Azo or Oze.  
**Rank:** great president.  
**Legions:** 20.  
**Appearance:** leopard; then human.  
**Offices:** liberal arts, divine and hidden answers, metamorphosis, insanity and false royal identity.  
**Hour:** the sceptre or royal condition is held for one hour.

The Latin opening is:

> “Azo, ut oze magnus preses apparet in similitudinem Leopardi…”

The remainder says that, after assuming human form, Oze renders a person discerning in all liberal arts; gives true answers concerning divine and hidden things; causes transformation; and, at the exorcist’s command, causes a person to become insane and believe himself to be what he is not. Wearing a crown and holding a sceptre, the victim thinks himself a king, and holds the sceptre for one hour. Twenty legions are stated.

**Important interpretation:** the crowned king is the transformed or deluded human, not unmistakably Oze himself.

## 2. *Livre des esperitz*

**Name:** Oze.  
**Rank:** great marquis.  
**Legions:** 25.  
**Appearance:** none given.  
**Offices:** truthful answers, changes of figure, illusion, madness.

This witness supplies the unusually concrete illusions of straw becoming a horse or an apparent gold or silver belt. It therefore emphasizes **altered appearance and perception**, not merely biological shapeshifting.

## 3. Folger MS V.b.26

**Name:** Oze.  
**Local number:** 9.  
**Rank and legions:** not supplied in the short entry.  
**Appearance:** leopard, then man.  
**Offices:** seven liberal arts, secrets, transformation.

This form is very close to the skeleton of Weyer’s entry.

## 4. Weyer’s Latin, 1577

**Edition:** *De praestigiis daemonum*, fifth edition, Basel, 1577, p. 928, §56.

The beginning reads:

> “Oze Præses magnus: procedit similis leopardo: sed hominem mentitus…”

A complete grammatical synopsis is:

1. **Oze is a great President.**
2. He comes forth like a leopard.
3. Feigning or assuming a man, he makes a person learned in the liberal arts.
4. He answers truly about divine and hidden matters.
5. He transforms the form of a human being.
6. He reduces or drives that person into such madness that the person believes himself to be what he is not.
7. Examples are believing oneself king or pope and believing one wears a crown.
8. The “reign” or condition lasts one hour.
9. Weyer gives **no legion count**.

The crucial Latin is *ad eam insaniam eum redigit*: “he drives/reduces him to such insanity.” That supports Scot’s “bringeth a man to that madness.” Peterson’s bracketed alternative “drives insanity away” is linguistically difficult and should not displace the ordinary reading.

[Latin and comparison, p. 928](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

## 5. Scot’s English, 1584

**Edition:** *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London, 1584, Book XV, chapter 2, p. 224.

Scot begins:

> “Ose is a great president, and commeth foorth like a leopard…”

His complete content follows Weyer closely:

1. Ose is a great president.
2. He comes forth like a leopard.
3. Counterfeiting himself to be a man, he makes a person skilled in the liberal sciences.
4. He answers truly concerning divine and secret things.
5. He transforms a person’s shape.
6. He causes madness or delusion, so that the person thinks himself something he is not.
7. Scot retains the examples of king, pope, and crown.
8. Scot leaves the last sentence in Latin: *Durátque id regnum ad horam*—“and that reign lasts for an hour.”
9. No legions are stated.

[Scot’s text, p. 224](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

## 6. Sloane 3825 and Peterson’s text

**Edition:** Peterson, 2001, p. 33; manuscript source Sloane 3825, fol. 108v and following.

The entry begins:

> “The 57 spirit is called Ose, he is a great president…”

The complete clause sequence is:

1. Ose is spirit 57 and a great president.
2. He appears first like a leopard.
3. After a little time he puts on the shape of a man.
4. He makes a person skilled in the liberal sciences.
5. He gives true answers concerning divine and secret things.
6. He changes a person into any shape desired by the exorcist.
7. The changed person believes himself truly to be the creature or thing into which he has been changed.
8. He governs three legions.
9. His seal is to be worn as a lamen.

This version replaces Weyer’s king, pope, crown, and one-hour limitation with a generalized transformation into “any shape.”

**Textual development:** the *Goetia* appears to strengthen the transformation claim. Weyer can be read as transformation plus delusion; the *Goetia* says the person is changed and simultaneously believes the change to be real. The text never cleanly distinguishes physical metamorphosis from induced perception.

[Peterson transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm); [Peterson 2001, p. 33](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf).

## 7. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**Edition:** *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, 1904, p. 29.

Its opening is:

> “The Fifty-seventh Spirit is Oso, Ose, or Voso. He is a Great President…”

The rest follows the Sloane form: leopard; human form after a short interval; liberal sciences; true answers about divine and secret things; transformation into any chosen shape; belief by the subject that the assumed identity is real; the seal.

### The legion problem in the 1904 edition

The printed body has **3**, followed by footnote marker 24. Crowley’s note conjectures that it “should be 30,” reasoning that the spirits are leaders of substantial numbers.

This produced several descendants:

- **3** — the manuscript and original body reading;
- **30** — Crowley’s conjectural correction, adopted silently by numerous reprints;
- **31** — an OCR or typographic fusion of “3” with a superscript note marker;
- **3024** — OCR fusion of the proposed “30” or body “3” with footnote number 24.

Only **3** is the Sloane *Goetia* reading. Crowley supplied no manuscript evidence for 30. [1904 facsimile](https://www.thelemistas.org/PDF/Goetia.pdf); [high-resolution scan page](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/057-Seal-of-Voso/).

## Rank, planet, metal, and timing

### President: Mercury and quicksilver

**Documented esoteric correspondence.** The *Goetia*’s planetary metal instructions assign:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince/prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury/quicksilver |
| Earl/count | Mixed Moon–Venus character | Copper and silver |

Ose’s presidential lamen is therefore assigned **Mercury/quicksilver**.

Mars and iron are conspicuously absent from this seven-rank table. Later authors sometimes attempt to regularize that omission, but such corrections are not the manuscript rule.

### Time of day

Weyer’s rank rule is textually awkward:

> “Item Præses in aliqua hora diei non potest constringi…”

The surrounding wording and punctuation have produced contradictory translations. Scot says a President may not be bound in any hour of the day unless the king he obeys is invoked, “nor in the shutting of the evening.” The *Goetia* tradition reverses or repunctuates the practical sense.

The 1904 text gives the intelligible working rule:

- presidents may be bound at any time;
- except twilight;
- at night they require the invocation of the king under whom they serve.

**Disputed:** whether the early Latin means “cannot be constrained in just any daytime hour” or whether a negative/punctuation problem entered transmission.

### Lunar timing

The *Goetia* advises working when the Moon is:

- 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old.

This is a general ritual instruction, not an Ose-specific property.

### The “one hour”

The one hour in Florence, Weyer, and Scot is the duration of the imposed kingship, papacy, crown, or sceptre-delusion. It is **not**:

- Ose’s planetary hour;
- a summons lasting exactly one hour;
- a daily period of Ose’s rule.

Collin de Plancy later changed it into “one hour per day,” helping spread that misunderstanding.

## The seal

### Origin in the source chain

**Documented textual history.**

- Florence has magical characters for spirits, but these are not securely identical to the later Goetic seals.
- Weyer and Scot give **no Ose seal**.
- The *Goetia* manuscripts add individual seals and instructions that they be worn as lamens.
- Peterson observes that these Goetic sigils are absent from the earlier *Offices of Spirits* texts and cannot simply be projected backward to Weyer.
- Sloane 3825 supplies a recognizable Ose seal.
- Sloane 2731 and related copies transmit minor graphic variants.
- Harley 6483 surrounds or supplements the demonic material with Rudd’s angelic apparatus.
- Mathers standardized the best-known printed form in 1904.

### Verbal description

In the familiar Mathers–Crowley form, the seal is:

- compact and approximately square;
- constructed around two diagonals forming an X-like crossing;
- divided into triangular or kite-like internal spaces;
- furnished with small circular nodes or ring terminals at several outer points;
- extended upward by a short vertical stem;
- extended downward into a pointed or triangular projection;
- broadened on the lateral sides by short angular or wing-like strokes.

A high-resolution reproduction measures roughly 28 mm square in the 1904 book. [1904 scan](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/057-Seal-of-Voso/); [public-domain vector/raster reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:57-Ose_seal.png).

### Variants

**Documented but graphically fluid.** Manuscript variation concerns:

- whether terminals are closed circles, open hooks, or ink blobs;
- relative width and height;
- the curvature of the lateral strokes;
- the exact intersection of the diagonals;
- whether an enclosing circle is supplied by a later ritual system;
- whether Hebrew and angelic names appear around or opposite it.

The underlying crossed, nodal, approximately square construction remains recognizable.

Claims that Ose’s original seal was merely “a circle with a cross” are modern simplifications. Conversely, an enclosing circle in a Rudd-derived presentation should not be assumed to be part of the earliest Sloane glyph.

## The angel: Nemamiah

## 1. The pairing

**Documented esoteric correspondence.** In the Rudd scheme, demon 57 is paired by ordinal number with angel 57:

> **Oso/Ose — Nemamiah**

Stephen Skinner’s tabulation reproduces Harley’s spelling **Oso**, the divine triad **NMM**, and the ruling angel **Nemamiah**. [Skinner’s tabulation](https://lyricaltokarev.com/public/dynamic/sync/stuff/pdf-documents-library/Complete%20Magician%27s%20Tables.pdf).

The pairing is not present in Weyer, Scot, or the ordinary Sloane *Goetia*. It is an early-modern Christian-kabbalistic overlay.

## 2. The 72-fold Name

The system uses Exodus 14:19–21. In the Hebrew text, each verse contains 72 letters. The later extraction procedure writes:

1. verse 19 in its ordinary direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its ordinary direction.

Reading vertical columns gives 72 groups of three letters—216 letters in total.

For number 57, the resulting triad is:

> **נ מ מ — N-M-M**

Christian kabbalists made many triads pronounceable by adding **-אל** (*-El*) or **-יה** (*-Yah*). The expanded name is commonly written:

> **נממיה — NMMYH — Nemamiah**

### What “Shem ha-Mephorash” historically means

**Scholarly qualification.** In early rabbinic usage, *shem ha-meforash*, “the explicit/distinctly pronounced Name,” generally referred to the divine Name, especially the Tetragrammaton. It was not originally the fixed title of 72 guardian angels.

The 72-fold Name developed as one among several expanded divine-name traditions—alongside 12-, 22-, and 42-letter Names. The identification of the 72 triads as 72 individually named angels is characteristic of medieval and especially Christian-kabbalistic reception. [Historical overview](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2017/html); [Laura Joffe on the 72-letter tradition](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205).

## 3. Jewish textual history

### Rashi and the *Bahir*

Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a describes the 72-fold construction from the three Exodus verses. *Sefer ha-Bahir*, compiled in Provence around the later twelfth century, also knows the 72-fold Name.

Abraham ibn Ezra offered a different explanation: the verses allude numerically to a 72-letter divine name, but he did not simply endorse every later angelic construction. [Ibn Ezra on Exodus 14:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Ibn_Ezra_on_Exodus.14.19.2).

### *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*

**Documented tradition and scholarly reconstruction.** The compilation known as *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* contains influential divine-name and angelological material. Its components are stratified:

- older Hekhalot and cosmological matter;
- material associated with medieval Ashkenazi pietists, especially Eleazar of Worms;
- later Sephardi and Byzantine kabbalistic elements;
- a commentary on the 72-fold Name plausibly dating to the fourteenth century;
- an editorial preface close to the first printing in Amsterdam in 1701.

Thus “from the *Sefer Raziel*” does not mean the whole printed book was written in antiquity or delivered intact by the angel Raziel.

**Legend:** Raziel revealed secret wisdom to Adam; the 72-fold Name was used by Moses in the crossing of the sea and by holy persons for protection, healing, exorcism, and wonders.

**History:** the surviving compilation is medieval and composite. [Modern study of the textual layers](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2017/html?lang=en); [Trachtenberg on Jewish divine-name magic](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

## 4. Christian Cabala: Reuchlin, Vigenère, Kircher

### Johannes Reuchlin, 1517

In *De arte cabalistica* (1517), Reuchlin treated the 72 triads, expanded by *-El* or *-Yah*, as angelic names and printed a list. This is a decisive stage in the passage from a divine-name construction to a Christian angelic catalogue.

### Blaise de Vigenère

Vigenère reproduced and developed the 72 angel names. Skinner and Rankine argue that Rudd drew heavily on Vigenère’s treatment.

### Athanasius Kircher

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) presented the 72 names within his enormous Christian-kabbalistic synthesis, connecting them with peoples, languages, divine names, and cosmic order. Kircher is an important transmitter, not the inventor of the Exodus extraction. [Kircher material](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm).

## 5. Thomas Rudd and Harley 6483

British Library Harley MS 6483 is an early-eighteenth-century manuscript in the group copied by **Peter Smart**. Its material was attributed to, or copied from papers ascribed to, **Thomas Rudd**.

- **Documented:** Smart copied manuscripts in the Rudd collection around 1699–1713.
- **Traditional attribution:** the system is “Dr Rudd’s.”
- **Disputed:** Harley 6483 is not Thomas Rudd’s autograph; how much derives from Rudd himself rather than Smart or an intermediate compiler remains uncertain.
- **Skinner–Rankine reconstruction:** a lost Rudd exemplar may have been assembled around 1655–1656.

### How the angel constrains the spirit

Rudd’s major innovation is a balancing or coercive lamen:

- one side or component bears the Goetic spirit’s seal;
- the opposite or surrounding component bears the corresponding Shem angel and divine name;
- the angel is invoked as the power authorized to bind, check, or compel the demon.

This is explicitly protective and hierarchical. Nemamiah is not Ose’s “guardian angel” in a biographical or friendly sense; within Rudd’s magic, Nemamiah is the divine counterforce assigned to constrain spirit 57.

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine published this recension as *The Goetia of Dr Rudd: The Angels and Demons of Liber Malorum Spirituum seu Goetia* (Golden Hoard, 2007). [Publisher/author information](https://www.sskinner.com/reviews.htm).

## 6. Nemamiah’s meaning

Lenain gives Nemamiah’s attribute as:

> “Dieu louable”

The most accurate English renderings are:

- “Praiseworthy God”;
- “God worthy of praise”;
- “God of highest praise.”

**Disputed/later variant:** “Lovely God” or “Amiable God” appears through confusion between French *louable* (“praiseworthy”) and *aimable* (“lovable”). Lenain’s 1823 text supports **praiseworthy**, not **lovable**.

The Hebrew triad נממ is not an ordinary lexical phrase meaning “praiseworthy.” The meaning is an assigned theological attribute.

## 7. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* (Amiens, 1823), pp. 85–86, systematizes Nemamiah as angel 57.

Lenain gives:

- attribute: **Dieu louable**;
- sacred foreign name: **Popa**, allegedly in the language of the Cyrenians;
- ray: **281st through 285th degree**, inclusive;
- “29th decade”;
- secondary genius: **Srô**;
- planetary influence: **Mars**;
- order: the eighth order, the **choir of Archangels**;
- daily invocation time: **6:40–7:00 p.m.**;
- psalm: Vulgate Psalm 113:19;
- scattered days: 15 May, 26 July, 6 October, 17 December, 27 February;
- beneficial offices: prosperity and liberation of prisoners;
- governance: captains, admirals, generals, and those fighting for a just cause;
- character: courage, activity, greatness of soul, endurance;
- contrary genius: treachery, discord among leaders, cowardice, and attacks on the defenseless.

[Lenain’s 1823 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf); [Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365).

## 8. Zodiacal degrees

Lenain’s 281°–285° should be understood carefully.

With the ordinary modern convention:

- Aries begins at 0°;
- Capricorn begins at 270°;
- 280°00′ is 10° Capricorn;
- 285°00′ is 15° Capricorn.

Consequently the five-degree sector is conventionally given as:

> **10°00′–14°59′ Capricorn**

Traditional tables that label the first degree as “1” rather than “0” display this as the inclusive integers 281–285 or “11th–15th degrees of Capricorn.” The difference is one of labelling, not a different sky sector.

Later guardian-angel calendars commonly make Nemamiah the solar angel of **1–5 January**, but that is a reformulation of the continuous zodiacal quinance. Lenain himself gives five scattered calendar dates, not 1–5 January.

## 9. Psalm verse

Lenain assigns:

> *Qui timent Dominum speraverunt in Domino: adiutor eorum et protector eorum est.*

This is:

- **Psalm 113:19** in the Vulgate/LXX numbering used by Lenain;
- **Psalm 115:11** in the Masoretic and most modern Protestant numbering.

Sense: those who fear the Lord trust in him; he is their help and shield.

The differing number is a psalm-numbering issue, not evidence for a different verse.

## 10. Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

### Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” helped reissue and popularize French occult-kabbalistic materials. His *La Cabbale* and his involvement with later editions of Lenain carried the 72-genius system into Martinist and fin-de-siècle occultism.

### Robert Ambelain

Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reproduced and extended the Lenain-style angelic tables, planetary and zodiacal assignments, moral characteristics, and ritual applications. [Ambelain-derived English material](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf).

### Kabaleb and Haziel

Kabaleb, followed by Haziel and other late-twentieth-century French-language angel authors, popularized a personal “guardian angel” framework based on:

- five consecutive birth dates;
- a twenty-minute daily period;
- sometimes separate physical, emotional, and intellectual angels;
- prayers, personality profiles, and life missions.

**Modern development:** this threefold guardian system is not in Weyer, Rudd, or Lenain’s original form. It expands Lenain’s astrological schema into modern angelic self-help and devotional literature.

## 2. Why Nemamiah and Ose have different zodiacal sectors

The angelic table divides the zodiac into 72 quinances of five degrees each. Angel 57 therefore receives the 57th quinance: Capricorn 10°–15°.

Crowley’s Goetic table instead:

1. divides the zodiac into 36 decans;
2. assigns spirits 1–36 as daytime rulers;
3. assigns spirits 37–72 as nighttime rulers;
4. pairs spirit 57 with daytime spirit 21 because 57 − 36 = 21.

Morax and Ose consequently share the third decan of Libra:

- Morax by day;
- Ose by night.

The two schemes were never mathematically designed to put demon 57 and angel 57 in the same zodiacal place.

## 3. Tarot card

### Nemamiah: Three of Disks

The second decan of Capricorn is Mars in Capricorn. In Golden Dawn tarot nomenclature it is:

- Three of Pentacles/Disks;
- “Lord of Material Works” or “Lord of Works.”

Each ten-degree pip-card decan contains two five-degree Shem angels:

- Nemamiah: first quinance/day;
- Yeialel: second quinance/night.

### Ose: Four of Swords

The third decan of Libra is Jupiter in Libra:

- Four of Swords;
- “Lord of Rest from Strife” in Golden Dawn terminology;
- “Truce” in Crowley’s *Book of Thoth* nomenclature.

Ose is the nocturnal/cadent Goetic ruler; Morax is the daylight ruler. [Original and revised *Liber 777* tables](https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/crowley/liber/libers/liber777.pdf).

## 4. Day or night

There are three different “day/night” questions:

1. **Goetic rank timing:** presidents are primarily available by day, with conditional night work under their king.
2. **Golden Dawn angelic quinance:** Nemamiah is the first/day angel of the Three of Disks; Yeialel is the second/night angel.
3. **Crowley’s demon-decan table:** Ose is explicitly the nighttime Goetic ruler of the third Libra decan.

These should not be collapsed into “Ose is simply a night demon.”

## 5. Enochian overlays

**Documented reception, not original identity.** Rudd and later Golden Dawn authors sought large synthetic systems linking Solomonic spirits, Shem angels, planetary hierarchies, zodiac, tarot, and material associated with John Dee.

Crowley’s 1904 volume placed Goetic material beside the “Bornless” invocation and Enochian material. His *777* further correlated the Goetic kings and spirits with Qabalistic tables.

No evidence has been found that:

- Ose occurs by name in Dee and Kelley’s angelic diaries;
- Ose is an original Enochian spirit;
- the Ose seal is derived from the Enochian tablets.

“Enochian Ose” is therefore a later synthetic overlay, not an early source claim.

## Iconography

## 1. Manuscript imagery

Ose’s earliest stable iconographic attribute is the **leopard**.

The source chain moves as follows:

- Florence: leopard, then human;
- Folger: leopard and human shape;
- Weyer: comes forth like a leopard, then counterfeits a man;
- Scot: same;
- *Goetia*: leopard first, man after a little time;
- modern artists: usually a leopard-headed or fully anthropomorphic leopard man.

The early sources do not specify:

- clothing;
- weapons;
- sex or age;
- wings;
- horns;
- color;
- whether the human form retains feline features.

Those are modern artistic decisions.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818 and went through repeated enlargement. The 1863 sixth edition, famous for Louis Le Breton’s engravings, has an entry under **Oze**, p. 512.

It calls Oze:

> “grand président des enfers”

The entry says that Oze appears as leopard or man; makes adepts skilled in the liberal arts; answers about divine and abstract matters; transforms and makes a person insane enough to believe himself king or emperor; and wears a crown, though his reign lasts only one hour per day. [1863 Oze entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Oze).

### De Plancy’s changes

Compared with Weyer:

- “pope” becomes **emperor**;
- the source’s victim becomes less clearly distinguished from Oze;
- the crown appears to be transferred to Oze himself;
- “lasts one hour” becomes **one hour per day**;
- *occultis* is rendered as “abstract” rather than hidden or occult.

These are documented nineteenth-century adaptations, not independent early traditions.

### Was there a Le Breton portrait?

No dedicated Oze engraving was located with the 1863 entry, nor in the standard Le Breton plate corpus. The entry is text-only on the consulted page.

Therefore:

- **Documented:** de Plancy has an Oze article.
- **Negative finding:** no authenticated Louis Le Breton portrait of Oze was found.
- **Common misattribution:** modern leopard-headed demon paintings are sometimes presented alongside de Plancy’s text and mistaken for his 1863 art.

[1863 volume at Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_1); [1818 first-edition portal](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/1re_%C3%A9dition,_1818).

## 3. Modern art

Modern imagery usually selects one of four approaches:

1. realistic leopard becoming a man;
2. leopard-headed humanoid;
3. crowned leopard king, influenced by de Plancy’s grammatical shift;
4. warrior or magician carrying the Goetic seal.

The two-sword, green-caped leopard warrior familiar from *Shin Megami Tensei* is a specific modern character design. Neither swords nor green clothing comes from the grimoires.

Modern pendants, tattoos, altar tiles, and sigil posters generally reproduce the Mathers–Crowley seal, frequently regularized for symmetry and enclosed in a circle.

## People and works

## 1. Johannes Trithemius

Trithemius did not print Ose’s entry, but his 1508 *Antipalus maleficiorum* catalogued forbidden books called *Liber officiorum* and *De officiis spirituum*. This anchors the wider textual genre from which the catalogue developed.

## 2. Johann Weyer

Weyer printed Oze in 1577. His larger argument treated elaborate demonological systems as deceptions and criticized the persecution of alleged witches. His catalogue nevertheless became the primary printed conduit for many later Goetic names.

## 3. Reginald Scot

Scot carried the catalogue into English in 1584. His *Discoverie* was designed to expose fraudulent magical claims and challenge witch prosecutions. The irony is that later magicians mined his exposé as a sourcebook.

## 4. Anonymous *Lemegeton* compilers

Seventeenth-century compilers converted the inherited list into the first book of a five-part ritual collection:

1. *Ars Goetia*
2. *Ars Theurgia-Goetia*
3. *Ars Paulina*
4. *Ars Almadel*
5. *Ars Notoria*

For Ose, their important additions were:

- fixed position 57;
- the seal and lamen;
- three legions;
- explicit transformation into any desired shape;
- integration into the full conjuration apparatus.

## 5. Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Rudd’s attributed papers supplied the angelic counterweights. Peter Smart was the copyist responsible for the surviving early-eighteenth-century Harley materials. The distinction matters: “Rudd’s Oso” is a recension and attribution, not an autograph statement beyond dispute.

## 6. Mathers and Crowley

S. L. MacGregor Mathers transcribed and normalized manuscript material, principally from Sloane 2731. Aleister Crowley edited and published it in 1904.

Crowley’s contributions to the reception include:

- the conjecture of thirty legions;
- incorporation of the preliminary “Bornless” invocation;
- a psychological essay interpreting spirits as portions of the human brain;
- later placement of Ose in the nighttime Libra decan in *777*.

Crowley’s psychological reading is an interpretation alongside, not a faithful paraphrase of, what the manuscripts claim.

## 7. A. E. Waite

Waite reproduced Ose in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic* and helped transmit Mathers’s standardized seals to a broader English readership. His treatment is derivative rather than a new manuscript witness.

## 8. Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition restored a more diplomatic manuscript text and exposed variants hidden by earlier normalized editions. For Ose, his crucial contributions are:

- Sloane 3825’s **3 legions**;
- Harley’s **Oso**;
- Mathers’s **Oso/Osé/Voso**;
- Weyer’s absence of a legion number;
- manuscript comparison of the seals and wording.

## 9. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s demon–angel method broadly available and argued for a Ruddian seventeenth-century archetype. Their historical reconstruction is influential but should be separated from the physical date and authorship of the surviving Smart copy.

## 10. Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* treats Ose as a patron of shapeshifters, werewolves, and leopard-men and uses modern zodiacal and directional correspondences.

These are **modern practitioner interpretations**. His reported ritual opinions—including claims that a manifested intelligence distinguished being a “spirit of evil” from being an intrinsically evil spirit—are personal magical testimony, not independently verifiable history.

Runyon’s use of Uranus for Presidents is a modern substitution and conflicts with the *Goetia*’s explicit Mercury/quicksilver table.

## 11. Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* (1992) popularized an accessible, psychological, and practitioner-oriented reading of the text.

No well-attested published DuQuette operation specifically with Ose was found. The frequently repeated personal evocation associated with DuQuette is generally Orobas, not Ose.

## 12. Frater Achad

No reliable Ose-specific working, essay, or correspondence by Frater Achad was located. Achad’s importance belongs to Crowleyan Qabalah generally; attaching him to Ose without a citation would be speculative.

## 13. Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent argued for interpreting goetia through classical daimonic religion, necromancy, underworld cult, and the broader Mediterranean grimoire tradition.

That framework may illuminate the category of spirits, but no Ose-specific ancient god proposed by Stratton-Kent has been securely demonstrated. A general theory that Goetic practice has classical roots does not prove that every corrupt early-modern name conceals a recoverable ancient deity.

## 14. Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Julien Véronèse, and Jean-Patrice Boudet

- Owen Davies supplies broad social history of grimoires and their circulation.
- Jan Veenstra studies learned medieval magic, spirits, and courtly contexts.
- Julien Véronèse’s scholarship is important for ritual magic and Solomonic textual traditions.
- Jean-Patrice Boudet is especially important for the *Livre des esperitz* edition and dating discussion.
- Brian Johnson supplies the most important presently published pre-Weyer Oze witness in the Florence manuscript.

These scholars provide historical context; none should be represented as endorsing the spirits’ objective existence.

## Grimoire ancestors and parallels

## 1. *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

This is the immediate textual family. Florence, Folger, the *Livre des esperitz*, and Weyer preserve overlapping spirit catalogues with different ranks, offices, orders, and legion totals.

## 2. Munich Handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook is an important parallel repository of ritual experiments and spirit catalogues.

A difficulty arises in Peterson’s comparative apparatus:

- one table associates **Otius** with Oze;
- more detailed comparison associates Otius with **Botis**.

Accordingly, Otius should not be confidently listed as an Ose spelling without renewed manuscript collation. No secure Ose entry has been demonstrated in Clm 849.

## 3. *Livre des esperitz*

This is a close continental relative, though the date of the surviving manuscript and its exact priority relative to other witnesses remain disputed. Its illusion examples may preserve a fuller branch of Oze’s office.

## 4. *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin’s demon lists participate in the same broad early-modern practice of cataloguing subordinate spirits. No secure Ose/Azo/Oze counterpart has been found there.

## 5. *Testament of Solomon*

The *Testament of Solomon* is an ancient or late-antique pseudepigraphic work in which Solomon questions and binds demons, learns their offices, and discovers the angels who thwart them.

It is a major conceptual ancestor of Solomonic demon catalogues, but:

- Ose does not appear by that name;
- the leopard-man description is not found there;
- Nemamiah is not paired with Ose there;
- the later Goetic seal is absent.

The *Testament* supplies a paradigm, not a demonstrated textual genealogy for Ose.

## 6. Solomon’s brazen vessel

The *Goetia* tells how Solomon confined spirits in a brass vessel, which Babylonians later broke open while seeking treasure.

**Legend:** this explains the demons’ release.

Versions of Solomon imprisoning demons or spirits in vessels occur in Jewish, Christian, Islamic, and medieval legendary materials. However, no early vessel story specifically names Ose. Its application to Ose is collective: Ose is one member of the later 72.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Did Ose exist before Weyer?

**Resolved in the affirmative, with dating caution.**

The Florence **Azo/Oze** entry is pre-Weyer and probably around 1494. Folger and the *Livre des esperitz* provide additional related witnesses, though their exact dates and relationships require care.

The statement “Ose first appears in Weyer in 1577” is true only if “appears” means **first printed appearance**, not earliest surviving manuscript attestation.

## 2. Was the earliest name Azo or Oze?

The Florence text reads “Azo, or Oze.” It is unclear whether:

- Azo was the principal name and Oze an alias;
- one was a correction;
- *ut* is corrupt for *vel* or otherwise being used anomalously;
- both descend from a still earlier lost reading.

Oze is more consistently transmitted later.

## 3. Is Voso ancient?

No. Its earliest secure appearance in the requested chain is Mathers–Crowley, 1904. It is likely scribal or editorial, but its exact mechanism remains unknown.

## 4. Is Ose a President or Marquis?

Both are documented:

- Florence: President;
- *Livre des esperitz*: Marquis;
- Weyer/Scot/*Goetia*: President.

Because the catalogues were fluid, a rank discrepancy need not imply two distinct spirits. The shared transformation and madness offices make identity or close textual relationship likely.

## 5. Three, twenty, twenty-five, or thirty legions?

All but thirty have manuscript or early-text support:

- **20:** Florence;
- **25:** *Livre des esperitz*;
- **none:** Weyer and Scot;
- **3:** Sloane *Goetia*;
- **30:** Crowley’s conjecture.

No principle proves that one number is the original. Twenty and twenty-five show that changing legion counts preceded Crowley by centuries.

## 6. Physical transformation or delusion?

The sources permit several readings:

1. **literal metamorphosis** — the person becomes another creature;
2. **perceptual glamour** — observers see a different figure;
3. **self-delusion** — the victim believes in a transformation that has not occurred;
4. **two separate offices** — transformation and induced madness.

Evidence:

- *Livre des esperitz* explicitly includes things appearing otherwise than they are.
- Florence separates transformation from making the person insane.
- Weyer similarly mentions transformed form and then false self-belief.
- The *Goetia* says the person is changed but emphasizes that the person believes the new identity.

The text does not resolve the metaphysics. Modern claims that Ose “literally shapeshifts anyone” select the strongest possible reading.

## 7. Who wears the crown?

Florence and Weyer grammatically make the transformed/deluded person believe that he wears a crown or holds a sceptre.

De Plancy’s abbreviated French makes Oze himself appear crowned. Modern art often follows de Plancy rather than Weyer.

## 8. Does the madness last one hour, or the reign?

Weyer says *id regnum*, “that reign,” lasts one hour. It may mean:

- the entire delusional condition;
- specifically the royal/papal identity;
- possession of the crown or sceptre.

Florence explicitly links the sceptre with the one-hour span. “All Ose’s powers last only an hour” is unsupported.

## 9. “One hour per day”

This is de Plancy’s nineteenth-century addition or interpretation. It is not in Weyer’s Latin.

## 10. Did Scot say Ose cures madness?

No persuasive basis exists for that reading. The Latin *ad eam insaniam eum redigit* and Scot’s ordinary text both indicate bringing someone **to** madness. Peterson’s bracketed alternative should be treated as a noted textual possibility, not the preferred translation.

## 11. Is Ose derived from Latin *os*?

Possible only as speculation. The early form Azo and the persistent z in Oze oppose treating later English Ose as transparent Latin.

## 12. Is Ose an ancient leopard deity?

No evidence currently demonstrates this. Ancient leopard symbolism is widespread and cannot by itself identify Dionysus, an Egyptian deity, Anzu, or an African hunting deity as Ose’s original.

## 13. Are Ose and Oso different spirits?

The manuscript apparatus treats them as variants of the same entry. There is no separate catalogue entry for an independent Oso with a different office.

## 14. Is Otius in the Munich Handbook Ose?

The comparative evidence is internally inconsistent. Detailed descriptions associate Otius more strongly with Botis. Until the manuscript and parallel tables are collated afresh, “Otius = Ose” should remain doubtful.

## 15. Is Nemamiah Ose’s Jewish opponent?

Not in early Jewish sources. Nemamiah’s triad belongs to the 72-fold divine Name, but the one-to-one opposition to Ose is Rudd’s Christian magical arrangement. Presenting it as an ancient rabbinic demonology is anachronistic.

## 16. Does Nemamiah mean “Lovely God”?

Lenain says *Dieu louable*, “Praiseworthy God.” “Lovely/Amiable God” likely comes from confusion with *aimable*.

## 17. Is Nemamiah 10°–15° or 11°–15° Capricorn?

Both notations can describe the same five degrees:

- continuous astronomical notation: 10°00′–14°59′;
- ordinal degree labels: degrees 11 through 15.

A table that literally assigns 11°00′–15°59′ would be shifted and is not equivalent.

## 18. Is Ose’s tarot card the Three of Disks or Four of Swords?

Both appear in later systems, for different reasons:

- **Three of Disks:** belongs to angel 57 Nemamiah’s Capricorn quinance;
- **Four of Swords:** belongs to Ose’s nighttime Libra decan in *777*.

Calling either the single “original tarot card of Ose” is misleading. The early grimoires predate these card correspondences.

## 19. Which directional king rules Ose?

The Folger tradition places Oze in a southern grouping associated with Amaymon. Later Goetic and modern authors assign spirits among different cardinal kings, sometimes producing a northern assignment under Ziminiar.

The inconsistency reflects competing classification systems. No directional ruler appears in Ose’s Weyer entry.

## 20. Is President ruled by Mercury or Uranus?

Mercury is the documented *Goetia* assignment. Uranus is a modern substitution used by some twentieth-century occultists.

## Open questions

1. **What is the exact palaeographic date of the Florence Oze entry?**  
   The codex’s 1494 colophon and hand establish a late-fifteenth/early-sixteenth-century context, but the individual entry lacks its own date.

2. **Which witness lies closest to the lost common ancestor?**  
   Florence is earliest securely datable; the *Livre des esperitz* may preserve independent archaic material; neither is simply the source of every later form.

3. **What does “Azo, ut oze” exactly represent?**  
   Alias, correction, corruption, or a translator’s formula remain possible.

4. **Can the *Livre des esperitz* manuscript be dated more narrowly?**  
   Its older traditional dating and Boudet’s later palaeographic assessment materially affect the stemma.

5. **Where did the leopard originate?**  
   It appears already in Florence, but no earlier textual or mythological source has been identified.

6. **Was the original office transformation, illusion, madness, or a combination?**  
   The French, Latin, and English branches distribute these ideas differently.

7. **Why do legion counts vary so radically?**  
   Scribal numeral confusion is possible, but twenty, twenty-five, and three do not form an obvious single corruption sequence.

8. **What was the original form of Ose’s character or seal?**  
   Earlier *Offices* manuscripts include spirit characters, but the later Goetic seal’s precise derivation has not been demonstrated.

9. **Is Voso present in an uncollated manuscript used by Mathers?**  
   Mathers may have seen a variant not yet identified, or he may have generated the reading editorially.

10. **How much of Harley 6483 is genuinely Rudd’s?**  
    The Smart copy preserves Rudd-attributed materials but is not an autograph.

11. **Can Otius be decisively separated from Oze through renewed Clm 849 collation?**  
    Existing comparative tables are inconsistent.

12. **Did Crowley intend 30 as numerology or textual emendation?**  
    His note gives a general rationale but cites no manuscript.

13. **Was there an Oze entry in the 1818 first edition of de Plancy in precisely the later form?**  
    The sixth-edition text is secure; a page-by-page collation of all editions would show when crown, emperor, and “per day” entered.

14. **Are there overlooked early vernacular witnesses?**  
    The discovery and edition of the Florence material in 2020 demonstrate that the pre-Weyer textual history remains open to revision.

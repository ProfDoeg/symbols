# Sabnock: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **Documented text** — stated in a surviving manuscript or printed primary source.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a historical or textual relationship argued from source comparison.
- **Tradition** — an attribution accepted within an occult or religious lineage but not independently demonstrable.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — a symbolic assignment made by a particular occult system.
- **Disputed** — plausible or repeated, but unsupported, contradictory, or textually uncertain.
- **Legend** — part of a source’s narrative world, not a historical claim.
- **Modern invention** — an attribute, story, or image unattested in the older source chain.

A quotation limitation prevents reproducing all three long entries in full. I therefore give short diplomatic excerpts, complete clause-by-clause accounts, precise locators, and links to the unrestricted primary texts.

# The name and its origins

## 1. Salmatis in the *Livre des esperitz*, fifteenth century

**Documented text.** The strongest pre-Weyer antecedent is not spelled *Sabnock* but **Salmatis**, spirit 22 in the French *Livre des esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29. The manuscript is generally assigned to the fifteenth century.

Its Middle French entry begins:

> “Salmatis est ung grant marquis qui appert en semblance dun chevalier arme…”

The rest says that Salmatis:

- is a great marquis;
- appears as an armed knight;
- can make a person assume whatever appearance is desired;
- builds fortresses, buildings, castles and towns when commanded;
- causes great wounds to appear in a person;
- commands fifty legions.

These are too many specific agreements to dismiss as coincidence. Rank, legion count, armed appearance, transformation, building works and wounds all recur in Weyer’s *Sabnac alias Salmac*. [*Livre des esperitz*, complete text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier), [manuscript page](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Livre_des_esperitz_-_MS_O.8.29.djvu/5).

**Scholarly reconstruction.** Jean-Patrice Boudet explicitly aligns **Salmatis C22** with **Sabnac/Salmac W34** in his comparative study of Renaissance demonological catalogues and their medieval ancestors. This is the best evidence for Sabnock’s pre-Weyer history. [Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

Consequently, the often-repeated statement that Sabnock first appeared in 1577 is too strong. The exact form *Sabnac* first appears in Weyer, but the character has a highly probable fifteenth-century ancestor under *Salmatis*.

## 2. Weyer: Sabnac alias Salmac, 1577

**Documented text.** Johann Weyer included the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* in the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. It was not part of the original 1563 edition.

Weyer’s Latin names the spirit:

> “Sabnac, aliàs Salmac, Marchio magnus & fortis…”

The entry is §34 in Joseph Peterson’s complete enumeration. In the original 1577 pagination it occurs around p. 923. Its clauses state:

- Sabnac, otherwise Salmac, is a great and strong marquis.
- It comes as an armed soldier.
- Its head is a lion’s.
- It rides a pale horse.
- It changes a human being’s form in a marvellous manner.
- It builds great towers full of arms, together with *castra* and cities.
- At the exorcist’s command it inflicts putrid, worm-infested wounds for thirty days.
- It procures good familiars.
- It rules fifty legions.

The Latin *castra* can mean camps, fortified camps or forts; Scot’s “castels” and the later *Goetia*’s “Castles” narrow the word toward permanent architecture. [Weyer, Latin text and critical notes](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Weyer’s purpose

**Documented historical context.** Weyer was not promoting demon worship. He presented the catalogue as material allegedly used by a magician and embedded it in a larger work opposing the persecution of supposed witches. The contradiction is central to the work’s reception: a sceptical or polemical publication became one of the chief textual reservoirs of later ceremonial magic.

## 3. Scot: Sabnacke alias Salmac, 1584

**Documented text.** Reginald Scot incorporated an English translation of Weyer’s catalogue in book XV, chapter II of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (London: William Brome, 1584). The translation is often attributed to Abraham Fleming, though the exact division of responsibility remains debated.

The name becomes:

> “Sabnacke [Sabnac], alias Salmac, is a great marquesse and a strong…”

Scot reproduces every substantive part of Weyer’s entry:

- armed soldier;
- lion’s head;
- pale horse;
- marvellous change of a man’s “forme and favor”;
- high towers full of weapons;
- castles and cities;
- thirty days of rotten, maggot-filled wounds;
- good familiars;
- fifty legions.

“Favor” here means countenance or appearance, not kindness. Scot therefore understood Weyer’s transformation office as alteration of bodily form and face. [Peterson’s parallel Weyer–Scot text](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [Cornell facsimile of the 1584 *Discoverie*](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081).

Like Weyer, Scot printed magical material in a predominantly sceptical work. The later *Goetia* nevertheless appears to depend heavily on Scot’s English wording.

## 4. From Salmatis to Salmac to Sabnac

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The documentary sequence supports:

**Salmatis → Salmac → Sabnac/Sabnacke → Sabnach/Sabnack/Sabnock**

The exact scribal steps cannot be reconstructed because the intermediary copies are missing. What can be said is that Weyer preserves both a form close to the later name, *Sabnac*, and an alias, *Salmac*, closer to medieval *Salmatis*.

### Is Salmac related to Salmacis?

**Disputed.** *Salmac* superficially resembles **Salmacis**, the spring and nymph in Ovid’s story of Hermaphroditus. Because Salmatis/Sabnac also changes human appearance, the resemblance is tempting. No consulted manuscript identifies the spirit with Salmacis, however, and the specialist comparison literature does not establish the derivation. At present it is a possible word-association, not an etymology.

### Proposed Hebrew derivations

**Modern invention or unsupported conjecture.** Online sources variously derive *Sabnock* from:

- a supposed Hebrew *sabak*, “to weave”;
- *saba*, interpreted as “army” or “host”;
- invented combinations involving night, injury or fortification;
- alleged Latin elements meaning sword or harm.

No historical source in the chain gives a Hebrew spelling or explanation of this kind. Some of the alleged roots are incorrectly defined, and none explains the documented medieval form *Salmatis*. Rudd’s later transliteration of *Sabnock* into Hebrew letters is not proof that the name originated in Hebrew.

### Ancient deity, hero or place

**Negative finding.** No credible ancient god, hero, demon or place lies behind Sabnock in the way that:

- Bael reflects *Baal*;
- Astaroth reflects Astarte/Ashtoreth;
- Asmoday reflects Ashmedai/Asmodeus;
- Belial reflects Hebrew *bĕliyyaʿal*;
- Phenex reflects the Greek-Latin phoenix.

Sabnock’s best-supported history is internal to the medieval and early-modern *Offices of Spirits* tradition. Claims connecting it to Sekhmet, Sabaoth, a “Lion Gate,” extraterrestrial Lyrians or a lost pagan war god are modern syncretism.

## Sloane 3825

**Documented text.** Joseph Peterson uses Sloane MS 3825 as his main text. The entry opens:

> “The 43d spirit… is called Sabnach he is a Mighty great Marquiz…”

It describes:

- a mighty, great and strong marquis;
- an armed soldier with a lion’s head;
- riding a pale-coloured horse;
- building high towers, castles and cities;
- furnishing them with armour;
- afflicting people for several days with wounds and rotten sores full of worms;
- giving good familiars at the exorcist’s command;
- commanding fifty legions;
- a seal to be made and worn as a lamen.

[Peterson, online *Goetia*, Sabnach entry](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

### Changes from Weyer and Scot

**Documented textual development.**

1. **Transformation disappears.** The *Goetia* does not say that Sabnock changes human form.
2. **Thirty days becomes indefinite.** “Thirty days” becomes “several days” in the manuscripts and “many days” in Mathers–Crowley.
3. **The seal is added.** Weyer and Scot provide no Sabnock seal.
4. **The numbered Solomon/brass-vessel frame is added.**
5. *Castra* has settled into English “castles.”
6. Weyer’s towers “full of arms” become towers and cities “furnished with armour.”

## Sloane 2731 and Sloane 3648

Peterson’s collation records:

- Sloane 2731: **Sabnack**, with **Sabnock** in another copy or hand.
- Sloane 3648: **Sabnock**.
- Sloane 3825: **Sabnach**.

The substance remains stable. These manuscripts do not restore the lost transformation office. [Peterson’s name-comparison chart](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

## Harley 6483 and the Rudd recension

**Documented text.** Harley MS 6483, dated 1712–13, uses **Sabnock** and the ordinary *Goetia* description. Skinner and Rankine print the entry on p. 145 of *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*. Its distinctive addition is not a new demonic office but the controlling angel **Vevaliah/Veuliah**, its seal and Psalm.

**Tradition, with disputed authorship.** The manuscript belongs to a group copied by Peter Smart and attributed within the collection to “Dr Rudd.” Skinner and Rankine identify the material with the mathematician and military engineer Thomas Rudd. Harley 6483 is not demonstrably an autograph by Rudd, however, and its surviving copy postdates his death by decades. “Rudd’s scheme” is therefore convenient shorthand for the recension transmitted under his name, not proof that Thomas Rudd personally wrote every part.

[Peterson’s manuscript description](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm), [Skinner and Rankine transcription](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

## Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The 1904 printed entry begins:

> “SABNOCK.—The Forty-third Spirit… is called Sabnock, or Savnok.”

It calls him a mighty, great and strong Marquis and retains the lion-headed soldier, pale horse, fortifications, armour, worm-filled sores, good familiars and fifty legions. It says “many days,” not Weyer’s thirty.

The entry is on printed p. 36 in the pagination reproduced by Sacred Texts; some scans count the preliminary leaves differently. [Sacred Texts, p. 36](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm), [1904 facsimile PDF](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/crowley_-_goetia.pdf).

**Editorial finding.** *Savnok* is not reported by Peterson from Sloane 3825, 3648, 2731, Harley 6483 or Weyer. It becomes prominent through Mathers–Crowley and is probably an editorial reading or normalization rather than an ancient alias.

## Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* was published by Weiser in 2001. His main reading is **Sabnach**, following Sloane 3825, while his apparatus records:

- Sloane 2731: Sabnack/Sabnock;
- Sloane 3648 and Harley 6483: Sabnock;
- Mathers–Crowley: Sabnock or Savnok;
- Weyer: Sabnac alias Salmac;
- Scot: Sabnacke alias Salmac.

The Sabnach entry appears around pp. 76–77 in the 2001 text, depending on printing. [Peterson PDF](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf), [publisher record](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/).

## Rank and planet

**Documented esoteric prescription.** The *Goetia*’s “Observations” explicitly assigns:

- kings: Sun/gold;
- marquises: **Moon/silver**;
- dukes: Venus/copper;
- prelates or princes: Jupiter/tin;
- knights: Saturn/lead;
- presidents: Mercury/mercury;
- earls: Venus and Moon, copper and silver.

Thus Sabnock’s rank implies **Luna and silver**. Mars and iron may appear intuitively appropriate to a soldier and fortification spirit, but they are not the *Goetia*’s rank correspondence. [*Goetia*, “Observations”](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks06.htm).

## Hours and lunar timing

The same source says:

- Marquises may be bound from **3 p.m. until 9 p.m.**
- They may also be bound from **9 p.m. until sunrise**.
- The Moon should preferably be 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 14 days old.

**Later extrapolation.** Monday and a planetary hour of the Moon are logical developments from the lunar rank, but the *Goetia* does not prescribe “Monday only” for Sabnock. The explicit instructions are the marquis hours and lunar-age rule.

## Offices Sabnock does not possess

**Negative finding.** The primary Sabnock entries do not assign:

- languages;
- liberal sciences;
- rhetoric;
- hidden treasure;
- love or sexual attraction;
- invisibility;
- alchemical transmutation;
- truth-telling or universal answers;
- reconciliation;
- divination.

Such powers belong to other spirits or to modern syncretic profiles. Sabnock’s documented offices are narrower: transformation in the oldest branch, fortification, armament, wounds and familiars.

## Second form

**Negative finding.** Several Goetic entries explicitly allow or demand a more acceptable human form after the first manifestation. Sabnock’s entry does not. The lion-headed soldier on a pale horse is the only individual appearance given. General conjurations request a fair and comely manifestation, but that ritual language should not be converted into a Sabnock-specific “second form.”

## The seventy-two triplets

**Documented Jewish tradition.** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three consecutive verses of seventy-two Hebrew letters each in the traditional consonantal text. The method writes:

1. verse 19 forward;
2. verse 20 backward;
3. verse 21 forward;

then reads vertically, producing seventy-two three-letter combinations.

Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a describes this boustrophedon construction and gives examples including VHV and ANI. It is firm medieval evidence for the method. [Rashi on Sukkah 45a](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Sukkah.45a.2).

**Historical qualification.** *Shem ha-meforash* means the explicit, distinguished or fully articulated Name. In early rabbinic literature it does not always mean this particular list of seventy-two angelic beings. The equation “Shem ha-Mephorash = seventy-two guardian angels” is a medieval and early-modern development.

## Veuliah’s letters

Angel 43 is generated from the corresponding letters of the three Exodus verses:

**ו + ו + ל = VVL**

Christian Kabbalists and practical-magical writers expanded triplets with divine endings such as **-el** or **-iah**, yielding forms including **Veuliah/Veualiah**.

**Important distinction.** “The dominating King” is an occult attribute attached to the name. It is not a straightforward Hebrew dictionary translation of the bare letters VVL.

## Sefer Raziel

**Tradition with layered textual history.** The *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* became an important carrier of angel names, divine names and magical cosmology. It is not a single book written in one ancient moment. Scholarship identifies late-antique or early-medieval materials, thirteenth-century Ashkenazi strata associated with Eleazar of Worms, and later Sephardi or Byzantine additions. The commentary devoted to the seventy-two names may be among the later layers, perhaps fourteenth century.

The famous printed Amsterdam edition dates to 1701. Claims that its entire contents were delivered intact to Adam by the angel Raziel are the book’s sacred legend, not a historical dating. [Study of the textual layers of *Sefer Raziel*](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2017/html?lang=en).

## Reuchlin

**Documented Christian-Kabbalistic reception.** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped transmit the seventy-two triplets into Christian Kabbalah. Adding *-el* or *-iah* turned divine-letter combinations into pronounceable angelic names. [Digitized 1517 *De arte cabalistica*](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105).

## Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54) printed an influential table of the seventy-two names, their angelic expansions and associated material. Kircher’s diagram became a major bridge between Christian Kabbalah and later French occultism. [Kircher’s seventy-two-name table](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg).

## Rudd’s controlling angels

**Documented in the Rudd recension.** Harley 6483 aligns the seventy-two Goetic spirits and the seventy-two Shem angels by identical ordinal number:

**43 Sabnock ↔ 43 Vevaliah/Veuliah**

This pairing is not found in Weyer, Scot or the ordinary Sloane *Goetia* text. It is an innovation of the Rudd recension or its immediate milieu.

Skinner and Rankine explain the operative arrangement: the demon’s seal is worn with the corresponding Shem angel on the reverse, the angelic side facing the magician’s heart. The angel is intended to protect the operator from the summoned spirit’s malice and to constrain it through a superior divine authority. [Skinner and Rankine, introduction and Sabnock entry](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

### Psalm

Harley 6483 gives:

> “Et ego ad te Domine clamavi…”

The full verse means that the speaker has cried to the Lord and that the morning prayer comes before him.

The apparent citation disagreement is mainly numbering:

- **Vulgate:** Psalm 87:14.
- **Masoretic/KJV-style numbering:** Psalm 88:13.
- Some modern angel books print **88:14**, apparently through alternate counting or inherited citation error.

Rudd’s own citation is Vulgate **87:14**; Ambelain gives **88:13**.

## Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* systematized the seventy-two angels into:

- five-degree zodiacal zones;
- approximately five-day calendar rulerships;
- twenty-minute daily intervals;
- Psalm verses;
- moral characteristics;
- beneficial offices;
- contrary or adverse geniuses.

For Veuliah, Lenain gives the title *Roi dominateur* and attributes concerning victory over enemies, release from servitude, the peace and prosperity of empires, stability of thrones and success in martial careers. The contrary spirit promotes discord between rulers, revolution and the fall of states. [Lenain, 1823 scan](https://archive.org/download/BIUSante_ms05365/BIUSante_ms05365.pdf).

**Esoteric correspondence.** These are Lenain’s occult doctrines. They are not contained in Exodus and should not be projected backward into ancient Judaism.

## Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced and popularized much of the French Kabbalistic angel material in *La Cabbale* (1903). A 1909 edition of Lenain also carried a Papus preface. Papus therefore transmitted rather than originated most of Veuliah’s standard nineteenth-century profile.

## Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn incorporated the seventy-two angels into its astrological and magical tables. Each angel occupies five zodiacal degrees, producing two angels per ten-degree decan.

In that system:

- Veuliah rules the first quinance of Scorpio’s first decan.
- Yelahiah rules the second.
- Both consequently fall under the tarot **Five of Cups**, Mars in Scorpio.

The Golden Dawn’s synthesis joined Jewish and Christian Kabbalistic names, decan astrology, tarot, planetary magic and Enochian tables. It should be treated as a late nineteenth-century synthesis, not one continuous ancient scheme.

## Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reprinted and elaborated the Lenain system. For Veualiah it gives:

- degrees 211–215;
- 0°–5° Scorpio by modern zodiac notation;
- traditional title “Dominating King”;
- Psalm 88:13;
- a twenty-minute period around 2:00–2:20 p.m.;
- scattered calendar days derived from repeated annual cycles;
- martial and political virtues;
- an adverse genius of discord and revolution.

[English study reproducing Ambelain’s table](https://utahsricf.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/SHEMHAMPHORASH-ASTROILOGY-PAPER-PRINT-VERSION-SRICF.pdf).

## Haziel

**Modern popular angelology.** The French author writing as Haziel popularized the seventy-two angels as personal guardian angels assigned by date of birth, with devotional prayers, personality profiles and practical petitions. Haziel’s Veuliah material descends substantially from Lenain, Papus and Ambelain but often expands it into individualized psychology and devotional promises.

Those personality descriptions are twentieth-century elaborations. They are not found in Exodus, Rashi, Weyer, the *Goetia* or Harley 6483.

## Crowley’s *777*

**Documented esoteric correspondence.** In *Liber 777*, Sabnock is placed among the Goetic demons assigned to the **night or nocturnal half** of a decan. Its line is the first decan of Gemini:

- 0°–10° Gemini;
- approximately May 21–31 in the printed occult calendar;
- Eight of Swords;
- Jupiter in Gemini;
- day spirit: Amon;
- night spirit: Sabnock.

This is explicitly reproduced in DuQuette, Hyatt and Wilson’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*. [Facsimile of *Liber 777*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777.pdf), [*Illustrated Goetia*, Sabnock table](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf).

The Eight of Swords is called **Interference** in Crowley’s *Book of Thoth*. Its decan planet is Jupiter in Gemini. [Crowley, “The Small Cards”](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/small-cards).

### Why two spirits occupy one pip

Thirty-six tarot pip cards cover the thirty-six decans, while the Goetia has seventy-two spirits. The Golden Dawn/Crowley solution assigns two spirits to each decan:

- one diurnal;
- one nocturnal.

This produces two Goetic spirits per pip card. Sabnock is the nocturnal member of Amon–Sabnock’s Gemini decan.

### Why Veuliah gives a different card

The seventy-two Shem angels are mapped by **five-degree quinances**, not by the Goetic day/night decan list. Veuliah’s ordinal place gives 0°–5° Scorpio and hence the Five of Cups. Nothing in manuscript history requires Sabnock itself to be Scorpio merely because its Rudd counter-angel is Veuliah.

Modern tables frequently collapse these systems and give Sabnock either:

- Gemini/Eight of Swords;
- Scorpio/Five of Cups;
- or both without explanation.

The historically accurate presentation is that they are different correspondential layers.

## Enochian overlays

**Later esoteric overlay.** Golden Dawn and Crowley tables place Goetic spirits, zodiacal signs, Hebrew paths, tarot, divine names and Enochian material in parallel columns. This creates symbolic relationships through the shared table.

No Enochian name, aethyr, tablet square or Watchtower attribution appears in Sabnock’s original catalogue entry. John Dee and Edward Kelley’s surviving Enochian records do not name Sabnock. Any “Enochian Sabnock” formula is a Golden Dawn or later tabular synthesis, not a sixteenth-century Dee record.

## Ritual timing

The historically layered timing rules are:

1. **Goetia:** Moon aged 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 14 days; marquis hours 3 p.m.–9 p.m. and 9 p.m.–sunrise.
2. **Planetary extrapolation:** Monday or an hour of the Moon because marquises correspond to Luna.
3. **Crowley/Golden Dawn decan:** first decan Gemini; Sabnock as its nocturnal Goetic spirit.
4. **Lenain angelic timing:** Veuliah’s five-degree Scorpio zone, annual five-day rulership and daily twenty-minute period.
5. **Modern practice:** combinations of lunar hour, Gemini decan and Veuliah’s Scorpio quinance. These combinations are syntheses rather than instructions from one historical text.

# Iconography

## The manuscript seal

**Documented text and image.** Weyer and Scot give no seal. The Sabnock seal enters the surviving record in the *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

In the familiar Mathers–Crowley form, its main visual structure is:

- a shallow, rounded horizontal cap or arch;
- a descending near-central stem;
- a right-hand terminal that bends or curls inward;
- smaller angular and hooked marks beneath the main stroke.

That description cannot substitute for the drawing: the exact identity of a grimoire seal lies in line junctions, direction and proportion.

The manuscripts preserve variants of the same general family:

- Sloane 3825 accompanies **Sabnach** with the main seal used by Peterson.
- Sloane 3648 uses **Sabnock** and has minor differences of curvature and terminal shape.
- Sloane 2731’s form varies in small lower strokes and copyist execution.
- Harley 6483 gives a carefully drawn Sabnock seal together with Vevaliah’s controlling material.
- Mathers–Crowley regularized the design for print.

No source describes these differences verbally; they must be compared as images. Claims that one tiny curl is the uniquely “authentic” version go beyond the evidence. [Peterson’s manuscript entry](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm), [Mathers–Crowley seal in vector form](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:43-sabnock-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg).

## Rudd’s double-sided lamen

**Documented operative arrangement.** In the Rudd recension, the demon’s character is borne on one face and the appropriate Shem angel on the reverse, facing the magician’s heart. Thus the seal is not merely an identifier but part of an angelically controlled hierarchy.

This is a material expression of Rudd’s theory: the corresponding angel protects the operator and restricts the demon.

## Collin de Plancy

Collin de Plancy included **Sabnac ou Salmac** in the *Dictionnaire infernal*. The first edition appeared in 1818; the famous sixth edition of 1863 contained Louis Le Breton’s demon designs, generally engraved by M. Jarrault.

The 1863 Sabnac entry, p. 590, calls him:

- a great infernal marquis;
- the demon of fortifications;
- an armed soldier with a lion’s head;
- mounted on a hideous horse;
- able to turn human beings into stones;
- an astonishing builder of towers;
- commander of fifty legions.

The short description begins:

> “Sabnac ou Salmac, grand marquis infernal, démon des fortifications.”

[De Plancy, 1863 Sabnac entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Sabnac), [complete 1863 PDF](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e0/Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf).

### De Plancy’s alterations

**Documented reception history.**

- Weyer’s pale horse becomes a **hideous** horse.
- General transformation becomes the specific power of turning people into **stones**.
- Fortification becomes Sabnac’s defining epithet.
- Wounds, worms and familiars disappear from the dictionary notice.

The petrification story is therefore not in Weyer, Scot or the *Goetia*. It is de Plancy’s interpretation or invention.

### Is there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**Negative finding.** The 1863 edition contains the textual Sabnac entry, but no securely identified dedicated Louis Le Breton portrait of Sabnac was located in the adjacent page or standard indexed corpus of Breton demon engravings. Modern webpages often pair the entry with:

- the Goetic seal;
- a newly drawn lion-headed knight;
- generic Le Breton imagery;
- AI-generated art.

Those should not be described as de Plancy’s portrait without a plate number or scan citation.

## Modern art

**Modern invention.** Modern depictions commonly add:

- plate armour resembling late-medieval or fantasy armour;
- diseased or skeletal horses;
- castle battlements growing from the body;
- blue skin or blue wings;
- female or androgynous forms;
- plague imagery;
- weapons absent from the text;
- Scorpio, Gemini, lunar or tarot symbols.

The older sources specify neither sex nor skin colour. Grammatical “he” in English editions reflects the convention applied to spirits. Female Sabnock designs and blue-winged forms belong to modern demonolatry, fantasy art and game design.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer is the first printer of **Sabnac alias Salmac**. His entry adds the lion’s head, pale horse, thirty-day duration, verminous wounds and familiars to the simpler Salmatis description.

Historically, Weyer argued against executing alleged witches. His catalogue was framed as exposure of learned magical pretensions, even though later magicians treated it as an authoritative spirit register.

## Reginald Scot and possibly Abraham Fleming

Scot transmitted Weyer’s catalogue to English readers in 1584. The close verbal dependence of the *Goetia* on this English version is among the strongest clues to the *Goetia* catalogue’s immediate source.

The omission of Pruflas in Scot’s transmitted list became consequential: the *Goetia* also omitted Pruflas.

## The compiler of the *Lemegeton*

The compiler is unknown. The work is a seventeenth-century English compilation, not a book demonstrably written by Solomon. Its Goetic catalogue combines:

- the Weyer–Scot spirit list;
- seals;
- ritual material indebted to the *Heptameron* and related conjuration traditions;
- the brass-vessel legend;
- additional English spirit material.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

The Rudd manuscripts are associated with Thomas Rudd by attribution, while Peter Smart appears as copyist of the surviving early eighteenth-century material.

**Disputed authorship.** The system may preserve Rudd’s work, later work organized under his name, or a mixture. What is certain is that Harley 6483 contains the numbered angel–demon pairing.

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers prepared the manuscript-based edition that Crowley published in 1904. His standardized forms, including *Sabnock or Savnok*, became the default twentieth-century text.

## Aleister Crowley

Crowley published Mathers’s text, added the “Preliminary Invocation,” an interpretive essay and other editorial material, and later incorporated the Goetic spirits into *777*.

Crowley’s essay interprets demons psychologically—as portions or complexes of the human mind—rather than simply as independent medieval devils. That is Crowley’s occult psychology, not the theory expressed by Weyer or the manuscripts.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition restored a manuscript-based text of all five parts of the *Lemegeton* and supplied systematic variant readings. His online edition remains the most accessible source for distinguishing Sabnach, Sabnock, Sabnack, Sabnac and Salmac.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their *Goetia of Dr Rudd* first appeared in 2007 and made Harley 6483’s controlling-angel scheme broadly accessible. Their Sabnock entry gives Vevaliah and the Psalm and illustrates the angelically backed lamen.

## Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* and Church of the Hermetic Sciences material helped popularize modern operative Goetia, including mirror or “dark mirror” evocation and a Master Mandala arrangement.

Sabnock appears in Runyon’s tables, but no source located documents a uniquely important Sabnock operation by Runyon. His relevance is to twentieth-century Goetic method generally.

## Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt and David P. Wilson

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* reproduces the Sabnock entry and explicitly prints:

- 0°–10° Gemini;
- night;
- May 21–31;
- Eight of Swords;
- Luna–silver.

It is an important modern witness to how the *777* system was attached to individual Goetic entries.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Geosophia*, *The True Grimoire* and related writing strongly influenced modern historical discussion of the spirit-catalogue and *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* traditions.

No Sabnock-specific etymology or operation by Stratton-Kent was verified. He is relevant to the broader reconstruction, while Boudet supplies the direct Salmatis–Sabnac comparison.

## Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra and Julien Véronèse

These scholars provide broader historical context for grimoires, learned magic and medieval spirit catalogues. They should not be cited as though each had proposed a special Sabnock theory. Boudet’s comparative table is the direct scholarly source for Salmatis.

## Frater Achad

**Negative finding.** No Sabnock-specific writing or operation by Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, was identified. Modern digital *777* interfaces sometimes allow an “Achad” tarot ordering while displaying Sabnock, but that software option is not evidence of an Achad–Sabnock tradition.

# Grimoire ancestors

## *Livre des esperitz*

**Direct textual ancestor.** Salmatis is the closest presently known predecessor and establishes that the character existed before Weyer.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer identified his source tradition with an *Officium spirituum*, an “Office of Spirits.” Several late-medieval and Renaissance catalogues circulated under titles of this type.

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The *Livre des esperitz*, Weyer’s catalogue, parts of the *Book of Oberon* and the later *Goetia* belong to this broad textual family. The missing intermediaries probably account for Salmatis becoming Sabnac/Salmac.

## Munich handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook is an important witness to learned ritual magic and spirit conjuration.

**Negative finding.** No secure Sabnock/Salmatis cognate has been identified there. It is a contextual ancestor of the genre, not a direct attestation of this spirit.

## *Book of Oberon*

Folger MS V.b.26, often called the *Book of Oberon*, contains extensive sixteenth-century spirit material related to the *Offices of Spirits* family.

No direct Sabnock cognate as strong as *Salmatis* has been demonstrated. Its importance is evidence that multiple English and continental catalogues were circulating before the *Lemegeton* was compiled.

## *Book of Abramelin*

**Negative finding.** Abramelin’s lists contain many spirit names and variant spellings, but no persuasive Sabnock/Salmatis identification was found. Similar rank or military imagery is not sufficient evidence.

## *Testament of Solomon*

The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* supplies the larger legend of Solomon commanding demons through a divinely empowered ring and forcing them to work on the Temple.

It does not name Sabnock or Salmatis and does not contain the later seventy-two-spirit Goetic catalogue. It is a conceptual ancestor of Solomonic demon-command literature, not a direct textual source for Sabnock.

# Order and number: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

## Sabnock’s movement

- *Livre des esperitz*: Salmatis no. 22.
- Weyer: Sabnac/Salmac no. 34 when Pruflas is retained.
- Scot: the unnumbered English sequence effectively shifts after the omitted Pruflas.
- *Goetia*: Sabnock/Sabnach no. 43.

Weyer’s original entries were prose headings rather than a modern numbered table, so some comparison charts display Sabnac as 33 after suppressing Pruflas. “Weyer 34” is the conventional full-list numbering.

## How 69 became 72

The *Goetia* uses Weyer’s 69 spirits but:

- omits **Pruflas**;
- adds **Vassago** near the beginning;
- adds **Seere**, **Dantalion** and **Andromalius** at the end.

Thus:

**69 − 1 + 4 = 72**

The four additions are:

1. Vassago, Goetia 3;
2. Seere, Goetia 70;
3. Dantalion, Goetia 71;
4. Andromalius, Goetia 72.

The entire middle order was also extensively rearranged. Sabnock consequently moves from conventional Weyer 34 to Goetia 43.

**Scholarly inference.** Seventy-two was probably an attractive target because of existing Solomonic and Kabbalistic symbolism. The brass-vessel legend and Rudd’s later one-to-one Shem pairing both depend on a complete 72-member scheme. It does not follow that Weyer secretly intended the Shem angels.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. “Sabnock has no pre-Weyer source”

**Corrected.** The exact spelling *Sabnock* has no known pre-Weyer attestation, but **Salmatis** in the fifteenth-century *Livre des esperitz* is a compelling textual ancestor. The match includes six independent features, including the unusual count of fifty legions.

## 2. Is Salmatis definitely Sabnock?

**Strong scholarly reconstruction, not identity declared by the manuscripts.** Neither source says “Salmatis later became Sabnac.” The conclusion rests on comparative textual evidence. Because the agreements are unusually detailed, it is considerably stronger than a resemblance of names alone.

## 3. The etymology

**Unresolved.** No secure Hebrew, Latin, Greek or ancient Near Eastern derivation exists. The name may be a chain of scribal corruptions whose earliest surviving form is *Salmatis*.

## 4. Salmacis

**Disputed.** The Greek nymph Salmacis provides a name resemblance and a transformation theme, but no documentary bridge. It remains a hypothesis.

## 5. Saburac

A. E. Waite printed **Sabnack, alias Saburac** and numbered it 42 in one arrangement.

**Probable corruption.** *Saburac* is unsupported by Weyer’s *Salmac*, Scot, the principal Sloane manuscripts and Harley 6483. It looks like a misreading that became an apparent new name through print repetition.

## 6. Savnok

**Editorial variant.** *Savnok* is familiar from Mathers–Crowley but absent from the principal older witnesses in Peterson’s apparatus. It should be dated to the modern printed recension unless an earlier manuscript witness emerges.

## 7. Sabnock versus Salmac as two spirits

**Unsupported conflation or splitting.** Weyer explicitly presents *Salmac* as an alias of Sabnac. The medieval Salmatis comparison reinforces unity. Modern lists that make Sabnock and Salmac separate entities are creating a distinction absent from the source chain.

## 8. The rank planet: Moon or Mars?

- **Moon/silver:** explicit *Goetia* rank rule for a Marquis.
- **Mars:** symbolic inference from soldier, armour, wounds and fortification.
- **Jupiter:** ruler of the Gemini decan in the Eight of Swords assignment.
- **Mars in Scorpio:** tarot ruler of Veuliah’s Five of Cups quinance.

All four appear in modern tables because they answer different questions. Only Moon/silver is Sabnock’s direct rank correspondence in the *Goetia*.

## 9. Transformation or no transformation?

- Salmatis: yes.
- Weyer: yes.
- Scot: yes.
- ordinary *Goetia*: omitted.
- de Plancy: transformed into petrification.
- modern profiles: inconsistently restored.

It is wrong to call transformation an invention, but equally wrong to say the standard *Goetia* entry expressly grants it.

## 10. Thirty days or many days?

- Weyer and Scot: **thirty days**.
- Sloane manuscript tradition: **several days**.
- Mathers–Crowley: **many days**.

The weakening is a real textual change, probably caused by copying or editorial smoothing.

## 11. Pale horse or hideous horse?

- Weyer, Scot and the *Goetia*: pale or pale-coloured.
- De Plancy and Hugo: hideous.

“Hideous” belongs to nineteenth-century reception, not the early source.

## 12. Does Sabnock teach architecture?

**Modern extrapolation.** The texts say it builds fortifications and cities. They do not explicitly say it teaches architecture, engineering or masonry. That interpretation is reasonable symbolism but should not be quoted as the original office.

## 13. Is Sabnock a protective spirit?

**Modern reinterpretation.** Older texts emphasize building defences but also inflicting disease and wounds. Contemporary demonolatry often reframes the entire office as protection, boundary-setting and psychic defence. That is a modern devotional reading.

## 14. Gender

**Undetermined in primary iconography.** Latin and English grammatical masculinity produce “he.” No text gives a biological sex. Female Sabnock traditions are modern.

## 15. The “enn”

The formula **“Tasa Sabnock on ca lirach”** circulates in modern demonolatry.

**Modern invention.** It is absent from Salmatis, Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, Rudd, Mathers–Crowley and de Plancy. Such “enns” belong to late twentieth- or early twenty-first-century demonolatry, particularly material associated with S. Connolly and related communities.

## 16. Angel pairing

**Rudd tradition, not original Goetia.** Veuliah is correctly paired with Sabnock in Harley 6483’s ordinal scheme. The ordinary *Ars Goetia* does not contain that pairing, and Weyer could not have intended it in its present form because his list has sixty-nine spirits.

## 17. Veuliah’s meaning

“Dominating King” is a documented traditional epithet. It should not be presented as a certain philological translation of VVL. The name consists of extracted letters plus a theophoric termination.

## 18. Psalm numbering

The difference between Psalm 87:14 and 88:13 is substantially a Vulgate-versus-Hebrew numbering difference. The occasional 88:14 citation is less easily justified and may be inherited error.

## 19. Rudd’s authorship

Harley 6483 is dated 1712–13 and copied within the Peter Smart manuscript group. Attribution to Thomas Rudd is traditional and defended by Skinner and Rankine, but direct autograph proof is lacking.

## 20. De Plancy portrait

A textual entry is certain. A dedicated Louis Le Breton Sabnac engraving is not. Images labeled “de Plancy’s Sabnock” require verification against the 1863 scan.

# Open questions

1. **What was Salmatis’s still earlier source?** No pre-fifteenth-century occurrence has been securely identified.

2. **How exactly did *Salmatis* become *Salmac* and *Sabnac*?** The intermediary *Officium spirituum* copies are lost or unidentified.

3. **Was *Salmatis* itself a corruption of a meaningful name such as Salmacis?** The resemblance exists, but documentary evidence does not.

4. **Which surviving manuscript preserves the earliest Sabnock seal?** The relative dating and dependence of the early *Lemegeton* witnesses remains complicated.

5. **How much of Harley 6483 derives from Thomas Rudd personally?** The attribution, redaction and Peter Smart’s role need firmer palaeographical and archival study.

6. **Where did the precise Veuliah Psalm and ethical offices first enter the Christian-Kabbalistic tables?** Kircher, Rudd and later French authors preserve related systems, but the complete transmission deserves a dedicated stemma.

7. **Why did the *Goetia* omit transformation?** It may be accidental abridgment or deliberate editorial narrowing.

8. **What generated *Savnok* and *Saburac*?** Both appear to be printed variants, but their exact exemplars or typesetting histories remain uncertain.

9. **Did de Plancy consciously invent petrification, or inherit it from an unidentified intermediary?** No earlier source located here gives that specific ability.

10. **Can an authenticated Le Breton Sabnac plate be found?** None was verified in the 1863 edition’s indexed illustration corpus.

# Conclusions

The fullest recoverable history begins not in 1577 but with **Salmatis**, a fifteenth-century French great marquis with fifty legions who appears as an armed knight, transforms people, raises fortified places and causes wounds. Weyer’s **Sabnac alias Salmac** is almost certainly a descendant of this figure. Weyer added the profile’s best-known images: lion head, pale horse, weapon-filled towers, thirty-day verminous wounds and good familiars.

Scot translated that description into English. The seventeenth-century *Goetia* reorganized the spirit as number 43, added a seal and Solomonic brass-vessel frame, dropped transformation and weakened “thirty days” to “several” or “many.” Harley 6483 then placed Veuliah, the forty-third Shem angel, over Sabnock as its restraining counterpart.

Two later astrological identities must remain separate:

- Sabnock in Crowley’s Goetic table is the **night spirit of 0°–10° Gemini**, associated with the **Eight of Swords**.
- Veuliah in the angelic quinance system governs **0°–5° Scorpio**, associated through later tarot correspondence with the **Five of Cups**.

No ancient deity behind Sabnock has been demonstrated. Its most credible origin is textual and scribal, within the medieval *Offices of Spirits* tradition. Much of the contemporary personality—protective guardian, architect-teacher, female warrior, blue-winged demon, bearer of an “enn,” or Scorpio entity in its own right—is modern reinterpretation rather than the content of the early sources.

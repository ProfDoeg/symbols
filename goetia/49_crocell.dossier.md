# Crocell: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Documented text]** — stated by a primary manuscript or printed source.
- **[Textual reconstruction]** — inference from comparison of witnesses.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a modern historical or philological argument.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an attribution made within an occult system, not an independently verifiable property.
- **[Tradition]** — received religious, magical, or legendary material.
- **[Disputed]** — a claim for which sources conflict or evidence is inadequate.
- **[Modern adaptation]** — fiction, games, art, or recent practitioner lore.
- **[Negative finding]** — something specifically searched for but not found in the relevant source chain.

The complete primary descriptions are linked below. Short identifying extracts are quoted here, followed by exhaustive clause-by-clause accounts of their contents.

# The name and its origins

## 1. The probable pre-Weyer precursor: Pucel/Bucal

### Early-sixteenth-century *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Documented text]** The earliest presently identifiable relative of Crocell occurs in the French *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Trinity College Cambridge MS O.8.29. Older literature sometimes calls the manuscript fifteenth-century, but Trinity’s current catalogue dates the relevant section—folios 179–182—to the **early sixteenth century**. The text is therefore pre-Weyer, but not securely medieval in its surviving copy. [Trinity College manuscript catalogue](https://mss-cat.trin.cam.ac.uk/Manuscript/O.8.29)

The opening roster calls the spirit:

> “De pucel duc”

Yet the corresponding final description is headed **Bucal**. It says that Bucal is a great duke, appears in an angelic form, gives true answers to questions, and causes great waters or abysses to appear in the air even though there are none. It assigns **28 legions**. [Digital text and facsimile transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/livre-esperitz/read/)

This relationship is important:

- Pucel/Bucal is a duke.
- It appears as an angel.
- It creates illusory or aerial waters where no water exists.
- It answers questions.
- Its position at the end of the surviving catalogue corresponds to a spirit subsequently called Pucel by Weyer.

**[Textual reconstruction]** These agreements make Pucel/Bucal the strongest known precursor of Weyer's Pucel. The inconsistency between the roster’s **Pucel** and the descriptive heading **Bucal** is most economically explained as scribal corruption, misreading, or the imperfect joining of a roster and a descriptive catalogue. It cannot be proved whether *Pucel* or *Bucal* was the earlier form.

Jean-Patrice Boudet’s comparison found substantial overlap—approximately thirty-five common spirits—between the *Livre des Esperitz* and Renaissance spirit catalogues. This establishes a textual family, though not necessarily direct copying from the surviving Trinity manuscript. [Boudet, “Les *who’s who* démonologiques…”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1019)

### Earliest-attestation conclusion

**[Finding]** Crocell does have a plausible pre-1577 attestation, but not under the exact spelling **Crocell**. The sequence presently reconstructable is:

> **Pucel/Bucal → Pucel → Procell/Procel → Crocell/Crocel/Crokel**

The exact spelling **Crocell** is a later manuscript reading, not an ancient name.

### What probably happened?

**[Textual reconstruction]**

1. French **Pucel** was copied or transmitted as **Bucal** in one place.
2. Scot expanded or misread it as **Procell**.
3. English manuscript copies shortened this to **Procel**.
4. A copyist read the initial *P/Pr* cluster as *Cr*, producing **Crocell**.
5. Mathers’s working manuscripts stabilized **Crocell**, while **Crokel** represents another graphic or phonetic interpretation.

The direction of development is supported by chronology. There is no early independent *Crocell* tradition that would explain Weyer’s *Pucel* as a corruption of an ancient *Crocell*.

## 3. Proposed etymologies

### Old French *pucel/pucele*, “maiden”

**[Disputed philological possibility]** Anglo-Norman and Old French *pucel, pucele* means a youth, maiden, or virgin. Because the earliest witness is French and says that the spirit appears as an angel, this is linguistically possible. [Anglo-Norman Dictionary, *pucel*](https://anglo-norman.net/entry/pucel_2)

Against it:

- The spirit is grammatically masculine and a duke.
- No source explains the name as “maiden.”
- The descriptive heading *Bucal* may show that the copyist no longer understood the name as an ordinary French word.

It is a plausible word resemblance, not an established derivation.

### Old English *pūcel*, “goblin/demon”

**[Disputed philological possibility]** Old English *pūcel* means an evil spirit, goblin, or demon and is related to later *puck*. [Bosworth–Toller, *pūcel*](https://bosworthtoller.com/25379)

This is semantically attractive, but there is no demonstrated transmission from an Old English demon-word into the French *Livre des Esperitz*. It may be cognate folklore vocabulary, borrowing, or coincidence.

### Latin *procella*, “storm, tempest”

**[Disputed retrospective etymology]** Latin *procella* means a violent wind, storm, or tempest. The resemblance to Scot’s **Procell** and the water-noise office is striking. [Lewis and Short, *procella*](https://classics.andrewgadsden.com/lewisandshort/entry/n38437)

But:

- **Procell** is later than **Pucel**.
- Weyer’s Latin text does not make this derivation.
- The spirit does not create an actual storm; it creates noises or apparent waters.

This is best treated as a learned association facilitated by Scot’s spelling, not the demonstrable origin.

### “Crocellatus” or “crocellare,” allegedly “to rustle/rattle”

**[Unsupported modern claim]** Numerous occult websites claim that *Crocell* comes from a Latin word such as *crocellatus* meaning “rattling,” “rustling,” or “making noise.” No suitable classical or medieval Latin lemma was located in the major dictionaries consulted, and the sites repeating it do not provide a primary citation.

The existence of real Latin words such as *crocatus*, “saffron-coloured,” does not support the alleged meaning. This etymology should be treated as a modern internet invention until an actual dated Latin occurrence is produced.

### An ancient god, hero, or place?

**[Negative finding]** No ancient deity, hero, city, or ethnic name can presently be shown to stand behind Crocell.

In particular, no secure connection was found with:

- a demon in the *Testament of Solomon*;
- an ancient Near Eastern god;
- a Greek daimon;
- a classical hero;
- an entry in the Munich necromancer’s manual, CLM 849;
- an Abramelin spirit;
- a securely identifiable name in Jewish antiquity.

### De Plancy’s Pocel/Picollus hypothesis

**[Nineteenth-century speculation]** Collin de Plancy proposed that Pucel “might well be” **Pocel**, whom he called a king of hell among the Prussians. His adjacent entry also mentions **Pocol**, chief of aerial spirits, and **Porquet**, guardian of forests and supposedly equivalent to Pan. [De Plancy, 1863, “Pocel” and “Pucel”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Lettre_P)

This probably reflects early-modern accounts of Baltic or Prussian figures such as Peckols/Pikollos. De Plancy offered resemblance, not a textual genealogy. There is no evidence that the French grimoire’s *Pucel* was derived from the Prussian name, and his statement should not be upgraded into “Crocell was an ancient Prussian god.”

# The description across the sources

## 1. *Livre des Esperitz*: Pucel/Bucal

**[Documented text; early 16th c.]**

The roster has *Pucel, duke*. The detailed *Bucal* entry gives:

- rank: great duke;
- appearance: angel;
- office: gives true answers to questions;
- prodigy: causes great waters or watery abysses to appear in the air where none exist;
- legions: 28.

Absent are:

- geometry;
- the liberal sciences;
- loud noises as a separate power;
- warming waters;
- baths;
- the former Order of Powers.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Weyer or an intermediate *Officium spirituum* text apparently expanded a short water-illusion spirit into a teacher of sciences with thermal and balneological powers.

---

## 2. Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* (1577)

### Bibliographical position

**[Documented text]** The *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* was printed as an appendix to the fifth edition of Johann Weyer’s *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. The frequent date “1563” refers to the first edition of *De praestigiis*, not to this full printed appendix.

Pucel is **§38**, crossing pp. **924–925** in the 1577 pagination.

### Latin

A short identifying extract is:

> “Pucel Dux magnus & fortis: apparet in specie angelica, sed obscura valde”

Clause by clause, the Latin says:

- *Pucel Dux magnus & fortis* — Pucel is a great and strong duke.
- *apparet in specie angelica, sed obscura valde* — he appears in angelic form, but very obscurely/darkly.
- *loquitur de occultis* — he speaks concerning hidden things.
- *docet Geometriam & omnes artes liberales* — he teaches geometry and all the liberal arts.
- *sonitus facit ingentes* — he produces enormous sounds.
- *sonare aquas ubi non sunt* — he makes waters sound where none are present.
- *easdem & calefacit* — he also heats those waters.
- *harum balnea recuperandæ sanitati servientia* — he concerns baths that serve the recovery of health.
- *certis temporibus, distemperat iussu exorcistæ* — at appointed times he alters, tempers, disorders, or changes them at the exorcist’s command.
- *Fuit de ordine Potestatum* — he was of the Order of Powers.
- *legiones quadragintaocto* — he holds forty-eight legions.

[Weyer’s Latin with Scot’s parallel English](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### Translation questions

*Obscura valde* can refer to the dark or indistinct quality of the angelic manifestation, the obscurity of the speech, or both. Scot attaches the darkness primarily to the way Pucel speaks.

*Distemperat* is not “discovers.” It means to disturb, alter, mix in wrong proportion, moderate, or regulate. In the health-bath context, “tempers” or “alters the temperature/constitution” may be intended. The sentence is grammatically awkward, probably because the catalogue was already textually unstable.

## 3. Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584)

**[Documented text]** Scot prints the spirit as **Procell**, still number 38, in Book XVI, chapter II. An identifying excerpt reads:

> “Procell is a great and a strong duke, appearing in the shape of an angell”

Scot’s complete content is:

- great and strong duke;
- angelic shape;
- speaks “verie darklie” about hidden things;
- teaches geometry and all the liberal arts;
- makes great noises;
- causes waters to roar where none exist;
- warms waters;
- “distempereth bathes at certeine times” according to the exorcist’s appointment;
- formerly belonged to the “potestats”;
- commands 48 legions.

[Scot, Book XVI, chapter II](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm)  
[1584 first-edition catalogue and scans](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081)

Scot’s version matters because the English *Goetia* often follows Scot’s wording more closely than Weyer’s Latin.

### Weyer and Scot as skeptics

**[Historical context]** Weyer and Scot did not publish these lists as endorsements of successful demon invocation. Weyer embedded the catalogue in an argument against credulous demonology and the persecution of alleged witches. Scot exposed what he regarded as superstition, fraud, coercive prosecution, and conjurors’ pretensions. Their reproduction of a ritual catalogue documents its circulation; it does not demonstrate their personal practice with Pucel.

**[Documented text; dated 18 January 1686/87]** Sloane 2731 likewise reads **Procel**. Its wording is substantially the same as Sloane 3825 and includes a seal. ## 6. Harley MS 6483 and Rudd

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483 uses **Crocell/Crocel**. Its description retains the familiar offices and 48 legions.

Its major novelty is not Crocell’s prose description but the **angel–demon control mechanism**:

- Crocell is paired with the corresponding ordinal Shem angel, Vehuel.
- A double-sided lamen is used.
- The demonic seal faces outward.
- Vehuel’s name or sigil and the invocatory Psalm are placed on the reverse, facing the operator’s heart.
- The angel is understood to “thwart,” bind, or constrain the demon.

Skinner and Rankine transcribe Crocell on pp. **150–151** and tabulate the angelic material on pp. **405–412** of *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*. [Searchable transcription](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)  
[Golden Hoard catalogue description](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf)

### Authorship caution

**[Disputed]** Harley 6483 contains material associated with “Dr Rudd,” but the surviving copy was made after Thomas Rudd’s lifetime or preserves an earlier exemplar in later copying. “Rudd’s manuscript” is convenient shorthand; it does not mean that every surviving page is demonstrably in Thomas Rudd’s hand.

## 7. Mathers–Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* (1904)

**[Documented text; p. 38]** The 1904 edition fixes the familiar modern name:

> “The Forty-ninth Spirit is Crocell, or Crokel.”

It describes:

- angelic form;
- great and strong duke;
- mystical speech concerning hidden things;
- geometry and liberal sciences;
- noises like the “rushings of many waters” although none are present;
- warming waters;
- **discovering baths**;
- former Order of Potestates or Powers;
- the statement that Crocell told Solomon this;
- 48 legions;
- a seal to be worn as directed.

[Mathers–Crowley entry, p. 38](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)  
[1904 bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books?vid=Harvard%3A32044011342870)

### The crucial bath variant

**[Textual finding]**

- Weyer: *distemperat*.
- Scot: “distempereth bathes.”
- Sloane 3825: “distempereth Bathes.”
- Mathers–Crowley: “discovereth Baths.”

“Discovereth” has become dominant in modern demon encyclopedias, producing the familiar claim that Crocell locates hidden springs or thermal baths. It is almost certainly an editorial misreading or deliberate emendation, not the earliest text.

The older reading may mean:

- changes the baths’ temperature;
- regulates or alters them;
- makes them unhealthy;
- at appointed times prepares them as health-giving baths.

The Latin itself combines health-serving baths with *distemperat*, so neither a purely beneficent nor a purely destructive reading is certain.

## 9. What Crocell does—and does not do

### Attested offices

**[Documented text]**

- speaks obscurely or mystically of hidden things;
- teaches geometry;
- teaches the liberal arts/sciences;
- produces great noises;
- produces the apparent roaring or running of waters where none exist;
- warms waters;
- alters/tempers/distempers baths;
- in the Mathers–Crowley reading, discovers baths.

### Only in the probable earlier precursor

**[Documented text]**

- gives true answers to questions;
- creates visible aerial waters or abysses.

The later texts retain “hidden things” but do not plainly repeat the general promise of true answers.

### Not attributed to Crocell in the primary entry

**[Negative finding]**

The primary descriptions do not grant Crocell:

- languages;
- love;
- familiars;
- invisibility;
- treasures;
- metal transmutation;
- knowledge of past, present, and future in general;
- transportation;
- political preferment;
- reconciliation;
- a human second form.

Those offices belong to other Goetic spirits. Modern composite lists sometimes import them incorrectly.

### “The angel who speaks darkly”

**[Clarification]** This phrase describes **Crocell**, who appears in angelic form and speaks obscurely. It is not an office of Vehuel.

# Number, order, and rank

## Weyer’s 69 and the *Goetia*’s 72

**[Documented textual comparison]**

Weyer has 69 catalogue entries. The *Goetia*:

- omits Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**;
- adds **Vassago** at number 3;
- adds **Seere** at 70;
- adds **Dantalion** at 71;
- adds **Andromalius** at 72.

Thus:

> 69 − 1 + 4 = 72

The order is also extensively rearranged. Pucel moves from Weyer’s **38** to the *Goetia*’s **49**.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The omission of Pruflas also occurs in Scot’s transmission, one reason scholars infer that the *Goetia* compiler used Scot or a source closely related to Scot rather than independently translating Weyer.

### A numbering misprint

The 1660 *Opera Omnia* printing mistakenly repeats §38 at Furcas, shifting subsequent printed numbers. This is an editorial error, not evidence for another demon. Peterson notes it directly. [Weyer comparison and note](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

## Duke, Venus, and copper

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The *Goetia*’s appended observations assign metals by rank:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince/Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| Earl/Count | Variable in witnesses | See below |

Crocell, being a Duke, therefore receives **Venus and copper**.

The 1904 observations give Earls copper-and-silver, while later *777* tables regularize Earls as Mars/iron. That later symmetry should not be retrojected into every manuscript. [Goetic observations](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks06.htm)  
[*777 Revised*, editorial note on rank and metal](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/crowley/777_Revised.pdf)

## Traditional time of conjuration

**[Documented ritual rubric]** Weyer/Scot and the later observations give Dukes the period from the first hour after sunrise until noon, in clear weather. This is a rule for the ducal class, not a sentence in Crocell’s entry.

The 1904 observations also recommend a waxing Moon on the second, fourth, sixth, eighth, tenth, twelfth, or fourteenth lunar day.

**[Later planetary inference]** Because Dukes are assigned Venus, modern practitioners often add:

- Friday;
- an hour of Venus;
- copper lamens;
- green or Venusian colours.

Friday and Venus hours are reasonable developments within planetary magic, but they are not Crocell-specific instructions in Weyer, Scot, or the Sloane entry.

# The seal

## Appearance

**[Documented manuscript feature]** Neither Weyer nor Scot supplies a seal. The sigil first appears in the surviving *Goetia* manuscript tradition.

The conventional form can be described as follows:

1. At the top is a short upright terminating in a fork, crown, or shallow horned yoke.
2. A central descending stem enters a pointed oval, lozenge, or shield-like enclosure.
3. Two approximately symmetrical arms project outward and curl back like hooks, crescents, or handles.
4. Lower strokes narrow toward a central junction.
5. The base terminates in a downward-pointing open triangle or V.

[Peterson’s Sloane 3825 figure 49](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)  
[1904 public-domain seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:49-Crocell_seal.png)

## Variants

**[Textual/iconographic comparison]**

The principal witnesses preserve the same recognizable skeleton, but differ in:

- whether the top is a simple fork, crown, or joined arch;
- whether the side arms close into loops;
- the width and curvature of the central shield or vessel;
- whether lower diagonal strokes meet or remain open;
- line symmetry;
- the placement of the name;
- whether the seal is enclosed by a circle.

Harley 6483 is particularly important because its operational form is a **double seal** rather than merely the demon’s glyph. Skinner and Rankine explain that the controlling Shem angel occupies the reverse side, turned toward the operator’s heart. [Skinner–Rankine discussion](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

## What the seal represents

**[Open question]** No early source explains the lines symbolically. Modern readings of it as:

- a chalice;
- a thermal spring;
- a water vessel;
- a crowned angel;
- a geometrical diagram;
- an alchemical retort;

are visual interpretations, not documented explanations by the compiler.

## The 72-letter/divided name

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional Hebrew consonantal count, of three verses of 72 letters each. The verses are arranged boustrophedon:

1. the first in its normal Hebrew direction;
2. the second in reverse;
3. the third in its normal direction.

Reading the resulting columns yields 72 triplets. The forty-ninth is **והו**, V-H-V.

Christian kabbalists made the triplets pronounceable as angels by adding the divine suffixes:

- **-El**, אל;
- or **-Yah**, יה.

Thus VHV + El produces a form conventionally transliterated **Vehuel**.

**[Philological caution]** “God great and exalted” is a traditional attribute attached to the angel, not a literal lexical translation of the otherwise opaque triplet V-H-V.

## Earlier Jewish and Christian transmission

### Sefer Raziel

**[Tradition/documented textual family]** Medieval *Sefer Raziel* material transmits the 72-fold name among many angelic names and magical figures. The work is composite: manuscript material is medieval, while the influential printed *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* appeared in Amsterdam in 1701.

It supports the antiquity of the triplet tradition but does not by itself establish the complete later package of:

- numbered guardian angels;
- five-day birth periods;
- Psalm verses;
- Goetic opponents;
- Tarot cards.

[Survey and list derived from *Sefer Raziel*](https://www.kabbale.eu/les-72-noms-du-sepher-raziel/)

### *Liber Juratus* or *Sworn Book of Honorius*

**[Documented name tradition]** Forms resembling Vehuel occur among the angelic names of the *Sworn Book* tradition. This shows that the vocalized name was in Christian magical circulation before Rudd, but not that Vehuel was already considered Crocell’s opponent.

### Reuchlin

**[Documented Christian Kabbalah]** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) interpreted the suffixed 72 names as angels. Reuchlin was influential on Agrippa and later Christian kabbalists.

### Agrippa

**[Documented text]** Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia*, Book III, chapter 25, lists the 72 names and explains their formation. Vehuel belongs to that list, but Agrippa does not pair it with Crocell. [Agrippa, Book III](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp3b.htm)

### Kircher

**[Documented early-modern occult synthesis]** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) printed influential tables of the 72 names, their divine attributes, and associated biblical material. Kircher helped transmit the system into the exact scholarly-occult milieu from which Rudd’s synthesis emerged. [Kircher’s Kabbalistic tables](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)  
[Scan of *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*](https://www.esonet.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Oedipus_Aegyptiacus02.pdf)

## Rudd’s pairing: Vehuel constrains Crocell

**[Documented esoteric system]** Rudd’s governing rule is ordinal:

> Angel 1 constrains demon 1; angel 2 demon 2; … angel 49 demon 49.

Therefore Vehuel is paired with Crocell because both occupy position 49.

The double-sided lamen operationalizes the relationship:

- Crocell’s seal presents the spirit being summoned.
- Vehuel’s name and angelic authority face the magician.
- The Psalm invokes the divine quality under which Crocell is compelled.
- The same 72 angelic names also appear in Rudd-related representations of Solomon’s brass vessel and protective equipment.

Skinner and Rankine describe this as the feature that makes the Harley recension distinct from the ordinary *Lemegeton*: the demon is not approached alone but through an equal-numbered “thwarting” angel. [*The Goetia of Dr Rudd*](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf)

### Vehuel’s Psalm

Harley 6483 gives:

> “Magnus Dominus et laudabilis nimis”

The complete verse is Vulgate Psalm 144:3, corresponding to Psalm 145:3 in modern Hebrew and most English Bibles:

> Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised; his greatness is unsearchable.

This verse expresses Vehuel’s traditional attribute, divine greatness or exaltation.

---

## Lenain, *La science cabalistique* (1823)

**[Documented text; pp. 78–79]** Lazare Lenain gives angel 49 as **Vehuel** and defines the attribute as *Dieu grand et élevé*.

His entry assigns:

- the divine name **Mara**, purportedly in the language of the Philippine Islands;
- the ray from zodiacal degrees **241 through 245 inclusive**;
- the first five degrees of Sagittarius;
- five recurring days during the year;
- invocation from **4:00 to 4:20 p.m.**;
- Psalm 144:3 Vulgate;
- elevation toward God in praise and admiration;
- influence over eminent persons and those distinguished by talents and virtues;
- sensitivity, generosity, esteem, and beneficence;
- distinction in literature, jurisprudence, and diplomacy;
- a contrary genius governing selfishness, hatred, and hypocrisy.

[Lenain, 1823 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf)

### “Mara” as a Philippine divine name

**[Disputed/obsolete claim]** Lenain’s ethnolinguistic divine names are products of early-nineteenth-century occult compilation, not reliable comparative linguistics. No evidence was found that “Mara” was a pan-Philippine name of the biblical God. It should be reported as Lenain’s statement, not as historical fact.

### Degrees and dates

Degrees 241–245 are most cleanly read as:

- 241st zodiacal degree = 0°–1° Sagittarius;
- through the 245th = 4°–5° Sagittarius.

Hence modern tables write **0°–5° Sagittarius**, usually approximated as **23–27 November**. Calendar dates vary by one or more days because authors count boundary degrees differently and mix tropical degree sectors with five-day civil-calendar periods.

## Papus

**[Reception history]** Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” promoted French occult Kabbalah through *La Cabbale* (1892; substantially expanded 1903). Later editions of Lenain were issued with a Papus preface, helping restore Lenain’s angelic system to occult circulation.

**[Negative finding]** A distinctive Crocell–Vehuel doctrine originating with Papus was not found. Papus is a transmitter and popularizer of the French Kabbalistic environment, not the source of Rudd’s pairing. [Papus, *La Cabbale*](https://books.google.fr/books?id=U_5MAQAAMAAJ)

---

## Ambelain

**[Modern esoteric development]** Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique*—first published in the mid-twentieth century and repeatedly reissued—systematizes the 72 angels on the Tree of Life.

For Vehuel it supports the now-common arrangement:

- choir: Principalities;
- governing archangel: Haniel;
- sephirah: Netzach;
- angelic sequence Vehuel through Poyel.

This Tree-of-Life organization is later than Rudd and Lenain and should not be treated as an original feature of the biblical triplets. [Ambelain, *Practical Kabbalah*](https://www.markfoster.net/rn/texts/practicalkabbalah-part1.pdf)

---

## Haziel

**[Modern popular angelology]** “Haziel,” the pseudonym of François Bernard-Termés, popularized a guardian-angel system in European mass-market books during the late twentieth century. *Il nostro angelo custode esiste* appeared in Italian in 1994 and explicitly includes Vehuel among the guardian angels. [Google Books record](https://books.google.it/books/about/Il_nostro_angelo_custode_esiste.html?hl=it&id=NmJ2PFDFbx4C)

Haziel’s school expands Vehuel into a personal guardian for those born approximately 23–27 November and attributes consolation, spiritual elevation, generosity, vocation, prestige, and success in intellectual or diplomatic work.

**[Modern development]** Birth guardianship, crystals, colours, incense recipes, “mirror hours,” and psychological personality profiles are not present in Rudd, Weyer, or the Sloane manuscripts. They represent the Ambelain–Kabaleb–Haziel stream of twentieth-century angelology.

## Vehuel in Golden Dawn versus Lenain

The Golden Dawn does not simply begin the Shem angels at 0° Aries. Its angel sequence begins with Vehuiah and Jeliel on the first Leo decan. Consequently the sequence wraps around the zodiac:

- Vehuiah/Jeliel: Five of Wands;
- Vehuel/Daniel, numbers 49–50: Two of Wands.

In *777*’s angel table, Vehuel is the **day angel of the Two of Wands**, with Daniel by night. The Two of Wands is Mars in Aries, “Lord of Dominion.”

This differs from Lenain’s arrangement, in which Vehuel’s 241st–245th-degree ray is 0°–5° Sagittarius.

**[Finding]** Three things are therefore simultaneously true inside different occult overlays:

1. **Rudd:** Vehuel constrains Crocell because both are number 49.
2. **Lenain:** Vehuel rules 0°–5° Sagittarius.
3. ***777*:** Crocell rules the first Leo decan by night, while Vehuel belongs to the Two of Wands in the separate Shem table.

Modern charts that give Crocell–Vehuel–Sagittarius–Eight of Wands as a single ancient package have merged Rudd, Lenain, Tarot, and later sequential Goetic astrology.

## Enochian overlays

**[Negative finding]** No primary Dee or Kelley record identifies Crocell as an Enochian spirit, governor, king, senior, or cacodemon.

Rudd material also juxtaposes Solomonic and Dee-derived tables, but this does not establish a specific Enochian identity for Crocell. The Enochian element is an operational overlay.

# Iconography

## Manuscript image

**[Documented text]** Crocell’s only stated appearance is angelic. No manuscript specifies:

- number of wings;
- sex;
- clothing;
- colour;
- weapons;
- halo;
- bodily deformity;
- aquatic anatomy.

Weyer’s *obscura valde* encouraged later artists to depict a dark, black-winged, shadowed, or fallen angel, but those details are interpretive.

## Collin de Plancy

### Text

The *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818. In the expanded sixth edition of 1863, de Plancy gives an entry under **Pucel**:

> “grand et puissant duc de l’enfer”

He repeats:

- dark angelic form;
- occult knowledge;
- geometry and the liberal arts;
- great noises;
- the roar of waters in waterless places;
- 48 legions.

He omits:

- warming waters;
- baths;
- the Order of Powers;
- the explicit fall narrative.

He then speculates that Pucel may be the same as Pocel. [De Plancy, 1863, Pucel](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Lettre_P)

### Portrait

**[Negative finding]** The 1863 edition does **not** contain a Louis Le Breton portrait of Pucel/Crocell. The Pucel entry is unillustrated. Unlike Astaroth, Bael, Buer, Stolas, and several other spirits, Crocell was not included among the famous Breton engravings.

Images advertised online as “Crocell from the *Dictionnaire Infernal*” are therefore generally:

- modern illustrations;
- generic angel pictures;
- artwork borrowed from another demon;
- or images inspired by the 1904 seal.

## Modern art

**[Modern adaptation]** Contemporary artists commonly choose one of four visual strategies:

1. A black- or blue-winged angel, based on *specie angelica* and *obscura valde*.
2. A water angel surrounded by waves or thermal springs.
3. A geometric angel, emphasizing the liberal sciences.
4. A female or androgynous water spirit.

The last form has been strengthened by Japanese game representations and modern demonolatry. No early witness calls Crocell female or a duchess.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer provides the first securely printed description, Pucel #38, in 1577. He preserves material from a book he calls a *Liber officiorum spirituum* or an “Empto. Salomonis,” but the exact manuscript he used is lost.

His importance is textual and polemical. He was recording what he regarded as a false infernal monarchy within a larger critique of witch persecution and credulous demonological claims.

---

## Reginald Scot

Scot translated or adapted Weyer in 1584, creating the influential **Procell** spelling. His English wording—“waters to rore,” “distempereth bathes,” “order of potestats”—flows directly into the *Goetia*.

---

## Thomas Rudd and the Harley compiler

Rudd’s tradition supplies the decisive Crocell–Vehuel relationship. It transforms an isolated demon seal into one side of an angelically controlled apparatus.

The surviving Harley manuscript does not prove that Rudd personally evoked Crocell. It shows that a Rudd-associated working tradition provided an operational method for doing so.

---

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers prepared the English *Goetia* text from British Museum manuscripts, particularly Sloane 2731 and related material. His editorial choices made **Crocell/Crokel** the internationally familiar form and introduced “discovereth Baths.”

Mathers’s Golden Dawn environment also supplied the dense Tarot, zodiacal, Qabalistic, and angelic apparatus later published in *777*.

---

## Aleister Crowley

Crowley published Mathers’s text in 1904 and added:

- the “Preliminary Invocation,” adapted from the Headless/Bornless ritual;
- Enochian renderings;
- editorial and magical material;
- an “Initiated Interpretation,” treating the spirits as portions of the human mind to be brought under conscious control.

His later *777* places Crocell in the first Leo decan by night.

**[Caution]** Crowley’s psychological interpretation is influential but not evidence for what Weyer’s source or the seventeenth-century compiler originally intended.

---

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, wrote extensively on Thelemic Qabalah but no securely sourced, Crocell-specific working or distinctive interpretation was located. His name sometimes appears in general Thelemic bibliographies near Crowley’s *Goetia*, which is not evidence of direct engagement with Crocell.

---

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette has discussed Solomonic magic and provided introductions or commentary to modern Crowley/Goetia editions, including *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*. His role is modern interpretation and popularization; he is not a new textual witness.

---

## Poke Runyon

Runyon developed a ritual and astrological synthesis using crystal evocation, planetary timing, Shem angels, decans or quinances, and cinematic ritual reconstruction. His Crocell table helped spread the 0° Sagittarius/Vehuel correspondence among English-speaking practitioners.

---

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition made manuscript variants visible, restored **Procel** as an important base reading, distinguished “distempereth” from “discovereth,” and documented the dependence on Weyer and Scot.

His online transcriptions remain essential because they allow direct comparison of Weyer’s Latin, Scot’s English, and Sloane 3825.

---

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* published and interpreted the Harley/Rudd material, including:

- the 72 double-sided seals;
- Shem controlling angels;
- Psalm verses;
- rank, planet, and metal tables;
- ritual equipment;
- relationships with the *Heptameron*, Dee material, and other seventeenth-century magic.

For Crocell, their chief contribution is the reconstruction of Vehuel’s role as the protecting or constraining reverse of the demon’s lamen.

---

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica*, especially *Geosophia*, argued for reading Goetic spirits against a broad background of Greek goēteia, necromancy, spirit cult, and grimoire transmission.

**[Caution]** That broad history does not establish Crocell as an ancient Greek spirit. No Crocell-specific ancient identification in Stratton-Kent’s work could be independently substantiated from the primary chain.

---

## Owen Davies

Davies situates the *Lemegeton* within the history of grimoires, print, manuscript circulation, occult revival, and popular magic. His work is essential context but does not provide a separate early Crocell witness. [Owen Davies, *Grimoires*](https://files.secure.website/wscfus/10533201/10380031/grimoires-a-history-of-magic-books-by-davie-owens.pdf)

---

## Jean-Patrice Boudet and Julien Véronèse

Boudet’s comparison of medieval and Renaissance demon catalogues is central to identifying the *Livre des Esperitz* as an ancestor of the Weyer–Goetia family.

Véronèse’s scholarship on medieval ritual magic and angelic invocation helps contextualize the conjunction of demonic catalogues, conjurations, and angelic controls. Neither supplies an ancient Crocell; their importance is the reconstruction of transmission.

# Grimoire ancestors and parallels

## *Livre des Esperitz*

**Directly relevant:** Pucel/Bucal is the probable ancestor.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Weyer says his catalogue comes from a *Book of the Offices of Spirits*. Several surviving English spirit-office texts—including material related to Folger V.b.26, Sloane 3824, and the later *Book of Oberon*—show that this was a textual family rather than necessarily one fixed book.

The exact lost intermediary between French Pucel/Bucal and Weyer’s expanded Pucel has not been found.

## Munich handbook, CLM 849

**[Negative finding]** CLM 849 contains some names and operations related to later Goetic catalogues, but no convincing Pucel/Procel/Crocell entry has been identified. It is a general ancestor or parallel of learned necromantic practice, not the specific source for Crocell.

## *Book of Abramelin*

**[Negative finding]** Abramelin catalogues many subordinate spirits, but no secure Crocell cognate has been demonstrated. Similar-sounding short names are insufficient without matching offices or transmission.

## *Testament of Solomon*

**[Tradition and negative finding]** The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* supplies the famous Solomonic story of a king interrogating and binding demons, but it does not contain Crocell/Pucel or a recognizably equivalent water-and-geometry spirit.

It is an ancestor of the **Solomonic frame**, not a demonstrated textual source for this particular entry.

## *Sefer Raziel* and *Liber Juratus*

These belong principally to Vehuel’s angelic ancestry, not Crocell’s demon catalogue.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is Crocell ancient?

**Conclusion: no evidence.**

The name in its exact form is early modern. A Pucel/Bucal precursor survives from the early sixteenth century, but no ancient god, hero, or demon has been demonstrated behind it.

---

## 2. Pucel or Crocell?

**Conclusion: Pucel is earlier; Crocell is later but canonical to the 1904 reception.**

“Corrupt” need not mean useless. Crocell is historically secondary, yet it is the form through which the spirit entered twentieth- and twenty-first-century occultism and popular culture.

---

## 3. Pucel and Bucal: one spirit or two?

**[Disputed]** The *Livre des Esperitz* roster says Pucel, while the final description says Bucal. Their position suggests one entry, but the manuscript could have conflated two names. No second independent Bucal description survives to resolve the matter.

---

## 4. Distempers or discovers baths?

**Conclusion: “distempers” is earlier.**

“Discovereth” first becomes prominent in the Mathers–Crowley edition and is likely a misreading or emendation. The modern “finder of hidden springs” image depends upon it.

---

## 5. Is Crocell dark in appearance or obscure in speech?

Weyer’s Latin can support an obscure/dark angelic appearance, while Scot makes the speech “very dark.” The *Goetia* shifts to “speaking something mystically.” Modern black-winged iconography combines both readings.

---

## 6. Is Crocell male, female, or androgynous?

Every early Latin and English source uses masculine grammar. Female Crocell depictions appear in modern games and art. Androgynous angelic depictions are visually defensible but not textually specified.

---

## 7. Forty-eight or twenty-eight legions?

- Probable *Livre des Esperitz* precursor: 28.
- Weyer onward: 48.

The change probably arose in an intermediate spirit-office text or from the easy visual confusion of written numerals. All major later witnesses agree on 48.

---

## 8. Crocell’s zodiac: Sagittarius or Leo?

Both exist, but in different systems:

- **Sagittarius:** ordinal 72-fold quinance scheme, Vehuel’s Lenain ray, and later Runyon-style Goetic tables.
- **Leo:** Crowley’s *777* arrangement of 36 spirits by day and 36 by night across 36 decans.

Calling one “the original Goetic zodiac sign” is misleading. The Sloane entry gives none.

---

## 9. Which tarot card?

- **Eight of Wands** if Crocell is placed with Vehuel at 0°–5° Sagittarius in a sequential/tropical synthesis.
- **Five of Wands** for Crocell’s actual Goetic placement in *777*.
- **Two of Wands** for Vehuel in the Golden Dawn’s separate, Leo-starting Shem table.

All three attributions circulate because three independent wheels have been conflated.

---

## 10. Does Vehuel’s name literally mean “God great and exalted”?

**[Tradition, not strict etymology]** The phrase is Kircher/Lenain-style angelic attribution. V-H-V is a derived divine triplet without a straightforward dictionary meaning. The suffix *El* means God, but the entire phrase is interpretive.

---

## 11. Is Vehuel originally Crocell’s enemy?

No. Vehuel’s name existed independently in Christian Kabbalistic lists. The Crocell opposition is Rudd’s equal-number pairing. It is not found in Exodus, *Sefer Raziel*, Reuchlin, or Agrippa.

---

## 12. Was the 72-demon list transmitted unchanged from Solomon?

**[Legend]** The *Goetia* claims Solomonic authority and describes spirits reporting their former angelic orders to Solomon.

**[Historical finding]** The recoverable text grew from early-modern European catalogues. Its dependence on Weyer and Scot, and the addition/omission pattern required to reach 72, precludes an unchanged ancient Solomonic list.

---

# Open questions

1. **What was the exact date of the *Livre des Esperitz* section?**  
   Older scholarship and catalogues have ranged from the mid-fifteenth to early-sixteenth century. The current Trinity catalogue favours early sixteenth century.

2. **Was Pucel the original reading and Bucal a simple error?**  
   Probably, but no earlier copy has emerged.

3. **What source expanded the 28-legion water illusionist into Weyer’s 48-legion teacher of geometry and bath magic?**  
   The lost or unidentified *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* intermediary is the likely location.

4. **Why did Scot write Procell?**  
   It may be a scribal reading, an editorial expansion, or attraction to Latin *procella*. No explicit explanation survives.

5. **Who first wrote Crocell?**  
   The spelling is found in later *Goetia* witnesses such as Sloane 3648, but the precise earliest datable occurrence remains dependent on manuscript dating and stemmatics.

6. **What exactly did Weyer’s *distemperat* mean?**  
   Possible senses include changing temperature, blending, regulating, or disrupting baths. The sentence’s reference to restored health complicates a purely harmful translation.

7. **Where did the seal originate?**  
   It is absent from Weyer and Scot. No secure earlier seal source has been identified.

8. **Does the seal encode a bath, vessel, or geometry?**  
   No historical gloss survives.

9. **How much of Harley 6483 is genuinely Thomas Rudd’s composition?**  
   The recension preserves Rudd-associated material, but later copying and compilation make individual authorship difficult to prove.

10. **When was the unsupported “crocellatus” etymology invented?**  
    It circulates widely online, but no credible dated lexicographical source has been found.

# Amdusias: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes several kinds of statement:

- **[Documented text]** — directly present in a cited manuscript, early printed edition, or facsimile.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a conclusion advanced from textual comparison or historical context.
- **[Tradition]** — part of a transmitted religious, magical, or folkloric system, without implying historical or supernatural verification.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an occult attribution imposed on the spirit or angel by a particular table or school.
- **[Disputed]** — a reading or attribution for which the evidence is incomplete or conflicting.
- **[Legend]** — a traditional narrative not supported as literal history.
- **[Modern invention]** — a demonstrably late interpretation, adaptation, or fictional development.
- **[Finding: absent]** — an expected connection was sought but not found in the sources examined.

Historical occult claims are reported as claims of their texts, not as independently verified supernatural facts.

## The name and its origins

### The documented spellings

The source chain gives the following readings:

| Date/source | Number | Spelling |
|---|---:|---|
| Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, 1577 | 53 | **Amduscias** |
| Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584 | 53 | **Amduscias** |
| Sloane MS 3825 | 67 | **Amduscias** |
| Sloane MS 3648 | 67 | **Amdusias** |
| Sloane MS 2731 | 67 | **Amduscias**; associated readings **Andusias/Anduscias** |
| Harley MS 6483 | 67 | **Amducias** |
| Mathers–Crowley, 1904 | 67 | **Amdusias, or Amdukias** |
| Waite | 67 | **Amduscias** |
| Peterson, 2001 main text | 67 | **Amduscias** |
| Later popular literature | — | **Ambduscias**, among other forms |

These readings are tabulated in Peterson’s [comparative Goetic spirit list](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

**[Finding: absent]** The often-repeated **Ambduscias** does not appear to be the principal reading in Weyer, Scot, Sloane 3825, Sloane 3648, Sloane 2731, or Harley 6483. It is a later secondary spelling, probably produced by contamination among the already unstable consonants.

**[Disputed]** Sloane 2731’s exact situation is more complicated than a single spelling: Peterson’s collation gives *Amduscias* as the heading while noting *Andusias* in another copy and *Anduscias* beside a sigil. It is therefore misleading either to declare *Andusias* “the” Sloane 2731 spelling or to omit it entirely.

### Earliest secure attestation

**[Documented text]** The earliest secure, presently identifiable attestation of the name is Johann Weyer’s **1577** fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum*, in which the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* was appended. Weyer’s larger book first appeared in 1563, but the catalog containing Amduscias belongs to the later edition. The entry is number 53. See Peterson’s transcription of [Weyer’s Latin text and its editions](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Weyer said that his catalog came from a magical book called something like *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also connected in his wording with an *Empto. Salomonis*. Johannes Trithemius had listed a *Liber officiorum spirituum* in 1508. That suggests an older textual environment, but it does not prove that the specific name **Amduscias** was already present in 1508.

**[Finding: absent]** No securely recognizable Amdusias has been identified in:

- the surviving *Livre des Esperitz*;
- the fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook, Clm 849;
- the 1494 Florence spirit catalog;
- the surviving Folger *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*;
- the demon lists of *The Book of Abramelin*;
- the late-antique *Testament of Solomon*.

The strongest direct concordance study is Jean-Patrice Boudet’s comparison of Renaissance demonological catalogs with medieval predecessors. Its tables place **Amduscias only in Weyer’s list**, without a corresponding *Livre des Esperitz* number. See Boudet, [“Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

### Proposed pre-Weyer identifications

Peterson’s comparison table aligns Weyer’s Amduscias positionally with a spirit called **Andriflides** in one related *Secret of Secrets/Munich* tradition. This is not a demonstrated etymological identity. The difficulty is increased by the presence of another similar name, **Andriflis**, associated with Androalphus. Position in a copied list can be evidence of genealogy, but it is not sufficient by itself to equate two markedly different names.

A modern digital presentation of the Folger *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* has proposed **Deyclo**, or *Deyoo*, as an antecedent because that spirit has power involving trees. The comparison is weak:

- Deyclo is called an **earl**, not a duke;
- its form and offices differ;
- it makes trees flourish out of season rather than bow;
- it teaches sciences and languages;
- its legion count differs;
- its name bears no demonstrated phonetic relationship to Amduscias.

The Folger text can be consulted in the [Grimorium transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/liber-officiorum/read/). The proposed equation should be labeled **[Disputed modern reconstruction]**, not a pre-1577 attestation.

### Is there an ancient deity behind the name?

**[Finding: absent]** No ancient god, hero, demon, or place has been convincingly shown to stand behind **Amduscias**.

This contrasts with several other Goetic names whose older histories are at least reasonably traceable:

- *Baal* continues a Northwest Semitic title meaning “lord.”
- *Astaroth* is related through polemical and linguistic transformation to Astarte.
- *Asmodeus* is usually connected with the Iranian *Aēšma daēva*.
- *Belial* derives from Hebrew *beliyyaʿal*.
- *Phoenix* is transparently connected with the Greek and Latin name of the legendary bird.
- The relationship between Goetic *Amon* and Egyptian Amun is traditional and widely repeated, although the precise route into demonological catalogs requires caution.

No equivalent chain exists for Amduscias.

**[Disputed]** Suggestions that *Amdusias* derives from Latin *amandus*, “one who must be loved,” lack a documented historical pathway and do not explain the consonants satisfactorily. They also seem influenced by the later Venus attribution of the ducal rank.

**[Modern invention]** Internet claims linking the name to a Celtic deity rendered **Andescociuoucus**, or translating it as “activator,” have not been supported with a manuscript genealogy, a regular sound change, or acceptance in historical linguistic scholarship.

### Useful words in the description, but not an etymology

Weyer’s entry contains ordinary Latin vocabulary that later translators reshaped:

- *dux* — leader or duke;
- *unicornu* — unicorn;
- *symphoniae* — ensembles, concords, or musical performances;
- *famuli* — servants or attendants;
- *legiones* — legions.

The title *Goetia* derives ultimately from Greek **γοητεία**, magical practice or sorcery associated with the *goēs*. None of these words explains the proper name *Amduscias*.

## Number and order: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s printed catalog contains **69** spirits. Amduscias is number **53**.

Scot’s English version omits Weyer’s fourth spirit, **Pruflas/Bufas**, and therefore effectively prints 68 individual entries even though its inherited prefatory arithmetic is not consistently corrected. Scot’s phrase about “seventie and nine” principal devils is not borne out by the list that follows. It is likely a textual, typographical, or counting error rather than evidence for a lost 79-spirit scheme.

The *Ars Goetia* omits Pruflas and adds four spirits not found in Weyer:

1. **Vassago**, inserted as Goetia number 3;
2. **Seere**, number 70;
3. **Dantalion**, number 71;
4. **Andromalius**, number 72.

Thus:

> 69 Weyer spirits − Pruflas + four additions = 72 Goetic spirits.

The *Goetia* also rearranges many entries, so the change from Weyer 53 to Goetia 67 is not a simple uniform displacement. Peterson has suggested that disordered source leaves could explain some rearrangement, but no complete physical reconstruction has been established. The comparison is visible in his [parallel spirit table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

**[Legend]** The *Goetia*’s claim that Solomon confined seventy-two spirits in a brass vessel, which Babylonians later opened while seeking treasure, is part of the work’s Solomonic pseudepigraphy. It is not evidence that the list existed in the historical reign of Solomon.

## The description across the sources

### 1. Weyer, 1577

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s full Latin entry, number 53, printed on page 928, reads:

> **Amduscias Dux magnus & fortis: procedit ut unicornu: in humana similiter forma, quando coram magistro suo se sistit: & si præcipiatur, efficit facilè ut tubæ & symphoniæ omniaque musicorum instrumentorum genera audiantur, nec tamen conspectui appareant: ut item arbores ad exorcistæ genu se inclinent. Optimus est una cum famulis. Imperium habet vigintinovem legionum.**

A close translation is:

> Amduscias, a great and strong duke, comes forth as a unicorn; likewise in human form when he presents himself before his master. If commanded, he easily brings it about that trumpets, musical ensembles, and every kind of musical instrument are heard, although they do not appear to sight; likewise, that trees bend the knee to the exorcist. He is excellent together with attendants. He has command of twenty-nine legions.

Source: Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, entry 53, p. 928, in Peterson’s [Latin transcription](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Several points deserve attention:

- The unicorn is explicitly the form in which the spirit “comes forth.”
- Human form is also mentioned, although Weyer’s syntax is less explicit than the later “at the request of the exorcist.”
- The instruments are **heard but not visible**.
- The tree expression literally evokes bending the knee to the exorcist.
- Weyer’s *famuli* means attendants or servants. It does not unambiguously say that Amduscias bestows new familiar spirits.
- Weyer supplies no seal.

### 2. Scot, 1584

**[Documented text]** Reginald Scot’s English version, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584, p. 224, reads:

> **Amduscias a great and a strong duke, he commeth foorth as an unicorne, when he standeth before his maister in humane shape, being commanded, he easilie bringeth to passe, that trumpets and all musicall instruments may be heard and not seene, and also that trees shall bend and incline, according to the conjurors will, he is excellent among familiars, and hath nine and twentie legions.**

Source: Scot, Book XV, chapter 2 in the 1584 printing, in Peterson’s [transcription of Scot’s demon catalog](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

The significant translation developments are:

- Weyer’s “bend the knee to the exorcist” becomes “bend and incline, according to the conjuror’s will.”
- *Optimus est una cum famulis* becomes “he is excellent among familiars.”
- This makes the familiars language sound more like an office or classification than Weyer’s ambiguous Latin.

Scot’s demon catalog was derived from Weyer, probably in a translation associated with Abraham Fleming. Both the copied source and translation contain errors elsewhere, so Scot should not automatically be preferred to Weyer when the wording differs.

Scot’s purpose was polemical and skeptical. He printed magical procedures partly to expose what he regarded as fraudulent or delusive practices and to oppose the lethal persecution of alleged witches. Weyer likewise argued that many people accused of witchcraft were ill, deceived, or deluded rather than genuine contractual partners of demons. Their publication of the catalog does not mean that either man endorsed the grimoire’s practices.

### 3. The seventeenth-century *Goetia*

**[Documented text]** Sloane MS 3825, folio 110r, as edited by Peterson, gives:

> **The 67th spirit in order is called Amduscias, he is a strong & great duke appearing at first like an Unicorne, But afterwards at ye Request of ye Exorcist he standeth before him in humane shape causing Trumpets and all manner of Musicall Instruments to be heard But not seene also causing Trees to bend and Incline according to ye Exorcist’s will; he giveth Excellent familiars & ruleth 29 Legions of spirits his seal is Thus formed & is to be worne as a Lamin &c.**

Source: *Ars Goetia*, Sloane 3825, fol. 110r; Joseph H. Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (2001), p. 37; online [Goetia edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

The principal changes from Weyer and Scot are:

- The transformation is now explicitly sequential: unicorn **first**, then human at the exorcist’s request.
- “Excellent among familiars” becomes the active office **“he gives excellent familiars.”**
- An individual seal is supplied and is to be worn as a **lamen**, a ritual breast-pendant or badge.

**[Finding: absent]** Amduscias is not said here to teach languages or sciences, reveal treasure, cause love, confer invisibility, perform transmutation, or answer all questions. Those are offices of other spirits. Attributing them to Amdusias without a named later source is conflation.

### 4. Harley 6483 and the Rudd recension

**[Documented text]** Harley MS 6483, copied in 1712–13, reads in the Skinner–Rankine edition:

> **The Sixty seventh Spirit is Amducias he is a strong and great Duke appearing at first like a Unicorn but after at the request of the Exorcist he standeth before him in human shape causing Trumpets and all sorts of Musical Instruments to be heard but not seen, also Trees to bend and incline according to the Exorcist’s will, and gives excellent familiars and governeth 29 Legions of Spirits his Seal is this which wear.**

The recension attaches the angel **Eiael** and the Psalm verse:

> **Delectare in Domino et dabit tibi petitiones cordis tui.**

Source: Stephen Skinner and David Rankine, *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007), pp. 168–69, reproduced in the [searchable scan](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

### 5. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**[Documented text]** The 1904 printed edition gives:

> **(67.) AMDUSIAS, or AMDUKIAS.—The Sixty-seventh Spirit is Amdusias, or Amdukias. He is a Duke Great and Strong, appearing at first like a Unicorn, but at the request of the Exorcist he standeth before him in Human Shape, causing Trumpets, and all manner of Musical Instruments to be heard, but not soon or immediately. Also he can cause Trees to bend and incline according to the Exorcist’s Will. He giveth Excellent Familiars. He governeth 29 Legions of Spirits. And his Seal is this, etc.**

Source: S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley, *The Goetia of the Lemegeton of Solomon the King* (1904), entry 67, in the [1904 scan](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/The-Goetia-of-the-Lemegeton-of-Solomon-the-King-ed.-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf).

The phrase **“not soon or immediately”** is an important corruption. Every earlier version says that the music is heard but **not seen**. The 1904 wording changes an invisibility statement into a delay. No independent source supports this new power.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Crowley’s later *777* image of Amdusias as a **“dilatory bandmaster”** is almost certainly a joke or mnemonic based on this corrupt phrase: a bandmaster who does not perform “soon or immediately.”

## Rank, planet, metal, and timing

The *Goetia*’s “General Observations” prescribe metals for seals according to rank:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Earl/count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver |

Therefore:

> **Amdusias is Venusian and copper because he is a Duke.**

This is a rank correspondence, not an office. It does not mean that his early texts grant love, beauty, sexuality, or reconciliation merely because later occultism associates those things with Venus.

The same instructional tradition states that dukes may be bound from the **first hour after sunrise until noon**, with **clear weather** observed. Scot prints substantially the same rule: “Dukes may be bound from the first houre till noone; and cleare wether is to be observed.” See [Scot’s text](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm) and Peterson’s [Goetia](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

The *Goetia* also favors operations when the Moon is two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, or fourteen days old. This is separate from Crowley’s decan table and Lenain’s Shem-angel hour.

Scot also transmits the grimoire convention that a legion consists of **6,666 spirits**. This is magical arithmetic, not the historical size of a Roman legion.

## The seal

### Source history

**[Documented text]**

- Weyer supplies no seal for Amduscias.
- Scot supplies no seal.
- Individual seals occur in the surviving *Goetia* manuscripts.
- Sloane 3825 directs that Amduscias’s seal be worn as a lamen.
- Harley 6483 surrounds or supplements Goetic seals with angelic and Hebrew material.
- Mathers redrew the seals for the 1904 publication.

The familiar printed seal is therefore part of the seventeenth-century *Goetia* tradition, not evidence from Weyer’s 1577 catalog.

### Description of the 1904 redrawing

The figure is an asymmetrical monoline sigil. Its main features are:

1. a small central circular or looped element crossed by a short bar;
2. two broad bowed strokes extending toward the left;
3. small cross-like terminals at the left extremities;
4. a longer stroke descending and curving toward the lower right;
5. a dot-like or knobbed terminal on the descending stroke.

See the [public-domain seal image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:67-Amdusias_seal.png).

**[Caution]** This is a description of the common Mathers redrawing, not an encoded interpretation. Early sources do not explain what its arcs, crosses, loop, or dot “mean.” Modern claims that each component symbolizes a planet, musical note, tree, or unicorn are later symbolic inventions unless supported by a separately cited system.

### Manuscript variation

Peterson’s collation indicates that the same basic sigil family is copied with ordinary scribal differences in stroke shape, closure, terminal form, and attached spelling. Harley 6483 is especially distinctive because it integrates the seal into Rudd’s angel-constraining apparatus.

**[Open paleographical problem]** A truly exact stemma of every stroke in Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825, Sloane 3648, and Harley 6483 would require side-by-side high-resolution manuscript images. Printed redrawings and textual collations do not preserve every pen lift or correction. Claims of an exact “original seal” should therefore be treated cautiously.

## The angel: Eiael or Eyael

### Identification

In the Rudd recension, the 67th Goetic spirit is paired by serial number with the 67th angel of the seventy-twofold Name:

> **Amduscias/Amducias — Eiael/Eyael.**

Principal modern spellings include **Eiael, Eyael, Eïael**, and occasionally **Ayael**. Harley material can be misread through handwriting or OCR as **Eisel**.

The usual Hebrew form is:

> **איעאל**

This consists of the consonantal triplet **איע** with the divine suffix **אל**, *El*, “God.”

**[Caution]** The triplet itself is not an ordinary Hebrew word with a securely recoverable dictionary meaning. English or French formulations such as “God, delight of the children of men” are traditional attributes or devotional glosses, not strict philological translations of איע.

### The seventy-two names and Exodus 14:19–21

**[Tradition]** The seventy-two triplets are derived from Exodus 14:19–21, three Hebrew verses traditionally counted as seventy-two letters each. The letters are arranged boustrophedon-style:

1. verse 19 in its normal right-to-left direction;
2. verse 20 reversed;
3. verse 21 in its normal direction;
4. the three letters in each column form one name.

Christian Kabbalistic systems then add **-El** or **-Yah** endings to form pronounceable angelic names.

The expression *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally means the “explicit,” “distinguished,” or “fully expressed” Name. In early rabbinic literature it primarily concerns the divine Name, especially the Tetragrammaton. Its later application to a seventy-twofold sequence is a historical development, not the term’s sole or original meaning. The medieval background is discussed in scholarship on the seventy-two-letter Name, including the [Journal for the Study of the Old Testament article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205) and the study [“72 in His Name”](https://e-univers.ru/upload/iblock/919/1d14cc5s1qdrw3vc1f1360nmgkaohtru.pdf).

### Historical transmission

A defensible outline is:

1. **Rabbinic and medieval Jewish traditions** develop several expanded divine Names, including 42- and 72-letter constructions.
2. **Rashi’s medieval commentary tradition** associates Exodus 14:19–21 with a seventy-twofold Name.
3. **Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh**, compiled from medieval materials and first printed in 1701, transmits forms of the seventy-twofold tradition.
4. **Johannes Reuchlin**, especially *De arte cabalistica* (1517), presents Christian Kabbalistic angelic forms generated with divine suffixes.
5. **Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa**, *De occulta philosophia* III.25, printed in 1533, disseminates a list of seventy-two angels.
6. **Blaise de Vigenère** transmits seal designs associated with the angels.
7. **Athanasius Kircher**, *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54), prints a widely reproduced diagram of the seventy-two names and their supposed national or linguistic correspondences. See the [Kircher diagram](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg).
8. **The Rudd manuscripts**, copied in 1712–14, systematically oppose the angels to the Goetic spirits.
9. **Lazare Républicain Lenain**, *La science cabalistique* (1823), gives zodiacal degrees, dates, times, Psalms, offices, and “contrary” spirits.
10. **Papus**, in the 1890s, republishes and popularizes Lenain’s tables.
11. **Golden Dawn materials** reorganize the names into a Leo-starting zodiacal scheme and attach them to its broader astrological and tarot system.
12. **Robert Ambelain**, beginning with *La Kabbale pratique* (1951), combines Lenain’s material with angelic seals and ritual instructions.
13. **Haziel**, especially in the early 1990s, popularizes the modern “guardian angel by birthday” presentation.

### Eyael in Lenain, 1823

Lenain’s entry, p. 92, reads:

> **67e. E'iael. Son attribut (Dieu, délice des enfants des hommes). Il correspond au nom Bogo, suivant la langue des Albanais. Son rayon commence depuis le 331e degré jusqu’au 335e, inclusivement, correspondant à la 34e décade et au génie nommé Abiou, sous l’influence de Saturne. Il préside les jours suivants: 25 mai, 5 août, 16 octobre, 27 décembre, 9 mars. L’on invoque ce génie depuis 10 heures du soir jusqu’à 10 heures 20 minutes; on prononce la demande avec les noms divins et le 4e verset du psaume 36.**

He continues that Eiael serves:

- for consolation in adversity;
- for acquiring wisdom;
- over changes;
- over the preservation of monuments;
- over longevity;
- over occult sciences;
- to reveal truth to those who invoke him in their work.

A person born under the influence is said to love solitude and to distinguish himself or herself in astronomy, physics, philosophy, and higher sciences. The “contrary angel” is said to rule error, prejudice, and the propagators of erroneous systems.

Source: Lenain, *La science cabalistique* (Paris, 1823), p. 92; see the [Google Books record and scan](https://books.google.com/books/about/La_science_cabalistique_ou_l_art_de_conn.html?hl=fr&id=BqzyzEEX3MAC&output=html_text).

### Zodiacal degrees

Lenain’s “331st through 335th degrees” uses ordinal counting through a zodiac beginning at Aries. In modern zero-based notation this is conventionally rendered:

> **0°00′–4°59′ Pisces**, or simply **0°–5° Pisces**.

It lies in the first decan of Pisces, traditionally ruled by Saturn in the Golden Dawn tarot scheme. If this independent quinance is overlaid on that tarot system, it falls within the **Eight of Cups**, Saturn in Pisces. That is a later cross-combination; Lenain himself does not call Eiael an Eight-of-Cups angel.

Lenain also assigns five discontinuous calendar days:

- 25 May;
- 5 August;
- 16 October;
- 27 December;
- 9 March.

These arise from a secondary daily cycle and should not be confused with the consecutive modern guardian-angel dates around 20–24 February.

### Psalm

Lenain and the Rudd recension use Psalm 36:4 in Vulgate numbering, Psalm 37:4 in most modern English numbering:

> **Delectare in Domino et dabit tibi petitiones cordis tui.**

Hebrew:

> **וְהִתְעַנַּג עַל־יְהוָה וְיִתֶּן־לְךָ מִשְׁאֲלֹת לִבֶּךָ**

English:

> Delight yourself in the LORD, and he will give you the desires of your heart.

Lenain’s invocation time is **10:00 p.m. to 10:20 p.m.**

### Rudd’s method of constraint

**[Documented ritual tradition]** Harley 6483 does not merely list an angel beside each demon. Its method places the appropriate angelic name and seal in a position of authority over the Goetic spirit. In the reconstructed Rudd procedure:

1. the operator invokes the corresponding Shem angel;
2. the angel’s seal or name is worn toward the heart;
3. the demon’s seal occupies the opposite face;
4. the demon is commanded under the divine and angelic authority;
5. the Psalm verse reinforces that authority.

The seventy-two angels therefore function as **constraints, correctives, or celestial superiors**, not as companions equal to the spirits. Skinner and Rankine explain this arrangement in [*The Goetia of Dr Rudd*](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

**[Disputed authorship]** The manuscripts contain attributions to “Dr Rudd,” but identifying their author directly with the engineer and mathematician Thomas Rudd is conjectural. Thomas Rudd died decades before the surviving Harley copies were made. The wording may mark ownership, transmission, or an earlier source rather than autograph authorship. Dan Harms discusses these problems in his reviews of the [Rudd edition, part 2](https://danharms.wordpress.com/2007/10/29/on-the-shelf-the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-part-2/) and [part 3](https://danharms.wordpress.com/2007/11/01/on-the-shelf-the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-part-3/).

### Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

**Papus.** Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced Lenain-derived angelic tables in works such as *Traité méthodique de science occulte* (1891) and *La Kabbale* (1892). His role is primarily one of synthesis and popularization. See the [Hermetic Library edition](https://hermetic.com/fr/encausse/traite-methodique-de-science-occulte/index).

**Ambelain.** Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* reproduces Eiael’s Lenain-derived powers and combines the seventy-two angels with seals and talismanic procedures. A later addendum claims, on the basis of Ambelain’s occult experiences, that some commonly printed seals belong to “contrary” entities and may be dangerous. That warning is **[Esoteric testimony]**, not textual proof about the medieval origin of the seals. See the [digitized text](https://djvu.online/file/O6LjS6ZXfyHrE) and [BnF catalog record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35147709s).

**Haziel.** Haziel’s *Communiquer avec son ange gardien* popularizes Eyael as the guardian angel of people born approximately **20–24 February**, with “transubstantiation” given as a spiritual quality or “essence.” This psychologized birthday-angel presentation is a late twentieth-century development, not part of Weyer, the seventeenth-century *Goetia*, or Lenain’s exact calendar method.

### Why each pip carries two demons

Crowley’s *777* assigns the seventy-two spirits across the thirty-six decans twice: one set by day and another by night. It does **not** divide each decan into two five-degree segments for the two demons.

For the first decan of Aquarius:

| Portion | Spirit | Tarot card |
|---|---|---|
| Entire 0°–10° decan, by day | Foras, #31 | Five of Swords |
| Entire 0°–10° decan, by night | Amdusias, #67 | Five of Swords |

Thus both spirits belong to the same ten-degree decan and pip card; day and night distinguish them. See Crowley, [*Liber 777*](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/0777-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf), and the [Five of Swords attribution in *The Book of Thoth/Tarot descriptions*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib78).

### Amdusias versus Eyael: two different zodiacal locations

| Entity/system | Zodiacal position |
|---|---|
| Amdusias in *777* | 0°–10° Aquarius, night |
| Eyael in Lenain’s Aries-start sequence | 0°–5° Pisces |
| Eyael if cross-mapped to tarot | First half of the Eight of Cups decan |
| Eyael in the Golden Dawn’s Leo-start Shem sequence | 0°–5° Cancer |

The disagreement is not necessarily a copying error. It reflects different starting points and different purposes:

- Lenain begins the angelic zodiacal succession at Aries.
- Golden Dawn materials place the start of the Shem sequence at the zodiacal position associated with **Cor Leonis**, the Heart of the Lion.
- Crowley’s demon table independently distributes the Goetic list through day and night decans.

Skinner and Rankine expressly discuss competing starting points—0° Aries, 0° Leo, and a Sirius-based position near 12° Leo—and warn that the choice materially changes every angel’s zodiacal place.

## Enochian and other later overlays

Crowley’s 1904 edition adds several elements that are not part of Weyer’s entry:

- the “Bornless” or “Preliminary Invocation”;
- Enochian-language versions or substitutions in the conjurations;
- temple diagrams and magical equipment;
- Crowley’s own editorial and initiatory framing.

These are **[Later magical overlays]**. There is no securely demonstrated medieval or Weyer-era “Enochian identity” for Amdusias.

Harley 6483 and related Rudd material sometimes integrate Goetic operations with John Dee-derived tables, angelic names, and a Table of Practice. Skinner and Rankine explore these correspondences, but no early source simply says that Amdusias is identical with a particular Enochian governor, cacodemon, or aethyric entity.

**[Finding: absent]** An Amdusias-specific Enochian name reliably attested in Dee’s original spirit diaries was not found.

## Iconography

### Before de Plancy

Weyer, Scot, and the prose *Goetia* do not provide a portrait. Their imagery is verbal:

- a unicorn;
- subsequently a man;
- invisible trumpets and instruments;
- bending trees.

The Goetic seal is not a portrait and contains no explicit unicorn or musical instrument.

### Collin de Plancy’s entry

Collin de Plancy included Amduscias in the *Dictionnaire infernal*, first published in 1818 and repeatedly revised. The verified sixth-edition entry of 1863, p. 24, reads:

> **Amduscias, grand-duc aux enfers. Il a la forme d’une licorne ; mais lorsqu’il est évoqué, il se montre sous une figure humaine. Il donne des concerts, si on les lui commande ; on entend alors, sans rien voir, le son des trompettes et des autres instruments de musique. Les arbres s’inclinent à sa voix. Il commande vingt-neuf légions.**

Translation:

> Amduscias, a grand duke in hell. He has the form of a unicorn; but when evoked, he shows himself in human form. He gives concerts if commanded; the sound of trumpets and other musical instruments is then heard without anything being seen. Trees bow at his voice. He commands twenty-nine legions.

Source: de Plancy, [*Dictionnaire infernal*, 6th ed., “Amduscias”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Amduscias); complete [1863 scan](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_2).

De Plancy makes two notable literary changes:

- the invisible instrumental effect becomes a **concert**;
- trees bow “at his voice,” rather than directly at the exorcist’s will.

He omits the gift of familiars.

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

The famous portrait belongs to the illustrated **1863 edition**, not to the unillustrated 1818 first edition. Louis Le Breton depicts a compact, crouching humanoid with:

- a large equine or unicorn head;
- a long horn;
- pointed ears or a spiked mane;
- clawed hands and feet;
- muscular humanlike limbs;
- musical objects or instruments at ground level.

See the [Le Breton image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Amduscias_(Dictionnaire_infernal).png) and the [high-resolution public-domain image](https://pdimagearchive.org/images/c5209967-9573-4e74-8184-d10e099d40d5/).

The image does not simply illustrate the two successive forms in the text. It hybridizes them into one grotesque body: human limbs and posture with the enormous horned head.

### Thunder, storms, and infernal cacophony

**[Modern expansion]** Later summaries frequently call Amdusias a demon of thunder, storms, cacophonous music, or the “musician of hell.” These characterizations are understandable elaborations of loud invisible trumpets and bending trees, but the specific words **thunder** and **storm** are absent from the verified Weyer, Scot, Goetia, and de Plancy entries.

The title “demon of music” is a reasonable modern shorthand. “Demon of thunder” should be identified as a later extrapolation, not quoted as an early office.

### Modern art

Modern occult decks, tattoos, game art, and online demonology illustrations commonly combine:

- Le Breton’s humanoid unicorn;
- the Goetic seal;
- trumpets or orchestral instruments;
- wind-bent trees;
- Venusian copper or green coloring;
- infernal lightning and storm imagery.

Only the unicorn, human form, invisible instruments, trees, and seal are rooted in the early textual chain. Copper and Venus come from the rank table. Lightning, storm control, musical notation encoded in the seal, and fixed colors are later artistic interpretation.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer preserved the earliest secure Amduscias text. His demon catalog should be read within his broader attack on witch prosecutions. He accepted a demonological worldview, but argued that many accused witches were melancholic, deceived, or exploited rather than powerful servants of Satan. His catalog consequently has a paradoxical history: intended partly to expose magical pretension, it became a major source for later ceremonial magic.

### Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot published the first English form in 1584. The translation is often attributed to Abraham Fleming. Scot’s overriding purpose was to challenge credulity, fraudulent conjurers, and the persecution of alleged witches. His wording nevertheless supplied much of the English inherited by the *Goetia*.

### The anonymous *Lemegeton* compilers

The *Lesser Key of Solomon* is a composite seventeenth-century compilation, not a book written by Solomon. Its first book, *Ars Goetia*, reorganized Weyer-derived material, added spirits, supplied seals, ritual instructions, and a Solomonic brass-vessel legend.

Sloane 3825 is Peterson’s principal witness because it is comparatively complete and textually useful. Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, and Harley 6483 preserve alternative readings and arrangements. See Peterson’s [introduction to the *Lemegeton* manuscripts](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm).

### “Dr Rudd”

The Rudd recension added the systematic Shem-angel constraint. It is traditionally associated with Dr Thomas Rudd, but the surviving manuscripts are later copies and the precise authorship remains disputed.

### Mathers and Crowley

S. L. MacGregor Mathers translated and edited the text; Aleister Crowley published it in 1904 with his own prefatory and ritual material. Their edition established **Amdusias** as the dominant modern spelling and introduced **Amdukias** as an alternative. It also propagated the “not soon or immediately” corruption.

Crowley’s *777* subsequently attached Amdusias to the first Aquarius decan by night, the Five of Swords, and the humorous “dilatory bandmaster” image.

### Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy transformed the short grimoire notice into an encyclopedia entry. Le Breton’s 1863 image became the definitive popular portrait, far more influential in modern art than the abstract Goetic seal.

### Lenain, Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

These authors belong primarily to **Eyael’s** reception:

- Lenain supplied the detailed zodiacal, calendrical, Psalmic, and moral attributes.
- Papus republished the French occult synthesis.
- Ambelain added practical Kabbalistic ritual and seals.
- Haziel popularized the guardian-angel birthday model.

Their material should not be read backward into the seventeenth-century Amdusias entry.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 *Lesser Key of Solomon* is a critical edition based principally on Sloane 3825, with manuscript variants and source comparisons. His online texts make Weyer, Scot, and the Goetia directly comparable. Peterson’s work is editorial and historical; it does not constitute a claim to have performed an Amdusias operation.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483 and its angelic counter-seals widely available. They argue for the practical importance of calling the Shem angel before constraining the demon, while also reconstructing ritual elements from related manuscripts.

### Poke Runyon

Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s modern Solomonic system uses mirror scrying, zodiacal placement, and angelic constraints related to the Rudd scheme. This is an important modern practical synthesis, but no independently documented, Amdusias-specific operation was found in the material examined.

### Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* repeats and expands the Crowley system. Its Amdusias correspondence gives approximately 20–29 January, the first Aquarius decan by night, the Five of Swords, and Venus/copper. It is a late twentieth-century interpretive edition, not an independent manuscript witness. See the [digitized edition](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf).

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent wrote extensively about the historical relationships among the *Goetia*, *Grimorium Verum*, spirit catalogs, folk magic, and necromantic traditions. His work is relevant to the larger genealogy, but no distinctive Amdusias etymology or pre-Weyer identification attributable specifically to him was verified.

### Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Jean-Patrice Boudet, and Julien Véronèse

These scholars are important for the history of grimoires, learned magic, and medieval demonological catalogs. Boudet’s concordance is directly useful for the negative pre-Weyer finding. Véronèse’s scholarship helps establish the medieval ritual-magic setting, but the direct Amdusias concordance should be credited to Boudet.

### Frater Achad

**[Finding: absent]** No specific Amdusias operation, doctrine, or substantial discussion by Frater Achad was located. His general importance to Thelemic and Qabalistic literature is not evidence of an Amdusias connection.

## Grimoire ancestors and analogues

### *Livre des Esperitz*

This French catalog is an important ancestor or close relative of the Weyer tradition. It contains many recognizable later Goetic names and offices, but not a securely identifiable Amdusias. See the [digital transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/livre-esperitz/read/).

### Munich Handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook contains procedures and spirits related to later European ritual magic. Some names resemble later catalog spirits. Amdusias is not securely among them.

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer’s named source belonged to a family of “books of the offices of spirits.” A surviving Folger manuscript represents that family but does not contain Amdusias by name. Similarity of title does not mean the surviving Folger copy is Weyer’s exact exemplar.

### *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin’s lists include many spirit names that entered early modern magical literature, but no unambiguous Amdusias was located.

### *Testament of Solomon*

The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* supplies the broad precedent of Solomon interrogating demons, learning their forms and powers, and constraining them through angels. It does not contain Amdusias. See the [English text of the *Testament of Solomon*](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm).

The Rudd method—an angel set over a demon—resembles the *Testament*’s general logic, but this does not prove direct transmission of Amdusias from antiquity.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Does Amdusias have an ancient origin?

**Finding:** No demonstrable one.

The name has no securely established Semitic, Greek, Latin, Iranian, Egyptian, or Celtic derivation. Weyer’s 1577 text is the earliest secure attestation. A lost earlier manuscript may have contained it, but its date and spelling cannot presently be reconstructed.

### 2. Is Andriflides an earlier Amdusias?

**Finding:** Possible list-position relationship, unproved identity.

The names are not close enough to establish derivation without intermediate forms, and the surrounding catalog contains other similar *Andrif-* readings. It belongs under disputed source genealogy.

### 3. Is Deyclo an earlier Amdusias?

**Finding:** The functional resemblance is insufficient.

A tree-related power is shared, but name, rank, form, offices, and legion count disagree. It may illustrate how spirit offices migrated between catalogs; it does not attest Amdusias.

### 4. Does Amdusias command thunder?

**Finding:** Not in the early texts.

Invisible trumpets and bending trees may evoke thunder and wind, but “thunder demon” is a modern interpretation.

### 5. Does he delay music?

**Finding:** Only because of a 1904 corruption.

The manuscript reading is “heard but not seen.” Mathers–Crowley prints “not soon or immediately.” Crowley’s “dilatory bandmaster” preserves the joke, not an older office.

### 6. Does he give familiars?

There is genuine textual development:

- Weyer: ambiguous excellence “with servants.”
- Scot: “excellent among familiars.”
- *Goetia*: “gives excellent familiars.”

The familiar-granting office is clearly documented in the *Goetia*, but less clear in Weyer’s Latin.

### 7. Is the human form a transformation?

Weyer mentions unicorn and human form somewhat compactly. The *Goetia* makes the sequence explicit: unicorn first, human afterward at the exorcist’s request. Modern artwork often creates a permanent unicorn-headed humanoid instead.

### 8. Is the seal ancient or Solomonic?

**Finding:** No evidence demonstrates that.

The sigil is absent from Weyer and Scot and first survives in seventeenth-century Goetic manuscripts. Calling it “Solomon’s original seal of Amdusias” is a statement of grimoire legend, not established provenance.

### 9. Is Thomas Rudd the author of Harley 6483?

**Finding:** Disputed.

The Rudd attribution may preserve an earlier owner or transmitter, but the surviving manuscript is not Thomas Rudd’s autograph and postdates his lifetime.

### 10. Is Eyael intrinsically Amdusias’s opposite?

**Finding:** Only within the Rudd system.

The Shem angel tradition developed independently of Weyer’s demon catalog. Rudd pairs the two because both are number 67. Earlier Jewish or Christian Kabbalistic sources do not identify Eyael as “the angel over Amdusias.”

### 11. Is Eyael at 0°–5° Pisces or 0°–5° Cancer?

Both readings occur in internally coherent but different systems:

- **0°–5° Pisces** — Lenain’s Aries-start sequence and most modern guardian-angel literature.
- **0°–5° Cancer** — the Golden Dawn’s Leo-start sequence.

A source should be named whenever the degree is given.

### 12. Is Amdusias’s tarot card the Eight of Cups or Five of Swords?

- **Five of Swords** is Amdusias’s explicit *777* assignment.
- **Eight of Cups** arises only if Eyael’s Lenain quinance at 0°–5° Pisces is independently overlaid on Golden Dawn decans.

Therefore the basic Amdusias answer is **Five of Swords**.

### 13. Does Venus make Amdusias a love demon?

**Finding:** No early textual basis.

Venus and copper follow automatically from the rank “Duke.” The early offices do not mention love. Modern practitioners may interpret Venus more broadly, but that is an esoteric expansion.

### 14. Are Amdusias, Asmodeus, and Amaymon the same?

**Finding:** No historical source chain establishes that identity.

Claims that Amdusias is a brother, mask, subordinate form, or aspect of Asmodeus or Amaymon belong mainly to modern demonolatry and personal revelation. The early catalogs treat them as distinct names and offices.

### 15. Is “Ambduscias” the oldest spelling?

**Finding:** No.

The documented early form is **Amduscias**. *Ambduscias* is a later spelling and should not be silently substituted into quotations.

## Open questions

1. **Weyer’s immediate exemplar:** His source is lost or unidentified. A discovery of that manuscript could move the name’s attestation earlier than 1577.

2. **The change from Amduscias to Amdukias:** Mathers’s source for *Amdukias* is not transparent. It may reflect a manuscript reading, editorial conjecture, or attempted transliteration.

3. **The Sloane 2731 spelling layers:** The relationship among *Amduscias*, *Andusias*, and *Anduscias* deserves direct study of the manuscript’s inks, hands, and sigil captions.

4. **Seal genealogy:** High-resolution comparison could determine which strokes are stable across Sloane 2731, 3648, 3825, and Harley 6483, and which were introduced by Mathers’s redrawing.

5. **The phrase *optimus est una cum famulis*:** A wider comparison with Weyer’s Latin usage might clarify whether it describes Amduscias’s own attendants, his usefulness with familiar spirits, or excellence as a familiar.

6. **Rudd attribution:** No decisive documentary bridge has yet shown how material associated with Thomas Rudd reached the 1712–14 Harley copies.

7. **Exact route of Eyael’s Psalm:** The Psalm assignment is present in the Rudd tradition before Lenain, but the immediate table or manuscript from which Rudd’s copyist obtained it could be further clarified.

8. **De Plancy’s first-edition wording:** A line-by-line comparison of the 1818 and 1863 entries would establish exactly which verbal embellishments appeared in which revision.

9. **Modern fiction:** Games and comics continually reuse Goetic names. Claims about *Persona*, *Helluva Boss*, films, or new titles require rechecking against primary credits and released material rather than fan lists.

## Conclusions

The historically secure Amdusias is a comparatively minor catalog spirit whose identity is remarkably stable from 1577 onward:

- a great and strong duke;
- first appearing as a unicorn;
- compelled into human form;
- producing invisible instrumental music;
- bending trees;
- associated, through an evolving translation, with excellent familiars;
- commanding twenty-nine legions.

Everything before Weyer is uncertain. No ancient god or demonstrable medieval name lies behind him. The seal enters with the seventeenth-century *Goetia*. Venus and copper derive from his rank. The “not soon or immediately” delay is a 1904 corruption, later immortalized by Crowley’s “dilatory bandmaster.”

Eyael’s connection to Amdusias is real within the Rudd recension but is not primordial. It is a systematic numerical pairing between two previously independent lists. Lenain’s 0°–5° Pisces quinance, 10:00–10:20 p.m. hour, Psalm, and moral offices belong to the angelic tradition; Crowley’s 0°–10° Aquarius, nighttime, and Five of Swords belong to a different demonological correspondence table.

The modern humanoid-unicorn image comes chiefly from Louis Le Breton’s 1863 engraving. Thunder, storm control, love powers, hidden ancient etymologies, and identities with other demons are later extrapolations or inventions rather than findings of the early sources.

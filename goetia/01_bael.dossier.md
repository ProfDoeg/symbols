# Bael: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes several kinds of statement:

- **[Documented text]** — stated in a named primary source or visible manuscript witness.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a historical or philological inference argued from the surviving witnesses.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim transmitted within religious, magical, or occult literature, without independent historical proof.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an occult attribution created by combining separate symbolic systems.
- **[Disputed]** — competing readings or insufficient evidence prevent a secure conclusion.
- **[Legend]** — a narrative presented as history within the tradition but unsupported historically.
- **[Modern invention]** — an identifiable recent interpretation, practice, image, or fictional adaptation.

A textual constraint should be stated openly. Weyer, Scot, the manuscript editions, and Mathers–Crowley are available online, but I cannot reproduce extended passages from each hosted edition verbatim. Accordingly, I give brief representative quotations, edition and page references, followed by complete clause-by-clause accounts of what each passage says. The links lead directly to the complete texts.

The three most important qualifications are:

1. **[Documented text]** Bael is earlier than Weyer. Cambridge University Library MS Dd.4.35, assigned broadly to the fifteenth century, already contains *Baal* with the three-headed appearance, hoarse voice, invisibility, and sixty-six legions. Weyer’s 1577 appendix is the first known print appearance, not the earliest surviving witness. [Cambridge manuscript description](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/ad3bd470-4461-4506-b43a-158fa74bf412/content)

2. **[Esoteric correspondence]** “Bael = 0°–5° Aries” is not what Crowley’s *777* actually says. *777* places Bael across the whole 0°–10° first decan of Aries by day. The five-degree Aries sector belongs to Vehuiah in Lenain’s separate angelic calendar; modern authors often merge the two.

3. **[Disputed]** Bael’s derivation from *Baal* is philologically compelling, but no medieval or early-modern Bael entry explicitly identifies the spirit with Baal-Hadad. Collin de Plancy was still posing the identification as a question in 1863.

## The name and its origins

### 1. Ancient *baʿal*

**[Documented linguistic history]** Northwest Semitic *baʿal*—Ugaritic 𐎁𐎓𐎍, Phoenician and Hebrew בעל—means an owner, master, husband, or lord. It was therefore both an ordinary noun and a divine title. Different cities and sanctuaries could have their own “Baal,” qualified by place or function: Baal of Peor, Baal of the Covenant, Baal of Ṣaphon, Baal of Heaven, and others. [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/ancient-religions/ancient-religion/baal), [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/religion/ba-al/)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** In the Late Bronze Age texts from Ugarit, *Baʿlu* “the Lord” normally denotes the weather and warrior god Haddu/Hadad. He is the cloud-rider, rain giver, thunderer, opponent of Sea/Yamm and Death/Mot, and central figure of the Ugaritic Baal Cycle. Thus “Baal-Hadad” is a useful scholarly identification when discussing the Ugaritic god—but it does not mean that every ancient divine title *Baal* designated precisely that deity. [Dictionary of Deities and Demons material](https://www.marquette.edu/maqom/baal.pdf), [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/baal-worship)

**[Disputed]** Older scholarship often treated “Baal” as one universal Phoenician or Canaanite god. Modern inscriptional and Ugaritic study requires a more careful formulation: Hadad is the most important referent, but *baʿal* could remain a title and could denote different local gods. Consequently, “Bael was originally Baal-Hadad” is plausible shorthand, not a demonstrated one-to-one biography.

**[Documented biblical reception]** Biblical authors polemicize against “Baal” and “the Baals.” The title’s association with forbidden worship helped make it available for later Christian demonology. That process also produced related but distinct names:

- **Baal-zebub / Beelzebub** — associated with the god of Ekron and later the prince of demons.
- **Baal-berith / Berith** — “Lord of the Covenant,” later a separate grimoire spirit.
- **Baal-peor / Belphegor** — a separate chain of reception.
- **Bel** — Akkadian *bēlu*, “lord,” used especially of Marduk; cognate in sense but not automatically the same divine personality.
- **Belial / Beliyaʿal** — from Hebrew *beliyyaʿal*, conventionally “worthlessness” or “wickedness,” not an etymological form of Baal.
- **Balam/Balaam** — a separate Goetic name.
- **Buer** — a separate Goetic president; the occasional form “Buel” should not be folded into Bael without manuscript evidence.

### 2. Does the Goetic name derive from Baal?

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The transition *Baal → Bael/Beal* requires little more than ordinary scribal and vernacular variation. The exact form *Baal* occurs in the fifteenth-century Cambridge manuscript; *Beal* occurs in the French *Livre des esperitz*; *Bael* appears in Weyer’s Latin. That chain makes derivation from the biblical or inherited demonological name *Baal* considerably more likely than coincidence.

**[Disputed]** What cannot be reconstructed is the intermediary narrative. No surviving witness says that the storm god lost a battle, fell from heaven, acquired three animal heads, or became an invisibility spirit. The animal appearance and office belong to the medieval magical catalogue, not to known Ugaritic myth.

**[Tradition]** Modern occultists such as Poke Runyon treat Bael as the continuing ancient god Baʿal and Astaroth as Astarte. Runyon consequently regards coercive Goetic curses as inappropriate when dealing with them. This is a modern religious identification, not the conclusion of the medieval sources. [Runyon’s account](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition)

## The description across the sources

## 1. Cambridge University Library MS Dd.4.35

**Date:** broadly fifteenth century.  
**Location:** fol. 28.  
**Name:** *Baal*.  
**Status:** **[Documented text]**, but the catalogue date “XV” applies broadly to the codex; a full paleographical study of the demonological unit would be desirable.

A short excerpt reads:

> “Baal est primus rex sub potestate orientis constitutus.”

The complete content, translated clause by clause, is:

- Baal is the first king established under the authority of Oriens.
- He appears with three heads.
- The first is a toad’s head.
- The second is a lion’s head.
- The third is a cat’s head.
- He always speaks with a hoarse voice.
- He is a leader of armed men and fighters.
- He makes a person invisible.
- He has sixty-six legions of noble, strong spirits beneath him.

The crucial difference is the **lion head**, where Weyer later has a **human head**. The military description resembles Weyer’s *insignis certator*, “distinguished fighter,” more closely than Scot or the *Goetia*, both of which omit it. [Cambridge transcription, pp. 1–2](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/ad3bd470-4461-4506-b43a-158fa74bf412/content)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This witness demonstrates that Weyer did not invent Bael’s entry. It also suggests that Weyer’s puzzling description of Bael as a fighter or “former of manners” reflects damaged or transformed wording in an older catalogue.

## 2. *Livre des esperitz*

**Date:** the textual tradition is commonly placed in the fifteenth or sixteenth century; Jean-Patrice Boudet dates the surviving Trinity College O.8.29 copy to the mid-sixteenth century.  
**Name:** *Beal*.  
**Rank:** great king, subject to Orient.  
**Legions:** six.

Briefly:

> “Beal est ung grant roy … et faict home invisible merveilleusement.”

Its complete substance is:

- Beal is a great king beneath and subject to Orient.
- He makes a person wonderfully invisible.
- He gives his grace or favor in all things.
- Six legions are beneath him.

No physical appearance, voice, wisdom, seal, or angel appears. [Boudet’s edition](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Jean-Patrice-Boudet-2/publication/30447646_Les_who%27s_who_demonologiques_de_la_Renaissance_et_leurs_ancetres_medievaux/links/5f946a79299bf1b53e43469a/Les-whos-who-demonologiques-de-la-Renaissance-et-leurs-ancetres-medievaux.pdf), [transcribed entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Beal)

**[Disputed]** The six legions may be an abbreviation, omission, or independent reading. Because the Cambridge manuscript already has sixty-six, it is unsafe to say that Weyer simply inflated six into sixty-six.

## 3. Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* (1577)

**Edition:** appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum*, Basel, 1577, original p. 914.  
**Name:** *Bael/Baël*.  
**Number:** 1.  
**Rank:** king.  
**Legions:** 66.  
**Seal:** none.

Representative Latin:

> “Primus Rex, qui est de potestate Orientis, dicitur Bael, apparens tribus capitibus…”

The complete Latin entry states:

- He is the first king.
- He belongs to the power of the East.
- He is called Bael.
- He appears with three heads: toad, man, and cat.
- He speaks with a hoarse voice.
- He is *formator morum et insignis certator*.
- He renders a person invisible and wise.
- Sixty-six legions obey him.

[Weyer’s Latin and Peterson’s comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### The disputed Latin phrase

**[Disputed translation]** *Formator morum* can mean a former, shaper, educator, or regulator of manners/character. Renderings such as “well formed,” “master of manners,” or “forms morals” found in later summaries are not equivalent. *Insignis certator* is more straightforward: an outstanding combatant, contestant, or fighter.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The earlier Cambridge text’s *ductor armatorum et pugnatorum*—leader of armed men and fighters—suggests that at least the fighting clause descends from an older military office. Whether *formator morum* is genuine, a learned recasting, or corruption remains unresolved.

### Weyer’s purpose

Weyer was not publishing an approved operations manual. **[Documented text]** He explains that he omitted material to make the work unusable and presented the catalogue in a skeptical attack on magical imposture and witch persecution. He named his source *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also styled *Empto Salomonis*. [Weyer introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

## 4. Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584)

**Book XV, chapter 2; original p. 217.**  
**Name:** heading *Baell*, text *Baëll*.  
**Number:** first.  
**Seal:** none.

Representative excerpt:

> “Their first and principall king … is called Baëll who when he is conjured up, appeareth with three heads.”

The full substance is:

- He is the first and principal king.
- He is of the eastern power.
- When conjured, he appears with three heads: toad, man, cat.
- He speaks hoarsely.
- He makes a person invisible.
- He commands sixty-six legions of devils.

Scot omits Weyer’s wisdom, character-forming, and combat clauses. [Scot, Book XV](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm)

**[Documented context]** Scot’s book is also skeptical. The catalogue appears amid an attempt to expose conjurors’ pretensions, not as Scot’s profession of belief.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Peterson argues that the English translator—probably Abraham Fleming—may have used a poor manuscript copy rather than one of Weyer’s printed editions. The many mistakes in Scot subsequently entered English Goetic transmission.

## 5. Folger MS V.b.26, the *Book of Oberon* complex

**Date:** approximately 1577–1583.  
**Spelling:** *Baall*.  
**Status:** **[Documented collateral tradition]** rather than a direct copy of the later *Ars Goetia*.

Several lists overlap:

- One Baall is a king under Oriens who teaches sciences and makes people invisible.
- Another “first” Baall has power over love between men and women, makes people invisible, appears royally, and speaks hoarsely.
- A separate *Boab vel Boall* is a prelate or soldier-like spirit with a lion’s head, black horse, ox teeth, and unrelated offices including transportation of metals and disclosure of secrets.

[Transcription of Folger V.b.26](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

**[Controversy]** The last figure should not automatically be merged with Bael. This manuscript proves how similar spellings could attach to different spirit entries within the same compilation.

## 6. Sloane MS 2731

**Date:** 1686.  
**Name:** *Baell*.  
**Number:** 1.  
**Status:** **[Documented text]**, one of the principal complete *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

The entry says:

- The first principal spirit is a king ruling in the East called Baell.
- He makes people invisible.
- He rules sixty-six legions.
- He appears in diverse shapes: cat, toad, man, or all simultaneously.
- He speaks very hoarsely.
- His character must be worn as a lamen before him; otherwise he will not render homage.

[Sloane 2731 digital edition and facsimile](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/)

This is essentially the standard *Goetia* form. The conspicuous changes from Weyer are:

- “Of the power of the East” becomes “ruling in the East.”
- Three simultaneous heads become optional, alternating shapes.
- Wisdom, combat, and formation of character disappear.
- A seal and lamen instruction are added.

## 7. Sloane MS 3825

**Date:** seventeenth century.  
**Name:** *Bael*.  
**Status:** **[Documented text]** and Peterson’s base manuscript.

The complete content agrees with Sloane 2731 in all essentials: eastern king, invisibility, sixty-six legions, cat/toad/man shapes separately or together, hoarse voice, and a seal worn as a lamen. [Peterson’s manuscript-based transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#bael)

**[Important absence]** Bael is not said here to teach languages or sciences, give treasure, secure love, answer questions, supply familiars, or transmute substances. Those are offices of other spirits—or, in Bael’s case, readings from collateral *Offices* witnesses. The standard *Goetia* assigns him only invisibility.

**[Important absence]** The magician is not told to demand a second or human form from Bael. The catalogue merely says he may appear as cat, toad, man, or all at once. Instructions to compel a spirit into human form occur elsewhere in the ritual and in other individual entries, but not as a special Bael office.

## 8. Harley MS 6483: the Rudd/Smart recension

**Copy date:** internally 1712–1713.  
**Attributed source:** papers owned or written by Thomas Rudd, who died in 1656.  
**Name:** *Bael*.  
**Number:** 1.  
**Angel:** Vehuiah.

The wording has two striking variants:

- Cat, **dog**, and man—not cat, toad, and man.
- He speaks **hastily**, not hoarsely.

Otherwise it retains invisibility, sixty-six legions, eastern kingship, and the breast-lamen requirement. Skinner and Rankine print the entry on pp. 102–103 of *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*. [Searchable edition text](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

**[Disputed]** “Dog” and “hastily” may be scribal substitutions, misreadings, or deliberate variants. They are real features of the Harley recension, but no earlier witness examined here supports them.

**[Disputed attribution]** Harley 6483 is not Rudd’s surviving autograph. It is a fair copy associated with Peter Smart and dated decades after Rudd’s death. Skinner and Rankine reconstruct a pre-1656 Rudd exemplar; critics note that “Sheets Dr Rudd” may indicate possession or attribution rather than authorship. The safest designation is “the Rudd/Smart recension.” [Manuscript problem discussed here](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/06/addenda-et-scholia-2.html)

## 9. Mathers–Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* (1904)

**Printed page:** 22.  
**Name:** *Bael*.  
**Number:** 1.

Representative excerpt:

> “The First Principal Spirit is a King ruling in the East, called Bael.”

The rest gives invisibility, sixty-six legions, the cat/toad/man forms separately or together, a very hoarse voice, and the requirement that the character be worn as a lamen. [1904 text, p. 22](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

**[Documented publication history]** Mathers translated and arranged the manuscript text; Crowley edited, annotated, wrote the preliminary matter, and published it at Boleskine in 1904 through the Society for the Propagation of Religious Truth. Crowley also supplied Enochian translations of the conjurations and his “Preliminary Invocation.” [1904 bibliography](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/goetia_1904/goetia_1904.htm)

**[Important distinction]** Crowley’s Enochian work is a ritual overlay. It does not give Bael a historical Enochian name or change Bael’s catalogue entry.

## 10. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* was published by Weiser in 2001. It uses Sloane 3825 as its principal text, collated against Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses. [Publisher record](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/)

Peterson’s importance is not a new Bael mythology but textual control:

- He restores manuscript spellings and readings.
- He separates Weyer from Scot.
- He records the four Goetic additions and Pruflas’s omission.
- He distinguishes early *Offices of Spirits* catalogues from the later *Lemegeton*.
- He demonstrates that the Goetic seals are not in Weyer or the known early office catalogues.
- He treats much of the *Goetia* ritual as compilation from older works such as the *Heptameron*.

## Number, order, and the construction of the seventy-two

### Weyer’s sixty-nine

**[Documented text]** Weyer prints sixty-nine numbered spirits, even though Belial’s entry refers to the traditional total of seventy-two.

### The Goetia’s seventy-two

The *Goetia* reaches seventy-two by:

- adding **Vassago** at number 3;
- adding **Seere** at number 70;
- adding **Dantalion** at number 71;
- adding **Andromalius** at number 72;
- omitting Weyer’s **Pruflas alias Bufas**, number 4.

Thus:

\[
69 + 4 - 1 = 72
\]

[Peterson’s comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Pruflas may have vanished because Scot omitted the entry; the *Goetia* often follows Scot’s English rather than Weyer’s Latin. The rest of the list is extensively reordered, almost as if groups of leaves or excerpts were rearranged. No conclusive mechanism has been demonstrated.

**[Disputed]** Peterson suggests that the total may have been brought deliberately into agreement with the seventy-twofold divine name. Rudd’s one-to-one angelic pairing makes that numerical interpretation explicit, but the earliest *Offices of Spirits* witnesses do not pair their demons with Shem angels.

**[Documented stability]** Bael remains number 1 across Weyer, Scot, and the *Goetia*. His position is therefore older and more stable than most of the Goetic order.

## The angel: Vehuiah

## 1. The seventy-twofold Name

**[Documented Jewish exegetical tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the relevant Hebrew text, of three verses of seventy-two letters each. They are arranged in alternating directions:

1. Exodus 14:19 in its normal order;
2. Exodus 14:20 reversed;
3. Exodus 14:21 in its normal order.

Reading the resulting columns yields seventy-two groups of three letters. The first is:

\[
ו + ה + ו = והו
\]

That is conventionally transliterated **VHV**, *Vehu*, *Vahu*, or *Vaho*. The method is discussed by Rashi on Sukkah 45a and in the *Sefer ha-Bahir*. [Summary with source references](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030908920202700205)

**[Historical qualification]** *Shem ha-meforash* originally meant the explicit or distinctly pronounced divine name, especially the Tetragrammaton. Its use for the seventy-two triplets is a later development. The triplets are divine-name components before they are individualized “guardian angels.”

## 2. From divine triplet to angel

**[Christian-kabbalistic tradition]** Adding the theophoric ending *-Yah* to VHV produces **VHVYH**, conventionally **Vehuiah/Vehuyah**. Other names receive *-El*. Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) was a major printed vehicle for treating the seventy-two forms as angelic names. [Scholarly study of Reuchlin](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1zjg3h9)

Principal spellings include:

- Vehuiah
- Vehuyah
- Vahuiah
- Vahuaih
- Véhuiah
- Véhouiah
- Veluiah in some Renaissance printings

**[Philological caution]** “Vehuiah means ‘God elevated and exalted above all things’” is not a literal Hebrew translation. The underlying VHV is mechanically generated from Exodus; *Yah* is a divine element. “God the Exalter” or Lenain’s longer French phrase is an assigned interpretive attribute.

## 3. *Sefer Raziel* and Jewish magical transmission

**[Documented tradition]** The seventy-twofold name appears prominently in the medieval *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*, a composite Hebrew magical work existing in multiple manuscript recensions and first printed at Amsterdam in 1701. Its legendary frame says the angel Raziel revealed a book of heavenly secrets to Adam or Noah.

**[Legend]** Claims that Raziel personally delivered the exact printed book, or that Moses used precisely these later angel names to divide the Red Sea, belong to the book’s sacred history rather than verifiable textual history.

**[Historical qualification]** The Jewish seventy-twofold Name should not be retroactively equated with Rudd’s demon-control table. That one-to-one Bael–Vehuiah pairing is a Christian magical synthesis.

## 4. Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, volume II, part I (Rome, 1653), reproduced a diagram and table of the seventy-two names, drawing Jewish material into a vast Christian antiquarian synthesis. [Heidelberg digitization](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/kircher1653bd2_1), [public-domain plate](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg)

**[Documented context]** Kircher reported such material while criticizing magical uses of Kabbalah. Later occultists nevertheless used his tables constructively.

## 5. Rudd’s pairing

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483 places each Goetic spirit’s seal beside an angelic seal. The first pair is:

\[
\text{Vehuiah} \longleftrightarrow \text{Bael}
\]

The spirit seal appears on the left and the angelic material on the right, with Hebrew names above and a Psalm text incorporated into or associated with the angel’s seal. Skinner and Rankine describe the arrangement as unique among the principal *Lemegeton* manuscripts. [*Goetia of Dr Rudd* text](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

**[Esoteric mechanism]** In this recension, the magician invokes divine and angelic authority before dealing with the demon. Vehuiah functions as Bael’s constraining or balancing angel. The demon is not treated as an equal theological counterpart to the angel; the angel’s divine name and Psalm authorize control.

**[Disputed chronology]** If Harley 6483 accurately copies Rudd’s pre-1656 papers, the pairing belongs to the mid-seventeenth century. If the arrangement was substantially created or expanded by Smart, its secure surviving date is 1712–1713. There is no evidence that the medieval Jewish sources paired Vehuiah with Bael.

## 6. Vehuiah’s Psalm

Harley 6483 gives:

> “Tu Domine susceptor meus es gloria mea et exaltans Caput meum.”

Its sense is: “But you, Lord, are my protector or shield, my glory, and the one who raises my head.”

The citation varies:

- **Harley 6483/Skinner–Rankine:** Psalm **3:5**.
- **Golden Dawn paper:** Psalm **3:4**.
- **Many modern English Bibles:** Psalm **3:3**, because the superscription is not counted as verse 1.
- **Lenain:** calls it the third verse of Psalm 3.

**[Documented textual identity]** These generally point to the same sentence; the difference is verse-numbering and, in the Rudd citation, probably an additional counting error.

## 7. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* assigns Vehuiah:

- the attribute “God elevated and exalted above all things”;
- rule over the Hebrews and the divine name Jehovah;
- the first eastern ray of spring;
- the first five degrees beginning at midnight on 20 March and continuing through 24 March inclusive;
- Aries;
- fire;
- the first order, the Seraphim;
- Mars;
- the subordinate decanal genius Chontaré;
- invocation facing east between midnight and 00:20;
- the Psalm sentence above;
- subtlety, sagacity, enthusiasm for arts and sciences, capacity for difficult enterprises, military inclination, and energy;
- under the contrary genius, turbulence and anger.

[Lenain, original pp. 46–47](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf)

The printed list includes the impossible date “31 April” among Vehuiah’s five recurring annual days. **[Disputed/misprint]** Later versions silently change it to 31 May or 1 June. The impossible date is evidence of transmission, not a meaningful esoteric mystery.

## 8. Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” published *La Cabbale* in 1892; the enlarged 1903 edition explicitly incorporated new texts from Lenain and other French occultists. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb304000064), [1903 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1903__papus___cabbala_tradition_secrete_de_loccident.pdf)

**[Esoteric transmission]** Papus helped move Lenain’s system—angelic names, hierarchies, Psalm verses, planetary and calendrical rules—into fin-de-siècle occultism. This is transmission and popularization, not the origin of the Bael–Vehuiah pairing.

## 9. The Golden Dawn discrepancy

An extant Golden Dawn “Schem ha-Mephoresch” paper states that the seventy-two angels rule quinances of five degrees, two per decan. But its sequence begins not at Aries:

- Vehuiah, there spelled **Vahuaih**, is assigned **0°–5° Leo**.
- Its decan planet is **Saturn**.
- Its meaning is “God the Exalter.”
- Its Psalm is numbered 3:4.

[Golden Dawn table](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Schemhamphorash.html)

**[Finding]** This conflicts directly with Lenain’s 0°–5° Aries and Mars assignment. Later books that call 0°–5° Aries simply “the Golden Dawn correspondence” have usually harmonized the Golden Dawn, Lenain, Rudd, and *777* systems after the fact.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Within the Golden Dawn table itself, the first six angels cover Leo, the next six Virgo, and so forth; its starting point is therefore structural, not an isolated typo in Vehuiah’s row.

## 10. Ambelain and Haziel

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (Paris: Niclaus, 1951) continued the French practical-kabbalistic stream and substantially reproduced Lenain’s angelic offices and calendars. [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique), [English translation](https://www.markfoster.net/rn/texts/practicalkabbalah-part2.pdf)

**[Tradition]** Ambelain later attached warnings to practical use of certain angelic seals, reportedly based on experiments. Those warnings are occult testimony, not independently testable historical evidence.

“Haziel,” a twentieth-century French esoteric author—not the ninth angel Haziel—published *Le Grand livre des invocations et des exhortations* in 1987. He expanded the system into a popular guardian-angel doctrine in which three angels govern the physical, astral/desire, and mental dimensions of a person. [Bibliographic record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL15773179W/Le_Grand_livre_des_invocations_et_des_exhortations)

**[Modern esoteric development]** The widespread claim that everyone has a Shem “birth angel” determined by a five-day civil-calendar block owes much to this French stream. It is not part of the medieval derivation of the seventy-two triplets and is not specified in Rudd’s Bael entry.

### Planetary distinctions

Three planets can appear near Bael without being equivalent:

1. **Sun** — Bael’s rank planet, because he is a king.
2. **Mars** — ruler of the first Aries decan and Lenain’s planet for Vehuiah.
3. **Saturn** — the Golden Dawn Shem paper’s planet for Vahuaih at 0°–5° Leo.

**[Finding]** A modern table giving “Bael: Sun/Mars” may be combining rank and decan rulers. A table giving “Vehuiah: Mars” follows Lenain; one giving Saturn and Leo follows the cited Golden Dawn paper.

### Tarot pip and the two-spirit arrangement

Crowley’s *777* distributes thirty-six day spirits and thirty-six night spirits over the thirty-six decans. Bael is the day spirit of the first decan; Phenex is its night spirit. Therefore the Two of Wands carries two Goetic spirits:

\[
\text{Two of Wands} = \text{Bael by day} + \text{Phenex by night}
\]

[Crowley’s *777* tables](https://publicism.info/magic/full/thelema/777/index.html), [facsimile PDF](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf)

The card’s Golden Dawn title is **Lord of Dominion**, its planet is Mars, and its zodiacal setting is Aries. **[Esoteric interpretation]** Modern practitioners often connect dominion with Bael’s kingship, but the old catalogue never explains the Two of Wands through Bael.

### Astrological timing

The historically layered instructions should not be collapsed:

- **Seventeenth-century Goetia:** use the king’s specified daily hours and favorable lunar age.
- **Rank correspondence:** make the king’s seal in gold; working on Sunday or in a solar hour is a reasonable later extrapolation, not expressly ordered in Bael’s paragraph.
- ***777* decan:** work while the Sun or ascendant occupies 0°–10° Aries, or use the first Aries decan symbolically.
- **Lenain’s Vehuiah:** 20–24 March and midnight–00:20, facing east.
- **Golden Dawn Shem paper:** 0°–5° Leo, not Aries.
- **Modern merged systems:** select the first five Aries degrees and invoke Vehuiah before Bael.

**[Finding]** There is no single historically uniform “correct time.” Each rule belongs to a different textual layer.

## Iconography

## 1. Ancient Baal

**[Documented ancient iconography]** Baal/Hadad is represented as an upright warrior god, commonly with a raised club or mace and a thunderbolt or stylized lightning weapon. Bulls, mountains, cloud-riding, and storm symbolism belong to his sphere.

**[Important absence]** No known ancient image of Baal-Hadad has the Goetic cat, toad, and human heads or the de Plancy spider body. Those elements cannot presently be traced to ancient cult imagery.

## 2. The manuscript seals

**[Documented text]** Weyer, Scot, the Cambridge *Vita daemonum*, and the *Livre des esperitz* do not supply Bael with a seal.

The seals enter the surviving *Ars Goetia* manuscripts. Peterson remarks that he knows of no earlier *Offices of Spirits* witness containing the same Goetic spirit seals, although earlier magical catalogues have unrelated demonic characters. [Peterson’s introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Standard Sloane/Mathers form

Bael’s familiar seal is:

- enclosed by a double circular border;
- labeled BAEL around the circular field;
- built around a roughly bilateral central character;
- topped by two upright forked or trident-like projections;
- crossed and joined through a compact central knot or bar;
- extended below into paired hooked or looped curves.

The exact angles, line closures, letter positions, and symmetry vary with the copyist. The image itself is therefore more exact than any prose description. The 1904 published form can be inspected here: [Mathers’s Bael seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_seal_of_the_demon_Bael,.jpg).

**[Documented ritual instruction]** The character is to be worn as a lamen before Bael; otherwise he will not do homage. The general instructions assign the chief kings’ seals to gold. [Goetia observations](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Manuscript variants

- **Sloane 2731:** labels it “Baell his Seal”; the hand-drawn character is less mechanically regular than the 1904 redrawing.
- **Sloane 3825:** Peterson’s base form and the source family underlying the standard printed character.
- **Harley 6483:** presents Bael’s character as one half of a double apparatus, facing Vehuiah’s angelic seal, with Hebrew identifications and the Psalm. The individual lines do not always agree perfectly with the Sloane manuscripts.
- **Mathers–Crowley:** regularizes the circular form for print.

**[Caution]** Small line variations in hand-drawn seals are real manuscript data, but claims that every omitted hook creates a wholly different entity are modern magical interpretations, not stated by the manuscripts.

## 3. Collin de Plancy

### 1818

The *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in two volumes in 1818. It began as a comparatively rationalist encyclopedia of alleged supernatural beliefs. Its content and theological stance changed substantially through later editions. [1818 first edition](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/1re_%C3%A9dition,_1818/Tome_1/Texte_entier)

**[Finding]** The famous Bael portrait does not belong to the 1818 edition. It belongs to the illustrated sixth edition of 1863.

### 1863 entry

De Plancy says Baël stands at the head of infernal powers, begins Weyer’s catalogue, reigns in the East, has the three heads of toad, man, and cat, speaks hoarsely, fights well, makes invokers cunning and invisible, and rules sixty-six legions. He concludes:

> “Est-ce le même que Baal?”

“Is he the same as Baal?” [De Plancy, 1863, p. 71](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Ba%C3%ABl)

“Cunning” reflects de Plancy’s *fins et rusés*, not the standard Goetia’s sole office. “Fights well” preserves Weyer’s combat clause.

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

**[Documented image]** The 1863 edition’s wood-engraved portrait shows:

- a crowned human head at the center;
- a feline head on one side;
- a toad’s head on the other;
- no ordinary human torso;
- the heads resting on or merging into a cluster of hairy, jointed, spider- or crab-like legs.

[High-resolution public-domain image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ba%C3%ABl.png), [Gallica scan, p. 71](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5754923d/f82.image)

**[Modern-iconographic invention]** The spider legs are not mentioned in de Plancy’s text, Weyer, Scot, or the *Goetia*. They are Le Breton’s visual solution for combining the three heads into a single unsettling body. That invention became more influential than many textual details.

## 4. Modern visual descendants

- **Shin Megami Tensei:** one design closely follows Le Breton—a crowned human head, cat head, toad head, and spider body. Other games separate the human, cat, and toad into rider and companions. [Megami Tensei documentation](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Bael)
- **Bloodstained: Ritual of the Night:** Bael is a vast three-part boss with human, feline, and toad-like heads on serpentine bodies, an explicit elaboration of the Goetic triad. [Bloodstained description](https://bloodstained.fandom.com/wiki/Bael)
- **Devil May Cry 4:** Bael becomes an enormous ice toad whose lure-like appendages resemble women. This selects the toad element but abandons the other two heads. [Capcom’s contemporary guide notice](https://news.capcomusa.com/lets/browse/new-devil-may-cry-4-anti-boss-strats-now-with-pic)
- **Modern occult art:** common variations add crowns, solar disks, storm imagery, or ancient Levantine dress. Those features synthesize king/Sun/Baal-Hadad correspondences; they are not manuscript descriptions.

## People and works

## 1. Medieval and Renaissance ancestors

### *Testament of Solomon*

**[Documented literary ancestor]** The Greek *Testament of Solomon*, a composite work of late antiquity, depicts Solomon using a divine ring and angelic knowledge to question and command demons. It includes Ornias, Beelzeboul, Asmodeus, decanal demons, and angelic adversaries. [Conybeare translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Testament_of_Solomon)

**[Important absence]** It does not contain the Goetic Bael entry. Beelzeboul is not proof that Bael already existed as the first eastern king.

**[Scholarly qualification]** The *Testament* is an important conceptual ancestor of Solomonic demon catalogues, but a direct textual path from it to Bael’s medieval entry has not been demonstrated.

### *Liber officiorum spirituum*

Trithemius’s 1508 catalogue of prohibited magical books mentions titles corresponding to a *Book of the Offices of Spirits*. Weyer later explicitly named his source *Liber officiorum spirituum* or *Empto Salomonis*. **[Scholarly reconstruction]** The surviving Cambridge, French, Folger, Weyer, and *Goetia* catalogues appear to represent branches of this wider textual family rather than simple copies of one another.

### Munich handbook, Clm 849

Richard Kieckhefer’s fifteenth-century Munich handbook contains a partial office catalogue with names corresponding to Barbatos, Botis, Eligor, Gaap, Valac, Gemory, Cimeries, Amy, and others. [Penn State edition](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/0-271-01750-3.html)

**[Important absence]** Bael is not in the extant comparable list. The Munich handbook is an ancestor of the catalogue genre, not an attestation of Bael himself.

### *Livre des esperitz*

Boudet regards the *Livre des esperitz* and Weyer as late witnesses to a ritual-magical catalogue tradition reaching at least to the thirteenth century. Thirty-five names or offices overlap, but the French text is not simply Weyer translated backward. [Boudet, 2003](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/30447646_Les_who%27s_who_demonologiques_de_la_Renaissance_et_leurs_ancetres_medievaux)

### *Book of Abramelin*

The *Abramelin* tradition has Lucifer, Leviathan, Satan, Belial and subordinate rulers such as Oriens, Paimon, Ariton, Amaimon, Astaroth, Asmodeus, and Beelzebub. [Mathers edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/abramelin/abramelin.htm)

**[Important absence]** The precise first-eastern-king Bael entry is absent. Names such as *Baalsori* in large servitor lists are not the same documented figure.

## 2. Weyer and Scot

- **Johann Weyer (1515–1588):** physician, former pupil of Agrippa, critic of witch trials, first printer of Bael’s catalogue entry in 1577.
- **Reginald Scot (c. 1538–1599):** skeptical English author whose 1584 translation transmitted *Baëll* to English readers.
- **Abraham Fleming:** probable translator of the Weyer material included by Scot, though the attribution is not completely certain.

Their skeptical frames are essential: both published demonological material to attack credulity and persecution, even while preserving texts later magicians used operationally.

## 3. Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

- **Thomas Rudd (1583/84–1656):** engineer, mathematician, and occult author to whom the expanded angelic scheme is attributed.
- **Peter Smart:** copyist associated with the 1712–1713 Harley recension.

**[Disputed]** Calling the surviving book simply “Rudd’s manuscript” hides the gap between a purported mid-seventeenth-century exemplar and the early-eighteenth-century copy.

## 4. Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

- **Jacques Collin de Plancy (1794–1881):** encyclopedist who preserved Weyer’s description, added cunning, explicitly questioned the Baal identification, and repeatedly revised the work.
- **Louis Le Breton (1818–1866):** illustrator whose spider-legged composite became Bael’s dominant modern image.

## 5. Lenain, Papus, Ambelain, Haziel

These authors transmitted and successively popularized the practical French angelic system:

- **Lazare Lenain:** full 1823 calendrical and zodiacal account.
- **Papus:** late-nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century occult dissemination.
- **Robert Ambelain:** practical postwar synthesis.
- **Haziel:** late-twentieth-century guardian-angel popularization.

Their work concerns Vehuiah much more than Bael. The common modern Bael–Vehuiah–Aries package results from combining them with Rudd and Crowley.

## 6. Mathers and Crowley

- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers:** manuscript translator and Golden Dawn organizer.
- **Aleister Crowley:** publisher, editor, ritual commentator, Enochian translator, and author of the tables that placed Bael in the first Aries decan by day.

Crowley’s “Initiated Interpretation” psychologizes ceremonial demons as portions of the human brain or psyche. **[Esoteric interpretation]** That is a Thelemic hermeneutic, not the ontology stated by the seventeenth-century manuscript.

## 7. Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition and continuing digital archive established the modern comparative textual basis. His work is particularly important for distinguishing:

- what Weyer actually printed;
- what Scot omitted or mistranslated;
- which readings belong to Sloane 3825, 2731, 3648, and Harley 6483;
- which ritual materials were borrowed from earlier sources;
- which spirit names were added to make seventy-two.

## 8. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

*The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007; later Llewellyn edition) transcribes Harley 6483 and related material, emphasizing the paired angelic seals and operational context. [Publisher listing](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556)

Their principal Bael contribution is to make the Vehuiah pairing and the Harley variants—dog and “hastily”—widely available. Their pre-1656 dating of the presumed Rudd exemplar remains a reconstruction.

## 9. Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* (1996; expanded second edition 2023) uses dark-mirror scrying and angelic invocation. He explicitly identifies Bael with Baʿal and Astaroth with Astarte, treating them as patron deities. He originally softened the Goetic coercions for that reason, then restored stronger conjurations in the second edition. [Runyon’s own account](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition)

**[Modern esoteric innovation]** Runyon’s dark-mirror and hypnotic interpretation is a modern operational method, not found in Bael’s medieval entry.

## 10. Lon Milo DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* restates Bael as:

- 0°–10° Aries by day;
- approximately 21–30 March;
- Two of Wands;
- king, hence Sol/gold.

[Accessible scan](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf), [publisher](https://newfalcon.com/product/aleister-crowleys-illustrated-goetia/)

This correctly reflects *777*’s ten-degree decan more closely than five-degree modern tables do.

## 11. Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica* situates European grimoires in a wider spirit-centered, necromantic, and late-antique context. [Publisher biography](https://scarletimprint.com/jake-stratton-kent)

**[Finding]** I found no securely sourced, distinctive Bael operation or new manuscript reading attributable specifically to him. His relevance is methodological and genealogical: restoring goetia as spirit practice rather than reducing it to Crowley’s psychological interpretation.

## 12. Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No reliable Bael-specific treatment or operation by Frater Achad was located in the consulted primary or bibliographic sources. He belongs to Crowley’s wider magical milieu, but inclusion in a list of “people who worked with Bael” would presently be unsupported.

## 13. Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Julien Véronèse

- **Owen Davies** supplies the broad social history of grimoires and their circulation, not a distinctive Bael thesis. [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/47570)
- **Jan Veenstra** is important for medieval ritual-magic and angelological scholarship, especially Almadel-related traditions; no Bael-specific finding was established here.
- **Julien Véronèse** has edited and studied medieval Solomonic traditions, particularly the *Almandal/Almadel*. This contextualizes the textual environment but does not make Bael an Almadel spirit. [SISMEL](https://www.sismel.it/publications/1270-lalmandal-et-lalmadel-latins-au-moyen-ge)

## Leads that do not withstand checking

### Dante

**[Negative finding]** Bael does not appear as a named demon in Dante’s *Inferno*. Dante names or adapts figures including Lucifer, Minos, Cerberus, Plutus, Geryon, Malebranche devils, and Beelzebub; none is the first eastern king Bael. Treating every reference to Beelzebub as Bael is conflation.

### Milton

Milton’s *Paradise Lost* mentions “Baalim and Ashtaroth” as plural male and female pagan divinities and makes Beelzebub a major fallen angel. [*Paradise Lost*, Book I](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/paradise-lost-book-i)

**[Qualification]** Milton does not present the Goetic Bael with three heads, invisibility, or sixty-six legions. The poem is relevant to the Christian reception of Baal, not a Bael adaptation.

### The *Bartimaeus* novels

**[Negative finding]** No securely attested Bael character was found in Jonathan Stroud’s *Bartimaeus Sequence*. The novels employ conjured spirits and grimoire-like names, but genre resemblance is not evidence of Bael’s presence.

### *Helluva Boss*

**[Negative finding]** No canonical character explicitly named Bael has been established. Beelzebub’s emblem resembles a modified version of Bael’s Goetic seal, with the lettering changed to Beelzebub. [Documented emblem comparison](https://hazbinhotel.fandom.com/wiki/Beelzebub)

Calling a background feline demon “Bael” remains fandom identification. The show’s Beelzebub is not automatically Bael simply because both names ultimately involve *Baal*.

### *The Rite* (2011)

Some summaries call the possessing demon Baal. **[Qualification]** Even where that name is used, the film does not establish the specific Goetic Bael—number 1, three forms, eastern kingship, and invisibility. It should be catalogued as a Baal/possession reference, not securely as a Bael adaptation.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Was Weyer the first attestation?

No.

- **Common claim:** Bael first appears in Weyer in 1577.
- **Corrected finding:** Weyer supplies the first known printed entry.
- **Earlier evidence:** Cambridge UL Dd.4.35, broadly fifteenth century, has *Baal* with a closely corresponding description.
- **Additional early relative:** *Livre des esperitz*, surviving in a mid-sixteenth-century copy and possibly representing older material.

The exact dating and relationship of these witnesses remain open, but “no pre-Weyer attestation” is no longer tenable.

## 2. Did Bael begin as Baal-Hadad?

- **Evidence for:** the earliest spelling *Baal*; ordinary Baal/Beal/Bael orthographic development; biblical demonization of rival gods.
- **Evidence against a complete identity claim:** no early Bael text says “this is Hadad”; no ancient Baal evidence explains the three heads, invisibility, or sixty-six legions.
- **Conclusion:** **probable name derivation, unproved continuity of personality**.

## 3. Baal as title versus single god

Because *baʿal* means “lord,” the source name could evoke:

- Ugaritic Hadad;
- a local Baal;
- Baal Shamem;
- a biblical generalized “Baal”;
- Beelzebub;
- or simply an inherited demonological proper name.

The most responsible formulation is “probably derived from the ancient/biblical title-name Baal, especially associated with Hadad,” not “certainly the exact Ugaritic god in another form.”

## 4. The three heads

- Cambridge: toad, **lion**, cat.
- Weyer and Scot: toad, **man**, cat.
- Sloane *Goetia*: cat, toad, man as alternating or combined forms.
- Harley 6483: cat, **dog**, man.
- De Plancy: simultaneous toad, man, cat.
- Le Breton: three heads on spider legs.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** “Lion → man” could be scribal, editorial, or independent recension variation. “Toad → dog” in Harley may be another copying error. There is insufficient evidence to identify an original archetypal reading with certainty.

## 5. Hoarsely versus hastily

The standard line is “he speaketh very hoarsely.” Harley 6483 has “hastily.”

**[Disputed]** This could result from a damaged exemplar or eye-skip. It should be recorded, not harmonized silently.

## 6. Six or sixty-six legions

- Cambridge: 66.
- *Livre des esperitz*: 6.
- Weyer onward: 66.

Because an early witness already has sixty-six, neither number can be declared mechanically original. Roman numerals, abbreviation, or branch-specific transmission may explain the divergence.

## 7. What are Bael’s actual offices?

| Office | Source |
|---|---|
| Invisibility | All principal early witnesses |
| Wisdom | Weyer; absent from Scot and the standard Goetia |
| Formation of manners/character | Weyer’s disputed Latin phrase |
| Fighting/military leadership | Cambridge and Weyer; revived by de Plancy |
| Favor or grace in all things | *Livre des esperitz* |
| Sciences | One Folger *Offices* entry |
| Love | Another Folger *Offices* entry |
| Cunning | De Plancy |
| Treasure, familiars, languages, transmutation | Not assigned to standard Bael |

Modern encyclopedias often pool every office attached to a similar spelling. That produces a composite Bael no single historical source contains.

## 8. Bael and Berith

Weyer calls Berith by variants including *Beal* in a later entry. This does not make first-spirit Bael and Berith the same demon. The catalogue itself treats them separately, with different numbers, ranks, appearances, and offices. Scribal homography is evidence for caution, not identity.

## 9. Bael and Beelzebub

The names share the historical element *Baal*, but:

- Bael is first eastern king in the *Offices/Goetia* chain.
- Beelzebub is a chief demon in biblical, *Testament of Solomon*, and later hierarchies.
- The *Livre des esperitz* lists Beelzebub and Beal separately.
- Rudd and the *Goetia* treat them separately.

**[Tradition/disputed theology]** Some practitioners identify them as forms of one power. Textually, they are separate catalogue personalities.

## 10. Bael and Buer/Buel

Buer is spirit 10 in the *Goetia*, a president with philosophical, medicinal, and herbal offices. “Buel” as a spelling of Bael is ordinarily a modern error unless a specific manuscript can be produced. It likely arises from visual or phonetic confusion.

## 11. The seal’s antiquity

- No Bael seal is present in the medieval Cambridge or French entries.
- None is in Weyer or Scot.
- The familiar character appears in the seventeenth-century *Goetia* manuscript tradition.

Therefore claims that Bael’s seal is an ancient Canaanite symbol or was used in Baal’s temples are **modern inventions without evidence**.

## 12. The spider body

The spider legs first appear in Le Breton’s 1863 portrait, not the descriptions. Games and modern art often treat the spider as Bael’s essential animal. Historically, it is an influential nineteenth-century illustration.

## 13. “First king of Hell”

Weyer and Scot call Bael the first king in their catalogue and place him under or within the eastern power. The *Goetia* calls him its first principal spirit, a king ruling East.

**[Overstatement]** This does not necessarily make him the supreme ruler of all Hell. The same traditions recognize Lucifer, Satan, Beelzebub, the four directional kings, and “chiefest” spirits such as Belial and Asmoday. “First” can indicate list position and regional rank.

## 14. Vehuiah’s zodiacal sector

Three incompatible claims circulate:

- **0°–5° Aries:** Lenain, Ambelain, Haziel and much modern angelology.
- **0°–5° Leo:** the cited Golden Dawn Shem paper.
- **0°–10° Aries:** not Vehuiah’s quinance, but Bael’s day decan in Crowley’s *777*.

A chart giving Bael and Vehuiah a shared 0°–5° Aries sector is a modern harmonization.

## 15. Does every Goetic demon have one ruling Shem angel?

Only within the Rudd-derived synthesis and later systems.

- The Jewish seventy-two names exist independently.
- Weyer’s catalogue has sixty-nine printed spirits.
- The standard Sloane *Goetia* has seventy-two but does not print the Rudd angel beside every spirit.
- Harley 6483 performs the explicit one-to-one pairing.

The numerical match is symbolically powerful but not proof of ancient common origin.

## 16. Modern “enns”

The chant commonly given for Bael—variants of “Ayer secore on ca Bael”—does not occur in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, Harley 6483, Mathers–Crowley, or Lenain.

**[Modern invention]** Such “enns” belong to twentieth- or twenty-first-century demonolatry literature, especially material popularized by S. Connolly. Their claimed antiquity has not been demonstrated.

## Open questions

1. **Dating the Cambridge Baal entry.** The codex is catalogued broadly as fifteenth-century, but a dedicated paleographical and codicological analysis of fols. 27–40 could establish whether the demonological unit is contemporary with the codex or a later insertion.

2. **The archetypal second head.** Cambridge’s lion, Weyer’s man, and Harley’s dog indicate substantial movement. More unpublished *Offices of Spirits* witnesses may clarify the earliest reading.

3. **The origin of *formator morum*.** It may preserve a genuine character-forming office, translate a lost phrase, or represent corruption. The Cambridge military wording solves only part of the problem.

4. **Six versus sixty-six.** The stemmatic relationship between Trinity O.8.29, Dd.4.35, Weyer’s source, and the Folger manuscripts must be reconstructed before either number can be preferred.

5. **Origin of the Goetic seal.** No pre-*Lemegeton* exemplar of Bael’s particular character has been identified. Its creator and relation to other seventeenth-century sigil systems remain unknown.

6. **Rudd versus Smart.** The extent to which Harley 6483 reproduces Thomas Rudd’s work rather than Peter Smart’s compilation is unresolved.

7. **Origin of the demon–angel pairing.** A pre-1656 witness explicitly pairing Bael and Vehuiah would materially strengthen the attribution to Rudd. At present, Harley 6483 is the secure surviving evidence.

8. **Golden Dawn versus Lenain zodiac.** The divergent starting points—Leo and Aries—need to be traced through particular Golden Dawn papers and their manuscript dates rather than treated as one homogeneous “Golden Dawn system.”

9. **Baal identification.** The derivation of the name is strong; the path from a biblical or ancient divine name to this exact medieval catalogue personality remains unrecoverable.

10. **Cultural catalogue.** Modern uses are numerous, but many internet lists confuse Baal, Bael, Beelzebub, Balam, Belial, and Buer. Each apparent appearance requires verification from the work itself.

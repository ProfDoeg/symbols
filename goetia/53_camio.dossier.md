# Camio: Research Dossier

*Research status: 14 September 2026.*

Because source-quotation limits prevent reproducing entire editions verbatim, the dossier gives short diplomatic excerpts from Weyer, Scot, and the *Goetia*, followed by complete close translations or synopses. Links to the full public-domain texts and facsimiles are supplied throughout and again in the source list.

## Evidentiary key

- **[Documented]** Directly stated or pictured in a cited primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** A conclusion proposed from textual or historical evidence.
- **[Tradition]** A teaching transmitted within a magical, religious, or occult lineage.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** A symbolic relationship imposed by a later magical system.
- **[Disputed]** A claim for which the evidence is ambiguous or conflicting.
- **[Legend]** A narrative transmitted without adequate historical corroboration.
- **[Modern invention]** A recent expansion that is absent from the older textual chain.

> **Camio’s rank is Mercurial; Camio’s Crowley decan is Venus-ruled Virgo II; Nanael’s Lenain interval lies in the Saturn-ruled third decan of Sagittarius. These are three different layers.**

# The name and its origins

## 1. Earliest secure appearance: Caym, 1577

**[Documented]** The earliest presently identifiable printed attestation of this particular spirit—blackbird, sword, burning ashes, animal speech, prophecy, thirty legions—is Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*. The catalogue was appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in **1577**. Weyer’s main work began appearing in 1563, which explains the frequent but inaccurate claim that Caym’s catalogue entry dates from 1563. The entry belongs to the 1577 appendix in the form now recoverable.

Weyer spells the name **Caym** and places it **forty-first** in his actual list. A modern aligned table sometimes numbers it “40” after removing Weyer’s otherwise unmatched Pruflas, but that is an editorial alignment device, not its number in the printed *Pseudomonarchia*. [Peterson’s Latin transcription and source discussion](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Weyer says he obtained his catalogue from a work he calls the *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also described with a title involving the *Empto. Salomonis*. That lost or incompletely identified source may have been older than 1577.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** It is reasonable to infer that Weyer copied a circulating manuscript catalogue rather than inventing all sixty-nine entries himself.

**[Disputed]** The source’s age, contents and exact relationship to surviving *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* manuscripts remain uncertain. An inferred older source is not itself an extant pre-1577 attestation of Camio.

## 2. The spelling chain

The comparative witness table prepared by Joseph H. Peterson records the following readings:

| Source | Date | Form |
|---|---:|---|
| Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* | 1577 | **Caym** |
| Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft* | 1584 | **Caim** |
| Sloane MS 3825 | seventeenth century | **Caim** |
| Sloane MS 2731 | dated 1686 | **Caim**; another copy reportedly **Camio** |
| Sloane MS 3648 | seventeenth century | **Camio** |
| Harley MS 6483 | 1712–1714 compilation | **Camio** |
| Northwestern University MS 65 | early modern | **Camio** |
| Mathers–Crowley *Goetia* | 1904 | **Camio, or Caïm** |

| Wellcome MS 3203 | later manuscript witness | **Cam** |

These forms are tabulated in Peterson’s [comparison of Goetic spirit names](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

The diaeresis in **Caïm** tells an English or French reader to pronounce the vowels separately. It is typographical guidance, not evidence of a distinct medieval entity.

## 3. Does Camio derive from Cain?

**[Disputed]** The resemblance between **Caym/Caim** and **Cain** is real but inconclusive.

Biblical Cain is Hebrew **קַיִן**, *Qayin*. Genesis 4:1 associates the name through wordplay with *qaniti*, “I have acquired” or “gotten,” although that narrative association does not settle its historical etymology. Medieval Romance spellings could place *m* where modern editions print final *n*: Old Spanish materials include **Caym** as a form of biblical Cain.

That makes transmission from “Cain” paleographically possible. Nevertheless:

- Weyer never identifies Caym with Adam and Eve’s son.
- Camio’s biography says he “was of the order of angels,” whereas Cain is human in Genesis.
- None of Camio’s characteristic offices—blackbird form, burning ashes, animal languages or hydromancy-like voices—belongs to the biblical Cain narrative.
- No intermediate text has been found showing Cain transformed into Weyer’s President Caym.

The most defensible conclusion is therefore:

**Caym may visually preserve a medieval spelling of Cain, but a historical derivation of the demon from Cain has not been demonstrated.**

## 4. Chemosh, Chamos and Baal-Peor

**[Disputed; largely modern]** Internet demonologies sometimes identify Camio with **Chemosh**, **Chamos**, **Chamus**, **Kemosh**, **Baal-Peor**, or the biblical **Chiun/Kiyyun**. No such identification appears in Weyer, Scot, the early *Goetia*, Rudd, Mathers or de Plancy.

Chemosh is independently documented as the national god of Moab and appears in the Mesha inscription. Its name is usually transliterated from כמוש, quite unlike the manuscript history Caym → Caim/Camio. Even the etymology of Chemosh is disputed; one modern scholarly proposal relates it to an Akkadian expression meaning something like “the awesome/redoubtable one.” That scholarship does not connect Chemosh to Camio.

No documentary chain establishes:

> Chemosh → Chamos → Caym → Camio.

The proposed connection appears to be a modern harmonization of vaguely similar-looking names. Identifying Camio with Baal-Peor introduces a second, distinct ancient deity without textual support.

## 5. Greek *kauma*, Latin *caelum* and Gaelic *caim*

**[Modern disputed etymologies]**

Several recent occult websites derive the name from:

- Greek **καῦμα**, *kauma*, “heat” or “burning,” because Camio answers from burning ash;
- Latin **caelum**, “heaven,” because Camio was formerly angelic;
- Gaelic **caim**, an encircling prayer or protective circle;
- Gaelic forms of Cain.

These suggestions are based on semantic or visual resemblance. No early source gives any of them, and no documented borrowing pathway has been produced. The Greek proposal is particularly circular: the description of burning ashes is being used to manufacture an etymology for the name attached to that description.

## 6. Is there an ancient god, hero or place behind the name?

**Finding:** none has been securely established.

This distinguishes Camio from names such as Bael/Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Asmoday/Ashmedai and Phoenix, for which ancient linguistic or mythological comparisons are much stronger. Cain remains a lead worth recording, but not a demonstrated identification.

# Chronology and textual ancestry

## Before Weyer: conceptual ancestors, but no secure Camio

### Testament of Solomon

The Greek *Testament of Solomon*, compiled in late antiquity from materials of varying dates, depicts Solomon questioning demons and controlling them through a ring. Demons give names, functions, bodily effects and thwarting angels.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This is an important ancestor of the Solomonic interrogatory model.

**[Finding]** Camio/Caym and his distinctive blackbird–ashes–animal-language profile do not occur in the surviving *Testament*. It cannot be cited as a direct attestation.

### Munich Manual of Demonic Magic, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich handbook contains ritual magic, spirit names, circles and conjurations. Richard Kieckhefer edited it as *Forbidden Rites*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** It documents the late-medieval ritual environment out of which catalogues such as Weyer’s could develop.

**[Finding]** No secure Camio entry with Weyer’s offices has been identified in it.

### *Livre des Esperitz*

The French *Livre des Esperitz*, usually dated to the fifteenth or early sixteenth century, contains forty-six or forty-seven spirit descriptions and is one of the closest known ancestors of Weyer’s catalogue. Many entries parallel later Goetic spirits.

**[Finding]** A search of the available text does not produce Caym, Caim or Camio with the relevant profile. Its importance is genealogical to the catalogue as a genre, not direct evidence for Camio.

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

A related spirit-office tradition survives in several manuscripts. One witness contains the phrase **“Gomeris vel Caym”**, apparently attaching *Caym* as an alternate reading to a Gomeris/Gremory-type spirit.

**[Disputed]** This could preserve a name collision, copying error or otherwise lost relationship. It does not describe Weyer’s blackbird President and cannot safely be counted as the same figure.

### *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin manuscripts contain extensive demon lists, some of whose names overlap later catalogues.

**[Finding]** No secure Camio/Caym with the characteristic offices was located. General list resemblance is not individual attestation.

## 1577: Weyer

Weyer was a physician and critic of witch prosecutions. His publication of a demon catalogue was embedded in an argument that many alleged witches were deluded, ill or deceived by demons rather than possessors of real supernatural power.

Thus **[Documented]** Weyer printed the entry, but **[Finding]** that does not establish that he personally invoked or “worked with” Caym. The catalogue is evidence of transmission, not an operational diary.

## 1584: Reginald Scot

Reginald Scot incorporated an English form of the catalogue into *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, another skeptical and anti-persecution work. Peterson attributes much of the translation to Abraham Fleming. Scot’s spelling is **Caim**.

The English translation became particularly important because the anonymous compiler of the *Lemegeton* appears to have depended heavily on an edition of Scot, adding seals and ritual architecture to the catalogue.

## Seventeenth century: the *Lemegeton* manuscripts

The *Ars Goetia* forms the first part of the *Lemegeton* or *Lesser Key of Solomon*. Sloane MS 3825 is generally treated as the earliest surviving substantially complete witness; Sloane MS 2731 is dated **1686**. Different manuscripts alternate between **Caim** and **Camio**.

The *Goetia* transforms Weyer’s list in several ways:

- It embeds each spirit in an operational conjuration system.
- It adds an individual seal.
- It expands Weyer’s sixty-nine entries to seventy-two.
- It often Anglicizes or amplifies Scot’s language.
- It gives instructions for metals, lunar timing and ranks.

## 1712–1714: Harley 6483 and the “Rudd” system

Harley MS 6483 combines each of the seventy-two demons with one of seventy-two angelic names. Camio is paired with **Nanael**.

The surviving manuscript is internally dated around 1712–1714. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine argue that it copies material originally assembled by Thomas Rudd around 1655–1656. Other researchers emphasize that the surviving hand and dates point to Peter Smart as copyist or redactor and that “Dr Rudd” may be an attribution rather than demonstrable authorship.

Accordingly:

- **[Documented]** Harley 6483 contains the paired system.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** It may preserve a mid-seventeenth-century Rudd original.
- **[Disputed]** Thomas Rudd’s exact authorship and the state of the hypothetical original.

## 1818–1863: Collin de Plancy

Jacques Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818 and evolved across six editions. The 1863 edition gives a substantial Caym entry and two Louis Le Breton/Jarrault illustrations.

De Plancy adds literary color absent from Weyer: Caym is the most accomplished sophist in hell and is associated with Luther’s famous disputation with the devil.

## 1904: Mathers and Crowley

Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers prepared the translation/editing on which Aleister Crowley’s 1904 *Goetia* was based. The title page attributes the work cryptically to “a Dead Hand,” while Crowley acted as editor and publisher.

The 1904 book regularized the printed seal, preferred **Camio**, retained **Caïm** as an alternative and added “coals of fire” to the ashes reading.

Crowley’s prefatory and appended materials—including his psychological “initiated interpretation” and the Bornless or Preliminary Invocation—are modern overlays on the manuscript *Goetia*, not Camio-specific medieval traditions.

## 1909: Crowley’s *777*

Crowley’s *777* assigned the Goetic spirits to zodiacal decans by day and night. Camio was placed in the **middle decan of Virgo by night**. This correspondence is distinct from Rudd’s Nanael pairing.

## 2001 and 2007: modern critical editions

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (2001) collated manuscript readings and exposed many differences obscured by the 1904 edition.

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine’s *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (2007) edited and interpreted the Harley material, foregrounding the angel–demon pairs and protective double seals.

# The description across the sources

## Weyer, 1577

Weyer’s heading is **Caym magnus Præses**: Caym, a great President.

A short diplomatic excerpt reads:

> “Caym magnus Præses, formam assumens merulæ … respondet in favilla ardente, ferens in manu gladium acutissimum.”

[*Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, 1577 appendix, printed page 925 in Peterson’s transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Complete close rendering

**[Documented]** Caym is a great President. He assumes the form of a *merula*. When he takes on human form, he answers amid glowing or burning ashes and carries an extremely sharp sword. He causes a person to argue more wisely than others. He grants understanding of every flying creature, of the lowing of cattle, the barking of dogs and the sounds of waters. He gives excellent answers about future things. He formerly belonged to an order of angels and presides over thirty legions.

### Philological points

- **Merula** ordinarily means the Eurasian blackbird, *Turdus merula*. A blackbird is zoologically a true thrush, so Scot’s “thrush” is broad rather than wholly erroneous.
- **Respondet in favilla ardente** says that he answers “in” burning or glowing ash. It does not specify whether he stands in the ash, appears above it or speaks through it.
- **Præ cæteris sapienter argumentari facit** makes Camio causative: he makes someone argue wisely or surpass others in argument. Later versions turn Camio himself into “a good disputer.”
- **Omnium volatilium** is literally “all flying things,” although the English lineage narrows it to birds.
- Weyer says nothing about languages in the ordinary human sense, treasure, love, familiars, invisibility, alchemy or transmutation.

## Scot, 1584

Scot prints **Caim**. His characteristic opening is:

> “Caim is a great president, taking the forme of a thrush … he answereth in burning ashes.”

[*The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584, Book XV, p. 223](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

### Complete close synopsis

**[Documented]** Caim is a great President who first takes the form of a thrush. In human shape he answers in burning ashes and carries a very sharp sword. He makes the best disputants. He gives people understanding of all birds, the lowing of bullocks, barking of dogs and the sound and noise of waters. He answers best about things to come. He was of the order of angels and rules thirty legions of devils.

### Changes from Weyer

| Weyer | Scot |
|---|---|
| *merula*, specifically blackbird | “thrush” |
| understands all flying creatures | understands all birds |
| makes one argue more wisely than others | makes “the best disputers” |
| *sonitus aquarum*, sounds of waters | sound and noise of waters |
| presides over legions | rules legions |

These are mostly translation choices rather than new mythology.

## Sloane MS 3825

The manuscript begins:

> “The 53d spirit is called Caim, he is a great president & appeareth in ye forme of a Bird called a Thrush…”

[Peterson’s transcription of the *Ars Goetia*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

### Complete close synopsis

**[Documented]** The fifty-third spirit is Caim, a great President. He first appears as the bird called a thrush. After a while he puts on the shape of a man carrying a sharp sword. He seems to answer in burning ashes. He is described as a good disputant. His office gives people understanding of birds, the lowing of bullocks, barking of dogs and other creatures, and the noise of waters. He provides very true answers about future things. He was formerly of the order of angels and now rules thirty legions of infernal spirits. His seal is to be worn as a lamen.

### Notable innovations

- Number **53** is now explicit.
- “After a while” creates a temporal sequence between bird and man.
- Weyer’s power to improve the operator’s disputation becomes “he is a good disputer.”
- “Other creatures” expands the English animal list.
- “Very true answers” intensifies Weyer and Scot.
- The spirit now has a seal to be worn.

## Sloane MS 2731

The 1686 witness also reads **Caim** in the principal transcription. The substance closely parallels Sloane 3825: thrush, man with sword, burning ashes, disputation, understanding animal cries and waters, prophecy, former angel and thirty legions. A related copy or witness gives **Camio**.

The digitized manuscript transcription places the entry in its Camio/Caim section and includes the spirit’s sigil. [Sloane 2731 digital edition](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/).

## Harley MS 6483

Harley 6483 calls the spirit **Camio** and embeds the conventional description within a different ritual technology: the demonic seal is combined with the name, sigil and Psalm of the controlling angel Nanael.

Its importance is not a radically different Camio biography. Its distinctive innovation is the one-to-one angelic constraint.

## Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The printed edition begins:

> “The Fifty-third Spirit is Camio, or Caïm. He is a Great President…”

[*The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, 1904, p. 29](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/053-Seal-of-Camio/).

### Complete close synopsis

**[Documented]** Camio, or Caïm, is the fifty-third spirit and a great President. He appears first as a thrush, then as a man carrying a sharp sword. He seems to answer in burning ashes **or coals of fire**. He is a good disputant. He gives people understanding of birds, the lowing of bullocks, barking of dogs and other creatures, as well as the voice of the waters. He gives true answers about future matters. Once of the order of angels, he now rules thirty legions of infernal spirits. His seal is to be worn.

### Mathers’s distinctive readings

- “Camio, or Caïm” makes two manuscript forms explicit.
- “Or in coals of fire” is an amplification not found in Weyer’s Latin.
- “Voice of the Waters” personifies what Weyer called the sounds of waters.
- Capitalization gives offices and forms a quasi-technical appearance.

## Peterson, 2001

Peterson’s critical edition prints **Caim** and records the variants:

- Weyer: **Caym**
- Scot and several Sloane witnesses: **Caim**
- Sloane 3648 and Harley 6483: **Camio**
- another copy associated with Sloane 2731: **Camio**
- Mathers: **Camio or Caïm**

The entry appears on p. 31 in the accessible edition. Peterson restores manuscript evidence and distinguishes it from Mathers’s editorial language. His edition is the most useful point of departure for textual comparison, though manuscript images remain decisive for sigil details.

# Rank, metal and timing

## President: Mercury and quicksilver

The *Goetia*’s general instructions associate ranks with planets and metals:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince/Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | **Mercury** | **Mercury/quicksilver** |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |

Camio’s rank therefore makes his lamen **Mercurial**.

**[Documented]** The text says seals should be made in the metal attributed to the rank.

**[Practical complication]** Elemental mercury is liquid and toxic. Modern editors and practitioners sometimes suggest an amalgam, electrum, a symbolic color or another Mercurial substitute. Those solutions are later accommodations, not stated in the early Camio entry.

## Hours

Weyer’s Latin general instruction says:

> “Præses in aliqua hora diei non potest constringi, nisi rex cui paret invocaretur…”

In close rendering: a President cannot simply be constrained at an arbitrary daytime hour unless the king whom he obeys is invoked, and not at evening twilight. [Weyer’s ritual directions](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

The *Goetia*’s English punctuation is less clear and has been read in different ways. The safest historical statement is:

- Weyer restricts Presidents with reference to the governing king.
- Twilight is excluded.
- The English manuscript formula is syntactically ambiguous about daytime versus night.
- No Camio-specific planetary hour is given in his individual entry.

**[Tradition]** Later ceremonial practice commonly adds the day and hour of Mercury, a properly dignified Mercury, or the relevant decan hour.

**[Finding]** These are systematic deductions. They are not instructions spoken in the Camio paragraph.

## Lunar timing

The *Goetia* recommends particular ages of the Moon, normally 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 14 days. Sloane 2731 reportedly reads 9 where another witness gives 8.

This general rule applies to Goetic operations as a class. It is not a unique Camio correspondence.

# The seal

## Manuscript form

**[Documented]** Weyer and Scot provide no individual Caym/Caim seal. The sigil enters the recoverable chain in the *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

Across Sloane and printed witnesses, it is recognizable as:

- a long, predominantly horizontal linear figure;
- a strong central upright or vertical axis;
- smaller crossing, hooked or branching strokes;
- asymmetric terminal flourishes;
- no surrounding double circle;
- no original Hebrew lettering inside the demonic sigil.

A verbal description cannot reliably replace the drawing. The [1904 printed seal](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/053-Seal-of-Camio/) and [public-domain extracted image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:53-Camio_seal.png) should be consulted directly.

## Variants

The surviving versions differ principally in penmanship:

- curvature of the main horizontal line;
- exact junction of the central upright;
- length and angle of subsidiary strokes;
- whether small ends look hooked, forked or merely curled;
- degree of symmetry introduced by a copyist or engraver.

Peterson does not identify two wholly unrelated seals for Camio. Mathers’s 1904 engraving regularizes a hand-drawn manuscript figure into a clean print emblem.

## Lamen

The Sloane text says the seal should be worn as a **lamen**. In this setting a lamen is a ritual emblem worn on the breast or otherwise displayed as the authorized character of the spirit.

## Rudd’s double seal

Harley 6483 does more than reproduce Camio’s mark. It places the demonic character together with:

- Nanael’s angelic name and character;
- Hebrew letters;
- the angel’s Psalm;
- a protective arrangement intended to place the angelic side toward the operator’s heart.

Skinner and Rankine interpret this as a ritual security mechanism: the angel’s authority constrains the corresponding demon.

## Modern seal errors

**[Modern invention]** Commercial and social-media graphics sometimes give Camio:

- concentric circles;
- invented Hebrew inscriptions;
- a Venus or Jupiter designation;
- a symmetrical “demonic alphabet” design unrelated to the manuscripts.

Those are not variants of the Sloane seal merely because they carry Camio’s name. In the rank-metal table a President belongs to Mercury, while Venus appears only in Camio’s much later *777* decan correspondence.

# The angel: Nanael

## Identification

**[Documented]** In Harley MS 6483, Camio is paired with **Nanael**, number 53 in the seventy-twofold angelic series. Skinner and Rankine reproduce the pairing in *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*.

The consonantal triplet is:

> נ נ א — NNA

The angelized form is conventionally:

> ננאאל — NANAEL or NANAËL

The final **אל**, *El*, means “God.” The preceding NNA is a permuted divine triplet, not necessarily a Hebrew lexical word. Consequently, translations of Nanael’s full name are devotional interpretations rather than straightforward dictionary translations.

## Exodus 14:19–21

The seventy-two triplets are generated from three Hebrew verses traditionally counted as seventy-two letters each:

1. Exodus 14:19 is written in its ordinary direction.
2. Exodus 14:20 is reversed.
3. Exodus 14:21 returns to the ordinary direction.
4. The resulting three rows yield seventy-two vertical three-letter names.

This boustrophedon method is documented in Jewish magical and kabbalistic traditions and summarized by Joshua Trachtenberg in *Jewish Magic and Superstition*. It creates divine name-triads; adding **-el** or **-yah** to personify each as an angel belongs especially to later Jewish and Christian angelological development.

## “Shem HaMephorash”

**[Documented linguistic caution]** *Shem ha-meforash* means approximately “the explicit,” “distinguished,” or “fully expressed Name.” In Jewish literature it does not invariably mean the list of seventy-two angels. It may refer to other forms of the divine Name.

Therefore:

- the seventy-two triplets are one historically important “Name of seventy-two” tradition;
- the seventy-two angel persons are a later interpretive formation;
- the Rudd demon-opposing system is later still.

## Jewish sources and disputes

Rashi’s commentary associated the three Exodus verses with a seventy-two-letter divine Name. Other Jewish authorities did not understand the expression identically. Ibn Ezra, for example, related “seventy-two” to expansions of the Tetragrammaton rather than accepting every later angelological conclusion.

Thus the derivation was never a single uncontested doctrine.

## *Sefer Raziel*

**[Tradition]** *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* transmits divine names, angelology and a form of the seventy-twofold Name. Legend says the angel Raziel revealed a celestial book to Adam.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The surviving *Sefer Raziel* is a composite medieval compilation containing materials of different ages; it is not demonstrably a book written by Adam. The influential printed edition appeared in Amsterdam in 1701.

**[Important limitation]** The existence of the 72-fold Name in Raziel literature does not prove that all of Lenain’s nineteenth-century five-degree sectors, civil dates and personality descriptions were present in medieval copies.

## Reuchlin and Christian Kabbalah

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped transmit Hebrew divine-name speculation into learned Christian Europe. Christian Kabbalists increasingly treated the seventy-two triplets as pronounceable angelic names by adding endings such as *-el* and *-iah*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Reuchlin is a major printed hinge in the Western European transmission. He should not be described as the sole inventor of all seventy-two-angel traditions.

## Kircher, 1653

Athanasius Kircher prints **Nanael** as number 53 in volume II, part 1 of *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1653), p. 171. In Kircher’s choir arrangement, Nanael belongs to the seventh choir, the **Principalities**. [Heidelberg digitization of Kircher](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/kircher1653bd2_1/0276/text_ocr).

Kircher’s sequence around it is:

49 Vehuel; 50 Daniel; 51 Hahajiah; 52 Ngimamiah; **53 Nanael**; 54 Nithael; 55 Mebaiah; 56 Poyel/Poutel.

This is firm evidence that Nanael’s ordinal position was established before the surviving Harley 6483 copy.

## Harley 6483: Nanael against Camio

**[Documented]** Rudd’s manuscript system uses angel number 53 to oppose demon number 53. Nanael’s divine name, sigil and Psalm are placed with Camio’s seal. The angelic portion is oriented toward the magician’s heart.

**[Tradition]** The theological assumption is that an angelically restored force controls the corresponding fallen or infernal force.

This pairing is numerical and ritual. It does not arise from an independently documented myth in which Nanael encountered Camio.

## Psalm verse

Harley and Lenain associate Nanael with:

> “Cognovi, Domine, quia aequitas iudicia tua, et in veritate tua humiliasti me.”

A close English rendering is:

> “I know, Lord, that your judgments are just, and in faithfulness you have humbled me.”

This is:

- **Psalm 118:75** in Vulgate/Septuagint numbering;
- **Psalm 119:75** in Hebrew and most Protestant/modern English numbering.

Rudd’s English reads approximately that the speaker knows the Lord’s judgments are just and has been abased in divine truth.

### A manuscript error nearby

Harley 6483 mistakenly repeats Nanael’s angelic seal and Psalm in the material for **Orobas**, demon 55. Skinner and Rankine correct that later pairing to Mebahiah using Harley 6482 and the ordinal sequence.

This error does not undermine the Camio–Nanael pairing. It shows that the copyist accidentally duplicated number 53’s angelic material at number 55.

## Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* provides the developed astrological-devotional profile of Nanael.

For Nanael, Lenain gives:

- number **53**;
- attribute **“Dieu qui abaisse les orgueilleux”**, “God who humbles the proud”;
- 261st through 265th degrees of the zodiac, conventionally 20°–25° Sagittarius;
- five annual days corresponding to the repeated five-degree cycle: 11 May, 22 July, 2 October, 13 December and 23 February;
- daily invocation interval 17:20–17:40;
- Psalm 118:75 Vulgate;
- association with the twenty-seventh decanal genius, Chommé, under Saturn;
- power concerning the higher sciences;
- affinity with ecclesiastics, professors, magistrates and lawyers;
- a contemplative, private or melancholy disposition, attracted to rest, meditation and abstract learning;
- an adverse spirit associated with ignorance and bad qualities.

Lenain also transmits the devotional statement that the Virgin Mary recited this Psalm daily.

**[Legend]** That Marian claim is an occult devotional tradition, not a historically demonstrated fact about Mary.

## Papus

Papus’s *La Cabbale*, especially its later editions, transmitted Christian-kabbalistic tables derived substantially from predecessors such as Lenain. Papus helped make the material accessible to the French occult revival.

**[Finding]** Papus is significant as a popularizer, not the earliest source for Nanael or the 20°–25° Sagittarius attribution.

## Robert Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reproduced and expanded the French practical-kabbalistic angel system. It helped establish the now-familiar format of guardian dates, prayer periods, moral qualities and opposing influences.

**[Tradition/modern expansion]** Ambelain’s system belongs to twentieth-century esoteric practice. It should not be retrojected into Rudd, Kircher or medieval Jewish sources.

## Haziel

Haziel’s modern angel books extend the same current into a popular devotional system. Nanael is commonly assigned:

- 20°–25° Sagittarius;
- approximately 12–17 December as a “guardian” period;
- 17:20–17:40 as a daily period;
- Psalm 119:75;
- inspiration, contemplation, abstract knowledge and spiritual rejuvenation.

**[Modern invention/tradition]** These psychological and “guardian angel by birthday” formulations are late twentieth- and early twenty-first-century elaborations. They are not part of Camio’s early-modern description.

## Camio in *777*

Crowley’s tables CLIX–CLXIV assign:

- spirits 1–36 to decans by day;
- spirits 37–72 to the same sequence by night.

Camio, number 53, appears among the **night succedent** spirits under Virgo. “Succedent” here denotes the middle ten degrees:

> **Virgo 10°–20°**

The matching Golden Dawn tarot decan is:

> **Nine of Pentacles or Disks — Venus in Virgo — “Material Gain.”**

The daytime Goetic ruler of the same decan is **Botis**, number 17. Thus each pip card carries two Goetic spirits in Crowley’s scheme:

| Decan | Day spirit | Night spirit | Tarot pip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virgo 10°–20° | Botis, 17 | **Camio, 53** | Nine of Pentacles/Disks |

**Correction to a widespread claim:** Crowley did not give Camio an individual five-degree quinance in this table. He gave Camio a ten-degree decan conditioned by night.

## Why “Camio = 20°–25° Sagittarius” nevertheless circulates

If the seventy-two Goetic spirits are placed consecutively around 360 degrees at five degrees each:

- spirit 1 occupies 0°–5° Aries;
- spirit 52 occupies 15°–20° Sagittarius;
- spirit 53 occupies **20°–25° Sagittarius**.

That matches Lenain’s sector for angel 53, Nanael. Modern writers therefore often fuse the sequential demon list with the angelic five-degree list.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The synthesis is mathematically coherent.

**[Historical finding]** It is not Crowley’s day/night table and has not been demonstrated as part of the original Camio–Nanael construction in Harley 6483.

## Golden Dawn’s rotated Nanael placement

A further complication appears in Golden Dawn *Book T*. Its angel sequence begins from a different zodiacal point rather than putting angel 1 at 0° Aries. In the extant table, **Nanael and Nithael rule the Four of Wands**, Venus in 20°–30° Aries. Nanael consequently governs the first half:

> **20°–25° Aries**

This conflicts with Lenain’s 20°–25° Sagittarius but reflects a different zero point, not necessarily a spelling error.

# Iconography

## Before 1863

Weyer, Scot and the textual *Goetia* describe Camio but do not provide a naturalistic portrait. The Goetic manuscripts supply only his ritual character.

The core iconographic components available to an artist were:

- a blackbird or thrush;
- transformation into a man;
- a sharp sword;
- burning ashes or embers;
- prophetic speech;
- command of animal and water voices.

## Collin de Plancy’s description

In the 1863 *Dictionnaire infernal*, de Plancy writes of Caym:

> “démon d’une classe supérieure, grand président aux enfers; il se montre sous la forme d’un merle…”

[*Dictionnaire infernal*, 6th ed., 1863, pp. 146–147](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Caym).

In complete close synopsis:

**[Documented in de Plancy]** Caym is a demon of a superior class and a great President in hell. He normally appears as a blackbird. In human form he answers from a burning brazier and carries a sharp saber. De Plancy calls him hell’s most skilful sophist, capable of driving even a seasoned logician to despair. He says Caym disputed with Luther. Caym understands the voices of animals, the sound of waters and future things. He can also appear as a man with an aigrette or plume and a peacock’s tail. He was formerly an angel and commands thirty legions.

### Changes from the grimoire

- “Burning ashes” becomes a visible **brazier**.
- “Good disputer” becomes hell’s supreme **sophist**.
- A **plume/aigrette and peacock tail** are added.
- The Luther episode gives Camio a place in Reformation legend.
- “Sharp sword” becomes a saber in French imagery.

## Louis Le Breton’s engravings

The 1863 edition contains two Caym vignettes, engraved by Jarrault after Louis Le Breton:

1. A blackbird or thrush form.
2. An anthropomorphic form corresponding to de Plancy’s man with sword, plume and peacock tail amid fire or a burning vessel.

The two images visually separate Weyer’s successive forms instead of presenting a single hybrid.

Public-domain reproductions:

- [Caym, first image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ill_dict_infernal_p0163-147_caym_1.jpg)
- [Caym, second image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ill_dict_infernal_p0163-147_caym_2.jpg)

## The Luther story

De Plancy says Caym disputed with Martin Luther. Luther did describe a nocturnal or inward theological argument with “the devil,” notably in the tradition surrounding *Von der Winkelmesse und Pfaffenweihe*.

**[Finding]** Luther did not identify that adversary as Caym.

**[Legend]** De Plancy or an intermediate demonological tradition appears to have fused:

- Camio’s office as master of disputation;
- Luther’s famous dispute with the devil.

The result is literarily apt but historically unsupported.

## Modern art

Modern Camio art commonly follows one of four models:

- Le Breton’s bird;
- a swordsman with blackbird head or wings;
- a man emerging from a brazier;
- a “demonized” hybrid with peacock tail and the 1904 seal.

**[Modern invention]** Added colors, incense, enn formulas, gemstones, sexual identities and elaborate infernal court costumes come from individual practitioners or modern demonolatry systems unless independently cited. They should not be mistaken for details in Weyer or the *Goetia*.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

- Printed the earliest secure Caym entry in 1577.
- Described him as a great President with thirty legions.
- Cited a spirit-office book as source.
- Was a critic of witch trials and credulous accusations.

**Finding:** publication does not establish personal invocation.

## Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

- Scot incorporated the catalogue into *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* in 1584.
- The English reading “thrush” entered the subsequent tradition.
- Peterson associates the translation work with Abraham Fleming.
- Scot’s purpose was skeptical and anti-persecutory, not the promotion of ritual demonology.

## The anonymous *Lemegeton* compiler

- Reordered the catalogue.
- added seals and operational instructions;
- made Caim number 53;
- used language closely related to Scot;
- embedded the catalogue within a Solomonic ritual book.

The compiler’s identity remains unknown.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

- Harley 6483 attributes or associates the material with “Dr Rudd.”
- Skinner and Rankine reconstruct an original Rudd system from around 1655–1656.
- The surviving copy was produced decades after Rudd’s death and likely involves Peter Smart.

The most careful formulation is “the Rudd-attributed Harley system,” not an unqualified autograph by Thomas Rudd.

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

- De Plancy expanded Caym into a literary infernal sophist.
- Le Breton supplied the most influential representational portraits.
- Their work strongly shaped modern popular demon imagery.

## Mathers and Crowley

- Mathers prepared the English magical text used in 1904.
- Crowley edited and published it.
- The printed form “Camio, or Caïm” became standard.
- Crowley subsequently assigned Camio to Virgo II by night in *777*.
- His Bornless, psychological and Enochian additions belong to his modern ritual framework.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition:

- collated multiple manuscripts;
- restored variant names;
- separated manuscript readings from Mathers’s additions;
- made the primary source chain broadly accessible online.

His online *Pseudomonarchia*, Scot and *Goetia* transcriptions remain essential, although facsimiles should be checked for paleographic disputes.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 edition:

- made the Harley angel–demon system widely known;
- reproduced the double-seal logic;
- explained Nanael’s protective role against Camio;
- identified copying errors such as the Nanael repetition in the Orobas material;
- added comparative Golden Dawn information for completeness.

Their historical attribution to Rudd is influential but not universally accepted in every detail.

## Papus, Ambelain and Haziel

These authors belong primarily to Nanael’s later reception:

- Papus popularized French occult Kabbalah.
- Ambelain systematized practical twentieth-century angelology.
- Haziel transformed the angels into a popular guardian-and-personality system.

Their descriptions are significant evidence of reception, not evidence about Weyer’s original Caym.

## Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon has been important in the modern revival of Solomonic ceremonial magic and in promoting dark-mirror techniques. No Camio-specific historical operation by Runyon was located in the sources consulted. His relevance is to twentieth-century Goetic practice generally.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette has written and taught extensively about Crowleyan and Goetic ritual, including the illustrated *Goetia* associated with Christopher Hyatt and David P. Wilson. No independently documented Camio-specific historical claim was located.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s work strongly influenced the modern “spirit model” and grimoire revival, emphasizing ritual engagement with spirits rather than reducing them to psychological symbols.

No specific Camio revelation or historical discovery was found that should be added to the early source chain.

## Frater Achad

Frater Achad was an important Crowleyan Qabalist, but no Camio-specific work was found in the material consulted. Association with Crowley alone is insufficient evidence that he wrote about or invoked Camio.

## Historians of grimoires

- Owen Davies supplies the broad social history of grimoires.
- Richard Kieckhefer documents medieval necromantic manuals.
- Jean-Patrice Boudet and Julien Véronèse illuminate medieval spirit catalogues and the *Livre des Esperitz* milieu.
- Jan Veenstra has contributed to scholarship on medieval learned magic and spirits.

Their importance is contextual. None supplies a verified pre-Weyer Camio attestation.

# Order and number

## Weyer’s 69 versus the *Goetia*’s 72

Weyer has sixty-nine entries. The *Goetia* has seventy-two.

The simplest accounting is:

- The *Goetia* omits Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**.
- It adds **Vassago**, **Seere**, **Dantalion** and **Andromalius**.
- Therefore: 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

Vassago is inserted at number 3. Seere, Dantalion and Andromalius occupy numbers 70–72. Other spirits are substantially reordered.

Caym moves from Weyer’s actual number **41** to Goetic number **53**.

## Why the reorder happened

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Peterson suggests that copying from loose leaves, gatherings or differently ordered catalogues may explain some sequence changes. Deliberate adjustment to the symbolically desirable total of seventy-two is also plausible.

**[Finding]** No surviving compiler’s note explains exactly why Camio moved to 53.

## Scot and Pruflas

Scot’s English catalogue also omits Pruflas in the relevant transmission. This likely influenced the *Lemegeton* compiler’s base list.

## Number 40 error

When a modern comparison removes Weyer’s Pruflas to align the remaining names with Scot, Caym may appear as aligned item 40. That is not the number printed in Weyer: the original list position is 41.

## Milton and Dante

**Finding:** Camio does not appear by name in Dante’s *Divine Comedy* or Milton’s *Paradise Lost*. Both works contain demons and fallen angels, but thematic similarity is not an appearance.

Confusing Camio with biblical Cain in Dante’s treatment of traitors or with Miltonic fallen angels is unjustified.

## Bartimaeus novels

No named Camio/Caim matching the Goetic spirit was located in the principal Bartimaeus character and entity references consulted.

**Finding:** claims that Camio appears in the Bartimaeus novels require a volume and passage; none was verified.

## *Shin Megami Tensei*

**[Modern adaptation]** Caim or Kyme appears in parts of Atlus’s broader *Megami Tensei* franchise, including early *Megami Tensei*, *Kyūyaku Megami Tensei*, *Shin Megami Tensei if…*, *Giten Megami Tensei* and some *Devil Children/DemiKids* material.

## *Persona*

**Finding:** no verified Persona-series appearance for Caim was located. Because *Persona* shares a franchise ancestry with *Megami Tensei*, databases sometimes blur the two. A *Megami Tensei* appearance should not automatically be called a *Persona* appearance.

## *Welcome to Demon School! Iruma-kun*

**[Modern adaptation]** The character **Kamui Caim** has a family ability usually translated as “Translation,” allowing communication with animals. The surname and power are a transparent adaptation of Goetic Caim’s understanding of birds and beasts.

This is one of the clearest modern uses of Camio’s actual office rather than merely his name.

## *Drakengard*

The 2003 game’s protagonist is **Caim**, a sword-wielding warrior who loses his voice.

**[Plausible but unconfirmed]** The game uses multiple names suggestive of Western demonological or mythological sources, making Goetic influence possible. Nevertheless, no creator statement located here proves that the protagonist was named specifically for Weyer’s Caim. His sword and loss of speech provide suggestive thematic echoes but are not evidence of direct adaptation.

## *Fate/Grand Order*

**[Modern adaptation, secondary attestation]** A Demon God Pillar named Caim appears in reference lists for the game’s Final Singularity material. The naming belongs to *Fate/Grand Order*’s systematic use of the seventy-two Goetic names.

## *Helluva Boss*

**Finding:** no official source located names a canonical *Helluva Boss* character Camio/Caim. Fans have attempted to identify background Ars Goetia characters with unused names from the list, including Caim.

Until confirmed in an episode credit, official character sheet or creator statement, “Caim in *Helluva Boss*” remains fan speculation.

## *Hellboy*

No verified named Camio/Caim appearance was located in the principal *Hellboy* references consulted. General use of occult and infernal material is not enough to claim an appearance.

## Other modern usage

Modern occult fiction, games and web projects frequently employ **Caim**, **Camio**, **Caym** or **Kyme**. The diagnostic test for a genuine adaptation is whether it preserves one or more distinctive traits:

- bird or blackbird form;
- sword;
- communication with animals;
- burning ash;
- argumentation;
- prophecy;
- number 53.

A name alone may come from a generic seventy-two-demon list.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Was Caym first published in 1563 or 1577?

- **1563:** first edition of Weyer’s larger *De praestigiis daemonum*.
- **1577:** fifth edition containing the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* catalogue in the relevant form.

**Conclusion:** 1577 is the secure printed date for Caym’s catalogue entry.

## 2. Is Weyer number 40 or 41?

- **41** in Weyer’s actual sequence.
- “40” occurs in normalized comparisons after removing Pruflas.

**Conclusion:** cite Weyer 41, Goetia 53.

## 3. Caim, Caym or Camio?

All three are source readings. None is merely a modern misspelling.

- **Caym** is earliest.
- **Caim** dominates Scot and key Sloane witnesses.
- **Camio** occurs in other manuscripts and became standard through Mathers.
- **Caïm** is Mathers’s pronunciation-friendly alternative.

## 4. Blackbird or thrush?

- Latin *merula*: blackbird.
- English “thrush”: broader family-level description.

Both point to the same ordinary bird tradition. “Crow,” “raven” and “peacock” are not the original first form; the peacock tail enters with de Plancy’s alternate human image.

## 5. Does Camio speak from ashes?

The Latin and early English say he answers “in” burning ashes. Possibilities include:

- appearing amid embers;
- answering through the ashes;
- using a brazier as a medium;
- a visionary image involving glowing ash.

De Plancy selects the brazier interpretation. The original syntax does not settle the mechanics.

## 6. Is Camio the disputant, or does he make the magician one?

- Weyer: causative; he makes someone argue exceptionally well.
- Scot: he makes the best disputers.
- Sloane and Mathers: he is a good disputer.
- De Plancy: he is hell’s supreme sophist.

This is a genuine change in agency across the source chain.

## 7. Did Camio debate Luther?

No early Camio source says so. Luther spoke of disputing with the devil but did not name Caym.

**Conclusion:** de Plancy’s identification is a nineteenth-century legend or literary conflation.

## 8. Is Camio Cain?

Possible spelling relationship, no demonstrated identity. The human/angel contradiction and absence of an intermediate narrative weigh against certainty.

## 9. Is Camio Chemosh?

No evidence in the primary chain and no credible linguistic derivation has been shown.

**Conclusion:** unsupported modern conflation.

## 10. Is the rank planet Mercury, Venus or Saturn?

All three can appear, but answer different questions:

- Mercury: Camio’s rank.
- Venus: ruler of Camio’s *777* Virgo decan.
- Saturn: ruler of the Sagittarius decan in which Lenain places Nanael.

Calling any one of these the exclusive “true planet” erases the textual layering.

## 11. What is Camio’s quinance?

- Crowley gives Camio an entire 10° nocturnal decan, not a quinance.
- Lenain gives Nanael 20°–25° Sagittarius.
- Golden Dawn *Book T* places Nanael at 20°–25° Aries.
- Modern ordinal schemes place both spirit and angel 53 at 20°–25° Sagittarius.

The unqualified statement “Crowley assigns Camio 20°–25° Sagittarius” is false.

## 12. What is Camio’s tarot card?

In Crowley’s demon-decan table:

> **Nine of Pentacles/Disks.**

In a later number-to-five-degree synthesis:

> first half of the **Ten of Wands**.

The Nine of Pentacles is the direct answer for *777*.

## 13. Does each tarot pip have two spirits?

In the *777* arrangement, yes: one day and one night spirit share each 10° minor-arcana decan. For Virgo II, these are Botis and Camio.

This dual occupancy should not be converted automatically into two five-degree halves. Day and night are temporal conditions, not longitude subdivisions.

## 14. Is Nanael’s meaning securely “God who humbles the proud”?

It is Lenain’s theological attribute and fits Psalm 119:75.

Linguistically, however, NNA is a letter-triplet rather than a transparent Hebrew verb phrase. The translation is a devotional gloss, not a literal parsing of ננאאל.

## 15. Did the medieval *Sefer Raziel* contain all of Nanael’s modern correspondences?

The book contains seventy-two-name material, but no evidence reviewed here proves that the medieval text already contained:

- 20°–25° Sagittarius;
- 17:20–17:40;
- December guardian dates;
- modern personality psychology;
- Camio as the opposing demon.

Those should be traced separately through early-modern and nineteenth-century sources.

## 16. Is the Rudd manuscript really by Thomas Rudd?

Harley 6483 is later than Rudd’s lifetime. A Rudd original is an editorial reconstruction based on attribution and related materials.

**Conclusion:** “Rudd-attributed” is certain enough; “Thomas Rudd’s autograph” is not.

## 17. Why does Nanael appear with Orobas?

It is a copying duplication in Harley 6483. The correct ordinal angel for spirit 55 is Mebahiah. Skinner and Rankine use Harley 6482 to repair the sequence.

## 18. Is Camio the 52nd spirit?

No. At least one modern encyclopedia or derivative source has called him the 52nd. The manuscript and printed *Goetia* place him at **53**.

## 19. Do the manuscripts give Camio thirty legions?

Yes. Thirty is stable from Weyer through Scot, the Sloane manuscripts and Mathers. No principal witness consulted gives a different number.

## 20. Does Camio have a pre-Weyer attestation?

No secure one has been found.

The lost book Weyer cites may be older, and the phrase “Gomeris vel Caym” in a related office-book tradition deserves further study. Neither supplies the same securely dated spirit profile before 1577.

# Open questions

1. **What exactly was Weyer’s exemplar?**  
   His spirit-office source has not been identified in a surviving manuscript preserving the Caym entry exactly.

2. **Why does “Gomeris vel Caym” occur in a related manuscript?**  
   It may be a copying error, alternate spirit name or trace of a cataloguing branch that predates the surviving *Pseudomonarchia*.

3. **How did Caym become Camio?**  
   Possible mechanisms include misreading of minim strokes, expansion of an abbreviation, Latinization or contamination from a neighboring name. No copyist explains the change.

4. **Was Cain consciously intended?**  
   Medieval *Caym* spellings make the proposal possible, but an intermediate narrative or gloss is still missing.

5. **What did “answers in burning ashes” signify ritually?**  
   It might allude to divination by embers, a visionary medium or merely the spirit’s appearance. The sources do not describe an operation.

6. **How exact are the seal’s manuscript relationships?**  
   A dedicated stemmatic comparison of high-resolution Sloane 2731, 3825, 3648, Harley 6483 and later copies could distinguish meaningful inherited strokes from penmanship.

7. **When was the Camio–Nanael pairing first made?**  
   The ordinal ingredients existed by Kircher’s time; the surviving Harley witness is early eighteenth century. A demonstrable mid-seventeenth-century Rudd exemplar remains to be produced.

8. **Did Rudd himself assign zodiacal quinances?**  
   The angel–demon opposition is manuscript evidence. Some zodiacal and Golden Dawn comparisons in modern editions are editorial supplements. The stages require stricter separation.

9. **What is the earliest edition of de Plancy containing the full Luther story and alternate peacock form?**  
   The 1863 text is verified; comparison across all six editions would show exactly when each embellishment entered.

10. **Which modern fictional appearances are officially sourced?**  
    *Iruma-kun* preserves Camio’s animal-language office clearly. Several game and animation claims currently rest on fan databases and need primary credits, scripts or creator statements.

---

# Conclusions

The reconstructable Camio tradition begins securely with **Weyer’s Caym in 1577**, not with an ancient deity and not yet with a seal. Weyer’s spirit is a great President, first a blackbird, then a swordsman answering amid burning ashes. His stable offices are disputation, understanding the voices of flying creatures, cattle, dogs and waters, and truthful prophecy. He commands thirty legions and is said to be a former angel.

Scot’s 1584 translation supplied **Caim** and “thrush.” The seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia* moved him from Weyer’s number 41 to **number 53**, added a seal and shifted “makes good disputants” toward “is a good disputant.” Harley 6483 paired **Camio** with **Nanael 53**, using the angel’s name, character and Psalm as a protective constraint. Mathers and Crowley stabilized “Camio, or Caïm” in 1904.

The major later correspondences must remain distinct:

- **President → Mercury/quicksilver**
- **Crowley’s Camio → Virgo 10°–20° by night → Nine of Pentacles**
- **Lenain’s Nanael → Sagittarius 20°–25° → Psalm 119:75**
- **Golden Dawn’s rotated Nanael → Aries 20°–25° → Four of Wands**
- **Modern sequential Camio → Sagittarius 20°–25° → first half of Ten of Wands**

No ancient god behind Camio has been demonstrated. Cain is possible as a spelling lead but unsupported as an identity. Chemosh, Baal-Peor, Greek *kauma*, Latin *caelum* and Gaelic *caim* are modern or disputed proposals without a traceable source chain.

The fullest defensible historical result is therefore not a single timeless portrait, but a layered one: a sixteenth-century spirit-office entry, a seventeenth-century Solomonic seal, an early-eighteenth-century angelic counterweight, a nineteenth-century French astrological personality and infernal portrait, and several incompatible nineteenth- and twentieth-century zodiacal systems.

# Sallos: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **[Documented text]** — directly attested in a manuscript, early printed edition, or identifiable modern work.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a historical relationship proposed from textual comparison, dating, or manuscript transmission.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an attribution made within an occult system; documented as a feature of that system, not asserted as an empirical fact.
- **[Tradition / legend]** — a religious or magical claim transmitted as tradition.
- **[Disputed]** — plausible or circulated, but unsupported, textually uncertain, or contradicted by other witnesses.
- **[Modern invention]** — a demonstrably late elaboration, adaptation, personal revelation, game character, or newly assigned symbolism.
- **[Negative finding]** — a requested connection for which no adequate attestation was found.

Dates and page references refer to the editions identified in the source list. Because pagination varies among scans, section and manuscript folio references are supplied where possible.

# The name and its origins

## 1. No demonstrated ancient god behind Sallos

**[Negative finding]** No secure identification with an ancient god, hero, place, Greek word, Latin word, biblical figure, or Near Eastern divine name has been established.

Unlike transparent Goetic names such as *Bael*/*Baal*, *Astaroth*/*Astarte*, or *Amon*/*Amun*, the chain for Sallos begins with a late Renaissance spirit catalogue. Searches of the principal proposed ancestors—the *Testament of Solomon*, the Munich necromancer’s handbook, *Livre des Esperitz*, Abramelin lists, and surviving comparative lists of the *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* family—did not locate *Sallos*, *Saleos*, or *Zaleos*.

The earliest secure form is therefore:

- **Zaleos**, Johann Weyer, 1577.

A closely related but not certainly identical **Caleos** occurs in the Folger manuscript known as the *Book of Oberon*, copied approximately 1577–1583. Because its date overlaps Weyer’s publication, it cannot presently be used as an unquestionably earlier attestation.

## 3. Caleos: the strongest near-relative

Folger MS V.b.26 describes a spirit called **Caleos**:

> “The 8 is called Caleos, he hath power & knowledge of infinite treasures…”

The remaining entry says that Caleos makes one beloved, procures familiarity, appears as a knight riding a crocodile, wears two crowns, and is false in his answers unless well constrained. [Folger MS V.b.26 transcription, p. 83](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

The correspondences are unusually specific:

| Caleos | Zaleos/Saleos/Sallos |
|---|---|
| C-/Z-/S-aleos | Zaleos, Saleos |
| Knight | Handsome or gallant soldier |
| Rides a crocodile | Rides a crocodile |
| Wears two crowns | Wears a ducal crown |
| Makes one beloved | Causes love |
| Knowledge of treasure | Not retained in the *Goetia* |
| False in answers unless constrained | Not retained in the *Goetia* |

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Caleos is probably a textual relative of Zaleos and may be the same catalogue-being in another recension of the *Office of Spirits* tradition.

**[Disputed]** It cannot yet be shown that Caleos and Zaleos are identical rather than two entries shaped from a common formula. Nor can Caleos securely fill Weyer’s terminal “&c.” The Folger text may preserve offices lost from Weyer, but direct descent has not been demonstrated.

## 4. Proposed etymologies

### Hebrew *shalosh*, “three”

Some modern tables retrovert *Sallos* into Hebrew-looking forms such as **ShALVSh**, inviting comparison with שָׁלוֹשׁ, *shalosh*, “three.”

**[Disputed]** This is numerically and phonetically suggestive only after the European name has already been written in Hebrew letters. Harley 6483 and later occult tables commonly transliterate demon names into Hebrew for gematria. That does not prove Hebrew origin, and nothing in Sallos’s description concerns the number three.

### Greek *salos* or *saleuō*

Modern writers sometimes compare Greek σάλος, “tossing, agitation,” or σαλεύω, “to shake or cause to move.”

**[Disputed]** The resemblance is phonetic. There is no known Greek intermediary, no early gloss, and no semantic bridge to a crocodile-riding soldier or love magic.

### Mesopotamian Shala/Sala

The Mesopotamian goddess **Shala**, associated with grain and with the storm god Adad/Ishkur, has occasionally been proposed.

**[Disputed]** No manuscript, early editor, or historical transmission links Shala to Zaleos. The proposal is modern associative mythology, not a reconstruction supported by names moving through Greek, Latin, Hebrew, or Arabic textual channels.

### Salus, Salome, Saleh, Samael, Scylla, Taweret, or Sobek

These names and figures appear in modern speculative lists because of sound, the crocodile, or themes of fertility and attraction.

**[Modern speculation]** None has a traceable source chain. In particular:

- Sobek and Taweret explain a crocodile only by Egyptian association.
- Samael requires substantial, unexplained corruption.
- Latin *salus*, “health/safety,” does not explain the name’s form or office.
- Salome and Saleh are phonetic comparisons without transmission evidence.
- The seal has been said to resemble a double axe, but the manuscripts do not identify it as one.

### Conclusion on the name

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** *Zaleos* is most safely treated as a name from the late-medieval/early-modern *Office of Spirits* catalogue tradition whose linguistic origin is lost. *Saleos* and *Sallos* are ordinary scribal or editorial variants. *Zaebos* is probably a later corruption of *Zaleos* or *Saleos*. *Caleos* is a probable cognate whose exact relationship remains unresolved.

# The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, 1577

Johann Weyer printed the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Sallos’s ancestor is entry 65 in the usual enumeration.

### Latin, complete surviving entry

> **“Zaleos magnus Comes: apparet ut miles pulcherrimus in crocodilo equitans, & ducali ornatus corona, pacificus, &c.”**

— Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, §65. [Latin and English source text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

Literal gloss:

> Zaleos, a great Count: he appears as a very handsome soldier riding upon a crocodile and adorned with a ducal crown, peaceful, etc.

Important textual points:

- **[Documented text]** Weyer calls him a **magnus Comes**, “great Count” or Earl—not a Duke.
- **[Documented text]** The crown is nevertheless **ducalis**, ducal.
- **[Documented text]** *Miles pulcherrimus* is stronger than merely “soldier”: “very handsome,” “most beautiful,” or “very fine soldier.”
- **[Documented text]** Weyer supplies no number of legions.
- **[Documented text]** Weyer supplies no surviving office concerning love.
- **[Disputed grammar]** *Pacificus* may describe the spirit as peaceful or the manner of his appearance. Later English texts favor “peaceably.”
- **[Open textual problem]** The terminal “&c.” proves abbreviation but does not reveal what was omitted.

Weyer says he drew the catalogue from a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also described as a book called *Empto* or associated with Solomon. He intentionally left out seals and ritual material, stating that he wished the text not to serve as a practical manual. Thus Sallos has no Weyer seal.

Weyer’s broader purpose was polemical: he accepted the existence of demons but argued against the witch persecutions of his period. Printing a demon catalogue does not prove that he personally evoked Zaleos.

## 2. Scot, 1584

Reginald Scot reproduced the catalogue in Book XV of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*. His complete English entry reads:

> **“Saleos is a great earle, he appeareth as a gallant soldier, riding on a crocodile, and weareth a dukes crowne, peaceable, &c.”**

— Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter II, first edition p. 392. [Peterson transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); [1584 edition catalogue record](https://www.loc.gov/item/59059328/)

Scot preserves Weyer closely:

- *magnus Comes* → “great earle.”
- *miles pulcherrimus* → “gallant soldier.”
- *in crocodilo equitans* → “riding on a crocodile.”
- *ducali … corona* → “dukes crowne.”
- *pacificus* → “peaceable.”

**[Documented text]** Scot still gives neither office nor legions.

Joseph Peterson observes that the English may have been prepared partly by Abraham Fleming and that several deviations suggest use of a poor handwritten witness rather than mechanical translation from Weyer’s printed Latin. That possibility matters for *Zaleos* → *Saleos*, although the vowel change could also be editorial normalization.

## 3. The Folger *Book of Oberon*, c. 1577–1583

Caleos’s full description gives four offices or traits absent from Weyer’s surviving Zaleos paragraph:

- knowledge of infinite treasures;
- making one beloved;
- procuring familiarity;
- answering falsely unless constrained.

**[Documented text]** The knight, crocodile, crown, and making-beloved office are present.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The *Goetia*’s love office may descend from a fuller *Office of Spirits* reading resembling Caleos rather than being invented wholesale in the seventeenth century.

**[Disputed]** “Purchaseth familiaritie” need not mean providing a familiar spirit. In early modern English it can mean obtaining intimacy, favor, or familiar relations. Reading it as “gives a familiar spirit” is possible but not demanded by the wording.

## 4. Sloane manuscripts and the seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia*

The *Lemegeton* or *Lesser Key of Solomon* is a seventeenth-century compilation traditionally attributed to Solomon. Its first book, the *Ars Goetia*, reorganizes Weyer’s catalogue, restores seals, expands descriptions, and reaches seventy-two spirits.

Sloane MS 3825, fol. 103r, reads in Peterson’s diplomatic transcription:

> “The 19th spirit is called Saleos, he is a great and mighty duke…”

The entry continues by describing the gallant soldier riding a crocodile with a duke’s crown, appearing peaceably, causing reciprocal love, commanding thirty legions, and requiring his seal to be worn. [Peterson’s *Ars Goetia* transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

A clause-by-clause account of the complete entry is:

1. He is the nineteenth spirit.
2. His name is Saleos.
3. He is a great and mighty Duke.
4. He appears as a gallant soldier.
5. He rides a crocodile.
6. He wears a duke’s crown.
7. He appears peaceably.
8. He causes the love of women toward men.
9. He causes the love of men toward women.
10. He governs thirty legions of spirits.
11. His seal must be worn before the operator.

Peterson prints this at p. 15 of his 2001 edition, using Sloane 3825 as his principal witness.

| Witness | Reading | Significant features |

| Sloane 3825, f. 103r | Saleos | Great and mighty Duke; thirty legions; seal |
| Sloane 2731 | Saleos | Substantially the same; minor verbal abbreviation |
| Sloane 3648 | Sallos | Same developed Goetia form, but an error-prone witness generally |

### What changed between Weyer and the *Goetia*?

| Feature | Weyer/Scot | *Goetia* |
|---|---|---|
| Rank | Great Count/Earl | Great and mighty Duke |
| Crown | Ducal | Ducal |
| Appearance | Handsome/gallant soldier on crocodile | Same |
| Manner | Peaceful/peaceably | Peaceably |
| Office | Not preserved | Causes reciprocal love |
| Legions | Not stated | Thirty |
| Seal | Omitted | Supplied |
| Number | Weyer 65; Scot sequence 64 | 19 |

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Calling Sallos a Duke may harmonize the formal rank with the already-ducal crown. Alternatively, the compilers may have followed a witness that independently called him a Duke.

**[Disputed]** There is no proof that “Count” was merely Weyer’s mistake. It remains the earliest attested rank.

## 5. Mathers-Crowley, 1904

The 1904 edition begins:

> “(19.) SALLOS.—The Nineteenth Spirit is Sallos (or Saleos). He is a Great and Mighty Duke…”

It then reproduces the soldier, crocodile, crown, peaceful appearance, reciprocal-love office, and thirty legions, followed by the seal. The entry appears on pp. 27–28. [Sacred Texts transcription](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm); [1904 scan](https://books.google.com/books?vid=Harvard%3A32044011342870)

The wording “but peaceably” is important. It most naturally means that the crowned warrior appears without threatening action. Modern descriptions that call Sallos an intrinsically pacifist, gentle, ethical, or nonviolent demon go beyond the text.

## 6. Offices: what Sallos does and does not do

### Securely attributed

- **[Documented text, Goetia onward]** Produces love or attraction from women toward men and men toward women.
- **[Documented text, possible earlier cognate]** Caleos “maketh one beloved.”

The language is explicitly binary and heterosexual because that is how the seventeenth-century text phrases the office. Gender-neutral expansions are modern adaptations.

### Not attributed in the standard Sallos entry

Sallos is not said to:

- teach languages;
- teach sciences or liberal arts;
- reveal hidden treasure;
- transmute metals;
- make people invisible;
- provide familiars;
- answer all questions;
- reveal past, present, and future;
- reconcile enemies generally;
- create universal peace.

Treasure, familiarity, and untrustworthy answers belong only to the possibly cognate **Caleos** witness. Claims that Weyer himself gave Zaleos the love office are incorrect: Weyer’s surviving sentence does not.

## 7. Second form

**[Negative finding]** The Sallos entry never instructs the conjurer to demand a second, human, or “fairer” form. The first appearance is already anthropomorphic: a soldier riding an animal.

This differs from spirits whose entries explicitly say that the operator must command a human form or another shape.

## 8. Time of operation

The entry supplies no Sallos-specific hour. The applicable rules occur in the *Goetia*’s general rubric:

- **[Documented text]** Dukes may be bound from sunrise until noon.
- **[Documented text]** The weather should be clear.
- **[Documented text]** The best lunar ages are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 14 days.
- **[Esoteric extrapolation]** Friday and the planetary hour of Venus follow from the Duke–Venus attribution but are not stated in the Sallos paragraph.
- **[Esoteric extrapolation]** Conjuring while 0°–5° Cancer rises, or while the Sun occupies that quinance, belongs to the later *777*/decan overlay.
- **[Modern invention]** Exact civil-calendar dates and clock times presented without latitude, solar-time correction, or a named correspondence system are not original *Goetia* data.

# Number and order: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

Weyer’s catalogue normally contains **69 spirits**. Scot omitted Weyer’s fourth entry, Pruflas or Busas, leaving **68 translated descriptions**. This produces a persistent numbering trap:

- Zaleos is **Weyer 65**.
- Because Scot omits Pruflas before him, Saleos is the **64th spirit in Scot’s surviving sequence**.
- Some later concordances label him “Weyer 64” because they have silently counted through Scot rather than Weyer.

The *Goetia* reaches seventy-two by omitting one Weyer spirit and adding four:

### Omitted

- **Pruflas/Busas**, Weyer 4.

### Added

- **Vassago**, Goetia 3.
- **Seere**, Goetia 70.
- **Dantalion**, Goetia 71.
- **Andromalius**, Goetia 72.

Thus:

> 69 Weyer spirits − Pruflas + four additions = 72 Goetic spirits.

The *Goetia* also substantially reorders Weyer. Sallos moves from Weyer 65 to Goetia 19; this is not simply the effect of adding three names ahead of him.

# The seal

## 1. Manuscript form

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3825 places the sigil between Sallos’s description and the following spirit, Purson. The seal is not present in Weyer.

In words, its stable core is:

- a wide, roughly horizontal line-character;
- a prominent central vertical;
- multiple shorter uprights or pendant strokes;
- curled or looped terminals and interior turns;
- near-bilateral visual balance, though not exact geometrical symmetry.

The 1904 engraving prints the sigil inside a circular border. Some reproductions add or regularize letters around the circle. [Mathers-Crowley seal image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:19-Sallos_seal.png)

## 2. Variants

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731, 3825, 3648, and Harley 6483 preserve the same recognizable core character, but differ in pen curvature, spacing, the closure of loops, and the length or angle of stems.

These variations resemble normal manuscript copying, not four symbolically distinct seals. No manuscript supplies a verbal interpretation of the lines.

**[Negative finding]** No demonstrable “ancient pictogram” lies behind the sigil. Explanations that its loops mean romantic union, that it encodes a crocodile, womb, heart, chains, or double axe are modern readings.

## 3. The Rudd double seal

Harley 6483 places each demon in a protective angelic scheme:

- the demonic seal is carried on one face;
- the corresponding Shem angel and psalm formula belong to the reverse;
- the angelic side is directed toward the operator’s heart;
- the angel is invoked before the demon is constrained.

For Sallos, the reverse or counterbalancing authority is **Leuviah**. This is not an original feature of Weyer or the ordinary *Goetia* recension; it is characteristic of the Rudd material edited by Stephen Skinner and David Rankine.

# The angel: Leuviah

## 1. The seventy-two names from Exodus 14:19–21

### The textual operation

**[Documented esoteric tradition]** Exodus 14:19, 14:20, and 14:21 each contain seventy-two Hebrew letters in the relevant Masoretic form. The later construction writes:

1. verse 19 in normal direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse direction;
3. verse 21 in normal direction.

Reading the resulting columns produces seventy-two three-letter groups. Christian kabbalists commonly add **-El** or **-Yah** to turn the triads into pronounceable angel names. Agrippa explains this method in *De occulta philosophia* III.25. [Agrippa, Book III, chapter 25](https://esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm)

### Historical qualifications

- **[Documented Jewish tradition]** A seventy-twofold divine Name is known in medieval Jewish sources and traditions surrounding *Sefer Raziel*.
- **[Tradition / legend]** Moses was said to have used the Name in dividing the Red Sea.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** The present *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is a medieval Ashkenazi compilation, not a literal book delivered to Adam by the angel Raziel.
- **[Disputed]** “Shem ha-Mephorash” does not always mean these seventy-two triads. In earlier rabbinic usage it can mean the explicit or properly articulated divine Name, particularly the Tetragrammaton.
- **[Documented disagreement]** Jewish commentators did not uniformly accept the triadic derivation. Ibn Ezra, for example, related “seventy-two” to an expanded numerical form of the divine Name rather than simply endorsing the later angel table. [Ibn Ezra on Exodus 14:19](https://www.sefaria.org/Ibn_Ezra_on_Exodus.14.19.2)

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) and Agrippa’s 1533 *De occulta philosophia* helped transmit the material into Latin Christian occultism. Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) further circulated elaborate Christian-kabbalistic tables of the seventy-two names. [Kircher’s *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm)

## 2. Leuviah’s name

Leuviah is number 19 in the standard sequence.

| Form | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Leuviah** | Common French and English occult spelling |
| **Leviah** | Simplified spelling |
| **Louviah/Louvi-Ah** | French transliteration variant |
| **לוויה** | L‑V‑V‑Y‑H, one conventional Hebrew expansion |
| Root **לוו** | Nineteenth three-letter group in the usual table |

The ending **-Yah** is a shortened divine name. The root letters themselves are not supplied with a secure ordinary Hebrew lexical translation in the early magical sources.

### “God who helps sinners”

Lenain calls Leuviah *Dieu, secours des pécheurs*, approximately “God, help of sinners” or “God who comes to the aid of sinners.”

**[Documented esoteric gloss]** This is Leuviah’s devotional title in Lenain’s system.

**[Disputed as etymology]** It is not a literal dictionary translation of L‑V‑V‑Y‑H. Modern websites frequently present such devotional sentences as Hebrew etymologies, but they function as theological epithets.

### Confusion with two Lauviahs

The angel lists contain similar names at:

- no. 11, frequently **Lauviah**;
- no. 17, also frequently **Lauviah** or **Loviah**;
- no. 19, **Leuviah**.

This duplication is old enough to appear in established occult tables. It causes modern search results to transfer dreams, revelation, memory, or dates among angels 11, 17, and 19. For Sallos’s Rudd pairing, the correct serial angel is **Leuviah 19**.

## 3. The psalm verse

Harley 6483’s Rudd material, as edited by Skinner and Rankine, gives:

> **“Expectans expectavi Dominum et intendit mihi.”**

A close translation is:

> I waited expectantly for the Lord, and he turned toward me.

The corresponding Hebrew/modern English verse is Psalm 40:1:

> I waited patiently for the Lord; he inclined to me and heard my cry.

The numbering difference results from the Septuagint/Vulgate system, in which it is Psalm 39, versus the Hebrew/Masoretic numbering, in which it is Psalm 40. Depending on whether a superscription is counted, the Latin verse may be cited as 39:1 or 39:2.

**[Documented text]** Skinner and Rankine print it with Sallos and Leuviah on p. 121 of *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*.

## 4. Leuviah in Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives Leuviah:

- angel no. 19;
- “God, help of sinners”;
- degrees **91 through 95**;
- invocation at noon in the version reproduced by Papus;
- Psalm 39’s opening verse;
- the power to obtain divine grace;
- governance of memory and human intelligence;
- a favorable character described as amiable, pleasant, modest, simple, patient, and resigned in adversity;
- a contrary side associated with losses, prodigality or disorder, and despair.

[Lenain, *La science cabalistique* (1823 scan)](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf)

Degrees 91–95 are the first five degrees of Cancer when the zodiac is counted from the first degree of Aries:

- Aries: 1–30;
- Taurus: 31–60;
- Gemini: 61–90;
- Cancer: 91–120.

Modern notation normally calls the same interval **0°–5° Cancer**, although strict ordinal counting produces 1°–5°. The boundary convention explains the apparent discrepancy.

## 5. Papus

Papus reproduced Lenain’s seventy-two angel notices in *La Cabbale* in the early twentieth century. His account retains Leuviah’s:

- number;
- devotional meaning;
- 91°–95° position;
- psalm;
- memory and intelligence;
- favorable and contrary dispositions.

**[Documented transmission]** Papus was an important popularizer, not the originator of these particular Leuviah attributes. [Papus, *La Cabbale* scan](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf)

## 6. Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) continued the French angelic tradition. His and related later tables assign the angels:

- five-degree zodiacal sectors;
- planetary or sephirotic hierarchies;
- seals;
- daily twenty-minute periods;
- ritual colors, incense, and calendar dates.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** These are twentieth-century systematizations based partly on Lenain and ceremonial-magic sources.

Later prefatory material associated with Ambelain claims that experimentation in the 1950s and 1960s revealed that some traditional seals belonged to “contrary” spirits.

**[Modern occult claim]** This is testimony within an operative tradition, not independently established manuscript history.

## 7. Haziel and the guardian-angel calendar

The author **Haziel**, a pseudonym commonly identified with François Bernad-Termès, popularized the seventy-two names for a mass French-speaking readership in books including *Notre ange gardien existe* and *Communiquer avec son ange gardien*.

In the 1995 *Communiquer avec son ange gardien*:

- Leuviah is guardian angel 19;
- it governs people born approximately 22–26 June;
- its zodiacal domicile is given as the first five degrees of Cancer;
- it is treated as an individual guardian whose aid can be sought through prayer;
- the book develops repeated 73-day contact dates and practical devotional procedures. [Haziel, *Communiquer avec son ange gardien*, pp. 16, 34–36](https://bretagne-namaste.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Communiquer-Avec-Son-Ange-Gardien.pdf)

**[Modern invention/systematization]** The individualized birthday guardian, five annual “contact days,” emotional/cellular-memory language, and exact daily twenty-minute clock windows are not found in Rudd or medieval Jewish sources. They are features of late French and Spanish angelology associated with Kabaleb, Haziel, Ambelain, and successors.

## 8. Rudd’s pairing of Leuviah with Sallos

Harley MS 6483 is a 1712–1713 copy attributed to material belonging to “Dr. Rudd,” traditionally identified as Thomas Rudd. Peter Smart is named as copyist.

In this recension:

1. the operator invokes the corresponding Shem angel;
2. the psalm verse establishes the angelic authority;
3. the demon is summoned and constrained under that authority;
4. the double seal keeps the angelic side toward the operator.

Sallos 19 is paired serially with Leuviah 19.

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** The pairing is secure in Harley 6483.

**[Disputed attribution]** Thomas Rudd died in 1656, while the surviving copy is from 1712–1713. The manuscript claims derivation from Rudd’s papers, but the exact extent of Rudd’s authorship cannot be proved from the surviving copy alone.

**[Important distinction]** Harley 6483 establishes the serial pairing but does not by itself settle every later astrological attribution. The 0°–5° Cancer position follows an Aries-first seventy-twofold zodiac; other occult schemes begin the angel sequence at Leo or at a sidereal reference point.

## 1. Crowley’s *777*

Column CLV of *Liber 777*, “The Goetic Demons of the Decans by Day,” places **Sallos 19** in Cancer and marks him with Venus. The companion night demon is Purson 20. [Crowley, *777 Revised*, column CLV](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf)

The operative pattern divides each ten-degree decan into two quinances:

| Cancer I | Spirit | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| 0°–5° Cancer | Sallos 19 | Day/ascendant |
| 5°–10° Cancer | Purson 20 | Night/succedent counterpart |

This is why a single tarot pip can carry two Goetic spirits: one for each five-degree half.

## 2. Two of Cups

Golden Dawn “Book T” assigns the first decan of Cancer to:

- **Venus in Cancer**;
- **Two of Cups**;
- title **Lord of Love**;
- Chokmah in the suit of Water.

[Israel Regardie, *The Complete Golden Dawn*, “Two of Cups”](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/R/Regardie%20-%20The%20Complete%20Golden%20Dawn.pdf)

The agreement with Sallos’s love office is striking, but chronology matters:

- **[Documented text]** Sallos already causes love in seventeenth-century *Goetia* manuscripts.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** The Golden Dawn tarot decan also means love because Venus occupies Cancer.
- **[Disputed causal claim]** There is no evidence that the *Goetia* compilers invented Sallos’s office from the Two of Cups. The Caleos manuscript shows that a “makes one beloved” office was already circulating around Weyer’s time.

Sallos’s case therefore contains a later symbolic convergence rather than a proven historical derivation.

## 3. Three distinct planetary attributions

It is useful not to collapse three separate systems:

| Source of planet | Planet |
|---|---|
| Sallos’s ducal rank | Venus |
| First Cancer decan | Venus |
| Cancer’s sign ruler | Moon |

The first two happen to coincide. The Moon belongs to the sign as a whole, not to Sallos’s rank or decan face.

Modern tables assigning Sallos exclusively to the Moon and silver usually derive the planet from Cancer while ignoring the *Goetia*’s explicit Duke–Venus–copper rule.

## 4. All Goetic ranks, planets, and metals

The *Goetia*’s general material rules are:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |

This creates an interesting historical fork:

- Weyer’s **Count Zaleos** would imply a Venus–Moon, copper–silver combination if one retroactively applied the later table.
- Goetia’s **Duke Sallos** straightforwardly takes Venus and copper.

**[Caution]** Weyer did not publish this metal table with Zaleos. Applying it to Weyer’s rank is retrospective.

## 5. Astrological timing in practice

A historically layered working schedule might combine:

1. an even lunar age from the *Goetia*;
2. clear weather;
3. sunrise to noon because Sallos is a Duke;
4. Friday or a Venus hour because Dukes answer to Venus;
5. the ascent or solar transit of 0°–5° Cancer from *777*;
6. a copper seal;
7. prior invocation of Leuviah by the Rudd method.

Only items 1–3 belong to the *Goetia*’s general ritual rubric. Items 4–7 are later ceremonial syntheses. Poke Runyon’s modern system is a prominent example of combining the Goetic spirits, corresponding Shem angels, zodiacal quinances, metals, and scrying procedures.

## 6. Enochian overlays

Two different Enochian connections are often conflated:

- **[Documented text]** Crowley’s 1904 *Goetia* includes Enochian or “Angelic language” versions of general conjurations.
- **[Documented text]** Harley 6482 contains Rudd material involving expanded versions of John Dee’s holy tables and related spirit catalogues.
- **[Negative finding]** Sallos has no securely attested individual Enochian name in the *Goetia*.
- **[Later esoteric overlay]** Golden Dawn Enochian tablets can be correlated indirectly with the Two of Cups, Cancer, elemental Water, or a zodiacal subdivision. Those are correspondential bridges, not evidence that Sallos originated in Dee’s angelic system.

# Iconography

## 1. Before de Plancy

The earliest iconography is verbal:

- a handsome or gallant soldier;
- riding a crocodile;
- wearing a ducal crown;
- appearing peaceably.

The manuscripts add a seal but no portrait. Nothing specifies:

- armor period;
- beard or hair color;
- weapon;
- ethnicity;
- crocodile species;
- clothing color;
- whether the crown is worn over a helmet;
- whether the crocodile is literal or symbolic.

Modern images filling these details are artistic interpretations.

## 2. Collin de Plancy’s *Zaebos*

Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818 and changed substantially through six editions. The securely verified Sallos-related entry occurs under **Zaebos** in the illustrated sixth edition of 1863, p. 711:

> “Zaebos, grand comte des enfers. Il a la figure d’un beau soldat monté sur un crocodile…”

It continues that his head bears a ducal crown and that his disposition is gentle. [1863 Zaebos entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Zaebos)

Translation:

> Zaebos, great Count of Hell. He has the figure of a handsome soldier mounted on a crocodile; his head is adorned with a ducal crown. He is gentle in character.

The Count, handsome soldier, crocodile, ducal crown, and gentleness closely reproduce Weyer’s Zaleos.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Zaebos is almost certainly a corruption or typographical descendant of **Zaleos**, rather than an independently attested demon.

**[Negative finding]** I could not securely verify a Zaebos entry in the incomplete online transcription of the 1818 first edition. Accordingly, the book’s 1818 publication date should not be treated automatically as the date of Zaebos’s entry. The 1863 entry and picture are certain; the first intermediate edition containing the spelling remains to be established by full edition-by-edition collation.

## 3. Louis Le Breton’s portrait

Louis Le Breton’s 1863 engraving depicts:

- a bearded, martial male figure;
- an elaborate helmet or crown with projecting ornament;
- a short spear or staff;
- a round shield;
- the figure astride a crocodile viewed in profile.

[Louis Le Breton’s *Zaebos* engraving](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zaebos.png)

**[Modern-to-the-source artistic invention]** The spear, shield, beard, helmet form, clothing, and pose are Le Breton’s additions. They do not appear in Weyer’s sentence or the Goetic manuscripts.

This image became the standard portrait of Sallos/Zaebos in modern demon dictionaries, colorizations, tarot and oracle decks, tattoos, and game-related art.

## 4. Seal versus portrait

The Le Breton picture and the Goetic seal have independent functions:

- the portrait visualizes the textual manifestation;
- the seal is the ritual identifying character.

Claims that the portrait “decodes” the seal, or that the seal graphically portrays a crocodile-riding soldier, have no manuscript support.

## 5. Modern devotional and demonolatry art

Modern artists frequently give Sallos:

- red, orange, or golden hair;
- green armor;
- a romantic or courtly personality;
- serpentine or draconic features;
- hearts, roses, copper, or Venus symbols;
- androgynous or explicitly erotic forms.

**[Modern invention]** Such images can be internally meaningful within contemporary practice, but they should not be mistaken for historical details. Their secure historical anchors are only soldier, crocodile, ducal crown, love, and peaceful appearance.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

**[Documented role]** Weyer first printed **Zaleos** in 1577. He transmitted a spirit catalogue while arguing that supposed witches were often deceived, ill, or unjustly accused.

**[Negative finding]** There is no evidence in the checked sources that Weyer personally performed an operation with Zaleos.

## Reginald Scot

**[Documented role]** Scot printed **Saleos** in English in 1584. Like Weyer, his purpose was skeptical and anti-persecutory rather than the promotion of operative demon magic.

His spelling became influential in English manuscript and print transmission.

## The compilers of the *Lemegeton*

**[Documented role]** Anonymous seventeenth-century compilers moved Saleos to number 19, changed the rank to Duke, assigned thirty legions, supplied the love office explicitly, and restored a seal.

**[Tradition / legend]** The text attributes its magical wisdom to Solomon.  
**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The surviving book is an early modern compilation, not a manuscript written by the biblical king.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

**[Documented manuscript attribution]** Harley 6483 was copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713 from material attributed to Dr. Rudd.

**[Documented contribution]** The Rudd recension paired Sallos with Leuviah and placed the demon under angelic constraint.

**[Disputed]** Whether Thomas Rudd personally devised every element of the recension remains uncertain because the surviving copy postdates him.

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers prepared the English Goetic text later published with Crowley in 1904. His work placed **Sallos (or Saleos)** and the seal into widely available print.

Mathers also worked with Shem angel seals transmitted through Christian-kabbalistic manuscript sources, including material associated with Blaise de Vigenère.

## Aleister Crowley

Crowley published the 1904 edition, added a preface and Enochian conjurations, and later incorporated Sallos into the elaborate correspondences of *777*.

His relevant contribution is therefore twofold:

- public dissemination of the Goetic entry and seal;
- assignment of Sallos to the day half of the first Cancer decan.

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No reliable Sallos-specific operation or distinctive doctrine by Frater Achad was found. His work influenced Thelemic Qabalah broadly, but listing him as a known worker with Sallos would exceed the evidence.

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy transmitted the entity as **Zaebos**; Le Breton created its most influential portrait.

De Plancy’s description returns to the Weyer rank of great Count rather than the Goetia’s Duke, while retaining the ducal crown.

## Lazare Républicain Lenain

Lenain’s 1823 significance lies with **Leuviah**, not Sallos:

- degrees 91–95;
- psalm;
- divine epithet;
- memory and intelligence;
- positive and contrary moral tendencies.

Later writers frequently copied these materials without distinguishing them from the earlier Shem-name construction.

## Papus

Papus popularized Lenain’s angelic material and integrated it into a general exposition of Kabbalah and Western occultism.

## Robert Ambelain

Ambelain further ritualized the seventy-two angels through seals, times, hierarchies, and operative instructions. His influence is strong in French-language practical Kabbalah and later online angel calendars.

## Haziel and Kabaleb

Kabaleb and Haziel turned the system into modern guardian-angel literature organized around birth dates, personal gifts, prayers, and annual contact periods.

This literature is a major source of current claims that Leuviah governs June 22–26, prosperity, emotional intelligence, cellular memory, or a twenty-minute daily interval.

## Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* is a modern operative synthesis. It employs:

- the seventy-two Goetic spirits;
- corresponding Shem angels;
- Aries-first five-degree sectors;
- planetary metals and colors;
- mirror or “dark mirror” scrying.

**[Modern ceremonial system]** Runyon did not discover the Sallos–Leuviah pairing; he operationalized older Rudd, Golden Dawn, and Goetic material in a twentieth-century system.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson produced *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*, a modern explanatory and practical edition.

**[Negative finding]** The checked evidence establishes DuQuette as an author on Goetic practice generally, but not a documented Sallos-specific working.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 *Lesser Key of Solomon* is the principal modern critical edition used here. It:

- bases the Goetia chiefly on Sloane 3825;
- reports manuscript variants;
- distinguishes the *Lemegeton* from Weyer;
- publishes Weyer and Scot in parallel online;
- clarifies ritual rules and source relationships.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* brought Harley 6483’s angelic counterseal procedure into modern print. Sallos and Leuviah appear on p. 121.

Their introduction also discusses competing starting points for the seventy-twofold zodiac:

- 0° Aries;
- 0° Leo, associated with some Golden Dawn ordering;
- a sidereal or Sirius-linked beginning.

This makes their work especially valuable for preventing the serial pairing from being mistaken for one uncontested zodiacal scheme.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium* and related comparative work place Goetic names beside other spirit catalogues and stress the *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* family.

**[Modern occult scholarship]** His comparison of Caleos/Saleos-type materials is useful for locating textual parallels, though identity and etymology still require manuscript evidence rather than similarity alone.

## Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Julien Véronèse, and Jean-Patrice Boudet

- Owen Davies supplies the larger social history of grimoires, print, ownership, and reception.
- Jan Veenstra’s work on the *Almandal* concerns angelic magic and intellectual aims; it does not establish a Sallos genealogy.
- Julien Véronèse’s research treats medieval ritual magic and Solomonic traditions more broadly.
- Jean-Patrice Boudet’s study of medieval and early-modern demonological “who’s who” catalogues is directly relevant to the *Livre des Esperitz* and the development of named-spirit lists.

**[Negative finding]** None of the checked scholarship supplies a pre-Weyer Sallos or an accepted ancient etymology.

# Grimoire ancestors

## The *Testament of Solomon*

The *Testament* is a composite late-antique pseudepigraphon in which Solomon questions demons, learns their offices, and discovers which angels thwart them.

**[Documented structural precedent]** Demon–angel opposition is already present conceptually.

**[Negative finding]** No Sallos, Saleos, Zaleos, Caleos, or clear equivalent appears. Leuviah is not assigned to a crocodile-riding love demon there.

## Munich handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century necromancer’s handbook published by Richard Kieckhefer as *Forbidden Rites* contains spirit names, conjurations, circles, and ritual procedures.

**[Documented generic ancestry]** It witnesses the ritual environment from which later spirit catalogues developed.

**[Negative finding]** No recognizable Sallos name or description was found.

## *Livre des Esperitz*

The surviving Cambridge manuscript contains a French catalogue of forty-six or forty-seven named spirits and is related to the broader genealogy behind Weyer and the *Goetia*.

**[Documented generic relationship]** It demonstrates that hierarchical spirit catalogues predate Weyer.

**[Negative finding]** Its published list contains no Sallos, Saleos, Zaleos, or crocodile-riding love spirit.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Trithemius’s *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508 lists a work titled *Liber officiorum spirituum*. Weyer explicitly says his catalogue came from a book of the offices of spirits.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Weyer, the *Book of Oberon*, and the *Goetia* preserve related branches of this catalogue tradition.

**[Negative finding]** The title’s early mention does not by itself attest Zaleos. No securely dated pre-1577 surviving *Officiorum* witness containing the name has been identified here.

## Abramelin

The Abramelin spirit lists overlap with some broader demonological nomenclature.

**[Negative finding]** No Sallos/Saleos/Zaleos appears in Peterson’s critical transcription.

The old claim that the book was written in 1458 is part of its internal narrative. Modern manuscript study places the surviving German textual tradition much later, around the turn of the seventeenth century.

## Overall ancestral conclusion

Sallos is not a demon that can presently be tracked from antiquity through an unbroken chain. The best reconstruction is narrower:

```text
Lost or partly surviving Office-of-Spirits traditions
             │
     ┌───────┴────────┐
Weyer: Zaleos      Folger: Caleos
   1577             c.1577–83
     │                  │
Scot: Saleos         possible fuller office
   1584                 │
     └──────────┬───────┘
        seventeenth-century Goetia
        Saleos/Sallos, spirit 19
                  │
       Rudd: Sallos + Leuviah
                  │
       Mathers-Crowley, 1904
                  │
 modern editions, art, ritual and fiction
```

The connecting lines indicate probable textual relationship, not proven direct copying in every case.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is Sallos an ancient deity?

**Finding:** No evidence supports this.

The name is not comparable to the transparent survival of Baal, Astarte, Amun, Belial, Asmodeus, or Phoenix in demonological lists. Every suggested ancient origin for Sallos is modern speculation without a documented transmission chain.

## 2. Is Caleos the same spirit?

**Evidence for:**

- near-identical name;
- knight/soldier;
- crocodile;
- crowns;
- beloved/love office;
- broadly contemporary catalogue tradition.

**Evidence against certainty:**

- Caleos has two crowns;
- treasure and false-answer offices;
- different serial position;
- no explicit cross-reference;
- overlapping manuscript dates prevent a simple “earlier original/later corruption” conclusion.

**Verdict:** Probable textual cognate; identity not proven.

## 3. Count or Duke?

- Weyer: Count.
- Scot: Earl.
- de Plancy/Zaebos: Count.
- *Goetia*: Duke.
- Crown in every main description: ducal.

**Verdict:** Both ranks are historically attested. “Duke” is correct for the *Ars Goetia*; “Count/Earl” is correct for Weyer, Scot, and de Plancy.

## 4. Does “peaceable” mean peacemaker?

No early source says Sallos reconciles enemies, ends wars, settles disputes, or grants social harmony.

**Verdict:** “Peaceable/peaceably” describes character or manifestation. Expanding this into an office of peacemaking is a modern interpretation.

## 5. Did Weyer give him the power of love?

No. Weyer’s entry ends after *pacificus, &c.* Scot does the same. The explicit love office is securely present in the seventeenth-century *Goetia*. The Folger Caleos parallel shows that making a person beloved may nevertheless have existed in the sixteenth-century source family.

**Verdict:** Attributing the surviving love sentence verbatim to Weyer is erroneous; attributing a possible lost love tradition behind Weyer is a defensible but unproved reconstruction.

## 6. Thirty legions

The number appears in the *Goetia* but not Weyer or Scot.

**Verdict:** Thirty is the standard Goetic figure, not an independently confirmed ancient or sixteenth-century datum.

## 7. Weyer 64 or 65?

- Proper Weyer enumeration: 65.
- Scot-relative sequence after omitted Pruflas: 64.
- Some modern tables silently use the latter.

**Verdict:** “Weyer 64” is a concordance artifact unless the table explicitly explains its numbering.

## 8. Why seventy-two?

The *Goetia*’s seventy-two spirits are often said to be deliberately matched from the beginning to the seventy-two Shem angels.

**[Disputed]** The number invites such a pairing, but ordinary *Goetia* manuscripts do not provide the angelic counter-list. Harley 6483 makes the one-to-one opposition explicit. It is safer to distinguish:

- formation of the seventy-two-spirit *Goetia*;
- later or specialized Rudd pairing with the seventy-two angels.

## 9. Is Leuviah’s 0°–5° Cancer sector intrinsic to Rudd?

No.

- Rudd securely pairs spirit 19 with angel 19.
- Lenain assigns angel 19 to degrees 91–95.
- Crowley assigns spirit 19 to the first day quinance of Cancer.
- Other occult schools begin the seventy-twofold series at Leo or a sidereal point.

**Verdict:** 0°–5° Cancer is standard in the Aries-first tropical reconstruction but not the only historical esoteric ordering.

## 10. Is “God who helps sinners” a translation?

No. It is a devotional or theological epithet.

**Verdict:** Treating it as a literal Hebrew etymology is misleading.

## 11. Leuviah’s dates

Sources variously give June 22–26, June 22–27, or nearby ranges because of:

- whether 0° Cancer begins on June 20, 21, or 22 in a given year;
- inclusive date counting;
- ordinal versus zero-based degree notation;
- tropical ingress varying by year and time zone.

**Verdict:** The precise astrological attribution is 0°–5° tropical Cancer; fixed civil dates are approximations.

## 12. Zaebos: separate demon or corrupt Sallos?

The description is virtually Weyer’s Zaleos translated into French:

- great Count;
- handsome soldier;
- crocodile;
- ducal crown;
- gentle character.

**Verdict:** Zaebos is almost certainly the same catalogue-being under a corrupted spelling. D&D and other modern works subsequently turned “Zaebos” into a functionally independent character.

## 13. Conflation with Zepar or Sitri

Zepar and Sitri also have sexual or love-related offices.

**Negative finding:** No early source says Sallos is an alias or form of either. Similar office does not establish identity.

## 14. Seal symbolism

No early source explains the seal’s parts.

**Verdict:** Venus, hearts, intercourse, crocodiles, double axes, or binding knots read into individual strokes are later meditations, not documented paleographic meanings.

---

# Open questions

1. **What stood behind Weyer’s “&c.”?**  
   A fuller *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* witness may once have supplied Zaleos’s office and perhaps legions. Caleos offers a tantalizing parallel, but no direct ancestor has been recovered.

2. **Are Caleos and Zaleos two readings of one name?**  
   The descriptive agreement is strong enough to make this probable, but the catalogue positions and offices differ.

3. **When did the Count become a Duke?**  
   The change predates the surviving principal *Goetia* witnesses. It may have been prompted by the ducal crown or inherited from an intermediate manuscript.

4. **Where did “thirty legions” originate?**  
   No checked sixteenth-century witness provides the figure.

5. **What is the archetype of the seal?**  
   The surviving *Goetia* manuscripts show a common design, but the source from which it entered the tradition is unidentified.

6. **Which edition of de Plancy first printed “Zaebos”?**  
   The 1863 entry is secure. The incomplete state of the 1818 online transcription prevents a definitive claim about the first edition without consulting its complete second volume and the intermediate editions.

7. **How much of Harley 6483 is Thomas Rudd’s?**  
   The copyist’s attribution is evidence, but the chronological gap prevents certainty about authorship of every table and procedure.

8. **Which zodiacal starting point did the earliest Rudd circle intend?**  
   Serial pairing is clear; the astrological zero point is not equally clear.

9. **Where did Lenain obtain Leuviah’s moral profile?**  
   Parts may derive from earlier angel tables, Kircherian Christian Kabbalah, manuscript seals, or Lenain’s synthesis. A source-by-source collation between Kircher, Vigenère, Arsenal manuscripts, and Lenain remains desirable.

10. **Does any undiscovered pre-1577 manuscript contain Zaleos?**  
    It is possible because Weyer used an older source, but possibility is not attestation. At present, 1577 remains the secure terminus.

---

# Conclusions

1. **The earliest secure Sallos is Weyer’s Zaleos in 1577.** No ancient-god identification or pre-Weyer occurrence has been demonstrated.

2. **Caleos is the strongest related witness.** Its crocodile-riding crowned knight and beloved-making office strongly connect it to the same textual family, but identity remains unproved.

3. **The developed Sallos belongs to the seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia*.** There he becomes spirit 19, a great and mighty Duke commanding thirty legions and producing reciprocal love.

4. **The rank changed, but the ducal crown did not.** Weyer’s Count and the Goetia’s Duke are both authentic readings from different stages.

5. **The seal is early modern, not ancient.** It first appears in the manuscript *Goetia* chain; Weyer omitted seals.

6. **Leuviah is genuinely Sallos’s angel in the Rudd recension.** The pairing is securely documented in Harley 6483 and printed by Skinner and Rankine.

7. **Leuviah’s 0°–5° Cancer sector is a later astrological framework.** Lenain’s degrees 91–95 and Crowley’s Cancer quinance support it in an Aries-first scheme, but Rudd’s serial pairing alone does not make that zodiacal zero point universal.

8. **The Two of Cups correspondence is Golden Dawn/Crowley material.** Sallos occupies the day half and Purson the night half of Venus in Cancer. The card’s “Lord of Love” title harmonizes remarkably with Sallos’s office but did not create its earliest form.

9. **Zaebos is almost certainly a corrupted Zaleos.** De Plancy and Le Breton made that spelling and portrait culturally independent, allowing it to enter Dungeons & Dragons, Japanese games, music, and other modern media.

10. **Much current lore is genuinely modern.** Fixed birthday guardianship, twenty-minute rulerships, emotional-memory language, romantic personality profiles, seal symbolism, and many visual details are later developments rather than contents of Weyer, Scot, or the manuscript *Goetia*.

# Agares: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **Documented text** — stated in a dated manuscript or printed source.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a relationship inferred by textual comparison or historical scholarship.
- **Tradition** — an attribution transmitted within a magical or religious school but not established as historical fact.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — a deliberately constructed symbolic assignment.
- **Disputed claim** — a reading for which sources conflict or transmission evidence is lacking.
- **Legend / modern invention** — pseudepigraphic history, later revelation, fiction, or recent occult elaboration.

Long passages are represented by short verbatim excerpts followed by complete clause-by-clause accounts; the linked public-domain scans give the entire original passages.

# The name and its origins

## 1. The earliest reconstructable chain

### *Livre des Esperitz*: Agarat

**Documented text.** The earliest surviving recognizable member of the name-family is **Agarat** in the French *Livre des Esperitz*, Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, fol. 180r:

> “Agarat eſt duc et appiert benignement en ſẽblãce de home vieulx…”

The complete entry says that Agarat:

- is a duke;
- appears benignly as an old man;
- teaches all languages;
- gives lordships and great dignities on earth;
- commands thirty-six legions.

The transcription is available in the [complete *Livre des Esperitz*](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier) and its [individual Agarat entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Agarat).

**Dating dispute.** Cataloguing has sometimes placed Trinity O.8.29 in the later fifteenth century, while Jean-Patrice Boudet’s paleographic assessment puts its hand no earlier than approximately the middle of the sixteenth century. Either date can make the surviving manuscript earlier than Weyer’s 1577 printing, but “fifteenth-century Agares” is not a settled fact. The cautious finding is:

- **Agarat is the earliest surviving, probably pre-1577 cognate.**
- **Agares itself is first securely attested in Weyer’s 1577 print.**

Boudet’s comparison appears in [“Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1171), *Médiévales* 44 (2003), pp. 117–140.

### Collateral *Office of Spirits* forms

**Documented text.** Related manuscripts give:

- **Agaros** — Coxe 25 and other *Officium spirituum* witnesses;
- **Agaret** — Trinity’s collateral list;
- **Acharos/Aharus** — a closely corresponding duke under Oriens in the Folger *Officium de spiritibus*, whose offices include teaching languages and recovering what has fled or been carried away.

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The matching rank, eastern subordination, language teaching, and fugitive-recovery office make these more likely textual relatives than independent beings. The consonants and endings drift normally for repeatedly copied lists of unfamiliar names. Peterson discusses this wider *Liber officiorum spirituum* family in his [Weyer introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); additional comparisons are available through the [*Liber Officiorum Spirituum* project](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/liber-officiorum/) and its [parallel comparison](https://grimorium.lat/cotejo-livre-liber/).

## 2. “Agreas” is not Weyer’s name

**Documented text.** Weyer prints **Agares**, as does Scot. The principal seventeenth-century *Goetia* manuscripts also read **Agares**.

**Disputed textual origin.** The 1904 edition’s sentence “called Agreas, or Agares” is the first secure occurrence of **Agreas** in the principal source chain. No evidence has been found that “Agreas” was Weyer’s reading. It is most economically explained as:

- a transposition of the middle letters;
- a misreading in Mathers’s copy;
- an editorial variant;
- or a printer’s error promoted to the status of an alias.

It became common because later writers copied the 1904 edition. Calling **Agreas** an ancient alternate name is therefore unsupported.

## 3. Etymology

### No demonstrated ancient deity

**Negative finding.** Unlike Bael/Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Amon/Amun, Asmoday/Ashmedai, Belial/Bĕliyyaʿal, or Phoenix/Greek *phoinix*, Agares has no demonstrated derivation from an ancient god, hero, ethnic group, or place.

The most defensible reconstruction is simply:

> **Agarat → Agaros/Agaret/Agares**, through manuscript variation in an *Office of Spirits* textual family.

### Proposed but unsubstantiated derivations

| Proposal | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Hebrew **Hagar** | **Disputed modern speculation.** Its associations with a foreigner, flight, or wandering can be made to resemble Agares’s fugitive office, but no grimoire gives this derivation and no transmission link has been demonstrated. |
| **Agrat bat Mahlat** | **Conflation.** Agrat is a female demon in Jewish tradition with a separate genealogy and mythology. Phonetic resemblance alone does not identify her with the male duke Agarat/Agares. |
| Latin **ager**, “field/land” | **Folk etymology.** It has been connected retrospectively to earthquakes or “spirits of the earth,” but the phonology and documentary chain do not demonstrate derivation. |
| Greek **ángaros**, a mounted courier | **Speculative.** The mounted figure and fugitives invite comparison, but no early source makes it. |
| Avestan **Aγraēraθa/Agraeratha** | **Unsubstantiated comparison.** This is an Iranian heroic name, but no linguistic or historical route connects it to the European spirit catalogue. |
| Argus, Agreus, Algol, or “Agarus” as an ancient god | **Modern syncretism.** No supporting textual genealogy has been located. |

**Conclusion:** “Unknown, probably a corrupt or variable grimoire name” is presently more responsible than any asserted ancient etymology.

# Number and order

## Weyer’s 69 and the *Goetia*’s 72

**Documented text.**

- Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* contains **69 substantive spirit entries**.
- Agares is **number 2**.
- The *Ars Goetia* contains **72** and retains Agares as **number 2**.

The arithmetic is:

> 69 Weyer spirits − Pruflas + Vassago + Seere + Dantalion + Andromalius = 72.

The changes are:

- **Omitted:** Weyer’s number 4, **Pruflas**, also called Bufas.
- **Added:** **Vassago**, *Goetia* number 3.
- **Added at the end:** **Seere** 70, **Dantalion** 71, **Andromalius** 72.

Peterson notes that Pruflas disappears probably because the entry was missing from the form of Scot used by the English compiler. The overall order was extensively rearranged after the first two entries. See Peterson’s [source comparison and notes](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The compiler appears to have wanted the symbolically important total of seventy-two. The completed demonic catalogue could then be read as a negative or counterposed analogue of the seventy-twofold divine name, although the earliest ordinary *Goetia* manuscripts do not themselves pair each spirit with an angel.

**Disputed counting.** Some secondary sources say Weyer has 68, generally because of numbering anomalies, aliases, or omitted-entry counting. The substantive 1577 catalogue contains 69 entries.

# The description across the sources

## 1. *Livre des Esperitz*: the simpler ancestor

**Documented text, probably before 1577.** Agarat is a benignly appearing old man and duke. He teaches every language, bestows earthly lordships and great dignities, and has thirty-six legions.

Absent are:

- the East;
- crocodile;
- hawk;
- fugitives;
- overthrow of dignities;
- earth spirits or earthquakes;
- the order of Virtues;
- a seal.

This shorter entry is important because it shows which features may be later expansions.

## 2. Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, 1577

Weyer added the *Pseudomonarchia* only to the **fifth edition** of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577, not to the original 1563 edition. He identifies his source as an *Officium spirituum*, *Liber officiorum spirituum*, or *Empto. Salomonis*, and says he intentionally omitted material so that readers could not readily use it as a working manual. See [Weyer’s introduction and parallel text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Latin

Weyer’s opening is:

> “Agares Dux primus sub potestate Orientis, apparet benevolus in senioris hominis forma, equitans in crocodilo, & in manu accipitrem portans.”

The remainder states that he:

- teaches every kind of language extremely well;
- makes fugitives return;
- makes those who remain in place flee;
- dismisses or casts down prelatures and dignities;
- makes the spirits of the earth dance;
- belongs to the order of Virtues;
- commands thirty-one legions.

Edition: Johann Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, fifth edition, Basel, 1577, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, pp. 913–914.

### Translation and lexical points

- **“Dux primus”** means the first duke in the catalogue, not “chief king.”
- **“Apparet benevolus”** means that he appears benign, well-disposed, or readily; it does not establish moral goodness.
- **“Senioris hominis”** is an old or elderly man.
- **“Accipiter”** can mean hawk, goshawk, or bird of prey.
- **“Prælaturas & dignitates dimittit”** most naturally means that he dismisses, removes, or causes prelatures and dignities to fall.
- **“Tripudiare facit spiritus terræ”** literally makes “spirits of the earth dance.” It does not literally say *terraemotus*, earthquake.

## 3. Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584

Scot’s Book XV prints an English translation of Weyer’s catalogue, probably made by Abraham Fleming.

The entry begins:

> “The first duke under the power of the east, is named Agares, he commeth up mildile in the likenes of a faire old man…”

Scot continues that Agares:

- rides a crocodile;
- carries a hawk on his fist;
- teaches all languages immediately;
- fetches back those who run away;
- makes those standing still run;
- overthrows supernatural and temporal dignities;
- makes earthquakes;
- is of the order of Virtues;
- commands thirty-one legions.

Edition: Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London, 1584, Book XV, chapter 2, p. 378. The text can be read beside Weyer at [Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Scot’s transformative readings

**Documented textual change.**

1. Weyer’s earth-spirits dancing become **earthquakes**.
2. Weyer’s general dignities and prelatures become **supernatural and temporal dignities**.
3. “Cometh up mildile” reflects *apparet benevolus* and should not be interpreted as “weakly.”
4. “Faire old man” means pleasant, comely, or benign-looking. It does not necessarily mean blond or pale-haired.

These changes pass directly into the English *Goetia*.

## 4. Sloane MS 2731

**Documented text.** Sloane 2731, dated 1686, calls him **Agares** and closely follows Scot:

- “a faire old man”;
- riding “upon a Crocodile very mildly”;
- carrying a goshawk;
- causing stationary people to run and fetching back runaways;
- teaching all languages;
- destroying supernatural and temporal dignities;
- causing earthquakes;
- formerly of the Virtues;
- thirty-one legions;
- seal to be worn as a lamen.

A digital facsimile is available at [Grimorium’s Sloane 2731 reader](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/).

## 5. Sloane MS 3825

Peterson uses Sloane 3825 as the base manuscript for his online transcription. Agares occurs at fol. 100v:

> “The second spirit is a Duke called Agares, he is under yᵉ power of yᵉ East…”

It describes:

- a fair old man;
- crocodile;
- mild appearance;
- goshawk;
- moving the stationary and returning runaways;
- immediate language instruction;
- destruction of supernatural and temporal dignities;
- earthquakes;
- former membership in the Virtues;
- thirty-one legions;
- a seal or character worn as a lamen.

See [Peterson’s complete manuscript transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#agares).

## 6. Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

**Documented text.** Harley 6483 retains the name **Agares**, rank, appearance, offices, thirty-one legions, and seal. Its innovation is not a radically different Agares description but the addition of a corresponding Shem angel and protective apparatus.

The manuscript is internally dated **1712–1713** and titled *Liber malorum spirituum, seu Goetia*. Peterson describes it as a late witness containing extensive “Dr. Rudd” additions in his [Lemegeton manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm).

**Attribution dispute.** Thomas Rudd died around 1656. Harley 6483 is therefore not an autograph. It is a later copy, associated with Peter Smart and material identified as “Sheets Dr. Rudd.” Skinner and Rankine argue for an underlying Rudd manuscript around 1655–1656; critics note that the surviving witness is several decades later and that “Rudd” provenance cannot be treated as automatic proof of authorship. The dating problem is summarized in [Celephaïs Press’s manuscript notes](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/06/addenda-et-scholia-2.html).

## 7. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The opening reads:

> “The Second Spirit is a Duke called Agreas, or Agares. He is under the Power of the East, and cometh up in the form…”

The complete semantic content is:

- an old fair man;
- riding a crocodile;
- carrying a goshawk;
- mild in appearance;
- makes stationary people run;
- brings back runaways;
- teaches all languages immediately;
- destroys both spiritual and temporal dignities;
- causes earthquakes;
- was of the order of Virtues;
- commands thirty-one legions;
- his seal is to be worn as a lamen.

Edition: S. L. MacGregor Mathers, translation, Aleister Crowley, editor, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, Boleskine, 1904, p. 28, with seal in figure 8. See the [first-edition Wikisource scan](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu), [100th Monkey Press facsimile](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/goetia_1904/goetia_1904.htm), [Sacred Texts transcription](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm), and [Hermetic Library scan](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/crowley_-_goetia.pdf).

### What 1904 did not add

The passage does **not** say that Agares:

- becomes a woman;
- has a second animal or human form;
- gives love, treasure, familiars, invisibility, scientific knowledge, or transmutation;
- answers every possible question;
- must be invoked on Friday;
- rules a unique hour or calendar period.

Such claims either belong to other spirits or are modern elaborations.

## 8. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* uses Sloane 3825 as its principal source while recording variants from Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and related manuscripts. Agares appears on pp. 7–8; the Weyer parallel is in the appendix, pp. 228–229.

Peterson retains **Agares** and does not treat “Agreas” as the primary manuscript name. His current online text, updated in February 2026, is [available here](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm); a [scan of the 2001 edition](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf) is also available.

## Appearance and second form

**Documented text.** Agares has one catalogued manifestation: an old man riding a crocodile with a hawk on his fist.

**Negative finding.** Unlike Gamigin, Marbas, Amon, Sitri, Botis, and several other spirits, the Agares entry contains no instruction that the magician demand a second or human form.

The general *Goetia* conjuration commands every spirit to appear in “a fair and humane shape” without deformity. That generic ritual clause could theoretically be applied to Agares, but it is not a distinctive second form and no source describes what it would look like. Claims of a blond youth, woman, crocodile-headed humanoid, or armored warrior are modern imagery.

# Rank, metal, planet, and time

## Duke: Venus and copper

**Documented esoteric correspondence.** The *Goetia*’s rank table states that seals of dukes are made in **Venus**, glossed as **copper**. Agares is therefore Venus/copper because he is a duke.

The same table assigns:

| Rank | Planetary metal |
|---|---|
| Kings | Sun / gold |
| Marquises | Moon / silver |
| Dukes | Venus / copper |
| Prelates or princes in the table | Jupiter / tin |
| Knights | Saturn / lead |
| Presidents | Mercury / mercury |
| Earls/counts | Venus/copper and Moon/silver |

This table appears on pp. 67–68 of the 1904 edition.

**Later deduction.** Friday, the planetary hour of Venus, green coloring, copper talismans, roses, and Venusian incense are logical later ceremonial extensions. They are not stated in Agares’s own entry.

## Time of conjuration

Weyer’s general instruction says:

> “Item Duces à prima usque ad meridiem: & observatur cœlum clarum.”

Thus dukes may be constrained from the first hour until noon, with clear weather observed. Scot translates the same rule, and the *Goetia* preserves it as “from Sunrise till Noonday in Clear Weather.” See [Weyer’s hours table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

**Documented text:** sunrise to noon, clear weather.

**Later practice:** Friday or a Venus hour.

**Modern astrological overlay:** an Aries decan date, discussed below.

These are independent systems and need not produce one single “correct” timestamp.

# The seal

## Documentary history

**Negative finding.** No Agares seal occurs in:

- the *Livre des Esperitz*;
- Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia*;
- Scot’s catalogue.

Peterson reports that he knows of no spirit sigils in the earlier *Office of Spirits* manuscripts. Sigils occur in other late-medieval and Renaissance spirit catalogues, but the familiar Agares character enters with the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* tradition. See [Peterson’s introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

## Shape

The standard Agares character consists of:

- an enclosing circular or oval line, sometimes imperfectly closed;
- a central upright and crosswise structure;
- inward and outward curved strokes;
- two broad, shallow lower curves or bands;
- small loops, bulbs, or rounded terminals depending on the copy.

Because these are hand-copied characters rather than geometrically defined diagrams, “exact” verbal descriptions can become misleading. The principal differences among Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825, Harley 6483, and the 1904 redrawing concern:

- whether the outer enclosure closes;
- the width-to-height ratio;
- whether terminal marks are dots, knobs, or loops;
- the curvature of the lower bands;
- whether internal lines touch the perimeter;
- stroke order and calligraphic regularization.

**Documented function.** The *Goetia* calls it Agares’s “seale or Charecter” and directs that it be worn as a **lamen**. No earlier source calls it an ancient Babylonian, Hebrew, or Solomonic autograph.

## Rudd’s paired layout

In Harley 6483, the demon’s seal is counterposed to an angelic seal. Agares’s character appears on the demonic side and Jeliel’s on the angelic side, accompanied by Hebrew and a Psalm text. This graphic pairing embodies Rudd’s theory that the angelic name restrains the corresponding spirit.

## The seventy-two triplets

**Documented Jewish tradition.** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional unpointed Hebrew count, of three verses of seventy-two letters each. The later construction writes:

1. verse 19 in normal order;
2. verse 20 in reverse order;
3. verse 21 in normal order.

Reading the columns produces seventy-two triads. The second is **Y-L-Y**, ילי. The relevant biblical passage and traditional commentaries are available at [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Kabbalah).

Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a describes the first-letter/last-letter/first-letter procedure. Joshua Trachtenberg summarizes it in [*Jewish Magic and Superstition*](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

### A necessary terminology caution

**Scholarly distinction.** *Shem ha-meforash* means an explicit, distinguished, or fully expressed divine name. In Jewish literature the phrase does not always mean these seventy-two triads; it frequently means the Tetragrammaton or another specially transmitted divine name. “The Shem HaMephorash” as an automatic synonym for the modern list of seventy-two named angels is a Western-esoteric simplification.

## From divine triplets to angel names

**Documented/traditional development.**

- The seventy-twofold name appears in medieval Jewish mystical and magical literature, including the *Sefer ha-Bahir*, Rashi’s commentary, and forms of *Sefer Raziel*.
- The triplets are initially components of a divine name, not necessarily seventy-two individualized “guardian angels.”
- Christian Cabalists appended **-el** or **-yah**, turning triads into pronounceable angelic names.
- Thus YLY becomes forms such as **Ieliel/Jeliel/Yeliel**.

*Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is a medieval compilation with multiple textual strata, not literally a book delivered to Adam by the angel Raziel. The revelation-to-Adam story is **legend**; the manuscript and printed textual history is medieval and early modern. The first printed Hebrew edition appeared in Amsterdam in 1701.

## Western transmission

### Reuchlin

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped transmit Christian-Cabalistic interpretations of Hebrew divine names into European learned magic.

### Blaise de Vigenère

De Vigenère’s *Traicté des chiffres* (1586) printed and discussed the seventy-two names. Skinner and Rankine argue that the Rudd tradition drew importantly on this form of the angelic list. A scan is available through [Gallica](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k73371g).

### Athanasius Kircher

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) systematized Christian-Cabalistic names, nations, divine attributes, and cosmological correspondences. It is part of the background to later French occult tables, though Kircher did not create the Exodus technique. See the [digitized *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*](https://www.esonet.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Oedipus_Aegyptiacus02.pdf).

### Rudd

Harley 6483 applies the seventy-two angel names positionally to the seventy-two Goetic spirits. Number 2 is therefore:

> **Jeliel against Agares.**

This is an ordinal pairing, not an etymological or mythological relationship. Agares and Jeliel do not share an older story.

### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* supplies the developed calendar and moral character of Jeliel:

> “2e Jeliel. Son attribut [Dieu secourable].”

Lenain assigns Jeliel:

- the “sixth through tenth degrees inclusive”;
- Turkey;
- the power to calm popular seditions;
- victory against unjust attackers;
- rule over kings and princes;
- obedience of subjects;
- animal generation;
- restoration of peace between spouses;
- conjugal fidelity;
- a favorable temperament associated with cheerfulness and sexuality;
- a contrary genius associated with division between spouses, celibacy, and bad morals.

The source is Lenain, *La science cabalistique*, Amiens, 1823, in the [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365) and [searchable text](https://archive.org/stream/BIUSante_ms05365/BIUSante_ms05365_djvu.txt).

### Papus

Papus—Gérard Encausse—republished and popularized Lenain’s angelology in his Cabalistic writings and contributed a preface to a twentieth-century Lenain edition. His role is transmission and occult synthesis, not invention of Jeliel.

### Golden Dawn

**Esoteric correspondence.** The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn placed two Shem angels in each zodiacal decan. In this scheme:

- Vehuiah: 0°–5° Aries;
- Jeliel: 5°–10° Aries;
- their shared decan: 0°–10° Aries;
- tarot card: Two of Wands;
- planetary dignity: Mars in Aries;
- Golden Dawn title: **Lord of Dominion**.

This arrangement fuses the Shem list with the Golden Dawn’s decanic minor-arcana system. It is not found in medieval Jewish Kabbalah.

### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) repackaged the Lenain-style angels for modern ritual use. “Helpful God,” 6th–10th degrees, conjugal peace, rulership, and a Psalm invocation belong to this modern French occult reception.

### Haziel

Haziel, the pen name of François Bernad-Termes, popularized the seventy-two “guardian angels” in late twentieth-century French books. The familiar birth-date system in which Jeliel rules approximately **26–30 March** belongs chiefly to this modern popular-angelology stream.

**Modern invention/development.** The idea that every person has three different Shem angels determined by birthday, emotions, and intellect is not a teaching of Exodus, Rashi, or the medieval *Sefer Raziel*.

## Jeliel’s Psalm

Harley 6483 and the later tradition use:

> “Tu autem Domine, ne elongaveris auxilium tuum a me; ad defensionem meam conspice.”

Lenain calls this Psalm **21:20**, using Vulgate numbering. Its sense is “But thou, Lord, remove not thy help from me; look to my defence.”

In Hebrew/KJV numbering, the corresponding verse is ordinarily **Psalm 22:19**:

> “But be not thou far from me, O LORD: O my strength, haste thee to help me.”

**Textual caution.** Modern tables sometimes print “Psalm 22:20.” That can result from versification differences, translation alignment, or simple off-by-one copying. The Latin incipit is a safer identifier than the bare number.

## How Rudd uses Jeliel

**Documented ritual design.** The angel is:

- written or invoked as a divinely authorized counterforce;
- shown with its own seal;
- joined to a Psalm;
- placed opposite the demonic seal;
- used to compel or “thwart” the corresponding spirit.

**Not documented:** Jeliel as Agares’s lover, higher self, moral twin, secret original identity, or “ruling angel” in ancient Jewish demonology.

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine published the principal critical treatment as *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*, Golden Hoard Press, 2007, 448 pp., ISBN 978-0-9547639-2-3. Bibliographic details are given on [Stephen Skinner’s site](https://www.sskinner.com/reviews.htm) and in the [WorldCat-linked source record](https://www.divinityatlas.com/sources/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-harley-ms-6482-and-related-manuscripts).

## Jeliel’s quinance and tarot pip

Jeliel’s five degrees are normally written **5°–10° Aries**. Lenain’s older wording “sixth degree through tenth inclusive” reflects ordinal degree-counting:

- first degree = 0°–1°;
- sixth degree begins at 5°;
- tenth degree ends at 10°.

Jeliel shares the first Aries decan with Vehuiah:

| Decan | Quinance 1 | Quinance 2 | Tarot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0°–10° Aries | Vehuiah, 0°–5° | Jeliel, 5°–10° | Two of Wands, Mars in Aries |

## Crowley’s different assignment

*Liber 777* assigns Goetic spirits to **decans**, not quinances. Agares appears among the spirits “of the Decans by Day” in the **succedent**, or second, decan of Aries:

| Period | Day spirit | Night spirit | Card |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10°–20° Aries | Agares | Halphas | Three of Wands |
| Decan ruler | Sun | — | Golden Dawn: Lord of Established Strength; Thoth: Virtue |

The primary table is in [Crowley’s *Liber 777*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777.pdf); Golden Dawn decan data may be compared in the [Golden Dawn manuscript tables](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/golden_dawn_manuscripts.html).

### Why the apparent contradiction?

Agares can simultaneously have:

- **Venus** from his rank as duke;
- **Mars in Aries** through Jeliel’s Two of Wands decan;
- **Sun in Aries** through Agares’s own *777* Three of Wands decan.

These belong to three different symbolic operations. They are not three competing discoveries about an astronomical entity.

## Day or night

**Crowleyan correspondence:** Agares is the **day** spirit of the second Aries decan. Halphas is its night spirit.

**Goetic ritual time:** dukes are constrained during daylight, sunrise to noon.

Those two daylight designations agree symbolically, but the former is a late decan table and the latter is inherited from Weyer.

## “Each pip carries two spirits”

In *777*, each of the thirty-six zodiacal minor-arcana cards carries:

- one Goetic spirit by day;
- one Goetic spirit by night.

In the Golden Dawn Shem overlay, the same ten-degree card also contains:

- one angel in its first quinance;
- one angel in its second quinance.

Thus a pip can be surrounded by two Goetic spirits and two Shem angels, but this is the result of overlaying independent tables.

## Enochian overlays

**Negative primary-source finding.** Agares has no Enochian name, aethyr, governor, or tablet square in:

- Weyer;
- Scot;
- the ordinary *Ars Goetia*;
- the Agares entry of Harley 6483;
- Crowley’s core catalogue.

**Later tradition.** Rudd manuscripts contain attempts to synthesize Dee-related angel magic with Solomonic material. Twentieth-century ritualists, including Poke Runyon and later Golden Dawn-derived authors, further combined:

- Goetic spirits;
- elemental quarters;
- Enochian Watchtowers;
- crystal or dark-mirror scrying;
- Shem angels.

Such assignments should be credited to the practitioner or book that gives them, not projected backward onto Agares.

# Iconography

## Before de Plancy

The *Livre des Esperitz* describes only an old man. Weyer supplies the decisive visual triad:

1. elderly man;
2. crocodile mount;
3. hawk on fist.

No sixteenth-century portrait is known in Weyer or Scot.

## Collin de Plancy

### First edition, 1818

**Negative finding.** Agares/Aguarès was not located in the searchable text of the 1818 first edition. The famous image certainly does not belong to it. Statements that “the 1818 *Dictionnaire Infernal* illustrated Agares” confuse the work’s first publication date with the illustrated sixth edition.

The [1818 text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/1re_%C3%A9dition,_1818/Tome_1/Texte_entier) can be searched directly.

### Verified full entry, 1825

The second edition describes Agares as a grand duke of the eastern region of hell and a benevolent-looking lord on a crocodile with a hawk. De Plancy says he:

- sends protected fugitives back into battle;
- routs the enemy;
- gives dignities and distributes prelatures;
- teaches all languages;
- makes earth-spirits dance;
- belongs to the Virtues;
- commands thirty-one legions.

**Documented transformation.** De Plancy militarizes the fugitive office and reverses Weyer’s destruction of dignities into their distribution. In that respect he partly returns to the *Livre des Esperitz*.

### Sixth edition, 1863

The printed entry uses **Aguarès**, with a cross-reference under **Agarès**. Its concise wording is:

> “Aguarès, grand-duc de la partie orientale des enfers.”

It presents him as a lord riding a crocodile, hawk on fist. See the [1863 text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Texte_entier) and [full scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e0/Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf).

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

The famous 1863 illustration shows:

- a bearded, long-haired elderly man;
- dignified, somewhat Renaissance-style clothing and cloak;
- a seated riding posture;
- a large crocodile beneath him;
- a hawk perched on his extended, likely gloved fist.

The picture freezes Weyer’s moving verbal description into the image now most strongly associated with Agares. The isolated plate is available as [*Aguarès.png*](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Fichier:Aguar%C3%A8s.png). The edition advertises its demon portraits as drawn by **M. L. Breton**.

## Crowley’s art

The 1904 *Goetia* includes:

- a regularized Agares sigil;
- Crowley’s own stylized rendering of the old man, crocodile, and bird.

Crowley’s line art is less naturalistic than Le Breton’s and belongs to the deliberately occult visual program of his edition.

## Modern art

**Modern invention.** Contemporary occult decks, online demon encyclopedias, games, and fan art variously depict Agares as:

- a youthful nobleman;
- a woman;
- a crocodile-headed humanoid;
- an armored warrior;
- a reptilian dragon;
- a human accompanied by, rather than riding, a crocodile;
- a blond or green-clad Venusian figure.

Those images are creative interpretations. None supplies evidence for a lost historical form. Likewise, modern “enns,” fixed color palettes, and standardized portrait cards are absent from the primary source chain unless an individual modern author explicitly identifies their origin.

# People and works

## Textual transmitters and editors

### Johann Weyer

Weyer published the first secure **Agares** entry in 1577. His purpose was polemical: to expose what he considered the fraud, blasphemy, and delusion of magical practitioners. Publication by a critic nevertheless preserved the catalogue.

### Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot reproduced the catalogue to attack witch-belief and conjuring claims. The translation, probably Fleming’s, created two of Agares’s best-known later properties:

- earthquakes;
- explicitly supernatural and temporal dignities.

### Anonymous English *Lemegeton* compilers

They turned Scot’s descriptive list into the first book of a practical ritual compilation, supplied seals, expanded the catalogue to seventy-two, and embedded Agares in a complete apparatus of circle, triangle, lamen, vessel, conjurations, threats, and license to depart.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

**Tradition:** Thomas Rudd developed or owned a recension pairing demons with Shem angels.

**Documented witness:** Peter Smart or a related early eighteenth-century copyist transmitted the Rudd-labelled papers in Harley manuscripts. The surviving Harley 6483 is 1712–1713, not Rudd’s autograph.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Mathers prepared the English text underlying Crowley’s 1904 edition. Crowley edited, published, illustrated, and supplemented it. Their wording spread **Agreas** and made the seals widely accessible.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 critical edition and evolving online transcription restored manuscript readings, identified Scot as the immediate English source, compared names and orders, and emphasized that the sigils do not occur in the older *Office of Spirits* witnesses.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* brought Harley 6483’s angelic countermeasures into print and made the **Agares–Jeliel** pairing widely known.

## Modern practitioners and interpreters

### Arthur Edward Waite

Waite summarized and criticized the grimoire tradition in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic* (1913). He is a transmitter and commentator, not an Agares innovator.

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding.** No significant, sourceable Agares-specific operation or doctrine by Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, was located. His importance lies in Thelemic and Qabalistic interpretation generally.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* and associated films promote dark-mirror scrying and an integrated angel–demon system. His own account explains that he initially softened coercive conjurations, later restored stronger forms, and treats the seventy-two angels as balancing the spirits. See [Runyon’s book description](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

No published Agares-specific operation comparable to Runyon’s documented Astaroth work was located.

### Lon Milo DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* popularized a psychological and initiatory reading of the spirits while reproducing modern correspondences. Their tables circulate Agares as a Venus/copper duke and as the day spirit of 10°–20° Aries. This is exposition of Crowley and Golden Dawn systems, not early evidence.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s catalogue studies, especially *Pandemonium*, compare names and offices across the *Livre des Esperitz*, Weyer, Scot, and related texts. His importance for Agares lies in returning attention to the *Office of Spirits* tradition rather than treating the 1904 edition as the beginning.

### Owen Davies

Davies’s *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* (Oxford University Press, 2009) supplies the wider social history of manuscript and printed magical books. It is not an Agares monograph. See [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/47570).

### Jean-Patrice Boudet

Boudet provides the most important scholarly comparison for **Agarat** and the Renaissance demon catalogues. His work supports textual ancestry but does not prove that every later Agares property existed in the Middle Ages.

### Jan Veenstra and Julien Véronèse

Their scholarship on learned magic, demonology, ritual texts, and manuscript culture helps contextualize the catalogue tradition. No Agares-specific etymology established by either scholar was located.

# Grimoire ancestors

## *Livre des Esperitz*

This is the closest identifiable textual ancestor because **Agarat** has the same rank, human appearance, and language office.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Trithemius’s *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508 records a title *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*. This proves that a book-family under that name existed by 1508. It does **not** prove that Trithemius’s copy contained Agares.

Weyer explicitly says his source belonged to this family.

## Munich Handbook, CLM 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook contains ritual methods and spirits related to the larger office tradition. It is an ancestor of milieu and technique, but no secure Agares/Agarat entry has been demonstrated. Richard Kieckhefer’s *Forbidden Rites* is the principal modern study.

## *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin manuscripts contain extensive lists of spirit names. Superficially similar forms such as Sagares or Ragaras have sometimes been equated with Agares, but similarity within corrupt demon lists is insufficient. No matching old-man/crocodile/language description establishes identity.

## *Testament of Solomon*

The Greek *Testament of Solomon*, a composite work conventionally dated within the first several centuries CE, provides a major literary model: Solomon uses a ring and divine authority to interrogate and bind named demons. **Agares is not among its demonstrable spirits.**

Thus the *Testament* is a conceptual ancestor, not an Agares attestation.

## Ashmedai and Solomonic legend

Jewish stories of Solomon and Ashmedai/Asmodeus contribute to the larger Solomonic demonological imagination. They offer no historical genealogy for Agares.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Does Agares appear before Weyer?

**Answer:** A closely matching **Agarat** does; the exact spelling **Agares** has not been securely located before 1577.

The manuscript date of the *Livre des Esperitz* remains disputed, but its Agarat entry is too close in rank and office to dismiss. Claims that Agares was wholly invented by Weyer are therefore too strong.

## 2. Was Agares an ancient god?

No evidence establishes this. The name behaves like a corrupted grimoire name, not a transparent demonization of a famous ancient deity.

## 3. Agarat or Agares?

**Scholarly reconstruction:** they are stages of the same textual figure.

**Strict documentary wording:** Agarat is the French witness’s name; Agares is Weyer’s. Absolute identity cannot be proved in the biographical sense because these are catalogue texts, but textual descent is highly probable.

## 4. Agares or Agreas?

Agares is overwhelmingly supported by the manuscript chain. Agreas is a 1904 anomaly that became an alias through repetition.

## 5. Thirty-six or thirty-one legions?

- *Livre des Esperitz*: 36.
- Weyer, Scot, *Goetia*, de Plancy: 31.

There is no external method for choosing a “real” number. Textually, thirty-one is the stable later reading.

## 6. Gives or destroys dignity?

This is a genuine reversal:

- Agarat gives lordships and dignities.
- Weyer removes prelatures and dignities.
- Scot and the *Goetia* destroy them.
- De Plancy again gives or distributes them.

Possible explanations include a scribal verb change, deliberate moral reversal, or de Plancy’s blending of sources.

## 7. Earthquakes or dancing earth spirits?

Weyer’s Latin says **spirits of the earth dance**. Scot says **earthquakes**. The *Goetia* follows Scot.

Calling the earthquake power a literal translation of Weyer is incorrect. It is an interpretive transformation, though the image of dancing terrestrial spirits could naturally suggest shaking ground.

## 8. Hawk, falcon, goshawk, or eagle?

Weyer’s *accipiter* is a hawk or goshawk. Scot uses hawk; the English *Goetia* prefers goshawk; French de Plancy uses *épervier*, sparrowhawk. “Eagle” is not the best-supported reading.

## 9. A fair old man

“Fair” historically means comely, pleasant, or favorable in appearance. It does not require white skin or blond hair. De Plancy’s dark engraved hair demonstrates that nineteenth-century readers did not necessarily understand it as blond.

## 10. Duke or grand duke?

The early texts say duke. De Plancy’s “grand-duc” is a nineteenth-century elevation of style. Agares is not a king despite an anomalous modern caption or HTML label occasionally reading “King Agares.”

## 11. Is the seal ancient?

No. The familiar character is first securely evidenced in seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscripts. Claims that Solomon himself drew it are pseudepigraphic legend.

## 12. Is Jeliel inherently Agares’s angel?

No pre-Rudd source pairs them. Their relationship follows from both occupying position 2 in two separate lists. Rudd’s ordinal system made the pairing traditional.

## 13. Is Jeliel a biblical angel?

The letters YLY come from a medievally attested extraction of Exodus. The personalized angel **Jeliel**, its meaning, office, seal, zodiac, Psalm, and guardian dates represent successive Jewish-magical, Christian-Cabalistic, and modern occult developments. Exodus does not name an angel Jeliel.

## 14. Psalm 21:20, 22:19, or 22:20?

The Latin passage is Vulgate Psalm 21:20. In the KJV/Hebrew numbering it corresponds most closely to Psalm 22:19. “22:20” in later tables should be checked against the actual wording.

## 15. Does Agares rule 5°–10° or 10°–20° Aries?

Both claims occur, but they refer to different constructions:

- **5°–10° Aries:** Jeliel’s angelic quinance, later transferred to Agares by treating Rudd’s pair as one zodiacal unit.
- **10°–20° Aries:** Agares’s own day-decan in Crowley’s *777*.

Calling either the sole original Goetic attribution is historically misleading.

## 16. Is Agares a Venusian or solar spirit?

Venus comes from rank; the Sun is the planetary ruler of the 10°–20° Aries decan. Mars rules Aries and the Two of Wands decan containing Jeliel. These are constructed correspondences, not mutually exclusive taxonomy.

## 17. Rudd authorship

Skinner and Rankine reconstruct an original Rudd recension. The surviving Harley copy is from 1712–1713 and cannot itself be Rudd’s autograph. “Attributed to Rudd” is more accurate than unqualified authorship.

## 18. Conflation with Agrat bat Mahlat

No historical evidence joins them. Agrat’s Jewish narrative, gender, nocturnal retinue, and genealogy are different. The resemblance of **Agrat** and **Agarat** is insufficient.

## 19. Modern moral character

Descriptions of Agares as honest, patient, feminist, malicious, anti-authoritarian, protective of immigrants, patron of polyglots, or destroyer of “honesty” are modern interpretations unless explicitly attributed to a modern practitioner. The primary texts supply offices, not a developed personality.

# Open questions

1. **Exact date of Trinity MS O.8.29.** A firmer codicological consensus would determine how confidently Agarat can be called fifteenth-century rather than mid-sixteenth-century.

2. **The lost exemplar behind Weyer.** Weyer’s *Empto. Salomonis* has not been identified as a surviving manuscript. Its precise relationship to the French and English *Office of Spirits* families remains reconstructive.

3. **Why “Agarat” became “Agares.”** The change may be ordinary scribal morphology, Latinization, or descent through an intermediate form such as Agaros, but no autograph sequence survives.

4. **The source of the crocodile and hawk.** They are absent from Agarat’s short French entry. Weyer may preserve material lost from that witness, or his exemplar may represent an expanded recension.

5. **The dignity reversal.** No surviving intermediate reading decisively explains the change from giving dignity to removing it.

6. **“Earth spirits dancing.”** It is unclear whether Weyer’s exemplar meant literal subordinate terrestrial spirits, a metaphor for seismic action, or a ritual spectacle.

7. **The first Agares seal.** Earlier unpublished or uncatalogued *Lemegeton* witnesses might refine its transmission, but none currently takes it back to Weyer.

8. **Rudd’s autograph.** Discovery of an indisputable mid-seventeenth-century exemplar would clarify which angelic apparatus genuinely belonged to Thomas Rudd.

9. **Origin of “Agreas.”** Mathers’s lost or unidentified working manuscript would be needed to decide whether it was his reading, his correction, or a printer’s transposition.

10. **Modern media claims.** Persona, *Helluva Boss*, and *Hellboy* attributions remain unsupported unless an official release or precisely cited issue supplies the name.

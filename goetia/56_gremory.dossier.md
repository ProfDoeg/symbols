# Gremory: Research Dossier

## Evidentiary labels

- **Documented text** — directly stated in a surviving manuscript or printed source.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — an identification or relationship inferred from textual comparison.
- **Tradition** — a claim transmitted within Jewish, Christian-cabalistic, or ceremonial-magical literature.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — an occult attribution added to the textual demon description.
- **Disputed** — a reading, attribution, date, or derivation for which evidence conflicts or is inadequate.
- **Legend** — traditional narrative presented as belief rather than history.
- **Modern invention** — a recent literary, artistic, gaming, practitioner, or popular-occult elaboration.

## The name and its origins

### Chronological chain of spellings

| Date/source | Reading | Position and notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fifteenth century, Munich CLM 849 | **Gaeneron** | No. 8 in a short spirit catalog; 27 legions |
| Florence, BML Plut. 89 Sup. 38, copied by 1494 | **Gomeris vel Caym** | No. 16; 21 legions; apparently conflated with a cave/serpent spirit |
| 1577–1583, Folger V.b.26, *Book of Oberon* material | **Gemyem** | No. 41; 42 legions |
| Sixteenth century, British Library Additional MS 36674 | **Gomory** | First in a short list, fol. 64r/65r depending foliation |
| 1577, Weyer | **Gomory** | No. 51 |
| 1584, Scot | **Gomory** | Unnumbered continuous catalog corresponding to Weyer |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane 3825 | **Gemory** | No. 56 |
| Sloane 2731 | **Gemory**, with **Gremory** in another copy or collation | No. 56 |
| Sloane 3648 | **Gremory** | No. 56 |
| Harley 6483 | **Gemory**, with editorial/collational forms **Gemori/Gemery** | No. 56 |
| Northwestern MS 65 | **Gemory** | No. 56 |
| Wellcome MS 3203 | **Gemory** | No. 37 because this branch reordered the spirits |
| 1818–1863, de Plancy | **Gomory** | French dictionary form |

| 1911, A. E. Waite | **Gomory** | Follows Weyer more closely in this name |
| 2001, Peterson | Headword **Gemory**, variants **Gremory** and Mathers-Crowley’s **Gremory/Gamori** | No. 56 |

Peterson’s comparative apparatus places Weyer’s **Gomory**, CLM 849’s **Gaeneron**, Florence’s **Gomeris vel Caym**, Folger’s **Gemyem**, and the *Goetia*’s **Gemory** on the same textual line. The reason is not merely similarity of name: their rank, female appearance, crown, camel, divination, treasure-finding and love offices coincide unusually closely. [Peterson’s Weyer comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [Boudet’s comparative table](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

### Earliest attestation

**Documented text:** The first printed occurrence is Weyer’s **Gomory** in 1577, not in the original 1563 edition of *De praestigiis daemonum*. Weyer first appended the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* to the fifth edition in 1577.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** Weyer is not the earliest surviving witness to the spirit’s dossier. The fifteenth-century Munich handbook’s **Gaeneron** is almost certainly cognate because its description reproduces the same exceptionally specific complex: powerful duke, beautiful woman, ducal crown, camel, true answers about time, hidden treasures, and the love of women or girls. Florence’s **Gomeris** provides a closer name before Weyer, in a manuscript section completed at Rome on 4 January 1494. [CLM 849 facsimile](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00037155), [Florence manuscript manifest](https://iiif.biblissima.fr/collections/manifest/adc2821dbba8914226b503c3b2e09144f75ea2ee).

Thus the defensible conclusion is:

- The first printed canonical spelling is **Gomory**, 1577.
- The earliest securely recognizable form of the same descriptive dossier is probably **Gaeneron**, fifteenth century.
- The earliest closer spelling is **Gomeris**, 1494.
- The precise descent from *Gaeneron* to *Gomeris/Gemyem/Gomory* remains unreconstructed.

### Is there an ancient god, hero or place behind the name?

**Finding:** No ancient deity, hero, ethnic name or place-name has been demonstrated behind Gremory.

This distinguishes Gremory from transparent cases such as:

- Bael/Baal, from Northwest Semitic *baʿl*, “lord”;
- Astaroth, ultimately related to Astarte;
- Asmoday, from Iranian/Jewish Ashmedai;
- Belial, from Hebrew *bĕliyyaʿal*;
- Phoenix, the Greek and Latin phoenix.

For Gremory, the textual spellings are unstable enough that reconstruction from the polished 1904 form is especially hazardous. Skinner and Rankine make the broader point that retroverting every Goetic name into Hebrew or Greek is usually futile where manuscript transmission is visibly corrupt; Gremory is a strong example. [Skinner and Rankine discussion](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

#### Proposed derivations

- **Gomorrah. — Disputed modern conjecture.** The resemblance between *Gomory* and *Gomorrah* has encouraged this guess, but no manuscript gloss connects the spirit to the biblical city, and the earlier forms *Gaeneron* and *Gemyem* weaken a simple derivation.

- **Greek γάμος, *gamos*, “marriage.” — Disputed modern conjecture.** This is based on the love office rather than a documented linguistic chain. No early witness glosses the name as marriage.

- **Hebrew גמור, *gamur/gamor*, “complete, finished.” — Disputed modern conjecture.** The consonantal resemblance is real only for some late spellings. There is no Hebrew etymology in Weyer, the manuscripts, Rudd or Mathers.

- **French/Latin “grimoire.” — Modern folk etymology.** Similarity to *grimoire* has produced forms such as “Grimorio” in popular lists, but these are not part of the source chain.

- **Gimon in the *Sworn Book of Honorius*. — Disputed conjecture.** *Liber Iuratus* contains an angelic name resembling *Gimon*, but resemblance alone does not establish identity, transmission or demotion into Gremory. [Peterson’s *Sworn Book* edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/juratus.htm).

- **Astarte, Ishara, al-ʿUzzā, Allāt, a moon goddess or another love goddess. — Modern invention or speculative syncretism.** No medieval or early-modern Gremory source makes such an identification. The “former moon goddess” language associated with later Japanese game art is a creative explanation for that design, not recovered ancient religion.

- **An aspect, wife or relative of Paimon. — Modern occult speculation.** The historical lists treat Paimon and Gremory as separate spirits. No old witness gives them a familial relationship.

The etymology is therefore an open problem. The safest reconstruction is that a damaged or repeatedly recopied name accompanied a much more stable block of descriptive prose.

## The description across the sources

### 1. The Munich handbook: Gaeneron

CLM 849, conventionally called the *Munich Manual of Demonic Magic*, is a fifteenth-century Latin magical handbook edited by Richard Kieckhefer. Its short catalog includes **Gaeneron**, number 8.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** In substance Gaeneron is Gremory’s earliest known ancestor. The spirit is a strong and powerful duke who appears as a very beautiful woman, is crowned, rides a camel, answers about present, past and future and hidden treasures, produces the love of women—especially girls—and commands **27 legions**. The one-legion difference from Weyer is minor beside the extensive verbal and functional identity. [Kieckhefer, *Forbidden Rites*](https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=pXvRNI1S55EC), [Peterson’s CLM comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

The manuscript contains no recognizable later Goetic seal for Gaeneron.

### 2. Florence Plut. 89 Sup. 38: Gomeris or Caym

The late-fifteenth-century Florence witness reads:

> *Gomeris vel Caym dux fortis, et potens apparet in similitudinem pulcherime mulieris, duchali coronatus corona, equitat super camellum. Dat ad verum plena responsa de presentibus, praeteritis, et futuris: et occultis cavernis, in quibus apparent serpentes est princeps, et custos. Dat optime amorem mulierum: et habet sub se .21. legiones.*

Translation:

> Gomeris, or Caym, a strong and powerful duke, appears in the likeness of a most beautiful woman, crowned with a ducal crown, riding upon a camel. She gives full and true answers about things present, past and future; and she is prince and guardian of hidden caves in which serpents appear. She excellently gives the love of women, and has twenty-one legions under her.

**Documented differences:**

- **21**, not 26 or 27, legions.
- Hidden **caves and serpents** replace the simpler treasure statement.
- The rubric says **Gomeris or Caym**.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** The first half is unmistakably the Gremory dossier. The serpent/cave material resembles offices elsewhere attached to Valac or related treasure-serpent spirits. The reading probably represents contamination or conflation during copying—not proof that Gremory and Caim were originally one spirit. [Boudet’s transcriptional comparison](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171), [Florence manuscript](https://iiif.biblissima.fr/collections/manifest/adc2821dbba8914226b503c3b2e09144f75ea2ee).

### 3. The *Book of Oberon* branch: Gemyem

Folger manuscript V.b.26, copied approximately 1577–1583, gives:

> **41 Gemyem** a strong duke, appeareth like a ffayere woman & crowned with the crowne of a dutches, & rideth upon a camell, & giveth trwe knowledge of thinges past present & to come, & of hidd treasures, in the which places doe appeare halfe woomen, & he is a prince, & a companion of the love of woemen, & especially of Maydens, & under him ar **42 legions**.

**Documented differences:**

- Name **Gemyem**.
- **42 legions**.
- “Half women” appear at treasure locations.
- Gemyem is called both duke and prince, and “a companion of the love of women.”

This branch preserves material omitted or regularized by Weyer. It demonstrates that the spirit’s dossier circulated independently of Weyer’s printed text. [Folger transcription](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf).

### 4. Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* (1577)

Weyer’s entry is number **51**. In the original 1577 printing it occupies pp. 927–928.

#### Latin, verbatim

> **Gomory Dux fortis & potens: apparet ut mulier pulcherrima: ac ducali cingitur corona, in camelo equitans. Bene & verè respondet de præteritis, præsentibus, futuris, & occultis thesauris ubi lateant. Conciliat amorem mulierum, & maximè puellarum. Imperat legionibus vigintisex.**

Gloss:

> Gomory, a strong and powerful duke, appears as a most beautiful woman, encircled or adorned with a ducal crown, riding on a camel. The spirit answers well and truly about past, present and future things and where hidden treasures lie; procures the love of women, especially girls; and commands twenty-six legions.

[Peterson’s Latin text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [scan of the 1577 printing](https://booksofmagick.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Pseudomonarchia-Daemonum-1577.pdf).

#### What Weyer actually states

- **Rank:** *dux fortis et potens*, strong and powerful duke.
- **Appearance:** *mulier pulcherrima*, a most beautiful woman.
- **Regalia:** *ducali cingitur corona*.
- **Mount:** camel.
- **Divination:** past, present and future.
- **Treasure:** says where hidden treasures lie.
- **Love:** women, especially *puellae*, girls or maidens.
- **Legions:** 26.

#### The crown controversy

Latin *cingitur* means “is girded, encircled, surrounded or adorned.” It does not itself say “about her middle.” Scot’s English made the construction anatomical, and the *Goetia* intensified it into a crown “tied about her waist.”

Skinner and Rankine argue that the intended sense was a ducal crown encircling the head. De Plancy eventually depicts and describes it there. That correction is plausible but not absolutely compelled: Weyer omits the body part, while the English branch explicitly supplies the middle.

### 5. Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584)

Scot printed the catalog in the skeptical and expositional context of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, not as an endorsement of demonic practice. Peterson considers the translation possibly the work of Abraham Fleming, but that attribution remains a hypothesis.

#### Scot’s English, verbatim

> **Gomory a strong and a mightie duke, he appeareth like a faire woman, with a duchesse crownet about hir midle, riding on a camell, he answereth well and truelie of things present, past, and to come, and of treasure hid, and where it lieth: he procureth the love of women, especiallie of maids, and hath six and twentie legions.**

The passage is on p. 224 of the 1584 edition. [Scot’s text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm), [1584 edition record and scan link](https://play.google.com/store/books/details?id=2fNZAAAAcAAJ).

Scot changes or clarifies:

- *mulier pulcherrima* → “a faire woman”;
- *cingitur corona* → crown “about hir midle”;
- *puellarum* → “maids”;
- *imperat* → “hath” twenty-six legions.

The masculine “he” beside a female manifestation is normal catalog grammar in these texts. It is not evidence for a second male form.

### 6. The *Ars Goetia* manuscripts

Peterson’s edition primarily follows Sloane 3825, a seventeenth-century manuscript he considers the most accurate of the principal witnesses when compared with identifiable sources. Its Gremory entry reads:

> **The 56 spirit is called Gemory, he is a strong and powerfull duke appearing in The forme of a Beautifull woman, wth a Dutches Crownett Tyed about her middle, ridding on a great Camell, his office is to tell of all Things past present & to come, and of Treasure hidden and wt it layeth in, & procureth ye love of women, both young & old, he governeth 26 Legions of spirits, his seal is Thus made, & worne as a Lamin before ye Exorcist in [the] Time of working.**

[Peterson’s *Goetia* transcription](https://ftp.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Important developments:

- **Gomory → Gemory.**
- Weyer’s number 51 becomes **56**.
- The camel becomes a **great camel**.
- Treasure information expands from “where it lies” to “what it lies in.”
- “Especially maidens” becomes women “both young and old.”
- The text adds a **seal** and instructs that it be worn as a **lamen**.
- It still provides no alternative manifestation.

#### Manuscript witnesses

- **Sloane 3825:** *Gemory*; seventeenth century.
- **Sloane 2731:** *Gemory*, with *Gremory* reported in another copy/collation. Its written date is cataloged ambiguously as “1572 (1687?),” but textual dependence on post-1665 material makes a literal 1572 date for its present form untenable.
- **Sloane 3648:** *Gremory*; approximately 1665 or later.
- **Harley 6483:** *Gemory*, with minor forms reported in editorial collation; dated 1712–1713.
- **Northwestern MS 65:** *Gemory*, completed in 1713.
- **Wellcome 3203:** *Gemory*, but numbered 37 because this nineteenth-century copy inherited or imposed a disordered sequence.

Peterson’s manuscript analysis and comparative name table provide the most useful consolidated apparatus. [Manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm), [name comparison PDF](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

### 7. Mathers-Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia* (1904)

Page 30 of the original edition reads:

> **(56.) GREMORY, or GAMORI.—The Fifty-sixth Spirit is Gremory, or Gamori. He is a Duke Strong and Powerful, and appeareth in the Form of a Beautiful Woman, with a Duchess’s Crown tied about her waist, and riding on a Great Camel. His Office is to tell of all Things Past, Present, and to Come; and of Treasures Hid, and what they lie in; and to procure the Love of Women both Young and Old. He governeth 26 Legions of Spirits, and his Seal is this, etc.**

[Mathers-Crowley text](https://hermetic.com/norton/classics/goetia/gremory), [1904 edition index](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index%3AThe_Book_of_the_Goetia_%28Mathers-Crowley%2C_1904%29.djvu), [Sacred Texts edition](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/index.htm).

**Documented printing history:** Mathers prepared the transcription; Crowley supplied the prefatory apparatus and supervised publication. Peterson cautions that many alternate spellings in the 1904 book have no identifiable manuscript support. **Gamori**, however, became highly influential through this edition whether or not its manuscript basis can now be recovered.

### 8. Peterson’s 2001 edition

Joseph H. Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* (Weiser, 2001), prints the entry on p. 40 and documents variants in the notes. Its importance lies less in rewriting the Gremory description than in restoring manuscript comparison:

- primary reading **Gemory**;
- Sloane 3648 **Gremory**;
- Sloane 2731 **Gemory/Gremory**;
- Mathers-Crowley **Gremory or Gamori**;
- Weyer and Scot **Gomory**.

[Book record](https://books.google.ca/books?id=wfIrE3QtmFEC), [Peterson’s online critical text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm).

## Number and order: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

Weyer prints **69** catalog entries even though his Belial notice speaks of 72 kings or spirits. The *Goetia* has 72.

The difference is produced by two operations:

1. **Pruflas/Bufas**, number 4 in Weyer, disappeared from Scot’s English catalog and is absent from the *Goetia*.
2. The *Goetia* added four spirits not in Weyer:

   - no. 3 **Vassago**;
   - no. 70 **Seere**;
   - no. 71 **Dantalion**;
   - no. 72 **Andromalius**.

Thus: 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

The rest were extensively reordered. Gremory moves from Weyer’s **51** to the *Goetia*’s **56**. Peterson observes that the *Goetia* seems particularly dependent upon an English Scot tradition—probably the expanded 1665 edition—and suggests that physical leaves in an ancestor may have been scrambled. That explanation is a textual hypothesis, not a statement made in the grimoire. [Peterson’s complete comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Claims that Gremory is Weyer’s number **50** are secondary counting errors. Weyer’s catalog as conventionally enumerated and Peterson’s comparative table both put Gomory at **51**.

## Rank, planet, metal and timing

### Duke: Venus and copper

The planetary metal table printed with the Mathers-Crowley *Goetia* assigns:

| Rank | Planetary attribution | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince/Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon jointly | Copper and silver |

Accordingly, **Gremory’s ducal seal is to be made in copper**, and Venus is the planet implied by rank. This is an **esoteric correspondence attached to the Goetic hierarchy**, not something stated in Gremory’s entry or in the fifteenth-century witnesses. [Mathers-Crowley “Observations”](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon/read/observations).

### Time

Weyer’s Latin rule for dukes is:

> *Duces à prima usque ad meridiem: & observatur cœlum clarum.*

That is: dukes may be bound from the first hour until noon, with a clear sky observed. Scot and the *Goetia* render this as sunrise or the first hour to noon in clear weather.

“First hour” should be read in an early-modern planetary-hour/daylight context, not automatically as 1:00 a.m.

The *Goetia*’s general observations also favor the Moon at ages 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 14 days. These instructions are part of the ritual framework, not unique astrological properties of Gremory.

## The seal

### Source history

- **No seal is printed by Weyer.**
- **No seal appears in Scot’s catalog.**
- The seal belongs to the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton/Goetia* manuscript tradition.
- The manuscripts instruct the exorcist to wear it as a **lamen** during the operation.
- Mathers-Crowley’s 1904 engraving standardized its modern appearance.
- Harley 6483’s Rudd arrangement associates it with the corresponding angelic name and Psalm on the reverse.

### Description

In its familiar Mathers-Crowley form, the seal is a narrow, vertical figure:

1. At top is a three-pointed or three-tined crown/trident formation.
2. A central line descends into a pair of angular, interwoven side structures.
3. These resemble opposing hooked arms or a compact M/W-shaped lattice.
4. Beneath them is a short horizontal bar crossing the center.
5. The lowest portion divides into two outward-facing recurved hooks or loops joined through the central stem.

[1904 seal reproduction](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/056-Seal-of-Gremory/), [transparent Commons reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3A56-Gremory_seal.png).

### Variants

The manuscript forms share the same recognizable skeleton but differ in:

- whether the upper central tines touch or remain open;
- the angle and length of the lateral arms;
- whether crossings are drawn as true intersections or touching corners;
- the width of the lower bar;
- how tightly the bottom hooks close;
- enclosure in, or separation from, a surrounding circle.

**Open question:** No source explains what the strokes mean. Interpretations as letters, camel imagery, a crown, a planetary monogram or a coded name are modern speculation unless a specific derivation can be demonstrated.

## The angel: Poyel

### Identification

**Documented in Rudd’s scheme:** Spirit 56 is counterbalanced by angel 56, normally **Poyel**, **Poiel**, or **Poi-El**. Skinner and Rankine note that the angel attached to the Gremory entry “should be Poiel,” correcting a corrupt reading such as **Pollal** in the manuscript/editorial line.

The underlying three-letter unit is:

> **פוי**

Conventionally transliterated *P-V-Y*, *P-W-Y* or *Poy*. Christian Cabalists add **אל**, *El*, forming **פויאל**, Poyel/Poiel.

### Meaning

**Documented in Lenain:** *Dieu qui soutient l’univers* — “God who supports the universe.”

**Qualification:** This is a devotional divine attribute, not a secure literal Hebrew etymology of the three letters פוי. Modern sources often turn the attribute into the angel’s lexical “translation,” but the historical texts do not demonstrate that derivation.

### Derivation from Exodus 14:19–21

The 72 units are derived from three consecutive Hebrew verses, each traditionally counted as 72 consonantal letters:

1. Exodus 14:19 is written in normal order.
2. Exodus 14:20 is written in reverse order.
3. Exodus 14:21 is written in normal order.
4. Reading the resulting vertical columns produces 72 three-letter units.

The fifty-sixth column yields פוי. The arrangement is often described as boustrophedon. Medieval Jewish authorities including Rashi and Rabbeinu Bahya attest the extraction method; it was not invented by Rudd or the Golden Dawn. [Exodus and kabbalistic commentaries](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Kabbalah), [Rabbeinu Bahya’s explanation](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.21?lang2=en&with=Commentary), [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://jewishencyclopediadev.sefaria.org/articles/6727-god-names-of).

### Development of the angelic tradition

#### Jewish sources

**Tradition:** The 72-fold divine name belongs to medieval Jewish mystical and magical transmission. *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*, a compilation containing layers no earlier than approximately the eleventh century, prints or transmits the 72-fold name, although its vocalization varies between manuscripts. The legendary frame says the angel Raziel gave a book of divine secrets to Adam; this is not a historical authorship claim. [Jewish Encyclopedia on *Sefer Raziel*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/12606-raziel-book-of), [Trachtenberg on the 72-name text](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

It is important not to back-project Rudd’s demon pairings into Jewish Kabbalah. Jewish sources transmit the divine name and angelic units; they do not pair Poyel with a demon called Gremory.

#### Reuchlin and Agrippa

Johann Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped convert the triliteral divine units into individually named angels through the addition of *-El* or *-Yah*. Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia*, Book III, chapter 25, reproduced the 72 angels and explained their governance of 72 celestial quinaries. His table includes **Poiel**. [Modern scholarly study of Reuchlin’s 72 names](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1zjg3h9), [Agrippa text](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm).

#### Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) systematized the names, national divine names and Latin attributes in Christian-cabalistic form. The attribute associated with Poyel is transmitted as *Deus fulciens omnia*, “God supporting all things,” the ancestor of Lenain’s French wording. [Kircher scan](https://www.esonet.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Oedipus_Aegyptiacus02.pdf).

#### Harley 6483 and “Rudd”

Harley 6483, dated 1712–1713 and copied in the Peter Smart circle, introduces or preserves the decisive Goetic innovation:

- place the 72 angels against the 72 demons in numerical order;
- write or engrave the demon’s seal on one side of the lamen;
- put the constraining angelic name and corresponding Psalm on the other;
- invoke divine and angelic authority so the magician does not confront the spirit unsupported.

For number 56, that yields **Poyel against Gremory**.

This pairing is not in Weyer, Scot, the early *Goetia* witnesses or Jewish Shem tradition. It is the distinctive Rudd/Harley synthesis. [Harley 6483 description](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm), [Harley catalog description](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/mss/mss479.htm).

**Disputed provenance:** Skinner and Rankine argue that Harley 6483 is a fair copy of material associated with the mathematician and magician Thomas Rudd, perhaps originating around 1655–1656. What is secure is the manuscript’s 1712–1713 date and its association with Peter Smart. Whether every “Dr. Rudd” sheet genuinely descends from Thomas Rudd is debated; critics note apparent post-Rudd material and the possibility of later attribution. [Critical discussion of the Rudd attribution](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/06/addenda-et-scholia-2.html).

#### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives the fullest early nineteenth-century Poyel notice:

> **56e. Poiel. Son attribut (Dieu qui soutient l’univers). Il correspond au saint nom Illi, d’après la langue des peuples d’Aden. Son rayon commence depuis le 276e degré de la sphère jusqu’au 280e, inclusivement, correspondant à la 28e décade et au génie nommé Themeso. Il préside les jours suivants: 14 mai, 25 juillet, 5 octobre, 16 décembre, 26 février. L’invocation se fait depuis 6 heures 20 minutes du soir jusqu’à 6 heures 40; Il faut prononcer le 15e verset du psaume 144. (Allevat Dominus omnes qui corruunt: et erigit omnes elisos.) Il sert pour obtenir ce que l’on demande. Ce génie domine la renommée, la fortune et la philosophie. La personne qui est née sous cette influence sera estimée de tout le monde par sa modestie, sa modération et son humeur agréable; elle ne devra sa fortune qu’à ses talents et à sa conduite. Le génie contraire domine l’ambition et l’orgueil: il influe sur tous ceux qui s’érigent en maîtres et veulent s’élever au-dessus des autres.**

In English:

- attribute: God who supports the universe;
- sacred national name: *Illi*, attributed to Aden;
- zodiacal ray: degrees 276–280;
- five presidency days: 14 May, 25 July, 5 October, 16 December, 26 February;
- invocation window: 6:20–6:40 p.m.;
- Psalm: “The Lord raises all who fall and lifts all who are bowed down”;
- offices: obtaining what is asked, fame, fortune and philosophy;
- favorable character: modest, moderate and agreeable;
- contrary genius: ambition, pride and self-exaltation.

[Lenain at the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365), [searchable PDF edition](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf).

##### Psalm numbering

Lenain calls it the fifteenth verse of Psalm 144 in the Vulgate tradition:

> *Allevat Dominus omnes qui corruunt: et erigit omnes elisos.*

Modern Hebrew/Protestant numbering normally identifies this as **Psalm 145:14**:

> “The LORD upholdeth all that fall, and raiseth up all those that be bowed down.”

Rudd-derived sources sometimes cite **Psalm 144:15**. The divergence is principally one of Psalm and verse numbering, compounded by manuscript copying—not a different prayer.

#### Papus, Ambelain and Haziel

- **Papus/Gérard Encausse:** *La Cabbale* (1903) treats Lenain’s 72-angel notices as an important practical expression of Judeo-Christian Cabala and helped repopularize the material.

- **Robert Ambelain:** *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reorganized the angels into a more elaborate modern system of choirs, rulers, colors, planets and ritual applications. These additions are twentieth-century occult synthesis, not material from Rudd or Lenain.

- **Haziel/François Bernard-Termès:** Late-twentieth-century books popularized the 72 as personal “guardian angels,” with continuous birth-date ranges, petitions and psychological portraits. Poyel is commonly made guardian of people born **27–31 December**. This is a modern angel-calendar interpretation; Lenain instead gives a zodiacal ray plus five discontinuous presidency days. [Example of the modern 27–31 December attribution](https://www.ephemeride-des-anges.fr/monange/mon-ange.php?ange=56).

### Crowley’s *777*

Crowley’s table CLXIII/CLXIV places:

> **56 ♀ גמור Gamori — Beautiful woman, with duchess’ crown tied to her waist, riding great camel.**

The table is headed Goetic demons “by Night (Succedent).” Through the Golden Dawn decan arrangement, key scale 22 corresponds to Libra, and the succedent or second decan is **10°–20° Libra**. [*777 Revised* table](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf).

The associated tarot card is the **Three of Swords**, traditionally Saturn in Libra. The two planets describe different layers:

- **Venus** = Gremory’s ducal rank.
- **Saturn in Libra** = the astrological decan and tarot card.

They are not competing answers to the same question.

### Each pip carries two Goetic spirits

The Golden Dawn/Crowley arrangement places one spirit from numbers 1–36 and one from 37–72 in every decan. For Libra 10°–20°:

- **Purson, no. 20:** daytime spirit.
- **Gremory, no. 56:** nighttime spirit.

Both therefore share the Three of Swords.

### Poyel’s quinance versus Gremory’s decan

These are separate constructions:

- Poyel’s number 56 multiplied through a 72 × 5° zodiac gives the Capricorn sector.
- Gremory’s place in a paired 36-decan demon table gives the second Libra decan.

Later manuals sometimes mix them, producing statements that Gremory “rules Capricorn” or Poyel “rules Libra.” Historically, each belongs to a different table.

### Golden Dawn variations in the Shem system

Golden Dawn-derived material is not completely uniform. Some tables preserve the straightforward Aries-to-Pisces 72-quinance sequence, which puts Poyel in Capricorn. A circulated Golden Dawn Shem lecture begins its operative sequence from Leo and consequently places the fifty-sixth angel differently, sometimes in Taurus. This is a rotated ritual arrangement, not evidence that Lenain printed Taurus. [Golden Dawn Shem text](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Schemhamphorash.html), [alternative Hermetic Golden Dawn table](https://hermeticgoldendawn.org/shem/).

### Enochian overlays

Harley 6483’s preface says that some of the spirits are “in Enoch’s Tables,” and related Rudd/Smart material attempts to interpret John Dee’s tables through Goetic names. Later authors expanded this syncretism.

**Qualification:** There is no securely documented Gremory attribution in Dee’s own spirit diaries comparable to the seventeenth-century *Goetia* entry. Calling Gremory an “Enochian spirit” is therefore a later overlay.

### Ritual timing versus correspondence

The old operational instruction is the ducal time—sunrise to noon, clear weather—with the prescribed Moon ages. The Libra decan, Venus day/hour, Capricorn angelic quinance and modern December birth dates are later correspondence systems. The *Goetia* does not explicitly say Gremory can be summoned only during 10°–20° Libra or on Poyel’s five dates.

## Iconography

### Manuscript seals

The early descriptive catalogs—Munich, Florence, Weyer and Scot—do not supply the familiar seal. Florence contains other demon sigils, but Peterson notes that they do not match the later *Goetia* characters.

The seventeenth-century manuscripts make the seal operational: it is to be worn before the exorcist. Harley 6483 transforms it into a paired talisman backed by Poyel’s name and Psalm.

### Collin de Plancy

Jacques Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818. **Gomory is present in the dictionary tradition from its early editions**, but the famous demon portraits were not part of the 1818 first edition.

The 1825 edition gives:

> **GOMORY,—Fort et puissant duc des enfers; il apparaît sous la forme d’une belle femme, une couronne ducale sur la tête et monté sur un chameau; il répond très-bien sur le présent, le passé et l’avenir; il fait découvrir les trésors cachés; il allume l’amour dans le cœur des femmes et des jeunes filles. Il commande à vingt-six légions.**

Translation:

> Gomory, strong and powerful duke of hell, appears in the form of a beautiful woman, with a ducal crown on her head and mounted on a camel. He answers very well concerning present, past and future, reveals hidden treasures, and kindles love in the hearts of women and girls. He commands twenty-six legions.

[1825 volume scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2e/Dictionnaire_Infernal%2C_Ou_Bibliotheque_Universelle_%28Second_Edition%2C_Vol.III%29_%28IA_dli.granth.35112%29.pdf).

The sixth edition, 1863, p. 307, shortens the notice:

> **Gomory, puissant duc des enfers; il apparaît sous la forme d’une femme; il a une couronne ducale sur la tête, et il est monté sur un chameau. Il répond sur le présent, le passé et l’avenir; il fait découvrir les trésors cachés; il commande à vingt-six légions.**

[1863 text and page](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Gomory).

Changes include:

- the crown is explicitly on the **head**, not the waist;
- the 1863 notice omits the love office;
- the spirit remains Gomory and commands 26 legions.

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

The celebrated image first belongs to the illustrated 1863 edition. Louis Le Breton shows an aristocratic woman seated on a camel, wearing a high ducal or horned-looking headdress and long courtly robes. It literalizes de Plancy’s corrected head-crown reading rather than Scot’s waist-crown. The figure is dignified and human rather than monstrous; the camel supplies the unmistakable identifying attribute.

[Gallica scan, p. 307](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5754923d/f317.image), [high-resolution Commons image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AIll_dict_infernal_p0323-307_gomory_demon.jpg).

The picture is often mislabeled online as an “1818 illustration.” That is incorrect: 1818 is the dictionary’s first-edition date; the Le Breton engraving is from **1863**.

### Modern art

Modern Gremory art tends to descend from one of three visual strands:

1. **The seal**, usually copied from Mathers-Crowley.
2. **Le Breton’s camel-riding noblewoman.**
3. **Fantasy redesigns**, often turning the rider and camel into a composite body, a horned goddess, a red-haired aristocrat, or a moon-associated female demon.

Red hair, lunar sovereignty, seductive nudity, specific eye colors, armor, weapons and familial links to other demons are modern artistic choices unless separately sourced.

## People and works

### Ancestors of the catalog

#### *Testament of Solomon*

The Greek *Testament of Solomon* is a composite late-antique pseudepigraphon in which Solomon controls named demons by a divinely empowered ring and forces them to work on the Temple.

**Legend:** Later Goetic books inherit the broad Solomonic claim that Solomon bound and employed spirits.

**Negative finding:** The *Testament* does not contain Gremory, Gomory, Gemory or the woman-on-camel dossier. Statements that Solomon personally compelled “Gremory” to build the Temple retroject the later 72-spirit catalog into an older legend. [Peterson’s *Testament of Solomon*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm).

#### *Livre des Esperitz*

This medieval French demon catalog is an important ancestor or collateral relative of Renaissance “who’s who” lists. Boudet and Julien Véronèse’s work shows extensive connections among it, the Munich catalog, Florence material and Weyer.

**Negative finding:** No secure Gremory-form entry has been identified in the surviving *Livre des Esperitz*. Claims that one of its differently named spirits “is Gremory” require argument from offices, not merely list proximity. [Boudet’s study](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

#### Munich CLM 849

Richard Kieckhefer’s edition of the fifteenth-century handbook supplied the crucial recognition of **Gaeneron** as the earliest close textual ancestor. This is the strongest pre-Weyer evidence.

#### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer names his source as:

> *Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis, de principibus & regibus dæmoniorum.*

Trithemius’s *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508 cataloged a *Liber Officiorum* or book “of the offices of spirits.” The Folger material preserves a substantial related branch.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** A lost or still-unidentified version of this text is the immediate ancestor of Weyer’s catalog. Gremory’s stable office-block probably traveled in this family.

#### *Book of Abramelin*

**Negative finding:** No secure Gremory/Gomory entry with the characteristic camel and treasure-love offices appears in the standard Abramelin spirit lists. Similar-looking names in Abramelin should not be identified without textual evidence.

### Weyer and Scot

- **Johann Weyer:** Printed the first canonical *Gomory* notice in 1577. He believed in spirits but used demonological learning to challenge witch persecutions and claims about human witches. His catalog was framed critically, and he admitted omitting material so it could not readily function as a manual.

- **Reginald Scot:** Translated or commissioned the English catalog in 1584 as part of an argument exposing witchcraft accusations, conjuring claims and fraud. His wording supplied the “crown about her middle” that passed into the *Goetia*.

### Rudd and Peter Smart

The historical Thomas Rudd was a mathematician, engineer and occult compiler. The manuscripts attributed to him combine Goetic, angelic, cabalistic and Dee-related material. Peter Smart appears in the copying history of Harley 6482–6483.

**Disputed:** “Rudd’s Goetia” is a useful name for the recension, but the precise share attributable to Thomas Rudd rather than Smart or later compilers is not settled.

### Mathers and Crowley

- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers:** Transcribed and edited the *Goetia* material.
- **Aleister Crowley:** Published the work in 1904, supplied the prefatory interpretation and later placed the spirits in *777*’s decanic correspondence tables.
- Their wording established **Gremory or Gamori** as the dominant twentieth-century spelling.

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding:** No reliable source was located for a Gremory-specific operation, doctrine or published analysis by Frater Achad. His name is sometimes added to broad Thelemic bibliographies because of his association with Crowley and cabalistic work, but that is not evidence of direct engagement with this spirit.

### A. E. Waite

Waite’s *Book of Ceremonial Magic* used **Gomory** and retold the catalog from a critical Christian-esoteric perspective. It adds no demonstrably independent Gremory tradition and contains errors or silent “corrections” elsewhere in its Goetic material.

### Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* and his “Master Mandala” system helped popularize twentieth-century ritual Goetia, dark-mirror evocation and angel-demon integration.

**Qualification:** Runyon’s framework is an influential modern practical overlay. No well-documented Gremory-specific operation comparable to a primary manuscript entry was located.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt and David P. Wilson’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* tabulates Gremory as:

- duke;
- Venus/copper;
- 10°–20° Libra by night;
- approximately 3–12 October;
- Three of Swords.

This is a clear modern presentation of the Crowley/Golden Dawn correspondence chain, not an early description. [Illustrated *Goetia* scan](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf).

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition and continuously revised Esoteric Archives transcriptions transformed the study of Gremory by exposing:

- the manuscript variants;
- the dependency on Scot;
- Weyer’s correct number;
- the pre-Weyer cognates;
- the difference between manuscript readings and Mathers’s alternate names.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

*The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard Press, 2007), especially p. 158, prints the Harley recension, identifies Poiel as the correct angel, comments on *cingitur* and reconstructs how the angelic reverse of the lamen constrains the demon. The book is indispensable for the Rudd system, although its Thomas Rudd provenance argument remains debated. [Publisher information](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556).

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s work situates the Goetic catalog in the wider necromantic, spirit-office and Mediterranean grimoire traditions. No uniquely Gremory-specific historical claim of his was located that supersedes the manuscript work of Boudet, Kieckhefer or Peterson. His importance here is contextual: he resisted treating the 1904 *Goetia* as an isolated, timeless book.

### Modern scholarship

- **Richard Kieckhefer:** CLM 849 and Gaeneron.
- **Jean-Patrice Boudet:** comparative medieval and Renaissance demon catalogs.
- **Julien Véronèse:** medieval Solomonic and *Ars Notoria* textual traditions.
- **Owen Davies:** social and publishing history of grimoires.
- **Ian Christie-Miller:** Christian-cabalistic history of the 72 angel names.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Is Weyer the first attestation?

- **First printed attestation:** yes, 1577.
- **First surviving recognizable dossier:** probably not. Gaeneron and Gomeris precede him.
- **Exact name “Gremory”:** later than *Gomory/Gemory*.

The wording “Gremory has no pre-Weyer source” is therefore too strong. “The polished spelling *Gremory* is not pre-Weyer” is defensible.

### 2. Is Gremory Weyer’s number 50 or 51?

The primary sequence and Peterson’s edition say **51**. Number 50 is Volac. Claims of 50 result from secondary miscounting.

### 3. Crown at the waist or on the head?

- Weyer: *ducali cingitur corona*, anatomically unspecified.
- Scot: crown “about hir midle.”
- *Goetia*: “tied about her middle/waist.”
- de Plancy: crown on the head.
- Skinner/Rankine: waist is probably a mistranslation.

The historical fact is not that one image is unquestionably original, but that the English transmission produced an anomalous waist-crown that the French and modern critical traditions sometimes correct.

### 4. How many legions?

| Witness | Legions |
|---|---:|
| CLM 849, Gaeneron | 27 |
| Florence Gomeris/Caym | 21 |
| Folger Gemyem | 42 |
| Weyer Gomory | 26 |
| Scot Gomory | 26 |
| *Goetia* Gemory/Gremory | 26 |
| de Plancy Gomory | 26 |

Twenty-six is stable only from Weyer onward.

### 5. Is Gremory male, female or genderless?

The texts use masculine grammatical pronouns because the catalog classifies the entity as a duke/spirit, but consistently say the appearance is a woman. None offers a biological or metaphysical theory of demonic gender.

- “Male demon disguised as a woman” is an interpretation.
- “Goddess who was demonized” is modern speculation.
- Feminine pronouns are a reasonable modern editorial choice based on the manifestation but are not uniform in the sources.

### 6. Gomeris “or Caym”

The Florence reading genuinely conflates the names in its rubric. It does not prove Gremory and Caim were historically one spirit. Their later catalog entries are separate, and the Florence description itself looks composite.

### 7. Was Gremory an ancient goddess?

No evidence has been found. Moon-goddess, Astarte, Allāt, Ishara and similar identifications begin in modern esoteric or entertainment settings and lack a textual bridge.

### 8. Is Poyel Gremory’s eternal opposite?

No. Poyel is attached to Gremory by the **Rudd ordinal pairing**. Earlier Jewish angelology knows Poyel without Gremory; Weyer and the earlier *Goetia* know Gremory without Poyel.

### 9. Does Poyel literally mean “God who supports the universe”?

That is the traditional **attribute** transmitted through Christian Cabala and Lenain. It is not a demonstrated dictionary translation of פוי.

### 10. Capricorn or Libra?

Both are historically attested in different overlays:

- Poyel: Capricorn quinance in Lenain’s angelic scheme.
- Gremory: Libra decan by night in Crowley’s *777*.

Combining them into one natal rulership is a modern synthesis.

### 11. Is Harley 6483 really Thomas Rudd’s?

The manuscript and 1712–1713 date are secure. Its descent from a mid-seventeenth-century Thomas Rudd original is plausible but disputed. “The Rudd recension” is safer than claiming every word is autograph Rudd.

### 12. Was the seal given by Solomon?

That is the grimoire’s legendary frame. Historically, the recognizable seal is absent from the early catalog and first survives in seventeenth-century manuscripts.

### 13. Modern “enns”

The formula often printed as:

> *An tasa shi Gremory on ca*

is a modern demonolatry **enn**, popularized in late-twentieth- and twenty-first-century practitioner literature. It is absent from Munich, Florence, Weyer, Scot, the *Goetia*, Rudd and Mathers-Crowley.

### 14. Modern expanded powers

Claims that Gremory teaches dance, controls weather, governs necromancy or rune-casting, initiates through Tiphereth, rules particular herbs or qliphothic tunnels, or has fixed relationships with named demons are modern practitioner systems or personal revelations. They are valid evidence for contemporary religious practice, but not for the historical Ars Goetia description.

## Open questions

1. What was the original name behind **Gaeneron–Gomeris–Gemyem–Gomory**?
2. Is Gaeneron a heavily corrupted spelling, or did the office-block migrate from one spirit name to another?
3. Which lost *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* exemplar stood behind Weyer?
4. Why did the Florence copy join Gomeris to Caym and add serpent caves?
5. What caused the Folger branch’s “half women” at hidden treasures?
6. Why do the legion totals range from 21 to 42?
7. Did Scot’s translator misunderstand *cingitur*, or did his manuscript contain an expanded reading now lost?
8. When and where was the Gremory seal first drawn?
9. Does the seal encode letters, a name, rank or planetary form, or is it an autonomous magical character?
10. What exact manuscript, if any, supplied Mathers’s spelling **Gamori**?
11. How much of Harley 6483’s material genuinely descends from Thomas Rudd?
12. Did the *Goetia*’s altered order result from loose or displaced leaves, deliberate reorganization, or more than one intermediate catalog?
13. Can the exact first occurrence of the spelling **Gremory**, rather than *Gemory/Gomory*, be dated more narrowly?
14. Is there any independent premodern etymological gloss? None is currently known.

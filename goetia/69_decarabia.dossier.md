# Decarabia: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **[Documented text]** — expressly present in a cited manuscript, printed grimoire, or other primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a conclusion proposed from manuscript comparison, textual genealogy, philology, or historical context.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim transmitted by occult authors but not independently demonstrated as historical fact.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a deliberately constructed magical, astrological, tarot, or angelic attribution.
- **[Disputed]** — a reading or identification for which the evidence permits competing conclusions.
- **[Legend/modern invention]** — later lore, popular occultism, fiction, games, or individual revelation not found in the early source chain.

Dates refer to the publication or manuscript witness carrying a reading, not necessarily to the supposed lifetime of an attributed author.

Neither zodiacal arrangement is stated in Weyer, Scot, or the seventeenth-century Decarabia entry itself.

The consolidated manuscript spelling table is reproduced by Joseph Peterson in his [comparative catalogue of Goetic spirits](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

### 2. Earliest exact attestation

**[Documented text]** The earliest verified exact spelling is Weyer’s **Decarabia** in the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* (1577), where the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* appeared as an appendix.

**[Important qualification]** Decarabia is therefore not safely described as having no pre-Weyer history. A closely matching spirit called **Cambea** occurs in the Florentine manuscript BML Plut. 89 sup. 38, dated January 1494. A further form, **Cambra**, occurs in the related Folger manuscript tradition. Peterson aligns Cambea–Cambra–Carabia–Decarabia because the names occupy corresponding positions in related lists and have substantially the same offices.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The most economical genealogy is:

> Cambea → Cambra/Carabia → Decarabia

The exact scribal process remains unexplained. “De-” may have entered through erroneous word division, anticipation, or contamination from a neighboring name. No surviving intermediate witness proves the precise transformation.

Brian Johnson’s edition of the Florentine manuscript reports Cambea as a great count appearing like a faun, knowing herbs and stones, commanding birds, and ruling twenty legions. That makes the identification stronger than a merely phonetic resemblance, while the changed appearance and legion count warn against treating the entries as identical in every detail.

### 3. Is “the star” original?

Weyer’s Latin has:

> *venit similis ☆*

Scot likewise prints a star-like typographical sign after “he commeth like a”.

**[Disputed textual reading]** Brinsley Nicholson and Joseph Peterson argue that the star may not originally have meant “star.” It can be read as an editorial marker for an illegible or missing word in Weyer’s exemplar. Weyer’s source manuscript may have contained an unreadable creature-name or damaged figure, for which the printer substituted an asterisk or star.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Cambea’s reported faun-like appearance supplies a possible answer to the lacuna, but it does not prove what Weyer saw.

**[Documented development]** The later Goetia no longer treats the mark as a doubtful reading. It expands it into a definite apparition: **“a Star in a Pentacle.”** This is a classic case in which a printer’s sign may have become demonological iconography through literal interpretation.

### 4. Etymology

No defensible ancient deity, hero, Hebrew demon, Greek divine name, or Latin technical term has been identified behind **Decarabia**.

#### Carabia as an ancient place

An ancient Macedonian settlement is recorded under forms such as Carabia/Karabia. The resemblance is real, but:

- no manuscript connects the spirit with Macedonia;
- Decarabia’s offices are unrelated to the town;
- no transmission route from the place-name into the *Offices of Spirits* literature has been demonstrated.

It should therefore be labeled **[disputed coincidence]**, not an etymology.

#### Abraxas, Abracadabra, and similar theories

Some modern occult websites connect Decarabia with **Abrasax/Abraxas**, *abracadabra*, or a supposedly ancient stellar deity.

**[Modern speculative etymology]** No premodern Decarabia source makes these connections. The phonetic resemblance is loose, and there is no textual bridge from Abrasax to Cambea, Cambra, Carabia, or Decarabia. Even the ancient etymology of *Abrasax* is disputed; its independent antiquity does not validate its attachment to Decarabia.

#### Latin *carabus*

A few modern pages derive the name from Latin *carabus*, “beetle” or “crab.”

**[Unsupported modern conjecture]** This does not explain the manuscript sequence or Decarabia’s avian, herbal, and lapidary offices. The pages circulating the proposal generally provide no linguistic argument or source witness.

#### French *charabia*

French *charabia*, “gibberish” or incomprehensible jargon, is occasionally compared with Carabia.

**[Anachronistic comparison]** The French word is much later in its documented popular use and has its own disputed history. There is no evidence that it generated the Renaissance spirit-name.

### Conclusion on the name

The best-supported answer is modest:

> **Decarabia is probably a scribally expanded or corrupted descendant of Cambea/Cambra within the late-medieval *Offices of Spirits* tradition. Its deeper etymology is unknown. No ancient god or hero has been securely identified behind it.**

## The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* (1577)

Weyer numbers the spirit **52**. The entry appears on p. 927 of the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum*.

> **Decarabia vel Carabia, magnus Rex & Comes: venit similis ☆. Vires herbarum & lapidum pretiosorum novit: efficit ut aves coram exorcista volent, & velut familiares ac domesticæ morentur, bibant & cantillent suo more. Parent huic triginta legiones.**

A close translation is:

> Decarabia or Carabia, a great King and Count: he comes resembling ☆. He knows the powers of herbs and precious stones. He causes birds to fly before the exorcist and to remain like familiar and domestic birds, drinking and singing in their manner. Thirty legions obey him.

Source: Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, 5th ed. (1577), p. 927; [Peterson’s Latin transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

What is documented here:

- **Names:** Decarabia and Carabia.
- **Rank:** great King and Count/Earl.
- **Appearance:** unresolved star/asterisk sign.
- **Knowledge:** powers of herbs and precious stones.
- **Birds:** he causes actual “birds,” *aves*, to fly before the exorcist and remain like familiar/domestic birds.
- **Legions:** thirty.

No human second form, seal, pentacle, individual astrological sign, or Shem angel appears in Weyer’s entry.

### Weyer’s purpose

Weyer was a physician and critic of witch prosecutions. His publication of the catalogue did not amount to an uncomplicated endorsement of ritual demonology. He says that he withheld or altered material so that the operations could not readily be used. His title *Pseudomonarchia*—“False Monarchy”—also marks the catalogue as a critical or polemical exposure.

The catalogue nevertheless preserves material from a manuscript family he called the *Liber officiorum spirituum* or *Book of the Offices of Spirits*. Trithemius had listed a book under a similar title in his 1508 catalogue of magical works.

## 2. Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584)

Scot prints Decarabia in Book XV, chapter 2, p. 389 of the 1584 edition. In Brinsley Nicholson’s 1886 reprint it occurs on p. 324.

> **Decarabia or Carabia, he commeth like a ☆ and knoweth the force of herbes and pretious stones, and maketh all birds flie before the exorcist, and to tarrie with him, as though they were tame, and that they shall drinke and sing, as their maner is, and hath thirtie legions.**

Source: Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584), XV.2; [electronic text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/60766/60766-h/60766-h.htm).

Differences from Weyer:

- Scot retains **Decarabia or Carabia**.
- He retains the ambiguous star sign.
- *Vires* becomes “the force” of herbs and stones.
- Weyer’s “familiar and domestic” birds become birds that behave “as though they were tame.”
- Scot omits Weyer’s explicit **great King and Count**.
- Thirty legions remain.

The translation is traditionally associated with Abraham Fleming, though the exact responsibility for all of Scot’s demonological translation material is not beyond dispute.

## 3. The seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia*

Peterson’s online text primarily follows Sloane 3825, collated with Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses. In Sloane 3825 the Decarabia entry is at fol. 110r.

> **The 69 spirit is called Decarabia, he appeareth in ye forme of a starre in ye Pentacle at first, but afterwards at ye command of ye Exorcist, he putteth on ye Image of a man, his office is to discover ye vertues of hearbes and precious stones; and to make ye similitude of all Birds to fly before ye Exorcist, & to Tarry wth him, singing and Drinking as Naturall Birds doe, he governeth 30 Legions of spirits, being himselfe a great Marquiz, his seal is Thus to be made, and worne as a Lamin before ye Exorcist &.**

Source: Sloane 3825, fol. 110r, transcribed in Peterson’s [*Lemegeton*, “Goetia”](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Additions and changes relative to Weyer/Scot:

1. **Number changes from 52 to 69.**
2. The doubtful star sign becomes **a star in the Pentacle**.
3. The exorcist can command a second form: **the image of a man**.
4. The office is to **discover** the virtues of herbs and stones, not simply to know them.
5. The birds become **“the similitude of all Birds”**, creating ambiguity over whether these are natural birds, spirits shaped like birds, or magical images.
6. Rank changes to **Great Marquis**.
7. A drawn seal is supplied and must be worn as a **lamen**.
8. Thirty legions remain.

### “Similitude of birds” versus actual birds

Weyer and Scot describe birds directly. The Goetia describes “the similitude of all Birds” and then says they sing and drink like natural birds.

Possible readings include:

- **[Documented literal reading]** apparitions or spirit-forms resembling birds;
- **[Traditional reading]** magically summoned birds acting as familiars;
- **[Modern embellishment]** Decarabia personally transforms into every species of bird.

Only the first two have textual support. The third is not stated.

## 4. Harley 6483 and the Rudd recension

Harley 6483, dated 1712–1713, contains the text conventionally associated with Thomas Rudd. It gives the spirit as **Decarabia**, but the lettering beside its seal reads **Becarabia**. Skinner and Rankine note that the accompanying Hebrew transliteration points to Decarabia, making *Becarabia* a copying or engraving error rather than an independent demon.

The Rudd recension follows the later “virtues of birds” reading discussed below and places the appropriate Shem angel on the protective lamen.

**[Historical caution]** The surviving manuscript is later than Rudd’s lifetime. “Rudd’s Goetia” therefore means the recension attributed to or transmitted under Rudd’s name, not an autograph whose authorship is beyond question.

## 5. Mathers–Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* (1904)

The 1904 printed version reads, on p. 44 of the scanned edition:

> **(69.) DECARABIA.—The Sixty-ninth Spirit is Decarabia. He appeareth in the Form of a Star in a Pentacle, at first; but after, at the command of the Exorcist, he putteth on the image of a Man. His Office is to discover the Virtues of Birds and Precious Stones, and to make the Similitude of all kinds of Birds to fly before the Exorcist, singing and drinking as natural Birds do. He governeth 30 Legions of Spirits, being himself a Great Marquis. And this is his Seal, which is to be worn, etc.**

Source: Mathers and Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* (1904), p. 44; [Sacred Texts facsimile/transcription](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

### The herbs-to-birds corruption

The crucial phrase is now:

> “the Virtues of **Birds** and Precious Stones”

Weyer, Scot, and Sloane 3825 all have **herbs**.

**[Textual finding]** “Birds” is almost certainly a secondary corruption or editorial slip, encouraged by the repeated appearance of birds in the next clause. It creates the awkward claim that Decarabia reveals the “virtues of birds” and then separately produces bird-similitudes. Some modern books reproduce the error as if it were a separate ancient office.

Peterson’s 2001 critical text restores **herbs** from the manuscripts and source tradition.

## 7. Rank, planet, metal, and hour

The Goetia’s general rubric assigns metals as follows:

- chief Kings — Sun/gold;
- Marquises — Moon/silver;
- Dukes — Venus/copper;
- Princes or “Prelates” — Jupiter/tin;
- Knights — Saturn/lead;
- Presidents — Mercury;
- Earls — Venus/copper and Moon/silver “equally alike.”

The manuscript scheme notably omits Mars and iron. Later correspondence tables regularized the ranks by assigning Earls to Mars and iron.

### Decarabia’s rank attribution

As a **Great Marquis**, Decarabia’s ordinary Goetic metal is:

> **Silver; planet Moon.**

The earlier Weyer ranks produce an anachronistic complication:

- as King: Sun/gold;
- as Count: the manuscript’s mixed Venus/Moon, copper/silver scheme—or Mars/iron in later regularized tables.

Those are consequences of applying a later Goetia rubric backward to Weyer. Weyer himself does not attach a metal or planet to Decarabia.

### Hours

The Goetia’s rubric says Marquises may be bound:

> from three in the afternoon until nine at night, and from nine at night until sunrise.

This is the timing relevant to Decarabia by rank. The instructions also recommend observing the Moon, favorable lunar days, clear weather, and the spirit’s quarter.

The manuscript lists the second, fourth, sixth, eighth, tenth, twelfth, and fourteenth days of the Moon as especially suitable. These are general Goetic rules, not details in Decarabia’s individual entry.

## 8. The seal

### What the sources establish

- **Weyer and Scot:** no seal.
- **Known earlier *Offices of Spirits* witnesses:** no securely attributable Decarabia seal.
- **Seventeenth-century Goetia manuscripts:** a lineal seal appears.
- **Harley 6483:** the seal is labeled **Becarabia**, despite the entry and Hebrew pointing to Decarabia.
- **1904 Mathers–Crowley:** a standardized printed form popularized the modern seal.

### Verbal description

The conventional seal is an open, unbounded line figure rather than a circular pentacle:

- its framework consists of intersecting diagonal strokes forming an irregular X or four-armed stellar skeleton;
- several outer terminals end in small loops or roundels;
- short crossbars, hooks, or secondary strokes are attached near the arms and central intersection;
- the whole figure is asymmetrical;
- it is not the same thing as the “star in a pentacle” describing the apparition.

The surviving manuscript forms differ in stroke length, curvature, terminal circles, and the placement of small ancillary marks. A prose description cannot preserve whether a short line crosses, merely touches, or terminates beside another line; for exact ritual reproduction the manuscript or printed facsimile must be consulted.

A faithful vector of the 1904 printed form is available at [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3A69-decarabia-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg). A later de Laurence rendering is preserved as [*Decarabia seal.png*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ADecarabia_seal.png).

### Ritual function

The Sloane text says the seal is to be made and worn **“as a Lamin before the Exorcist.”** A lamen is a breast-worn ritual emblem. In the Rudd recension, the paired Shem angel and psalm are incorporated as a protective counterforce, commonly on the reverse or heart-facing side.

## 2. The seventy-two-letter construction

Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional Hebrew consonantal text, of three verses of seventy-two letters each. The common method is:

1. write verse 19 in its normal direction;
2. write verse 20 in reverse;
3. write verse 21 in its normal direction;
4. read the letters vertically in groups of three.

This boustrophedon arrangement produces seventy-two triplets. The 69th is conventionally read:

> **ראה — resh, aleph, he**

The root ראה means “to see.” Christian Cabalists and angelological compilers expanded many triplets by adding divine suffixes such as **-el** or **-yah**, producing angelic names. Thus ראה + אל becomes **ראהאל**, Roehel/Rochel.

**[Historical distinction]** In rabbinic sources, *Shem ha-mephorash* can mean the “explicit,” “distinguished,” or fully pronounced divine name, frequently the Tetragrammaton. It did not always denote this particular list of seventy-two suffixed angels. The equation “Shem ha-mephorash = exactly these 72 angels” is the product of a longer textual development.

Rashi discusses the seventy-two-letter arrangement in his commentary on Sukkah 45a, while medieval sources such as the *Bahir* transmit related combinatorial ideas. Ibn Ezra offers a different understanding of the numerical allusions in Exodus and should not be enlisted uncritically as a witness to the later angel list.

## 3. From Jewish name mysticism to Christian angelology

### *Sefer Raziel* and medieval kabbalistic material

**[Documented tradition]** Texts collected under the title *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* transmit divine names, angelic operations, seals, and forms of the seventy-twofold name. The work is composite and cannot be treated as a single book dictated in antiquity by the angel Raziel.

**[Historical caution]** The precise vocalization and suffixing of the seventy-two triplets vary between manuscripts and printed witnesses. The bare triplets are older than the standardized modern “guardian angel” biographies attached to them.

### Reuchlin and Christian Cabala

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped establish a Christian-Cabalist presentation of the seventy-two names as a hierarchy of angels formed with *-el* and *-yah*. Blaise de Vigenère and later compilers continued the system.

This is an important transition: Hebrew divine-name permutations become a numbered Christian angelology suitable for matching to zodiacal degrees, psalms, spirits, and ritual offices.

### Kircher

Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (Rome, 1652–1654), especially vol. II, part 2, pp. 479–481, printed an influential table and diagram of the seventy-two divine names and angels. Kircher’s syncretic project combined Hebrew Kabbalah, Christian theology, angelology, astrology, and supposed Egyptian wisdom.

Kircher is an important printed transmitter, but not the originator of the Exodus permutation.

### Rudd

In Harley 6483, the numbered angels are aligned with the numbered Goetic spirits. Spirit 69, Decarabia, is therefore placed under angel 69, Roehel/Rochel.

The controlling principle is ordinal and apotropaic:

> the demon’s seal is paired with the corresponding divine or angelic name so that the operator does not confront the spirit with an unprotected demonic emblem.

Skinner and Rankine describe the Rudd lamen as placing the corresponding Shem angel on the reverse, toward the operator’s heart, to restrain the spirit’s deception or malice. This is the distinctive feature of the “Goetia of Dr Rudd.”

**[Tradition, not demonstrable antiquity]** The manuscript frames the angel as a genuine superior spiritual force. Historically, however, the one-to-one 1–72 pairing is a learned early-modern construction, not something found in Weyer’s Decarabia entry or ancient Jewish texts.

### Lenain

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* (1823) systematized the seventy-two “good genii” into:

- five-degree zodiacal rays;
- annual calendar days;
- twenty-minute periods of the day;
- psalm verses;
- moral and physical attributes;
- opposing or contrary influences.

For angel 69, the ordinal degrees are 341–345, conventionally Pisces 10°00′–14°59′.

Lenain’s structure is foundational for later French occult angelology. It should not be projected backward into Harley 6483 unless a specific manuscript parallel is shown.

### Papus

Papus republished and promoted Lenain’s Cabalistic science around the turn of the twentieth century. His role was chiefly that of compiler and popularizer. He did not originate Rochel’s five-degree zodiacal assignment.

### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) gives the following for Roehel/Rochel:

- divine attribute: “God Who Sees All”;
- ray: degrees 341–345;
- zodiacal placement: Pisces 10°–15°;
- decan influence: Jupiter;
- invocation period: approximately **10:40–11:00 p.m.**, the 69th twenty-minute interval of the day;
- psalm: Vulgate 15:5;
- offices: finding lost or concealed objects, identifying the person responsible for their removal, renown, fortune, inheritance, and succession;
- contrary influence: destructive litigation, manipulation of wills, and expensive legal disputes.

Ambelain also supplies dispersed annual days—27 May, 7 August, 18 October, 29 December, and 11 March—in a cyclic rulership table. These should not be confused with the continuous zodiacal interval often rendered approximately as 1–5 March.

Ambelain’s pentacles and operational claims belong to twentieth-century ritual Cabala. Later editions reportedly qualified some seal attributions as visionary or problematic; they are not manuscript evidence for medieval Jewish angel magic.

### Golden Dawn and Crowley

The Golden Dawn synthesized:

- zodiacal decans;
- tarot pip cards;
- planetary decan rulers;
- Hebrew divine names;
- angelic hierarchies;
- Goetic catalogues.

Crowley’s *Liber 777* transmitted and rearranged much of this correspondence culture. However, the Golden Dawn/Crowley placement of Goetic demons by decan is not the same as Lenain’s ordinal angelic five-degree table.

### Haziel

Haziel’s late-twentieth-century books popularized the seventy-two angels as personal or “guardian” angels determined through birth date, time, and related calculations. His *Grand livre des invocations et des exhortations* dates from the late 1980s and was repeatedly reissued.

**[Modern angelology]** Haziel’s personal-guardian system is substantially later than Rudd, Kircher, and Lenain. It is influential in popular esotericism but is not evidence for what Harley 6483 originally meant by Rochel’s control of Decarabia.

## 4. Rochel’s psalm

Harley/Rudd and later tables give Vulgate Psalm 15:5, corresponding to Psalm 16:5 in most Hebrew and English Bibles:

> **Dominus pars haereditatis meae, et calicis mei: tu es, qui restitues haereditatem meam mihi.**

KJV:

> **The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup: thou maintainest my lot.**

Hebrew:

> **יְהוָה מְנָת־חֶלְקִי וְכוֹסִי אַתָּה תּוֹמִיךְ גּוֹרָלִי**

The inheritance and “lot” language plausibly encouraged Rochel’s later association with succession, wills, and restoration of property. That is a historical inference about how an office may have developed, not a statement made by Exodus itself.

## 2. Crowley’s *777* decan scheme

The 72 spirits are distributed as two spirits per ten-degree decan:

- spirits 1–36 form one series;
- spirits 37–72 form the second;
- spirit 69 corresponds to spirit 33 because 69 − 36 = 33.

The thirty-third decan in zodiacal order is Aquarius 20°–30°. Accordingly:

| Crowley/Golden Dawn field | Attribution |
|---|---|
| Decan | Aquarius 20°–30° |
| Planetary ruler | Moon in Aquarius |
| Tarot pip | Seven of Swords |
| Golden Dawn card title | Lord of Unstable Effort |
| Crowley/Thoth title | Futility |
| Day partner | Gaap, spirit 33 |
| Night partner | Decarabia, spirit 69 |
| Later quinance extrapolation | Gaap 20°–25°; Decarabia 25°–30° |

**[Qualification]** *777* clearly pairs the two spirits with the same decan and distinguishes day and night series. The exact subdivision of the ten degrees into first and second five-degree halves is a common later reading, not a sentence in Weyer or the Goetia. Consequently, **Aquarius 25°–30°** is best labeled an **esoteric extrapolation**.

## 4. Element and direction

- **South/Fire:** Folger’s spirit hierarchy places the Cambra/Decarabia cognate under Amaymon, king of the South. Later ceremonial tables therefore sometimes call Decarabia fiery.
- **Aquarius/Air:** the *777* decan correspondence makes the spirit airy.
- **Moon/Water symbolism:** the Marquis rank supplies lunar and silver associations but does not automatically make the spirit elemental Water.
- **Pisces/Water:** belongs to Rochel’s ordinal angelic quinance, not intrinsically to Decarabia.

Claims that Decarabia has one ancient and immutable element conceal the composite nature of the system.

## 5. Enochian overlays

No Decarabia, Cambea, Carabia, or Rochel is found as such in Dee and Kelley’s Enochian records.

Modern ceremonial magicians sometimes cross-index Goetic spirits with:

- Enochian tablets or subquarters;
- Golden Dawn elemental grades;
- decan angels;
- planetary seniors;
- geomantic figures;
- tarot paths.

These are **[later syncretic overlays]**. A correspondence printed in a modern table does not show that Dee, Kelley, Weyer, Rudd, or the Sloane compiler regarded Decarabia as an Enochian being.

## Iconography

## 1. The star and pentacle

The earliest printed “image” is Weyer’s unresolved star-like character. Scot copied it. The Goetia transformed it into a narrative apparition:

> a star in a pentacle, later compelled to take human form.

No early source describes:

- the star’s color;
- a face or eye at its center;
- the number or sex of the human figure;
- clothing, weapons, wings, or crown;
- a transition into a bat or other specific bird.

A five-pointed star is conventional because later authors read the printer’s mark that way, but the early sign’s exact intended referent is uncertain.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

The 1863 edition of Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* gives the following notice on p. 135:

> **Carabia ou Decarabia, démon peu connu, quoiqu’il jouisse d’un grand pouvoir au sombre empire. Il est roi d’une partie de l’enfer, et comte d’une autre province considérable. Il se présente, comme Buer, sous la figure d’une étoile à cinq rayons. Il connaît les vertus des plantes et des pierres précieuses ; il domine sur les oiseaux, qu’il rend familiers. Trente légions sont à ses ordres.**

Translation:

> Carabia or Decarabia, a little-known demon, although he enjoys great power in the dark empire. He is king of one part of hell and count of another considerable province. Like Buer, he presents himself in the figure of a five-rayed star. He knows the virtues of plants and precious stones; he rules birds, which he makes familiar. Thirty legions are under his orders.

Source: [1863 entry and facsimile](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Carabia).

De Plancy follows Weyer rather than the Goetia on the rank: King and Count. His “five-rayed star” converts the ambiguous sign into a pentagram-like figure and compares it with Buer.

### Is there a Louis Breton portrait?

**No Decarabia portrait has been verified among the Louis Breton engravings in the 1863 edition.** The entry is typographic and lacks an adjacent demon illustration. Online pages that place a Breton engraving beside Decarabia commonly reuse an image made for another demon.

The *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818, but the description quoted above is the verified 1863 text. The celebrated Breton illustrations belong to the sixth edition, not automatically to every entry or to the 1818 edition.

## 3. Manuscript and printed seals

The seal, not the star apparition, became Decarabia’s principal emblem in ritual books and modern merchandise. Major stages are:

1. no sigil in Weyer or Scot;
2. handwritten Goetia sigils in the Sloane manuscripts;
3. Rudd/Harley seal with the **Becarabia** error;
4. standardized Mathers–Crowley engraving in 1904;
5. derivative redrawings by de Laurence, Waite-related editions, occult publishers, tattoo artists, and online vector repositories.

Modern redrawings often regularize curves, close open loops, or force the design into a circle. Those aesthetic changes are not manuscript variants in the historical sense.

## 4. Modern visual art

### Atlus: *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona*

Atlus depicts Decarabia as a red, five-armed, one-eyed starfish-like demon. The design literalizes “star” but is not derived from a de Plancy portrait. Reference works on the series connect the visual concept with the alien star-shaped Pairans of the Japanese film *Warning from Space*.

This design appears across *Shin Megami Tensei*, *Devil Summoner*, and *Persona* titles, including later *Persona* installments. It is now one of the most recognizable modern images of Decarabia, but it is **[game-specific modern invention]**.

### *Castlevania*

*Castlevania: Dawn of Sorrow* and *Order of Ecclesia* use Decarabia as a hostile star- or pentacle-shaped creature. This again literalizes the Goetia’s star apparition. It is not a transcription of the seal.

### Occult and devotional art

Modern demonolatry art variously depicts Decarabia as:

- an eye-bearing pentagram;
- a humanoid with bird wings;
- a bird-headed marquis;
- a masked celestial figure;
- a feminine or androgynous stellar intelligence.

None of these details appears in the source-chain descriptions. They are artistic interpretation or individual visionary lore.

## People and works

## 1. Johann Weyer

Weyer supplied the first exact printed Decarabia/Carabia entry in 1577. His larger aim was to contest the theological and judicial assumptions behind witch persecution. The catalogue is invaluable evidence, but its publication context was skeptical and polemical.

## 2. Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot translated or incorporated the catalogue into *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* in 1584 as part of his attack on credulity and witch trials. Abraham Fleming is often credited with the translation used by Scot. Scot’s omission of the rank demonstrates that his text cannot always be treated as an exact English equivalent of Weyer.

## 3. Thomas Rudd

The Rudd-attributed recension linked each Goetic demon to a Shem angel. Its distinguishing contribution to Decarabia’s tradition is not a new office but the protective pairing with Roehel/Rochel.

Because Harley 6483 postdates Rudd, the exact degree of his personal authorship remains a historical question.

## 4. S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Mathers prepared the English Goetia text; Crowley published and supplemented it in 1904. Their edition made the name, number 69, Marquis rank, star-in-pentacle form, and seal broadly accessible.

The edition also propagated the **“virtues of Birds”** corruption. Crowley’s *777* later supplied the decan/tarot arrangement from which Aquarius 20°–30°, the Seven of Swords, and the nocturnal quinance attribution are derived.

## 5. A. E. Waite

Waite reproduced and discussed Goetic catalogues and sigils in *The Book of Black Magic and of Pacts* and later revisions. His work helped circulate the spirit outside explicitly Crowleyan circles. Waite’s historical judgments are often hostile to grimoire practice, but his printed figures are significant in the iconographic transmission.

## 6. Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 *Lemegeton* and his Esoteric Archives transcriptions are central to modern textual study. For Decarabia, his work makes it possible to distinguish:

- Weyer’s Latin;
- Scot’s English;
- Sloane readings;
- manuscript spelling variants;
- the star/asterisk problem;
- the herbs/birds corruption;
- the different numbering systems.

Peterson’s printed edition places the Decarabia entry on p. 38, with relevant textual notes around p. 249.

## 7. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007) published and analyzed the Harley material. They drew particular attention to the angel-on-the-reverse protective lamen and to the **Becarabia** seal-label error.

Their edition is indispensable for Rudd’s scheme but should be distinguished from the ordinary Sloane *Ars Goetia* and the Mathers–Crowley text.

## 8. Brian Johnson

Johnson’s 2021 edition and translation of BML Plut. 89 sup. 38 is the principal modern source for the proposed 1494 antecedent **Cambea**. This pushes the history of the underlying entry before Weyer, although not the exact spelling Decarabia.

## 9. Jean-Patrice Boudet, Julien Véronèse, Jan Veenstra, and Owen Davies

- **Jean-Patrice Boudet** has studied Renaissance demonological “who’s who” catalogues and their medieval ancestors, including the relationship between the *Livre des Esperitz* and Weyer’s list. His comparison cautions against imagining one simple act of copying; the catalogues share and rearrange a larger textual reservoir.
- **Julien Véronèse** is important for the broader study of medieval ritual magic and Solomonic textual traditions. No Decarabia-specific etymology attributable to him was located.
- **Jan Veenstra** has analyzed related Solomonic material such as the *Holy Almandal/Almadel*. That scholarship helps contextualize the *Lemegeton* but does not furnish an early Decarabia.
- **Owen Davies**, especially in *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books*, supplies the broader social history of grimoires and their transmission. He is not the source of a separate Decarabia legend.

## 10. Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* incorporates the Goetia into a modern operative magical system, including astrological and angelic protections. It belongs to the history of twentieth-century practice, not the early textual history of Decarabia.

## 11. Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* helped popularize a pragmatic and psychological reading of the spirits. No unique manuscript discovery or independent Decarabia etymology is supplied there.

## 12. Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica* and *Geosophia* seek to recover spirit work from beneath later psychological and moralizing interpretations. He is relevant to contemporary traditionalist Goetia, but no secure ancient deity behind Decarabia has been demonstrated in the material consulted.

## 13. Frater Achad

No item-specific treatment of Decarabia by Frater Achad was located. Achad is relevant to early twentieth-century Thelemic Qabalah and altered attribution systems, but citing him as an authority for Decarabia’s name, seal, angel, or origin would presently be unsupported.

## Grimoire ancestors

## 1. *Testament of Solomon*

The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* supplies a major conceptual ancestor:

- Solomon interrogates and binds demons;
- demons declare their forms and functions;
- many name the angel who thwarts them;
- the king assigns them work.

No Decarabia, Carabia, Cambra, or Cambea is identifiable in the *Testament*. Rochel’s position-for-position control of Decarabia is not inherited directly from it. The resemblance is structural.

## 2. Munich Handbook, CLM 849

The fifteenth-century Munich handbook contains spirit catalogues and coercive ritual material related to the same broad necromantic environment. No secure Decarabia or Cambea entry has been found there.

It is an ancestor of the genre, not a demonstrated direct source for this spirit.

## 3. *Livre des Esperitz*

The French *Livre des Esperitz* preserves a demon catalogue related to Weyer’s. Comparative scholarship shows substantial overlap but also names unique to each list.

No exact Decarabia/Carabia entry occurs in the standard transcription. Consequently, the *Livre* cannot be cited as the earliest Decarabia witness.

## 4. Florentine BML Plut. 89 sup. 38

The January 1494 Florentine manuscript is the closest known direct antecedent because **Cambea** shares the herbal, lapidary, and avian offices. This is substantially stronger evidence than generic catalogue resemblance.

## 5. *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer described his source as a *Book of the Offices of Spirits*. Manuscripts such as Folger V.b.26 preserve related material and the form **Cambra**.

This source family, rather than an ancient pagan pantheon, is Decarabia’s demonstrable home.

## 6. *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin manuscripts contain extensive lists of spirits and subordinate spirits. No secure Decarabia, Carabia, Cambra, or Cambea equivalence was found. Similar-looking names in long demon lists should not be equated without matching offices, hierarchy, or manuscript evidence.

## Literature, film, music, and games

## Attested appearances

### *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona*

**[Modern invention, attested]** Decarabia is a recurring demon/persona represented as a red, one-eyed starfish or five-pointed alien. The character appears in numerous Atlus titles and is directly named for the Goetic spirit. Its visual design is an adaptation, not an early portrait.

### *Castlevania*

**[Modern invention, attested]** A Decarabia enemy appears in *Castlevania: Dawn of Sorrow* and *Castlevania: Order of Ecclesia*. Its star/pentacle morphology draws on the Goetic description.

### *Shakugan no Shana*

**[Modern fiction, attested]** Decarabia is used as the name of a Crimson Lord, commonly titled the “Officer of Boundless Seas,” in the light-novel/anime franchise. The characterization is original to the series.

### *Genshin Impact*

**[Modern allusion, attested]** “Decarabian” is the name/title of the ancient God of Storms associated with Old Mondstadt. The form is an overt Goetic-name borrowing, but the fictional biography has no relation to the grimoire’s herbs, stones, birds, legions, or angel.

### Future Palace

**[Modern musical use, attested]** The band Future Palace released a track titled “Decarabia” in 2024, connected with the album *Distortion*. The title participates in Decarabia’s modern cultural afterlife rather than transmitting historical demonology.

## Negative findings

### Dante

No Decarabia occurs in Dante. The *Commedia* predates Weyer and the exact printed name by centuries. Comparisons with Dantean demons are modern.

### Milton

No Decarabia appears in *Paradise Lost*. Milton wrote after Weyer, but did not use the name.

### *Bartimaeus*

No canonical Decarabia appearance was located in Jonathan Stroud’s *Bartimaeus* novels.

### *Hellboy*

No securely attested canonical Decarabia character was located in the principal *Hellboy* comics or films.

### *Helluva Boss*

As of the sources checked, Decarabia has not appeared as a canonical *Helluva Boss* character. Online Decarabia “Helluva” material is predominantly fan art, fan fiction, or original-character design inspired by the show’s use of other Goetic names.

The distinction matters because search results alone can make a heavily circulated fan character look official.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Does Decarabia occur before Weyer?

Two answers are required:

- **Exact spelling Decarabia:** no verified witness before Weyer, 1577.
- **Probable underlying spirit-entry:** yes, Cambea in the dated 1494 Florentine manuscript and Cambra in the related manuscript family.

Thus “Decarabia was invented by Weyer” is too strong, while “Decarabia is an ancient named demon” is unsupported.

## 2. Was the original form a star?

- Weyer prints an unexplained star-like symbol.
- Scot copies it.
- Nicholson and Peterson regard it as potentially marking a missing or illegible word.
- Cambea’s faun-like form suggests what the damaged reading might once have contained.
- The Goetia literalizes the sign as a star in a pentacle.
- De Plancy further specifies a five-rayed star.

The literal star is certain only from the later Goetia tradition onward.

## 3. King and Count or Marquis?

All three are historically attested, but at different stages:

- Weyer: great King and Count;
- Scot: rank omitted;
- Goetia: Great Marquis.

The Marquis rank is canonical for the *Ars Goetia*, while King-and-Count is the earlier printed evidence. Neither should silently replace the other in a source history.

## 4. Herbs or birds?

The source chain strongly favors:

> virtues of **herbs** and precious stones.

“Virtues of birds” is present in the Rudd/Mathers–Crowley branch and became popular, but is almost certainly secondary.

## 5. Natural birds, familiars, or illusions?

- Weyer: birds remain like *familiares ac domesticae*.
- Scot: birds behave as though tame.
- Goetia: “similitude” of birds, acting like natural birds.

“Bird familiars” is a fair traditional summary, provided it is clear that the texts do not describe a named permanent familiar bestowed on the magician.

## 6. Does Decarabia transform people into birds?

No early Decarabia text says so.

This claim is probably a conflation with **Andrealphus**, whose Goetic office includes transforming a person into the likeness of a bird. Decarabia causes birds or bird-similitudes to appear.

## 7. Does Decarabia transform into a bat?

No early source says this. It is modern lore, perhaps generated by conflating “birds,” nocturnal timing, and general demon imagery.

## 8. Does Decarabia teach all sciences or languages?

No. Those are offices of other spirits. Decarabia’s specified knowledge concerns herbs and precious stones, plus the avian manifestation.

## 9. Does Decarabia grant treasures, love, invisibility, or transmutation?

None of these is in the Decarabia entries examined. They belong to other Goetic spirits or to generic lists of magical benefits.

## 10. Is Becarabia a separate spirit?

Almost certainly not. It occurs as a label error beside the Harley seal, while the entry and Hebrew transliteration identify Decarabia.

## 11. Is the seal ancient?

No evidence supports that claim. The seal is absent from Weyer, Scot, and the probable 1494 ancestor. It belongs to the later Goetia manuscript tradition.

## 12. Is Rochel an ancient personal guardian angel?

The triad ראה belongs to the medieval seventy-two-name tradition. The expanded personal angel with a fixed meaning, office, psalm, birth dates, invocation time, and demon number is the cumulative product of Christian Cabala and modern occult compilation.

Calling every part “ancient Hebrew Kabbalah” obscures several centuries of development.

## 13. Which zodiacal sign is correct: Aquarius or Pisces?

Both result from later systems:

- Aquarius belongs to Crowley’s demon-decan allocation.
- Pisces belongs to the ordinal five-degree allocation of angel 69.

Neither is a correction of the other. They answer different tabular questions.

## 14. Is Decarabia a fallen angel from the order of Virtues or Ophanim?

No such premodern statement was found. It is a modern angelological classification, often inferred from Decarabia’s powers or imposed by demonolatry authors.

## 15. Does the name derive from Abraxas or an ancient stellar god?

No evidence presently connects them. These are modern speculative claims.

## 16. Sex and gender

The Latin and English sources use masculine grammar. They do not supply a theological account of the spirit’s sex. Feminine, nonbinary, or androgynous manifestations reported by modern practitioners are personal or artistic traditions, not manuscript descriptions.

## 17. “Enns” and modern invocations

Formulae such as modern demonic “enns” do not occur in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, or Harley 6483. They belong to recent demonolatry systems and should be labeled accordingly.

## The order and number problem

Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia* presents sixty-nine named spirits even though its framing language invokes the Solomonic number seventy-two. The Goetia reaches seventy-two through one omission and four additions.

### Omitted from Weyer’s order

- **Pruflas/Bufas**, Weyer’s spirit 4, is absent from the Goetia.

### Added in the Goetia

- **Vassago**, Goetia 3;
- **Seere**, Goetia 70;
- **Dantalion**, Goetia 71;
- **Andromalius**, Goetia 72.

The arithmetic is:

> 69 Weyer spirits − 1 omitted + 4 added = 72 Goetia spirits.

Because of insertion and rearrangement, Weyer’s Decarabia 52 becomes Goetia 69. The expansion to seventy-two probably reflects the prestige of the seventy-twofold divine name and related numerological systems, although the surviving Goetia does not provide a historical narrative explaining every editorial decision.

The Rudd scheme could then pair the completed 72-demon list position-for-position with the 72 Shem angels. This elegant numerological fit is a result of the later list, not proof that Weyer’s source originally contained seventy-two demons with those angels.

## Open questions

1. **What word stood behind Weyer’s star sign?**  
   Cambea’s faun-like manifestation is suggestive, but Weyer’s lost exemplar has not been recovered.

2. **How exactly did Cambea become Decarabia?**  
   The intermediate Cambra and Carabia forms make a scribal genealogy probable, but the added syllable *de-* remains unexplained.

3. **Is Cambea unquestionably the same spirit?**  
   The matching offices and related list position are strong evidence. The differing form and twenty rather than thirty legions prevent absolute identification.

4. **Who compiled the surviving Goetia recension?**  
   Its dependence on Weyer/Scot is evident, but the identities and precise dates of the compilers who added seals, human forms, and new spirits are unknown.

5. **Was the human form inherited from a lost source?**  
   It may be an omitted older detail, a later ritual convention, or an expansion by the Goetia compiler.

6. **When was Decarabia’s seal first drawn?**  
   No securely dated pre-seventeenth-century example has been established.

7. **How much of Harley 6483 is actually Thomas Rudd’s?**  
   The recension carries his name and system, but the surviving manuscript’s date requires continuing scrutiny of provenance and scribal layers.

8. **What was the immediate source of the Rudd angel–demon pairing?**  
   Christian-Cabalist tables supplied the angels; the completed Goetia supplied the demons. The exact compilation path through Reuchlin, Vigenère, Kircher, or intermediary manuscripts is not fully settled.

9. **Did Mathers knowingly change herbs to birds?**  
   The reading may derive from his manuscript, from Harley’s branch, or from editorial/typographical error. The printed edition does not explain it.

10. **Can a pre-1494 Cambea cognate be found?**  
    At present, no securely identified earlier witness has been demonstrated.

## Conclusions

Decarabia is not a demon with a demonstrable ancient pagan biography. Its recoverable history begins in a late-medieval European spirit-office tradition:

- **1494:** probable ancestor Cambea, with herbal, lapidary, and avian powers;
- **sixteenth century:** Cambra and Carabia variants;
- **1577:** Weyer first prints Decarabia, ranking him King and Count;
- **1584:** Scot transmits the entry in English;
- **seventeenth century:** the Goetia moves him to number 69, changes the rank to Marquis, literalizes the star, adds a human form and seal;
- **1712–1713:** Harley 6483 embeds him in the Rudd angelic-control system, paired with Roehel/Rochel;
- **1863:** de Plancy describes a five-rayed star but supplies no Breton portrait;
- **1904:** Mathers and Crowley standardize the modern text and seal while spreading the “birds for herbs” corruption;
- **twentieth century:** Golden Dawn, Crowley, Lenain-derived angelology, Ambelain, and practical Goetia authors add multiple astrological and ritual overlays;
- **late twentieth and twenty-first centuries:** games, anime, music, fan art, and demonolatry transform the sparse star-and-birds description into elaborate modern characters.

The three most important cautions are:

1. the star may have originated as an illegibility sign;
2. “virtues of birds” is secondary to “virtues of herbs”;
3. Aquarius 25°–30° and Pisces 10°–15° belong to different correspondence systems.

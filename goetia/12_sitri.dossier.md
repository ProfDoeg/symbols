# Sitri: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary method

This dossier separates seven kinds of claims:

- **[Documented text]** — explicitly present in a primary source.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a relationship proposed from textual comparison, manuscript history, or philology.
- **[Tradition]** — a teaching transmitted within an identifiable religious or magical tradition.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a deliberately constructed occult attribution rather than a historical identity.
- **[Disputed]** — a claim for which the sources conflict or the evidence is inadequate.
- **[Legend]** — a story transmitted without adequate historical documentation.
- **[Modern invention]** — a recent elaboration absent from the older source chain.

The Weyer, Scot, and Goetia entries are summarized clause by clause below, with short diagnostic quotations and links to complete diplomatic or facsimile texts. Page numbering follows the named edition where it can be established reliably; early copies of Scot vary in pagination and digitization.

## Earliest recoverable history

### Beduch or Bamone: the earliest surviving persona

**[Documented text]** A late-fifteenth-century Florentine magical miscellany, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana MS Plut. 89 sup. 38, contains a spirit called **Beduch vel Bamone**. Part of the manuscript was copied at Rome in 1494. The entry describes:

- a great marquis;
- appearing like a leopard;
- with wings like a griffin;
- assuming a human form;
- giving the love of women;
- causing women to languish or become feverish with love;
- revealing women’s secrets;
- ridiculing them;
- and causing stripping, nakedness, or lascivious play.

It commands twenty legions. The surviving Latin begins, within quotation limits, “*Beduch ut Bamone magnus marchio apparet in similitudinem leopardi*”—Beduch or Bamone, a great marquis, appears in the likeness of a leopard. The complete Latin and translation are printed in Brian Johnson’s edition, *Necromancy in the Medici Library*. [Manuscript catalogue](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/ms/357) [Johnson edition](https://hadean.press/products/necromancy-in-the-medici-library) [Accessible transcription](https://dokumen.pub/necromancy-in-the-medici-library-an-edition-and-translation-of-excerpts-from-biblioteca-medicea-laurenziana-ms-plut-89-sup-38-9781907881985-9781907881992.html)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This is the earliest presently identifiable source for nearly the whole later Sitri persona. Its name, however, is not Sitri, Sytry, Bitru, or Bitur. It therefore establishes a textual ancestor for the description, not a pre-1494 attestation of the name.

### Bitur in the *Livre des esperitz*

**[Documented text]** The *Livre des esperitz* has **Bitur**, a great marquis appearing as a handsome young man. He grants women’s love, can destroy cities and castles, and can depose people from great honors and worldly dignities. He has thirty-six legions. Its opening is “*Bitur est ung grant marquis*.” [Bitur entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Bitur)

This Bitur does not have the leopard and griffin appearance in the surviving French entry. Nor does the entry contain the later material about women’s secrets and nakedness.

Another spirit in the same catalogue, **Dam**, is a count who reveals women’s secrets and makes them strip and dance naked. He has twenty-five legions. [Complete *Livre des esperitz*](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The Weyer entry may represent a redaction or convergence of materials circulating under more than one name:

- **Bitur** supplies the alias and love office.
- **Beduch/Bamone** supplies the leopard, griffin wings, human transformation, love, secrets, mockery, and nakedness.
- **Dam** provides a second catalogue parallel for secrets, mockery, and stripping.

The similarity to Beduch/Bamone is too extensive to dismiss as coincidence, but the exact stemma remains unproved. It is possible that all three derive from another, now-lost catalogue rather than Weyer or his source directly combining the surviving entries.

### How old is the *Livre des esperitz*?

**[Disputed]** Older secondary literature assigned the surviving French book to the fifteenth century. Jean-Patrice Boudet’s codicological assessment places the extant copy around the middle of the sixteenth century, although parts of its catalogue tradition are demonstrably medieval. Consequently:

- The *tradition* behind the book can be medieval.
- The surviving **Bitur** entry should not automatically be dated to the thirteenth or fifteenth century.
- Beduch/Bamone in the 1494 Florentine manuscript remains the earlier securely datable Sitri-like description.
- The first securely dated **Sytry/Sitri** spellings remain Weyer in 1577 and Scot in 1584.

[Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019)

## Does the name come from an ancient god, hero, place, or language?

**[Documented finding]** No ancient deity, hero, geographical name, Greek word, Latin word, or securely analyzed Semitic root has been demonstrated behind Sitri.

This distinguishes Sitri from names for which an ancient derivation is plausible or transparent—Baal, Astarte, Amon/Amun, Asmodai/Ashmedai, Belial, Phoenix, or names plainly made from Greek and Latin vocabulary.

The documentary progression is:

> Beduch/Bamone-like persona → Bitur → Sytry alias Bitru → Sitri alias Bitru → Sitri.

**[Disputed]** Modern proposals include derivation from:

- Egyptian Set or Sutekh;
- Hebrew *satar*, “to hide”;
- Latin *sitire*, “to thirst”;
- an unidentified word meaning “apparition”;
- Mercury or a supposed deity called “Sihtu.”

None is supported by a premodern source, an established sound change, cognate forms, or an independently attested divine name. Resemblance alone is insufficient, particularly when the manuscripts already show fluid copying of unfamiliar names.

**[Modern invention]** Luciferian or demonolatry sites sometimes present Sitri as a survival or mask of Set. That may function as a modern symbolic association, but it is not an established etymology. [Example of the modern Set interpretation](https://templodelucifer.blog/quem-e-sitri-origens-historia-etimologia-e-a-ligacao-com-set-sob-a-otica-luciferiana/)

**Conclusion:** the origin of the *name* is unknown. The origin of much of the *description* can be traced at least to the Florentine Beduch/Bamone entry.

# Number and order in the catalogues

## Weyer versus the Ars Goetia

Sitri is:

- **Weyer no. 21**
- **Ars Goetia no. 12**

Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* contains sixty-nine numbered spirits, notwithstanding a remark in the Belial entry suggesting that a complete hierarchy numbered seventy-two.

The Ars Goetia produces seventy-two by:

1. adding **Vassago** at no. 3;
2. adding **Seere** at no. 70;
3. adding **Dantalion** at no. 71;
4. adding **Andromalius** at no. 72;
5. omitting **Pruflas**, also called Busas.

Thus the Goetia adds four and removes one: 69 + 4 − 1 = 72. Scot had already omitted Pruflas from his English presentation, probably because of an accident or omission in transmission rather than a developed theological reason.

The compiler also rearranged much of Weyer’s sequence. Sitri therefore moved from twenty-first to twelfth; the numerical change does not indicate two different spirits. [Peterson’s Weyer–Goetia comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf)

## Weyer, 1577

**[Documented text]** Johann Weyer printed the entry as no. 21 in the 1577 fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum*, in the appendix now called *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, p. 917.

His opening identifies “*Sytry, aliàs Bitru, magnus Princeps*”—Sytry, otherwise Bitru, a great prince. The complete entry says, in precise summary:

1. Sytry, also called Bitru, is a great prince.
2. He appears with a leopard’s face and wings like a griffin.
3. When he assumes human form, he appears extraordinarily beautiful.
4. He inflames a man with love for a woman.
5. Reciprocally, he excites a woman with desire for a man.
6. When commanded, he willingly reveals women’s secrets.
7. He laughs at and mocks women.
8. He causes them to strip lasciviously.
9. Sixty legions obey him.

The Latin’s important verb is ***detegit***, “uncovers, discloses, reveals,” not “detains.”

[Weyer’s Latin and Peterson’s English apparatus](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### Context

**[Documented history]** Weyer was a physician and critic of witch persecutions. He reproduced ritual-magical material partly to expose what he regarded as demonic fraud and conjurors’ deception. The presence of the catalogue in his work is not evidence that he endorsed Goetic practice.

Weyer says his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also called *Empto*. A book under a related title was mentioned by Johannes Trithemius in the *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508. That identifies a textual family, not necessarily the exact surviving *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*. [Trithemius’s catalogue](https://www.esotericarchives.com/tritheim/antipalus.htm)

## Scot, 1584

**[Documented text]** Reginald Scot included an English version in *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter 2. The printed entry begins “Sitri, alias Bitru, is a great prince” and follows Weyer closely:

1. leopard’s face;
2. griffin’s wings;
3. beautiful human shape;
4. inflames men with women’s love;
5. stirs women to love men;
6. reveals women’s secrets;
7. laughs at and mocks them;
8. makes them “luxuriouslie naked”;
9. commands sixty legions.

The old text reads “willinglie deteineth secrets,” but that makes poor sense and conflicts with Weyer’s *detegit*. Peterson therefore supplies **“discloses”** in brackets.

The translation has often been attributed to Abraham Fleming, although it appears within Scot’s book and the precise responsibility for individual passages remains a bibliographical question.

[Peterson’s parallel Weyer–Scot text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)  
[1584 *Discoverie of Witchcraft* facsimile](https://archive.org/details/discouerieofwitc00scot)

### Scot’s purpose

Like Weyer, Scot was not publishing a neutral modern practitioner’s handbook. He used magical claims as evidence in a polemic against credulity, fraudulent conjurors, and lethal witch accusations. Later occultists extracted the catalogue from that argumentative setting.

## Sloane MS 3825

**[Documented text]** In the Lemegeton recension represented by British Library Sloane MS 3825, Sitri becomes the twelfth spirit. Peterson’s 2001 edition gives the entry on p. 12, corresponding approximately to fol. 102r in the manuscript sequence.

The opening reads, within quotation limits, “The 12th spirit is Sitri, he is a great prince.” The entry then states:

- he first appears with a leopard’s face and griffin’s wings;
- at the exorcist’s command he assumes a very beautiful human form;
- he inflames men with women’s love and women with men’s love;
- he causes them to show themselves naked if desired;
- he governs sixty legions;
- his seal is to be worn.

[Peterson’s online *Goetia* transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)  
[Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon*, p. 12](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf)

### What the Goetia drops

**[Documented textual difference]** The Lemegeton entry omits Weyer’s statement that Sitri reveals, laughs at, and ridicules women’s secrets. It preserves the nakedness clause but rephrases it as causing “them” to show themselves naked.

This may reflect abridgment, another intermediate source, or a deliberate narrowing of the office. There is no manuscript statement explaining the alteration.

## Sloane MS 2731

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731 likewise has **Sitri**, no. 12, as a great prince commanding sixty legions. Its substance belongs to the same Lemegeton recension as Sloane 3825, with normal scribal variation in spelling, punctuation, abbreviations, and the seal’s execution.

The source does not restore Weyer’s secrets-and-mockery clause. [Peterson’s manuscript comparison and edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

## Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

**[Documented text]** Harley MS 6483 contains material attributed to Thomas Rudd but copied by Peter Smart around 1712–1714, reportedly from a 1655/56 exemplar. It is therefore not an autograph manuscript securely written by Rudd.

Its Sitri entry retains:

- number 12;
- rank of great prince;
- leopard face;
- griffin wings;
- beautiful human form;
- inflaming men with women’s love;
- naked exposure;
- sixty legions.

The surviving wording is compressed and, as printed by Skinner and Rankine, does not fully repeat the reciprocal “women with men’s love” phrase found in the other Goetia copies.

The defining innovation is not the demon’s prose description but the addition of an opposing Shem angel and a “double seal” intended to place that angel over the spirit. [Skinner and Rankine edition metadata](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556)

## Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**[Documented text]** Mathers and Crowley’s *Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, printed in 1904, gives the entry on printed p. 16. It opens “The Twelfth Spirit is Sitri. He is a Great Prince” and regularizes the manuscript’s spelling and punctuation.

Its full substantive content is:

1. Sitri is the twelfth spirit and a great prince.
2. Initially he has a leopard’s head and a gryphon’s wings.
3. At the master’s command he assumes a very beautiful human shape.
4. He inflames men with women’s love and women with men’s love.
5. He causes them to show themselves naked if desired.
6. He governs sixty legions.
7. His seal is worn as a lamen before the operator.

[Mathers–Crowley 1904 facsimile](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu)  
[PDF scan](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf)  
[Sacred Texts transcription](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/index.htm)

**[Documented editorial history]** Mathers prepared the translation and manuscript collation; Crowley edited and published it and supplied material including his Enochian transliteration of the Preliminary Invocation. The result is an influential working edition, not a modern critical edition reporting every manuscript variant.

## Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon* returns to the manuscripts, principally Sloane 3825, and supplies comparative readings. His Sitri entry appears on p. 12. Footnote 26 records Weyer’s form **“Sytry, alias Bitru”** and Scot’s corresponding reading.

Peterson is consequently the most useful accessible starting point for distinguishing:

- Weyer from the Lemegeton;
- manuscript text from Mathers–Crowley’s normalization;
- early sigils from twentieth-century redrawings;
- source text from later correspondence systems.

[Peterson 2001 edition](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf)

## Offices Sitri does not possess in the early sources

**[Documented negative finding]** Sitri’s entries do not say that he:

- teaches languages;
- teaches sciences or liberal arts;
- discovers treasure;
- provides familiars;
- grants invisibility;
- transmutes metals;
- reconciles friends generally;
- gives authoritative answers about past, present, and future.

Those are offices of other Goetic spirits. Applying the whole catalogue’s possible functions to Sitri is a modern generalization.

The only stable Sitri offices are erotic attraction and sexual exposure. Revelation and mockery of women’s secrets are present in Beduch/Bamone, Weyer, Scot, and De Plancy but absent from the principal Goetia text. Destruction of fortifications and removal of rank belong specifically to the earlier **Bitur** entry.

# Rank, planet, metal, and timing

## Prince: Jupiter and tin

**[Documented Goetic rubric]** The Lemegeton’s general instructions associate ranks with metals:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or “prelate” | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| Earl/Count | Moon and Venus | Silver and copper in equal parts |

Sitri’s seal would therefore be made in **tin** under the attribution of **Jupiter**.

**[Later esoteric variant]** Crowley’s *Liber 777* regularizes the seven ranks into a more systematic planetary ladder. In that arrangement earls become Mars/iron, while princes remain Jupiter/tin. Sitri’s own rank correspondence is therefore stable even though the system as a whole differs.

## Time of day

**[Documented Goetic rubric]** The Goetia says prelates may be bound “in any hour of the day.” Since Sitri is a prince and the rubric treats that category among prelates, the source-derived time is daylight, not a unique planetary hour.

The same preliminary instructions recommend work when the Moon is two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, or fourteen days old. This is a general Goetic election, not a Sitri-specific date.

**[Later tradition]** Lenain assigns Hahaiah the interval **3:40–4:00**, obtained by dividing the day into seventy-two twenty-minute periods. That is the angel’s later daily interval, not an hour stated in Sitri’s early entry.

**[Modern synthesis]** Runyon and later tables place Sitri in the last five degrees of Taurus and sometimes convert this to approximately May 15–19 or May 16–20. This comes from identifying demon no. 12 with angelic quinance no. 12. It is not the same as Crowley’s Cancer attribution.

# The seal

## Manuscript form

**[Documented image]** Sitri’s Lemegeton sigil is an abstract linear figure. Its stable elements are:

- a central horizontal bar;
- three short upright stems or posts above the bar;
- small circular terminals or nodes;
- a broad, cup-like or U-shaped lower curve;
- a descent toward a central circular terminal.

[Peterson’s Sitri seal image](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g12.jpg)

“Exactly” describing a hand-drawn sigil is necessarily limited: the sources preserve drawings, not prose instructions governing stroke order, dimensions, or orientation.

## Variants

- **Sloane manuscripts:** proportions, the curvature of the lower loop, placement of the small circles, and lengths of the uprights vary with the copyist.
- **Harley 6483:** the demonic sigil is incorporated into Rudd’s double-seal apparatus with the constraining angel’s name, seal, and psalmic material.
- **Mathers–Crowley:** the seal is redrawn more symmetrically and placed in a circular presentation with the letters of SITRI arranged around it. That circular name-border is part of the printed presentation, not an invariant feature of the earliest manuscript drawing.
- **Modern vector forms:** frequently regularize nodes and curves, producing a clean logo that may conceal the variability of manuscript hands.

A Wikimedia file titled “Sitri seal” has metadata identifying its contents as Beleth’s seal. This is a modern cataloguing error and illustrates why filenames should not replace manuscript comparison. [Correct 1904 Sitri seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:12-Sitri_seal.png) [Mislabeled modern file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sitri_seal_1134x1134.jpg)

## How early is the seal?

**[Documented negative finding]** The Sitri sigil does not occur in Weyer, Scot, the *Livre des esperitz*, or the Florentine Beduch/Bamone entry. It belongs to the surviving Lemegeton manuscript tradition. Claims that it is ancient, Egyptian, or attached to Bitur in a medieval source have not been demonstrated.

## The seventy-two names from Exodus

**[Jewish tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three consecutive Hebrew verses conventionally counted as seventy-two letters each. The standard construction is:

1. write the first verse in its normal direction;
2. write the second in reverse;
3. write the third in its normal direction;
4. read the resulting seventy-two vertical triplets.

Rashi’s commentary on *Sukkah* 45a explicitly describes the alternating forward–backward–forward procedure. [Rashi on *Sukkah* 45a](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Sukkah.45)

The twelfth triplet is conventionally read **ההע**. Adding the divine suffix **יה** produces **ההעיה**, Hahaiah.

**[Important distinction]** The biblical verses do not themselves name seventy-two angels, assign them five zodiacal degrees, give individual psalms, or pair them with Goetic spirits. Those are subsequent Kabbalistic and Christian-occult developments.

## Sefer Raziel and medieval Kabbalah

**[Tradition]** Materials transmitted under the title *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* preserve traditions of divine names, angelic names, amulets, and the seventy-two-fold Name. Its textual strata are medieval and composite.

**[Disputed attribution]** Later occult works sometimes present the complete modern table—angel name, moral office, zodiac degrees, psalm, dates, and contrary demon—as though it appeared in an ancient, unitary *Sefer Raziel*. That overstates the evidence. The older Jewish materials transmit the divine name and angelic techniques; the standardized European tables grew through several later stages.

## Reuchlin

**[Documented historical development]** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped Christian Kabbalists circulate the seventy-two triplets as pronounceable angel names by adding divine endings such as **-el** and **-iah**. This is a major bridge between Jewish name traditions and later ceremonial-magic tables. [Reuchlin edition](https://archive.org/details/onartofkabbalahd0000reuc)

## Agrippa

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa’s *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*, Book III, chapter 25, places the seventy-two names over seventy-two five-degree divisions of the zodiac. This supplies the conceptual foundation for the quinance system later used by Golden Dawn authors. [Agrippa, Book III](https://libraryofagartha.com/Occultism/%27Aryan%27%20Occultism/Hermeticism/Three%20Books%20of%20Occult%20Philosophy%20-%20Agrippa.pdf)

## Kircher

**[Documented esoteric compilation]** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) printed an influential table of the seventy-two divine names and angelic forms. Kircher’s work did not discover the sequence; it monumentalized Christian-Kabbalistic forms already in circulation and made them available for subsequent occult compilation. [Kircher’s Cabala material](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm) [Kircher’s table](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_72_Names_of_God_(Oedipus_Aegyptiacus,_1652%E2%80%9354).jpg)

## Rudd and Harley 6483

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** The material in Harley 6483 pairs each of the seventy-two Goetic spirits with the correspondingly numbered Shem angel:

- spirit 1 with angel 1;
- spirit 2 with angel 2;
- …
- Sitri 12 with Hahaiah 12.

This is a deliberately numbered scheme. It is not evidence that the Goetia’s anonymous compiler originally designed Sitri and Hahaiah as primordial opposites.

Rudd’s “double seal” places the angel’s name and psalmic authority over the demon’s sigil. Skinner and Rankine interpret the construction as a safety mechanism: the angel is invoked to bind, control, or neutralize the corresponding spirit.

The manuscript is a Peter Smart copy from 1712–1714, attributed through its exemplar to Thomas Rudd and dated retrospectively to 1655/56. “Rudd’s scheme” is appropriate shorthand; “Rudd’s autograph” is not. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine published the material as *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* in 2007. [Publisher catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf) [2010 edition metadata](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556)

## Psalm verse

Harley 6483’s Latin verse for Hahaiah is:

> “*Ut quid Domine recessisti longe…*”

Its sense is: Why, Lord, have you withdrawn far away; why do you disregard times of trouble?

This is **Psalm 9:22 in the Vulgate numbering** and **Psalm 10:1 in most modern English Bibles**.

**[Modern error]** Websites sometimes assign Hahaiah Psalm 110:1 or Matthew 22:44. That conflicts with the direct Rudd/Lenain line and is probably caused by table-shifting, numbering confusion, or copying an adjacent angel’s verse.

## Lenain, 1823

**[Documented esoteric system]** Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives angel 12 as **Hahaiah** and supplies:

- attribute: *Dieu refuge*, “God refuge”;
- Greek divine name: *Théos*;
- celestial ray: degrees 56–60;
- psalm: 9:22;
- daily interval: 3:40–4:00;
- five annual “days of influence” distributed through the year;
- offices involving dreams and hidden mysteries;
- virtues of wisdom, spirituality, and discretion;
- contrary qualities of indiscretion, lying, and abuse of trust.

[Lenain 1823 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf)

Degrees 56–60 on a zodiacal wheel beginning at 0° Aries equal **25°–30° Taurus**:

- Aries: degrees 1–30;
- Taurus: degrees 31–60;
- therefore 56–60 = the final five degrees of Taurus.

Lenain’s scattered annual days are not the same thing as assigning one uninterrupted five-day birthday period.

## Papus

**[Documented transmission]** Papus reproduced and popularized much of the French Christian-Kabbalistic angel table in *La Cabbale* and related works around the turn of the twentieth century. His presentation helped move Lenain’s material into modern occult manuals. [Papus, *La Cabbale*](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/abg405b2732165.pdf)

## The Golden Dawn

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Golden Dawn tables combined:

- the Shem angel sequence;
- five-degree zodiacal divisions;
- decans and planetary rulers;
- tarot pip cards;
- hierarchical angelic orders;
- psalm or divine-name material.

In this arrangement Hahaiah governs 25°–30° Taurus, the second or nocturnal half of the third Taurus decan. The whole decan, 20°–30° Taurus, corresponds to the **Seven of Pentacles/Disks**, Saturn in Taurus.

The first half, 20°–25°, belongs to angel 11, commonly Lauviah; the second half, 25°–30°, to Hahaiah. Hence one tarot pip card carries two Shem angels. [Regardie, *The Complete Golden Dawn*](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06Numerologia_Cabala/I.Regardie_Complete_Golden_Dawn_%28II%20ed.deluxe%29.pdf)

## Ambelain

**[Modern esoteric tradition]** Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* systematized the angelic names, seals, invocations, moral qualities, and practical uses for a twentieth-century French readership.

Ambelain later warned that some seals he had previously published were dangerous or represented contrary forces. This is an internal esoteric judgment, not historical evidence that the medieval Shem tradition possessed demonic counterseals. [Ambelain text](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf) [Bibliographical record](https://books.google.com/books/about/La_Kabbale_pratique.html?id=jF8ntAEACAAJ)

## Haziel

**[Modern devotional tradition]** The author writing as Haziel popularized the seventy-two angels through late-twentieth-century French books, cards, birthday calendars, prayers, and angelic psychology.

Haziel’s continuous five-day birth periods and personality descriptions are modern popularizations of older degree and calendrical schemes. They should not be attributed directly to Exodus, *Sefer Raziel*, or Rudd. [Haziel angel cards](https://www.editions-tredaniel.com/haziel-les-cartes-des-anges-p-4053.html)

## Crowley’s *777*

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Crowley maps the seventy-two Goetic spirits onto the thirty-six decans by assigning two spirits to every decan. The first is diurnal and the second nocturnal.

In this scheme:

- Cancer’s third decan is 20°–30° Cancer.
- Its tarot card is the **Four of Cups**, “Luxury.”
- Its planetary ruler is the **Moon in Cancer**.
- Sitri governs its first/day five degrees.
- Haagenti governs its second/night five degrees.

Crowley’s sequence therefore puts Sitri at approximately **20°–25° Cancer** if “first half of the decan” is counted literally, not 25°–30° Cancer. Some later tables reverse the halves or describe the quinance only as “third decan Cancer.” The primary table should be consulted for the edition’s column alignment. The decisive point is that Crowley’s demonic decan is **Cancer**, not Hahaiah’s Taurus.

[*Liber 777 Revised*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf)  
[DuQuette–Hyatt–Crowley, *Illustrated Goetia*, correspondence discussion](https://libraryofrickandria.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Aleister-Crowleys-Illustrated-Goetia-Aleister-Crowley-Christopher-S_-Hyatt-Lon-Milo-Duquette-Third-1992-2000-New-Falcon-Publications.pdf)

## Planet of rank versus planet of decan

Sitri can consequently have more than one planetary attribution without a textual contradiction, because the planets answer different questions:

- **Jupiter:** planet of the rank Prince.
- **Moon:** ruler of the Four of Cups/Cancer decan in Crowley’s demonic zodiac.
- **Saturn:** ruler of the Seven of Disks/Taurus decan containing Hahaiah.
- **Venus:** ruler of Taurus as a zodiacal sign.

Confusion arises when a modern chart prints one planet without identifying which layer it represents.

## Enochian overlays

**[Documented later synthesis]** Rudd-related manuscripts combine the Lemegeton with material derived from John Dee’s angelic or Enochian system, including versions of the *Tabula Sancta cum Tabulis Enochi*. Skinner and Rankine reproduce and discuss this material.

Crowley’s 1904 edition also prints an Enochian transliteration of the Preliminary Invocation. Neither fact establishes an ancient or manuscript-original “Enochian ruler of Sitri.” These are synthetic overlays joining formerly separate ritual corpora.

## Directions and elemental quarters

**[Disputed/later correspondence]** Different modern sources place Sitri under:

- one of the four kings or cardinal directions;
- a zodiacal element;
- a planetary hour;
- a particular color, incense, or candle;
- an archangel such as Auriel.

These depend on the chosen system. The individual early Sitri entry specifies none of them. Poke Runyon, for example, gives Sitri a Taurus quinance, Jupiter as rank planet, Ziminiar as directional king, and Auriel as archangel. That is Runyon’s coherent modern synthesis, not wording found in Weyer or the Lemegeton. [Runyon, *The Book of Solomon’s Magick*](https://attfinnasanningen.se/dokument_offentligt/books/mystery_school/magic/poke%20runyon%20book%20of%20solomons%20magick.pdf)

# Iconography

## Before the nineteenth century

The primary visual identity consisted of:

1. a prose image—leopard’s face or head and griffin’s wings;
2. the later Lemegeton seal.

No early manuscript miniature of Sitri has been identified in the source chain discussed here. The Florentine manuscript gives the hybrid appearance in words. Weyer and Scot do not supply a portrait.

## Collin de Plancy

### The text

**[Documented text]** The 1863 sixth edition of Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal*, p. 645, lists **“Sytry ou Bitru.”** It calls him a great prince of Hell, describes a leopard with griffin wings and a beautiful human form, and says he inflames passions and reveals women’s secrets in order to ridicule them.

Its most conspicuous alteration is: “*Soixante-dix légions lui obéissent*”—seventy legions obey him. Weyer and the Goetia say sixty. [De Plancy’s Sytry entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Sytry) [Facsimile page](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf/653)

The book’s separate infernal-monarchy list calls Sytry a **duke**, conflicting with the dictionary entry’s prince and with Weyer. [“Monarchie infernale”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Monarchie_infernale)

### Is there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**[Documented negative finding]** No individual Louis Le Breton portrait accompanies the 1863 Sytry entry. The page contains text but no Sitri engraving.

The sixth edition is famous for Breton’s demon portraits, but not every listed demon received one. Modern images labeled “de Plancy’s Sitri” may be:

- another Breton demon;
- a later artist’s interpretation;
- fan art;
- generative imagery;
- or the 1904 sigil presented as a portrait.

The 1818 first edition predates the Breton illustration series. The exact wording quoted above is verified specifically in the 1863 sixth edition; it should not automatically be projected backward to 1818 without checking the relevant first-edition page. [1818 volume scan](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal-1818-T2.djvu) [1863 complete scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e0/Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf)

## Twentieth- and twenty-first-century art

**[Modern invention]** Modern artists usually elaborate the minimal textual description by adding:

- a fully leopard-headed humanoid;
- feathered or bat-like gryphon wings;
- erotic or androgynous beauty;
- princely crowns and Jovian colors;
- feline claws or tails;
- occult circles incorporating the seal.

These details are interpretive unless present in the specific artwork’s source. The early texts say nothing about clothing, crown, skin color, weapons, tail, sex of the human form, or the color of the wings.

The illustrations in Crowley/Hyatt/DuQuette’s *Illustrated Goetia* are modern artistic and magical interpretations, not reproductions of medieval Sitri portraits.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer supplies the first securely dated **Sytry** entry and the alias **Bitru**. His larger project opposed witch persecution and interpreted much alleged witchcraft through fraud, illness, and demonic deception. Later magicians detached the catalogue from this skeptical framework.

## Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot’s *Discoverie* transmits the first securely dated English **Sitri** spelling. The work attacks witchcraft prosecutions and exposes conjuring literature. Abraham Fleming is often credited with the Weyer translation, although the passage is conventionally cited under Scot’s name.

## The anonymous Lemegeton compiler or compilers

The seventeenth-century compiler:

- moved Sitri to no. 12;
- preserved the sixty legions;
- abridged the offices;
- provided or transmitted the sigil;
- embedded the entry within a complete Solomonic conjuration system.

No evidence identifies the compiler with Solomon, Weyer, Rudd, or any known medieval author.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Rudd is traditionally credited with matching the seventy-two spirits to the seventy-two Shem angels. Peter Smart is the identifiable copyist of Harley 6483. That distinction matters because textual features cannot automatically be treated as Rudd’s personal wording.

## Mathers and Crowley

Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers translated and collated the Goetia material; Aleister Crowley edited and published the 1904 volume, adding interpretive and Enochian material. Their typography and sigil redrawing became the visual standard for many later editions.

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy preserves the Weyer-style secrets clause but changes the legion count to seventy. Breton illustrated many demons in the 1863 edition but apparently not Sitri.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition made manuscript readings, sigils, and Weyer/Scot comparisons widely accessible. His work is especially important because many internet summaries silently combine Weyer, Mathers–Crowley, Rudd, and twentieth-century tables.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* brought the Harley 6483 angelic-control recension into modern print and argued for the functional importance of the angel–demon double seals.

## Poke Runyon

Runyon developed a practical modern system employing dramatic evocation, mirror techniques, astrological assignments, directional kings, and the Shem angel pairings. His Sitri–Hahaiah–Taurus table is influential but should be labeled a twentieth-century synthesis.

## Lon Milo DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt

Their *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* combines Crowley’s text, modern commentary, autobiographical magical material, visual art, and the *777* decanic correspondences. It is a practitioner-oriented interpretation rather than a critical manuscript edition.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent emphasized the importance of earlier spirit catalogues, Weyer’s sources, the *Livre des esperitz*, the *Livre des conjurations*, and continental grimoire traditions. Proposed equations among similarly officered spirits in his and related practitioner scholarship can be suggestive but are not automatically textual identities. [Stratton-Kent, *The True Grimoire*](https://lib.wildla.in/book/Kent%2C%20Jake%20Stratton%20-%20The%20Trve%20Grimoire.pdf)

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No significant Sitri-specific operation, doctrine, or historical contribution by Frater Achad was located. His relevance is general—to Thelemic and Qabalistic number symbolism—not demonstrably specific to Sitri.

## Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Julien Véronèse, and Jean-Patrice Boudet

- Owen Davies provides broad social and publishing history for grimoires.
- Jan Veenstra’s work is relevant to medieval learned magic and spirits.
- Julien Véronèse studies ritual magic, Solomonic textual traditions, and the *Livre des esperitz* milieu.
- Boudet provides the most directly useful comparative demon-catalogue work for the Bitur/Sytry chain.

Among these, Boudet’s catalogue comparison is the most directly probative for Sitri’s ancestry. [Boudet article](https://doi.org/10.4000/medievales.1019)

# Grimoire ancestors and parallels

## *Testament of Solomon*

**[Documented negative finding]** No Sitri, Sytry, Bitru, or clearly corresponding leopard-and-griffin spirit appears under that name in the *Testament of Solomon*.

The *Testament* is nevertheless a major conceptual ancestor: Solomon interrogates demons, learns their functions and thwarting angels, and binds them through divine authority. Rudd’s numbered guardian angels resemble this broader logic but are not taken directly from a Sitri episode in the *Testament*.

## Munich Handbook, CLM 849

The fifteenth-century Munich Handbook contains necromantic operations, spirit names, circles, and coercive rites. It illustrates the ritual world from which later Solomonic manuals emerged.

**[Negative finding]** No securely identified Sitri/Bitur entry has been demonstrated in it. Calling it a general grimoire ancestor is justified; calling it Sitri’s direct textual source is not. [Kieckhefer, *Forbidden Rites*](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/0-271-01750-3.html)

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Catalogues called *Books of the Offices of Spirits* circulated before and after Weyer. Weyer explicitly invokes such a source, and the Folger manuscript V.b.26 preserves one branch of the genre.

**[Disputed relationship]** The exact manuscript Weyer used has not been identified with the surviving Folger copy. Nor has a direct Sitri entry been established there. The title describes a family of texts, not necessarily one stable book. [Folger record](https://digitalcollections.folger.edu/bib228887-238418) [Transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/liber-officiorum/read/)

## *Book of Abramelin*

**[Documented negative finding]** Standard Abramelin demon lists do not furnish a secure Sitri, Sytry, or Bitru. The work belongs to the larger early-modern tradition of hierarchical spirit command but is not established as Sitri’s source.

## *Livre des esperitz*

This is the most important named precursor because it has **Bitur** and because Weyer calls Sytry “alias Bitru.” Even here, however, the full Sitri profile is closer to Beduch/Bamone than to the surviving Bitur entry.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is Sitri pre-Weyer?

The answer depends on what is meant:

- **The exact form Sytry:** securely attested in Weyer, 1577.
- **The exact form Sitri:** securely attested in Scot, 1584.
- **The alias Bitru/Bitur:** Bitur occurs in the *Livre des esperitz* before or around Weyer’s period.
- **The recognizable persona:** Beduch/Bamone preserves most of it in a manuscript partly dated 1494.
- **An ancient god named Sitri:** no evidence.

A claim that “Sitri first appears in 1577” is accurate for the principal name but incomplete for the textual persona. A claim that “Sitri is a medieval demon documented in 1494” overstates the evidence because the 1494 name is Beduch/Bamone.

## 2. Is Sitri Bitur?

**[Documented]** Weyer explicitly says Sytry is “alias Bitru,” so within Weyer’s catalogue they are one spirit.

**[Textual complication]** The earlier Bitur entry differs in rank details, legion count, appearance, and offices. Weyer or his source may have merged traditions. Modern sites that present Sitri and Bitru as entirely separate demons contradict Weyer; sites that claim every Bitur detail always belonged to Sitri flatten genuine textual change.

## 3. Beduch/Bamone versus Bitur

Beduch/Bamone is descriptively the strongest precursor. Bitur is nominally the strongest precursor. A lost intermediary could explain why Weyer joins the name of one with the profile of another.

Whether Beduch, Bamone, Bitur, and Dam were once independent spirits or scribal partitions of a circulating composite remains unresolved.

## 4. Prince, marquis, count, or duke?

- Beduch/Bamone: marquis.
- Bitur: marquis.
- Dam: count.
- Weyer: prince.
- Scot: prince.
- Goetia: prince.
- De Plancy dictionary entry: prince.
- De Plancy monarchy appendix: duke.

The stable Sitri rank from 1577 onward is **Prince**. The other ranks reveal ancestry or textual error, not alternative ranks simultaneously assigned by the primary Goetia.

## 5. Sixty or seventy legions?

- Beduch/Bamone: 20.
- Bitur: 36.
- Dam: 25.
- Weyer/Sitri tradition: 60.
- De Plancy 1863: 70.

No evidence establishes seventy as an earlier or more authentic Sitri count. It first appears here as a de Plancy alteration or error.

## 6. Does Sitri reveal secrets?

- Yes in Beduch/Bamone.
- Yes in Weyer.
- Yes in Scot, despite the “deteineth” error.
- No in the principal Sloane Goetia entry.
- No in Mathers–Crowley.
- Yes again in De Plancy, who follows Weyer.

Accordingly, “Sitri reveals women’s secrets” is historically documented but belongs to the Weyer branch rather than every Goetia recension.

## 7. What does “makes women naked” mean?

The sources are overtly sexual and gendered. Beduch/Bamone and Weyer describe exposure coupled with ridicule or lascivious conduct. The Goetia abbreviates this to causing “them” to show themselves naked if desired.

**[Later reinterpretation]** Psychological readings—exposing secrets, stripping away inhibition, unveiling truth—are modern symbolic interpretations. They may be meaningful within contemporary practice but do not erase the literal sexual wording of the source.

## 8. Is Sitri inherently male?

The texts assign the title Prince and use masculine pronouns. They say the imposed human form is very beautiful but do not explicitly specify its sex in every version.

Modern female, androgynous, or gender-fluid depictions are artistic or theological developments rather than textual impossibilities; the early source simply does not discuss the question in modern terms.

## 9. Is the leopard–griffin image symbolic of an ancient cult?

No source explains its symbolism. Interpretations such as leopard = lust, griffin = solar power, or hybrid body = Set are later readings. The imagery may simply belong to the conventional bestiary of spirit catalogues.

## 10. Is Hahaiah Sitri’s eternal opposite?

**[Documented early-modern esoteric scheme]** Rudd’s recension places angel 12 over spirit 12.

**[Disputed metaphysical extension]** Nothing in Exodus, Rashi, the medieval Shem tradition, Weyer, Scot, or the ordinary Sloane Goetia says that Hahaiah and Sitri are ontological opposites from creation. That is an inference from Rudd’s controlling apparatus.

## 11. Which zodiac sign belongs to Sitri?

There is no zodiac sign in the early entry.

Later answers vary:

- Taurus through numerical identification with Hahaiah or Runyon’s system.
- Cancer through Crowley’s *777* Goetic decans.
- Jupiter through rank.
- Moon through the Cancer tarot decan.

These are layered correspondences, not corrections of one historical datum.

## 12. Misprints that became doctrine

Notable examples include:

- Scot’s **“deteineth”** for Weyer’s *detegit*, “reveals.”
- Manuscript uncertainty between “if he” and “if it be desired.”
- De Plancy’s seventy legions.
- De Plancy’s separate list calling Sytry a duke.
- A modern Wikimedia filename labeling a Beleth seal as Sitri.
- Psalm-table shifts assigning Hahaiah an unrelated verse.
- Internet tables treating the Taurus angelic quinance and Cancer demonic quinance as though both came from *777*.

---

# Open questions

1. **What was Weyer’s exact manuscript source?**  
   His *Liber officiorum spirituum* has not been identified with certainty.

2. **How are Beduch/Bamone and Bitur related?**  
   The descriptive and nominal evidence point in different directions. A lost common source remains plausible.

3. **Was Dam directly involved?**  
   Dam’s secrets-and-nakedness office is suggestive, but Beduch/Bamone already combines those elements with the leopard and griffin.

4. **When was the surviving *Livre des esperitz* exemplar copied?**  
   Modern codicological work favors the mid-sixteenth century, but older datings continue to circulate.

5. **Where and when did Sitri’s seal first enter the tradition?**  
   It is present in seventeenth-century Lemegeton manuscripts but absent from identified earlier descriptions. The designer is unknown.

6. **Did Thomas Rudd personally create the angel pairings?**  
   Harley 6483 is a later copy. The attribution is historically important but does not provide autograph proof.

7. **Did the first 1818 *Dictionnaire infernal* already contain exactly the later Sytry notice?**  
   The sixth-edition text is verified. A page-specific collation with the first edition remains desirable.

8. **Is Crowley’s Sitri quinance the first or second half of Cancer III in every printing?**  
   Later charts sometimes reverse or misalign the day/night columns. Edition-specific checking is necessary.

9. **Does any earlier manuscript contain a transitional form such as Sitr, Sytri, Bittur, or Bedur?**  
   No such witness has yet resolved the name’s etymology.

10. **Does Sitri derive from an ancient divine name?**  
    No evidence presently supports the claim. Discovery would require an independently attested ancient or medieval name and a defensible chain of transmission, not phonetic resemblance alone.

---

# Conclusions

The fullest defensible reconstruction is that Sitri is not a fallen ancient god whose identity can presently be recovered. He is a catalogue spirit whose textual persona grew through late-medieval and early-modern manuscript transmission.

The oldest close profile is **Beduch/Bamone** in a Florentine manuscript associated with 1494. The oldest nominal precursor is **Bitur** in the *Livre des esperitz*. Weyer’s 1577 **Sytry alias Bitru** fuses the Bitur-like name with the Beduch/Bamone-like appearance and offices, promotes the spirit from marquis to prince, and gives him sixty legions. Scot changes or stabilizes the spelling as **Sitri** in 1584.

The seventeenth-century Ars Goetia moves him from Weyer’s no. 21 to no. 12, supplies his seal, and abridges the secrets-and-mockery material. Mathers and Crowley make that recension famous in 1904. Rudd’s recension assigns him the twelfth Shem angel, **Hahaiah**, whose authority and psalm are used to constrain him.

The later occult record contains several independent overlays:

- Jupiter and tin from Sitri’s princely rank;
- Hahaiah at 25°–30° Taurus and the Seven of Disks;
- Sitri in Crowley’s Cancer decan and the Four of Cups;
- Rudd’s numbered angelic opposition;
- Lenain’s psalm, qualities, dates, and daily interval;
- modern Runyon, demonolatry, and popular-angel systems.

The evidence supports all of these as historical traditions at their respective dates. It does not support collapsing them into one ancient, unchanged system.

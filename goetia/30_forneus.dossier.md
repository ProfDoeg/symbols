# Forneus: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statement:

- **[Documented text]** — explicitly present in a cited primary text or manuscript transcription.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a modern historical or philological inference based on source comparison.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim transmitted as religious, magical, or legendary doctrine, without implying historical or empirical truth.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a symbolic attribution created within a particular occult system.
- **[Disputed]** — a proposed identification or reading for which the evidence is conflicting or insufficient.
- **[Modern invention]** — an attribution absent from the early sources and first visible in recent occult writing, fiction, games, or fan culture.
- **[Negative finding]** — something expressly sought but not found in the surviving evidence consulted.

The words “angel,” “spirit,” “demon,” “conjuration,” and similar terms below describe what the sources say. They do not assert the independent existence or efficacy of those beings or rites.

They should not be silently combined.

## The name and its origins

### 1. The earliest possible ancestor: Florence, 1494

**[Documented manuscript]** A spirit list in Florence MS Plut. 89 sup. 38, dated January 1494, contains a fourth spirit transcribed by Joseph Peterson as **“Fameis vel Fionone.”** Jean-Patrice Boudet’s comparative table gives **“Fameis vel Fronone.”** It is described as a great marquis with twenty legions. Peterson places the relevant entry at fol. 263r; Boudet’s discussion uses a different foliation sequence for the tract. The discrepancy appears bibliographical rather than substantive. [Peterson’s Weyer apparatus](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [Laurenziana manuscript record and images](https://teca.bmlonline.it/ImageViewer/servlet/ImageViewer?idr=TECA0001170387)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Because *Fionone/Fronone* resembles *Forneus*, possesses the same rank, and occurs in a demonstrably related spirit-list tradition, it is a plausible textual ancestor. It is not, however, the exact name **Forneus**, and its legion count differs: 20 rather than 29.

**[Negative finding]** The 1494 sigils elsewhere in this manuscript do not match the later *Ars Goetia* seals. Consequently, neither the Forneus seal nor its later angelic reverse can presently be carried back to 1494.

### 2. First secure attestation: Weyer, 1577

**[Documented text]** The first secure occurrence of the exact spelling **Forneus** is §25 of Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Peterson’s source comparison correctly identifies it as Weyer §25. [Weyer text and apparatus](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Correction of a common error]** *De praestigiis* first appeared in 1563, but the *Pseudomonarchia* was added in 1577. Statements that “Forneus first appeared in Weyer in 1563” conflate the first edition of the parent book with the later appendix.

Weyer calls his source:

> *Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis…*

That is, a “Book of the Offices of Spirits,” also called *Empto. Salomonis*. He admits abridging or suppressing operative material so that the work could not readily be used. The exact age and contents of Weyer’s lost exemplar remain unknown. [Peterson’s introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### 3. Scot, 1584

**[Documented text]** Reginald Scot retained **Forneus** in Book XV, chapter 2 of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*. The catalogue is an English rendering of Weyer, probably made by Abraham Fleming, although the attribution to Fleming is Peterson’s reconstruction rather than a signed translation.

Scot omitted Weyer’s Pruflas/Bufas. Forneus is therefore:

- Weyer’s explicitly numbered §25;
- the 24th spirit actually appearing in Scot’s surviving sequence;
- no. 30 after the *Goetia* rearrangement.

Scot printed the material as part of an attack on credulity and witch persecution, not as an endorsement of the catalogue’s truth. The rhetorical context matters: the same book repeatedly characterizes conjuring inventories as imposture, vanity, and evidence of human deception. [1584 scan](https://archive.org/details/discouerieofwitc00scot), [1886 diplomatic reprint](https://archive.org/details/discoverieofwitc00scot)

This results from counting the Weyer-derived sequence after Pruflas has effectively been removed. The raw 1577 text and Peterson’s longer comparison table establish that the original printed number is 25. It is an editorial numbering inconsistency, not evidence for two Weyerian orders.

### 5. Etymology

No historical source in the chain supplies an etymology.

#### *Fornus/furnus*, “oven” or “furnace”

**[Disputed modern claim]** Modern encyclopedias repeatedly derive *Forneus* from Latin *fornus* or *furnus*, “oven/furnace.” *Furnus* is a genuine Latin word, but no consulted premodern source makes the connection; neither the aquatic appearance nor the offices of rhetoric, reputation, and languages explain it. The proposal seems to have circulated through modern demonological lists and Wikipedia-derived summaries rather than through Weyer or a manuscript gloss.

Its evidential value is therefore low: phonetic resemblance without an attested historical bridge.

#### *Forensis*, “of the forum”

**[Modern conjecture]** Latin *forensis* means “of the forum,” hence public, legal, or forensic. Because Forneus grants rhetorical ability and reputation, it is semantically attractive. No manuscript spelling, gloss, or early commentator derives the name from *forensis*, so this remains an ingenious modern conjecture.

#### Proteus

**[Modern invention/disputed]** Websites sometimes identify Forneus with the Greek sea god Proteus because Proteus is marine and shape-changing. The comparison is iconographically intelligible but historically unsupported:

- *Proteus* is not an alias in Weyer, Scot, the *Lemegeton*, Rudd, de Plancy, or Mathers–Crowley.
- Forneus is not assigned prophecy or voluntary shape-changing.
- No phonological transmission from *Proteus* to *Forneus* has been demonstrated.
- The “black-haired human form” associated with this identification is also absent from the early catalogue.

The claim appears to be a recent mythographic overlay, not an ancient survival.

#### Phoroneus

**[Legend/modern personal gnosis]** A second proposal identifies the name with Phoroneus, the Argive culture hero sometimes associated with primordial kingship and the acquisition of fire. This occurs principally in recent practitioner discussion. It lacks manuscript, phonological, and historical evidence.

#### Eureur

**[Negative finding]** *Eureur* is now repeated as an alias on Portuguese and English occult sites, but no occurrence was found in the primary source chain. Its origin may be a modern compilation error, translation artifact, or unreferenced practitioner tradition.

### Conclusion on origin

**[Scholarly conclusion]** The best current reconstruction is textual rather than mythological:

> a corrupted late-medieval spirit name—possibly represented by *Fameis vel Fionone/Fronone* in 1494—entered Weyer’s lost *Offices of Spirits* exemplar and was stabilized in print as **Forneus** in 1577.

There is no demonstrated Baal-, Astarte-, Amun-, Ashmedai-, Belial-, Phoenix-, Proteus-, or Phoroneus-type ancient deity behind the name.

For comparison, the title *goetia* does possess a genuine Greek etymology: Greek γοητεία, *goēteia*, “sorcery/enchantment,” related to γόης, *goēs*, an enchanter or charlatan and traditionally connected with ritual lamentation. That etymology belongs to the genre-name, not to *Forneus*. [Etymonline, “goetia”](https://www.etymonline.com/word/goetia)

### Weyer’s Latin

Johann Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, fifth edition, Basel: ex officina Oporiniana, 1577, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* §25, col. 920:

> “Forneus magnus Marchio, similis monstro marino, reddit hominem in Rhetoricis admirabilem…”

The remainder says that he ornaments a person with an excellent reputation and skill in languages, makes that person pleasing to friends and enemies, and commands twenty-nine legions drawn partly from Thrones and partly from Angels. [Latin and aligned English text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [1577 bibliographic record](https://iucat.iu.edu/iupuc/3154182)

Important lexical points:

- *magnus Marchio* — great marquis;
- *similis monstro marino* — like or resembling a sea monster, not necessarily “is literally a whale”;
- *in Rhetoricis admirabilem* — admirable or remarkable in rhetoric;
- *optima fama* — excellent reputation or very good public name;
- *linguarum peritia* — skill or expertise in languages;
- *amicis … inimicis gratum* — pleasing or acceptable to friends and enemies.

**[Negative finding]** Weyer gives no seal, no human second form, no treasure-finding, no familiars, no invisibility, no transmutation, no healing, and no promise of answers about past, present, or future.

### Scot’s English

Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London: William Brome, 1584, Book XV, ch. 2; reprinted by Brinsley Nicholson, London: Elliot Stock, 1886, p. 319:

> “Forneus is a great marquesse, like unto a monster of the sea…”

Scot continues that he makes people wonderful in rhetoric, adorns a person with a good name and knowledge of tongues, and makes one beloved by foes as well as friends. Twenty-nine legions serve him, partly from Thrones and partly from Angels. [Scot transcription beside Weyer](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm), [1886 page-context OCR](https://archive.org/stream/discoverieofwitc00scot/discoverieofwitc00scot_djvu.txt)

The early-modern spelling is worth preserving:

- *marquesse*
- *woondeffull/woon­derfull*
- *rhetorike*
- *toongs*
- *freends*

Scot’s “beloved” is a slightly more affective English rendering of Weyer’s *gratum*, “pleasing, welcome, acceptable.”

### Sloane 3825 and Peterson’s base text

Sloane MS 3825, fol. 104v, is Peterson’s principal witness. It begins:

> “The 30th spirit is called Forneus, he is a mighty great Marquiz…”

It then gives the great sea-monster form, rhetoric, a good name, understanding of tongues, love from foes and friends, twenty-nine legions, and the two former angelic orders. It concludes that the seal is to be made and worn as previously instructed. [Peterson’s manuscript-based *Goetia*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

Peterson regards Sloane 3825 as the most accurate surviving *Lemegeton* manuscript when checked against identifiable sources. His 2001 printed edition reproduces the entry on p. 20. [Peterson, *The Lesser Key of Solomon*](https://books.google.com/books?id=29i7GgAACAAJ)

### Sloane 2731 and 3648

**[Documented manuscript comparison]**

- Sloane 2731: **Forneus**, no. 30.
- Sloane 3648: **Forneus**, no. 30.
- Sloane 3825: **Forneus**, no. 30.

Their catalogue descriptions do not materially change Forneus’s rank, appearance, offices, orders, or legion count. Differences lie chiefly in spelling, modernization, punctuation, and the precise hand-drawn seal. [Peterson’s spelling table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf)

Peterson dates Sloane 3825 to the seventeenth century and Sloane 3648 to approximately 1665 or later. Sloane 2731 is a careful composite drawing upon more than one version; its old catalogue attribution to 1572 cannot stand because the work incorporates later seventeenth-century printed sources. [Peterson’s *Lemegeton* introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

### Harley 6483: Rudd’s recension

Harley MS 6483 reads **Forners**, probably a scribal alteration. Skinner and Rankine transcribe the opening:

> “The thirtieth spirit is Forners … a great and mighty Marquis…”

The remainder agrees closely with the ordinary *Goetia*: great sea monster, rhetoric, good name, languages, love from foes and friends, twenty-nine legions, Thrones and Angels. Skinner and Rankine note that Sloane 2731, 3648, and 3825 read *Forneus*. *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*, London/Singapore: Golden Hoard Press, 2007, p. 132. [Searchable edition excerpt](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

Harley 6483 adds the angelic counterseal and verse discussed below. It is internally associated with the dates 1712–13 and appears to have been copied or assembled by Peter Smart. The attribution of its underlying scheme to the earlier magician Thomas Rudd is traditional and editorially plausible, but not a signed authorial certainty.

### Mathers–Crowley, 1904

S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s transcription, edited and published by Aleister Crowley as *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* in 1904, p. 31, begins:

> “The Thirtieth Spirit is Forneus. He is a Mighty and Great Marquis…”

The entry gives the “Great Sea-Monster,” wonderful knowledge in rhetoric, a good name, knowledge and understanding of tongues, love from foes and friends, twenty-nine legions, and the orders of Thrones and Angels. The seal is to be worn. [Sacred Texts transcription with printed pagination](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm), [1904 edition index](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu), [Hermetic.com entry](https://hermetic.com/norton/classics/goetia/forneus)

This is not a new description invented by Crowley; it is a normalized edition of the seventeenth-century text ultimately dependent on Scot and Weyer.

### What Forneus does—and does not do

**[Documented offices]**

- teaches rhetoric;
- makes a person exceptionally knowledgeable or capable in rhetoric;
- gives a “good name,” meaning reputation, public esteem, or social credit;
- grants knowledge or understanding of languages;
- makes one pleasing or beloved to enemies as well as friends.

**[Not in the early record]**

- treasure discovery;
- recovery of lost objects;
- familiars;
- invisibility;
- longevity;
- metal transmutation;
- answers about past, present, and future;
- prophecy;
- divination;
- healing;
- protection from other demons;
- politics or commerce;
- sea travel;
- control of storms;
- reconciliation rites as a separately named office;
- “all arts and sciences” generally.

Several of those powers belong to neighbouring spirits such as Ronove, Foras, Gaap, or Focalor. Their modern transfer to Forneus is usually expansion by association or catalogue conflation.

### Appearance and demanded form

**[Documented text]** The individual entry specifies only a sea monster. It supplies no second form.

**[General *Goetia* ritual rule]** The operator’s hexagram is described as compelling spirits to obedience and to assume a human shape. This is a general apparatus, not a statement that Forneus possesses a canonical black-haired man, winged deer, shark-man, or dragon form. [Peterson’s ritual figures and notes](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

**[Modern invention]** Whale-shark hybrids, a human torso emerging from a fish, a winged deer, a dark-cloaked man, or a leviathan with arms are modern artistic elaborations.

### Rank, metal, planet, and hours

The *Goetia*’s “Observations” state:

- marquises’ seals are to be made in **silver**;
- marquises may be bound from **3 p.m. to 9 p.m.**, and again from **9 p.m. until sunrise**;
- favoured lunar ages are the Moon’s 2nd, 4th, 6th, 8th, 10th, 12th, and 14th days.

[Peterson’s “Observations”](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Silver signifies the Moon. Crowley’s *777* accordingly prints the lunar glyph beside Forneus because the spirit is a marquis. It does not mean that the original Forneus paragraph explicitly called him “lunar.”

**[Negative finding]** No unique weekday or planetary hour appears in Forneus’s catalogue entry. Monday, a lunar hour, a waxing Moon, or other refinements are later practitioner deductions from the rank/metal table.

## The number and the order problem

### Weyer’s 69 and the *Goetia*’s 72

**[Documented text]** Weyer prints 69 catalogue entries, although the Belial narrative within the work speaks of Solomon imprisoning 72 kings.

**[Documented comparison]** The *Ars Goetia* obtains 72 as follows:

- 69 Weyerian entries;
- minus **Pruflas/Bufas**, absent from Scot and the *Goetia*;
- plus **Vassago**, **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius**.

Thus: 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

[Peterson’s Weyer–Goetia comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

Forneus moves from Weyer §25 to *Goetia* no. 30 because the whole middle order was rearranged. Peterson remarks that the change resembles a disturbed stack of separate leaves, but this is an explanatory hypothesis, not a proven event.

### Immediate neighbours

- Weyer: **Bune – Forneus – Roneve – Berith – Astaroth – Forras**.
- Scot: the same local sequence, but Pruflas has disappeared earlier.
- *Goetia*: **Ronove – Berith – Astaroth – Forneus – Foras – Asmoday**.

The description remains stable even as its position changes.

## The angel: Omael

## 1. The 72-letter or 72-part Name

**[Documented biblical basis]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the Masoretic Hebrew text, of three verses conventionally counted as 72 letters each. The later construction writes:

1. verse 19 in ordinary order;
2. verse 20 in reverse order;
3. verse 21 in ordinary order;

then reads the resulting columns as 72 trigrams. This boustrophedon arrangement is the foundation of the later 72-name system. [Exodus 14:19–21 in Hebrew](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21)

**[Historical distinction]** Hebrew *Shem ha-meforash*—שם המפורש—originally meant the “explicit,” “distinguished,” or fully pronounced divine Name, especially the Tetragrammaton. It did not originally mean “72 guardian angels opposed one-for-one to the Goetic demons.”

### Medieval Jewish and Latin transmission

**[Tradition/documented textual history]**

- The 72-fold Name is discussed in medieval Jewish mystical literature, including the *Sefer ha-Bahir* and traditions incorporated into *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*.
- A Latin *Liber Semamphoras* or Latin Raziel tradition was known in thirteenth-century Europe; Roger Bacon refers to such material.
- Medieval use concerns divine names, angels, cosmology, and talismanic practice—not the later *Ars Goetia* list.

### Reuchlin, 1517

**[Documented Christian Kabbalah]** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) presents the 72 trigrams as angelic names by adding **-El** or **-Yah**. Reuchlin explains *Yah* through divine beneficence and *El* through divine strength or power. [Daniel J. Lasker and Johannes Niehoff-Panagiotidis et al., *72 in His Name*](https://e-univers.ru/upload/iblock/919/1d14cc5s1qdrw3vc1f1360nmgkaohtru.pdf), [1517 digitization](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105)

The thirtieth trigram is:

- א — aleph;
- ו — vav;
- מ — mem;

forming **אום**, conventionally vocalized *Oma/Aum/Ovm*. With אל, *El*, it becomes **אומאל**, *Omael*.

**[Correction]** Modern sites sometimes print the first letter as ayin, yielding forms such as עמא or עומ. That is not the ordinary no. 30 trigram generated from Exodus 14 in the standard sequence.

### Vigenère and Kircher

**[Documented early-modern transmission]** Blaise de Vigenère transmitted a Christian-kabbalistic list of the 72 names after Reuchlin. Athanasius Kircher treated the 72-fold Name and related angelic/talismanic diagrams in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (Rome, 1652–54). Kircher described such amulets while often criticizing their magical use; citation by Kircher is not endorsement. [Kircher digitizations](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp67641), [Kircher’s discussion of a 72-letter amulet](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm)

Neither Reuchlin nor Kircher pairs Omael with Forneus. That one-to-one opposition belongs to Rudd’s later synthesis.

## 2. Rudd’s constraining scheme

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** Harley 6483 couples each of the 72 Goetic spirits with the correspondingly numbered Shem angel. Thus no. 30 Forneus/Forners is opposed or constrained by no. 30 **Omael**.

The lamen is double-sided:

- the spirit’s seal faces outward;
- the corresponding angelic name and seal face inward, toward the operator’s heart;
- a selected Psalm verse invokes divine authority.

Skinner and Rankine interpret this as the missing protective balance in the ordinary *Goetia*: the angel controls or binds the demon. That interpretation accurately describes Harley 6483’s design, although its attribution to Thomas Rudd remains historical rather than autograph certainty. [Skinner and Rankine, *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

### Rudd’s verse for Omael/Forneus

Harley 6483 gives:

> “Quoniam tu es potentia mea Domine. Domine spes mea a iuventute mea…”

Skinner and Rankine translate it as “For Thou art my strength, O Lord … my hope from my youth,” and cite **Psalm 70:6** in Vulgate numbering.

There is a textual complication:

- Rudd: **potentia mea**, “my strength/power”;
- Lenain: **patientia mea**, “my patience”;
- common Vulgate forms: **expectatio mea**, “my expectation/hope”;
- Hebrew/modern English numbering: **Psalm 71:5**.

The Hebrew verse is:

> כִּי־אַתָּה תִקְוָתִי אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה מִבְטַחִי מִנְּעוּרָי

Meaning approximately: “For you are my hope, Lord YHWH, my confidence from my youth.” [Psalm 71:5](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.71.5)

The “strength,” “patience,” and “hope” readings therefore reflect different Latin textual traditions or scribal substitutions. They should not be treated as identical wording.

## 3. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* supplies the form in which much modern French angelology circulates. Printed p. 65 calls the thirtieth angel:

> “Omael. Son attribut (Dieu patient.)”

Lenain assigns:

- ray: the **146th through 150th degrees** of the zodiac;
- ordinary zodiacal conversion: **25°–30° Leo**;
- invocation time: **9:40–10:00 a.m.**;
- Psalm: Vulgate **70:6**, corresponding broadly to modern Psalm 71:5;
- choir: **Dominations/Dominions**;
- qualities: patience against sorrow and despair;
- governance of the animal kingdom, generation, multiplication, and perpetuation of species;
- influence over chemists, physicians, surgeons, anatomy, and medicine;
- contrary spirit: opposition to propagation and “monstrous phenomena.”

[Lenain 1823 scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365)

Lenain also associates each angel with five scattered annual days. The Omael entry has been read as 18 April, 29 June, 9 September, 20 November, and 31 January, although the scan/OCR makes the first date uncertain. These are not the same thing as the consecutive five solar days later used in “guardian angel by birthday” books.

**[Tradition, not lexical fact]** “Patient God” is an assigned divine attribute. It is not a transparent Hebrew translation of א־ו־ם.

## 4. Golden Dawn and Crowley

**[Esoteric correspondence]** In Golden Dawn tables, the 72 Shem angels are assigned in pairs to the 36 tarot decans, one by day and one by night. Omael occupies the latter half or night side of the Mars-in-Leo decan and consequently shares the **Seven of Wands** with Reiyel.

The Seven of Wands is:

- 20°–30° Leo;
- Mars in Leo;
- “Lord of Valour”;
- Reiyel associated with the first/day portion;
- Omael associated with the second/night portion in later quinance presentations.

Crowley’s *777* acknowledges that its Shem tables give a day and night angel for each pip. [*777* tables and notes](https://publicism.info/magic/full/thelema/777/index.html)

This is the origin of the concise modern attribution **Omael = 25°–30° Leo = Seven of Wands**. It is not stated in Exodus, *Sefer Raziel*, Weyer, or the ordinary *Ars Goetia*.

## 5. Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

### Papus

**[Documented reception]** Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced and popularized Lenain’s angelic material in *La Cabbale*, especially the enlarged early-twentieth-century editions. He also wrote a preface for the 1909 Dujols and Thomas reissue of Lenain. [Papus, *La Cabbale*](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf), [BnF record for the 1909 Lenain edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30789329z)

### Robert Ambelain

**[Modern esoteric development]** Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (Paris: Niclaus, 1951) reorganized the Shem angels into a practical magical system involving seals, prayers, zodiacal periods, choirs, and planetary correspondences. It is an influential twentieth-century synthesis, not an independent medieval witness. [Bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/La_Kabbale_pratique.html?id=jF8ntAEACAAJ)

### Haziel

**[Modern popular tradition]** “Haziel,” the pen name of François Bernard-Termès, popularized the 72 guardian angels in French books from the late twentieth century, including *Le Grand livre des invocations et des exhortations* (1990) and *Communiquer avec son ange gardien* (1995). His system emphasizes guardian angels determined by birth period, personal prayer, and threefold angelic guardianship. [Publisher record](https://www.editionsbussiere.com/product_info-products_id-41.html)

Haziel is important to modern reception but does not supply historical evidence for Rudd’s authorship, Lenain’s sources, or an ancient Forneus–Omael relationship.

### The direct *777* entry

Crowley’s row 26, column CLIX, gives:

> “Forneus — Sea monster.”

It places Goetic spirit no. 30 among the “Goetic Demons of Decans by Day (Cadent).” Key-scale row 26 is Capricorn. Hence:

- cadent or third decan of Capricorn;
- 20°–30° Capricorn;
- Sun in Capricorn;
- Four of Pentacles/Disks;
- day spirit Forneus;
- night counterpart no. 66, Cimeies/Kimaris.

[*777* table](https://publicism.info/magic/full/thelema/777/index.html), [Golden Dawn *Book T*: Four of Pentacles](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/mathers-and-felkin-golden-dawn-book-t-the-tarot-1888.pdf)

### Is the *777* attribution a quinance?

**[Disputed interpretation]** *777* explicitly labels spirits “by Day” and “by Night” and assigns both to the same ten-degree decan. It does not explicitly print:

- Forneus = 20°–25° Capricorn;
- Cimeies = 25°–30° Capricorn.

Later tables frequently convert the day/night pair into consecutive five-degree quinances. That produces the useful working value **20°–25° Capricorn**, but it is a reconstruction rather than a literal datum in Crowley’s table.

### Astrological timing in the traditions

A historically controlled summary is:

1. **Early *Goetia*** — lunar-age rule; marquis hours; silver lamen.
2. **Rudd** — angelic counterseal and Psalm; no securely fixed tropical zero-point is necessary to operate the number pairing.
3. **Lenain** — Omael’s 146°–150° ray and 9:40–10:00 a.m. angelic period.
4. **Golden Dawn/Crowley** — tarot decans, day/night pairs, planetary and Hebrew correspondences.
5. **Modern synthesis** — Monday, lunar planetary hour, waxing Moon, 25°–30° Leo for Omael, or 20°–25° Capricorn for Forneus.

Because these layers disagree, there is no single historically universal “correct election” for Forneus.

### Enochian overlays

**[Documented but general]** Crowley’s 1904 book includes generic Goetic conjurations rendered into the “Magical or Angelic Language,” meaning the Enochian language associated with John Dee and Edward Kelley.

**[Negative finding]** No distinct Enochian name, aethyr, governor, tablet square, or call uniquely assigned to Forneus was found in the early sources. Modern charts that give one are later integrations.

## Iconography

## 1. The sea monster

**[Documented text]** The entire early visual description is *monstrum marinum*—a sea monster. No species is specified.

Possible visualizations include a whale, great fish, shark, ray, sea serpent, or composite leviathan, but these are interpretations. The manuscripts do not specify:

- scales;
- fins;
- tentacles;
- wings;
- arms;
- human head;
- colour;
- size;
- sex or gender;
- clothing.

This openness explains the extreme variety of modern depictions.

## 2. The seals

### Weyer and Scot

**[Negative finding]** Neither Weyer nor Scot supplies Forneus with a sigil.

### Sloane manuscripts

**[Documented images]** The seals first appear in the *Lemegeton* manuscript tradition. Sloane 3825’s Forneus entry and figure occur around fol. 104v. Sloane 2731 and 3648 preserve the same basic emblem, with normal scribal differences in:

- curvature of the rounded compartments;
- the closure or openness of loops;
- lengths of cross-strokes;
- the angle of outer terminals;
- whether touching strokes visibly cross or merely meet.

The design is not described verbally by the manuscript, so any prose account is necessarily secondary to the facsimile.

### Harley 6483

**[Documented Rudd variant]** The demon seal belongs to a two-sided lamen: Forneus/Forners outward, Omael inward. This functional alteration is more important than minute calligraphic differences.

### Mathers–Crowley

**[Documented printed reception]** The 1904 seal is a clean redrawing of the manuscript emblem. A modern public-domain vector is available on Wikimedia Commons, but the vector itself dates from 2026 and is not an additional historical witness. [Forneus seal vector and provenance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:30-forneus-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg)

**[Negative finding]** No early source says what the lines “mean.” Claims that the seal depicts a whale, three mouths, the Moon’s phases, a marine skeleton, or the letters of the name are modern interpretations.

## 3. Collin de Plancy

The sixth edition of Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* (Paris: Henri Plon, 1863), p. 281, says:

> “Fornéus, marquis infernal, semblable à un monstre marin.”

It then states that he instructs a person in the highest affairs, benefits friends, harms enemies, and commands twenty-nine legions of Thrones and Angels. De Plancy cites Wier/Weyer. [1863 French entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Forn%C3%A9us), [Gallica scan](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5754923d), [Library of Congress record](https://www.loc.gov/item/63056292/)

This is not a faithful translation of Weyer:

- rhetoric becomes “the highest affairs”;
- languages and good reputation disappear;
- being pleasing to enemies becomes doing harm to enemies.

The last change may be mistranslation, moral normalization, or dependence on an intermediate summary.

### Is there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**[Negative finding]** No Le Breton engraving accompanies the Fornéus entry in the 1863 edition. Forneus is not one of the spirits given a recognizable Breton portrait there. Images circulated online as “de Plancy’s Forneus” are consequently misidentified unless their actual source is separately demonstrated.

The 1818 first edition of the *Dictionnaire infernal* was consulted through its index and scans, but the exact wording of a Forneus entry could not be securely verified from the incomplete transcription. The quotation above belongs specifically to 1863, not automatically to 1818.

## 4. Modern art

**[Modern invention]** Common modern forms include:

- whale–shark or manta-like monsters;
- fish or sea-serpent bodies with human torsos;
- dark-haired human magicians;
- winged marine dragons;
- Lovecraftian tentacled beings;
- luminous or mechanical rays;
- black cats, where fiction freely reuses only the name.

These images develop the two words “sea monster”; they are not recoveries of a lost Renaissance portrait.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

**[Documented history]** Weyer was a physician, former associate or student of Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, and an opponent of mass witch prosecution. His *Pseudomonarchia* printed Forneus while simultaneously treating ceremonial demonological literature as deception, impiety, and evidence relevant to his critique of witch-hunters.

His importance is textual: he supplied the first secure printed name and description.

### Reginald Scot and probably Abraham Fleming

Scot translated or commissioned the translation of Weyer’s catalogue in 1584. Peterson attributes the translation probably to Abraham Fleming on stylistic and bibliographic grounds. Scot’s book exposed conjuring literature in order to undermine magical credulity and witch persecution.

The later *Goetia* appears to depend particularly on the 1665 edition of Scot, because it reproduces spellings and errors peculiar to that edition. [Peterson’s source analysis](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

### Anonymous *Lemegeton* compilers

The *Lemegeton* is a composite seventeenth-century handbook. Its five books draw from different textual families, some medieval and some demonstrably dependent on mid-seventeenth-century print. The *Goetia* catalogue expands Scot’s Weyer-derived list, rearranges it, and adds seals and a much larger ritual apparatus.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

**[Tradition/disputed authorship]** Thomas Rudd is credited with restoring the 72 Shem angels as controls over the 72 Goetic spirits. Harley 6483 preserves this system, but Peter Smart appears to be responsible for the surviving early-eighteenth-century manuscript. Whether every innovation originated personally with Rudd cannot be proven.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Mathers made the English transcription underlying the 1904 *Book of the Goetia*. Crowley edited, introduced, commissioned or managed publication, added “The Initiated Interpretation of Ceremonial Magic,” an adapted preliminary invocation, and Enochian versions of general conjurations.

Crowley later situated Forneus astrologically in *777*. That table is often described as “Golden Dawn,” but exact authorship of each individual demon–decan placement is not documented. Crowley acknowledges extensive Golden Dawn material alongside his own research.

### Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No reliable Forneus-specific operation, diary entry, or doctrine by Charles Stansfeld Jones/Frater Achad was located. His Qabalistic and Thelemic importance does not by itself make him a documented Forneus practitioner.

### Carroll “Poke” Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* first appeared in 1996 and presents a modern operational Goetia, including the 72 spirits, mirror scrying, and a less hostile attitude toward some entities. The 2023 second edition reinstates stronger traditional conjurations and adds records and commentary. [Author/publisher description](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition)

No public source consulted establishes a Forneus-specific operation by Runyon; his relevance is to the modern working system as a whole.

### Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia: Sexual Evocation* appeared from New Falcon in 1992, with commentary by DuQuette and Hyatt and illustrations by Wilson. It reproduces the spirit list and interprets Goetic work psychologically and operationally. [WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/aleister-crowleys-illustrated-goetia-sexual-evocation/oclc/32357167), [Museum of Witchcraft catalogue](https://museumofwitchcraftandmagic.co.uk/library/aleister-crowleys-illustrated-goetia/)

Its imagery and psychological theories are twentieth-century interpretation, not manuscript evidence.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 *Lesser Key of Solomon* and continually updated Esoteric Archives editions are the most useful public collations of Sloane 3825, 2731, 3648, Harley 6483, Weyer, Scot, and later editions. His transcriptions make it possible to separate manuscript readings from Mathers’s normalization.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their *Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard Press, 2007) published Harley 6483’s double-seal system and the Forners/Omael/Psalm complex. They also discuss the multiple possible zodiacal starting points in angelic tables rather than presenting one arrangement as uncontested.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium: A Discordant Concordance of Diverse Spirit Catalogues* (Hadean Press, 2016) compares the *Livre des Esperitz*, *Book of Offices*, Weyer, Scot, the *Goetia*, and related catalogues. Its importance is methodological: Goetic spirits are treated as members of changing textual families rather than as a fixed list dropped intact from antiquity. [Hadean Press description](https://hadean.press/products/pandemonium)

No independently documented Forneus operation by Stratton-Kent was located.

### Modern historical scholarship

- Jean-Patrice Boudet provides the crucial Florence/Forneus comparison and analysis of the demonological catalogues.
- Richard Kieckhefer’s *Forbidden Rites* establishes the fifteenth-century Munich handbook context.
- Owen Davies’s *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* situates these works in the social history of magic.
- Julien Véronèse’s work on medieval ritual magic and Solomonic texts clarifies the broader manuscript environment.
- Jan R. Veenstra’s studies of courtly magic, spirits, and learned demonology illuminate the intellectual setting.

These scholars do not collectively assert that Forneus is an ancient god. Their work instead supports a layered late-medieval and early-modern textual history.

## Grimoire ancestors

### *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Negative finding]** The surviving Cambridge Trinity College MS O.8.29 catalogue contains numerous spirits related to Weyer but no secure Forneus/Fronone entry. Boudet counts 35 spirits shared with Weyer and 15 present in the *Livre* but absent from Weyer. [Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019), [manuscript transcription](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz)

It is therefore a close relative of the source family, not a direct attestation of exact-name Forneus.

### Munich Handbook, Clm 849

**[Documented contextual ancestor]** The fifteenth-century Munich handbook contains several recognizable ancestors of Weyerian spirits. It does not contain Forneus. One spirit, Sucax, is a great marquis who grants languages and love, making it a possible thematic analogue, but the name, appearance, offices, and legion count do not identify it as Forneus. [Peterson’s comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer explicitly says his source belonged to this “Offices of Spirits” family. Independent witnesses preserve overlapping lists, but the exact Forneus paragraph has not been identified in a securely earlier surviving copy. The 1494 *Fionone/Fronone* entry is presently the best lead.

### *Book of Abramelin*

**[Negative finding]** Abramelin contains extensive lists of subordinate spirits but no securely identifiable Forneus. Similar rank structures or shared major names such as Lucifer, Leviathan, Satan, Belial, Oriens, Paimon, Ariton, and Amaimon do not prove a Forneus connection.

### *Testament of Solomon*

**[Negative finding]** The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* is a major literary ancestor of the legend that Solomon interrogated and constrained demons. Its demon catalogue does not contain Forneus. [English text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Testament_of_Solomon)

The brass-vessel legend, numbered hierarchies, and later Goetic catalogue grew through long transmission; they should not be collapsed into a single ancient book.

The best-attested modern transformations select one of four features:

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Was Forneus first printed in 1563 or 1577?

**Resolution:** 1577. The parent work *De praestigiis daemonum* began in 1563; the *Pseudomonarchia* appendix first appears in the fifth edition of 1577.

### 2. Weyer no. 24 or no. 25?

**Resolution:** Weyer’s printed section is §25. Scot’s list becomes 24 entries deep at Forneus because Pruflas is missing. Modern comparison tables sometimes transfer that shifted number back into the Weyer column.

### 3. Is *Fionone/Fronone* really Forneus?

**Disputed.** Rank, source family, list position, and name-shape support the identification. Different spelling, twenty rather than twenty-nine legions, and the absence of the mature description prevent certainty.

### 4. Does Forneus occur in the *Livre des Esperitz*?

**Negative finding:** not under a securely equivalent name in the surviving list. The work is a catalogue relative, not a direct exact-name witness.

### 5. Does the name derive from a furnace?

**Disputed and probably folk etymology.** The Latin word exists; the historical bridge does not.

### 6. Is Forneus Proteus or Phoroneus?

**No documented source-chain support.** Both are modern mythographic identifications.

### 7. Is the human black-haired form traditional?

**No.** The specific form is modern.

### 8. Is Forneus benevolent?

The early catalogue says he makes a person acceptable to friends and enemies. It does not call him benevolent, protective, kind, or “one of the least evil demons.” Those are ethical interpretations by later readers.

### 9. Does he reconcile enemies or harm them?

- Weyer/Scot/*Goetia*: makes one pleasing or beloved to enemies.
- De Plancy 1863: does good to friends and harm to enemies.
- Modern occult practice: often reframes the office as reconciliation.

The first is the earliest and best-attested reading; the second is a nineteenth-century transformation; the third is a reasonable practical interpretation but not the original wording.

### 10. Does he teach every science?

No. The early text specifies rhetoric and languages. “All arts and sciences” is expansion or confusion with other spirits.

### 11. Does he answer questions or prophesy?

No such office appears in his early entry. The Proteus identification has encouraged prophecy claims, but those claims do not descend from Weyer.

### 12. Twenty or twenty-nine legions?

- possible 1494 precursor: 20;
- Weyer onward: 29.

This may reflect textual expansion, misreading, or a mistaken precursor identification. It cannot presently be resolved.

### 13. Forneus or Forners?

*Forners* is Harley 6483’s scribal reading. The agreement of Weyer, Scot, three principal Sloane witnesses, Northwestern MS 65, and Mathers supports **Forneus** as the dominant form.

### 14. Which zodiacal degrees are “correct”?

There are at least three answers because different tables ask different questions:

- Omael by Lenain/Rudd number: 25°–30° Leo;
- Forneus by *777*: 20°–30° Capricorn, by day;
- modern quinance division of *777*: commonly 20°–25° Capricorn.

They are system-specific, not competing observations of an objective ancient assignment.

### 15. Which tarot card belongs to Forneus?

- through Omael’s numbered quinance: Seven of Wands;
- through Crowley’s direct Goetic decan: Four of Pentacles/Disks.

Any source giving only one without explaining the mapping hides a real historical contradiction.

### 16. Does de Plancy portray him?

No verified Louis Le Breton portrait appears with the 1863 entry.

### 17. Is the seal ancient or medieval?

No. It is absent from Weyer and Scot, absent from the probable 1494 precursor in its later form, and first securely attested in seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

### 18. Are the seal’s lines letters or a picture of the monster?

The manuscripts do not say. Such readings are modern symbolic interpretation.

### 19. Is Omael an ancient Jewish guardian angel of Forneus?

No. The elements have different histories:

- Jewish 72-fold divine-name speculation is medieval;
- angelizing the trigrams is prominent in Renaissance Christian Kabbalah;
- number-for-number opposition to the *Goetia* is Rudd’s early-modern synthesis;
- birth-date guardian-angel practice is chiefly nineteenth- and twentieth-century popularization.

### 20. “Patientia,” “potentia,” or “expectatio”?

All occur in the later Psalm tradition:

- Lenain’s *patientia* underwrites “Patient God”;
- Rudd’s *potentia* underwrites “strength”;
- ordinary Vulgate *expectatio* agrees more closely with Hebrew “hope.”

This is a genuine textual fault line, not merely different English translation.

## Open questions

1. **What exactly does the Florence manuscript read?** High-resolution palaeographic re-examination is needed to decide between *Fionone*, *Fronone*, or another reading.

2. **Does a still-unedited *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* witness contain the mature Forneus paragraph?** Such a discovery could bridge 1494 and Weyer.

3. **How did 20 legions become 29?** Possibilities include scribal corruption, deliberate expansion, or mistaken identification of the Florence spirit.

4. **Who designed the *Lemegeton* seal?** No derivation or explanatory key has been identified.

5. **Why was the Weyer/Scot order rearranged?** Loose or displaced leaves are plausible, but no physical ancestor proving the event survives.

6. **Who added the four non-Weyerian spirits?** Vassago, Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius have not been traced to one demonstrated common source.

7. **Was the double-sided angel/demon lamen actually Thomas Rudd’s invention?** Harley 6483 preserves the doctrine, but the surviving copy is later and associated with Peter Smart.

8. **Which Psalm wording stood in Rudd’s immediate source?** *Potentia* may be intentional, a scribal substitution, or a corruption of *patientia* or *expectatio*.

9. **Did Lenain inherit the 146°–150° attribution from an identifiable manuscript?** His immediate sources are only partly exposed.

10. **When did “Forneus = Proteus” first appear?** It is absent from the principal historical sources and appears to be recent, but its exact first publication remains unidentified.

11. **When did *Eureur* enter modern lists?** No primary witness consulted explains it.

12. **Does the 1818 *Dictionnaire infernal* contain the same Forneus notice as 1863?** The available transcription is incomplete; the 1863 text should not be backdated without checking the printed page.

13. **Did Crowley or a Golden Dawn predecessor devise the demon–decan table?** *777* transmits it, but the individual authorship of the placements is uncertain.

14. **Should day/night demon pairs be treated as five-degree quinances?** Later tables commonly do so, but *777* itself assigns each to the decan “by day” or “by night,” not explicitly to consecutive halves.

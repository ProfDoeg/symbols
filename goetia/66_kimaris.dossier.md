# Kimaris: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes the following:

- **[Documented text]** — stated directly in a manuscript, early printed edition, or identifiable modern publication.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a historical relationship argued from wording, manuscript transmission, palaeography, or bibliography.
- **[Tradition]** — a religious or magical claim made by the tradition itself, without implying that the supernatural event is historically verifiable.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an occult attribution created by a particular system.
- **[Disputed]** — a proposed identification or derivation for which evidence is weak or contradictory.
- **[Legend]** — narrative presented within a source as sacred or magical history.
- **[Modern invention]** — a post-grimoire addition, adaptation, personal revelation, fictional use, or unsupported Internet accretion.

Because full web-hosted passages cannot be reproduced here at unrestricted length, the requested Weyer, Scot, and *Goetia* texts are represented by short exact excerpts followed by complete clause-by-clause summaries. Page, folio, and section references are supplied so the full public-domain texts can be checked directly.

### The medieval *Tvueries*

**[Documented text]** Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek Clm 849 is a fifteenth-century necromantic handbook. Its *Tvueries* is a great, strong marquis, appears as a knight on a black horse, teaches the trivium, reveals hidden things and treasures, and commands thirty legions. It also enables rapid passage across seas and rivers.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The conjunction of rank, black-mounted military appearance, trivium, treasures, and rapid motion makes accidental resemblance very unlikely. Peterson’s source table accordingly equates *Tvueries* with Weyer’s *Cimeries*. Jean-Patrice Boudet and Richard Kieckhefer print the medieval material in their respective studies.

**[Open palaeographical question]** It cannot yet be demonstrated whether:

1. *Tvueries* was corrupted into *Cimeries*;
2. both descend from a lost third form;
3. or *Tvueries* itself is already a corrupt copy.

The claim sometimes encountered online that “Tuvries is a mistranscription of Cymries” reverses the chronological evidence: *Tvueries* is earlier, while **Cymries** is a modern conjectural spelling.

### Is Cimeries derived from the Cimmerians?

**[Disputed]** Wade Baskin’s *Dictionary of Satanism* proposed the Cimmerians. Greek Κιμμέριοι, Latin *Cimmerii*, and Assyrian *Gimirri* designate an ancient people associated historically with the Pontic steppe and, in Homeric geography, with a land deprived of sunlight. The mounted warrior and “dark” association make the suggestion superficially attractive.

Evidence against treating it as established:

- No early grimoire identifies the spirit with the Cimmerians.
- Weyer offers no etymology.
- The medieval *Tvueries* weakens a derivation based solely on the later spelling *Cimeries*.
- The spirit’s African territory does not correspond to the historical Cimmerians.
- Similarity of sound does not establish a transmission route.

The Cimmerian derivation is therefore a **twentieth-century speculative etymology**, not an ancient identification. For the historical Cimmerians, see [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cimmerians-nomads/).

### Is the name derived from the Chimera?

**[Disputed/modern invention]** Another Internet-era proposal connects *Cimeries* with Greek χίμαιρα, Latin *Chimaera*. This is weaker than the Cimmerian proposal:

- The spellings do not follow an obvious historical development.
- Kimaris has no composite lion-goat-serpent appearance.
- Neither Weyer nor the *Goetia* connects him with Lycia, Bellerophon, fire-breathing, or the underworld.
- The comparison with the Goetic Phenex does not prove a similar derivation for Cimeries.

### “Khem-our,” Horus, and “black light”

**[Modern disputed occult speculation]** Some modern demon lists propose that Crowley’s Hebrew transliteration **KYMAVR** was intended to evoke *Khem-our*, glossed “black light,” and connect it with Horus or Blavatsky.

No statement by Crowley establishing this intention has been found. His *777* Hebrew is more economically explained as a Qabalistic transliteration of **Kimaris**, not as an Egyptian etymology. Claims that this proves Kimaris to be an ancient Egyptian god are unsupported.

### The Coptic “Akathama Chamaris” theory

**[Documented but unrelated text]** A late-antique Coptic magical text contains the sequence *Akathama Chamaris*. A German scholarly discussion translates a passage about a divine *Dynamis* whose name is given in this form. The relevant Coptic ritual tradition is represented by British Library Oriental MS 6796 and associated texts.

**[Disputed identification]** Modern demonological webpages have asserted that this is the earliest Kimaris. The proposed connection has no demonstrated textual bridge:

- *Chamaris* is only approximately similar in sound.
- The Coptic context and office do not resemble those of *Tvueries/Cimeries*.
- There is a gap of roughly nine centuries before the Munich handbook.
- *Akathama* is part of the magical name, not demonstrated to be Sanskrit “wordless.”
- Neither Meyer and Smith nor the manuscript is shown arguing that this being became the Goetic marquis.

It is therefore evidence for an ancient magical name **Chamaris**, not evidence that Kimaris was worshipped in Coptic Egypt.

### Cimeriel and the Enochian material

**[Documented separate name]** Rudd-associated material in Harley MS 6482 includes **Cimeriel** in an angelic or cosmological table associated with twenty-four mansions.

**[Disputed conflation]** Similarity between *Cimeriel* and *Cimeries* has encouraged claims that they are the same being, that Cimeriel “taught Enochian” to John Dee, or that *-el* proves “darkness of God.” No supporting etymology or identification appears in the *Goetia*. Cimeriel should be kept separate unless new manuscript evidence demonstrates otherwise.

### Conclusion on the name

**[Best-supported reconstruction]** Kimaris has no securely identifiable ancient god, hero, or place behind it. The defensible chain is:

> fifteenth-century **Tvueries** → 1577 **Cimeries** → seventeenth-century **Cimeies** → 1904 **Cimejes/Kimaris**

The exact-name form **Cimeries** is first securely attested in 1577, but the same spirit-profile is very probably present before Weyer as *Tvueries*.

## The list: Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s catalogue has sixty-nine spirits in modern editorial counting. The *Ars Goetia* has seventy-two and substantially rearranges the sequence. Cimeries moves from Weyer §60 to *Goetia* 66.

The arithmetic is:

- **Added:** Vassago, inserted as Goetia 3.
- **Added at the end:** Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius, Goetia 70–72.
- **Omitted:** Weyer’s Pruflas/Bufas.

Four additions minus one omission produce the net increase from sixty-nine to seventy-two. Peterson’s [Weyer comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm) documents the order.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Pruflas is already omitted in Scot’s English transmission. That shared omission, together with English verbal correspondences, is evidence that the *Goetia* compiler depended heavily on Scot’s Weyer rather than directly upon the Latin alone.

**[Esoteric interpretation]** The adjustment to seventy-two permitted the catalogue to be correlated with the seventy-two angelic names. Whether numerological completeness motivated all four additions cannot be proved, but the completed number made Rudd’s later one-to-one counter-angel scheme possible.

**[Numbering trap]** Because Scot omits Pruflas, Cimeries is the fifty-ninth surviving entry in Scot’s printed sequence even though modern tables label it “60” to preserve alignment with Weyer. Claims that Cimeries is “Weyer 59” usually count the surviving Scot entries rather than Weyer’s complete catalogue.

### Weyer, 1577

**Edition:** Johann Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, fifth edition, Basel, 1577, appendix *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, §60. The online diplomatic text is not assigned a stable original page number.

Short exact excerpt:

> “Cimeries magnus Marchio & fortis: imperans in partibus Africanis: docet perfectè Grammaticam, Logicam & Rhetoricam.”

**Gloss:** “Cimeries, a great and strong marquis, ruling in African regions, teaches grammar, logic, and rhetoric perfectly.”

The remainder states, in order, that he reveals treasures and opens hidden things; makes a man appear to be changed into a soldier with extreme speed; rides a great black horse; and presides over twenty legions. [Peterson’s Latin and aligned Scot text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

#### Points of interpretation

- **[Documented text]** *Thesauros detegit, & occulta aperit* concerns treasures and concealed things.
- **[Documented ambiguity]** *videatur transmutari in militem* literally makes the man “seem to be transformed into a soldier.” It does not unambiguously promise bodily transformation.
- **[Documented text]** The horse is *niger et grandis*: black and large/great.
- **[Negative finding]** Weyer supplies no seal.
- **[Negative finding]** Weyer supplies no second form, angel, zodiacal degree, tarot card, incense, color, or personal psalm for Cimeries.

### Scot, 1584

**Edition:** Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London, 1584, Book XVI, chapter 2, p. 225.

Short exact excerpt:

> “Cimeries is a great marquesse and a strong, ruling in the parts of Aphrica.”

Scot continues that he teaches grammar, logic, and rhetoric perfectly; discovers treasures and hidden things; causes a man to seem expeditiously turned into a soldier; rides a great black horse; and rules twenty legions. [Scot text and page transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Peterson notes that the translation may owe something to Abraham Fleming. Scot’s book attacks credulous witchcraft claims; reproducing the catalogue does not show that Scot practiced its conjurations.

**[Transmission detail]** Scot’s English “seeme with expedition to be turned into a soldier” closely follows Weyer’s language of appearance and speed. It does not say that Cimeries trains the operator in military science.

### Sloane MS 2731

**[Documented text]** Sloane 2731 calls him **Cimeies**, number 66: a great, mighty, strong, powerful marquis appearing as a valiant soldier riding a goodly black horse. He rules spirits in parts of Africa, teaches grammar, logic, and rhetoric, discovers lost or hidden things and treasures, and governs twenty chief spirits.

The accessible Sloane 2731 reading omits the explicit sentence about making a man seem like a soldier. See the [diplomatic manuscript transcription and facsimile](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/).

### Sloane MS 3825 and Peterson

**Edition:** Joseph H. Peterson, ed., *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis*, Weiser, 2001, pp. 36–37; online edition principally after Sloane MS 3825.

Short exact excerpt:

> “appearing like a valiant souldier, riding on a goodly Black horse”

The entry then assigns him dominion over spirits in Africa; the teaching of grammar, rhetoric, and logic; discovery of treasure and things lost or hidden; the ability to make a man appear like a soldier of his own likeness; twenty legions of chief but inferior spirits; and a seal to be worn as a lamen. [Peterson’s *Goetia* transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Textual details:

- Sloane 3825 reads singular “part of Africa”; Sloane 2731, 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses read “parts.”
- The word is **goodly**, modifying the black horse. “Godly warrior,” common on modern webpages, is a misreading.
- “Chief spirits, but more inferior than himself” distinguishes his subordinate commanders from Kimaris.
- The lamen is the inscribed seal worn by the operator; it is not a portrait.

### Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd edition

**Edition:** Stephen Skinner and David Rankine, eds., *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*, Golden Hoard Press, 2007, p. 167.

**[Documented text]** Harley 6483 reads **Cimeries** and gives substantially the Sloane 3825 account: mighty great marquis; valiant soldier on a goodly black horse; authority in Africa; grammar, logic, rhetoric; treasure and lost or hidden things; making a man seem like a soldier in his own likeness; twenty legions.

**[Documented manuscript history]** Harley 6483 is dated 1712–13 and contains additions absent from the main Sloane witnesses.

**[Disputed attribution]** Skinner and Rankine argue that the manuscript preserves material owned or developed by the seventeenth-century magician Thomas Rudd. It is not an autograph manuscript demonstrably written by Rudd, and its copy date is later than his death. “Rudd’s scheme” is therefore a useful conventional label, not proof that every stroke originated with the historical Thomas Rudd.

### Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**Edition:** S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, Boleskine, 1904, p. 43, figure 115.

Short exact excerpt:

> “CIMEJES, or CIMEIES, or KIMARIS.—The Sixty-sixth Spirit”

The printed entry describes a mighty, great, strong, powerful marquis, appearing as a valiant warrior on a goodly black horse; ruling spirits in parts of Africa; teaching grammar, logic, and rhetoric; discovering lost or hidden things and treasures; commanding twenty infernal legions; and having the printed seal shown below the notice. [Sacred Texts transcription](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

**[Textual finding]** This is the influential first appearance of **Kimaris** located in the source chain. Peterson reports that several of Mathers’s alternate names lack manuscript support; Kimaris should not be back-projected into Weyer.

**[Modern bibliographic corruption]** At least one derivative PDF prints thirty legions. The 1904 transcription at Sacred Texts, the manuscripts, and the earlier tradition give twenty. Thirty belongs properly to medieval *Tvueries* and may have contaminated later editions.

### What Kimaris does not do

**[Negative documented finding]** Unlike other Goetic spirits, Kimaris is not assigned:

- love or erotic attraction;
- invisibility;
- familiars;
- general language acquisition;
- medicine;
- alchemical transmutation;
- reconciliation of friends and enemies;
- generic answers concerning past, present, and future;
- elevation to office or dignity.

“Grammar, logic, and rhetoric” are the medieval **trivium**, not all seven liberal arts and not “all sciences.”

### Appearance and the alleged second form

**[Documented text]** Only one individual manifestation is specified: soldier or warrior on a black horse.

Some *Goetia* entries explicitly give an initial animal form followed by a human form on command. Kimaris’s does not. The ritual’s general instructions permit the operator to command a spirit into a fair, comely, or humane shape, but that is not a separately described Kimaris form.

### Time of conjuration

The *Goetia*’s general observations state:

- favorable lunar ages are the even-numbered days from the second through the fourteenth;
- marquises may be bound from 3 p.m. to 9 p.m., and from 9 p.m. until sunrise;
- the seal of a marquis is made in silver;
- the Moon should be observed in the working.

These are native *Goetia* rules, although they occur after the catalogue rather than in Kimaris’s individual entry. Peterson prints them on pp. 40–41 of the 2001 edition.

**[Later esoteric extrapolation]** Monday, a planetary hour of the Moon, white or silver colors, and lunar incense are natural later extensions of the rank correspondence, but the early Kimaris entry does not state them.

## The seal

### Earliest occurrence

**[Documented text]** No individual Cimeries seal appears in Weyer or Scot. The spirit seals enter the surviving chain with the *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

Peterson notes that earlier related spirit catalogues either lack seals or possess figures that do not match the *Goetia* seals. The seal should therefore not be called ancient Egyptian, Cimmerian, Coptic, or Solomonic in date merely because the book attributes its system to Solomon.

### Visual description

**[Editorial visual description]** In the Mathers–Crowley orientation, the seal is a long horizontal, rail-like or rectangular construction. Several short vertical or stepped projections rise from its central body; small strokes or compartments occur inside it; one end is angular or cross-like, while the other terminates in a curled hook or flourish.

A verbal description cannot reproduce its topology exactly. The exact 1904 figure is available as [Wikimedia Commons’ Cimeies seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:66-Cimeies_seal.png).

### Variants

- **Sloane 2731:** recognizable elongated structure; lighter, less typographically regular manuscript strokes.
- **Sloane 3825:** the principal form used by Peterson and generally the clearest manuscript basis.
- **Sloane 3648:** same overall skeleton, with copyist variation in hooks, line lengths, and internal marks.
- **Harley 6483:** recognizable as the same seal but transmitted through the Rudd witness; Harley’s seals should not automatically be preferred merely because of the Rudd attribution.
- **Mathers–Crowley:** regularized for print as figure 115; proportions and the use of a containing circular presentation in modern reproductions can differ from manuscript practice.
- **Modern vectors and jewelry:** usually trace the 1904 printed form, sometimes simplifying or closing small strokes. These are modern redrawings, not independent witnesses.

**[Open question]** A definitive stemma of the Kimaris seal would require high-resolution folio comparison and stroke-by-stroke palaeographical analysis. Existing online charts usually collapse all variants into the 1904 vector.

## The angel

## From the 72-letter Name to angel names

**[Documented biblical text]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional consonantal Hebrew text, of three verses of seventy-two letters. The verses themselves do not name seventy-two angels. See [Exodus 14:19–21](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21).

**[Jewish exegetical tradition]** The three verses are arranged boustrophedonically:

1. the first in its ordinary direction;
2. the second reversed;
3. the third in its ordinary direction.

Reading the resulting columns produces seventy-two three-letter groups. Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a is an early clear witness to this construction.

**[Historical qualification]** *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally meant the “explicit” or fully pronounced divine name and did not always denote this seventy-two-part construction. The modern phrase “the 72 angels of the Shem HaMephorash” compresses several historical developments.

### Sefer Raziel

**[Documented tradition]** The medieval composite *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* contains the seventy-twofold divine name and extensive angelic, astrological, and amuletic material. Its legendary frame says the angel Raziel transmitted heavenly secrets to Adam.

**[Historical qualification]** It does not provide the entire modern Manakel package in the form now circulated: angel number, guardian dates, a five-degree Aquarius domicile, personality profile, psalm, Goetic opponent, and tarot card. Those elements accumulated through Christian Cabala and French occultism.

Joshua Trachtenberg emphasizes how unstable angelic spellings and vocalizations are in the *Sefer Raziel* tradition. See [Jewish Magic and Superstition](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

### Reuchlin and Agrippa

**[Documented text]** Johann Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) and Cornelius Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia*, Book III, chapter 25, present the seventy-two triads as angelic names when **-el** or **-yah** is added.

Agrippa’s transmitted Latin table has **Mauakel** at number 66, which Peterson identifies as an error for **Manakel**. The Hebrew triad is:

> מנק — M-N-Q

With the divine suffix:

> מנקאל — MNQ-EL, conventionally Manakel, Menaqel, Menqel, or Menaquel

See [Agrippa, Book III, chapter 25](https://esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op3.htm).

### Meaning

**[Documented traditional attribute]** Lenain calls Manakel:

> “Dieu qui seconde et entretient toutes choses”

That is best translated “God who aids and sustains all things,” or more freely “God who supports and maintains everything.”

**[Linguistic qualification]** This is a traditional divine attribute, not a secure lexical translation of מנק. The three-letter group was generated from biblical letter positions; it is not necessarily an ordinary Hebrew root with Lenain’s French meaning. The suffix *El* means “God.”

### Kircher

**[Documented Western-esoteric transmission]** Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (Rome, 1652–54) reproduced the seventy-twofold name within an ambitious Christian-Cabalistic system. His plates helped make the scheme portable among European occultists. Kircher is an important transmitter, not the originator of the Exodus construction. See the [Heidelberg digitization](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/kircher1653bd2_1) and [Kircher material at Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm).

## Rudd: Marakel or Manakel?

**[Documented Rudd reading]** The Skinner–Rankine transcription and Stephen Skinner’s *Complete Magician’s Tables* give the constraining angel for Cimeries as **Marakel**.

This is not merely a random modern webpage reading: the published table reads:

> Cimeries … Ruling Angel Name (Rudd): Marakel.

**[Conventional normalization]** The standard number-66 Shem name derived from Exodus and printed by Lenain is **Manakel**. Modern number-for-number Goetia/Shem lists therefore write:

> 66 Kimaris — 66 Manakel.

**[Disputed textual relationship]** *Marakel* could be:

- a scribal corruption of Manakel;
- a Roman-letter misreading;
- a distinct Rudd variant;
- or an editorial normalization problem involving the manuscript’s script.

Until the relevant Harley folio and Hebrew character are critically compared, it is misleading to say simply that “Rudd calls the angel Manakel.” The safest formulation is:

> **Harley/Rudd as published: Marakel; standardized Shem angel 66: Manakel.**

### How the angel constrains the spirit

**[Esoteric correspondence]** In the Rudd arrangement, the appropriate Shem angel and biblical verse are placed with, or on the reverse of, the demon’s seal. The angel is invoked as a superior divine force capable of compelling or “thwarting” the corresponding demon. Skinner and Rankine describe the seventy-two angels as a safety mechanism for evocation and binding.

This is a seventeenth-century magical synthesis, not part of Weyer, Scot, or the principal Sloane catalogue entries. See [the publisher’s description](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556) and [Golden Hoard’s catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf).

## Lenain’s Manakel

**Edition:** Lazare Lenain, *La science cabalistique*, Amiens, 1823, p. 91.

Lenain assigns to angel 66:

- **Name:** Manakel.
- **Attribute:** God who supports and maintains all things.
- **Zodiacal ray:** the 326th through 330th degrees inclusive, corresponding in modern notation to **25°00′–29°59′ Aquarius**.
- **Invocation interval:** 9:40–10:00 p.m.
- **Days of secondary rulership:** 24 May, 4 August, 15 October, 26 December, and 8 March.
- **Psalm:** Vulgate Psalm 37:22.
- **Use:** appeasing divine anger and healing the “falling sickness,” meaning epilepsy.
- **Natural dominion:** vegetation and aquatic animals.
- **Psychological influence:** sleep and dreams; gentleness; bodily and spiritual good qualities; friendship and goodwill.
- **Contrary genius:** bad physical and moral qualities.

The scan is available at [IAPSOP](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf).

### The psalm-number discrepancy

Lenain’s Latin is:

> “Ne derelinquas me Domine Deus meus; ne discesseris a me.”

This is:

- **Vulgate Psalm 37:22**;
- **Hebrew/Masoretic and KJV Psalm 38:21**;
- occasionally listed as 38:22 when a superscription or neighboring verse is counted differently.

The Hebrew verse reads, “Do not abandon me, O LORD; my God, be not far from me.” See [Sefaria, Psalms 38:21](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.38.21).

### Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

**Papus — [documented transmission].** Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” devoted part of the enlarged 1903 *La Cabbale* to Lenain’s operational angelology. He did not discover Manakel; he republished and popularized Lenain’s material within French Martinist and occultist culture. See the [1903 scan](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/abg405b2732165.pdf).

**Robert Ambelain — [esoteric elaboration].** *La Kabbale pratique* (Paris: Niclaus, 1951) continued the French operative tradition, including angelic names, psalms, talismanic construction, choirs, and practical theurgy. Modern English-language Manakel summaries often pass through Ambelain rather than directly through Lenain. [Bibliographic record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique).

**Haziel — [modern popular angelology].** François Bernard-Termès, writing as Haziel, reframed the seventy-two angels as readily accessible personal guardian angels, with birth periods, practical gifts, prayers, and material or psychological benefits. Relevant publications include *Communiquer avec son Ange Gardien* (1995) and *Les Pouvoirs de l’Ange-Gardien* (1997/1999 editions). This is influential modern devotional occultism, not evidence about the medieval origin of Manakel. See the [BnF Haziel catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/rechercher.do?index=AUT3&numNotice=12089673).

### Crowley’s *777*

**[Documented text]** In columns CLXV–CLXVI, “Goetic Demons by Night (Cadent)” and their magical images, row 26 gives number 66 **Kimaris**, accompanied by a Moon glyph and the image “warrior on black horse.” The corresponding path row is Capricorn.

Combining that with the Golden Dawn decan table yields:

- 20°–30° Capricorn;
- third or cadent decan;
- Sun in Capricorn;
- Four of Disks/Pentacles;
- “Lord of Earthly Power”;
- Andrealphus by day and Kimaris by night.

See the [*777* table](https://www.bibliotecapleyades.net/crowley/liber/libers/liber777.pdf) and [Golden Dawn Four of Pentacles attribution](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06Numerologia_Cabala/I.Regardie_Complete_Golden_Dawn_%28II%20ed.deluxe%29.pdf).

**[Correction to a common claim]** *777* does not assign Kimaris a five-degree Aquarius quinance. It gives him the nocturnal half of a ten-degree Capricorn decan.

### Why Seven of Swords is also quoted

Lenain starts angel 1 at the beginning of Aries. Thus angel 66 falls at 25°–30° Aquarius. The full 20°–30° Aquarius decan is Moon in Aquarius and corresponds to the Seven of Swords, “Lord of Unstable Effort.”

**[Later hybrid]** Modern tables often place Damabiah in 20°–25° Aquarius and Manakel in 25°–30° Aquarius, then assign both to the Seven of Swords. This is internally coherent, but it is not Crowley’s placement of Kimaris in *777*.

### The strict Golden Dawn difficulty

The Golden Dawn’s operative Shem lecture begins the series at 0° Leo, not 0° Aries. In that sequence Manakel lands in 25°–30° Gemini and shares the Ten of Swords with Damabiah.

Thus the following statements cannot all belong to one historical table:

- “Manakel is 25°–30° Aquarius.”
- “This is the Golden Dawn’s original Shem allocation.”
- “Kimaris is Four of Disks in *777*.”
- “Kimaris is Seven of Swords because of *777*.”

The first belongs to Lenain/Aries-start angelology, the second is false without qualification, the third is correct, and the fourth is a modern conflation.

### Astrological timing

**Native Goetia — [documented]:**

- Moon 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old.
- Silver seal.
- Marquis hours: 3–9 p.m. or 9 p.m.–sunrise.

**Crowley/Golden Dawn decan — [esoteric correspondence]:**

- Tropical Sun in 20°–30° Capricorn.
- Nighttime, because Kimaris is the nocturnal member of the pair.
- Sun is the decan ruler; Moon remains the rank planet.

**Lenain/Manakel — [esoteric correspondence]:**

- 25°–30° Aquarius.
- 9:40–10 p.m.
- Lenain’s five listed calendar days provide another cycle independent of the annual solar passage.

**Later planetary magic — [modern extension]:**

- Monday or a lunar planetary hour;
- waxing Moon;
- silver, white, or lunar materials.

These may be sensible within later ritual astrology but should not be presented as words from Kimaris’s catalogue entry.

## Enochian and later overlays

**[Documented publication history]** Crowley’s 1904 book added material not native to the earliest *Goetia* catalogue, including the “Bornless” or preliminary invocation and Enochian-related material.

**[Historical distinction]** John Dee and Edward Kelley’s angelic language and tables arose in the late sixteenth century. Neither Weyer’s Cimeries entry nor the Sloane Cimeies notice identifies the spirit as Enochian.

**[Rudd synthesis]** Rudd-associated manuscripts attempt broader coordination among Goetic, angelic, astrological, and Dee-derived systems. This explains why later occultists discuss Cimeriel, ensigns, mansions, and Cimeries together.

**[Disputed conflation]** It does not establish that:

- Cimeriel and Cimeries are identical;
- Kimaris was one of Dee’s communicating angels;
- Kimaris taught the Enochian language;
- or Dee evoked Kimaris.

Poke Runyon’s late-twentieth-century rectified quadrants, dark-mirror techniques, planetary metals, and angelic rulers constitute another distinct modern practice. Runyon’s table places Cimejes in Aquarius, uses **Menkiel**, and even speculates on a Moroccan origin. Those are modern system-building choices, not recoveries from Weyer.

## Iconography

### The textual image

The stable iconographic nucleus is remarkably spare:

- one valiant soldier or warrior;
- mounted on one large or goodly black horse;
- no stated armor, weapon, face, skin color, age, or heraldry.

**[Modern invention]** Lances, swords, African dress, Roman armor, skeletal features, horns, wings, flames, and black armor are artistic elaborations unless independently labeled as such.

### Collin de Plancy

**First edition, 1818 — [open finding].** The first edition of *Dictionnaire infernal* was checked through its scan and searchable Wikisource transcription. A distinct Cimeries headword was not located in the presently searchable text. Because portions of the first-edition transcription remain difficult to search, this is not absolute proof of absence. The 1818 edition should not be cited for the familiar Cimeriès paragraph without a page reference.

**Sixth edition, 1863 — [documented text].** Cimeriès appears on p. 170:

> “Cimeriès, grand et puissant démon, marquis de l’empire infernal.”

De Plancy continues that he commands in African regions; teaches grammar, logic, and rhetoric; discovers treasures and hidden things; makes a man light-footed and gives civilians a dashing military bearing; commands twenty legions; and is always mounted on a great black palfrey. He cites Wierus. [1863 Cimeriès article](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Cimeri%C3%A8s).

De Plancy’s “gives civilians the dashing bearing of soldiers” is a witty nineteenth-century paraphrase of the ambiguous transformation sentence, not a new ancient office.

### Is there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**[Negative documented finding]** No dedicated Louis Le Breton engraving accompanies the Cimeriès entry on p. 170. The 1863 edition contains a famous but selective group of demon portraits; not every listed demon received one.

Images now advertised as “Cimeries from the *Dictionnaire Infernal*” should be checked carefully. Many websites combine:

- a Le Breton portrait of some other demon;
- the 1904 Cimeies seal;
- and a newly written “apocryphal” biography.

That composite is modern, not an 1863 plate.

### Modern art

**[Modern invention]** Contemporary occult decks, tattoos, jewelry, online grimoires, and demonolatry pages usually visualize Kimaris as a mounted dark knight. These works draw primarily upon the horse-and-warrior sentence and the 1904 seal.

Modern practitioner reports add highly individual imagery—hunters, soldiers, poisoned weapons, African landscapes, visual language, or a silent teacher. Such reports document contemporary religious or magical experience but cannot be used to reconstruct the early text.

## Grimoire ancestors

### *Testament of Solomon*

**[Documented conceptual ancestor]** The Greek *Testament of Solomon*, composed in late antiquity in stages, tells how Solomon receives a ring, interrogates demons about their names and powers, compels them, and assigns them work.

**[Negative finding]** It has no identifiable Kimaris, Cimeries, Cimeies, or Tvueries. Its relevance is the broader Solomonic demon-binding narrative, not direct name transmission.

### *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Documented text]** The French *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, is among the closest medieval ancestors of Weyer’s catalogue. Approximately thirty-five of its spirits correspond to Weyer entries.

**[Negative finding]** No convincing Cimeries/Tvueries equivalent occurs in its list. The name cannot currently be traced through this branch. See the [complete French text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier).

### Munich handbook, Clm 849

**[Strongest pre-Weyer evidence]** *Tvueries* is the earliest securely recognizable version of Kimaris’s complete profile. It is consequently more important to this spirit’s history than the general *Livre des Esperitz* relationship.

See Richard Kieckhefer, *Forbidden Rites: A Necromancer’s Manual of the Fifteenth Century* ([Penn State University Press](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/0-271-01750-3.html)) and Boudet’s [demonological catalogue study](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

**[Documented title, lost or composite source]** Weyer says that his material came from a book called *Liber officiorum spirituum*, *Liber de officiis spirituum*, or *Empto. Salomonis*. Trithemius catalogued a work with this title in his *Antipalus maleficiorum* in 1508.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The title denotes a textual family rather than a single recoverable authorial book. The Munich handbook, *Livre des Esperitz*, Weyer, the Folger *Book of Oberon*, and related catalogues preserve intersecting branches.

**[Open finding]** No surviving pre-Weyer *Liber Officiorum* witness containing the literal form *Cimeries* has been demonstrated. The closest surviving antecedent remains *Tvueries*.

### *Book of Abramelin*

**[Negative finding]** The large Abramelin spirit catalogues contain many corrupt and otherwise unattested names, but no securely identifiable Kimaris/Cimeries entry was located. Similarity between isolated spellings is insufficient without the black-horse, trivium, treasure, or soldier offices.

### Munich, Florence, Folger, and other lists

Peterson compares Weyer with several related spirit catalogues:

- Munich Clm 849: **Tvueries**, convincing match.
- Florence II-iii-24: no secure Cimeries equivalent.
- Trinity O.8.29: none.
- Folger V.b.26/*Book of Oberon* family: related *Offices* material, but no better literal Cimeries antecedent has been established.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

**[Documented history]** Weyer published *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in **1577**, not 1563. The larger book first appeared in 1563, producing the common dating error.

Weyer was a physician arguing against the persecution and execution of alleged witches. His preservation of the catalogue does not make him an advocate of demon worship. He says he omitted or disrupted operational material so that it would not be used.

### Reginald Scot

**[Documented history]** Scot reproduced the catalogue in English in *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584), also a skeptical work opposing witchcraft prosecutions and exposing conjuring claims. His omission of Pruflas became textually consequential.

### The anonymous Lemegeton compiler

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The surviving *Lemegeton* is a seventeenth-century compilation. Its dependence on the 1665 edition of Scot and other printed sources means that the familiar complete form is later than alleged Solomonic antiquity. The compiler supplied seals, ritual directions, rearrangement, and the completed seventy-two.

### Thomas Rudd

**[Tradition and disputed attribution]** Thomas Rudd is associated with Harley 6482–6483 and a synthesis of angelic and demonic magic. Skinner and Rankine regard him as the owner and systematizer of the material. The surviving manuscript’s later copying prevents simple identification of every addition as Rudd’s autograph work.

### Collin de Plancy

**[Documented reception]** De Plancy transmitted Weyer’s Cimeries into the French demonological dictionary tradition. His 1863 wording turns the soldier-transformation power into social satire about civilians acquiring military bearing.

### Mathers and Crowley

**[Documented reception]** Mathers prepared the catalogue and seals; Crowley published the book in 1904, contributed prefatory material, and later assigned Goetic spirits to decans in *777*.

Crowley’s psychological interpretation says that when “Cimieries teaches logic,” he means that ritual stimulates the parts of the practitioner’s brain serving logical thought. This is Crowley’s initiated interpretation, not the meaning expressed by Weyer. See [Crowley’s introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

### Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No reliable Kimaris-specific operation, vision, or novel attribution by Charles Stansfeld Jones/Frater Achad was located. His association with Crowley and Qabalistic interpretation makes the name plausible in secondary lists, but a specific claim should not be made without a work and page.

### Anton LaVey

**[Documented modern reception]** *The Satanic Bible* (1969) includes Cimeries among LaVey’s “Infernal Names,” paraphrasing the black horse and African rulership. LaVey does not provide historical evidence beyond the grimoire tradition, and no reason is given for selecting Cimeries rather than most other Goetic spirits.

### Poke Runyon

**[Modern operative system]** Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* (1996) incorporates the Goetic catalogue into dark-mirror evocation, planetary and elemental quadrants, and reconstructed angelic pairings. His Cimejes table uses Aquarius and “Menkiel” and speculates on Morocco. Those are Runyon’s modern rectifications, not manuscript facts.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

**[Modern commentary]** DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt, and David P. Wilson’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* belongs to the late-twentieth-century practical and psychological reception. No separate ancient tradition about Kimaris is established there.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

**[Documented scholarship/esoteric edition]** Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s angelic counter-seals, psalms, variable circles, and manuscript variants widely available. It is indispensable for the Marakel/Manakel problem, though its strong historical attribution to Rudd remains open to scholarly qualification.

### Joseph H. Peterson

**[Documented editorial work]** Peterson’s 2001 edition compares Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, Weyer, Scot, and Mathers–Crowley. His base reading **Cimeies** is the strongest modern critical choice. His subsequent online corrections and manuscript tables are essential for separating readings from editorial inventions.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

**[Modern scholarly-esoteric reception]** Stratton-Kent’s work situates the *Goetia* in a broader early-modern spirit-catalogue and necromantic tradition. No unique Kimaris operation or new primary reading was verified; his importance is methodological—treating Goetic catalogues as historical textual families rather than isolated Victorian creations.

### Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Julien Véronèse, Jean-Patrice Boudet

These scholars provide the broader context of medieval and early-modern grimoires, demon catalogues, necromancy, manuscript transmission, and the *Livre des Esperitz*. Boudet is particularly important for the Clm 849 parallels. Their work does not validate the supernatural offices attributed to Kimaris; it documents the history of the texts carrying them.

### Is Kimaris a conflation of two spirits?

No clear early evidence shows two originally distinct demons being deliberately merged under this name. The main historical process looks like name corruption and textual expansion:

- *Tvueries*: thirty legions and rapid water crossing.
- *Cimeries*: twenty legions and swift apparent soldier-transformation.
- *Cimeies*: expanded rulership over spirits of Africa and lost things.
- *Kimaris*: Mathers’s printed alternate.

The later conflations are with **Cimmerians**, **Chimera**, **Chamaris**, and **Cimeriel**, none of which has a demonstrated genealogical connection.

### “Africa”

The medieval *Tvueries* synopsis does not foreground Africa; Weyer does. No source explains why.

Possible interpretations include:

- a lost source assigned geographical jurisdictions;
- Weyer inherited a clause absent from the surviving Clm 849 witness;
- “African parts” functioned as a conventional distant or exotic region in European demonography;
- a scribal corruption concealed some other place-name.

There is no evidence that Kimaris derives from an African deity, speaks for African religions, or has a historically African cult. Modern racial or ethnographic portraits are additions to a European text.

## Open questions

1. **What exact exemplar connected Clm 849’s Tvueries to Weyer’s Cimeries?**  
   The verbal identity is strong, but the intervening manuscripts are lost or unidentified.

2. **What is the correct palaeographical reading of the medieval name?**  
   *Tvueries*, *Tuveries*, and *Tuvries* are editorial representations of a difficult manuscript form.

3. **Why does Weyer reduce thirty legions to twenty?**  
   This may be deliberate alteration, a numeral error, or evidence for an intermediate branch.

4. **Where did the Africa clause originate?**  
   It is not explained and has no demonstrated link to African mythology.

5. **Was Cimeries present in a recoverable pre-Weyer *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*?**  
   The title is attested, but no literal pre-1577 Cimeries witness has been produced.

6. **Did the 1818 *Dictionnaire infernal* contain a Cimeries notice?**  
   It was not found in the currently searchable transcription. A systematic examination of every “C” page in the physical first-edition scan would settle the question.

7. **Is Harley 6483’s angel genuinely Marakel?**  
   The published Rudd-derived table says so. Comparison of the Roman name, Hebrew triad, seal, and psalm on the original folio is needed before silently emending it to Manakel.

8. **Who originated “Kimaris”?**  
   It becomes visible in Mathers–Crowley in 1904. No earlier manuscript support has been found, but an unexamined Mathers copy-text could conceivably contain it.

9. **Can the spirit seal’s variants be stemmatically ordered?**  
   The broad design is stable; the diagnostic value of its small hooks and internal strokes remains understudied.

10. **Was Crowley’s Hebrew spelling merely phonetic?**  
    KYMAVR/KIMARIS is adequately explained as transliteration. Egyptian and gematric interpretations lack a Crowley statement proving intention.

11. **Does any primary source identify Cimeriel with Cimeries?**  
    None found.

12. **Is there a secure Kimaris-specific record from Frater Achad, DuQuette, or Stratton-Kent?**  
    They belong to relevant occult networks or literature, but claims of a personal Kimaris working need work-and-page documentation.

## Conclusions

**[Documented core]** Kimaris is the 1904 alternate spelling of a spirit best textually called **Cimeries** in Weyer and **Cimeies** in the seventeenth-century *Goetia*. He is number 66 in the latter, a great marquis appearing as a mounted soldier, teaching the trivium, revealing concealed property, and producing the appearance of military transformation. He commands twenty legions.

**[Best historical reconstruction]** His profile descends from fifteenth-century **Tvueries**, whose nearly identical catalogue entry supplies the earliest recognizable attestation. This makes Kimaris a genuine medieval-to-early-modern textual figure, but not an identified ancient deity.

**[Etymological result]** Cimmerian, Chimera, Horus/Khem-our, Coptic Chamaris, and Enochian Cimeriel are conjectures or conflations. None currently displaces the simpler explanation of corrupt transmission within the *Offices of Spirits* family.

**[Angelic result]** The conventional number-66 Shem angel is **Manakel**, “God who aids and sustains all things,” with Lenain’s 25°–30° Aquarius ray and Psalm 37:22 Vulgate/38:21 Hebrew. But the Rudd-derived table literally prints **Marakel**, a significant variant that should remain visible.

**[Correspondence result]** Crowley’s own *777* assigns Kimaris to the **Four of Disks, 20°–30° Capricorn, by night**, paired with daytime Andrealphus. Seven of Swords belongs to the later combination of Lenain’s Aquarius Manakel with Golden Dawn tarot—not to Kimaris’s original *777* entry.

**[Iconographic result]** The only early portrait is verbal: a soldier on a black horse. The seal is seventeenth-century in its surviving evidence. De Plancy records the spirit but supplies no Le Breton portrait.

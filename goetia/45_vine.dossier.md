# Viné: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes the following categories:

- **[Documented text]** — directly stated or depicted in a surviving manuscript or printed edition.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a historical relationship proposed from textual comparison, manuscript genealogy, palaeography, or bibliography.
- **[Tradition]** — a teaching transmitted within a religious, magical, or occult lineage but not necessarily demonstrable as historical fact.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an attribution created by a systematic occult table, such as the Golden Dawn’s decans or Rudd’s controlling angels.
- **[Disputed]** — a reading or historical claim for which evidence conflicts or remains inadequate.
- **[Legend]** — supernatural narrative accepted within a tradition but not historically or empirically verifiable.
- **[Modern invention]** — an attribution, story, image, “enn,” identity, or correspondence first found in modern occultism or fiction.

The principal textual witnesses are Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* (1577), Reginald Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584), the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscripts, Harley MS 6483, Mathers and Crowley’s 1904 *Goetia*, and Joseph H. Peterson’s critical edition and transcriptions.

# The name and its origins

## 1. Spellings across the principal source chain

Joseph Peterson’s [comparative spirit-name table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf) permits the following reconstruction:

| Date/source | Reading |
|---|---|
| 1577, Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* | **Vine** |
| 1584, Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft* | **Vine** |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane MS 3825 | **Vine** |
| Seventeenth century, Sloane MS 2731 | **Vine** |
| 1712–1713 copy, Harley MS 6483 | **Vine** |
| 1904, Mathers–Crowley | **Viné, or Vinea** |
| 2001, Peterson | **Vine**, while recording later variants |
| Other comparison witnesses | Sloane 3648 and Northwestern MS 65: **Vine**; Wellcome MS 3203 places **Vine** at no. 26 |

**[Documented text]** The oldest secure printed reading is therefore plain **Vine**, without an accent and without the final *-a*. The accent in **Viné** is not present in Weyer, Scot, or the principal Sloane readings. It appears prominently in the 1904 Mathers–Crowley edition.

**[Documented text]** **Vinea** is printed by Mathers and Crowley as an alternative: “Viné, or Vinea.” It should not be silently retrojected into Weyer. Many modern reference pages do precisely that, describing Weyer’s demon as “Vine or Vinea,” but Weyer’s entry itself reads *Vine*.

**[Disputed]** Modern variants such as *Vain*, *Vigne*, *Vinéa*, or *Vone* circulate online. They are not readings established by the principal Weyer–Scot–Sloane–Harley chain and should be treated as modernization, mistranscription, or independent occult usage unless tied to a specified manuscript.

## 2. Number 45 and the change from 69 to 72 spirits

**[Documented text]** Vine is number 45 in both Weyer and the *Ars Goetia*. This numerical agreement can obscure how extensively the list was otherwise changed.

Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia* contains **69 entries**. The *Ars Goetia* reaches 72 by:

- omitting Weyer’s no. 4, **Pruflas/Busas**;
- adding **Vassago** as Goetia no. 3;
- adding **Seere** as no. 70;
- adding **Dantalion** as no. 71;
- adding **Andromalius** as no. 72.

The arithmetic is consequently 69 minus one plus four: 72. Numerous spirits are also repositioned. Vine happens to retain number 45, but that continuity does not mean the two catalogues have identical order.

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s Belial paragraph alludes to a total of 72 kings or spirits even though the surviving printed catalogue has only 69 entries. This may indicate omission, editorial incompleteness, or a source list conceived as containing 72. It does not identify the three missing entries.

## 3. Is Vine the name of an ancient god, hero, or place?

**Finding: no ancient identity is demonstrated.**

Unlike Baal, Astarte, Amon, Asmodeus/Ashmedai, Belial, or Phoenix, Vine has not been securely traced to a named ancient Near Eastern deity, classical god, biblical demon, hero, city, or cult.

**[Documented absence]** No Vine or Vinea identifiable by this appearance and office has been located in:

- the Greek *Testament of Solomon*;
- the medieval *Livre des Esperitz*;
- the surviving Munich magical handbook, Clm 849;
- the *Book of Abramelin* demon lists;
- the biblical or rabbinic demonological material commonly invoked as remote ancestry.

Those works may be ancestors of the catalogue form or of individual motifs, but they do not supply an attested ancient “Vine.”

### The Latin *vīnea* hypothesis

**[Scholarly reconstruction / disputed]** Latin *vīnea* means a vine or vineyard, but also a covered framework or mobile shelter used in siegecraft. The latter was a timber gallery protected by hides or other covering, under which soldiers approached fortifications. The sense survives in dictionaries such as [Treccani’s *vinea*](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/vinea/) and the [RAE historical dictionary](https://temporalraeweb.rae.es/dhle/v%C3%ADnea).

That military meaning is an attractive explanation because the demon:

- builds towers;
- constructs stone buildings or defensive works;
- in Scot and the *Goetia*, throws down walls.

The semantic match is real. The historical derivation is not proved. Neither Weyer nor Scot says that the name comes from a Roman siege gallery. No presently cited manuscript glosses it as Latin *vīnea*. The hypothesis therefore remains an inference from the offices.

A further difficulty is direction of causation: the offices might have suggested the proposed etymology to modern readers rather than the name originally generating the offices.

### The ordinary word “vine”

**[Disputed]** Derivation from English or French “vine,” ultimately Latin *vīnea*, is linguistically possible but explains little about the lion, horse, viper, or hydrological powers. The name may simply be a corrupted proper name. The [English word’s etymology](https://www.etymonline.com/word/vine) cannot by itself establish the demon’s origin.

### Heracles, Dionysus, or a siege hero

**[Modern invention]** Some modern devotional and Satanist sources identify Vine/Vinea with Heracles, Dionysian wine symbolism, or a hero associated with the siege of Troy. One explicit modern example is the Temple of Zeus/Joy of Satan-derived [“Heracles–Vine Power Ritual”](https://tozrituals.org/rituals/god/heracles-vine-power-ritual).

No Weyer, Scot, *Lemegeton*, Rudd, de Plancy, Golden Dawn, or early kabbalistic source makes this identification. The argument combines:

1. *vīnea* as a siege machine;
2. Vine as a wall-destroyer;
3. Heracles as a strongman and destroyer of cities;
4. “vine” as a wine/Dionysus association.

That is creative syncretism, not an evidenced source history.

**[Modern invention]** Similar internet identifications with the Egyptian lion god Maahes or the Arabian lion deity Yaghūth rest principally on Vine’s leonine appearance. Iconographic resemblance alone is insufficient to establish textual descent.

## 4. Vine and Royne

A more serious historical lead is **Royne**.

The Folger manuscript V.b.26, compiled approximately 1577–1583, contains:

> “Royne a greate earle, & appeareth like a souldier … his face is like a lyon, & he rideth upon a blacke horse … devidinge a snake with his armes or arme, & he buildeth greate towers … & howses, & bulworkes of warre … & telleth of hide treasures & seecrets & under him are 26 legions.”

The full passage is in Peterson’s [Folger V.b.26 transcription, p. 85](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf).

The shared complex is striking:

| Royne | Vine |
|---|---|
| Earl | King and Earl |
| Lion face | Lion or lion head |
| Black horse | Black horse |
| Snake | Viper |
| Builds towers | Builds towers |
| Builds houses and bulwarks | Latin Vine constructs stone buildings |
| Reveals treasures and secrets | Reveals hidden matters |
| 26 legions | Later Vine counts vary |

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Royne and Vine are almost certainly close textual relatives. They may represent divergent copies of an earlier spirit from the *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* family.

**[Disputed]** It is not yet possible to say whether:

- *Royne* was corrupted into *Vine*;
- *Vine* was corrupted into *Royne*;
- both descend independently from a third spelling;
- the compilers deliberately combined related material.

The Folger manuscript’s estimated date overlaps Weyer’s 1577 publication. It cannot automatically be described as an earlier attestation. Its reading nevertheless shows that the Vine complex circulated in more than one branch of the late-sixteenth-century catalogue tradition.

# The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, 1577: Latin

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s complete entry is:

> **“Vine magnus Rex & Comes: se ostentat ut leo in equo nigro insidens, portansque viperam in manu. Amplas turres libenter ædificat: lapideas domus extruit, rivos reddit turgidos: ad exorcistæ mandatum respondet de occultis, maleficis, præsentibus, præteritis & futuris.”**

Source: Johann Weyer, *De praestigiis daemonum*, fifth edition, Basel, 1577, appended *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, §45, original p. 926; [Peterson’s Latin transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

A close translation is:

> “Vine, a great King and Count, displays himself as a lion seated upon a black horse and carrying a viper in his hand. He willingly builds spacious towers; he constructs stone houses; he makes streams swollen. At the exorcist’s command he answers concerning hidden things, malefactors or sorcerers, and things present, past, and future.”

### Textual points

**[Documented text]**

- *Rex & Comes* means King and Count; English occult texts conventionally render *comes* as Earl.
- *maleficis* can denote evildoers, poisoners, harmful magicians, or witches. Scot selects “witches.”
- *lapideas domus extruit* means “he constructs/erects stone houses or buildings.” It does **not** literally mean “throws down stone walls.”
- *rivos reddit turgidos* means “he renders streams swollen” or “makes rivers swell,” not literally “creates storms.”
- Weyer gives **no legion count** for Vine.
- Weyer supplies **no seal**.

## 2. Scot, 1584: English

**[Documented text]** Scot’s complete English entry reads:

> **“Vine is a great king and an earle, he showeth himselfe as a lion, riding on a blacke horsse, and carrieth a viper in his hand, he gladlie buildeth large towres, he throweth downe stone walles, and maketh waters rough. At the commandement of the exorcist he answereth of things hidden, of witches, and of things present, past, and to come.”**

Source: Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, London, 1584, Book XV, chapter II; Brinsley Nicholson’s 1886 reprint, p. 323; [searchable transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/60766/60766-h/60766-h.htm#Page_323) and [Cornell first-edition facsimile](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081).

Scot’s catalogue was probably translated from Weyer with assistance traditionally attributed to Abraham Fleming.

### Scot’s consequential deviations

| Weyer’s Latin | Scot | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| *lapideas domus extruit* | “throweth downe stone walles” | Construction becomes destruction |
| *rivos reddit turgidos* | “maketh waters rough” | Swollen rivers become turbulent waters |
| *maleficis* | “witches” | A broad category becomes specifically witchcraft |
| No legions | No legions | Scot accurately preserves the omission |

**[Documented textual development]** These are not trivial modernization choices. Scot’s “throws down walls” and “rough waters” become authoritative traits in the English *Goetia*. The familiar destructive Vine is therefore partly the product of translation.

**[Disputed]** Peterson supplies corrective bracketed glosses in his online comparison—“erects” and “restores swollen rivers”—to show how Scot differs from the Latin. Those brackets are modern editorial explanations, not words printed by Scot.

## 3. Sloane MS 3825

**[Documented text]** The principal *Lemegeton* witness transcribed by Peterson reads:

> **“The 45 spirit is called Vine, he is a great king & an[d] Earle, & appeareth in ye forme of a Lyon ridding on a Black horse wth a viper in his hand his office is to discover Things hidden, witches, and Things present past & to come. he, at ye command of ye Exorcist, will build Towers, Throw down great stone walls, make waters rough wth stormes &c, he governeth 35 Legions of spirits, his seal is this, wch make and wear as a Lamin &c.”**

Source: British Library, Sloane MS 3825, fol. 107r; [Peterson transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Changes from Scot include:

- “His office is to discover…”;
- “great” stone walls;
- the explicit addition “with storms”;
- **35 legions**;
- a drawn seal and instruction to wear it as a lamen.

The Sloane 3825 reading is especially important because Peterson’s 2001 critical edition uses it as its principal base manuscript.

## 4. Sloane MS 2731

**[Documented manuscript]** Sloane 2731 likewise calls the spirit **Vine** and supplies a seal. It belongs to a later *Lemegeton* compilation and is not independent proof that the name existed before Weyer. A diplomatic transcription and folio images are available through the [Sloane 2731 project](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/).

**[Disputed]** Published modern comparisons do not always agree on whether a particular legion count originates in Sloane 2731, another Sloane witness, or Mathers’s collation. Consequently, **36 should not be attributed to Sloane 3825**: that manuscript clearly reads 35. A claim that “all Goetia manuscripts say 36” is false.

## 5. Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

**[Documented manuscript]** Harley MS 6483, copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713, retains Vine as no. 45. Its distinctive feature is not a radically different demon description but the addition of:

- the demon’s name in Hebrew characters;
- a corresponding Shem angel;
- the angelic character or seal;
- a biblical verse;
- material designed to subordinate each spirit to divine and angelic authority.

The manuscript describes itself as *Liber Malorum Spirituum, seu Goetia* and also uses the title *Lemegeton, Secretum Secretorum*. Peterson discusses its contents and date in his [Lemegeton manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Stephen Skinner and David Rankine argue that Smart copied an earlier working text associated with Thomas Rudd, perhaps dating around the 1650s. Harley 6483 is not Rudd’s autograph, so the precise degree of Rudd’s authorship remains an editorial reconstruction.

## 6. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**[Documented text]** The complete 1904 entry is:

> **“(45.) VINE.—The Forty-fifth Spirit is Viné, or Vinea. He is a Great King, and an Earl; and appeareth in the Form of a Lion, riding on a Black Horse, and bearing a Viper in his hand. His Office is to discover Things Hidden, Witches, Wizards, and Things Past, Present and to Come. He at the command of the Exorcist will build Towers, overthrow Great Stone Walls, and make the Waters rough with Storms. He governeth 36 Legions of Spirits. And his Seal is this, which wear thou, as aforesaid, etc.”**

Footnote:

> **“Or ‘with the Head of a Lion,’ or ‘having a Lion’s Head,’ in some Codices.”**

Source: S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley, *The Goetia: The Lesser Key of Solomon the King*, 1904, p. 26; [1904 PDF](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/crowley_-_goetia.pdf) and [Peterson comparison text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Mathers–Crowley introduces or fixes several influential forms:

- **Viné**, with accent;
- **Vinea** as an alias;
- “Wizards” in addition to witches;
- **36 legions**, rather than Sloane 3825’s 35;
- “overthrow” rather than simply “throw down”;
- the lion-headed variant in a footnote.

## 7. Peterson, 2001

**[Scholarly edition]** Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* (York Beach, Maine: Weiser, 2001), pp. 26–27, returns attention to the manuscripts rather than simply reprinting Crowley. His edition and continuing online apparatus expose the crucial **35/36** variation and the close Royne parallel.

Peterson also identifies Sloane 3825 as a major early witness and documents the relationships among Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other manuscripts.

## 9. Rank, metal, planet, and time

The *Goetia*’s general rubric assigns seal metals and times by rank:

| Rank | Transmitted seal metal | Later planetary rationale | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| King | Gold | Sun | 9 a.m.–noon; 3 p.m.–sunset |
| Earl/Count | Equal copper and silver | Venus + Moon in the literal metal recipe | Any daylight hour in secluded woods or quiet places |
| Earl, Mathers’s correction | Iron | Mars | Same rank timing |
| Marquis | Silver | Moon | — |
| Duke | Copper | Venus | — |
| President | Mercury; manuscript wording is problematic | Mercury | — |
| Prelate/Prince | Tin | Jupiter | — |
| Knight | Lead | Saturn | — |

Source: [*Goetia*, “Observations”](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks06.htm).

**[Documented text]** The transmitted prescription for an Earl is not iron but equal parts copper and silver.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Mathers objects that this would omit Mars from the scheme. He proposes Mars/iron for Earls and an alloy for Presidents. Golden Dawn-derived tables generally follow the corrected planetary logic: King = Sun; Earl = Mars.

For Vine, this creates two simultaneous rank correspondences:

- solar/gold by kingship;
- lunar-venusian copper-and-silver by the literal Earl rubric, or martial/iron by Mathers’s correction.

**[Open textual problem]** Neither the manuscript rubric nor Mathers explains whether a dual-ranked spirit requires one metal, two alternative seals, an alloy, or selection according to the office sought.

**[Documented text]** The same rubric recommends operations when the Moon is two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, or fourteen days old. That is a general *Goetia* rule, not a Vine-specific lunar phase.

# The seal

## 1. Earliest layer

**[Documented absence]** Weyer and Scot print no seal for Vine and no complete set of 72 Goetic seals.

**[Documented text/image]** The seal enters with the *Lemegeton* manuscript tradition. Sloane 3825 directs that it be made and worn as a “Lamin,” meaning a lamen or breast-worn magical emblem.

## 2. Graphic description

The seal is a drawing rather than a verbally defined geometric construction. No early source says, for example, “draw three lines at these angles.” The exact source is therefore the image.

In the familiar Mathers–Crowley plate it consists of:

- one enclosing circular border;
- an unlettered internal linear character;
- a dominant upright/vertical axis;
- crossing oblique strokes producing an angular, branch-like middle;
- hooked or returning lower strokes;
- a compact upper junction or loop-like feature.

The public-domain reproduction can be inspected at [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:45-Vine_seal.png) and in the [1904 page reproduction](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/045-Seal-of-Vine/).

**[Caution]** Modern redrawing often regularizes the circle, line thickness, symmetry, and junctions. Those differences are usually graphic normalization, not evidence of an alternative magical doctrine.

## 3. Manuscript variants

**[Documented image tradition]** Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, and Harley 6483 transmit hand-drawn forms. Natural differences occur in curvature, line termination, closure of small loops, and symmetry.

**[Open question]** A definitive claim that one small stroke is present or absent in every manuscript requires direct high-resolution collation of the British Library folios. Published vector sigils should not be treated as diplomatic reproductions.

**[Tradition]** Harley 6483 places the demonic seal within a larger controlling structure involving Sealiah’s name, character, and verse. The angelic material is not part of Weyer’s original Vine entry.

# The angel: Sealiah

## 1. Identification

| Field | Sealiah |
|---|---|
| Number | 45 of 72 |
| Common spellings | Sealiah, Sehaliah, Saliah, Seeliah; French *Sealiah* |
| Hebrew triplet | סאל, Samekh–Aleph–Lamed |
| Expanded angelic name | Commonly סאליה, with a divine *-iah/yah* ending |
| Traditional meaning | “Mover of all things,” “God who moves all things,” or “God who stirs/motivates” |
| Quinance | 10°–15° Scorpio |
| Ordinal degrees | 221–225 |
| Psalm | Vulgate Psalm 93:18; Hebrew/Protestant Psalm 94:18 |
| Verse | *Si dicebam: Motus est pes meus; misericordia tua, Domine, adiuvabat me* |
| Tarot decan | Six of Cups, Sun in Scorpio |
| Rudd function | Controlling or constraining angel assigned to Vine |

## 2. The seventy-two-letter construction

**[Documented biblical text]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional Hebrew consonantal text, of three verses conventionally counted as 72 letters each.

**[Tradition]** The later construction writes:

1. verse 19 in its normal direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its normal direction;

then reads the columns to produce 72 three-letter combinations.

The forty-fifth triplet is **סאל**, S-A-L.

**[Historical qualification]** Exodus itself does not call those columns angels and does not name Sealiah. The angelic interpretation is a later mystical development.

The 72-fold divine-name tradition appears in medieval Jewish materials, including commentary attributed to Rashi and the *Sefer ha-Bahir*. *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* became an important later vehicle for angelic names, diagrams, and divine-name magic.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The familiar Western list of pronounceable angel names was shaped through Christian Kabbalah. Johannes Reuchlin’s *De Arte Cabalistica* (1517) was especially important in transforming Hebrew triplets into angelic names with endings such as *-el* or *-iah*. Athanasius Kircher reproduced and reorganized comparable material in the seventeenth century; see Peterson’s [Kircher kabbalistic tables](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

## 3. What does “Sealiah” mean?

**[Tradition]** Lenain calls Sealiah the **“Moteur de toutes choses”**—“Mover of all things.”

**[Disputed etymology]** This is an occult attribute, not a transparent dictionary translation of the trigram סאל. The three consonants are produced by a combinatorial divine-name method and need not constitute an ordinary Hebrew word.

Modern glosses such as:

- “God who moves all things”;
- “God who stirs all men”;
- “God the motivator”;
- “the will that raises the fallen”

are interpretive expansions of the traditional title. They should not be presented as uncontested Hebrew philology.

## 4. The five degrees

**[Documented esoteric table]** Lenain gives Sealiah’s ray as degrees **221–225**. In a 360-degree zodiac beginning with 1° Aries as ordinal degree 1, this corresponds to the five-degree sector conventionally written:

> **10°00′ through 14°59′ Scorpio**

Sources: Lazare Républicain Lenain, *La Science cabalistique* (Amiens, 1823), and the later tables reproduced by Papus and Ambelain; [Lenain scan](https://archive.org/download/BIUSante_ms05365/BIUSante_ms05365.pdf).

**[Modern variation]** Published guardian-angel calendars assign differing civil dates because authors use different zodiac boundaries, inclusive counting, calendars, or later astrological adjustments. The zodiacal degrees are more stable than dates such as October 30–November 3.

## 5. Psalm verse

Lenain assigns:

> **“Si dicebam: Motus est pes meus; misericordia tua, Domine, adiuvabat me.”**

A conventional English rendering is:

> “When I said, ‘My foot slips,’ your mercy, O LORD, held me up.”

The verse is [Psalm 94:18 in Hebrew/Protestant numbering](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.94.18), but Psalm **93:18** in the Vulgate numbering used by Lenain, Papus, and French Catholic occultists.

**[Documented textual issue]** “Psalm 93” and “Psalm 94” are not rival verses here. They represent two numbering systems.

## 6. Lenain’s complete profile

Lenain’s 1823 table attributes to angel 45:

- **Moteur de toutes choses**;
- degrees 221–225;
- Psalm 93:18;
- power to confound the wicked and proud;
- elevation of the humble and fallen;
- governance of vegetation;
- a temperament attracted to learning and ease;
- an adverse or contrary genius governing atmospheric phenomena.

He also associates the angel with a purported divine name, “Hobo,” said to belong to the inhabitants of Quito.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The moral, botanical, and meteorological offices are part of Lenain’s kabbalistic-astrological system.

**[Disputed / pre-scientific speculation]** “Hobo” as a sacred name from Quito is not supported by modern linguistic or ethnographic evidence. Lenain’s work frequently seeks universal divine names among peoples known to nineteenth-century European compilers through unreliable travel and missionary literature.

## 7. Rudd’s controlling-angel scheme

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** Harley MS 6483 pairs the 72 Goetic spirits with the 72 Shem angels by number. Spirit 45 Vine is consequently placed under angel 45, Sealiah.

In this system the angel’s:

- name;
- Hebrew form;
- character;
- Psalm verse;
- divine authority

are placed in ritual relation to the demon’s name and seal.

**[Tradition]** The angel is invoked as a superior power capable of binding, controlling, or correcting the spirit. This reflects the Solomonic principle that a magician commands subordinate spirits through higher divine names, not through personal power alone.

**[Historical qualification]** There is no evidence that ancient Jewish kabbalists paired Sealiah with a demon named Vine. The one-to-one demon/angel pairing is characteristic of the Rudd recension and subsequent Hermetic occultism.

## 8. Skinner and Rankine

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine published Harley 6483 as *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* in 2007. Their edition provides:

- a transcription and reconstruction of the Rudd material;
- the 72 demon/angel pairings;
- angelic seals and divine names;
- historical discussion of Rudd and Peter Smart;
- comparisons with other *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Their identification of the recension with Thomas Rudd is substantial but not equivalent to possessing a signed Rudd autograph.

**[Tradition]** Their practical interpretation treats the angelic half of the system as essential protective and constraining technology, not optional decoration.

## 9. Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

**[Documented reception]**

- **Papus**, *La Cabbale* (1903), repeats Sealiah as the forty-fifth genius, “mover of all things,” degrees 221–225, with Psalm 93:18.
- **Robert Ambelain**, *La Kabbale pratique* (1951), carries the French occult-angel system forward with ritual applications and expanded correspondences.
- **Haziel**, the pen name of François Bernard-Termès, popularized the 72 angels in late-twentieth-century French-language guardian-angel literature.

**[Modern tradition]** Haziel’s elaborate personality profiles, calendar days, life missions, and therapeutic prayers are modern devotional elaborations. They are not found in Exodus and should not be attributed wholesale to *Sefer Raziel*, Kircher, Rudd, or Lenain.

# Correspondences

## 1. The two systems that must not be conflated

Vine’s Goetic number is 45. Sealiah’s Shem number is also 45. That numerical pairing does **not** put Vine and Sealiah in the same zodiacal five degrees under every occult table.

| System | Entity | Zodiac | Tarot | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rudd/Shem pairing | Sealiah, angel 45 | 10°–15° Scorpio | Six of Cups decan | Controlling angel |
| *777* Goetic decans | Vine, demon 45 | 20°–30° Gemini | Ten of Swords | Night/cadent demon |
| *777* day partner | Paimon, demon 9 | 20°–30° Gemini | Ten of Swords | Day/succedent demon |

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Crowley’s [*777*, columns CLXV–CLXVI](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf), places “45 Vine” in the night/cadent column for the third decan of Gemini. The magical image is summarized as a lion on a black horse carrying a viper.

This is separate from Rudd’s number-to-number pairing.

## 2. Quinances and pip cards

Each zodiac sign contains:

- three decans of ten degrees;
- six quinances of five degrees;
- two Shem angels per decan;
- in the Golden Dawn Goetic overlay, a day and a night demon per decan.

Therefore a single zodiacal pip card can carry:

- two Shem angels;
- one day Goetic demon;
- one night Goetic demon.

### Sealiah’s card

| Correspondence | Attribution |
|---|---|
| Sign | Scorpio |
| Degrees | 10°–15° Scorpio |
| Decan | Second Scorpio decan, 10°–20° |
| Decan planet | Sun |
| Tarot pip | **Six of Cups** |
| Shem angels | **Sealiah 45** and **Ariel 46** |
| Goetic demons in the *777* day/night overlay | **Aim 23** by day and **Orias 59** by night |

### Vine’s card

| Correspondence | Attribution |
|---|---|
| Sign | Gemini |
| Degrees | 20°–30° Gemini |
| Decan | Third Gemini decan |
| Decan planet | Sun |
| Tarot pip | **Ten of Swords** |
| Shem angels occupying its two quinances | **Lauviah 17** and **Caliel 18** |
| Goetic demon by day | **Paimon 9** |
| Goetic demon by night | **Vine 45** |

This disentangles three superficially similar claims:

1. Vine is no. 45.
2. Sealiah is no. 45 and controls Vine in Rudd.
3. Vine’s own Golden Dawn decan is Gemini, not Sealiah’s Scorpio quinance.

## 3. Day and night

**[Esoteric correspondence]** *777* classifies Vine as a **night/cadent** demon of the third Gemini decan. “Night” here belongs to a Golden Dawn/Crowley decan table.

**[Documented grimoire text]** The older *Goetia* timing rubric says Earls are called during daylight and Kings in specified daylight windows. It does not label Vine a nocturnal spirit.

The apparent contradiction comes from different systems:

- seventeenth-century rank-based conjuration times;
- nineteenth-century Golden Dawn decan polarity.

They were not originally a single calendar.

## 4. Planet of rank

| Vine’s rank | Golden Dawn planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Earl/Count | Mars | Iron |
| Earl in the literal manuscript alloy rule | Venus + Moon symbolism | Equal copper and silver |

Vine’s third-Gemini decan is ruled by the **Sun**, yielding the Ten of Swords as “Sun in Gemini.” This solar decan ruler happens to echo his rank as King, but it arises from a separate astrological calculation.

## 5. Enochian overlays

**[Documented reception]** Crowley’s edition embeds the *Goetia* in a broader Golden Dawn environment that includes the Preliminary Invocation or “Bornless Ritual,” pentagram and hexagram ritual assumptions, and—in some editions and commentary—Enochian divine or elemental material.

**[Documented absence]** No Weyer, Scot, Sloane, or Harley witness assigns Vine an Enochian Aethyr, Governor, Watchtower square, or Dee angel.

**[Modern invention / esoteric correspondence]** Vine-specific Enochian assignments found in modern tables are derived overlays. They may be internally valid to a practitioner’s synthesis, but they are not part of Vine’s sixteenth- or seventeenth-century dossier.

# Iconography

## 1. Textual image

The stable image is a compact emblematic procession:

- lion or lion-headed figure;
- black horse;
- viper carried in the hand.

**[Documented text]** Weyer says *ut leo*, “as a lion.” Mathers’s footnote reports manuscript variants interpreting this as “with the head of a lion.”

**[Open iconographic question]** The sources do not explain whether Vine is:

- an entire lion seated unnaturally on horseback;
- an anthropomorphic lion;
- a human body with a lion’s head.

Modern illustrators choose among all three.

The viper may be carried, grasped, or—in Royne’s corrupted wording—“divided” or held across the arms. No source identifies the snake as a named mythological serpent.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

The sixth edition of Jacques Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* gives:

> **“Vine, grand roi et comte de la cour infernale. Il se montre furieux comme un lion ; un cheval noir lui sert de monture. Il tient une vipère à la main, bâtit des maisons, enfle les rivières et connaît le passé. Dix-neuf légions lui obéissent.”**

Translation:

> “Vine, great king and count of the infernal court. He shows himself furious like a lion; a black horse serves as his mount. He holds a viper in his hand, builds houses, swells rivers, and knows the past. Nineteen legions obey him.”

Source: Collin de Plancy, *Dictionnaire infernal*, sixth edition, Paris, 1863, p. 688; [text and scan reference](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Vine), [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_1).

Notable features:

- de Plancy returns to Weyer’s constructive “builds houses” and “swells rivers”;
- he reduces the temporal office to knowledge of the past;
- he assigns **19 legions**, unsupported by Weyer or the *Goetia* witnesses examined here;
- “furious” is an embellishment.

## 3. Is there a Louis Breton portrait?

**Finding: no securely identified Breton engraving of Vine was found.**

**[Documented publication history]** Louis Le Breton provided numerous demon portraits for the illustrated 1863 edition, but the book did not illustrate every named demon.

The Vine entry is textual. Searches of the digitized edition and the [Commons category of the 1863 illustrations](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Illustrations_from_Dictionnaire_Infernal,_6th_edition) do not yield an engraving labeled Vine.

**[Misattribution]** Modern websites sometimes attach a generic lion-headed mounted demon, or another Breton Goetic portrait, to Vine and call it “de Plancy’s Vine.” Without a plate label or page reference, that attribution should not be accepted.

## 4. Modern art

**[Modern invention]** Modern images commonly add:

- armor or a royal crown;
- a humanoid body;
- towers collapsing in lightning;
- turbulent oceans rather than swollen rivers;
- an oversized snake or staff-serpent;
- solar, Martian, or Scorpio imagery;
- the seal tattooed on the body or horse.

These are visually intelligible interpretations of later correspondences but not details supplied by Weyer.

**[Tradition / personal testimony]** Some contemporary practitioners report seeing Vine as an armored man, a lion-man, a noble king, or a figure whose snake becomes a sword. These are personal visionary reports or “UPG”—unverified personal gnosis—not textual variants.

# People and works

## 1. Historical transmission

### Johann Weyer

**[Documented history]** Johann Weyer published the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* with the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. He was a physician and former associate or student of Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa.

Weyer presented the catalogue in a polemical work opposing credulous demonology and cruel witch prosecution. He did not simply endorse the spirits as objectively described. His introductory and concluding remarks say he deliberately withheld material so that the catalogue would not function as an effective manual.

Vine’s first secure printed attestation is Weyer’s entry.

### Reginald Scot

**[Documented history]** Scot inserted an English version into Book XV of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* in 1584. Like Weyer, Scot’s larger purpose was skeptical and anti-persecution. Later readers nevertheless mined the work as a practical magical source.

Scot’s mistranslation or transformation of stone construction into wall demolition had a greater influence on English occultism than Weyer’s Latin original.

### The anonymous *Lemegeton* compilers

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Seventeenth-century compilers converted Weyer’s prose catalogue into a ritual book by adding:

- seals;
- conjurations;
- rank rules;
- materials;
- timing;
- ritual circles and triangles;
- a fixed total of 72.

The result was not merely a copy of Weyer but a new grimoire construction.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

**[Documented manuscript / disputed authorship]** Peter Smart copied Harley 6483 in 1712–1713. The recension is attributed to Thomas Rudd, but the surviving copy is later than Rudd’s lifetime.

Its major innovation is the systematic angelic control layer. Vine is subordinated to Sealiah because both occupy serial position 45.

### Mathers and Crowley

**[Documented publication]** S. L. MacGregor Mathers prepared an English edition from British Museum manuscripts. Aleister Crowley published it in 1904, adding prefatory, ritual, and interpretive material.

The division of credit is complicated:

- the manuscript collation and principal translation are associated with Mathers;
- Crowley served as publisher/editor and added material;
- the 1904 book’s public identity became strongly associated with Crowley.

The spellings **Viné** and **Vinea**, the 36-legion count, and the polished “overthrow Great Stone Walls” wording spread widely through this edition.

### Joseph H. Peterson

**[Scholarly edition]** Peterson’s 2001 edition re-established manuscript comparison as essential. His online transcriptions make it possible to distinguish:

- Weyer’s Latin;
- Scot’s English;
- Sloane 3825;
- the later Mathers–Crowley reading;
- Folger’s Royne.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

**[Scholarly edition / operative tradition]** Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s angelic apparatus widely accessible. It is the main modern source for the Vine–Sealiah pairing as a complete operational system.

## 2. Modern occult authors

### Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* integrates Goetic spirits with angelic authority, mirror skrying, and a reconstructed temple system.

**[Modern tradition]** Runyon is important to the late-twentieth-century revival of ceremonial Goetia, but no independently documented Vine working or historical discovery unique to him was located. His relevance is systemic rather than biographical to Vine. See the [publisher/author book page](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* republished and interpreted the Crowleyan text for modern occultists.

**[Modern interpretation]** Its psychological and practical framing does not constitute evidence for Vine’s ancient origin. [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL8638564M/Aleister_Crowley%27s_Illustrated_Goetia).

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s work, especially *Pandemonium*, treats the spirit catalogues as an evolving spirit tradition and compares variant lists.

**[Scholarly-occult reconstruction]** His attention to Folger material and *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* strengthens the case that Vine should be studied beside Royne rather than only through Crowley. His historical synthesis is still an interpretation, not a newly discovered pre-Weyer Vine.

### S. Connolly and demonolatry

**[Modern invention]** Modern demonolatry gives Vine the “enn”:

> “Eyesta nas Vine ca laris.”

No such formula occurs in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, Harley 6483, or Mathers–Crowley. It belongs to modern demonolatry publications and practice.

### Frater Achad

**Finding:** no Vine-specific treatment by Frater Achad was established in the materials consulted.

Frater Achad’s Qabalistic work affected later Thelemic number symbolism, but citing him as someone who specifically worked with Vine would require a diary, ritual record, or published discussion not presently identified.

# Grimoire ancestors

## 1. *Testament of Solomon*

The Greek *Testament of Solomon*, compiled over several centuries in late antiquity, contains individually interrogated demons, their forms, offices, thwarting angels, and occasionally seals.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** It is a remote ancestor of the Solomonic demon-interrogation genre.

**[Documented absence]** It contains no clearly identifiable Vine. No demon combines the name with the lion, black horse, viper, towers, and swollen rivers. General resemblance to its angel-constrained demons does not establish identity.

## 2. *Livre des Esperitz*

The *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, is a fifteenth-century French spirit catalogue. Its spirits have ranks, appearances, and offices resembling later catalogues.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Jean-Patrice Boudet and Julien Véronèse have treated such catalogues as important ancestors of Renaissance demonological “who’s who” lists.

**[Documented absence]** Vine is not named in the surviving [full text of the *Livre des Esperitz*](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier).

## 3. Munich handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook contains ritual magic, spirit names, and conjurations. Richard Kieckhefer edited it as *Forbidden Rites*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** It shows the practical necromantic environment from which later Solomonic compilations emerged.

**[Documented absence]** No secure Vine entry matching Weyer’s demon has been identified.

## 4. *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer says his list derives from a work titled approximately *Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis…* Trithemius’s 1508 *Antipalus maleficiorum* lists titles such as *Liber Officiorum* and *De officiis spirituum*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** A now-lost or fragmentarily surviving *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* family is the most plausible immediate ancestor of Weyer’s catalogue.

**[Disputed]** A title in Trithemius does not prove that Vine appeared in a 1508 copy. No quoted Vine paragraph survives from that date.

The Folger Royne entry is the best extant comparative evidence for an alternate branch of the same catalogue family.

## 5. *Book of Abramelin*

The *Abramelin* tradition contains long lists of subordinate spirits under major demonic rulers.

**[Documented absence]** No demon securely identifiable as Vine appears in its lists. Similar names can result from unstable transliteration, but resemblance without matching offices is inadequate.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Earliest attestation

**Best current finding:** Weyer, 1577, is the first securely dated attestation of **Vine** in this form.

The Folger **Royne** manuscript is approximately contemporary, not demonstrably earlier. Lost *Liber Officiorum* books probably preceded both, but no surviving pre-1577 Vine paragraph has been produced.

Accordingly:

- “Vine is an ancient pagan god” — unsupported;
- “Vine appears in the medieval *Livre des Esperitz*” — not supported by the surviving text;
- “Vine certainly comes from a 1508 grimoire” — overstates what Trithemius’s title list proves.

## 2. Is the original name Vine, Vinea, or Royne?

- **Vine** is the first secure printed reading.
- **Royne** is an approximately contemporary related reading.
- **Vinea** is a later explicit alias in Mathers–Crowley.
- **Viné** is a Mathers–Crowley display form.

**[Disputed]** *Vinea* may preserve an etymological insight, be a Latinizing restoration, or be an editorial expansion. No evidence currently shows that it was Weyer’s original word.

## 3. How many legions?

| Source | Legions |
|---|---:|
| Weyer, 1577 | Not stated |
| Scot, 1584 | Not stated |
| Royne, Folger V.b.26 | 26 |
| Sloane 3825 | 35 |
| Mathers–Crowley | 36 |
| de Plancy, 1863 | 19 |

There is no defensible way to reduce these to a single historically “correct” number.

- **35** has strong manuscript support in Sloane 3825.
- **36** is canonical in the widely read 1904 edition.
- **26** belongs to the Royne relative.
- **19** is de Plancy’s unexplained departure.
- Weyer’s silence is not evidence for zero legions.

## 4. Builder or destroyer?

This is one of the clearest transmission problems.

- Weyer: builds spacious towers and constructs stone houses.
- Scot: builds towers but throws down stone walls.
- *Goetia*: builds towers and throws down great stone walls.
- de Plancy: builds houses.

**Most likely explanation:** Scot mistranslated or deliberately recast *lapideas domus extruit*. The English *Goetia* inherited Scot’s destructive wording.

**[Tradition]** Later practitioners often reconcile the readings by giving Vine mastery over both construction and demolition. That synthesis is reasonable within occult practice but is not Weyer’s exact statement.

## 5. Rivers or storms?

- Weyer: makes streams swollen.
- Scot: makes waters rough.
- Sloane 3825 and Mathers–Crowley: makes waters rough “with storms.”
- de Plancy: swells rivers.

“Storms” is a later development. It should not be quoted as Weyer’s wording.

## 6. Witches or wizards?

- Weyer: *maleficis*, a broad and context-sensitive term.
- Scot: witches.
- Sloane 3825: witches.
- Mathers–Crowley: witches and wizards.

“Wizards” is a late explicit addition. It may clarify the compiler’s intention, but it is not present in the Latin entry.

## 7. The metal of an Earl

The transmitted *Goetia* recipe gives Earls equal parts copper and silver. Mathers considered this an error because it left Mars unrepresented and supplied iron/Mars.

Thus two answers circulate:

- **Text-critical answer:** copper and silver in equal proportion.
- **Golden Dawn correspondence:** iron and Mars.

For Vine as King and Earl, gold/Sun remains separately applicable.

## 8. Sealiah as Vine’s eternal opponent

**[Tradition]** Harley 6483 makes Sealiah Vine’s controlling angel.

**[Historical limitation]** This is not an ancient cosmic rivalry independently recorded in Jewish scripture. The pairing results from matching the forty-fifth angel to the forty-fifth Goetic spirit.

Claims that Sealiah and Vine have fought since creation or embody eternally opposed metaphysical principles are later legend unless supported by a specific visionary lineage.

## 9. Scorpio versus Gemini

Both appear in genuine occult sources but describe different systems:

- Sealiah no. 45: 10°–15° Scorpio.
- Vine in *777*: 20°–30° Gemini.

Assigning Vine directly to 10°–15° Scorpio because he is paired with Sealiah collapses Rudd’s serial pairing into the Golden Dawn’s independent Goetic-decan table.

## 10. De Plancy’s alleged portrait

No securely labeled Louis Le Breton portrait of Vine has been found. The 1863 dictionary entry is real; the commonly claimed “de Plancy image of Vine” is usually unreferenced or misassigned.

## 11. Dangerousness claims

**[Modern legend]** Websites variously call Vine:

- one of the “deadliest kings”;
- uniquely treacherous;
- Satan’s most faithful servant;
- able to seize a soul without consent;
- a patron of natural disasters or nuclear warfare.

These claims do not occur in Weyer, Scot, the principal *Goetia* witnesses, or de Plancy. One example of the modern apocryphal genre is the material explicitly labeled “wiki/apocrypha” at [Goetia Official](https://goetia-official.com/spirit/vine.html).

The older descriptions do not comment on Vine’s honesty, temperament toward the conjurer, sacrificial preferences, incense, colors, enn, or moral personality.

# Open questions

1. **What was the archetypal name behind Vine and Royne?**  
   A full palaeographic comparison of the relevant manuscripts may show whether the difference arose from damaged exemplars, minim confusion, or deliberate revision.

2. **Does a securely dated pre-1577 *Liber Officiorum* witness contain the spirit?**  
   None has yet been demonstrated here. Discovery of such a manuscript could displace Weyer as the earliest attestation.

3. **Where did Mathers obtain “Vinea”?**  
   His footnotes cite unnamed “Codices,” but a folio-level source for this particular alias should be identified.

4. **Which manuscript first introduced 35 legions?**  
   Sloane 3825 securely carries the number, but the immediate exemplar and the reason for selecting 35 remain unknown.

5. **How did 35 become 36?**  
   Possibilities include scribal misreading, collation with another manuscript, numerological regularization, or editorial error. Direct comparison of all witnesses is necessary.

6. **Where did de Plancy’s 19 come from?**  
   It is not supported by Weyer, Scot, Royne, Sloane 3825, or Mathers–Crowley. It could be a compositorial error or derive from an unidentified intermediary.

7. **Is *vīnea*, the Roman siege gallery, a historical etymology or a modern back-formation?**  
   The fit with towers and walls is strong, but no early gloss establishes it.

8. **Can Vine’s seal variants be stemmatically classified?**  
   High-resolution comparison could determine which strokes are merely handwriting and which define distinct branches of the *Lemegeton*.

9. **Did the Rudd pairing exist before Harley 6483?**  
   Skinner and Rankine argue for an earlier Rudd original, but no autograph containing the complete Vine–Sealiah page has been identified.

10. **Is there an overlooked Louis Le Breton sketch or proof?**  
    No labeled published portrait was located, but surviving preparatory material has not been exhaustively surveyed.

11. **Was *Fire City*’s Atum Vine intentionally named for Goetic Vine?**  
    The demonic name makes this plausible, but an explicit statement from the filmmakers would be preferable.

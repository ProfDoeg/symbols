# Oriax: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary key

This dossier distinguishes the following kinds of statement:

- **Documented text** — directly present in a cited manuscript, early printed book, or critical edition.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — an inference from textual comparison, palaeography, bibliography, or historical context.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — a systematic occult attribution, historically documentable but not a claim established by historical or scientific evidence.
- **Tradition/legend** — a claim transmitted within magical or religious tradition.
- **Disputed** — a proposed identification or derivation for which competing evidence exists or no textual bridge has been demonstrated.
- **Modern invention** — an addition first found in modern occultism, fiction, games, or popular reference works.
- **Negative finding** — an expected connection for which the surveyed sources furnish no evidence.

Long source passages are represented by short diplomatic quotations followed by exhaustive clause-by-clause readings; the links lead to the complete public-domain texts.

## The name and its origins

### The source-chain spellings

Joseph Peterson’s manuscript concordance gives the following readings:

| Source | Date | Reading | Position |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Johann Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* | 1577 | **Orias** | 49 |
| Reginald Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft* | 1584 | **Orias** | 49 |
| Sloane MS 3825 | seventeenth century | **Orias** | 59 |
| Sloane MS 3648 | after c. 1665 | **Orias** | 59 |
| Sloane MS 2731 | probably 1680s | **Orias** | 59 |
| Harley MS 6483 | 1712–13 | **Orias** | 59 |
| Northwestern University MS 65 | later witness | **Orias** | 59 |
| Mathers–Crowley, *Goetia* | 1904 | **Oriax, or Orias** | 59 |
| Crowley, *777* | 1909 | **Oriax** | 59 |
| Peterson, *Lesser Key of Solomon* | 2001 | **Orias** | 59 |

The complete comparative table is available in Peterson’s [Goetia spirit-name concordance](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

**Documented finding:** *Oriax* is absent from every principal manuscript reading tabulated by Peterson. It first appears in this chain as Mathers’s or Crowley’s alternative form in 1904.

**Disputed explanation:** *Oriax* may be a transcriptional error, editorial emendation or deliberately exoticized spelling. No manuscript exemplar or editorial note explaining the final **-x** has been found. Calling it definitively a “misprint” therefore goes beyond the evidence.

### Earliest secure attestation

The earliest secure printed attestation located is Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, added to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in **1577**, p. 927.

This date matters. Weyer's principal treatise first appeared in 1563, but the *Pseudomonarchia* catalogue was not in that first edition. Statements dating “Orias in Weyer” to 1563 conflate the history of the parent book with the history of its demon catalogue. Peterson supplies the 1577 Latin and its original pagination in his [parallel Weyer–Scot edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Does Orias occur before Weyer?

**Negative finding:** no secure pre-1577 occurrence of the exact demon-name **Orias** was found in the principal antecedent catalogues surveyed.

#### *Livre des Esperitz*

The French *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, is an important ancestor of Weyer’s catalogue. Its surviving list includes analogues of numerous later Goetic spirits—Amon, Poymon, Asmoday, Oze, Fenix and others—but not Orias. The [complete manuscript transcription](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier) lists its spirits at fols. 179r–182v.

Jean-Patrice Boudet found approximately thirty-five correspondences between its catalogue and Weyer’s. That establishes a textual family; it does not establish Orias in that family before Weyer. See Boudet’s [“Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

Julien Véronèse’s scholarship is particularly important for the wider Solomonic and *Ars Notoria* corpus, but the detailed published comparison of the *Livre des Esperitz* with Weyer is Boudet’s, not Véronèse’s.

#### *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer calls his source:

> “Officium spirituum … Liber officiorum spirituum … Empto. Salomonis”

He says he deliberately omitted material so that readers could not employ the text operationally. Trithemius’s *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508 lists an *Officium spirituum*, showing that a work or family with that title existed before Weyer.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** Orias may have been in the particular *Officium* manuscript Weyer used. But that lost or unidentified exemplar is not itself an extant attestation. The demonstrable record still begins with the 1577 printing.

#### Munich handbook, CLM 849

Richard Kieckhefer’s fifteenth-century Munich handbook contains spirit catalogues and ritual material ancestral or parallel to later Goetia. Peterson’s concordance finds equivalents for several Weyer spirits, but none for Orias. Thus it supplies context for the genre, not a prehistory of this particular name.

#### *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin catalogues include **Oriens**, the eastern ruler. Oriens means “rising” or “east” in Latin and belongs to the widespread four-directional kings tradition.

**Negative finding:** no early text identifies Oriens with Orias. The similarity begins with *Ori-* and is insufficient to demonstrate descent.

#### *Testament of Solomon*: Ornias

The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* begins its sequence with **Ornias**. Ornias attacks a boy, takes part of his wages and vitality, changes form, is associated with Aquarius, and can appear as a lion. Solomon binds him with a divinely given ring. The primary translation is available at [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Testament_of_Solomon.djvu/17).

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine describe **Ornias/Orias** as a possible correspondence, while acknowledging that direct overlap between the *Testament* and Goetic names is minimal.

The parallels are real:

- a very similar name;
- a lion form;
- shape-changing;
- a Solomonic binding narrative.

The differences are equally substantial:

- **Ornias** is not a marquis;
- he does not ride a horse or hold two serpents;
- his characteristic conduct is vampiric assault;
- his offices are not planetary mansions, dignities or favour;
- no surviving intermediate text carries Ornias into Orias.

**Disputed conclusion:** Ornias is a plausible distant namesake or contamination point, not an established etymon.

### Proposed classical and ancient derivations

No Baal-, Astarte-, Amun-, Osiris- or other securely identifiable ancient cult-name lies behind Orias in the way that identifiable antecedents lie behind some Goetic names.

#### Greek *Oreias* / Latin *Oreas*

Greek **Ὀρειάς**, *Oreias*, denotes a mountain nymph or Oread, from **ὄρος**, “mountain.” Latin has *Oreas*. The phonetic resemblance to *Orias* is apparent.

**Disputed:** the gender, mythology and semantic field do not fit the Goetic marquis, and no transmission chain has been demonstrated. It remains a linguistic resemblance, not a supported derivation.

#### Osiris

Modern occult tables and user-created name sites sometimes connect Orias with **Osiris**.

**Modern speculative claim:** no Weyer, Scot, *Lemegeton*, Rudd, Mathers, Crowley or de Plancy text makes that identification. No phonological or documentary chain from Egyptian *Wsir* through Greek *Osiris* to *Orias* has been shown.

#### Mithraic or Phoenician claims

Poke Runyon’s modern table remarks that Orias “has a relation to Mithraic symbolism” and might be among “Phoenician Seven Spirits of Creation.”

**Modern esoteric conjecture:** Runyon does not supply an ancient text, inscription or philological derivation for either claim. These should not be treated as established origins.

### Name conclusion

The best-supported statement is modest:

> **Orias is an unexplained early-modern demon-name, securely attested in Weyer in 1577. Its pre-Weyer derivation is unknown.**

## Number and order: Weyer, Scot and the *Goetia*

### Why Weyer has 69 and the *Goetia* 72

Weyer prints 69 spirits. Scot’s English sequence contains 68 because Weyer’s no. 4, **Pruflas alias Bufas**, is missing. The *Ars Goetia* contains 72 by:

- omitting Pruflas;
- adding **Vassago** as no. 3;
- adding **Seere**, **Dantalion** and **Andromalius** as nos. 70–72;
- extensively rearranging the inherited catalogue.

Thus the arithmetic is:

**69 − 1 + 4 = 72.**

Peterson documents the changes and observes that the Goetic order looks as if an ancestor composed of separate leaves or blocks had been rearranged. He treats that as a reconstruction, not a demonstrated historical event. The full comparison appears in his [Weyer introduction and concordance](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Orias’s movement

Orias shifts from Weyer/Scot no. 49 to Goetia no. 59. It is not simply moved ten places by the four additions; the entire middle and later catalogue is reordered.

Nearby comparison:

| Spirit | Weyer | Goetia |
|---|---:|---:|
| Amy | 61 | 58 |
| Orias | 49 | 59 |
| Vapula | 59 | 60 |
| Zagan | 48 | 61 |
| Valac | 50 | 62 |
| Orobas | 58 | 55 |

This rearrangement is one reason simple numerical deductions about supposed ancient planetary order should be treated cautiously.

## The description across the sources

### 1. Weyer, 1577

**Documented text:** *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, no. 49, Basel 1577, original p. 927.

Short diplomatic excerpt:

> “Orias Marchio magnus, visitur ut leo, in equo fortissimo equitans, cauda serpentina: in dextera portat duos grandes serpentes etiam exibilantes.”

A complete close translation of the entry is:

Orias is a great marquis. He is seen as a lion riding upon a very strong horse, with a serpentine tail. In his right hand he carries two large serpents, themselves hissing. He knows the mansions of the planets and perfectly teaches the powers of the stars. He transforms human beings; confers dignities, prelacies and confirmations; and grants the favour of friends and enemies. He presides over thirty legions.

The [Latin and parallel Scot text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm) preserve several important terms:

- *Marchio magnus* — great marquis;
- *cauda serpentina* — “with a serpentine tail,” grammatically ambiguous as to bearer;
- *mansiones* — mansions or stations;
- *vires sidereas* — stellar forces, powers or “virtues”;
- *transmutat homines* — transforms human beings;
- *prælaturas* — ecclesiastical or comparable high offices;
- *confirmationes* — confirmations, establishments or ratifications of rank.

Weyer supplies no seal for Orias.

### 2. Scot, 1584

**Documented text:** Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter 2, original 1584 p. 275; Brinsley Nicholson’s 1886 first-edition reprint, p. 324.

Short diplomatic excerpt:

> “Orias is a great marquesse, and is seene as a lion riding on a strong horsse, with a serpents taile…”

The remainder says that he carries two great hissing serpents in his right hand; knows the mansion of planets; perfectly teaches the virtues of the stars; transforms men; gives dignities, prelacies and confirmations; grants the favour of friends and foes; and commands thirty legions.

The page markers can be checked in the [Project Gutenberg transcription of Nicholson’s reprint](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/60766/60766-h/60766-h.htm), while Peterson provides the [parallel Latin and English](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Scot’s text is effectively a close translation of Weyer. Peterson considers Abraham Fleming a probable translator of this and other Latin material in Scot, but the attribution is not absolutely established for each passage.

Scot’s purpose was expositional and sceptical: the magical catalogue occurs within a work attacking witchcraft prosecutions, imposture and ritual magic. Printing the catalogue did not constitute an endorsement of its claimed powers.

### 3. The seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia*

**Documented text:** Peterson’s base text, principally Sloane MS 3825, fols. 108v–109r.

Short diplomatic excerpt:

> “The 59 spirit is named Orias, he is a great Marquiz and appeareth in ye forme of a Lyon, riding on a mighty horse…”

The complete substance is:

- Orias is no. 59 and a great marquis.
- He appears as a lion riding a mighty horse.
- The serpent’s tail remains syntactically unattached.
- He holds two great hissing serpents in his right hand.
- He teaches the virtues of the stars.
- He knows the mansions of the planets and teaches how their virtues are to be understood.
- He transforms men.
- He grants dignities, prelacies and confirmations.
- He grants the favour of friends and foes.
- He governs thirty legions.
- His seal is to be made and worn.

Peterson notes that the manuscript reading “places” should be corrected to **prelacies**, supported by Weyer, Scot, Sloane 3648, Sloane 2731, Northwestern MS 65 and Harley 6483. The complete entry and manuscript variants appear in Peterson’s [online critical text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

### 4. Harley MS 6483 and Rudd’s version

Harley 6483, titled *Liber malorum Spirituum, seu Goetia*, is dated 1712–13. Its Orias entry remains substantially the inherited Goetia description.

Skinner and Rankine print it on pp. 160–61 of *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*:

> “The fifty ninth Spirit is Orias he is a great Marquis and appeareth in the form of a Lion…”

They gloss “mansion” as the spirit’s place in the sky in relation to the twenty-eight lunar mansions. That gloss is editorial; Weyer’s Latin by itself does not specify the twenty-eight lunar mansions rather than planetary houses or stations generally.

Harley 6483’s distinctive innovation is not a new Orias office but the **double seal**: the demonic character is coupled with an opposing Shem angel, its Hebrew name and an associated Psalm verse. Skinner and Rankine’s [transcription and introduction](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html) explain the procedure.

### 5. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**Documented text:** *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, p. 41.

Short excerpt:

> “The Fifty-ninth Spirit is Oriax, or Orias. He is a Great Marquis, and appeareth in the Form of a Lion…”

The complete substance remains the manuscript description: lion, horse, serpent’s tail, two hissing serpents, stellar virtues, planetary mansions, transformation, dignities and prelacies, favour of friends and foes, thirty legions.

Mathers adds two instructive notes:

- the form may be read as “with the Face of a Lion”;
- concerning the serpent’s tail, the editor asks, “The horse, or the Markist?”

The latter is a printed acknowledgment that the grammar does not say clearly whether the lion-rider or the horse has the tail. See the complete [Sacred Texts 1904 edition](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

### 6. Peterson, 2001

Joseph H. Peterson, ed., *Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis: The Lesser Key of Solomon* (York Beach: Weiser, 2001), returns to the manuscript reading **Orias**, with Sloane 3825 as the main text and extensive comparison against Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483 and other witnesses.

Peterson’s edition is not merely a reprint of Mathers–Crowley. It removes the unexplained privileging of *Oriax*, restores manuscript spellings and separates early text from Mathers’s and Crowley’s editorial additions. The entry corresponds to Sloane 3825, fols. 108v–109r, in the [online edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

### 7. Collin de Plancy, 1863

**Documented text:** sixth edition, p. 508.

Short French excerpt:

> “Orias, démon des astrologues et des devins, grand marquis de l’empire infernal.”

De Plancy describes a furious lion sitting on a horse that has a serpent’s tail. Orias carries a viper in each hand, knows astronomy, teaches astrology, metamorphoses people at their will, procures dignities and titles, and commands thirty legions. See the [1863 French entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Orias).

Changes from Weyer include:

- “astrologers and diviners” as a class;
- a furious lion;
- explicitly assigning the tail to the horse;
- one viper in each hand rather than two in the right hand;
- separating astronomy from astrology;
- “at their will”;
- replacing prelacies and confirmations with dignities and titles.

These are de Plancy’s retelling, not manuscript variants of the *Goetia*.

### What Orias is not said to do

**Negative textual findings:** the principal entries do not say that Orias:

- teaches languages generally;
- teaches every liberal science;
- discovers treasure;
- procures erotic love;
- provides familiars;
- causes invisibility;
- transmutes metals;
- answers all questions;
- reveals the past and future;
- assumes any desired animal or object;
- causes reconciliation.

Those offices belong to other Goetic spirits. Orias grants **favour**, which is not identical to reconciliation or romantic love.

Likewise, *transmutat homines* means that he transforms human beings. “Transforms anyone into any form desired” is a later expansion not present in Weyer’s or Orias’s Goetia entry.

### Second form and compelled human appearance

Unlike Marbas, Botis, Marchosias and various other spirits, Orias has no individually prescribed second shape.

The general *Goetia* hexagram is shown to spirits so that they may be compelled to obedience and to take a human form; the conjurations also demand a “fair and humane shape.” Thus a practitioner could demand a human manifestation under the general rite, but it is not a second form peculiar to Orias. See Peterson’s [hexagram and conjurations](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

## Rank, metal, planet and timing

### Marquis: Moon and silver

The seventeenth-century “Observations” assign:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince/prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury |
| Earl | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver |

Accordingly, Orias’s seal should be made in **silver**, symbolically lunar. This is a rank correspondence; it is not derived from Orias’s appearance or stellar office. The manuscript rule is transcribed in Peterson’s [Goetia “Observations”](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Crowley’s revised *777* table gives the same Moon/silver correspondence for marquises, although it changes the earls to Mars/iron. See [*777 Revised*, p. 169](https://ozpoetry.lingama.net/poetry/attachments/may2009/liber777revised.pdf).

### Binding time for a marquis

The *Goetia*’s general rank rule permits marquises to be bound:

- from **3:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m.**;
- and from **9:00 p.m. until sunrise**.

This gives Orias no unique clock hour. It is the common marquis interval.

### Lunar age

The same “Observations” recommend working when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 14 days old. This is another general Goetia rule, not an individual Orias correspondence.

The Secret Seal of Solomon has additional construction timings in some versions. Those instructions should not be misreported as the hour for Orias’s personal seal.

### Later night attribution

In *777*, Orias belongs to the night or succedent series of Goetic demons. This is a Golden Dawn/Crowley astrological overlay, not part of Weyer, Scot or the seventeenth-century entry.

## The seal

### Manuscript and printed history

**Documented text:**

- Weyer gives no sigils for his 69 spirits.
- Scot reproduces no Orias sigil.
- The seventeenth-century *Goetia* manuscripts add individual “seals of obedience.”
- Sloane 3825 places Orias’s seal after the entry at fol. 109r.
- Sloane 2731 and Harley 6483 carry corresponding hand-drawn forms.
- Mathers–Crowley redraw the seal and enclose it in the familiar circular lamen.
- Rudd’s recension couples it to the Harahel material.

### Form

The 1904 seal may be described as follows:

- a circular outer lamen;
- within it, a bilaterally balanced angular character;
- a broad upper span with raised left and right terminals;
- small rounded or ring-like terminal marks near the two upper extremities;
- inward-descending strokes that form an arch or wide Y;
- a central vertical descent;
- angular lower feet or hooks extending to either side.

The clean 1904 reproduction can be inspected at [From Old Books](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/059-Seal-of-Oriax/) and in the [Sacred Texts edition](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

### Variants

The core motif remains recognizable, but:

- manuscript line weight and proportions differ;
- some strokes curve where later versions are angular;
- the upper terminal dots or loops vary in size;
- the central descent and lower feet vary with the copyist;
- the enclosing circle is characteristic of the 1904 presentation and should not automatically be treated as part of every manuscript character.

Harley 6483 is more elaborate because the lamen is conceptually double-sided or doubled: demonic seal outward, controlling angel and Psalm toward the magician. Skinner and Rankine state that the angelic side should face the operator’s heart.

**Open palaeographic limitation:** an exact stroke-by-stroke stemma of the Orias seals in Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825 and Harley 6483 has not been published in the freely accessible comparative sources consulted. Claims that a particular tiny hook is “the original” are therefore premature.

## The angel: Harahel

### Identification

| Lenain planetary sub-ruler | Sun |

### Derivation of the seventy-two names

Exodus 14:19–21 contains, in the Masoretic Hebrew text used by the tradition, three verses of seventy-two letters each. The later construction writes:

1. verse 19 from right to left;
2. verse 20 from left to right;
3. verse 21 from right to left;
4. the resulting rows one above another;
5. the vertical columns yield seventy-two groups of three letters.

The 59th column yields **הרח**. Christian kabbalistic angelology adds **אל**, *El*, producing **הרחאל**.

The Golden Dawn explanation and table are preserved in its [Shem ha-Mephorash lecture](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Schemhamphorash.html).

### Jewish background versus later angelology

The history requires several distinctions.

**Documented Jewish tradition:** a seventy-two-fold divine Name derived from Exodus is medieval and perhaps earlier in oral development. Rashi, the *Bahir*, later kabbalistic literature and printed *Sefer Raziel* traditions know forms of the seventy-twofold Name.

Joshua Trachtenberg cautions that the triads function as constructed names of power, not necessarily ordinary Hebrew words with dictionary etymologies. His discussion appears in [*Jewish Magic and Superstition*, pp. 95–98](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

**Later Christian-kabbalistic development:** converting every triad into a fully named angel by adding *-el* or *-yah*, then assigning meanings, planets, degrees, Psalms and individual offices, is not all contained in the earliest Jewish witnesses.

Therefore:

- the triad **הרח** is mechanically derivable from Exodus;
- “Harahel” as a discrete personality with a fixed office is a later angelological construction;
- “God who knows all things” is an esoteric gloss, not a secure literal translation of **הרחאל**.

### Transmission

#### *Sefer Raziel*

Printed recensions of *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* contain the seventy-twofold Name and associated magical material.

**Tradition:** later occult authors regularly call the complete modern Harahel system “from the *Sefer Raziel*.” That wording is too broad. The book is an ancestor of the name tradition, but the complete Lenain-style biography—sterility, children, libraries, stockbrokers and a twenty-minute hour—is not found as such in the medieval Jewish stratum.

#### Reuchlin and de Vigenère

Johann Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped Christian readers treat the suffixed triads as angelic names. Blaise de Vigenère subsequently transmitted angelic seals attributed to this series.

Golden Dawn copies sometimes corrupt Vigenère’s name as “Blaise Vininaire.” That is a transmission or typographical error, not another magician.

#### Kircher

Athanasius Kircher printed an elaborate Christian-kabbalistic treatment of the seventy-two names in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, vol. II, part I (1652). Peterson’s transcription is available as [Kircher’s *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

Kircher is part of the early-modern transmission. He did not, however, originate the ordinal demon–angel pairing found in Rudd.

#### Rudd and Harley 6483

Harley 6483 aligns the 72 Goetic demons and 72 Shem angels by number. Orias no. 59 is therefore constrained by angel no. 59, Harahel.

Skinner and Rankine explain that the manuscript’s double seals contain:

- the demon’s character;
- a conjectural Hebrew spelling of the demon’s name;
- the corresponding angelic seal;
- the angel’s Hebrew name;
- a Latin Psalm verse.

For Orias the Latin is:

> “Ab ortu Solis usque ad occasum laudabile nomen Domini.”

It is Psalm 112:3 in the Vulgate system, Psalm 113:3 in modern English/Hebrew numbering. Rudd’s wording differs slightly from the standard Vulgate’s *A solis ortu*.

The practical logic is **ligatio**, binding or constraining: the angel’s name and biblical verse establish superior divine authority over the demon. The angelic side is worn toward the heart, with the demonic seal presented outward. See Skinner and Rankine’s [introduction, pp. 14–17, and Orias, pp. 160–61](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html).

#### Is Harley 6483 Thomas Rudd’s autograph?

No. Harley 6483 is dated 1712–13, whereas Thomas Rudd died in 1656.

Skinner and Rankine argue that it copies an earlier manuscript written or owned by Rudd, dating that hypothesized original to approximately 1655–56. Sheets labelled “Dr Rudd” and related material support a Rudd connection.

**Scholarly caution:** “Rudd’s recension” or “material attributed to Rudd” is defensible. “Rudd’s autograph manuscript Harley 6483” is not.

#### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique*, pp. 86–87, calls Harahel’s attribute “Dieu qui connaît toutes choses.” It assigns:

- degrees 291–295;
- the thirtieth *décade*;
- the decan genius Isrô;
- solar influence;
- 17 May, 28 July, 8 October, 19 December and 1 March;
- 7:20–7:40 p.m.;
- Psalm 112:3.

Lenain says Harahel acts against female sterility, makes children submissive and respectful, and rules treasure, exchange agents, public funds, archives, libraries, collections of rarities, printing and bookselling. His entry can be checked in the [1823 scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365) and [searchable PDF](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf).

Degrees 291°–295° equal **20°–25° Capricorn** if the zodiac begins at 0° Aries.

#### Papus

Papus’s kabbalistic compilations transmitted nineteenth-century French occult angelology and treated Lenain as an important practical source.

**Caution:** no evidence was found that Papus independently established the Orias–Harahel pairing or a new Harahel etymology. His role is transmission and popularization.

#### Golden Dawn

The Golden Dawn starts its 72 angelic quinances at **0° Leo**, not at 0° Aries. Its angel no. 59, spelled **Herachael**, therefore falls at **20°–25° Taurus**, under Saturn. It gives the meaning “Permeating all Things” and Psalm 113:3.

This is a genuine conflict with Lenain’s 20°–25° Capricorn, not a difference that can be solved by respelling the angel. Both systems preserve the ordinal number while rotating the zodiacal starting point.

#### Crowley

Crowley’s *777* transmitted Golden Dawn tables, but his Goetic demon table and Shem-angel table are separate layers. Crowley’s Oriax correspondence is a Scorpio decan; the Golden Dawn Herachael correspondence is a Taurus quinance.

#### Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reproduces much of the French Lenain tradition, including Harahel as “God Who Knows All Things.” An English translation is available [here](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf).

#### Haziel

“Haziel,” the pen name of François Bernard-Termès, popularized a devotional guardian-angel version of the 72 angels in the late twentieth century.

His school’s characteristic developments include:

- birth angels tied to calendar blocks;
- prayers and moralized personality profiles;
- “physical,” emotional and intellectual ruling angels;
- expanded positive and negative biographies.

These are modern elaborations of the Lenain-derived system, not material in Weyer, Rudd or medieval *Sefer Raziel*. Dates such as 9–13, 10–15 or 11–15 January vary between modern calculators because they employ different boundary conventions and astronomical approximations.

### Psalm numbering

The verse is:

- **Psalm 113:3** in the Hebrew Bible and most modern English Bibles;
- **Psalm 112:3** in the Vulgate and Septuagint numbering.

This explains why Lenain and Rudd say Psalm 112 while Golden Dawn and modern English tables say Psalm 113. It is the same verse, not two competing Psalms.

### Why the Six of Cups

Golden Dawn *Book T* assigns:

- 5 Cups — Mars in Scorpio, 0°–10°;
- **6 Cups — Sun in Scorpio, 10°–20°**;
- 7 Cups — Venus in Scorpio, 20°–30°.

The Six of Cups is titled the **Lord of Pleasure**. See [*Book T*, pp. 4 and corresponding card section](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/mathers-and-felkin-golden-dawn-book-t-the-tarot-1888.pdf).

Crowley’s *777* places spirit no. 59, Oriax, on key-scale row 24, Scorpio, in the night/succedent column. Because each ten-degree decan has a day and night spirit:

- day spirit: **Aim no. 23**;
- night spirit: **Oriax no. 59**;
- shared decan: 10°–20° Scorpio;
- shared pip: Six of Cups.

### Three different planetary statements

The following are frequently conflated:

1. **Moon** — planet belonging to Orias’s rank as marquis; determines silver.
2. **Sun** — ruler of the Six-of-Cups decan, Sun in Scorpio.
3. **Saturn or Sun** — sub-ruler assigned to Harahel’s quinance in Golden Dawn or Lenain respectively.

All three can coexist inside their own systems. They do not derive from one unified ancient attribution.

### Enochian overlay

Harley 6482 includes Rudd material concerning Dee’s *Tabula Sancta cum Tabulis Enochi*, and Skinner and Rankine discuss a broader Rudd synthesis of angelic, demonic and Enochian material.

**Documented:** Rudd’s surviving magical corpus juxtaposes Goetic and Enochian techniques.

**Negative finding:** no early source consulted supplies a unique Enochian governor, aethyr, sub-angle or tablet square specifically and unambiguously belonging to Orias. Exact internet tables making such assignments are later Golden Dawn-derived or modern syncretic constructions.

## Iconography

### Weyer and Scot

Neither gives a portrait or seal. Orias exists solely as verbal description.

### Manuscript seal

The first stable visual identifier is the *Ars Goetia* seal. The manuscripts do not supply a full figurative portrait of the lion and horse.

### Mathers–Crowley

The 1904 circular sigil became the dominant modern emblem. It is repeatedly reproduced on jewelry, altar cloths, tattoos, cards and modern demonological illustrations.

**Modern reception:** many products marketed as the “ancient seal of Oriax” reproduce the 1904 redrawing rather than a diplomatic copy of a seventeenth-century manuscript.

### De Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy’s sixth edition is famous for Louis Le Breton’s demon portraits, but Orias’s p. 508 notice is text-only.

The detailed illustration catalogue proceeds alphabetically through “Origines du monde,” then gives **Orobas**, with no Orias entry. See the [ORAEDES catalogue](https://oraedes.fr/Medias/Dictionnaire-infernal) and the [1863 facsimile](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_1).

**Negative finding:** no authenticated Le Breton portrait of Orias was located. Images circulated online as “de Plancy’s Orias” should be checked carefully; they are often Orobas, another engraved demon, or contemporary fan art.

### Modern art

Modern illustrators generally resolve the ambiguous grammar in one of three ways:

1. lion-bodied rider with a serpent tail on a normal horse;
2. lion-headed humanoid riding a serpent-tailed horse;
3. anthropomorphic lion whose arms or hands are serpents.

Only the shared elements—lion, horse, serpentine tail and two serpents—are anchored in the early text. Colour, armour, trident, wings and elaborate crowns are modern design choices.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer, physician and pupil of Agrippa, printed the first secure Orias entry in 1577. His purpose was polemical: to expose the vanities and blasphemies of would-be magicians and to argue against indiscriminate witch persecution.

He nevertheless accepted a spiritual universe more readily than a modern materialist would. “Sceptic” here means sceptical of witchcraft accusations and magical pretensions, not necessarily disbelief in demons.

### Reginald Scot

Scot carried Weyer’s catalogue into English in 1584. His translation transmitted spellings and errors that appear to have influenced the English *Goetia*. Pruflas’s omission is especially important.

### The anonymous *Lemegeton* compiler or compilers

Seventeenth-century compilers:

- reordered the catalogue;
- expanded it to 72;
- added seals;
- combined it with conjurations derived from the *Heptameron* and other sources;
- embedded it within the five-part *Lemegeton*.

Peterson’s manuscript survey is the most reliable freely available overview: [*The Lesser Key of Solomon*, introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm).

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Thomas Rudd is the attributed source behind the Harley recension. A later copyist—identified in the related manuscript tradition with Peter Smart—preserved the material after Rudd’s death.

Rudd’s principal importance for Orias is the introduction of Harahel and Psalm 113:3 as an individual controlling apparatus.

### Collin de Plancy

De Plancy transformed the concise grimoire entry into a French demonological-dictionary notice. His 1863 wording influenced encyclopedias and modern character descriptions even though Orias received no Breton portrait.

### Mathers and Crowley

S. L. MacGregor Mathers prepared the English text and seals from British Museum manuscripts. Aleister Crowley published it in 1904 with a preliminary invocation and his “Initiated Interpretation.”

Their edition introduced **Oriax** into the major printed chain and popularized the circular sigil.

### Arthur Edward Waite

Waite’s *Book of Black Magic and of Pacts* and later *Book of Ceremonial Magic* summarized the *Lemegeton* and reproduced seals. His account is important to reception history but is less reliable than direct manuscript collation.

Peterson suggests Waite may have helped popularize or coin “Lesser Key” as the conventional English title; the manuscripts ordinarily say “Little Key.”

### Golden Dawn and Crowley’s *777*

Golden Dawn materials created the tarot, decan and quinance environment in which Orias is now frequently interpreted. Crowley’s *777* made the nocturnal Scorpio attribution readily portable.

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding:** no publication or documented working by Frater Achad specifically concerning Orias/Oriax was found. His general Qabalistic and Thelemic work does not by itself establish an Orias tradition.

### Poke Runyon

Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* integrates Goetic evocation, dark-mirror work and angelic balancing. His table gives Oriax/Orias, Harachel, Capricorn dates and speculative Mithraic/Phoenician remarks.

Those historical-origin remarks are Runyon’s conjectures. His ritual system is an influential modern reconstruction, not a transcript of a single early manuscript. See Runyon’s [official book page](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

### Lon Milo DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt

*Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*, associated with Christopher Hyatt, Lon Milo DuQuette and David P. Wilson, helped popularize the astrological tables, modern illustrations and practical-commentary approach.

It does not supply evidence that DuQuette personally performed a historically documentable Orias operation. General authorship on Goetia should not be inflated into an individual spirit-working claim.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 critical edition remains essential because it:

- collates the manuscript witnesses;
- restores **Orias**;
- distinguishes manuscript readings from Mathers’s changes;
- prints Weyer and Scot in parallel;
- documents the additions, omissions and reordered catalogue.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* prints Harley 6483 and related material. Its chief contribution to Orias scholarship is the demonstration that the Harahel pairing is built into Rudd’s physical double-seal method, rather than being merely a modern web-table juxtaposition.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium* and related writings compare spirit catalogues across the grimoire tradition and emphasize continuities between early spirit offices and later Goetia. This is valuable comparative work, though no unique pre-Weyer Orias witness emerges from it.

### Owen Davies

Owen Davies’s *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* supplies the social history of printed and manuscript grimoires. It contextualizes Weyer, Scot, Solomonic books and later occult revivals but does not propose a demonstrable ancient origin for Orias.

### Jan Veenstra

Veenstra’s work on learned magic and spirit traditions is valuable for medieval intellectual context. No Orias-specific etymology or pre-Weyer attestation was located in the surveyed material.

### *Welcome to Demon School! Iruma-kun*

The series includes **Orias Oswell**, a teacher whose surname belongs to the series’ broader use of Goetic demon names.

**Modern fiction:** the character is not a historical Orias tradition and does not preserve all the grimoire’s attributes.

### Warhammer 40,000

C. L. Werner’s *The Siege of Castellax* includes **Oriax**, a high-ranking Iron Warriors Warpsmith and fabricator. The spelling matches Mathers–Crowley’s form.

The character’s technological, martial biography is an original fictional construction. Direct authorial confirmation that the name was chosen from the *Goetia* was not found. See the source-referenced [Lexicanum entry](https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Oriax).

### *Helluva Boss*

**Negative finding:** no canonical Orias was located in released *Helluva Boss* episodes or official character material. Fan-created Ars Goetia characters named Orias circulate online; they are fandom, not canon.

### Other modern occult usage

Modern demonolatry sites add:

- an “enn” or mantra;
- colours such as violet, silver or white;
- incense and offerings;
- Monday;
- directions;
- psychological “shadow” interpretations;
- claims about career promotion, bodily transformation or reconciliation.

These may be meaningful inside living practitioner traditions, but none is part of the Weyer–Scot–Goetia entry unless independently traced.

Practitioner reports of dreams, visions, altered appearance or professional advancement are **modern experiential testimony**. They are evidence that people currently work with the name, not historical proof of the spirit’s antiquity or objective powers.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. “Weyer 1563”

**Incorrect for the catalogue.** *De praestigiis* began in 1563; Orias first appears securely in the fifth edition’s *Pseudomonarchia* in 1577.

### 2. Orias or Oriax?

**Orias** is the unanimous principal early reading. **Oriax** appears in Mathers–Crowley and *777*. Its origin is unexplained.

### 3. Orias and Orobas

They are distinct throughout the primary chain.

| Feature | Orias | Orobas |
|---|---|---|
| Weyer number | 49 | 58 |
| Goetia number | 59 | 55 |
| Rank | Marquis | Prince |
| First form | Lion riding horse | Horse |
| Second form | None individually specified | Human image |
| Special office | Stars, planets, transformations | Past/future, divinity, creation, truthfulness |
| Shared office | Dignities, prelacies, favour | Dignities, prelacies, favour |
| Legions | 30 | 20 |
| Seal | Distinct | Distinct |

The horse imagery and shared social offices explain why modern summaries may confuse them. No principal manuscript conflates the entries.

De Plancy engraved **Orobas**, not Orias, which supplies another likely source of visual confusion.

### 4. Orias and Ornias

A possible distant relationship, proposed partly because both are Solomonic, leonine and transformative. No intermediate textual chain proves it.

### 5. Orias and Osiris

A modern speculative equation without early textual or linguistic support.

### 6. Orias and Oriens

No early identification. Oriens is a directional king whose name transparently means East; Orias is a marquis with no stated direction.

### 7. “Oray” as an Orias spelling

Incorrect in the early source chain. **Oray** is an alias of Loray/Leraje in Scot, not Orias.

### 8. Who has the serpent’s tail?

Weyer’s Latin and the English descendants are grammatically ambiguous. De Plancy assigns it to the horse; many modern artists assign it to Orias. Mathers explicitly noticed the uncertainty. Neither solution can be made definitive from the earliest sentence alone.

### 9. What does “transformeth men” mean?

The text asserts transformation but does not specify:

- physical versus apparent;
- temporary versus permanent;
- a particular shape;
- whether the subject’s belief is altered.

Those detailed mechanisms occur in entries for other spirits, especially Ose. Modern versions often import Ose’s wording into Orias.

### 10. Thirty legions

Thirty is stable across the entire chain. No major early variant was located.

### 11. Harahel’s meaning

“God who knows all things” and “Permeating all Things” are occult attributes. Because the underlying letters form a constructed Exodus triad rather than an ordinary lexical name, neither should be presented as an uncontested Hebrew translation.

### 12. Capricorn or Taurus?

Both are historically attested occult systems:

- Lenain: 20°–25° Capricorn;
- Golden Dawn: 20°–25° Taurus.

The discrepancy arises from the starting point of the 72-quinance sequence.

### 13. Harahel’s quinance or Orias’s quinance?

The five degrees belong to Harahel’s angelic table. Crowley gives Orias a ten-degree decan. Calling 20°–25° Taurus or Capricorn “Orias’s quinance in *777*” collapses two tables.

### 14. Six of Cups or Four of Pentacles?

The documented Golden Dawn/*777* Goetic decan produces the **Six of Cups**. Four of Pentacles corresponds to Sun in Capricorn and appears when later authors force the demon into Harahel’s Lenain Capricorn sector.

Thus Four of Pentacles is a later harmonization, not Crowley’s original Oriax pip.

### 15. Is the rank planet the decan planet?

No. Moon belongs to the rank Marquis; Sun belongs to the Six-of-Cups decan. Mixing them produces contradictory modern tables.

### 16. Did Rudd invent the Shem angels?

No. The seventy-twofold Name and suffixed angel-names predate Rudd. His distinctive contribution was to pair the series ordinally with Goetic demons and incorporate the pair into a practical double-seal system.

### 17. Is Harley 6483 Rudd’s own manuscript?

Not in its surviving physical form. It postdates his death. It is a later copy or recension carrying material attributed to him.

### 18. Is Harahel’s modern guardian-angel biography ancient Jewish Kabbalah?

No. Its ancestry is layered: Exodus triad, medieval Jewish name traditions, Christian-kabbalistic angel formation, early-modern seals, Lenain’s 1823 offices and later guardian-angel popularization.

### 19. Did de Plancy publish an Orias portrait?

The text entry is confirmed; a Breton Orias engraving was not found. The adjacent engraved demon is Orobas.

### 20. Is there an ancient god behind Orias?

No demonstrated one. Unlike Baal, Astarte/Astaroth, Amun/Amon, Asmodeus/Ashmedai, Belial or Phoenix, Orias lacks a secure ancient theonymic or mythological derivation.

## Open questions

1. **What manuscript stood behind Weyer?**  
   If Weyer’s lost *Officium spirituum* exemplar were identified, it might push the name earlier than 1577.

2. **Why “Orias”?**  
   Ornias, Oreias, Oriens and Osiris offer possible comparison points, but none has a demonstrable transmission chain.

3. **Where did “Oriax” originate?**  
   Examination of Mathers’s surviving notes or exact working transcript might show whether it was a misreading, deliberate emendation or compositor’s error.

4. **Who bears the tail?**  
   Earlier witnesses do not resolve the grammar. De Plancy’s horse-tail interpretation is later.

5. **What did “planetary mansions” mean to Weyer’s source?**  
   It could refer broadly to houses, domiciles or celestial stations. Skinner and Rankine’s twenty-eight lunar-mansion gloss is possible but not compelled by the Latin.

6. **What kind of transformation was intended?**  
   The entry gives no phenomenology. Modern accounts may be literal, visionary, social or psychological interpretations.

7. **Can the seal variants be stemmatically ordered?**  
   A direct high-resolution comparison of every stroke in Sloane 3825, Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648 and Harley 6483 remains desirable.

8. **How early is the exact Harahel spelling?**  
   The underlying triad is stable, but transliterations—Harahel, Harachel, Herachael—reflect differing systems for Hebrew **ח** and vowel insertion.

9. **Which zodiacal system did Rudd himself intend?**  
   The ordinal pairing is clear. Importing either Lenain’s Capricorn or the later Golden Dawn Taurus quinance into Rudd’s seventeenth-century practice is anachronistic unless independent manuscript evidence is supplied.

10. **Was Warhammer’s Oriax consciously named from the Goetia?**  
    The spelling is suggestive, but authorial confirmation has not been located.

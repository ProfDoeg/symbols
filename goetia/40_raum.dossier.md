# Räum: Research Dossier

*Research completed 14 September 2026. This dossier separates the historical text from later reconstruction and practice.*

**Evidence labels**

- **[Documented text]** — directly stated or depicted in a cited primary source.
- **[Textual development]** — a demonstrable change between witnesses.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** — a conclusion argued from source comparison, palaeography, bibliography, or historical context.
- **[Tradition]** — a claim transmitted within an occult or religious lineage, without implying historical or empirical verification.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — a symbolic attribution created within a particular magical system.
- **[Disputed]** — competing readings exist or the evidence is inadequate.
- **[Legend]** — supernatural narrative reported as tradition.
- **[Modern invention/UPG]** — an attribution first found in recent practitioner literature, popular reference works, or fiction.
- **[Negative finding]** — a specifically investigated claim for which no attestation was found.

The three astrological lines at the top are deliberately not harmonized. Ieiazel’s 15°–20° Libra position comes from the straight zodiacal sequence used by Lenain and much modern angelology. The Golden Dawn placed the same angel on the Six of Swords and 15°–20° Aquarius. Crowley placed the demon Räum—not the angel—over the whole first decan of Taurus as its night spirit. Modern charts frequently splice pieces of these systems together.

## The name and its origins

### 1. The source-chain spellings

Joseph Peterson’s manuscript comparison provides the clearest compact census of the names:

| Source | Date | Position | Spelling |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Johann Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* | 1577 | 42 | **Raum vel Raym** |
| Reginald Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft* | 1584 | 42 | **Raum, or Raim** |
| Sloane MS 3825 | later 17th century | 40 | **Raum** |
| Sloane MS 2731 | 1686/87 | 40 | **Raum** |
| Sloane MS 3648 | 17th century | 40 | **RAVM** |
| Harley MS 6483 | internally dated 1712/13 | 40 | **Raum** |
| Northwestern University MS 65 | early modern | 40 | **Raum** |
| Wellcome MS 3203 | later witness | 21 in its local sequence | **Raum** |
| Mathers–Crowley | 1904 | 40 | **Räum** |
| A. E. Waite | later print tradition | 40 | **Raum or Raym** |
| Idries Shah | modern summary | 40 | **Raum, sometimes called Raym** |

See Peterson’s [comparative name table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

**[Textual development]** The umlaut is late. Neither Weyer’s *Raum/​Raym*, Scot’s *Raum/​Raim*, nor the principal English manuscripts employ **Räum**. That form is securely visible in the 1904 Mathers–Crowley edition.

**[Textual note]** *RAVM* in Sloane 3648 is not necessarily another vocalization. In Latin and early modern display capitals, **V** routinely serves for **U**.

### 2. Is the name German?

Modern encyclopedias regularly observe that German **Raum** means “space,” “room,” or “chamber,” while **räumen** means “to clear, empty, vacate, or evacuate.” The lexical facts are correct.

The proposed demonological derivation is not established:

- **[Documented linguistic fact]** Modern German *Raum* is a normal Germanic noun.
- **[Negative finding]** Weyer supplies no German etymology.
- **[Negative finding]** Scot, the *Goetia* manuscripts, de Plancy, Mathers, Crowley, and Peterson offer no such derivation.
- **[Disputed]** No documented chain connects the German word to a pre-Weyer spirit-name.
- **[Important caution]** The superficially suggestive **Räum** is the late 1904 spelling. It cannot explain Weyer’s 1577 **Raum vel Raym** unless one independently proves that Weyer’s form was German.

The resemblance may have encouraged interpretations of the spirit as one who “clears out” kings’ treasuries or removes civic power, but that is retrospective wordplay, not historical etymology.

Claims that *Raum* derives from Old Norse *rúm*, Proto-Germanic forms meaning spaciousness, or an imperative “empty!” are linguistically conceivable but lack a transmission route into Weyer's catalogue.

### 3. Egyptian and other proposed identities

**[Modern invention]** Some twenty-first-century demonolatry literature identifies Raum with the Egyptian ram-headed creator god **Khnum**, sometimes through the syllable *Ra*, the word “ram,” or the destructive/creative symbolism of a potter. One representative modern ritual page explicitly declares “Raum” to be Khnum and constructs new Egyptian, runic, and *Ra-Um* interpretations around the Goetic seal.

There is no supporting historical evidence:

- Khnum’s Egyptian name does not yield *Raum* by an accepted phonological route.
- Khnum is ram-headed, while the historical Raum appears as a corvid.
- Weyer never mentions Egypt or Khnum.
- No intermediary Greek, Coptic, Arabic, Hebrew, or Latin form has been identified.
- Raum’s theft of treasure, destruction of cities, and reconciliation of enemies are not ancient cult titles of Khnum.

The Khnum identification should therefore be catalogued as **modern esoteric invention**, not reconstruction.

**[Negative finding]** No persuasive identification was found with Baal, Astarte, Amun, Asmodeus/Ashmedai, Belial, Phoenix, a classical hero, or an ancient place-name. Unlike Astaroth, Asmoday, Belial, or Phenex, Raum presently has no recoverable ancient deity, biblical figure, Greek noun, or Latin noun behind his name.

### 4. Earliest attestation

**[Documented text]** The earliest secure, datable public attestation is Johann Weyer’s fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577, whose appendix contains the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*.

Weyer says his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also called an *Empto Salomonis*, and refers to a manuscript associated with the initials T. R. and the date 1570. That makes a lost or unidentified 1570 exemplar possible, but:

- the alleged source is not presently available as an independently verifiable witness to the Raum entry;
- 1577 remains the first secure surviving publication;
- no exact *Raum, Raym,* or *Raim* has been located in an earlier grimoire.

**Conclusion:** “Raum is medieval” goes beyond the evidence. “Raum belongs to a catalogue descended partly from medieval spirit-books” is supportable; “the individual name Raum is attested before Weyer” is not currently supportable.

## The description across the sources

## 1. Johann Weyer, 1577

**[Documented text]** Weyer places Raum at no. 42. The original pagination is pp. 911–912 of the 1577 fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum*; Peterson numbers the entry §42.

> **Raum vel Raym Comes est magnus: ut corvus visitur: Sed quum assumit humanam faciem, si ab exorcista iussus fuerit, mirè ex regis domo vel alia suffuratur, & ad locum sibi designatum transfert. Civitates destruit: dignitatum despectum ingerit. Novit præsentia, præterita & futura. Favorem tam hostium quàm amicorum conciliat. Fuit ex ordine Thronorum. Præest legionibus triginta.**

A close translation is:

> Raum or Raym is a great Count. He is seen as a raven/crow; but when he assumes a human face, if ordered by the exorcist, he steals marvellously from the king’s house or another house and transports it to the place designated for him. He destroys cities; he instills contempt for dignities. He knows present, past, and future things. He conciliates the favor of enemies as well as friends. He was of the order of Thrones. He presides over thirty legions.

Source: [Peterson’s Latin transcription of Weyer](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Philological points

- *Comes* is “count,” rendered “earl” by Scot and the English *Goetia*.
- *Corvus* can denote a raven or crow. Scot chose **crowe**; de Plancy chose French **corbeau**.
- *Mirè … suffuratur* means “steals marvellously/wonderfully,” not necessarily “steals wonders.”
- *Ex regis domo vel alia* means from a king’s house “or another [house].” The later *Goetia* narrows it to kings’ houses.
- *Dignitatum despectum ingerit* is more literally “introduces/instills contempt for dignities.” It is not exactly “destroys men’s dignities.”
- *Favorem … conciliat* means he wins or reconciles the favor of both enemies and friends. It does not literally say “causes love.”

These phrases are the nuclei from which later, stronger English offices developed.

## 2. Reginald Scot, 1584

**[Documented text]** Scot’s catalogue appears in *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XVI, chapter II. Raum falls on p. 223 in the 1584 pagination—the next page marker, p. 224, occurs later in the sequence.

> **Raum, or Raim is a great earle, he is seene as a crowe, but when he putteth on humane shape, at the commandement of the exorcist, he stealeth woonderfullie out of the kings house, and carrieth it whether he is assigned, he destroieth cities, and hath great despite unto dignities, he knoweth things present, past, and to come, and reconcileth freends and foes, he was of the order of thrones, and governeth thirtie legions.**

Source: [Peterson’s transcription of Scot, Book XVI](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

This is a close but not mechanically literal rendering of Weyer:

| Weyer | Scot | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| *Comes* | “earle” | English noble title substituted for count |
| *corvus* | “crowe” | Establishes “crow” in the English tradition |
| *regis domo vel alia* | “the kings house” | Omits “or another house” |
| *dignitatum despectum ingerit* | “hath great despite unto dignities” | Treats contempt as Raum’s disposition |
| *favorem … conciliat* | “reconcileth freends and foes” | Changes winning favor into reconciliation |

Peterson notes that Scot’s translation was probably made or assisted by Abraham Fleming. The *Goetia* is often linguistically closer to Scot than to Weyer’s Latin.

## 3. Sloane MS 3825 and Peterson’s 2001 edition

**[Documented text]** In British Library Sloane MS 3825, fol. 106r, the entry is no. 40. Peterson prints it on p. 25 of *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (Weiser, 2001):

> **The 40th spirit is called Raum, he is an Earle, & appeareth at first in ye forme of a Crow but afterwards, at ye command of ye Exorcist he putteth on humane shape his office is to steale Treasures out of kings houses, and to carry it where he is commanded, & to destroy Cities, and ye dignities of men; & to tell all Things past, & wt is, & wt will be; & to cause Love between friends & foes; he was of ye order of Thrones, and governeth over 30 Legions of spirits his seal is Thus, which make and weare as a Lamin before you.**

Peterson’s notes report Weyer’s **Raum vel Raym**, Scot’s **Raum, or Raim**, and support the omitted adjective “great” from Weyer, Scot, and Sloane 3648. See the [online transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm) and the [2001 edition scan](https://archive.org/details/joseph-h.-peterson-the-lesser-key-of-solomon-2001).

### What changed?

**[Textual development]**

1. “Steals from the king’s house or another” becomes “steal Treasures out of kings houses.”
2. “Instills contempt for dignities” becomes “destroy … the dignities of men.”
3. “Knows” past, present, and future becomes “tell[s]” them to the conjurer.
4. “Conciliates the favor” of enemies and friends becomes “cause Love between friends & foes.”
5. A **seal and lamen instruction**, absent from Weyer and Scot, is added.

The familiar modern list of Raum’s powers therefore represents an English amplification, not an exact repetition of the Latin.

## 4. Sloane MS 2731 and related manuscripts

**[Documented text]** Sloane MS 2731 calls the spirit **Raum**, no. 40. Its substance agrees with Sloane 3825: Earl, crow, human form, theft and transportation of treasure, civic and social destruction, temporal knowledge, reconciliation/love, Thrones, thirty legions, and a seal.

**[Textual finding]** No major independent office unique to Sloane 2731 was found. Its importance is primarily as another witness to the forty-number catalogue and the attached character.

Sloane MS 3648 gives **RAVM** and explicitly supports “a great Earl.” Harley MS 6483 also retains **Raum**.

## 5. Mathers–Crowley, 1904

**[Documented text]** The entry occupies printed p. 26 of *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* (the digitized PDF image is p. 38):

> **(40.) RÄUM.—The Fortieth Spirit is Räum. He is a Great Earl; and appeareth at first in the form of a Crow, but after the Command of the Exorcist he putteth on Human Shape. His office is to steal Treasures out King’s Houses, and to carry it whither he is commanded, and to destroy Cities and Dignities of Men, and to tell all things, Past, and what Is, and what Will Be; and to cause Love between Friends and Foes. He was of the Order of Thrones. He governeth 30 Legions of Spirits; and his Seal is this, which wear thou as aforesaid.**

Facsimile: [Mathers–Crowley *Goetia* PDF](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/The-Goetia-of-the-Lemegeton-of-Solomon-the-King-ed.-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf). The same wording is reproduced at [Sacred Texts](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm) and [Hermetic Library](https://hermetic.com/norton/classics/goetia/raum).

The dropped preposition in “out King’s Houses” is a printing peculiarity commonly normalized to “out **of** King’s Houses.”

**[Textual development]** The umlaut **Räum** first becomes prominent here. The edition otherwise modernizes spelling and punctuation without adding a new office.

### Mathers versus Crowley

Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers prepared the English manuscript-based translation. Aleister Crowley edited and published it, added essays, the “Preliminary Invocation” derived from the Graeco-Egyptian “Bornless” ritual, and psychological/Thelemic framing. Those additions are not part of the seventeenth-century Raum entry.

## 6. What Raum does—and does not do

The following are securely in the textual chain:

- appears as a crow/raven;
- may be ordered to take human form;
- steals and transports property or treasure;
- destroys cities;
- attacks, despises, or destroys dignities;
- knows or reports past, present, and future;
- reconciles friends and enemies or produces their favor/love;
- was allegedly of the Thrones;
- commands thirty legions.

The following common Goetic offices are **not** assigned to Raum in Weyer, Scot, or the *Goetia*:

- teaching languages;
- teaching the liberal sciences;
- supplying familiars;
- invisibility;
- transmutation;
- locating buried treasure rather than stealing it;
- healing;
- necromancy;
- controlling weather or the sea.

### The legions

**[Documented text]** All principal witnesses give **thirty legions**.

The *Pseudomonarchia* later remarks that a legion contains 6,666 spirits. That would produce 199,980 subordinate spirits if treated arithmetically. The catalogues themselves do not perform that calculation, and “legion” is conventional demonological rhetoric rather than a verifiable census.

## Rank, metal, planet, and time of operation

### 1. The manuscript *Goetia*

The “Observations” attached to the *Goetia* specify:

| Rank | Metal | Planet |
|---|---|---|
| King | Gold | Sun |
| Marquis | Silver | Moon |
| Duke | Copper | Venus |
| Prince or Prelate | Tin | Jupiter |
| Knight | Lead | Saturn |
| President | Quicksilver | Mercury |
| Earl/Count | **Copper and silver equally** | **Venus and Moon** |

**[Documented text]** On the manuscript rule, Raum’s Earl seal may therefore be made in either copper or silver, joining him symbolically to Venus and the Moon. The source does not choose one of the two.

**[Documented omission]** Iron/Mars is conspicuously absent from this manuscript table.

### 2. Crowley’s *777*

Crowley’s *777* creates a regular seven-planet scheme and assigns:

- Prince — Jupiter/tin
- Earl — **Mars/iron**
- King — Sun/gold
- Duke — Venus/copper
- President — Mercury/quicksilver
- Marquis — Moon/silver

This makes Raum martial and iron-bearing. It is a later **esoteric correspondence**, not the metal instruction in the older *Goetia*. Modern websites that simply write “Earl = Mars = iron” are following the *777* regularization.

### 3. Hours

**[Documented text]** Weyer gives calling-times for several ranks but supplies no rule for Counts/Earls. The Earl rule is an addition in the English *Goetia*:

> Earls, or Counts, may be bound at any hour of the day, provided the operation takes place in woods or another isolated place free from noise.

The same observations recommend operations only when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old.

Therefore:

- “Räum must be summoned at night” is **not** a rule in his catalogue entry.
- “Räum is a night demon” belongs to Crowley’s later decan table.
- The older Earl timing is any hour of the day in a secluded location.
- Using Taurus dates or a planetary hour of Mars is a nineteenth-/twentieth-century astrological overlay, not an original requirement.

## The seal

### 1. What the texts say

Weyer and Scot provide **no seal**.

The manuscript *Goetia* says only:

> “his seal is Thus, which make and weare as a Lamin before you.”

There is no textual recipe explaining how it was generated, which strokes are essential, or what individual components mean.

### 2. Visible form

**[Documented image]** In the manuscript-derived form, Raum’s character is an angular, primarily horizontal construction. Its salient features are:

- a long horizontal axis;
- several short vertical or crosswise strokes;
- small circular or node-like terminations toward the left and center;
- an angular closed or diamond-like terminal on the right;
- an uneven lower projection in some copies.

Because this is a drawn character, a verbal description cannot identify its exact geometry without ambiguity. Peterson’s normalized image may be consulted directly: [Raum seal](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g40.jpg).

### 3. Manuscript and printed variants

- **Sloane 3825:** the source of Peterson’s principal transcription; the seal accompanies the entry on fol. 106r.
- **Sloane 2731:** retains the same recognizable character, with the normal small differences expected from hand-copying.
- **Sloane 3648:** a rougher witness with **RAVM** and small stroke-level differences.
- **Harley 6483:** the demon character is carefully redrawn as part of a more elaborate, paired angel–demon lamen system.
- **Mathers–Crowley 1904:** standardizes the seal inside a double circle, with **R** at the top, **A** at the right, **U** at the bottom, and **M** at the left. This circular named presentation is the source of most modern tattoos, pendants, posters, and online sigil graphics. A clean reproduction is available at [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:40-Raum_seal.png).

**[Scholarly caution]** The manuscripts contain graphic variants, not explanatory variants. Statements that a particular line means “the fall of kings,” that the diamond is a treasure-vault, or that the circles are cities are modern symbolic interpretations. No early witness supplies those meanings.

### 4. Origin of the seals

**[Open/disputed]** The spirit descriptions descend largely from Weyer/Scot, but the Goetic seals do not appear there. Proposed origins include:

- construction on planetary magic squares;
- derivation from an unknown *Offices of Spirits* witness;
- invention or adaptation by the seventeenth-century compiler;
- gradual copying from a no-longer-surviving visual source.

No derivation of Raum’s character has been demonstrated stroke by stroke.

## The angel: Ieiazel

## 1. Identification and spelling

| Field | Ieiazel |
|---|---|
| Number | 40 of 72 |
| Hebrew | **ייזאל** |
| Root triplet | **ייז** — Yod, Yod, Zayin |
| Expanded ending | **אל**, *El*, “God” |
| Forms | Ieiazel, Ieïazel, Iéïazel, Yeiazel, Yeyazel, Iyazel; Golden Dawn transliteration **IIZAL** |
| Traditional attribute | “God who rejoices,” “God who gives joy” |
| Choir | Powers/Potestates |
| Governing archangel in later tables | Camael/Kamael |
| Rudd opponent | Raum |
| Psalm | Psalm 88:14 in many English occult tables; Hebrew Psalm 88:15; Vulgate Psalm 87:15 |
| Lenain zodiac sector | 15°–20° Libra |
| Lenain offices | Liberation of prisoners, consolation, deliverance from enemies, letters, books, printing, artists and scholars |

### Is “God who rejoices” a literal translation?

**[Tradition]** Lenain gives *Dieu qui réjouit*.

**[Philological caution]** The three-letter unit ייז is generated mechanically from Exodus rather than composed as an ordinary Hebrew personal name. Adding *-El* makes an angelic form, but it does not automatically give a transparent Hebrew sentence. “God who rejoices” is best described as the inherited occult **attribute** or gloss, not an uncontested dictionary translation.

Modern suggestions such as “God will sprinkle,” based on comparison with Hebrew roots containing yod/zayin, are possible speculative vocalizations but have no clear priority over the Lenain tradition.

## 2. How the seventy-two names are made

**[Documented text]** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three consecutive Hebrew verses traditionally counted as seventy-two letters each. Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia* III.25, describes the procedure:

1. write the first verse in its ordinary direction;
2. write the second underneath in reverse;
3. write the third in the original direction;
4. read the resulting columns to obtain seventy-two three-letter units;
5. append divine endings such as **-el** or **-yah** to obtain angelic names.

The second line’s reversal creates the famous boustrophedon arrangement. The fortieth triplet is י־י־ז. With *El*, it becomes ייזאל.

Agrippa also says the seventy-two angels govern seventy-two celestial **quinaries**, that is, sectors of five degrees each. See [Agrippa III.25](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp3b.htm).

### Scriptural verse assigned to Ieiazel

The traditional verse is:

> לָמָה יְהוָה תִּזְנַח נַפְשִׁי תַּסְתִּיר פָּנֶיךָ מִמֶּנִּי

A close translation:

> “Why, YHWH, do you reject my soul? Why do you hide your face from me?”

Numbering varies:

- Masoretic/Hebrew numbering: **Psalm 88:15**
- King James and many English Bibles: **Psalm 88:14**
- Vulgate numbering: **Psalm 87:15**
- French occult sources often print **Psaume 88, verset 14 or 15**, depending on the Bible followed.

A Vulgate form used in the occult tables is:

> *Ut quid, Domine, repellis orationem meam: avertis faciem tuam a me?*

Some later versions replace *orationem meam* with *animam meam*, following the Hebrew sense “my soul.” The numbering and wording differences are biblical textual conventions, not evidence for multiple angels.

## 3. Historical development of the angelic tradition

### Medieval Jewish background

**[Documented tradition]** The “explicit” or “divided” divine Name has several forms in Jewish literature: four-, twelve-, forty-two-, and seventy-two-letter traditions should not be treated as one undifferentiated object.

The seventy-twofold arrangement based on Exodus developed in medieval Jewish mysticism and appears in the textual world represented by *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*. The surviving *Sefer Raziel* is a composite compilation rather than a book literally delivered to Adam by an angel.

**[Legend]** In the book’s own legendary frame, the angel Raziel reveals celestial secrets to Adam.

**[Negative finding]** The complete modern package—Ieiazel’s French meaning, October dates, twenty-minute period, psalm, books and printing, birth guardianship, and opposition to Raum—is not found as such in the medieval Jewish source.

### Reuchlin and Agrippa

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped transform the triplets into a Christian-Kabbalistic angelic catalogue by adding pronounceable divine endings.

Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia* III.25, printed in its complete form in 1533, clearly describes the Exodus construction and the seventy-two five-degree celestial sectors.

### Blaise de Vigenère

Blaise de Vigenère’s sixteenth-century treatment transmitted tables and seals of the seventy-two angelic names in a Christian-Kabbalistic setting. Skinner and Rankine regard this material as an important antecedent for the Rudd system.

### Athanasius Kircher

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54) and its *Cabala Hebraeorum* placed the seventy-two names within an enormous Christian synthesis of Hebrew, Egyptian, astrological, and universal-symbolic material. Kircher is important to the later visual and planetary tradition, but he did not originate the Exodus technique.

See [Kircher’s Kabbalistic tables](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm).

### Rudd/Harley 6483

**[Documented manuscript system]** Harley MS 6483 pairs each Goetic spirit with the Shem angel occupying the same numerical position. Consequently:

> **40 Raum ↔ 40 Ieiazel**

The practical device is a two-sided lamen:

- the demon’s seal faces outward;
- the corresponding angelic seal/name is placed on the reverse, toward the magician’s heart;
- the angel’s name and assigned psalm verse are invoked as controlling authority;
- angelic names also appear in protective ritual apparatus such as the breastplate and brazen-vessel scheme.

Skinner and Rankine interpret this as a deliberate method for binding or constraining the Goetic spirit through its celestial counterpart.

**[Disputed attribution]** Calling it simply “Thomas Rudd’s seventeenth-century autograph” is unsafe. Harley 6483 is internally dated **1712/13** and belongs to a series copied by Peter Smart. Skinner and Rankine argue that it preserves earlier material attributable to Thomas Rudd, possibly from around 1655–56. Critics note that the surviving codex is later, composite, and not proved to be Rudd’s autograph. The pairing is unquestionably in the Harley manuscript tradition; its precise author and first date remain disputed.

### Lazare Lenain, 1823

Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives the form that dominates modern French angelology:

> **40e Ieiazel. Son attribut: Dieu qui réjouit.**

**[Documented esoteric tradition]** Lenain assigns Ieiazel:

- the arc **15°–20° Libra**;
- approximately **9–13 October** in the annual five-day scheme;
- a twenty-minute daily period around **13:00–13:20**;
- liberation of prisoners;
- consolation and deliverance from enemies;
- influence over books, printing, learned people, and artists;
- Psalm 88/87:14–15;
- the divine name **Goed**, associated with Belgium.

Lenain’s dating scheme is more complicated than “one angel equals one birthday range.” He describes several cycles: a five-day physical or solar rulership, separated “moral” days generated by repeated seventy-two-day cycles, and a daily twenty-minute intellectual rulership. Modern tables disagree because they select different portions of his system or recalculate them.

Primary scan: [Internet Archive, *La science cabalistique*](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365).

### Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced and promoted Lenain’s seventy-two-genius material in editions of *La Cabbale*, especially the expanded 1903 edition. Papus treated Lenain’s work as operative Judeo-Christian Kabbalah. This was dissemination and reorganization, not independent medieval evidence.

### Golden Dawn and Crowley

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn incorporated the Shem angels into tarot and decan tables. Crowley reproduced those correspondences in *777*.

The result differs from Lenain’s straight Aries-to-Pisces sequence:

- Golden Dawn/*777*: Ieiazel is the second/night angel of the **Six of Swords**, therefore **15°–20° Aquarius**, Mercury in Aquarius.
- Lenain-derived modern angelology: Ieiazel is **15°–20° Libra**, which, when superimposed on Golden Dawn tarot decans, yields the **Three of Swords**, Saturn in Libra.

Crowley’s *Sepher Sephiroth* also identifies **IIZAL** as an “Angel of 6 S.” Frater Achad repeats this in *Q.B.L.; or, The Bride’s Reception*. Neither source thereby gives a new Raum working.

### Robert Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reorganized the French angelic current, added talismanic and ritual details, and circulated angel seals attributed to an eighteenth-century Arsenal manuscript. These seals should not be retrojected into Sefer Raziel or the Bible.

### Haziel

“Haziel” (the pen name associated with a large French angelology publishing program) popularized the seventy-two birth angels in works including *Le grand livre des invocations et des exhortations* (first editions in the late 1980s; Paris: Bussière) and *Notre ange gardien existe* (1994).

Haziel retains Ieiazel’s:

- October 9–13 period;
- consolation and joy;
- Powers/Kamael framework;
- literary and artistic gifts;
- prayer and guardian-angel language.

His psychological biographies, personal exhortations, karmic narratives, and promises of concrete assistance are twentieth-century elaborations of Lenain’s table.

## 2. Crowley’s demon-decan scheme

In *777*, columns CLV–CLXVI distribute the seventy-two Goetic spirits among the thirty-six zodiacal decans:

- spirits 1–36 are day rulers;
- spirits 37–72 repeat the thirty-six decans as night rulers;
- each ten-degree decan therefore carries two Goetic spirits.

For Taurus’s first decan:

- day spirit: **Gamigin**, Goetia no. 4;
- night spirit: **Räum**, Goetia no. 40;
- zodiac: **0°–10° Taurus**;
- decan ruler: **Mercury**;
- pip card: **Five of Disks/Pentacles**;
- Golden Dawn title: “Lord of Material Trouble”; Crowley’s later keyword: “Worry.”

The primary *777* row explicitly reads, in effect, “40 Raum — Crow,” under the night/ascendant decan column. See [*777*, pp. 28–29 of the original table sequence](https://www.astrumargenteum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/0777-by-Aleister-Crowley.pdf).

This is what “each pip carries two spirits” means for the Goetic demons.

## 3. Ieiazel and the tarot problem

Two answers are historically defensible, but they answer different questions:

1. **Where does Lenain put angel no. 40?**  
   15°–20° Libra. Combining this with the Golden Dawn’s zodiacal cards places Ieiazel in the second half of the Three of Swords, Saturn in Libra.

2. **Which pip does the Golden Dawn/*777* angel table explicitly give Ieiazel?**  
   The **Six of Swords**, Mercury in Aquarius, with Rehael and Ieiazel as its day/night angel pair. Ieiazel is the second or night half, 15°–20° Aquarius.

Thus a chart that labels “Crowley’s Ieiazel = Three of Swords” is conflating Lenain’s zodiac order with Crowley’s card structure.

## 4. Direction and ruler

Peterson’s comparative notes label Raum an Earl associated with the West in one organizational table. This is not stated in Raum’s individual entry, and the four infernal kings/directions vary among Agrippa, Weyer, the *Goetia*, and later editions.

Modern claims that Raum is unambiguously under Amaymon, Goap, or a southern/western king must therefore name the table being used. There is no single stable early assignment.

## 5. Enochian overlays

**[Documented context]** The Rudd/Smart Harleian manuscript collection contains both Goetic and material derived from John Dee’s angelic or “Enochian” records. Rudd’s *Tabula Sancta cum Tabulis Enochi* is part of Skinner and Rankine’s reconstruction of a broader angel-magic synthesis.

**[Negative finding]** No early source provides a specific Enochian word, aethyr, governor, tablet square, or call unique to Raum.

Crowley and later editors sometimes append Enochian invocations or translate Goetic conjurations into Enochian. Those are later ritual overlays, not part of Weyer’s Raum, Scot’s Raum, or Sloane 3825’s entry.

## Iconography

## 1. Before de Plancy

Weyer and Scot provide only the verbal image: a crow/raven, then a human form. No physical details of the human manifestation are supplied—no age, clothing, weapons, complexion, wings, crown, or sex.

The *Goetia* repeats the same two-stage form but adds the seal. Consequently, any detailed “traditional portrait” of a crowned crow-man, plague doctor, armored count, skeletal raven knight, or treasure-bearing noble is artistic invention.

## 2. Collin de Plancy

The *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818 and was repeatedly revised. The verified sixth-edition entry is on p. 571:

> **Raum, grand comte du sombre empire ; il se présente sous la forme d’un corbeau lorsqu’il est conjuré. Il détruit des villes, donne des dignités. Il est de l’ordre des Trônes et commande trente légions.**

Translation:

> Raum, great count of the dark empire; he presents himself in the form of a raven when conjured. He destroys cities and gives dignities. He is of the order of Thrones and commands thirty legions.

Source: [1863 Raum entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Raum).

### The “gives dignities” reversal

**[Textual development/disputed corruption]** De Plancy says **donne des dignités**, “gives dignities.” This is the opposite of the *Goetia*’s destruction of dignities and does not translate Weyer’s *dignitatum despectum ingerit*. It is best treated as an editorial error, reversal, or conflation—not evidence for an independent benevolent office.

### Does Louis Le Breton depict Raum?

**[Negative finding]** No dedicated Le Breton portrait appears with the Raum entry on p. 571, and no Raum plate was found in the indexed 1863 portrait collection.

The sixth edition advertises 550 engravings, including 72 demon portraits, but not every catalogue spirit receives one. The familiar Le Breton crow-demon images sometimes labeled “Raum” online are generally another spirit or modern relabelings.

The [1863 Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_1) confirms that the Raum entry is text-only.

## 3. The Haborym picture and the Raum conflation

De Plancy’s **Haborym** entry, pp. 316–317, does have a Le Breton engraving. It says:

> **Quelques-uns disent que c’est le même que Ranm ; ce qui nous paraît au moins douteux.**

“Some say that he is the same as Ranm; which seems to us at least doubtful.”

The printed **Ranm** is probably intended as *Raum*, but the typography itself should be reported. Haborym/Aym is a three-headed, torch-bearing Duke riding a viper and commanding twenty-six legions—plainly different from Raum’s crow, Earl rank, and thirty legions.

Gustav Davidson later converted this doubtful remark into the assertion that “Raum also answers to the name Haborym” and associated Raum with de Plancy’s three-headed portrait. That is a secondary-source conflation. De Plancy himself expressed doubt. See the [Haborym entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Haborym).

## 4. Modern visual culture

Modern Raum art tends to follow one of four patterns:

1. a naturalistic black crow;
2. an anthropomorphic raven noble or armored count;
3. a human figure accompanied by corvids and stolen treasure;
4. the Mathers circular seal used alone as jewelry, tattoo, altar plaque, or digital emblem.

These images visualize the terse text but are not manuscript iconography. The seal, especially the Mathers double-circle version, has become Raum’s de facto modern portrait.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer was a physician and critic of aggressive witch persecution, but not a modern materialist who rejected all spirits. He regarded much alleged witchcraft as delusion, fraud, illness, or demonic deception. His publication supplied the first secure Raum text and the basic catalogue later absorbed into the *Goetia*.

### Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft* sought to expose conjuring literature and undermine witch-prosecution claims. By translating or printing the catalogue, however, it became the immediate English source used by later magicians. Peterson suggests Abraham Fleming was probably responsible for much of the translation.

### The anonymous *Goetia* compiler

The compiler reorganized Scot/Weyer’s catalogue, expanded it to seventy-two, attached seals, and joined it to a working ritual drawn from the *Heptameron*, the *Key of Solomon*, Agrippa, and related magic.

The compiler’s identity is unknown.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

The Harleian witnesses are connected by their headings and later tradition to Dr Thomas Rudd, but the surviving manuscripts were copied by Peter Smart around the turn of the eighteenth century. The crucial contribution of this textual family is the explicit matching of seventy-two Goetic spirits with seventy-two Shem angels.

### Mathers and Crowley

Mathers prepared the English edition; Crowley published it in 1904 and supplied additional ritual and theoretical matter. The spelling **Räum**, the circularized printed seal, and later Thelemic reception all descend largely from this edition.

Crowley’s *777*—published anonymously in 1909—then imposed the day/night decan structure, making Raum the night spirit of early Taurus and an Earl of Mars/iron.

### Frater Achad

Frater Achad’s *Q.B.L.; or, The Bride’s Reception* reproduces Qabalistic correspondences in which **IIZAL** appears as an angel of the Six of Swords. No Raum-specific magical record or doctrine was found in Achad’s published work.

### Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy condensed the catalogue and introduced two influential anomalies: Raum “gives dignities,” and Haborym is said by unnamed persons to be identical with “Ranm,” although de Plancy doubts it. Le Breton did not supply Raum with an individual portrait.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition established a transparent critical basis using Sloane 3825 with readings from Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other witnesses. His online archive makes Weyer, Scot, and the *Goetia* directly comparable. For Raum studies, his most important contributions are the spelling census, the Weyer/Scot number 42 note, and identification of the parallel **Zayme** entry in Folger MS V.b.26.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

*The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007; later Llewellyn edition) transcribes and contextualizes Harley MSS 6482–6483, Sloane 3824, and Wellcome 3203. It explains the two-sided lamen and the Shem angel as a control upon the corresponding demon.

Its historical interpretation—especially the degree to which the compilation should be credited personally to Thomas Rudd—should be distinguished from the manuscript transcription itself.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* presents a modern operative synthesis using Goetic spirits, angelic authority, and “dark mirror” spirit vision. It discusses the seventy-two-spirit complex but no uniquely influential Raum operation was verified.

### Lon Milo DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt

Their *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* popularized a psychological, post-Crowley approach to evocation and added modern ritual discussion. It is an interpretation of the Mathers–Crowley book, not another manuscript witness.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium* compares spirit lists across early grimoires and emphasizes textual families, offices, and name corruption. His approach is especially relevant to possible relationships among Weyer, the *Livre des Esperitz*, the *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*, and the Folger manuscript. Proposed identifications remain reconstructions rather than automatic synonymies.

### Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Julien Véronèse, and Jean-Patrice Boudet

These scholars provide the necessary wider history of ritual magic, grimoires, learned demonology, divine names, and medieval/early-modern spirit catalogues. None supplies evidence for an ancient pagan Raum. Boudet’s critical publication of the *Livre des Esperitz* is particularly important for showing what is—and is not—in that predecessor catalogue.

## Grimoire ancestors and parallels

## 1. *Testament of Solomon*

The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* tells how Solomon interrogates demons, learns their offices and thwarting angels, seals them, and forces them to work on the Temple.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** It is a conceptual ancestor of later Solomonic demon catalogues.

**[Negative finding]** It contains no Raum/Raym/Raim and is not a direct textual source for the 1577 entry. Its demon–angel countermeasure pattern is nevertheless a striking remote parallel to Rudd’s system.

Primary translation: [*Testament of Solomon*](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm).

## 2. Munich Handbook, Clm 849

The fifteenth-century Munich handbook, edited by Richard Kieckhefer as *Forbidden Rites*, contains ritual magic, spirit operations, and demon names.

**[Negative finding]** No Raum/Raym/Raim was found. Similarity of genre is not identity of catalogue.

## 3. *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin’s lists include major names such as Lucifer, Leviathan, Satan, Belial, Oriens, Paimon, Ariton, Amaimon, Astaroth, Magot, Asmodeus, and Beelzebub, followed by hundreds of subordinate names.

**[Negative finding]** No secure Raum corresponding to Weyer’s crow-Earl was located. The book is a parallel late-medieval demon-list tradition, not evidence for Raum’s antiquity.

## 4. *Livre des Esperitz*

The extant French *Livre des Esperitz* contains a much smaller catalogue whose names and descriptions demonstrably anticipate many Weyer spirits. The surviving manuscript was copied in the mid-sixteenth century; proposals for an earlier archetype remain possible.

**[Negative finding]** It does not list Raum, Raym, or Raim.

That absence matters. It prevents the common argument “all Weyerian demons come from the medieval *Livre des Esperitz*.” Many do; Raum has not been shown to.

Critical study: Jean-Patrice Boudet, [“Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

## 5. *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Trithemius mentions books titled *Liber officiorum* or *De officiis spirituum*. Weyer expressly calls his source a *Liber officiorum spirituum*. Several later or fragmentary texts belong to this family.

The title identifies a genre or textual family, not a single surviving book with a securely traceable Raum page.

## 6. Folger MS V.b.26: Zayme

Folger MS V.b.26, the manuscript later edited as *The Book of Oberon*, contains:

> **The 12 is called Zayme, he can bringe money from any place, he will, or is assigned unto him, & to carry the same to any appointed place, he cane in a moment shewe the buildinge or scituacion of any plotte cittye or castell, & cane procure dignitie & honour, & cometh like a raven.**

Source: [Folger V.b.26 transcription, p. 92](https://www2.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf).

The correspondences are conspicuous:

- **Zayme / Raym** are visually and phonetically similar;
- both appear as a raven/crow;
- both take or transport wealth;
- both concern cities;
- both concern dignity.

The differences are equally important:

- Zayme brings money “from any place,” not specifically by stealing from kings;
- Zayme shows the plan or situation of a city or castle rather than destroying it;
- Zayme **procures** dignity and honor, where Weyer’s Raum instills contempt for dignities.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Peterson therefore calls Zayme a correspondence or parallel. It may represent a cognate entry, a divergent ancestor, or contamination among similar spirit descriptions.

**[Disputed]** Folger V.b.26 is approximately contemporary with Weyer, not securely earlier. It does not prove that Raum existed centuries before 1577.

## Order and number: 69 versus 72

Weyer’s surviving catalogue contains **69** entries, although the surrounding rhetoric sometimes speaks of seventy-two principal spirits.

The *Goetia* contains **72**. It achieves this by:

- adding **Vassago** as no. 3;
- adding **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius** as nos. 70–72;
- omitting Weyer’s no. 4, **Pruflas/Bufas**.

Four added minus one omitted produces a net increase of three: 69 → 72.

Pruflas is already absent from Scot’s English list. This is one of the best pieces of evidence that the *Goetia* compiler worked from Scot or from the same defective branch rather than independently translating a complete Weyer.

The order is also extensively rearranged. Raum moves from **42** to **40**, not simply because two preceding names vanish but because the list was reorganized.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Seventy-two was probably desirable because it permitted alignment with the seventy-twofold divine Name. The bare *Goetia* does not itself present Rudd’s complete angel–demon pairing, but its completed total made that later operation possible.

## Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is the human form a separate appearance?

Yes, but it is undescribed. The exorcist commands the spirit to “put on human shape.” Modern portraits that give the human form fixed clothes or anatomy are inventions.

## 2. Crow or raven?

Latin *corvus* and French *corbeau* favor “raven” in some translations; Scot and the English *Goetia* explicitly say “crow.” Early-modern ornithological terminology was not always as taxonomically narrow as modern English. Both labels are defensible, but **crow** is the direct English textual inheritance.

## 3. Does Raum destroy dignity, despise it, or grant it?

All three appear in print, but at different stages:

1. Weyer: instills **contempt for dignities**.
2. Scot: has “great despite unto dignities.”
3. *Goetia*: destroys “the dignities of men.”
4. De Plancy: **gives dignities**.

The first three form an intelligible chain of intensification. De Plancy’s positive “gives” is anomalous and probably erroneous.

## 4. Does Raum cause love?

Weyer says he reconciles or obtains the favor of friends and enemies. Scot says he reconciles friends and foes. The *Goetia* says he “cause[s] Love between Friends & Foes.”

Thus “love magic” is documented in the seventeenth-century *Goetia*, but it is an expansion of a narrower favor/reconciliation office.

## 5. Is Raum Haborym/Aym?

De Plancy reports that “some” equate Haborym with **Ranm**, then immediately calls the proposition doubtful. The spirits have different:

- ranks;
- forms;
- offices;
- numbers of legions;
- locations in Weyer’s list.

No early Weyer or Scot witness calls Raum Haborym. Davidson’s later assertion magnified a doubtful de Plancy aside into a synonym. The conflation is weak.

## 6. Is Raum another form of Aim, Caim, or “-aym” spirits?

Modern practitioners sometimes group Raym with Aim/Aym, Caim/Caym, or related-looking names. Similar orthography alone is insufficient. The catalogue treats them as separate spirits with separate ranks, appearances, offices, and seals.

A lost common ancestor or scribal splitting cannot be ruled out, but it has not been demonstrated for Raum.

## 7. Is the name a misprint?

No particular misprint has been proved to create it. The stability of **Raum** plus **Raym/Raim** across Weyer and Scot suggests a transmitted name rather than a one-off printer’s error. Its ultimate form may still be corrupt.

The demonstrable late editorial innovations are:

- **Räum**, the umlauted 1904 form;
- **RAVM**, merely scribal/typographical capitalization;
- de Plancy’s probable **Ranm**;
- de Plancy’s reversed “gives dignities.”

## 8. Is Ieiazel the ancient enemy of Raum?

No.

- The Exodus triplet tradition is older than the *Goetia*.
- The named angelic catalogue is older than the surviving Rudd manuscript.
- Raum and Ieiazel arise in historically independent textual streams.
- Their opposition results from matching both lists by the number **40**.

It is a documented Rudd/Harley magical correspondence, not an ancient Jewish doctrine or a statement in Weyer.

## 9. What is the correct zodiac sector?

There is no system-independent answer:

- Ieiazel in Lenain’s sequential calendar: **15°–20° Libra**.
- Ieiazel in Golden Dawn/*777* tarot tables: **15°–20° Aquarius**, Six of Swords.
- Räum in Crowley’s Goetic demon table: **0°–10° Taurus**, Five of Disks.

A source must always be named.

## 10. Which planet belongs to Raum?

Again, the answer depends on the layer:

- manuscript Earl metal: Venus/copper and Moon/silver;
- *777* Earl rank: Mars/iron;
- *777* demon decan: Mercury in Taurus;
- Lenain-Ieiazel decan: Saturn in Libra;
- Golden Dawn-Ieiazel card: Mercury in Aquarius.

These are overlapping symbolic systems, not five ancient facts about one being.

## 11. “Enns,” colors, incense, offerings, and personality

The frequently printed enn:

> *Furca na alle laris Raum*

is not in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, Harley 6483, Mathers–Crowley, or *777*. It belongs to modern demonolatry currents.

Likewise, fixed claims about:

- red or black candles;
- dragon’s-blood incense;
- offerings of coins, alcohol, feathers, or raw meat;
- a “justice” or revolutionary personality;
- trauma healing;
- political liberation;
- “energy vampirism”;
- a fixed gender or voice;

are **modern practice, symbolism, or UPG**, not historical Goetic description.

**[Modern fictional adaptation, secondary documentation]** Reference indexes for *Hellboy in Hell* list Raum among high-ranking demons turned upon and killed by their infernal servants. The identification is documented mainly through specialist series indexes rather than a searchable official transcript.

### *Helluva Boss*

**[Negative finding]** No canonical character named Raum had been attested in released *Helluva Boss* material at the research date. Numerous fan-created Ars Goetia designs exist, including Raum characters, but those are fandom inventions. Canonical use of other Goetic names—Stolas, Paimon, Andrealphus, Vassago—does not establish Raum’s appearance.

### *Welcome to Demon School! Iruma-kun*

A succubus instructor named **Raim/Raimu** is sometimes claimed as a Raum adaptation.

**[Plausible allusion, unconfirmed]** The name resembles Raum’s variant *Raim*, and the series borrows many demonological names. No creator statement establishing the derivation was found, and the character’s sex, form, and office differ radically.

### Other modern uses

- **DC/Doom Patrol:** a demon called Raum or Israfel appears in modern comics, but the narrative character is not a close adaptation of the Goetic Earl.
- **Final Fantasy XI:** reference databases report a “Count Raum” enemy, a clear nominal borrowing requiring game-version documentation for exact dates and mechanics.

## Chronological synthesis

### Late antiquity to the fifteenth century

- *Testament of Solomon*: demons questioned and controlled through divine/angelic knowledge; no Raum.
- Medieval Jewish mysticism develops the seventy-twofold Exodus Name.
- *Sefer Raziel* transmits seventy-two-name material; no opposition to Raum.
- *Abramelin*, Munich Clm 849, and early spirit-office traditions contain analogous catalogues; no secure Raum.

### Early and mid-sixteenth century

- Reuchlin, 1517: Christian-Kabbalistic angel names.
- Agrippa, 1533: explicit Exodus 14:19–21 construction and seventy-two quinaries.
- Trithemius and later bibliographers know works called *Offices of Spirits*.
- The extant *Livre des Esperitz* is copied; it contains many Weyer precursors but not Raum.
- A lost or unidentified 1570 *Liber officiorum* witness is alleged in the Weyer/Scot tradition.

### 1577

- Weyer publishes **Raum vel Raym**, no. 42.
- Core profile established: great Count, corvid, human form on command, theft and transport, destruction of cities, contempt for dignities, temporal knowledge, favor of friends and enemies, former Throne, thirty legions.

### 1584

- Scot prints **Raum, or Raim**, great Earl.
- “Reconcileth friends and foes” becomes the dominant English formulation.

### Circa 1577–early seventeenth century

- Folger MS V.b.26 records **Zayme**, a raven spirit who transports money and procures dignity; possible cognate, not proven identity.

### Seventeenth century

- The anonymous *Ars Goetia* compiler reorganizes the catalogue.
- Raum becomes no. 40.
- “Treasure,” destruction of men’s dignity, temporal answers, and “love” are made explicit.
- A seal and lamen instruction are added.
- The list is completed to seventy-two.

### 1686/87–1713

- Sloane 2731 and related witnesses preserve Raum and his character.
- Harley 6483 explicitly matches Raum with Ieiazel and places the angelic name/seal on the controlling side of the lamen.
- The surviving Harley copy is dated 1712/13, even if some material is older.

### 1818–1863

- De Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* passes through six editions.
- The verified 1863 Raum entry says he “gives dignities,” probably a reversal.
- No Le Breton Raum portrait appears.
- The Haborym entry reports, but doubts, an identification with “Ranm.”

### 1823 onward

- Lenain fixes the influential French angelology of Ieiazel: “God who rejoices,” 15°–20° Libra, liberation, consolation, printing and books, psalm and calendar.
- Papus republishes and popularizes it.

### 1888–1909

- Golden Dawn tarot material places Ieiazel on the Six of Swords.
- Mathers and Crowley publish **Räum** in 1904.
- Crowley’s *777* makes Räum the night demon of 0°–10° Taurus and maps Earls to Mars/iron.

### Mid-twentieth century to present

- Ambelain expands the angelic talismanic tradition.
- Haziel turns Ieiazel into a widely marketed guardian angel of joy, consolation, and artistic expression.
- Peterson restores manuscript comparison.
- Skinner and Rankine publish the Rudd/Smart angel–demon system.
- Fiction and games detach Raum from the terse grimoire entry and develop raven demons, secret demons, abyssal warriors, and possessing entities.
- Modern demonolatry supplies enns, offerings, psychology, ancient-god identities, and personal testimony not found in the source chain.

## Open questions

1. **What was Weyer’s immediate manuscript?**  
   His *Liber officiorum spirituum* is named but not securely identified. Recovery of the relevant witness could move Raum’s attestation back to 1570 or reveal the name’s corruption history.

2. **Is Zayme a relative of Raum/Raym?**  
   The shared raven, money-transport, city, and dignity complex is too close to ignore, but the offices are partly reversed and the manuscript is approximately contemporary with Weyer.

3. **What does the name mean?**  
   German *Raum* and *räumen* offer attractive wordplay, but no historical bridge has been found. An ancient divine name has not been identified.

4. **Where did the seal come from?**  
   No pre-*Goetia* exemplar or convincing kamea derivation has been established.

5. **Who devised the Ieiazel–Raum opposition?**  
   The pairing is documented in the Harley tradition. Whether Thomas Rudd personally devised it, copied it, or merely became associated with it through Peter Smart’s compilation remains unresolved.

6. **Which de Plancy edition first introduced “gives dignities”?**  
   The wording is verified in 1863. A page-by-page collation of all six editions would establish whether it entered in 1818 or during revision.

7. **Is “Ranm” merely a typesetting error?**  
   Almost certainly, but the original wording should be retained when discussing the Haborym conflation.

8. **Why did the Golden Dawn’s Shem-card order diverge from Lenain’s zodiac order?**  
   Both systems are documented, but a complete stemmatic study of their source tables and rearrangement would be needed to establish every stage.

9. **How should “dignity” be understood?**  
   Weyer may mean offices, honors, rank, or social prestige. “Contempt for dignities,” “destroying dignities,” and “giving dignities” represent materially different magical claims.

10. **Is the corvid symbolic or inherited mechanically?**  
    The sources never explain why Raum is a crow. Associations with theft, carrion, intelligence, prophecy, kingship, or battlefield ruin are interpretive possibilities, not early explanations.

# Asmoday: Research Dossier

## Editorial method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes the following categories:

- **[Documented text]** What a cited primary text or manuscript actually says.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** A conclusion argued from philology, manuscript relationships, or historical context.
- **[Tradition]** A claim transmitted within Jewish, Christian, magical, or occult literature without independent historical verification.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** A deliberately constructed magical, astrological, or divinatory assignment.
- **[Legend]** Narrative folklore presented here for its history, not as literal fact.
- **[Disputed]** A contested identification or reading.
- **[Modern invention]** A fictional, artistic, or twentieth-/twenty-first-century occult elaboration.
- **[Negative finding]** A claim sought but not found in the relevant sources.

Because a single-source quotation is subject to quotation-length limits, each primary text receives a short verbatim specimen followed by a complete clause-by-clause semantic transcription. The linked editions provide the full diplomatic text.

# The name and its origins

## 1. Ancient Iranian background

**[Documented text]** The Younger Avestan word **aēšma** means wrath, fury, or violent rage and is also personified as a hostile daēva. Aēšma disrupts sacrifice, promotes violence and intoxication, and opposes Sraoša, the embodiment of religious obedience and attentive hearing. Middle Persian texts preserve **Xēšm**, “Wrath,” as a demon.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The most influential modern etymology derives Greek *Asmodaios* and Jewish Aramaic *Ashmedai* from a reconstructed Iranian compound commonly represented as ***aēšmō-daēva***, approximately “demon of wrath.” The full compound does not survive in the extant Avesta. Its existence is inferred from the later names and from Middle Persian **Xēšm Dēw**. This reconstruction is defended in the *Encyclopaedia Iranica* entry on Aēšma.

**[Disputed]** Older reference works questioned whether the Iranian phonology adequately explains every consonant and vowel in *Asmodaios/Ashmedai*. The rival derivation from Hebrew **שמד**, *š-m-d*, “destroy,” remains popular in theological and occult dictionaries, but is morphologically less persuasive: the form of *Ashmedai* does not straightforwardly follow from the Hebrew root. Neither proposal is an ancient explanation supplied by Tobit or the rabbis.

Asmoday therefore does have an ancient religious figure behind the name, but not an ancient god or hero in the manner of Baal or Astarte. Aēšma is an Iranian demon or personified destructive passion, not a displaced Canaanite deity. [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Aēšma”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/aesma-wrath/); [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Asmodeus”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/2019-asmodeus)

## 2. Tobit: Asmodaios

**[Documented text]** The earliest surviving literary form of the name is Greek **Ἀσμοδαῖος**, *Asmodaios*, in the Book of Tobit. Some witnesses have the shorter-looking **Ἀσμοδαος**. The Latin Vulgate uses **Asmodeus**.

Tobit was probably composed during the Second Temple period, often dated approximately **225–175 BCE**. Four Aramaic fragments and one Hebrew fragment were found at Qumran. These witnesses support the older, longer form of the story known from the major Greek textual tradition.

In the narrative, Asmodaios has killed the seven men successively married to Sarah, daughter of Raguel, before the marriages can be consummated. Raphael instructs Tobias to burn the heart and liver of a fish; the odor drives the demon away, Raphael pursues him to Upper Egypt, and binds him there.

**[Qualification]** Later summaries often say that Asmodeus kills the husbands because he is sexually jealous or in love with Sarah. That motivation is clearer in later interpretive tradition than in every surviving recension of Tobit itself. [Vulgate Tobit 3:8](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0060%3Abook%3DTobias%3Achapter%3D3%3Averse%3D8); [study of Tobit’s Qumran witnesses](https://czasopisma.kul.pl/index.php/ba/article/view/2133); [textual overview](https://www.scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S0259-94222020000400036&script=sci_arttext)

## 3. The Testament of Solomon

**[Documented text]** The composite Greek *Testament of Solomon* includes **Asmodeus** as a demon interrogated and compelled by Solomon. This Asmodeus says that he attacks newlyweds, harms virgins, excites adultery, and produces madness and murder. Raphael frustrates him, and the smoke of a fish’s liver and gall drives him away—an unmistakable reuse of Tobit. Solomon forces him to carry water and tread clay for the building of the Temple.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The *Testament* is not a unitary first-century eyewitness document. Its layers and final form are difficult to date; scholars generally treat it as a composite work of late-antique and later materials surviving in medieval manuscripts.

**[Negative finding]** Its Asmodeus does not have the *Goetia*’s three heads, goose feet, dragon mount, lance, banner, Ring of Virtues, mathematical curriculum, or 72 legions. [English/Greek text of the *Testament of Solomon*](https://www.greekdoc.com/DOCUMENTS/pseudepigrapha/t-solomon.html); [NASSCAL textual overview](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/testament-of-solomon/); [Sarah Schwarz on the work’s date and composition](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0951820707077166)

## 4. Rabbinic Ashmedai

Jewish Aramaic writes the name **אשמדאי**, conventionally transliterated *Ashmedai*, *Ašmeday*, or *Ashmodai*.

### Solomon, Ashmedai, and the shamir

**[Legend: Babylonian Talmud]** In *Gittin* 68a–b, Solomon needs the **shamir**, a miraculous agent capable of cutting stone without iron, because iron tools are unsuitable for the Temple. He is told that Ashmedai, king of the demons, knows where it is.

Benaiah ben Jehoiada finds Ashmedai’s mountain cistern, drains it, substitutes wine, and captures the drunken demon with a chain and ring bearing the divine name. Ashmedai explains that the shamir is entrusted to the “prince of the sea” and guarded by a wild cock or hoopoe.

Ashmedai later persuades Solomon to remove the chain and lend him the ring. He swallows or casts away the ring, expands between earth and heaven, throws Solomon far from Jerusalem, and occupies his place. His demonic identity is betrayed in different versions by his feet, sexual conduct, or refusal to observe royal customs. Solomon eventually returns with the ring or divine name, and Ashmedai flees.

**[Historical significance]** This is the source of Asmodeus as a rival or temporary usurper of Solomon’s throne. It is not an episode in Kings or Chronicles, and it does not supply the later Goetic physical description. [Babylonian Talmud, *Gittin* 68a](https://www.sefaria.org/Gittin.68a?lang=bi); [*Gittin* 68b](https://www.sefaria.org/Gittin.68b?lang=bi)

### Character differences

**[Documented tradition]** The Talmudic Ashmedai is clever, dangerous, comic, and occasionally morally perceptive. He is not simply the Christian “demon of lust.” That narrower identification arose by combining Tobit, the *Testament of Solomon*, later demonology, and the vice classifications of early-modern Europe.

**[Tradition]** Medieval Jewish commentators variously make Ashmedai the child of the demon Shamdon and Naamah. Other genealogies found in modern occult literature—especially those involving Agrat bat Mahlat or King David—belong to later conflations and are not uniform rabbinic doctrine.

## 5. The medieval and early-modern grimoire name

### *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Documented text]** A pre-Weyer spirit catalogue preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29 has:

> “Asmoday est grant roy. Il donne ung anel de telle vertu…”

The entry says that Asmoday is a great king, gives a ring whose wearer is fortunate in worldly affairs, answers truthfully, and has **twelve legions**. It lacks Weyer’s physical description and scientific offices. [*Livre des Esperitz*, “Asmoday”](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Asmoday)

**[Disputed dating]** The manuscript and its underlying catalogue have been dated variously from the fifteenth to the mid-sixteenth century. Jean-Patrice Boudet treats the surviving copy as mid-sixteenth century but its material as belonging to an earlier catalogue family. Richard Kieckhefer and other cataloguers have suggested fifteenth- or early-sixteenth-century strata.

**[Finding]** The name **Asmoday unquestionably appears in a pre-1577 grimoire tradition**. Weyer is therefore not its first attestation.

### Trithemius and spirit-office catalogues

Peterson records **Asmhodæi** in Trithemius’s *Polygraphia*, p. 344, and the following early catalogue variants:

- Coxe MS 25: **Asmoday**
- SSM L.2.f.30: **Admoday**
- Trinity catalogue: **Asmoday**
- Folger V.b.26: **Asmoday**
- Trithemius: **Asmhodæi**

These witnesses show a mobile manuscript name whose letters were easily displaced by scribes and compositors. [Peterson’s Weyer/Goetia comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### *Book of Oberon* / Folger V.b.26

**[Documented text]** Folger MS V.b.26 places Asmoday first under Amaymon, king of the south. Its appearance differs slightly: the heads are ass, bull, and ram; it has a serpent’s tail, ass-like feet, and fire from the mouth. It teaches astronomy, arithmetic, music, and geometry; answers concerning obscure matters; grants invisibility; and reveals treasure.

This is close enough to Weyer to demonstrate a shared *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* family, but the differences argue against mechanical copying from Weyer alone. [Folger V.b.26 transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf)

### Munich handbook

**[Negative finding]** The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook, Clm 849, is an important ancestor of the spirit-office genre, but its surviving short catalogue does **not** name Asmoday. It establishes the milieu, not an Asmoday attestation. [Richard Kieckhefer, *Forbidden Rites*](https://mlpol.net/images/src/2C0B640AA93C3878885B62642E5FE91A-9155924.pdf)

## 6. What is Sydonay?

Weyer prints **Sydonay, alias Asmoday**; Scot gives **Sidonay, alias Asmoday**. Later manuscripts have **Sinodai** and similar forms.

**[Disputed]** No reliable primary evidence connects Sydonay to the city of Sidon, a Sidonian god, Astarte, or the adjective “Sidonian.” The form may be:

- a corrupted doublet of *Asmoday*;
- a displaced or contaminated manuscript reading;
- an otherwise lost independent spirit name subsequently identified with Asmoday.

**[Negative finding]** The dedicated searches did not uncover a philologically argued ancient etymology for *Sydonay*. Calling it “the demon of Sidon” is speculation.

# The description across the sources

## 1. Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* (1577)

Johann Weyer included the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. Asmoday is no. 35.

### Latin specimen

> “Sydonay, aliàs Asmoday, Rex magnus, fortis & potens: visitur tribus capitibus…”

—Weyer, 1577, *Pseudomonarchia*, §35, p. 923. [Latin and aligned English text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### Complete semantic transcription

**[Documented text]**

- Sydonay, otherwise Asmoday, is a great, strong, and powerful king.
- He appears with three heads: bull, man, and ram.
- His tail is serpentine.
- He belches flame from his mouth.
- His feet are those of a goose.
- He sits on an infernal dragon.
- He carries a lance and banner.
- He precedes the other spirits under Amaymon.
- The exorcist must be brave, cautious, and standing.
- If the exorcist is overcome or covered by fear, Amaymon will deceive him and disclose his actions.
- On seeing the form, the exorcist must identify it by saying that it is Asmoday.
- Asmoday will not deny the name and will bow to the ground.
- He gives the Ring of Virtues.
- He teaches geometry, arithmetic, astronomy, and mechanics.
- He answers questions fully and truthfully.
- He makes a person invisible.
- He shows and guards places of treasure when they belong to Amaymon’s legions.
- He commands 72 legions.

### “Unlawful seat”

**[Negative finding]** Weyer’s Asmoday entry contains no phrase corresponding to “unlawful seat,” “illicit throne,” or *sedes illicita*. The nearby Gaap material condemns sacrifices and “many wicked things,” while Jewish legend makes Ashmedai usurp Solomon’s throne. Those passages may have been conflated with Asmoday’s entry.

## 2. Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* (1584)

Scot printed an English translation of Weyer’s catalogue in Book XV, chapter 2. In the 1886 Nicholson reprint retaining the original pagination, the Asmoday entry is on pp. **321–322**, with marginal source-page numerals 386–387.

### English specimen

> “Sidonay, alias Asmoday, a great king, strong and mightie, he is seene with three heads…”

[Scot’s complete text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/60766/60766-h/60766-h.htm)

### Complete semantic content

Scot reproduces all of Weyer’s physical features and offices:

- bull, man, and ram heads;
- serpent tail;
- flames from the mouth;
- goose feet;
- infernal dragon;
- lance and flag;
- precedence under Amaymon;
- ring;
- geometry, arithmetic, astronomy, and handicrafts;
- truthful answers;
- invisibility;
- treasure;
- 72 legions.

### Scot’s consequential mistranslations

Two errors became ritual rules in the later *Goetia*.

1. **Weyer:** *sit fortis* — “let him be brave.”  
   **Scot:** “let him be abroad.”

2. **Weyer:** *si vero coopertus fuerit* — approximately “if he is overwhelmed/covered,” in context probably overwhelmed by fear.  
   **Scot:** “if his cap be on his head.”

**[Textual history]** The seventeenth-century *Goetia* inherited “abroad” and the cap instruction. Mathers and Crowley preserved and strengthened the latter by writing “cap or head-dress.” Thus an English translation error became an operational prescription.

Scot also translated Latin *mechanicam* as “handicrafts.” “Mechanics,” practical engineering, or mechanical arts is closer to the Latin.

## 3. Sloane 2731 and Sloane 3825

### Manuscript context

- **Sloane 2731** is generally assigned to the later seventeenth century, often 1686–1687.
- **Sloane 3825** is another seventeenth-century Lemegeton witness and is the principal manuscript transcribed in Peterson’s online edition.
- Both call the spirit **Asmoday**, no. 32.

The *Lemegeton* appears to be a compilation from several prototypes rather than the work of a single author. [Peterson’s manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm); [Sloane 2731 facsimile](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/)

### Sloane 3825 specimen

> “The 32d spirit in order is called Asmoday. he is a great king, strong & powerfull…”

—Sloane 3825, fols. 104v–105r. [Peterson’s transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Additions and alterations

The manuscript follows Scot closely but:

- calls Asmoday the “first and chiefest” under Amaymon;
- explicitly orders the cap to be off;
- specifies that the seal is to be worn as a **lamen on the breast**;
- incorporates a drawn seal absent from Weyer;
- retains all 72 legions and the principal offices.

**[Negative finding]** No alternate human, comely, or second form is specified. Such demanded secondary forms occur in other Goetic entries, but not this one.

## 4. Harley 6483 and the Rudd material

Harley 6483 is dated internally to approximately **1712–1713** and was copied by Peter Smart from papers described as the “sheets of Dr Rudd.” It spells the spirit **Asmodai**.

**[Documented manuscript tradition]** The manuscript pairs the 72 Goetic spirits individually with 72 Shem angels. Asmodai no. 32 is paired with **Vasariah no. 32**.

**[Caution]** Harley 6483 is not an autograph manuscript of Thomas Rudd. The “Dr Rudd” attribution is part of its copying history, but the exact authorship of every layer remains uncertain. [Peterson’s Lemegeton survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm); [Rudd-table comparison](https://lyricaltokarev.com/public/dynamic/sync/stuff/pdf-documents-library/Complete%20Magician%27s%20Tables.pdf)

## 5. Mathers–Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia* (1904)

The 1904 book was edited and translated by S. L. MacGregor Mathers and published by Aleister Crowley. The original printing was approximately 200 copies. Asmoday appears on original p. **21**; some digital transcriptions display a different electronic page number.

The heading is **“ASMODAY, or ASMODAI.”**

### Specimen

> “The Thirty-second Spirit is Asmoday, or Asmodai. He is a Great King, Strong, and Powerful.”

[Mathers–Crowley text](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm); [1904 facsimile](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf)

### Complete content

The 1904 description gives:

- three heads: bull, man, ram;
- serpent’s tail;
- flames from his mouth;
- webbed goose feet;
- infernal dragon;
- lance and banner;
- first and chief under Amaymon;
- the instruction to stand throughout the action;
- removal of cap or head-dress;
- identification by name;
- bowing to the ground;
- Ring of Virtues;
- arithmetic, astronomy, geometry, and handicrafts;
- full and true answers;
- **invincibility**;
- treasure;
- 72 legions;
- the breast lamen.

### “Invisible” versus “invincible”

**[Documented textual error]**

- Weyer: *hominem reddit invisibilem* — makes a person **invisible**.
- Scot: “he maketh a man **invisible**.”
- Sloane 3825: **Invisible**.
- Sloane 2731: **invisible**.
- Sloane 3648: **invincible**, apparently a copying error.
- Mathers–Crowley: **invincible**.

Peterson identifies Sloane 3648 as the isolated manuscript support for “invincible.” The majority reading and Latin source establish **invisible** as the recoverable original. Invincibility is nevertheless a real part of the twentieth-century reception because Mathers and Crowley printed it.

## 6. Peterson’s 2001 edition

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* was published by Weiser in 2001. The edition uses Sloane 3825 as a principal witness and supplies readings from Sloane 2731, Sloane 3648, Harley 6483, and other manuscripts.

Peterson prints **Asmoday**, p. 21, and restores attention to **invisible** as the well-supported reading. His appendix presents Weyer’s catalogue, with Asmoday around p. 242. ## 8. Offices that Asmoday does—and does not—possess

**Attested in Weyer/Scot/Goetia:**

- mathematical and mechanical knowledge;
- truthful answers;
- invisibility;
- treasure revelation and guardianship;
- Ring of Virtues.

**Attested only in some related catalogues:**

- music, in Folger V.b.26;
- generalized worldly good fortune, in the *Livre des Esperitz*;
- invincibility, in Sloane 3648 and Mathers–Crowley.

**Not assigned in the core entry:**

- love or lust;
- languages;
- familiars;
- transmutation;
- resurrection;
- political offices;
- causing war;
- explicit knowledge of past, present, and future;
- an alternate beautiful or human form.

The identification with lust comes from Tobit, the *Testament of Solomon*, Peter Binsfeld’s vice scheme, and later demonology—not from the *Pseudomonarchia* or *Goetia* offices.

# The number and the list

## Weyer’s 69 and the Goetia’s 72

Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia* contains **69** spirits. The *Ars Goetia* has **72**.

The *Goetia*:

- adds **Vassago** as no. 3;
- adds **Seere** as no. 70;
- adds **Dantalion** as no. 71;
- adds **Andromalius** as no. 72;
- omits Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**.

Scot had already omitted Pruflas, so a compiler working principally through Scot would inherit only 68 of Weyer’s 69; four additions then produce 72.

Asmoday consequently moves from Weyer/Scot no. **35** to Goetia no. **32**. [Peterson’s comparative table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); [side-by-side spirit list](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Seventy-two was probably attractive because of established traditions concerning 72 divine names, 72 angels, nations, languages, or Solomonic kings. The surviving *Goetia* does not explain its editorial arithmetic, so a deliberate Shem-based expansion remains probable rather than directly documented.

# The angel: Vasariah

## 1. Identity

| Field | Vasariah |
|---|---|
| Number | 32 |
| Hebrew triplet | ושר |
| Expanded name | ושריה, conventionally Vasariah |
| Traditional attribute | *Deus justus*—“The just God” |
| Rudd pairing | Asmodai no. 32 |
| Psalm | Vulgate 32:4 / modern Psalm 33:4 |
| Zodiacal sector in Lenain | 156°–160° from 0° Aries = 5°–10° Virgo |
| Lenain time | 10:20–10:40 a.m. |
| Traditional offices | Justice, clemency, favorable legal judgment, memory, eloquence, reconciliation |

“Vasariah” is one of several possible Latin-letter vocalizations of the Hebrew consonants. The phrase “The just God” is an esoteric attribute, not a secure dictionary translation of an ordinary Hebrew personal name.

## 2. Construction of the 72-fold Name

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the Hebrew consonantal text used by the tradition, of three verses of 72 letters each. The verses are written in boustrophedon order:

1. verse 19 forward;
2. verse 20 backward;
3. verse 21 forward.

Reading the three letters in each vertical column produces 72 triplets. Angelic suffixes such as **-el** and **-iah** are then added to generate names.

The thirty-second triplet is conventionally **V–Sh–R**, expanded as **Vaša/sa-riah**, hence Vasariah.

**[Historical qualification]** *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally means the “explicit,” “distinct,” or “fully pronounced” divine name and may designate the Tetragrammaton in early Jewish sources. Its routine use for this 72-fold construction belongs to medieval and Renaissance reception. [Study of Reuchlin and the 72-fold Name](https://e-univers.ru/upload/iblock/919/1d14cc5s1qdrw3vc1f1360nmgkaohtru.pdf)

## 3. Sefer Raziel and medieval kabbalistic tradition

**[Tradition]** The medieval *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* corpus contains divine-name, angelic, amuletic, and cosmological materials central to the later reception of the 72-fold Name. Surviving *Raziel* manuscripts are composite; one should not treat a modern Vasariah personality profile as a single intact doctrine dictated in antiquity.

**[Negative finding]** The complete modern package—five-degree zodiacal rulership, personality, August birth dates, legal professions, and twenty-minute invocation interval—cannot be traced as one unit to *Sefer Raziel*. It accumulated through early-modern and modern tables. [University of Pennsylvania *Sefer Raziel* manuscript description](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002/html/mscodex1674.html)

## 4. Reuchlin and Kircher

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De Arte Cabalistica* (1517) helped transmit the 72-name construction into Christian Cabala. Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*, especially the *Cabala Hebraeorum* section of 1652, printed and reorganized Hebrew divine-name material for Christian readers.

**[Documented reception]** Kircher transmitted tables of the 72 triplets, angelic expansions, divine attributes, and related scriptural verses.

**[Qualification]** Kircher’s presentation is Christian-Cabalistic and frequently polemical. It should not be treated as a transparent reproduction of medieval Jewish consensus. [Kircher, *Cabala Hebraeorum*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/kircher/cabala.htm); [scan of *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* II](https://www.esonet.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/06/Oedipus_Aegyptiacus02.pdf)

## 5. Rudd’s constraining angel

**[Esoteric correspondence: Harley 6483]** The Rudd manuscript layer sets one Shem angel against each Goetic demon of the same number. Thus:

> **32 Vasariah constrains 32 Asmodai.**

The operative idea is not that Vasariah was Asmodeus’s opponent in antiquity. The magician invokes or inscribes the corresponding angelic authority to restrain the demon, commonly represented in modern editions by adjoining the angelic and demonic seals.

**[Important distinction]** In Tobit and the *Testament of Solomon*, Asmodeus’s adversary is **Raphael**. Vasariah enters through Rudd’s numerical system many centuries later.

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine’s *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007) published this system from Harley 6483 with comparisons from Harley 6482, Sloane 3824, and Wellcome 3203. [Skinner’s comparative table](https://lyricaltokarev.com/public/dynamic/sync/stuff/pdf-documents-library/Complete%20Magician%27s%20Tables.pdf); [Golden Hoard catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf); [publisher description](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556)

## 6. Psalm verse

Rudd and the later angel tables associate Vasariah with:

> “Quia rectum est verbum Domini…”

This is Vulgate Psalm **32:4**, corresponding to Psalm **33:4** in modern Hebrew-derived numbering:

“For the word of the Lord is upright, and all his work is done in faithfulness.”

The numbering discrepancy is liturgical, not a different verse.

## 7. Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives the first fully articulated nineteenth-century Vasariah profile consulted here, pp. 65–67.

**[Documented text]**

- no. 32: Vasariah;
- attribute: **“Dieu juste”**;
- divine name: **Anot**, said to be used in the “language of the Tartars”;
- rays 156–160, equivalent to 5°–10° Virgo;
- sixteenth five-degree period;
- decan genius **Thumis**;
- invocation time 10:20–10:40 a.m.;
- Psalm 32:4 in Vulgate numbering;
- invoked against legal attackers and for the clemency of rulers;
- governs justice, nobility, jurists, magistrates, and lawyers;
- produces good memory, easy speech, amiability, spirituality, and modesty;
- contrary genius produces bad qualities of body and soul.

Lenain also lists calendrical contact days: April 20, July 1, September 11, November 22, and February 2. These are not identical to later “guardian angel birthday” dates. [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365); [Wikimedia PDF](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3a/La_science_cabalistique_ou_lart_de_conna%C3%AEtre_les_bons_g%C3%A9nies_qui_influent_sur_la_destin%C3%A9e_des_hommes_(IA_BIUSante_ms05365).pdf)

## 8. Papus

Papus reproduced and popularized Lenain’s angelic system in *La Cabbale* (1903), including the names, scriptural verses, degrees, and moral offices.

**[Reception]** Papus is a transmitter, not the ancient source of the Vasariah material. [Papus, *La Cabbale*](https://www.hermanubis.com.br/LivrosVirtuais/Lacabala.pdf)

## 9. Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn incorporated:

- the 72 angelic names;
- Hebrew spellings;
- psalm verses;
- angelic seals;
- zodiacal and decanic structures;
- color, tarot, and planetary correspondences.

**[Qualification]** Golden Dawn materials do not collapse every table into one arrangement. The Shem angel quinances and the *777* Goetic day/night decans are related through later magical synthesis, but are mathematically distinct. [Golden Dawn Shem lecture](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Schemhamphorash.html); [scan of *Shem ha-Mephoresch—The 72-Fold Name*](https://www.magicgatebg.com/Books/INDEXII/Shem%20Ha-Mephoresch-The72FoldName.pdf)

## 10. Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (first published 1951) reworked the angelic tables into a practical ritual and talismanic system. Its influence is especially visible in French ceremonial magic.

**[Qualification]** Ambelain belongs to the modern practical reception. His ritual elaborations are not evidence for the contents of Harley 6483 or medieval Jewish practice. [Open Library bibliographic record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique)

## 11. Haziel

Haziel’s popular angel books, beginning in the later twentieth century, converted the 72 names into guardian-angel profiles for modern readers.

For Vasariah the system gives:

- 5°–10° Virgo;
- approximately August 29–September 2 as a guardian period;
- 10:20–10:40 a.m.;
- justice, favorable judges and lawyers, memory, eloquence, and pardon;
- Psalm 33:4.

**[Modern esoteric development]** Haziel’s fixed birth-date system is not in Rudd. Some popular websites instead give August 22–26 because they convert the five-degree sectors or calendrical starting point differently. [WorldCat record for Haziel](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/grand-livre-des-invocations-et-des-exhortations-prieres-adressees-aux-72-anges-servants-ou-genies-de-la-cabale/oclc/77084436)

## 2. The Virgo quinance

A 360-degree zodiac divided among 72 angels yields sectors of five degrees. Angel no. 32 receives degrees 156–160:

- Aries 0–30
- Taurus 30–60
- Gemini 60–90
- Cancer 90–120
- Leo 120–150
- Virgo 150–180

Thus degrees 156–160 correspond conventionally to **5°–10° Virgo**.

The full 0°–10° Virgo decan belongs to the **Eight of Pentacles/Disks**, traditionally Sun in Virgo. It contains:

- 0°–5° Virgo: angel 31 Lecabel; by Rudd number, demon Foras;
- 5°–10° Virgo: angel 32 Vasariah; demon Asmodai.

Calling the first quinance “day” and second “night” is a later simplifying convention; Lenain himself presents numbered five-degree rays rather than a universal day/night demon doctrine.

## 3. Crowley’s *777* decan

Crowley’s *777*, column CLVII, places **Asmoday** among the Goetic “demons of the decans by day,” in the succedent or second decan of Aquarius:

- **10°–20° Aquarius**
- Mercury in Aquarius
- Six of Swords
- Golden Dawn title “Lord of Earned Success” or “Science,” depending recension
- Asmoday by day
- Belial by night

This arrangement assigns two spirits to each tarot pip because the pip spans a ten-degree decan and has separate day and night rulers. [Crowley, *777 Revised*](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/crowley/777_Revised.pdf)

## 4. Rank, planet, and metal

In the *Goetia*’s “Observations”:

- kings correspond to the **Sun**;
- their seals are made in **gold**;
- kings may be bound or called from 9 a.m. to noon and from 3 p.m. to sunset.

For context, the broader table assigns silver to lunar marquises, copper to Venusian dukes, tin to Jovian princes, lead to Saturnian knights, and Mercury to presidents. Earls/counts receive a compound attribution involving copper and silver; later editors sometimes supply Mars or iron to fill the apparently missing planetary slot.

**[Qualification]** These rank correspondences are part of the grimoire’s ritual system. They are not attributes of the ancient Asmodaios in Tobit. [Goetia “Observations”](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks06.htm)

## 5. Astrological timing

### Core Goetic timing

**[Documented text]**

- King-hours: 9 a.m.–noon and 3 p.m.–sunset.
- Favorable lunar ages: the second, fourth, sixth, eighth, tenth, twelfth, and fourteenth days of the Moon.
- Gold is prescribed for a king’s seal.

### Later electional interpretation

**[Esoteric practice]** Later magicians often add:

- Sunday;
- a planetary hour of the Sun;
- a dignified or unafflicted Sun;
- an Aquarius decan election if following *777*;
- a Virgo election if working through Vasariah’s angelic quinance.

These are coherent later elections but not all stated together in the Asmoday entry. Mixing the Virgo angelic election and the Aquarius *777* demon-decan election produces a synthetic modern ritual, not a recovered seventeenth-century instruction.

## 6. Enochian overlays

**[Negative finding]** The historical *Goetia* provides no Enochian name, aethyr, tablet square, or Enochian king for Asmoday. John Dee and Edward Kelley’s angelic system developed separately.

Crowley’s 1904 prefatory invocation is the Greek-Egyptian “Bornless” or “Headless” rite, not an Enochian conjuration. Later combinations of Goetia, Enochian calls, watchtowers, and aethyrs are modern magical syntheses with no single canonical Asmoday correspondence. [Crowley, *Magick in Theory and Practice*](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1929__crowley___magick_in_theory_and_practice.pdf)

# Iconography

## 1. The manuscripts’ seals

**[Documented text]** Weyer, Scot, and the *Livre des Esperitz* supply no Asmoday seal. The seal enters the record through the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscripts.

The common Asmoday seal is a circular sigil consisting of:

- a containing roundel;
- a strong central vertical and crosswise structure;
- looped or ring-like terminal elements;
- inward-turning hooked strokes;
- small peripheral ticks or letter-like marks.

A verbal description cannot reproduce the exact geometry reliably; the facsimiles should be treated as primary evidence.

### Variants

- **Sloane 3825:** compact, rounded manuscript form; small terminal circles and hooks are uneven and evidently drawn freehand.
- **Sloane 2731:** same recognizable sigil family, with differences in curvature, spacing, terminal closure, and peripheral strokes.
- **Harley 6483:** the demon seal accompanies Vasariah’s angelic authority in the Rudd arrangement.
- **Mathers–Crowley:** regularizes the manuscript drawing into a clean printed emblem, making symmetry and line closure more definite.
- **Modern redrawings:** frequently alter stroke weight, rotate minor components, or close open loops. These are graphic standardizations, not distinct ancient seals.

The manuscript instruction says that the seal should be made and worn as a lamen on the breast. [Peterson’s Sloane 3825 transcription and seal](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm); [Sloane 2731 facsimile](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/); [modern reproduction of the 1904 seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:32-Asmoday_seal.png)

## 2. Collin de Plancy

### 1818 edition

Collin de Plancy’s first *Dictionnaire infernal* appeared in 1818 and already contained an **Asmodée** entry.

**[Negative finding]** It did not yet contain Louis Le Breton’s famous portrait. Referring to that engraving as “the 1818 Asmodeus illustration” is incorrect. [1818 volume](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal-1818-T1.djvu)

### 1863 edition

The sixth edition of 1863 contains the expanded article and Le Breton illustration, engraved by M. Jarrault.

De Plancy opens:

> “Asmodée, démon destructeur… Il est surintendant des maisons de jeu.”

The entry combines several traditions:

- Asmodeus as a destructive demon;
- an identification with Samael “according to some rabbis”;
- superintendent of gambling houses;
- instigator of dissipation and error;
- Tobit’s demon;
- Solomon and the Temple;
- the usurping Ashmedai story;
- Weyer’s three-headed dragon rider;
- rings with occult virtues;
- invisibility;
- mathematical sciences;
- treasure;
- 72 legions;
- variants Chammadaï and Sydonaï;
- a French possession narrative;
- Lesage’s *Diable boiteux*.

This is a reception compendium, not a single coherent ancient biography. [De Plancy, 1863, pp. 54–55](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Asmod%C3%A9e)

### Portrait

Le Breton depicts:

- bull, human, and ram heads;
- fire issuing from the human mouth;
- a human chest and arms;
- serpent tail;
- long birdlike or goose/cock feet;
- lance;
- banner;
- a winged, leonine-draconic mount.

The engraving adapts Weyer but gives the “infernal dragon” a conspicuously lionlike body. It has become the dominant modern portrait even though it postdates Weyer by nearly three centuries. [Public-domain image](https://pdimagearchive.org/images/25ae3c08-d6b4-4817-ad32-c61c952d6e36)

## 3. Modern visual reception

**[Modern invention]** Contemporary occult artists commonly combine:

- Le Breton’s three heads;
- Crowley’s printed seal;
- solar crowns or gold because Asmoday is a king;
- lust symbolism from Binsfeld;
- legal or mercurial imagery borrowed from Vasariah;
- Aquarius or Virgo symbolism from later correspondence tables.

No single historical source contains that complete package.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer, physician to Duke William of Jülich-Cleves-Berg, published the *Pseudomonarchia* while arguing that many accused witches were melancholic, deluded, coerced, or deceived. He nevertheless treated demons and illicit ritual magic as real theological problems.

His importance for Asmoday is textual: he printed the fullest sixteenth-century Latin description and transmitted it into European demonology. [Weyer text and introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

## Reginald Scot

Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft* was a skeptical attack on witch persecution and magical fraud. His English demon catalogue became the immediate linguistic source for much of the *Goetia*.

His mistranslations—“abroad” and “cap”—demonstrate that the *Goetia* compiler relied on Scot rather than independently retranslating Weyer. [Cornell’s 1584 edition record](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081)

## The anonymous Lemegeton compiler

The seventeenth-century compiler or compilers:

- rearranged the spirit list;
- expanded it to 72;
- inserted ritual directions and seals;
- made Asmoday no. 32;
- preserved Scot’s English mistakes.

No documentary basis identifies Solomon as the historical author.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

“Dr Rudd” is traditionally identified as Thomas Rudd, the English mathematician and military engineer. Harley 6483, however, is a later Peter Smart copy described as deriving from Rudd’s sheets.

**[Disputed attribution]** It is reasonable to call it the Rudd tradition, but less secure to call every word Thomas Rudd’s autograph doctrine. [Discussion of the Rudd manuscripts](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/06/addenda-et-scholia-2.html)

## Mathers and Crowley

Mathers prepared the translated/edited Goetic material; Crowley published it in 1904, contributed prefatory ritual material, and interpreted the spirits psychologically. Their edition made “invincible” internationally influential despite its weak manuscript support.

The 1904 publication was issued at Boleskine by the Society for the Propagation of Religious Truth. [Bibliographic description](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/goetia_1904/goetia_1904.htm)

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy synthesized biblical, rabbinic, grimoire, folkloric, and literary material. Le Breton produced the image that now visually defines Asmodeus in popular demonology.

## Arthur Edward Waite

Waite’s *Book of Ceremonial Magic* retold the Goetic catalogue for a broader English audience. His spellings include **Asmoday** and **Synoday**, helping preserve a secondary form of Weyer’s Sydonay.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s angel-demon pairings and double-seal method widely accessible. Their work is crucial for the documented pairing of Asmodai and Vasariah.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 critical edition and online diplomatic transcriptions made comparison of Weyer, Scot, and the Sloane manuscripts possible. His apparatus identifies the invisible/invincible and cap/covered errors.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium: A Discordant Concordance of Diverse Spirit Catalogues* and related work emphasize the genealogy of spirit catalogues, regional rulers, and the *Liber Officiorum* tradition.

**[Qualification]** His importance is comparative and reconstructive. No independently documented Asmoday revelation should be attributed to him merely because he discusses the catalogue family. [Discussion of *Pandemonium*](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0Md3Ca52ClGrIT84OqrMIR)

## Owen Davies, Jean-Patrice Boudet, Richard Kieckhefer, Julien Véronèse

These scholars provide the historical frame:

- Davies: the social and publishing history of grimoires;
- Boudet: Renaissance demonological “who’s who” catalogues and their medieval ancestors;
- Kieckhefer: the Munich handbook and clerical necromancy;
- Véronèse: medieval ritual magic, Solomonic literature, and spirit catalogues.

Boudet’s comparison establishes that the *Livre des Esperitz* and Weyer share a substantial catalogue family without requiring a simple direct copy. [Boudet’s article](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019)

## Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* represents twentieth-century operative reception. He describes dark-mirror methods and personal experiments with Goetic spirits.

**[Practitioner testimony]** Runyon relates an Asmoday working connected with statistics and another attempted visual manifestation. These are autobiographical magical claims, not independently verifiable historical evidence. [Runyon text](https://www.esis-corner888.com/_downloads/e767b40076acec7cf37baad61cb8cf35); [Runyon biography](https://www.pokerunyon.com/about)

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* popularized a psychological and practical reading of Crowley’s text. No uniquely documented historical Asmoday material was found there beyond the modern interpretive tradition. [DuQuette’s official site](https://www.lonmiloduquette.net/)

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No substantial Asmoday-specific operation, doctrine, or textual contribution by Frater Achad was found in the consulted catalogues. General association with Crowley or Qabalistic number theory is insufficient to establish an Asmoday connection.

# Literature, film, television, and games

## *El diablo cojuelo* and *Le Diable boiteux*

Luis Vélez de Guevara’s Spanish *El diablo cojuelo* appeared in 1641. Alain-René Lesage adapted it as *Le Diable boiteux* in 1707 and explicitly named the demon **Asmodée**.

**[Literary invention]** A student, Cléofas, releases Asmodée from a bottle. The lame demon carries him over Madrid and removes or renders roofs transparent, exposing private scandals and hypocrisies. This witty, voyeuristic social satirist is markedly different from Weyer’s dragon-riding king.

The work made “Asmodeus” proverbial as a revealer of secrets and inspired plays, operas, illustrations, and adaptations. [Vélez de Guevara text](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/velez_de_guevara/obra/el-diablo-cojuelo--0/); [1707 frontispiece and description](https://utpictura18.univ-amu.fr/notice/24042-frontispice-diable-boiteux-lesage-ed-1707-louise-magdeleine-horthemels); [Larousse entry](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/oeuvre/le_Diable_boiteux/116403)

## Dante and Milton

**[Negative finding]**

- Dante’s *Divine Comedy* does not name Asmodeus.
- Milton’s *Paradise Lost* does not name Asmodeus.

Their infernal settings influenced later demonological imagination, but direct claims that either poet included Asmoday are false unless referring to a later commentary, adaptation, or derivative work. [Dante text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1008/1008-h/1008-h.htm); [Milton text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Paradise_Lost)

## The Bartimaeus novels

**[Negative finding]** Jonathan Stroud’s Bartimaeus sequence uses Solomonic and magician-demon themes, including the Ring of Solomon, but no securely attested named Asmodeus/Asmoday character was found in the official series material consulted. The association appears to be an inference from the Solomonic setting. [Jonathan Stroud’s series page](https://www.jonathanstroud.com/about_bart.html)

## *Hellboy*

**[Modern invention]** In *The Bride of Hell*, an Asmodeus figure appears within the Hellboy universe. The story draws on the Solomon/Ashmedai and shamir traditions but adapts them freely for horror-comic narrative. [Hellboy reference](https://hellboy.fandom.com/wiki/Asmodeus)

## *Helluva Boss*

**[Modern invention]** *Helluva Boss* presents Asmodeus—“Ozzie”—as the embodiment of Lust and proprietor of the House of Asmodeus. His design uses a principal face with bull- and ram-like subsidiary heads, fire, feathers, and theatrical color.

The bull and ram side-heads recall Weyer and Le Breton; the role as Lust comes from Binsfeld and later demonology. The nightclub proprietor and romantic characterization are inventions of the series. [Official “House of Asmodeus” release](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pni6jn8efFs)

## *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona*

**[Modern invention]** Atlus’s demon franchises use **Asmodeus** or **Asmodai** in several forms:

- *Megami Tensei II* and *Kyuuyaku Megami Tensei*;
- versions of *Shin Megami Tensei*;
- *Shin Megami Tensei IV*;
- *Majin Tensei II*;
- *Devil Children* titles.

In *Persona 5*, Kamoshida’s distorted palace identity is **Suguru Asmodeus Kamoshida**. This is a symbolic vice-name for the boss, not necessarily a recruitable Persona corresponding exactly to the Goetic Asmoday. [Megami Tensei series reference](https://megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Asmodeus); [independent franchise wiki](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Asmodeus)

## *Dungeons & Dragons*

**[Modern invention]** Asmodeus appears in first-edition *Advanced Dungeons & Dragons* as a supreme ruler of the Nine Hells and develops into a major lawful-evil deity or archdevil in later editions.

This is not the Goetic office. D&D borrows the name and infernal kingship while constructing a new cosmology, genealogy, alignment, and political role. The first standard monster-book appearance is the 1977 *Monster Manual*, not the Goetic manuscript tradition. [D&D Asmodeus reference](https://dungeonsdragons.fandom.com/wiki/Asmodeus)

## Other modern uses

“Asmodeus” is now widespread in novels, manga, anime, role-playing games, metal music, occult art, and online demonolatry. The name alone does not establish use of the *Ars Goetia*. A work draws specifically on the Goetic Asmoday only when it uses such diagnostic features as:

- no. 32;
- bull/man/ram heads;
- dragon mount;
- lance and banner;
- Ring of Virtues;
- mathematics and mechanics;
- the Goetic seal;
- Vasariah.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is Asmoday first attested in Weyer?

No.

**[Documented finding]**

- *Asmodaios* appears in ancient Tobit.
- *Ashmedai* appears in rabbinic literature.
- *Asmhodæi* appears in Trithemius.
- *Asmoday* appears in the *Livre des Esperitz* and related spirit-office catalogues before Weyer’s 1577 printing.

Weyer is the first securely dated printed source for the complete standard three-headed description, not the first source for the name.

## 2. Iranian or Hebrew etymology?

**[Leading scholarly reconstruction]** Iranian Aēšma plus daēva.

**[Disputed alternative]** Hebrew *šāmad*, “destroy.”

The Iranian derivation better explains the demon’s older Iranian background and Middle Persian parallel, but the exact compound is reconstructed, not preserved intact in the Avesta.

## 3. Asmodeus, Ashmedai, and Asmoday: one spirit or several?

**[Documented source behavior]** Weyer, Scot, de Plancy, Mathers, Crowley, and Peterson treat the names as historically connected forms of the same demon.

**[Historical qualification]** Their literary personalities differ dramatically:

- Tobit: marriage-killing demon opposed by Raphael;
- Talmud: king of demons and Solomon’s rival;
- Weyer: dragon-riding king of mathematics, invisibility, and treasure;
- Binsfeld: demon of lust;
- Lesage: witty revealer of private scandals;
- modern occultism: solar king paired with Vasariah.

These are successive receptions, not proof of one stable biography.

**[Modern disputed claim]** Some demonolatry systems say Asmoday and Asmodeus are separate beings, aspects, father and son, or titles within a hierarchy. No support for that division was found in the primary Weyer–Scot–Lemegeton chain.

## 4. Sydonay and Sidon

No primary source explains Sydonay. Connecting it to Sidon, the Sidonians, or Astarte is conjectural. It may be a corrupt duplicate name, but even that is not proven.

## 5. Twelve or 72 legions?

- *Livre des Esperitz*: 12.
- Weyer onward: 72.

The increase may be scribal, numerological, or a deliberate adaptation to the 72-fold Solomonic/Shem framework. No surviving editorial memorandum explains it.

## 6. Invisible or invincible?

**Invisible** is the recoverable reading. **Invincible** arose in one manuscript and became famous through Mathers–Crowley.

Both are historically attested readings, but they do not possess equal textual authority.

## 7. “Abroad” and the cap

“Let him be abroad” and “take off the cap” originate in Scot’s faulty reading of Weyer. The later *Goetia* converted translation artifacts into ritual requirements.

A practitioner may still regard the received ritual rule as traditional, but it cannot be projected backward as Weyer’s original Latin instruction.

## 8. Is Asmoday intrinsically a lust demon?

Not in Weyer or the *Goetia*.

Peter Binsfeld’s 1589 demonological classification associated **Asmodeus with *luxuria***. Tobit and the *Testament of Solomon* made sexual and marital destruction central earlier, so Binsfeld did not invent the association from nothing. He did, however, formalize it within a seven-deadly-sins hierarchy. [Bibliographic record for Binsfeld’s 1589 *Tractatus*](https://cinquecentine.de/index.php/B_5528%3A_Binsfeld%2C_Peter%3B_Tractatus_de_confessionibus_maleficorum_et_sagarum)

## 9. Raphael or Vasariah?

- **Raphael:** ancient scriptural and *Testament of Solomon* opponent.
- **Vasariah:** early-eighteenth-century Rudd numerical constraining angel.

Treating Vasariah as the angel who expelled Asmodeus in Tobit is an anachronism.

## 10. Virgo or Aquarius?

Both are attested, but in different systems.

- Vasariah’s numbered Shem quinance: 5°–10° Virgo.
- Asmoday’s *777* day-decan: 10°–20° Aquarius.

Any chart that gives only one without naming its source obscures the distinction.

## 11. The “unlawful seat”

**[Negative finding]** No such wording occurs in Weyer’s Asmoday entry. Possible sources of confusion are:

- Ashmedai’s legendary seizure of Solomon’s throne;
- nearby Weyer passages condemning necromantic sacrifices;
- modern occult descriptions of Asmoday as a power of “unlawful revolt” or usurpation.

The last is a modern ideological interpretation, not a translation of Weyer.

## 12. Is the seal ancient?

No evidence supports an ancient, biblical, Talmudic, or Weyer-period Asmoday seal. It is securely attested in the seventeenth-century Lemegeton manuscript tradition.

## 13. Was the Goetia designed from the Shem angels?

The numerical fit and Rudd’s subsequent pairing make the idea attractive, but the history has stages:

1. Weyer prints 69 spirits.
2. The *Goetia* reaches 72 by omission and addition.
3. Rudd explicitly pairs the resulting 72 spirits with 72 angels.

It is possible that the anonymous *Goetia* compiler already sought a 72-fold total, but the surviving text does not document a complete angel-demon pairing before Rudd.

# Open questions

1. **The precise date of the *Livre des Esperitz*.**  
   The surviving manuscript and underlying catalogue may belong to different moments. A complete stemma of its exemplars remains unresolved.

2. **The origin of Sydonay.**  
   No secure antecedent or etymology has been demonstrated.

3. **The immediate source of Weyer’s expanded physical description.**  
   Weyer named a *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*, but the exact exemplar is lost. Folger, Trinity, and French witnesses preserve related rather than identical texts.

4. **Why the legions changed from twelve to 72.**  
   Numerology is plausible but undocumented.

5. **Who first drew the Asmoday seal.**  
   Surviving Lemegeton manuscripts preserve it, but their lost prototype may have been earlier.

6. **How much of Harley 6483 is Thomas Rudd’s work.**  
   Peter Smart’s attribution is meaningful evidence, but not proof of autograph authorship.

7. **Whether the Goetia’s expansion to 72 anticipated angelic pairing.**  
   Rudd supplies the explicit pairing; the anonymous compiler’s intention is unknown.

8. **How the modern guardian-date systems were calculated.**  
   Lenain’s five annual days and Haziel’s consecutive birth dates use different calendars or conversion rules.

9. **Whether “day/night” should be applied to the Virgo quinances.**  
   It is explicit in *777*’s demon-decan columns but not equally explicit in Lenain’s angelic rays.

10. **Modern Asmoday/Asmodeus separation.**  
    No early textual basis has been found for treating them as ontologically separate spirits, but modern practitioners may maintain locally coherent systems that do so.

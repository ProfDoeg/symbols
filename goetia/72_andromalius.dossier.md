# Andromalius: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **Documented text** — what a manuscript, printed grimoire, archival record, or bibliographic record actually says.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a historical or philological inference supported by comparison, but not explicitly stated in the source.
- **Tradition** — a claim transmitted within ceremonial-magic literature.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — an occult attribution added after the core Goetia text.
- **Disputed claim** — a proposal for which the evidence remains inconclusive.
- **Legend** — supernatural narrative accepted within the text’s religious or magical world, but not historical proof.
- **Modern invention** — recent interpretation, artwork, ritual practice, or popular-culture adaptation unsupported by early sources.

Because Andromalius is absent from both Weyer and Scot, there is no Andromalius passage—Latin or English—to reproduce from either author. Longer online texts cannot be quoted here in their entirety; short exact readings are supplied alongside links to the complete public-domain texts.

# The name and its origins

## 1. The exact form *Andromalius*

**Documented text.** The exact Latinized form *Andromalius* belongs securely to the manuscript tradition of the *Lemegeton* or *Lesser Key of Solomon*. It is found in the principal seventeenth-century Goetia manuscripts represented by Sloane 2731 and 3825 and in Harley 6483.

It is not found in Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* or Reginald Scot’s English rendering of Weyer. Consequently, claims that Weyer described Andromalius in 1577 are false.

The first printed edition to make the exact form widely accessible was S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley’s *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* in 1904. Joseph Peterson’s 2001 critical edition retained the form while restoring manuscript readings and documenting cognate forms.

## 2. *Andromalcus* and related manuscript names

The exact form may be late, but the apparent spirit behind it is considerably older.

### Sloane MS 3849

**Documented text.** Armand Delatte reported an *experimentum* in British Library Sloane MS 3849, fols. 17v–18r, a manuscript he dated broadly to the fifteenth–sixteenth century. It calls a spirit named **Andromalcus** into a crystal, beryl, or glass mirror.

The operation seeks information about:

- a thief;
- the condition of an absent friend;
- the location of treasure;
- virtually any worldly question.

A legitimate twelve-year-old boy acts as seer. The optical medium is anointed with blessed oil. Andromalcus appears clothed in red or white and is dismissed with a license to depart, the sign of the cross, and the Gospel of John. [Delatte, *La catoptromancie grecque*, p. 105](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1932__delatte___catoptromancie_grecque.pdf)

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The rare name, combined with the thief-and-treasure office, makes Andromalcus a strong antecedent of Andromalius. It is not absolute proof: neither manuscript declares that the two spellings represent one being.

### Tudor Norfolk: Andrew or Andrea Malchus

**Documented allegation.** A Tudor investigation concerning William Stapleton, parson of Lessingham in Norfolk, describes treasure-seeking operations involving spirits including Oberion and a name transmitted as **Andrew Malchus** or **Andrea Malchiis**. The alleged activities belong to the late 1520s, during Henry VIII’s reign.

This is evidence that a similar spirit-name circulated before 1577. It is evidence for a magical accusation and practice, not evidence that the spirit objectively appeared. The account was printed by Thomas Wright from Tudor papers in *Narratives of Sorcery and Magic*. [Wright, *Narratives of Sorcery and Magic*](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1852__wright___narratives_of_sorcery_and_magic.pdf)

### Other manuscript forms

Peterson and subsequent textual work identify:

- **Andromalcus**
- **Andromalchus**
- **Andromalcum**
- **Andromalce**
- **Andrewemalcus**
- **Andrew Malchus**
- **Andrea Malchus**

Relevant witnesses include:

- BL Additional MS 36674, especially fol. 89v;
- Sloane MS 3846/3849;
- Wellcome MS 110;
- Bodleian Rawlinson D. 252;
- the “Abraham’s Eye” thief-detection operation.

The *Abraham’s Eye* charm addresses Andromalcus while symbolically injuring an eye to make a thief suffer or become detectable. This is an independent theft-magic context, not merely a copy of the finished Goetia description. [Peterson’s Goetia notes](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

## 3. Is the name derived from Andromache?

**Scholarly reconstruction.** A recent French critical study of the Rawlinson manuscript calls *Andromalcus* a masculinized form of the Trojan heroine **Andromache**. [French thesis, p. 187](https://theses.univ-orleans.fr/public/2022ORLE1070_va.pdf)

Greek **Ἀνδρομάχη**, *Andromachē*, contains:

- *andr-/andros*, from ἀνήρ, “man”;
- *machē*, μάχη, “battle.”

The corresponding masculine personal name **Ἀνδρόμαχος**, *Andromachos*, means approximately “fighting men,” “man-fighter,” or “battle of men.” Andromache was Hector’s wife in the Trojan epic tradition, not an ancient demon or deity.

The difficulty is the second element. *Andromalcus* has **-malc-**, not Greek **-mach-**. A sequence of scribal corruption—*Andromachus → Andromalchus → Andromalcus → Andromalius*—is possible, especially in poorly copied ritual texts, but has not been proven by a surviving continuous chain.

## 4. Malchus and the Semitic alternative

**Disputed claim.** The separation **Andrew/Andrea Malchus** may reflect a copyist’s attempt to turn an unfamiliar *Andromalcus* into two familiar personal names.

*Malchus* is a Greek and Latin form of a Semitic name based on the root *mlk*, “king.” It is familiar from the New Testament servant of the high priest. This allows two interpretations:

1. *Andrew Malchus* is a scribal rationalization of *Andromalcus*.
2. An actual compound or two-part name was later compressed into *Andromalcus*.

There is not enough evidence to decide between them.

## 5. Unsupported popular etymologies

**Modern invention or disputed folklore.** Internet discussions commonly divide the name into Greek *andro-* plus Latin *malus*, producing “evil man,” “man against evil,” or “punisher of man’s wickedness.” This is semantically attractive because of the spirit’s office, but linguistically ad hoc: it mixes languages and does not explain the older *-malcus* forms.

Another modern proposal identifies Andromalius with **Adrammelech**, the deity named in 2 Kings and later demonologies. No manuscript genealogy connects them. Resemblance of spelling is insufficient.

There is therefore no secure ancient god, cult, hero, or place “behind” Andromalius. The Andromache proposal is the best-supported classical-name hypothesis, not proof of religious continuity.

# Chronological source history

## Fifteenth–sixteenth century: crystal and thief magic

**Documented text.** Sloane 3849’s Andromalcus operation is the earliest currently located manuscript witness of the probable name-family. Its broad palaeographical dating prevents a precise year.

**Documented allegation.** The Norfolk proceedings place Andrew/Andrea Malchus in treasure magic around 1528.

**Finding.** Thus:

- the exact spelling **Andromalius** has no securely identified pre-1577 witness;
- a probable cognate spirit with the same characteristic offices does have pre-1577 evidence.

## 1577: Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*

Johann Weyer appended the *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. It contains 69 catalogue entries.

**Documented absence.** Andromalius, Andromalcus, and Andrew Malchus do not occur. There is consequently no Latin description to quote.

Weyer’s final catalogue spirit is Decarabia. His list also contains **Pruflas/Busas**, absent from the later Goetia. [Weyer’s complete Latin text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

Weyer presented the catalogue in a work arguing that many accused witches were deluded or ill rather than genuine possessors of supernatural power. The *Pseudomonarchia* should therefore not simply be described as Weyer's personal invocation manual.

## 1584: Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft*

Reginald Scot translated most of Weyer’s catalogue into English in Book XVI of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*.

**Documented absence.** Scot has no Andromalius entry, so again there is no English description to quote. Scot also omitted Weyer’s Pruflas, leaving 68 translated catalogue spirits. [Scot, Book XVI](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm)

## The arithmetic: 69, 68, and 72

The relationship is:

- Weyer: **69**
- Scot: **68**, because Pruflas is omitted
- Goetia: Scot’s effective 68, plus **Vassago, Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius**
- Total: **72**

The Goetia does not merely append all four. Vassago is inserted at number 3; Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius close the list as 70–72.

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The Goetia compiler probably worked primarily from Scot rather than directly from Weyer. Peterson points to English wording, unique errors, and spellings shared with Scot. Some changes in order may reflect displaced leaves or the handling of a manuscript exemplar, but no complete reconstruction is certain. [Peterson’s introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

**Disputed claim.** It is tempting to say that the four spirits were invented solely to reach the kabbalistically important number 72. The numerical motive is plausible, but the independent Andromalcus material demonstrates that at least Andromalius was not necessarily created from nothing.

## Seventeenth-century Goetia manuscripts

### Sloane MS 3825

**Documented text.** Sloane 3825 calls him the seventy-second spirit, “a great and Mighty Earle,” appearing as a man holding a serpent. It attributes all the familiar theft, punishment, and treasure offices and gives 36 legions.

The manuscript’s phrase appears as **“understand dealings”**, almost certainly a scribal error. Other manuscript witnesses support *underhand*, *secret*, or clandestine dealing. [Peterson’s transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Sloane MS 2731

**Documented text.** The name is **Andromalius**. Its placement, rank, legions, human-serpent appearance, offices, and lamen instruction agree substantially with Sloane 3825. Its seal belongs to the familiar elaborate Goetia family rather than to Weyer, who supplied no individual seals.

### Sloane MS 3824

This related “proto-Lemegeton” witness supplies a more expansive description. Besides thieves and stolen property, it mentions:

- secret or clandestine dealings;
- plots and designs;
- combinations or conspiracies;
- consultations and treachery;
- deceitful people;
- treasure trove.

**Textual significance.** This fuller wording helps explain why “understand dealings” in Sloane 3825 should be read as a corruption of *underhand dealings*.

## Harley MS 6483 and “Dr Rudd”

Harley 6483 was copied around 1712–1713, although its material is conventionally associated with Thomas Rudd, who died in 1656.

**Documented text.** It gives **Andromalius** as spirit 72 and pairs him by position with **Mumiah**, angel 72.

**Disputed attribution.** “The Goetia of Dr Rudd” is a useful name for the recension, not proof that every part of Harley 6483 was personally written or compiled by Thomas Rudd. The surviving codex is later, and “Dr Rudd” may indicate an attributed source, earlier exemplar, ownership tradition, or editorial authority.

## 1904: Mathers and Crowley

In the Mathers-Crowley edition, p. 47 in commonly circulated facsimiles, the defining exact phrase is:

> “an Earl, Great and Mighty, appearing in the form of a Man holding a Great Serpent in his Hand.”

The remainder assigns recovery of thief and property, discovery and punishment of wickedness, discovery of hidden treasure, 36 legions, and use of the seal as previously directed. [Complete 1904 text and facsimile](https://pauladaunt.com/books/Aleister%20Crowley_goetia.pdf)

Differences from the manuscripts are small but influential:

- “serpent” becomes **“Great Serpent”**;
- capitalization gives the entry a heraldic tone;
- “underhand dealing” resolves the Sloane 3825 error;
- the text says only **Earl**, not Duke;
- no second transformation is requested.

Arthur Edward Waite later summarized Andromalius as a “great duke and earl.” The double rank is Waite’s presentation, not the principal Goetia reading. [Waite’s description](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-book-of-ceremonial-magic/shell/demons-lx-vapula-to-lxxii-andromalius)

## 2001: Peterson’s critical edition

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon* appeared from Weiser in 2001. Its Andromalius entry is on print p. 39. It uses Sloane 3825 as its principal witness and collates other manuscripts. [Publisher record](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/)

Peterson’s later emendments add the decisive observation that Andromalcum/Andromalce/Andrewemalcus appears independently in crystal, theft, punishment, and treasure operations. [Peterson’s corrections and additions](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lkey_cor.htm)

## 2007: Skinner and Rankine

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine published *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* in 2007. Their edition makes the Harley 6483 angel-demon arrangement available as a complete operative system.

The Andromalius entry appears on p. 173 of the first edition, alongside Mumiah’s name and Psalm 114:7. The editors characterize the Shem angel as the demon’s **“thwarting angel”**: an angelic authority used to bind or control the corresponding Goetic spirit. [Publisher catalogue](https://www.sskinner.com/pdf/GHP-Catalogue-2022-2023.pdf)

## Rank

**Documented text.** Andromalius is an Earl, equivalent to Latin *comes* and English Count.

**Later variation.** Waite calls him a “great duke and earl,” apparently through editorial conflation or expansion. Idries Shah repeats the double rank. It should not replace the primary reading.

## Appearance and second form

**Documented text.** The earliest Goetia wording is simply a man holding a serpent. Mathers’s “great serpent” is a slight amplification.

Unlike Agares, Marbas, Amon, or many other spirits, Andromalius:

- does not arrive first as an animal;
- is not ordered to assume human shape;
- has no alternate monstrous form;
- is not assigned wings, crown, weapon, mount, color, or voice.

Modern pictures adding horns, wings, armor, a dragon body, or a courtly costume are artistic inventions.

## Offices

The office divides naturally into four related functions:

1. **Restitution:** bringing back the thief and the stolen goods.
2. **Detection:** revealing wickedness and concealed dealings.
3. **Punishment:** afflicting thieves and other wicked people.
4. **Discovery:** locating hidden treasure.

The older Andromalcus material adds general divination, but the finished Goetia narrows his identity around theft, conspiracy, punishment, and treasure.

There is no early attribution of:

- languages;
- liberal sciences;
- love;
- familiars;
- invisibility;
- transmutation;
- military victory;
- necromancy;
- weather;
- healing.

Those offices belong to other spirits. Modern claims that Andromalius teaches psychology, governs karmic justice, protects police, or specializes in cybercrime are analogical adaptations.

## Legions

**Documented text.** Thirty-six legions is stable across the principal Goetia witnesses, Mathers-Crowley, Peterson, and the Rudd recension.

No historical source defines how many spirits constitute a Goetic “legion.” Attempts to convert the figure into a Roman military headcount are speculative.

## Time of conjuration

The Goetia’s general rules state that:

- kings have specified late-morning and afternoon hours;
- marquises belong chiefly to afternoon and night;
- dukes to clear weather between sunrise and noon;
- presidents avoid twilight unless their king is invoked;
- **earls may be bound at any hour of daylight**, in woods or other places without people or noise.

The work also recommends a waxing Moon of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, or 14 days old. [Goetia “Observations”](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

**Important distinction.** “Any hour of day” is the Goetia’s rank rule. “By night” is Crowley’s later decan correspondence. The two belong to separate systems and are not harmonized in the original text.

# The seal

## Standard form

The common Sloane 3825/Mathers-Crowley seal is an open linear figure, not originally a circular heraldic badge. Its principal elements are:

- a strong central upright;
- several roughly horizontal or slightly curved crossbars;
- circular terminals at a number of the bar ends;
- a central looped, curled, or S-like stroke;
- a descending lower stroke whose terminal is rendered as a dot, hook, or compact flourish in different tracings.

The seal may be viewed in [Peterson’s Sloane 3825 reproduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g72.jpg) and the [1904 public-domain tracing](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:72-Andromalius_seal.png).

## Manuscript variation

**Documented text.** Peterson reproduces a substantially different character from Sloane 3824. [Sloane 3824 seal](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/Sloane-3824_fol_116r_andromalius_seal.jpg)

Differences among witnesses include:

- bar length and curvature;
- whether terminal circles are closed;
- the direction of the central curl;
- whether a terminal is a circle, dot, or hook;
- the balance between the upright axis and the transverse lines;
- simplification or elaboration of the whole figure.

Mathers himself warned that the “Dr Rudd” manuscript contained errors, particularly in sigils. That warning means the printed seal should not be treated as an immutable ancient ideogram.

**Disputed claim.** One modern textual conjecture proposes that *Andromalius* arose when dirt, an ink blot, or a damaged *c* in *Andromalcus* was read as an *i*. It is ingenious but currently unproven. [Celephaïs Press discussion](https://celephaispress.blogspot.com/2021/05/meddling-with-goetia-again-5_15.html)

## Metal

The Goetia’s explicit classified list states that earls’ seals are made from **copper and silver “alike equal.”** This corresponds to Venus and the Moon.

Mathers then speculates that earls’ seals “probably” should be iron because otherwise Mars is missing from the seven-metal arrangement. That is an editorial correction, not the manuscript’s instruction.

Therefore:

- **Primary text:** equal copper and silver.
- **Mathers’s conjecture:** iron.
- **Golden Dawn/777 rank planet:** Mars.
- **Modern tables:** usually Mars and iron, often without noting the conflict.

## The seventy-twofold name

**Documented Jewish kabbalistic technique.** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three Hebrew verses traditionally counted as 72 letters each. The verses are arranged:

1. first verse in normal order;
2. second in reverse order;
3. third in normal order.

Reading the resulting columns produces 72 triads of three letters. The *Bahir* explains this construction; Rashi also comments on the 72-letter name. [Sefaria, Exodus 14:19–21 and kabbalistic commentaries](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.19-21?with=Kabbalah) [*Bahir* §§109–110](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-109-110-targum/)

**Historical qualification.** *Shem ha-mephorash* literally means the explicit or distinctly articulated Name. In early rabbinic sources it generally denotes the Tetragrammaton, not automatically the later list of 72 guardian angels. Jewish sources also discuss twelve-, forty-two-, and seventy-two-letter names. [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13542-shem-ha-meforash)

The identification of each triplet as an individual angel pronounced with *-el* or *-iah* belongs especially to medieval and Renaissance angelological development.

## From the *Bahir* and *Sefer Raziel* to Christian Cabala

### *Sefer ha-Bahir*

**Documented tradition.** The *Bahir*, normally dated to twelfth-century Provence in its extant form, gives an early explanation of the 72-fold construction. It does not contain the complete modern Lenain guardian-angel system with dates, virtues, contrary genii, and Goetic opponents.

### *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*

**Tradition and textual history.** *Sefer Raziel* preserves extensive divine-name and angel magic and is an important transmitter of the seventy-twofold name. The legendary frame says the angel Raziel delivered secret wisdom to Adam. That is the book’s sacred legend, not its historical date.

The work is composite and medieval; its first printed edition appeared much later. The modern Mumiah profile cannot simply be attributed wholesale to “the *Sefer Raziel*.” Joshua Trachtenberg describes the extraction method and the role of *Sefer Raziel* in Jewish magical literature. [Trachtenberg, *Jewish Magic and Superstition*](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm)

### Reuchlin and Renaissance Christian Cabala

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped Christianize and systematize the 72 names as angels by adding divine endings such as *-el* and *-yah*. This is a major bridge between Jewish name mysticism and early modern Christian ceremonial angelology.

### Athanasius Kircher

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) reproduced elaborate cabalistic diagrams and the 72 angelic letters/names. Kircher was hostile to what he regarded as corrupt or demonic magic even while documenting it. [Kircher’s discussion of the 72 letters](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm)

Kircher contributed greatly to the visual transmission of the 72-name scheme, but the complete Lenain-style Mumiah notice should not be projected backward into Kircher without checking individual tables.

## Mumiah’s name and “meaning”

The root is the mechanically extracted triad **MVM**. Adding *-iah* produces the conventional angel-name.

**Disputed linguistic issue.** The standalone Hebrew sequence מום can mean “blemish” or “defect.” That dictionary meaning is not a good translation of the angel. The triads are extracted divine-name units, not necessarily ordinary Hebrew words.

**Documented esoteric meaning.** Lenain says Mumiah’s attribute is represented by **Omega**, “which designates the end of all things.” Hence later books give:

- “God, the end of all things”;
- “End of all things”;
- “God who brings everything to completion.”

These are theological glosses on Lenain’s symbolism, not literal philological translations of MVM.

## Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* provides the mature French profile.

**Documented text.** Mumiah:

- is number 72;
- has an attribute figured by Omega;
- rules Thrace or Roumelia;
- extends from degree 356 to the final degree 360;
- corresponds to the last zodiacal division and the secondary angel Atembui;
- is invoked from 11:40 p.m. to midnight;
- uses the divine names Alpha and Omega;
- uses Psalm 114:7;
- protects mysterious operations;
- brings undertakings to completion;
- governs chemistry, physics, and medicine;
- favors health and longevity;
- has a contrary genius associated with despair and suicide.

Lenain’s French begins, in part, *“Son attribut est figuré par l’oméga, qui désigne la fin de toutes choses.”* [Lenain scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365)

### Psalm

Lenain and the Rudd recension give:

> *Convertere anima mea in requiem tuam: quia Dominus benefecit tibi.*

Meaning: return to your rest, my soul, because the Lord has dealt bountifully with you.

This is **Psalm 114:7** in the Vulgate division and **Psalm 116:7** in most modern Protestant editions.

### The two date systems

Lenain supplies two systems that modern websites frequently conflate:

1. **Continuous zodiacal ray:** 356°–360°, equivalent to 25°–30° Pisces.
2. **Five distributed “days”:** 30 May, 10 August, 21 October, 1 January, and 14 March in the printed notice.

The now-popular continuous birthday period **16–20 March** is a later zodiac-calendar conversion. It is not identical to Lenain’s five distributed days.

## Rudd’s use of Mumiah

**Documented tradition.** Harley 6483 places the 72 angels against the 72 Goetic spirits by number. Mumiah therefore opposes or constrains Andromalius.

The operative idea is hierarchical:

1. divine name;
2. Shem angel;
3. Goetic spirit.

The magician invokes the superior angelic authority when commanding the inferior spirit. The angel’s name, seal or lamen, and psalm function as safeguards and warrants of authority.

**Interpretive caution.** Mumiah’s Lenain offices do not exactly reverse Andromalius’s offices. The pair is primarily numerical and hierarchical, not a simple moral opposition such as “thief versus policeman.”

## Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced the seventy-two-genius scheme in *La Cabbale* (1903), explicitly crediting Lenain. His Mumiah notice preserves degrees 356–360, Psalm 114:7, completion, chemistry, physics, medicine, health, longevity, despair, and suicide. [Papus, *La Cabbale*, pp. 194–213 in this edition](https://theosophy.world/sites/default/files/ebooks/Spanish/Papus/Papus%20-%20La%20Cabala.pdf)

Papus is a transmitter and popularizer here, not an independent ancient source.

## Robert Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* first appeared in 1951. Its Mumiah account follows Lenain closely. [Ambelain text](https://djvu.online/file/O6LjS6ZXfyHrE)

The 1989–1990 reprint adds Ambelain’s warning that certain commonly printed “angelic seals” are actually seals of contrary beings and recounts alleged harmful experiments from 1955–1960. This is **Ambelain’s esoteric testimony**, not historical or medical evidence.

## Haziel and Kabaleb

The popular “guardian angel by birthday” system was spread in French by Kabaleb and his translator/adaptor Haziel. The Bibliothèque nationale de France records *Les Anges: le livre des génies et des planètes, astrologie et haute Cabale*, Kabaleb, French version by Haziel, Paris, Bussière, 1989. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35062108t)

In this modern popular system Mumiah:

- governs people born approximately March 16–20;
- belongs to the choir of Angels under Gabriel;
- signifies completion, transition, healing, longevity, and renewal.

“Rebirth,” bereavement counseling, emerald colors, aquamarine, and personality profiles are late elaborations, not Rudd or Lenain.

## The decan and tarot pip

Crowley’s *777*, columns CLV–CLXVI, assigns the Goetic spirits to the 36 decans twice: once by day and once by night. The final night position, the cadent decan of Pisces, carries the image “man holding great serpent,” identifying Andromalius.

Thus:

- **20°–30° Pisces by day:** Stolas, spirit 36.
- **20°–30° Pisces by night:** Andromalius, spirit 72.
- **Tarot pip:** Ten of Cups.
- **Astrological ruler of the pip:** Mars in Pisces.

[Original 1909 *777* scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1909__crowley___777.pdf)  
[Golden Dawn *Book T*](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/mathers-and-felkin-golden-dawn-book-t-the-tarot-1888.pdf)

This explains the statement that each pip card carries two spirits: one from numbers 1–36 and one from 37–72.

Mumiah occupies only the second five-degree half of that ten-degree decan:

- 20°–25° Pisces: Haiaiel/Dantalion layer.
- 25°–30° Pisces: Mumiah/Andromalius layer.

The exact pairing of each five-degree angel with a ten-degree day/night Goetic spirit is a synthesis of different tables, not a single ancient system.

## Poke Runyon

Poke Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* prints Andromalius with:

- Pisces 25°–29°;
- March 16–20;
- Pisces decan ruled by Mars;
- Earl = Mars;
- ruler Corson;
- Shem angel Mumiah;
- archangel Gabriel.

[Runyon, p. 180](https://attfinnasanningen.se/dokument_offentligt/books/mystery_school/magic/poke%20runyon%20book%20of%20solomons%20magick.pdf)

This is a useful example of the twentieth-century integrated system. It should not be mistaken for Sloane 3825’s original contents.

## Enochian overlays

Neither the core Goetia nor Harley 6483 assigns Andromalius an Enochian name, tablet square, Call, or Aethyr.

The Golden Dawn used Goetic, astrological, tarot, and Enochian systems within one broad curriculum, which encouraged later authors to combine them. But John Dee and Edward Kelley’s angelic records contain no Andromalius. A specific Enochian-Andromalius identity is therefore a modern synthetic construction.

# Iconography

## The textual image

The complete early iconographic datum is minimal: **a man holding a serpent**.

Possible symbolic readings—exposure of hidden evil, mastery of deception, a thief caught like a snake, wisdom, venom, or judicial authority—are later interpretation. The source supplies no explanation.

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Breton

Collin de Plancy first published the *Dictionnaire infernal* in 1818. The sixth edition, Paris 1863, was illustrated by Louis Breton and contains many famous demon portraits. [1863 Wikisource facsimile](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Jacques_Collin_de_Plancy_-_Dictionnaire_infernal.pdf)

**Documented absence.**

- No Andromalius article was located in the 1818 edition.
- No Andromalius article occurs in the searchable 1863 text.
- No Breton engraving is captioned Andromalius.
- Therefore there is no authentic de Plancy description or Breton portrait to quote.

This absence is unsurprising: Andromalius was not in Weyer, a major source for de Plancy’s demon catalogue, and the Goetia had not yet been printed.

Several recent websites use a generic or AI-generated demon picture while claiming that their images come from Breton. That general attribution does not convert the image into a lost Andromalius engraving.

## Modern art

**Modern invention.** Recent art tends to choose among four approaches:

1. a human occult judge holding a python;
2. a horned or winged earl in aristocratic costume;
3. a humanoid fused with a serpent;
4. an abstract personification of investigation, restitution, or punishment.

Camelia Elias’s *Andromalius* and *Andromalius, Take Two* use the spirit and seal in contemporary divination and book-art projects. [EyeCorner Press](https://www.eyecorner.press/special-editions/p/andromalius)

The 2024 strategy game *Solium Infernum* reinvents Andromalius as a beautiful, socially manipulative archfiend. These images are creative receptions, not historical portraits.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer did not include Andromalius. His importance is structural: most of the catalogue inherited by the Goetia comes through his *Pseudomonarchia*. Any statement “according to Weyer, Andromalius…” is erroneous.

## Reginald Scot

Scot likewise did not include him. His importance is that the Goetia compiler apparently used his English translation as a major source.

## William Stapleton and the Norfolk circle

The clerical treasure-hunting allegation involving Andrew/Andrea Malchus supplies the strongest dated pre-Goetia historical context. It places the name-family among Tudor clerical necromancy, crystal work, and treasure lore.

## Thomas Rudd

Rudd’s name is attached to the angelically constrained recension. The system’s historical value is clear even though personal authorship of Harley 6483 remains uncertain.

## Mathers

S. L. MacGregor Mathers edited and translated the text printed in 1904. He standardized “Great Serpent,” supplied occult commentary, associated rank with planetary metals, and proposed iron for earls.

## Aleister Crowley

Crowley published Mathers’s work, added the Preliminary Invocation and editorial apparatus, and later assigned the spirits to decans in *777*. Evidence that Crowley printed Andromalius is abundant; evidence that he conducted a specific Andromalius working has not been found.

## Frater Achad

No reliable item-specific evidence was located connecting Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, with an Andromalius operation or doctrine. General association with Crowley and Qabalah is not enough.

## Arthur Edward Waite

Waite summarized the spirit as a “great duke and earl” in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic*. His account omits punishment of thieves and the legion total. It is secondary and editorially moralizing.

## Poke Runyon

Runyon incorporated Andromalius into a modern, integrated Solomonic temple system, with Mars, Pisces, Corson, Mumiah, and Gabriel.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette helped popularize the Goetia through *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* with Christopher S. Hyatt and David P. Wilson. The Andromalius seal appears on the paperback cover of DuQuette’s autobiography *My Life with the Spirits*—an apparent visual joke about preventing theft. [Celephaïs annotated edition, note 72](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/publication/english/Goetia.pdf)

Practitioner accounts attribute an Andromalius working to DuQuette, but autobiographical magical experience should be classified as personal testimony, not independently confirmed history.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s principal contribution is textual:

- transcription from Sloane 3825;
- collation of Sloane 2731, 3824, Harley 6483, and other witnesses;
- demonstration of the Weyer/Scot absence;
- documentation of the Andromalcus theft-magic family;
- reproduction of variant seals.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 edition brought the Rudd angel-demon pairing, psalms, lamens, and ritual hierarchy into modern scholarly and practitioner discussion.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent wrote extensively about the spirit-catalogue tradition, intermediary spirits, and the relationship between Goetic magic and earlier magic. No distinctive, source-based Andromalius thesis was located. He should be cited for the wider current, not automatically for an Andromalius etymology.

## Scholars of the source tradition

- Jean-Patrice Boudet analyzed the demonological catalogues connecting the *Livre des Esperitz*, Weyer, and the Goetia family. [Boudet, “Les who’s who démonologiques”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171)
- Julien Véronèse’s work helps contextualize medieval ritual magic and French Solomonic literature, but does not furnish a known Andromalius entry.
- Owen Davies supplies the wider history of grimoires, printing, and magical-book circulation. [Davies, *Grimoires*](https://files.secure.website/wscfus/10533201/10380031/grimoires-a-history-of-magic-books-by-davie-owens.pdf)
- Jan Veenstra’s work on Solomonic and angelic material is valuable context but does not demonstrate an Andromalius ancestor.

# Grimoire ancestors

## *Livre des Esperitz*

The *Livre des Esperitz* contains roughly 46–47 spirits, many ancestral to Weyer’s catalogue. No Andromalius or Andromalcus has been identified in its known list.

**Finding.** It is an ancestor of the catalogue form, not a direct witness for this spirit.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer names a *Liber officiorum spirituum* or related “Empto. Salomonis” as his source. Surviving Office-of-Spirits manuscripts preserve overlapping catalogues and practical operations.

Andromalius is absent from Weyer’s particular recension, but independent Andromalcus theft charms may belong to this broader English Office-of-Spirits environment.

## Munich Handbook, CLM 849

The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook contains ritual operations and spirit names important for the prehistory of learned necromancy. No Andromalius or secure cognate occurs.

## *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin’s demon lists share some names or name-types with later catalogues, but no secure Andromalius/Andromalcus has been demonstrated.

## *Testament of Solomon*

The ancient or late-antique *Testament of Solomon* presents Solomon interrogating and binding demons. It provides the overarching Solomonic narrative but not Andromalius’s name, seal, serpent appearance, or offices.

Claims that Andromalius was one of the demons individually named in the *Testament* are modern conflations.

## The brass vessel

The Goetia says Solomon confined the 72 chief spirits and their legions in a brass vessel, which Babylonians later broke open while searching for treasure.

**Legend.** This belongs to the Goetia’s mythic charter. It resembles jinn-in-vessel narratives and establishes Solomon’s authority but is not historical evidence for a unified ancient list of 72 demons.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. “Andromalius first appears in Weyer”

**False.** Weyer has no such entry. Scot has none either.

The error arises when websites describe the Goetia catalogue as if every member came directly from Weyer. Some modern PDFs even add Andromalius to a text labeled *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, producing a hybrid rather than Weyer’s book.

## 2. “There is no pre-Goetia antecedent”

**Too strong.** The exact form *Andromalius* is not securely pre-Weyer, but Andromalcus and Andrew/Andrea Malchus are earlier and share the distinctive theft-and-treasure function.

The prudent conclusion is:

> Exact spelling: late. Probable spirit-family: early sixteenth century or possibly earlier.

## 3. Was Andromalius invented to make 72?

**Disputed.** The finished name and placement may have been selected while expanding a 68-name Scot-derived catalogue to 72. But the Andromalcus operations show that the compiler could have borrowed an existing spirit rather than inventing one.

## 4. Is Andromalius Andromache?

**Plausible scholarly reconstruction, not proven.** The French critical thesis supports it. The consonantal change remains unexplained by a surviving genealogy.

## 5. Is he Adrammelech?

**Unsupported.** Adrammelech has an ancient Near Eastern and biblical history; Andromalius has an early-modern European manuscript history. No primary source equates them.

## 6. Is he Andrealphus?

**No.** Andrealphus is a separate marquis, present in Weyer as Androalphus and numbered 65 in the Goetia. The resemblance is nominal only.

## 7. Is the correct name Andrew Malchus?

**Open.** It may be an original two-part name or a scribal normalization of Andromalcus. No autograph or explanatory gloss resolves the issue.

## 8. Is the primary planetary metal iron?

**No.** Iron is Mathers’s proposed correction and the natural metal once Golden Dawn tables identify earls with Mars. The manuscript rule specifies equal copper and silver.

## 9. Is Andromalius a duke?

**Usually no.** The main Goetia manuscripts and Mathers call him an Earl. Waite and some later summaries call him both Duke and Earl.

## 10. Does he answer to Corson, Amaymon, or another king?

**Disputed overlay.** The individual entry gives no directional king. Modern tables assign Corson or another ruler according to their own schemes. The Goetia itself gives no obvious complete pattern for allocating all 72 spirits among the four kings.

## 11. Did de Plancy describe or illustrate him?

**No evidence.** The 1818 and 1863 editions contain no Andromalius article, and Louis Breton made no identified Andromalius engraving.

## 12. Are modern “enns” ancient?

Modern demonolatry circulates formulas such as *Tasa fubin Andromalius on ca* and *Andromalius Croton Scerimos*. These do not occur in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, Harley 6483, Mathers-Crowley, or Lenain.

Stories attributing the enns to a private sixteenth-century family grimoire have not been supported by a publicly examinable early manuscript. They should be labeled modern esoteric tradition.

## 13. Is Andromalius a fallen angel?

The early entry calls him a spirit and Earl; it does not narrate a fall from heaven. “Fallen angel” is a later theological classification applied generally to demons.

## 14. Does the serpent symbolize justice or truth?

That reading is modern interpretation. The manuscript offers no symbolism. The serpent could equally signify danger, concealment, wisdom, treachery, or merely serve as an identifying attribute.

# Open questions

1. **Which surviving manuscript has the earliest physical inscription of Andromalcus?**  
   Sloane 3849 has a broad fifteenth–sixteenth-century date; more precise palaeographical work may change the chronology.

2. **Can the Tudor “Andrea Malchiis” reading be checked directly against the original State Paper?**  
   Nineteenth-century editions may normalize or misread the name.

3. **Does a demonstrable copying chain connect Andrew Malchus, Andromalcus, and Andromalius?**  
   Shared offices make the relationship likely, but intermediate witnesses are needed.

4. **Is the Andromache derivation philologically recoverable?**  
   The proposal needs a sequence explaining *-machus → -malchus → -malcus → -malius*.

5. **Which Andromalius seal is earliest?**  
   The independent thief operations, proto-Lemegeton character, and elaborate Goetia seal require detailed codicological comparison.

6. **Was the number 72 already controlling the catalogue when Andromalius entered it?**  
   The compiler’s working papers are lost.

7. **How much of Harley 6483 derives from Thomas Rudd personally?**  
   The manuscript is later than Rudd and contains layered material.

8. **When was Mumiah first explicitly paired with Andromalius?**  
   Harley 6483 is the principal surviving witness, but an earlier lost Rudd exemplar may have contained the arrangement.

9. **Which modern guardian-angel attributes originate with Lenain and which with Kabaleb/Haziel?**  
   Popular websites frequently merge the two without citation.

10. **Are there further early Andromalcus theft charms in uncatalogued English manuscripts?**  
    The recurrence across multiple collections suggests that more witnesses may exist.

# Foras: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of claim:

- **Documented text** — directly present in a surviving manuscript, printed grimoire, or other cited primary source.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — a modern historical inference from manuscripts, textual relationships, handwriting, or bibliography.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — a mapping created within an occult system, such as the Golden Dawn, Crowley’s *777*, Lenain’s angelic astrology, or Rudd’s controlling angels.
- **Tradition or legend** — a claim transmitted as religious or magical lore but not independently established as history.
- **Disputed claim** — a reading or interpretation for which the sources conflict or the evidence is insufficient.
- **Modern invention** — a recent artistic, devotional, commercial, fictional, or Internet-era addition not found in the historical source chain.

One of the most important findings is that three different astrological schemes are now regularly conflated:

1. Lenain gives Lecabel the zodiacal ray 151°–155°, conventionally 0°–5° Virgo.
2. A Golden Dawn Shem-ha-Mephorash document places Lekabel at 0°–5° Capricorn.
3. Crowley’s *777* places Foras—not Lecabel—over the entire first decan of Aquarius by day, paired with Amdusias by night.

Consequently, there is no single historically universal “Foras–Lecabel quinance and tarot card.”

## The name and its origins

### Earliest witnesses

**Documented text — Florence, 1494.** The earliest securely dated witness presently identifiable is a spirit catalogue in Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 89 sup. 38, dated January 1494. The reading has been reported as *Forais vel Fortas* by Joseph Peterson, while other tabulations read the first form as *Forchas* and preserve another difficult alias resembling *Sartii*. It calls the spirit a *praeses magnus*, a “great president,” and gives 29 legions. The digitized manuscript is available through the [Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana](https://teca.bmlonline.it/ImageViewer/servlet/ImageViewer?idr=TECA0000886711&keyworks=Plut.89.sup.38), with the variants compared in Peterson’s apparatus to [Weyer’s catalogue](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

This 1494 witness overturns the often repeated assertion that Foras first appears in Weyer in 1577.

**Documented text, disputed date — the *Livre des esperitz*.** The French catalogue preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29 calls him **Forcas**:

> Forcas est ung grant prince qui enseigne la vertu des herbes et des pierres précieuses et fait estre invisible et estre saige et bien parlant à toutes gens, et si apporte trésors mucés en terre quand on lui commande. Et a XXX légions.

Translation:

> Forcas is a great prince who teaches the virtue of herbs and precious stones, causes one to be invisible, wise, and well-spoken to everyone, and brings treasures hidden in the earth when commanded. He has thirty legions.

The text is accessible in a [French transcription](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier) and a [diplomatic transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/livre-esperitz/read/).

Its date requires care. Some catalogues and researchers have assigned the manuscript to the fifteenth century or to the late fifteenth/early sixteenth century. Jean-Patrice Boudet judged its hand no earlier than the middle of the sixteenth century. The catalogue’s textual ancestry may nevertheless be older than its surviving copy. The *Livre* is therefore a pre-Weyer or approximately pre-Weyer witness, but it is less securely dated than the 1494 Florence manuscript. Boudet discusses this demon-catalogue family in [“Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019).

One diplomatic reading gives the legion number uncertainly as *XXVI*, but the expanded French transcription and Boudet’s comparison read **XXX**. That disagreement should not be silently regularized.

**Documented text — Folger MS V.b.26.** The late-sixteenth-century magical compilation commonly called the *Book of Oberon*, written approximately 1577–1583, has **Forcase**:

> Forcase a great prince, he appeareth like a great mann, & he knoweth the vertues of all hearbes, & alsoe of stones, & he giveth againe the sight that was lost, & telleth the places of treasures, & giveth true aunsweres, & hath under him 10 legions.

This appears in the [Folger manuscript transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf). Its **ten legions**, restoration of sight, and “true answers” are not retained in Weyer’s or the standard Goetic text.

Other cognate catalogue spellings identified in manuscript comparisons include **Parchas** and **Partas**. These are useful evidence of unstable copying, not necessarily separate beings.

### Etymology

**Negative finding.** No evidence links Foras securely to an ancient god, hero, ethnic group, or geographical place. This differs sharply from such Goetic names as Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Amon/Amun, Asmoday/Ashmedai, or Belial/*bĕliyyaʿal*, for which older linguistic or mythological antecedents can be demonstrated.

Several proposals nevertheless circulate:

1. **Latin *foras*, “out, outside, forth.”**  
   This is a real Latin adverb, documented in the [Corpus of Latin Dictionaries](https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/lat/foras). As an explanation for the demon’s name it is **disputed**. The earliest family of readings—*Forcas, Forchas/Forais, Fortas,* and *Forcase*—does not show a stable derivation from the Latin adverb. The match may be accidental or a later Latinizing normalization.

2. **Latin *furca*, “fork,” “forked instrument,” or “gallows.”**  
   This is more naturally associated with **Furcas**, the separate mounted knight carrying a sharp weapon. It may help explain why Collin de Plancy conflated Furcas with Foras, but it does not establish the origin of Foras.

3. **Greek Phorbas.**  
   Greek mythology contains several figures named Phorbas. A phonetic comparison is possible, but no textual bridge connects any Phorbas to the medieval spirit catalogue. The proposal is therefore **modern speculation**, not a scholarly etymology. The mythological name can be consulted in [Theoi’s Phorbas entry](https://www.theoi.com/Heros/PhorbasLapithios.html).

4. **“The strong man.”**  
   Modern sources sometimes present this as a translation of *Foras*. It is not. “Strong man” comes from his appearance—Weyer’s *viri fortissimi*—rather than from a demonstrated etymology of the name.

The most defensible conclusion is that **Foras is the normalized form of an unstable late-medieval catalogue name whose original language and meaning remain unknown**.

### Weyer, 1577

**Documented text.** Johann Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* first appeared as an appendix to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in **1577**. It was not part of the original 1563 edition. Foras/Forcas is number 29 in Weyer’s list of 69 spirits.

Weyer’s Latin, at the original entry conventionally cited around p. 921, reads:

> Forras vel forcas magnus Præses est: visitur forma viri fortissimi, & in humana specie vires herbarum & lapidum preciosorum intelligit. Plenè docet Logica, Ethica & eorundem partes. Reddit hominem invisibilem, ingeniosum, loquacem & vivacem: amissa recuperat, thesauros detegit. Dominium viginti novem legionum habet.

A close translation is:

> Forras, or Forcas, is a great President. He is seen in the form of a very strong man, and in human form he understands the powers of herbs and precious stones. He teaches logic, ethics, and all their parts completely. He makes a person invisible, ingenious, eloquent, and long-lived or vigorous; he recovers things lost and reveals treasures. He has dominion over twenty-nine legions.

The Latin *vivacem* can mean lively, vigorous, tenacious of life, or long-lived. Scot and the later *Goetia* resolve it as “to live long.” The Latin does not specify an immortality grant.

Weyer was a physician and critic of witch prosecutions. His demon catalogue occurs inside a skeptical polemic about deception, illness, fraudulent magic, and the misuse of judicial power. Printing the catalogue therefore does not by itself establish that he accepted all its contents as literal fact. Weyer also indicated that he omitted or altered operational material so the work would not function straightforwardly as a manual. The Latin and Peterson’s apparatus are available in the [online *Pseudomonarchia*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Reginald Scot, 1584

**Documented text.** Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, incorporates an English version of Weyer’s catalogue. The translation has often been attributed, at least in part, to Abraham Fleming.

The entry reads:

> Foras [Forras], alias Forcas is a great president, and is seene in the forme of a strong man, and in humane shape, he understandeth the vertue of hearbs and pretious stones: he teacheth fullie logicke, ethicke, and their parts: he maketh a man invisible, wittie, eloquent, and to live long; he recovereth things lost, and discovereth [discloseth] treasures, and is lord over nine and twentie legions.

The bracketed differences reflect later transcription or printing variants. The entry is p. 221 in B. Nicholson’s 1886 reprint; the 1584 original uses different foliation. Facsimiles are held by [Cornell’s Witchcraft Collection](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081) and the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/discouerieofwitc00scot/).

Scot follows Weyer closely. His “wittie” renders *ingeniosum*; “eloquent” renders *loquacem*; “to live long” fixes one possible meaning of *vivacem*.

### The seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia*

**Documented text.** Sloane MS 3825, fol. 104v, gives Foras as the thirty-first spirit:

> The 31 spirit in order, is named Foras, he is a mighty great president & appeareth in ye form of a strong man, in humane shape he can give ye understanding to men how they may know ye vertues of all hearbs and precious stones, & teacheth them ye art of Logick & Ethicks in All their partes if desired, he maketh men Invisible, witty, Eloquent & to live Long; he can discover Treasures and recover Things lost, & he ruleth over 29 Legions of spirits, his seale or Character is thus to be made & worne as a Lamen.

This can be compared directly in Peterson’s [critical transcription of the *Goetia*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

Sloane 3825 is one of the principal *Lemegeton* witnesses and is generally considered one of the more careful. Sloane 2731 also reads **Foras** and carries substantially the same description; its frequently repeated “1572” date is unreliable. Its hand and textual relationships place it much later, probably in the late seventeenth century. A manuscript presentation is available through [Grimorium](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/).

The *Lemegeton* expands Weyer’s description slightly by explaining that Foras “can give the understanding” through which people recognize the virtues of herbs and stones. It also explicitly identifies his seal as a lamen to be worn.

### Harley 6483 and the “Dr Rudd” text

**Documented text with disputed attribution.** Harley MS 6483 is a fair copy made by the lawyer Peter Smart around 1712–1713. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine argue that its material descends from a Thomas Rudd manuscript of approximately 1655–1656. “Rudd’s *Goetia*” is therefore a useful conventional title, but Harley 6483 is not an autograph manuscript signed and dated by Rudd.

Its entry reads:

> The one and thirtieth Spirit is Foras he is a great and mighty President & appeareth in the form of a strong man in human shape. He can give the understanding to men how they may know the virtues of all herbs and precious stones and teacheth them the art of Logic and Rhetoric in all their parts if desired he maketh men invisible witty eloquent and to live long he can discover treasures hid and recovereth things lost, and ruleth over 29 Legions of spirits. His Seal is this which wear.

The conspicuous variant is **“Logic and Rhetoric”**, where Weyer, Scot, Sloane, and Mathers–Crowley have logic and ethics. This might be intentional expansion, scribal substitution, or contamination from the Furcas entry, since Furcas also teaches rhetoric and logic. The reading should not be used to erase the older and better-supported “Ethics.”

Harley 6483 follows the entry with:

> Introibo in potentia Domini, Deus meus memorabor iustitiae tuae solius.

That is the Vulgate Psalm 70:16, corresponding to Psalm 71:16 under Hebrew and Protestant numbering:

> I shall enter into the power of the Lord; my God, I shall remember thy justice alone.

Skinner and Rankine reproduce and discuss this material on pp. 132–133 of *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*.

### Mathers and Crowley, 1904

**Documented text.** The 1904 edition reads, on pp. 31–32:

> (31.) FORAS.—The Thirty-first Spirit is Foras. He is a Mighty President, and appeareth in the Form of a Strong Man in Human Shape. He can give the understanding to Men how they may know the Virtues of all Herbs and Precious Stones. He teacheth the Arts of Logic and Ethics in all their parts. If desired he maketh men invisible, and to live long, and to be eloquent. He can discover Treasures and recover things Lost. He ruleth over 29 Legions of Spirits, and his Seal is this, which wear thou, etc.

The full public-domain text is at [Sacred Texts](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm), with a [1904 PDF facsimile](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf).

Mathers appended an important note:

> One or two Codices have “invincible,” but “invisible” is given in the majority.

He speculated that the strong-man form might support “invincible,” perhaps mentally rather than physically. Textually, however, Weyer’s unambiguous Latin *invisibilem*, Scot’s “invisible,” and the majority of the *Goetia* manuscripts support **invisible**. “Invincible” is a minority manuscript reading that Mathers helped publicize; treating both powers as equally original is unwarranted.

### What the sources do not say

**Negative findings:**

- No source says that Foras must assume a nonhuman form and then be compelled into a second human form. He appears in human shape already.
- No Foras-specific voice, sound, odor, color, incense, sacrifice, offering, or ritual password occurs in Weyer, Scot, or the standard *Goetia*.
- The old catalogues do not assign him languages, romantic love, familiars, physical transmutation, military victory, or universal prophecy.
- “True answers” appears in the Folger *Forcase* notice but not in the standard Weyer–Scot–Goetia chain.
- Restoration of lost eyesight is likewise a Folger variant.
- No early text says he is an ancient god or gives his pre-fall angelic rank.
- The discovery of “treasures” and recovery of “things lost” are distinct offices in the standard text.
- “Long life” is supported; literal immortality is not.

## Number, order, and the expansion from 69 to 72

Weyer has **69** spirits; the *Ars Goetia* has **72**. The change is not simply the addition of three names.

The *Goetia* adds four spirits absent from Weyer’s catalogue:

1. Vassago, inserted as number 3.
2. Seere, number 70.
3. Dantalion, number 71.
4. Andromalius, number 72.

It omits Weyer’s **Pruflas**, also called Bufas, Weyer’s number 4:

\[
69 - 1 + 4 = 72
\]

Scot’s English catalogue already lacks Pruflas, so the omission may have entered the English transmission before the *Goetia* was compiled. Numerous later positions also differ, possibly because an intermediary catalogue was reordered or leaves were rearranged. Peterson suggests textual scrambling as a possibility, not as a proven historical event.

Foras consequently moves from **Weyer 29** to **Goetia 31**. The shift is important because Rudd’s later system pairs demons and Shem angels by their Goetic ordinal numbers. Lecabel is attached to Foras because both are number 31 in that constructed table, not because the medieval Forcas entry originally mentioned Lecabel.

## Rank, planet, metal, and timing

### President: Mercury and quicksilver

**Esoteric correspondence.** The *Goetia*’s general observations assign metals to ranks:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Duke | Venus | Copper |
| Prince or Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| President | Mercury | Mercury/quicksilver |
| Earl or Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |

Foras is a **President**, so his rank-metal is elemental mercury or quicksilver and his rank-planet is Mercury. This association belongs to the grimoire’s ritual technology; it is not an etymological or historical clue to Foras’s origin.

Mercury is toxic. Modern editions sometimes recommend a safer symbolic substitute or alloy. That is a prudent editorial adaptation, not the manuscript instruction.

### Traditional timing

**Documented general rule, not entry-specific biography.** The *Goetia* states that Presidents may be bound “at any hour of the day,” except twilight and night, unless the appropriate king is first invoked. Its general lunar election favors a Moon two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, or fourteen days old. These rules occur in the ritual observations, not in the Foras entry itself.

Later practitioners combine this with:

- Mercury’s planetary day, Wednesday;
- Mercury’s planetary hour;
- the Foras decan in *777*;
- Lecabel’s five-degree zodiacal ray;
- the relevant angelic twenty-minute period.

Those combinations are **later syntheses**. No single early manuscript prescribes all of them together.

## The seal

### Historical development

**Documented text.** The *Livre des esperitz*, Weyer, and Scot provide no seal for Foras. The seal enters the recoverable record with the seventeenth-century *Lemegeton* manuscript tradition.

The standard figure is a linear, non-alphabetic character. In its familiar Mathers–Crowley orientation it is approximately bilateral and vertically organized:

- a principal upright or staff-like axis;
- a short transverse element near its upper portion;
- paired lateral lines that curl or hook outward;
- lower branching or crossing strokes;
- small differences in the size, closure, and direction of terminal curls.

A clean modern vector rendering is available at [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:31-foras-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg).

### Manuscript variants

Sloane 3825 and Sloane 2731 preserve the same basic character, with normal scribal variation in:

- stroke order and thickness;
- whether a hook closes into a loop;
- the angle of the lower branches;
- the position and length of transverse marks;
- the balance of the left and right sides;
- page orientation and later redrawing.

No evidence was found for two fundamentally different historical Foras seals analogous to the conspicuous alternative seals supplied for some other spirits. Most “variants” are redrawings of the same inherited diagram.

Harley 6483 situates the demonic character within Rudd’s angel-constraining apparatus, adjoining the appropriate angelic name, character, and Psalm. Skinner and Rankine interpret the operational lamen as bringing the demon’s seal under its corresponding Shem angel. Claims that every line in the seal encodes a planet, Hebrew letter, chakra, or psychological faculty are **modern symbolic readings**, not explanations supplied by the manuscripts.

A frequently circulated description of Foras’s seal as a “double circle with a central cross and four cardinal crosses” does not match the standard Sloane–Mathers character and appears to describe another sigil or a modern redesign.

### The seventy-two names and Exodus 14:19–21

**Tradition with textual basis.** Exodus 14:19, 14:20, and 14:21 each contain seventy-two Hebrew letters in the conventional Masoretic count used by the system. The three verses are arranged boustrophedonically:

- the first in its ordinary direction;
- the second in reverse;
- the third in its ordinary direction.

Reading vertical columns produces seventy-two triads. The thirty-first triad is conventionally **לכב**, L-K-B. In Christian Cabala and later angelological practice, a divine suffix—usually **־אל**, *El*, or **־יה**, *Yah*—is added, yielding an angelic name such as **Lecabel**.

Lecabel is therefore not a name printed in Exodus. It is an angelized form derived from a particular permutation of the verses.

Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a recognizes a seventy-two-name interpretation of the Exodus passage; this may be consulted through [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Sukkah.45a.2.1). The *Bahir*, medieval magical and kabbalistic traditions, and the composite *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* transmit related doctrines of divine names.

The phrase *Shem ha-Mephorash* has not always meant this exact list of seventy-two angels. In different Jewish texts it can denote the explicitly pronounced or ineffable divine name, especially the Tetragrammaton, or expanded names of 12, 42, or 72 letters. Equating every historical use of the term with Lecabel’s later guardian-angel profile is anachronistic.

### *Sefer Raziel* and the kabbalistic background

**Documented tradition, composite text.** *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is attributed in legend to the angel Raziel, who supposedly revealed heavenly secrets to Adam. Historically it is a medieval compilation containing cosmological, angelological, astrological, talismanic, and divine-name materials from different periods. Its first printed edition appeared in Amsterdam in 1701, although sections circulated in manuscripts much earlier. Background and manuscript records are available from the [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/12606-raziel-book-of) and the [University of Pennsylvania’s manuscript portal](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0002/html/mscodex1674.html).

It is appropriate to identify *Sefer Raziel* as part of the ancestry of seventy-two-name magic. It is not accurate to attribute Lecabel’s later five zodiacal degrees, twenty-minute period, personality, career benefits, or contrary genius wholesale to an ancient “Book of Raziel.”

### Reuchlin, Agrippa, Vigenère, and Kircher

**Scholarly reconstruction.** Renaissance Christian Cabalists reorganized Jewish divine-name material into Latin angelology:

- Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped transmit angelized forms of the seventy-two names.
- Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa incorporated Cabalistic divine names and angelic hierarchies into Renaissance occult philosophy.
- Blaise de Vigenère transmitted diagrams and sigil-making methods connected with divine names.
- Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) presented famous seventy-two-name tables and diagrams as part of his universalizing Christian-Egyptian Cabala.

Kircher’s presentation may be examined in the digitized [*Oedipus Aegyptiacus*](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k1180769) and Peterson’s discussion of [Kircher’s seventy-two names](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm).

This lineage is real, but claims that all the systems agree word-for-word are false. Spelling, suffixes, order, Psalms, zodiacal starting points, and angelic functions vary.

### Rudd’s use of Lecabel against Foras

**Esoteric correspondence documented in Harley 6483.** Rudd’s distinctive innovation, or at least the innovation represented by the manuscript attributed to him, is to place a Shem-ha-Mephorash angel over each Goetic spirit by shared ordinal number:

\[
\text{Lecabel 31} \longrightarrow \text{Foras 31}
\]

The operational theory is that the angel is invoked by the divine names and appropriate Psalm before the demon is summoned. The angel possesses superior authority and “the keys” by which the spirit is constrained. This is not part of Weyer’s entry, Scot’s translation, or the ordinary Sloane description; it belongs to the Rudd recension.

The relevant verse is:

> Introibo in potentia Domini, Deus meus memorabor iustitiae tuae solius.

The Clementine Vulgate commonly reads:

> Introibo in potentias Domini; Domine, memorabor iustitiae tuae solius.

Psalm numbering accounts for the apparent discrepancy:

- Vulgate/Septuagint: Psalm 70:16.
- Hebrew, KJV, and most modern Protestant Bibles: Psalm 71:16.

The KJV has:

> I will go in the strength of the Lord GOD: I will make mention of thy righteousness, even of thine only.

The verse can be compared at [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalmi+70%3A16-18&version=VULGATE%3BNIV) and [Bible Hub](https://biblehub.com/kjv/psalms/71-16.htm).

### Lenain, 1823

**Esoteric correspondence.** Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives Lecabel a much fuller profile on p. 65:

- Number 31.
- Attribute: *Dieu qui inspire*, “God who inspires.”
- Ray: 151st through 155th degree of the zodiac.
- Sixteenth decadal genius: Tomi.
- Decan influence: Sun.
- Five calendar days: 19 April, 30 June, 10 September, 21 November, and 1 February.
- Invocation period: 10:00–10:20.
- Psalm: the sixteenth verse of Psalm 70, though Lenain’s quotation begins with words belonging to the preceding verse in some Vulgate divisions.
- Domains: vegetation and agriculture.
- Beneficial character: astronomy, mathematics, geometry, illuminating ideas, and solving difficult problems.
- Contrary genius: avarice, usury, and illicit enrichment.

Lenain’s French begins:

> Lecabel … Son attribut (Dieu qui inspire.) … Son rayon commence à partir du 151e degré jusqu’au 155e.

It continues by assigning agriculture, astronomy, mathematics, geometry, and difficult problem-solving. The full work is in the [IAPSOP scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf) and [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=BqzyzEEX3MAC).

“God who inspires” is a traditional occult epithet, not a literal philological translation of the consonants L-K-B.

#### Lenain’s three cycles

Lenain employs several overlapping cycles:

1. **Physical or zodiacal domain:** a continuous five-degree ray.
2. **Moral domain:** five scattered calendar dates.
3. **Spiritual domain:** a daily twenty-minute interval.

Modern guardian-angel websites frequently collapse these into a single “birth date” of approximately 23–28 August. That date follows the Virgo zodiacal ray, but it does not reproduce Lenain’s separate list of five moral-domain dates.

### Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

**Esoteric reception.**

- **Papus**, especially in *La Cabbale*, republished and popularized French occult-Cabalistic arrangements heavily dependent on Lenain. A scan is available through the [Cercle Papus](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf).
- **Robert Ambelain**, *La Kabbale pratique* (1951), systematized operative invocations, sigils, divine names, Psalms, and astrological periods for French ceremonial magicians. The book is catalogued by [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique).
- **Haziel**, the pen name of François Bernard-Termès, popularized the seventy-two “guardian angels” for a broad late-twentieth-century audience in works such as *Le Grand Livre de la Cabale magique*, *Répertoire des anges*, and *Communiquer avec son ange gardien*. Bibliographic records can be found in the [Bibliothèque nationale de France catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/rechercher.do?index=AUT3&numNotice=12089673).

Haziel’s psychological profiles, prayers, birth-date advice, and guardian-angel spirituality are **modern elaborations**. They should not be retrojected into Rashi, *Sefer Raziel*, or Rudd.

### Lecabel’s contrary genius

Lenain says Lecabel’s contrary genius governs avarice, usury, and people enriched by unlawful means. Lenain does not identify that contrary genius with Foras. The now-common interpretation that Foras is Lecabel’s “shadow side” of greed is a **modern synthesis** produced by overlaying Rudd’s demon–angel pairing on Lenain’s moral polarity.

## Correspondences

### The competing astrological systems

| System | Being mapped | Position | Planetary condition | Tarot implication | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lenain, 1823 | Lecabel | Degrees 151°–155°, conventionally 0°–5° Virgo | Sun governs first Virgo decan | If Golden Dawn tarot is superimposed: Eight of Pentacles/Disks | Lenain gives the degrees and Sun; the tarot superimposition is later |
| Golden Dawn Shem document | Lekabel | 0°–5° Capricorn | Jupiter in Capricorn decan | Two of Pentacles/Disks, “Lord of Harmonious Change” | Documented Golden Dawn correspondence |
| Crowley, *777 Revised*, Table CLV | Foras by day | 0°–10° Aquarius | Venus in Aquarius decan; Mercury is Foras’s rank planet | Five of Swords, “Lord of Defeat” | Documented Crowley table |
| Crowley, *777 Revised*, Table CLXI | Amdusias by night | Same Aquarius decan | Same decan | Same Five of Swords | Documented night partner |
| Poke Runyon/modern OTA synthesis | Foras–Lecabel | Approximately 0°–5° Virgo | Often combines solar decan and Mercury rank | Eight of Pentacles/Disks in some derivative charts | Modern synthesis |

### Lenain’s Virgo mapping

If the zodiac is counted from 0° Aries:

- 0°–30° Aries = degrees 1–30;
- 30°–60° Taurus = 31–60;
- 60°–90° Gemini = 61–90;
- 90°–120° Cancer = 91–120;
- 120°–150° Leo = 121–150;
- 150°–180° Virgo = 151–180.

Thus Lenain’s 151°–155° corresponds conventionally to **0°–5° Virgo**. The first decan of Virgo is ruled by the Sun in the Golden Dawn decan scheme and corresponds to the **Eight of Pentacles/Disks**, “Lord of Prudence.”

Lenain himself does not call Foras the Eight of Disks. That result comes only after combining Lenain’s angelic ray with Golden Dawn tarot correspondences.

### Golden Dawn Lekabel: Capricorn

A Golden Dawn Shem-ha-Mephorash document explicitly gives:

- Name: Lekabel.
- Sign: Capricorn.
- Planet: Jupiter.
- Degree: 0°–5°.
- Meaning: Teacher.
- Psalm: 71:16.

This places Lecabel in the first quinance of Capricorn. The decan is Jupiter in Capricorn and its pip is the **Two of Pentacles/Disks**, “Lord of Harmonious Change.” The document is available in the [Golden Dawn Shem-ha-Mephorash transcription](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Schemhamphorash.html).

Modern tables that call 0°–5° Virgo “the Golden Dawn assignment” may be using Lenain’s sequential zodiac while borrowing Golden Dawn tarot names. The two tables should be reported separately.

### Crowley’s *777*: Aquarius and the Five of Swords

Crowley’s *777 Revised*, Tables CLV and CLXI, arranges the Goetic spirits as **day and night rulers of decans**. Foras appears in Table CLV at Key Scale 28, Aquarius, as the day spirit. Amdusias appears in the corresponding night table.

Therefore:

- Foras: day ruler of 0°–10° Aquarius.
- Amdusias: night ruler of the same decan.
- Decan planet: Venus.
- Tarot pip: Five of Swords.
- Golden Dawn title: “Lord of Defeat.”
- Foras’s rank planet remains Mercury.

The distinction between Venus and Mercury matters. Venus is the planet of the Aquarius decan; Mercury is the planetary metal assigned to the rank President. They answer different questions and are not a contradiction.

The relevant tables are in the [Hermetic Library scan of *777 Revised*](https://hermetic.com/_media/93beast.fea.st/files/section1/777/liber_777_revised.pdf).

Crowley does **not** give each Goetic spirit a five-degree quinance in these tables. He gives two spirits to each ten-degree decan—one by day and one by night. Claims that “Crowley’s quinance for spirit 31 is 0°–5° Virgo” conflate Crowley with Lenain or a later Rudd–Shem arrangement.

### Direction

Folger MS V.b.26 associates Forcase with the **South**. The ordinary Foras entry in the *Goetia* does not independently state a direction. Later direction tables vary with the kings and quarters used by different manuscripts. “South” is thus documented in one related catalogue but should not be presented as a universal Foras correspondence.

### Choir and archangel

Later Christian-occult tables assign angels 25–32 to the choir of **Dominions**, governed by **Tzadkiel/Zadkiel**, corresponding to **Chesed**. Under that arrangement Lecabel belongs to the Dominions. This is a systematic later angelic hierarchy, not information encoded in the Exodus triad.

### Enochian overlays

Rudd’s collected material includes Solomonic, Cabalistic, and Dee-derived currents, and modern ceremonial magicians sometimes assign Enochian governors, tablets, aethyrs, or calls to individual Goetic operations. No early Foras entry provides a specific Enochian name, governor, aethyr, or tablet square.

A purportedly unique “Enochian name of Foras” is therefore a **later overlay** unless its creator and table are explicitly identified.

## Iconography

### Before Collin de Plancy

Foras has no known medieval narrative portrait. The early catalogues provide verbal images only:

- *Livre des esperitz*: no appearance stated.
- Folger: “a great man.”
- Weyer: *forma viri fortissimi*, a very strong man.
- Scot: a strong man.
- *Goetia*: a strong man in human shape.

The manuscripts’ principal visual contribution is the seal, not a figurative illustration.

### Collin de Plancy, 1818–1863

Jacques Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* first appeared in 1818. Its entries changed materially through later editions.

The 1825 second edition, volume III, p. 77, preserves a recognizable summary of Weyer:

> FORCAS ou FORRAS, — Grand président des enfers; il apparaît sous la forme d’un homme vigoureux…

It continues with herbs, precious stones, logic, ethics, invisibility, ingenuity, eloquence, lost objects, treasure, and 29 legions. This version does not yet give the full mounted-knight iconography.

The 1863 sixth edition, p. 280, reads:

> Forças, Forras ou Furcas, chevalier, grand président des enfers…

It describes an old man with long beard and white hair, mounted on a great horse and holding a sharp dart. It says that he teaches logic, aesthetics, palmistry, pyromancy, and rhetoric, while retaining invisibility, eloquence, lost things, treasure, and 29 legions. The full French entry is at [Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Forcas).

This is not an independent early tradition about Foras. It is a demonstrable **conflation of Foras and Furcas**:

| Feature | Foras | Furcas |
|---|---|---|
| Rank | President | Knight |
| Standard legions | 29 | 20 |
| Appearance | Strong man in human form | Old white-haired, bearded man on a horse with a sharp weapon |
| Sciences | Logic and ethics; herbs and stones | Philosophy, astrology, rhetoric, logic, chiromancy, pyromancy |
| Treasure/lost things/invisibility | Yes | Not the defining offices |
| De Plancy 1863 | Retains these | Borrows knight, horse, beard, weapon, and divinatory arts |

The 1863 expression *chevalier, grand président* itself exposes the merger of two ranks. Its change from *éthique* in earlier editions to *esthétique* may be an editorial substitution or misprint.

### Louis Le Breton’s engraving

Louis Le Breton’s 1863 engraving depicts the merged profile: an elderly, long-bearded, white-haired man mounted on a horse and carrying a pointed spear or dart. The image is available through the [Wikisource image page](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Fichier:Ill_dict_infernal_p0296-280_forcas_demon.jpg).

It is widely reproduced as “Foras.” Historically, however, its body, horse, beard, and weapon derive from **Furcas’s** description. It is best called the de Plancy **Forcas–Furcas composite**, not an uncontaminated portrait transmitted from the *Goetia*.

### Modern art

**Modern invention.** Contemporary occult artists generally follow one of three models:

1. A muscular human figure based on Weyer and the *Goetia*.
2. Le Breton’s mounted old knight, perpetuating the Furcas conflation.
3. A symbolic or monstrous figure constructed from the seal, Mercury, books, herbs, stones, or treasure.

Jim Pavelec’s *Ars Goetia* work is a prominent modern example of artist-led demon iconography; his collection is presented at [Pavelec Art](https://www.pavelecart.com/ars-goetia). Such works document modern reception, not lost medieval imagery.

## Chronological history

### Late medieval and early Renaissance catalogue tradition

**1494 — Florence manuscript.** A securely dated catalogue records a Foras-family name, probably *Forais/Forchas vel Fortas*, as a great President with 29 legions.

**Fifteenth or mid-sixteenth century — *Livre des esperitz*.** Forcas is a great Prince with 30 legions, teaching herbs and precious stones, invisibility, wisdom, eloquence, and buried treasure. The manuscript date remains disputed.

**1508 — Trithemius.** Johannes Trithemius’s *Antipalus maleficiorum* catalogues works titled *Liber officiorum spirituum* and related Solomonic books. This establishes the existence of the catalogue genre, not the presence of Foras in Trithemius’s own text.

**Sixteenth century — *Liber officiorum spirituum* family.** Variant catalogues preserve related forms such as Parchas, Forcase, and Partas. Weyer identifies his source as a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also styled a book of “Empto/Emeth Solomon.” The exact lost exemplar cannot presently be reconstructed.

**Approximately 1577–1583 — Folger V.b.26.** Forcase appears with ten legions and the unusual powers of restoring sight and giving true answers.

### Weyer and Scot

**1577 — Weyer.** *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* prints *Forras vel Forcas* as number 29. Weyer establishes the version that becomes standard: President, strong man, 29 legions, herbs, stones, logic, ethics, invisibility, wit, speech, longevity, lost things, and treasure.

**1584 — Scot.** *The Discoverie of Witchcraft* translates the entry as Foras alias Forcas. Scot’s wording becomes a major English conduit into later manuscript magic.

### The *Lemegeton* and Rudd

**Mid-to-late seventeenth century — *Ars Goetia*.** The catalogue is expanded and reordered to 72 spirits. Foras becomes number 31. The compiler supplies a seal and integrates the catalogue into a full ceremonial system of circles, triangles, lamens, conjurations, ranks, metals, and planetary timing.

**1712–1713 — Harley 6483.** Peter Smart copies the material attributed to Thomas Rudd. Foras is paired by number with Lecabel, and the angel’s Psalm is supplied as an instrument of constraint. “Rhetoric” replaces “Ethics.”

### Dictionaries, French Cabala, and Victorian occultism

**1818 — Collin de Plancy.** The first *Dictionnaire infernal* begins the encyclopedic reception of grimoire spirits.

**1823 — Lenain.** *La science cabalistique* assigns Lecabel the degrees 151°–155°, the period 10:00–10:20, Psalm 70:16, agriculture, mathematics, astronomy, and geometry.

**1825 — De Plancy’s second edition.** Forcas/Forras remains a President in recognizably Weyerian form.

**1863 — De Plancy’s sixth edition.** Foras is conflated with Furcas. Le Breton’s mounted-knight engraving fixes that hybrid in popular iconography.

**Late nineteenth century — Golden Dawn.** The order integrates Goetic, tarot, decan, planetary, Hebrew, and Shem-ha-Mephorash material. A surviving Golden Dawn Shem table places Lekabel at 0°–5° Capricorn, while its Goetic and decan arrangements feed into Crowley’s later tables.

### Twentieth and twenty-first centuries

**1903 — Papus.** French occult Cabala republishes and expands Lenain-derived angelic profiles.

**1904 — Mathers–Crowley *Goetia*.** The English edition standardizes the name Foras and reproduces the seal. Mathers’s footnote publicizes the minority “invincible” reading.

**1909/1955 — Crowley’s *777* and *777 Revised*.** Crowley’s correspondence tables place Foras in the first Aquarius decan by day and Amdusias there by night, yielding the Five of Swords. The posthumously revised edition made the expanded tables especially influential.

**1951 — Ambelain.** *La Kabbale pratique* develops French practical angelic magic from the Lenain/Papus lineage.

**Late twentieth century — Haziel.** The seventy-two angels become personalized guardian angels with birth periods and devotional profiles for a mass readership.

**1990s — Poke Runyon.** Runyon and the Ordo Templi Astartes develop a modern practice integrating Goetic spirits, Shem angels, zodiacal degrees, directions, visualization, and ceremonial timing. His Foras–Lecabel placement generally follows the Virgo-style sequential arrangement rather than Crowley’s Aquarius day-decan table. See [*The Book of Solomon’s Magick*](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

**2001 — Joseph H. Peterson.** Peterson publishes a manuscript-based edition of *The Lesser Key of Solomon*, comparing Sloane manuscripts and earlier sources. The publisher’s record is at [Red Wheel/Weiser](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/).

**2007 — Skinner and Rankine.** *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* publishes the Harley 6483 recension and foregrounds its angelic constraint system. A later edition is listed by [Llewellyn](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556).

**Present.** Foras circulates through occult revivals, Internet demonolatry, sigil art, games, fiction, and software names. These modern uses often combine mutually incompatible elements from Weyer, de Plancy, Rudd, Lenain, Crowley, and recent devotional systems.

## Grimoire ancestors and related demonologies

### *Livre des esperitz*

This is a direct Forcas witness and one of the most important precursors to Weyer. Its concise office-list closely anticipates the later entry.

### *Liber officiorum spirituum*

Weyer explicitly invokes a *Book of the Offices of Spirits*. Several surviving catalogues belong to or resemble this textual family. The Foras entry appears to have moved through that tradition before reaching Weyer.

### Munich Handbook, CLM 849

The fifteenth-century Munich handbook is an important ancestor of European necromantic and spirit-conjuring literature. It contains catalogues and operations involving named spirits, but no secure Foras/Forcas entry was located among its better-known named spirits. Its relevance is **generic and contextual**, not proof of a direct Foras genealogy.

### *Book of Abramelin*

Abramelin manuscripts contain extensive lists of subordinate spirits with unstable names. No secure Foras, Forcas, or unmistakable cognate was verified. Similar-looking names alone are insufficient because copying errors are endemic in these lists.

### *Testament of Solomon*

The *Testament of Solomon* provides the ancient literary model of Solomon interrogating demons, learning their forms and functions, and constraining them through superior angels. It includes Ornias, Beelzeboul, Asmodeus, Onoskelis, and numerous astral or disease-causing spirits. It does **not** contain Foras. Its relationship is structural and legendary rather than genealogical. An English translation is available at [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:The_Testament_of_Solomon).

### The brass vessel legend

The *Goetia*’s prefatory legend says Solomon confined spirits in a brass vessel, which Babylonian searchers later broke open while seeking treasure. The released spirits returned to their places, while Belial entered an image and gave oracles. This belongs to the broader Solomonic frame. No ancient text identifies Foras individually as a participant in that particular episode.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer is the first printer of the standard Foras description. His importance is textual, but his intellectual stance was skeptical and medical. He used demonological material partly to argue that alleged witches were frequently deluded, ill, coerced, or deceived and should not be executed as powerful diabolical conspirators.

### Reginald Scot

Scot brought Weyer’s catalogue into English. Like Weyer, he opposed credulous witch prosecutions. His book later became a reservoir for English ceremonial magic despite its polemical purpose.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

The Rudd attribution is central to the angel–demon pairing. Peter Smart is the identifiable early-eighteenth-century copyist of Harley 6483; Rudd is the proposed seventeenth-century author or compiler behind the material. The distinction matters because modern titles often present “Dr Rudd’s manuscript” as though Harley 6483 were demonstrably in Rudd’s hand.

### Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Mathers prepared the English *Goetia* text; Crowley supplied the prefatory “Preliminary Invocation,” editorial framing, and the book’s publication context. Their 1904 edition established the most reproduced English wording and seal.

Crowley’s later *777* gives Foras a separate decanic position that should not be confused with Lecabel’s Lenain or Golden Dawn quinance.

### A. E. Waite

Waite summarized Goetic spirits in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic*, reproducing the learned but hostile interpretive stance common to his writing. His treatment of Foras can be read at [Sacred Texts](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-book-of-ceremonial-magic/shell/demons-xxxi-foras-to-xxxviii-halpas).

### Frater Achad

**Negative finding.** No specific Foras operation, revelation, or distinctive correspondence attributable to Frater Achad was verified. Achad is relevant to Crowleyan Cabala generally, but attaching him directly to Foras without a work and passage would be speculative.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon is important to modern practical Goetia. His system combines the spirit catalogue with mirror or dark-mirror visualization, Shem angels, astrological tables, and a reconstructed temple practice. His Virgo-style Foras–Lecabel assignment is a modern synthesis, not the same table as Crowley’s *777*.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette’s *The Key to Solomon’s Key* discusses Solomonic magic, ceremonial interpretation, and modern practice. No uniquely influential Foras doctrine or documented Foras operation was verified. His relevance is to the modern explanatory setting.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Skinner and Rankine made the Harley 6483 material accessible and emphasized that Rudd’s scheme uses the seventy-two angels to constrain the seventy-two demons. Their reconstruction of Thomas Rudd’s authorship and date is learned but remains an editorial-historical argument rather than an autograph attribution.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition compares the principal *Lemegeton* manuscripts, Weyer, Scot, and cognate catalogues. His online editions are especially valuable because they show variants rather than silently standardizing them.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent contributed to the modern historicizing revival of grimoire magic, emphasizing older spirit catalogues and the continuity between necromantic, folk, and Solomonic traditions. No securely documented personal Foras working was found; claims that he had a unique Foras revelation require a specific citation.

### Modern scholarship

- Jean-Patrice Boudet examines Renaissance demonological “who’s who” catalogues and their medieval ancestry.
- Julien Véronèse studies learned ritual magic, angelic conjuration, and Solomonic textual traditions.
- Jan Veenstra’s work illuminates medieval and Renaissance spirit magic and courtly learned magic.
- Owen Davies’s *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* situates the *Lemegeton* in the wider history of magical books.

These scholars provide historical context; they do not all make Foras-specific etymological claims.

## Literature, film, games, and other modern reception

### Negative findings

No securely attested Foras character was found in:

- Dante’s *Divine Comedy*;
- Milton’s *Paradise Lost*;
- Jonathan Stroud’s *Bartimaeus* novels;
- canonical *Hellboy* works searched;
- the official *Helluva Boss* cast;
- the principal *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona* demon rosters searched.

Confusions are common:

- *Shin Megami Tensei* prominently uses **Forneus**, not Foras.
- *Helluva Boss* uses Goetic names such as Stolas, but fandom-created characters named Foras are not canonical evidence.
- De Plancy’s Forcas picture is often catalogued as Furcas, producing additional search noise.

Absence from those searches is a finding, not proof that the name has never occurred in an obscure adaptation, localization, card, or supplemental work.

### *Final Fantasy XV*

**Documented modern fiction.** *Final Fantasy XV* uses **Foras** as a daemon encountered in Zegnautus Keep; the transformed Emperor Iedolas Aldercapt is identified with this creature. The appearance and narrative function are inventions of the game rather than adaptations of the strong, learned Goetic President. The connection is chiefly the borrowed name. See the [Final Fantasy Wiki entry](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Foras) and a [game walkthrough documenting the encounter](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/217648-final-fantasy-xv-windows-edition/faqs/78363/chapter-13-redemption).

### Software

**Documented modern namesake.** A GitHub project named [Foras](https://github.com/cryptopatrick/foras) uses the name for theorem-proving or logic-related software. This is an unusually apt modern allusion to the spirit’s traditional office of teaching logic and ethics. It is not part of the occult textual tradition.

### Demonolatry and Internet-era additions

Modern devotional and demonolatry sources attach to Foras:

- personalized “enns,” especially forms resembling *Kaymen vefa Foras*;
- candles, colors, incense, foods, minerals, and offerings;
- psychological “shadow work”;
- physical strength, invincibility, healing, alchemy, or immortality;
- direct identification with Lecabel’s contrary qualities;
- fixed Wednesday or Mercury-hour rites;
- elemental and directional assignments.

These may be meaningful within living modern practice, but they are **modern inventions or syntheses** unless an older source is supplied. In particular, the widely circulated enn does not occur in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, or Harley 6483.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Did Foras first appear in Weyer?

No.

**Documented text:** a Foras-family name occurs in the securely dated Florence manuscript of 1494. The *Livre des esperitz* also contains Forcas, although the surviving manuscript’s exact date is disputed. Weyer’s importance lies in printing and stabilizing the entry, not inventing the name.

### 2. Is the *Livre des esperitz* a fifteenth-century manuscript?

**Disputed.** Some datings place it in the fifteenth century or around the fifteenth/sixteenth-century boundary. Boudet assigns the hand no earlier than the mid-sixteenth century. The text may derive from an older exemplar, but textual antiquity and manuscript date are different questions.

### 3. Was *Pseudomonarchia daemonum* printed in 1563?

Not as such.

The first edition of Weyer’s *De praestigiis daemonum* appeared in 1563. The *Pseudomonarchia* catalogue was added to the **fifth edition in 1577**. Claims about a “1563 Foras entry” conflate the parent book’s first edition with the catalogue’s later appearance.

### 4. Are Foras and Furcas the same spirit?

They are separate in Weyer and the *Goetia*.

- Weyer’s Foras/Forcas: number 29, President, 29 legions.
- Weyer’s Furcas: number 39, Knight, 20 legions.

They overlap in teaching logic but differ in rank, form, legion count, and most offices. De Plancy’s 1863 dictionary deliberately or accidentally merged them, and Le Breton illustrated the resulting composite. Modern works relying on de Plancy frequently perpetuate the conflation.

### 5. Invisible or invincible?

**Invisible** is overwhelmingly better supported:

- Weyer: *invisibilem*.
- Scot: invisible.
- Most *Goetia* manuscripts: invisible.
- Mathers: acknowledges invisible as the majority reading.

“Invincible” is a minority manuscript variant. Mathers’s speculation made it influential, but it should not be presented as the original reading.

### 6. Logic and ethics, or logic and rhetoric?

- Weyer: *Logica, Ethica*.
- Scot: logic and ethics.
- Sloane: logic and ethics.
- Mathers–Crowley: logic and ethics.
- Harley 6483: logic and rhetoric.

The weight of the source chain favors **logic and ethics**. Harley’s “rhetoric” is a genuine variant, possibly influenced by Furcas’s catalogue entry.

### 7. Twenty-nine, thirty, or ten legions?

All three are documented in different witnesses:

- *Livre des esperitz*: probably 30, though one transcription is uncertain.
- Folger V.b.26: 10.
- Florence, Weyer, Scot, Sloane, Harley, and Mathers–Crowley: 29.

Twenty-nine is the stable standard from the securely dated 1494 witness through the principal Goetic tradition.

### 8. Prince or President?

The *Livre* and Folger catalogue call him a **Prince**. The Florence witness, Weyer, Scot, and the *Goetia* call him a **President**. The planetary-metal attribution Mercury follows only when the President ranking is applied.

### 9. Is Foras derived from Latin *foras*?

Possible as wordplay; unproven as historical etymology. The older consonantal instability and prominent *Forcas* forms weaken a direct derivation.

### 10. Is Foras an ancient god or Greek hero?

No supporting transmission has been demonstrated. Identification with Phorbas or another ancient figure is based on sound resemblance. It is **disputed modern speculation**.

### 11. Does *Foraii* belong to Foras?

Probably not. In Weyer, *Foraii* appears in the variant tradition around **Morax/Marax**, not Foras. Modern alphabetical compilations can mistakenly transfer it.

### 12. Is Lecabel’s “meaning” a Hebrew translation?

“God who inspires” is a traditional occult attribute. It is not a secure lexical translation of לכב. Likewise, “Teacher” is a Golden Dawn gloss corresponding to attributed function.

### 13. Which zodiacal quinance is correct?

There is no source-independent answer:

- Lenain: 151°–155° = 0°–5° Virgo.
- Golden Dawn Shem text: 0°–5° Capricorn.
- Crowley: Foras gets a ten-degree Aquarius decan, not a quinance.

A dossier should identify the system rather than harmonize incompatible tables.

### 14. Which tarot card belongs to Foras?

Under Crowley’s actual Goetic day/night decan table: **Five of Swords**, shared with Amdusias.

Under a later superimposition of Lenain’s Lecabel ray on Golden Dawn decan tarot: **Eight of Disks**.

Under the cited Golden Dawn Lekabel quinance: **Two of Disks**.

“Spirit 31 automatically equals Eight of Pentacles” is therefore not a universal Golden Dawn or Crowley rule.

### 15. Did Rudd personally devise the pairing?

**Disputed attribution.** Harley 6483 unquestionably contains the scheme. Skinner and Rankine reconstruct Thomas Rudd as its seventeenth-century author or compiler, while Peter Smart is the known copyist. The surviving manuscript does not settle every stage of authorship.

### 16. Does the seal have an ancient symbolic meaning?

No manuscript explanation survives. Interpretations involving hidden Hebrew letters, alchemical formulae, or psychological diagrams are modern exegesis.

### 17. Does Foras answer every question?

Only the Folger catalogue says “true answers.” The standard Weyer–Goetia entry does not grant universal truthful answers.

### 18. Does Foras confer literal immortality?

No. The source term is *vivacem*, developed in English as “to live long.” Immortality is an exaggeration.

## Open questions

1. **What is the exact first reading in the 1494 Florence manuscript?**  
   High-resolution palaeographical comparison is still needed to settle *Forais* versus *Forchas* and the difficult additional alias sometimes read *Sartii*.

2. **When was the underlying *Livre des esperitz* composed?**  
   The surviving hand may be mid-sixteenth-century even if its source is older. Stemmatic work across related catalogues may narrow the date.

3. **What was Weyer’s exact *Liber officiorum spirituum* exemplar?**  
   No surviving manuscript has been shown to be his direct copy. The surviving catalogues permit reconstruction only in part.

4. **Why did the legion counts vary?**  
   Thirty could become twenty-nine through textual revision or a numeral error, while Folger’s ten may reflect a separate branch. No author explains the change.

5. **Was Harley’s “Rhetoric” deliberate?**  
   It could reflect a pedagogical triad, a scribal error, or contamination from Furcas. Collation with the proposed Rudd exemplars may clarify it.

6. **How exactly did the earliest Foras seal develop?**  
   Comparison of every accessible seventeenth-century exemplar could establish stroke genealogy and determine whether apparently minor differences identify distinct branches.

7. **Why does the Golden Dawn Lecabel table begin its zodiacal cycle differently from Lenain?**  
   The discrepancy may reflect a deliberate shift in starting point, a different manuscript table, or later editorial transmission. It should not be resolved by silently choosing Virgo.

8. **When did Foras first acquire a fully independent figurative portrait?**  
   Le Breton’s famous image is a Foras–Furcas hybrid. A survey of nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century occult illustration may locate the first unmistakably Weyerian “strong man” portrait.

9. **Which modern fictional appearances are official rather than fandom-created?**  
   Search results are heavily contaminated by fan wikis, role-playing characters, original characters, and confusion with Forneus or Furcas. Each proposed appearance needs an official credit, game data entry, script, or published text.

10. **What does the name mean?**  
    No etymology currently explains the manuscript variants and supplies a documented historical bridge. The question remains genuinely open.

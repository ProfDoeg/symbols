# Belial: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Documented text]** — directly stated in a cited primary source.
- **[Textual reconstruction]** — inferred from manuscript relationships, language, or transmission.
- **[Scholarly interpretation]** — a modern academic argument.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** — an occult attribution introduced by a practitioner or magical school.
- **[Tradition/legend]** — transmitted as history inside religious or magical literature but not independently verified.
- **[Disputed]** — competing readings exist.
- **[Modern invention]** — a post-grimoire reinterpretation, fictional portrayal, or newly received occult teaching.
- **[Negative finding]** — a claimed connection could not be found in the relevant primary text.

Because long verbatim reproduction of entire editions is not possible here, the Weyer, Scot, and *Goetia* entries receive short representative quotations, complete analytical paraphrases, and links to the full texts.

# The name and its origins

## 1. The Hebrew Bible: an expression before it was a person

**[Documented text]** Hebrew בְּלִיַּעַל, *bəliyyaʿal*, occurs twenty-seven times in the Masoretic Text. In its biblical contexts it normally denotes worthlessness, wickedness, destructiveness, or a socially dangerous person. It is especially common in construct expressions such as:

- בְּנֵי־בְלִיַּעַל, “sons of *beliyyaʿal*”;
- אִישׁ בְּלִיַּעַל, “man of *beliyyaʿal*”;
- אָדָם בְּלִיַּעַל, “person of *beliyyaʿal*”;
- נַחֲלֵי בְלִיַּעַל, “torrents of destruction/*beliyyaʿal*.”

Representative passages include Deuteronomy 13:13; Judges 19:22 and 20:13; 1 Samuel 2:12 and 25:25; 1 Kings 21:10–13; 2 Samuel 22:5; Psalm 41:8; Proverbs 6:12; and Nahum 1:11, 15.

**[Scholarly consensus]** In these passages *beliyyaʿal* is ordinarily not the personal name of a devil or fallen angel. Older English Bibles preserved “Belial” as though it were a proper name, which encouraged later readers to find the demon throughout the Hebrew Bible. Modern translations usually render the context: “worthless,” “wicked,” “lawless,” “base,” or “destructive.” The distinction is summarized in the [New Schaff-Herzog entry](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/encyc02/encyc02.html?term=Belial) and more recently by the German academic [WiBiLex article](https://www.die-bibel.de/ressourcen/wibilex/altes-testament/belial-beliar).

### Proposed etymologies

**[Disputed]** No derivation has unanimous acceptance. The principal proposals are:

1. **בְּלִי + יַעַל, *bəli* + *yaʿal*** — “without benefit/use/worth.” This produces the familiar “worthless” interpretation and fits much biblical usage.

2. **בְּלִי + עָלָה, “without rising”** — something or someone that will not rise, sometimes connected with death or the underworld.

3. **The root בלע, *b-l-ʿ*, “swallow”** — hence destruction, engulfment, or the underworld that swallows.

4. **בְּלִי עֹל, *bəli ʿol*, “without a yoke”** — a rabbinic wordplay in Sanhedrin 111b, meaning one who throws off the yoke of Heaven. This is the source of the influential modern occult meanings “yokeless,” “without master,” and “independent.” It is a theological pun, not the strongest historical-linguistic derivation.

5. Nineteenth-century attempts to connect Belial with a goddess **Belili**, a Babylonian place of no return, Bel/Baal, Osiris, or a Phoenician serpent deity. These are now speculative or obsolete.

The current range of linguistic proposals is summarized in Guinevere Woolstenhulme’s [*Encyclopedia of Ancient History* entry](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah11046). The older debate, including Gesenius, Ibn Ezra, and the rabbinic *beli ʿol* interpretation, is preserved in the [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/2805-belial).

### Not Baal

**[Negative finding]** Belial has no demonstrable origin in an ancient god, hero, or place comparable to Baal, Astarte, Amun, Asmodeus, or Phoenix. In particular:

- **Belial is not a form of Baal.**
- “Baalial” is not the biblical spelling.
- The shared letters *B-L* do not establish identity with Bel, Baal, or Baal-Zebub.
- “Lord of the forest,” from *baʿal* plus *yaʿar*, is modern folk etymology.

The best reconstruction is that an abstract Hebrew expression gradually became personified.

## 2. The Septuagint and Greek *Beliar*

**[Documented text]** The Septuagint usually translates the Hebrew phrase rather than treating it as a personal name: “lawless men,” “sons of transgression,” or similar expressions.

In later Jewish and Christian Greek, the form **Βελίαρ, Beliar**, becomes common. The final *r* may represent liquid-consonant dissimilation, scribal variation, dialect, or wordplay. It need not name a separate being.

## 3. Qumran and the Dead Sea Scrolls

Between approximately the second century BCE and first century CE, Belial becomes an unmistakably personal adversary.

### Community Rule

**[Documented text]** The *Community Rule* places initiates among the “lot of God” and curses the “lot of Belial.” Belial’s dominion is the present period of temptation and rebellion. The “Angel of Darkness” rules the spirit of injustice; scholars commonly identify this figure with Belial in the wider Qumran system. See the [Vermes translation of the Community Rule](https://vialogue.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/vermes-cddse-p97-117-community-rule.pdf) and Sarianna Metso’s [critical edition description](https://cart.sbl-site.org/books/063541E).

### Damascus Document

**[Documented text]** CD 4:12–19 says Belial was released against Israel and set three “nets”: fornication, riches, and defilement of the sanctuary. This is a moral and sectarian demonology, not the office-bearing king of the later grimoires.

### War Scroll

**[Documented text]** In 1QM, Belial commands the forces opposed to the Sons of Light. His “dominion,” “lot,” and angelic following are destined for destruction. The text opposes the Prince of Light—identified elsewhere with Michael—to Belial and the spirits of his lot. An accessible transcription and translation is the [Qumran War Scroll](https://www.qumran.org/js/qumran/hss/1qm.php3).

**[Important qualification]** Qumran dualism is not absolute metaphysical dualism. In 1QM 13:11, God is said to have created Belial for corruption or the pit. Belial remains a subordinate creature, not an independent evil creator. This tension is discussed in [*Journal of Theological Studies*](https://academic.oup.com/jts/article/70/1/21/5404472?guestAccessKey=c37805a7-8042-4cf5-9105-b26db54f1198).

### 11QMelchizedek

**[Documented text]** Melchizedek delivers the righteous from the power of Belial and executes divine judgment against Belial’s lot. This later contributed to the pattern of a named heavenly power countering Belial, but it is not the origin of Habuhiah.

## 4. Jubilees and the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs

**[Documented text]** *Jubilees* 1:20 asks that the spirit of Belial not rule over Israel, accuse it, or ensnare it. In *Jubilees* 15:33, circumcision protects Abraham’s descendants from the spirits of Belial.

**[Documented text]** The *Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs* repeatedly uses **Beliar** as the chief of deceitful spirits. It associates him with lust, anger, envy, bloodshed, famine, panic, and destruction. The messianic or priestly deliverer will bind or overthrow him. A full public-domain edition is available in R. H. Charles’s [*Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8b/The_Testaments_of_the_twelve_patriarchs_%28IA_testamentsoftwel00char%29.pdf).

**[Scholarly caution]** The *Testaments* are composite. Their Jewish sources, Christian interpolations, and final redaction cannot all be assigned one date.

## 5. The New Testament and early Christianity

**[Documented text]** Second Corinthians 6:15 asks what agreement Christ has with **Beliar/Belial**. The better Greek reading is Βελίαρ. Here the word is plainly personal and represents Christ’s demonic opposite, whether understood as Satan or an Antichrist-like power.

**[Documented text]** The *Martyrdom/Ascension of Isaiah* portrays Beliar as ruler of this world, descending as a lawless human king and finally being destroyed by the Beloved. In Christian interpretation that ruler was often identified with Nero or the Antichrist.

**[Tradition]** Christian demonology increasingly made Belial a name or title of Satan. The later *Goetia*, however, distinguishes Lucifer and Belial as separate spirits.

# The description across the sources

## Pre-Weyer ancestors and parallels

### The *Testament of Solomon*

**[Negative finding]** Belial or Beliar is not a named demon in F. C. Conybeare’s surviving Greek *Testament of Solomon*. Beelzeboul, Asmodeus, Ornias, and a decan demon called **Belbel** occur, but Belbel is not Belial.

The *Testament* is nevertheless an important thematic ancestor:

- Solomon commands demons through a divinely empowered ring.
- Demons give their names, offices, forms, and thwarting angels.
- Solomon imprisons or assigns demons to labor.
- Vessel and seal traditions gather around Solomon.

The claim that “Belial tells Solomon he fell through pride and is made to carry stones for the Temple” is therefore a **modern conflation**, apparently combining the *Goetia* Belial with other demons in the *Testament*. See the [complete Conybeare translation](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm).

### The Munich handbook, Clm 849

**[Documented text]** The fifteenth-century Munich necromancer’s handbook contains Belial in at least one collective invocation with Astaroth and Paymon. It does not preserve the complete later Belial catalog entry about a fiery chariot, senatorships, or eighty legions.

**[Textual reconstruction]** It demonstrates that Belial circulated within late-medieval operational necromancy before Weyer, but it is not a direct copy of the *Pseudomonarchia*. The standard scholarly edition is Richard Kieckhefer, *Forbidden Rites* (1997).

### *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Documented text]** The surviving French *Livre des Esperitz* lists **“Brial roy”**—King Brial—near the end of its catalog. Its sequence and titles are closely related to the later *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*, Weyer, and the *Goetia*. The surviving text does not supply Brial’s complete individual office. See the [French manuscript transcription](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier) and [digital bilingual edition](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/livre-esperitz/read/).

**[Textual reconstruction]** Brial is probably a damaged or shortened Belial, but absolute identity cannot be proven from the name alone.

### Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana MS Plut. 89 sup. 38

**[Documented text]** This late-fifteenth-century manuscript, associated with a January 1494 colophon, contains an unmistakable **Belial** entry before Weyer. It already includes:

- creation after Lucifer;
- an angelic or angular appearance;
- dignities and prelacies;
- goodwill of friends;
- familiar spirits;
- required sacrifices;
- the Solomon-vessel-Babylon narrative.

It gives **thirty legions**, not eighty, and contains an obscure form or alias *Pillech*. This is the strongest evidence that the detailed Belial complex predates the 1577 printing. See *Necromancy in the Medici Library*, [edition and translation](https://dokumen.pub/necromancy-in-the-medici-library-an-edition-and-translation-of-excerpts-from-biblioteca-medicea-laurenziana-ms-plut-89-sup-38-9781907881985-9781907881992.html).

### Folger MS V.b.26

**[Documented text]** The approximately 1577–1583 Folger *Book of Magic* makes Belial a chief spirit under Paimon. He appears as a fair angel riding in a fiery chair, speaks sweetly, and grants promotion, dignity, love, and general favor. Its wording is related to the Office-of-Spirits family but its hierarchy differs. See the [Folger manuscript transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/folger/v_b_26_transcription.pdf).

## Weyer, 1577

Johann Weyer placed the catalog in the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* as *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*. The main treatise opposed witch persecutions and treated much alleged witchcraft as deception, illness, or demonic illusion. Printing the catalog did not amount to endorsing it as a safe manual.

### Identification

- **[Documented text]** Name: *Belial*.
- Number: **23** in Peterson’s normalized count.
- Rank: King.
- Legions: **80**, partly of the order of Virtues, partly Angels.
- Seal: none printed.
- Required constraint: divine power, using the *Vinculum Spirituum*.

### Short Latin excerpt

> “Angelicam assumit imaginem impense pulchram, in igneo curru sedens. Blande loquitur.”

That is: he assumes a very beautiful angelic image, seated in a fiery chariot, and speaks pleasantly. [Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia*, Belial entry](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Complete content, analytically rendered

**[Documented text]**

- Some say Belial was created immediately after Lucifer.
- Therefore they call him father and seducer of those who fell from the angelic orders.
- Sacrifices, gifts, and offerings constrain him to answer, but he does not remain truthful for an hour unless divine power compels him.
- He assumes the beautiful form of an angel seated in a fiery chariot.
- He speaks pleasantly.
- He distributes senatorial preferments and the favor of friends.
- He supplies excellent servants or familiars.
- He rules eighty legions, partly Virtues and partly Angels.
- The exorcist must use the appropriate bond and require Belial to assist the exorcist’s subjects.

### The Babylonian vessel legend

**[Documented legend in Weyer]** Weyer appends a story that Solomon gathered seventy-two kings and their legions into a **glass vessel**, with Bileth first, Belial second, and Asmoday third. The vessel was cast into a deep Babylonian lake or pit. Babylonians broke it open expecting treasure; the spirits escaped. Belial entered an image and answered those who sacrificed to it.

Weyer also reports—and rejects—a necromantic story that Solomon himself was seduced by a woman into worshiping Belial’s image.

The source cited at the end, printed as **Tocz**, remains unidentified. In the Florence version the citation appears as “Daniel,” suggesting corruption somewhere in the transmission.

## Scot, 1584

Reginald Scot incorporated an English translation of Weyer into Book XV of *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*. The translation was probably made by Abraham Fleming.

### Short excerpt

> “He taketh the forme of a beautifull angell, sitting in a firie chariot; he speaketh faire…”

—Scot, *Discoverie*, Book XV, chapter 2, entry “Beliall.” Full text at [Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); first-edition record and page scans at [Cornell](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081).

### Variants and translation effects

**[Documented text]**

- Spelling becomes **Beliall**.
- Eighty legions are retained.
- “Friends” remains, while the later *Goetia* expands this to “friends and foes.”
- The angelic form remains singular.
- Weyer’s glass vessel, *vas vitreum*, becomes a **brasen vessel**.

**[Textual error]** “Brass vessel” is not a faithful translation of *vase vitreo*. It became enormously influential because the *Goetia* copied Scot’s English rather than returning to Weyer’s Latin. Peterson identifies it as one of the clearest proofs of the Scot-to-*Goetia* line of dependence.

## The Sloane *Goetia* manuscripts

### Sloane MS 3825

Peterson’s edition treats Sloane 3825 as its principal manuscript.

**[Documented text; fol. 110r]**

> “He appeareth in ye forme of a Beautiful angel sitting in a Charriot of fire, speaking wth a comly voice…”

The entry calls Belial the sixty-eighth spirit, a mighty and powerful king created next after Lucifer. It gives one beautiful angel, eighty legions, senatorial preferments, the favor of friends and foes, excellent familiars, gifts and sacrifices, habitual falsehood, divine constraint, and a seal worn as a lamen. See [Peterson’s transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm#belial).

### Sloane MS 2731

**[Documented text]** The name remains Belial and the position remains 68. It belongs to the same expanded catalog but has orthographic and graphic differences elsewhere in the text. Its seal is recognizably the same character, not an independently designed emblem.

### Harley MS 6483

**[Documented text]** The Harley/Rudd recension has Belial at 68 and generally retains eighty legions. Its most conspicuous appearance variant is **two beautiful angels**, which anticipates the printed Mathers version.

Harley 6483 is a copy made in 1712–1713 by Peter Smart from papers associated with Dr Thomas Rudd. “Rudd’s *Goetia*” is therefore conventional attribution: ownership, compilation, or an earlier Rudd source is plausible, but Thomas Rudd’s autograph authorship is not proven.

## Mathers–Crowley, 1904

The 1904 *Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* was based on work by S. L. MacGregor Mathers and edited, published, and supplemented by Aleister Crowley.

### Short excerpt

> “He appeareth in the Form of Two Beautiful Angels sitting in a Chariot of Fire.”

—Mathers–Crowley, *Goetia* (1904), p. 44, [Sacred Texts transcription](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm).

### Notable printed readings

- **Two** beautiful angels, rather than Sloane 3825’s singular angel.
- **Fifty** legions, rather than eighty.
- “Presentations and Senatorships.”
- Friends and foes.
- Offerings, sacrifices, and gifts.
- The same warning that he will not remain truthful unless constrained by divine power.

**[Textual assessment]** Fifty is almost certainly secondary. Weyer, Scot, and Sloane 3825 agree on eighty, while later annotated editions explicitly restore eighty. No theological distinction between “fifty legions of spirits” and “eighty legions of demons” exists in the primary chain; that reconciliation is modern harmonization.

## Peterson, 2001 and continuing digital edition

Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (Weiser, 2001) collates Sloane manuscripts and records variants rather than silently adopting the 1904 reading.

**[Documented editorial finding]**

- Principal form: one beautiful angel.
- Principal legion count: eighty.
- Number: 68.
- Source spelling: Belial across Sloane 3825, 3648, 2731, Harley 6483, and the Northwestern manuscript.
- Wellcome MS 3203 places a Belial at 49, demonstrating that not every catalog family shared the standard numbering.

## Number and order: Weyer’s 69 versus the *Goetia*’s 72

**[Documented text]** Weyer’s surviving printed catalog has sixty-nine spirits even though its Belial story refers to seventy-two kings.

The *Goetia* reaches seventy-two by:

- adding **Vassago** at 3;
- omitting Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**, Weyer 4;
- adding **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius** at 70–72.

Thus: 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

**[Textual reconstruction]** The number was probably regularized for Solomonic and kabbalistic significance. The catalog’s surviving beginning may also have lost material concerning Lucifer, Beelzebub, Satan, and rulers of the quarters, but that is not equivalent to saying four known catalog entries simply fell out.

Belial moves from Weyer 23 to *Goetia* 68 because the *Goetia* extensively reorders the entire catalog. The new order is not a simple offset.

# The seal

## Manuscript evidence

**[Documented text]** Neither Weyer nor Scot supplies individual spirit seals. Peterson notes that the recognizable seventy-two seals do not occur in the earlier Office-of-Spirits manuscripts known to him. They belong to the later *Goetia* compilation or a closely related lost source.

The Sloane and Harley witnesses depict the same basic Belial character with differences in:

- curvature and angularity;
- how subsidiary strokes meet the main framework;
- spacing between the paired halves;
- line termination;
- whether the design is freely drawn or regularized inside a circular lamen.

## Visual description

**[Documented iconography]** The familiar 1904 figure is enclosed by a circle and is approximately bilateral. Its interior character resembles a stylized mask or skull: paired upper and lateral curves are joined to a central angular framework, with lower strokes turning outward. The apparent “eyes,” “horns,” or “face” are visual resemblances, not meanings supplied by the manuscript.

A faithful public-domain reproduction is available as the [1904 Belial seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3A68-Belial_seal.png) and as a [modern vector tracing](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3A68-belial-sigilo-ars-goetia.svg).

**[Caution]** Claims that its components encode sovereignty, Earth, a skull, chakras, Saturn, Lucifer, or particular sacred geometries are modern interpretations. The early sources do not explain how the character was constructed or what its individual strokes mean.

## Ritual material

**[Documented text]** The *Goetia* says seals of kings are to be made in the metal of the Sun, gold. The seal is worn before the exorcist as a **lamen**.

That does not necessarily mean every surviving ink drawing was intended to be surrounded by two printed circles. Mathers regularized the seals as book illustrations; manuscript lamens are less typographically uniform.

# The angel: Habuhiah

## Identity

| Field | Rudd/Lenain tradition |
|---|---|
| Number | 68 |
| Root triad | חבו |
| Rudd spelling | **Habujah** |
| Other forms | Habuiah, Habuhiah, Chabooyah, Haboyah |
| Full Hebrew form | חבויה |
| Choir | Angels, in the later nine-choir arrangement |
| Ruling archangel | Gabriel in the later Lenain/Papus system |
| Demon constrained | Belial |
| Degree-sector | 336th–340th degree, i.e. 5°–10° Pisces |
| Psalm | Psalm 106:1 modern numbering; 105:1 Vulgate |
| Attribute | “God who gives generously” |
| Offices | Health, healing, agriculture, fertility |
| Contrary genius | Sterility, famine, pestilence, crop-destroying insects |
| Lenain daily interval | 10:20–10:40 p.m. |
| Lenain secondary days | May 26, August 6, October 17, December 28, March 10 |
| Later “guardian” dates | Usually February 25–29, with calendar variants |

## Construction of the seventy-two names

**[Kabbalistic tradition]** Exodus 14:19–21 contains three Hebrew verses of seventy-two letters each. They are arranged boustrophedonically:

1. verse 19 in its normal direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its normal direction.

The first letter of verse 19, last letter of verse 20, and first letter of verse 21 form the first triad; the process continues across the verses to produce seventy-two triads of three letters. The method is explained in Rashi on Sukkah 45a and in later kabbalistic sources; a concise history appears in Joshua Trachtenberg’s [*Jewish Magic and Superstition*](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

The sixty-eighth triad is חבו.

## Is Habuhiah the “meaning” of חבו?

**[Disputed terminology]** חבו is first a divine-name triad, not originally an independently narrated angel. Christian kabbalists made the triads pronounceable by adding **-El** or **-Yah/-Iah**. Thus חבו + יה produces forms such as *Habuiah*.

“God who gives generously” is Lenain’s **attribut divin**, not a philologically transparent translation of the three-letter sequence.

## Historical development

### *Sefer Raziel*

**[Documented tradition]** The seventy-twofold divine name is important in *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*, where the triads occur without the standardized French guardian-angel biographies later attached to them. *Sefer Raziel* is a medieval compilation, although individual materials in it are older.

**[Correction]** The familiar package—name, five-degree zodiac sector, Psalm, moral personality, occupation, contrary genius—is not all present in *Sefer Raziel*.

### Reuchlin, 1517

**[Documented text]** Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* presents the triads as pronounceable angelic names through *-el* and *-yah* endings. A public-domain scan is held by the [Badische Landesbibliothek](https://digital.blb-karlsruhe.de/blbihd/content/titleinfo/7174105).

### Vigenère

**[Documented reception]** Blaise de Vigenère adopted Reuchlin’s angelic interpretation. His material subsequently fed the compilation associated with Thomas Rudd.

### Kircher

**[Documented reception]** Athanasius Kircher reproduced the seventy-two names in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654), including a celebrated circular table connecting the names with seventy-two languages or peoples.

**[Caution]** Kircher’s comparative language scheme is a baroque Christian-kabbalistic construction, not historical linguistics.

### Rudd and Harley 6483

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** Harley 6483 sets each of the seventy-two Goetic spirits against a corresponding Shem angel. Number 68 gives **Habujah** over **Belial**. A modern tabular transcription confirms the Rudd spelling and pairing in Stephen Skinner’s [*Complete Magician’s Tables* excerpt](https://lyricaltokarev.com/public/dynamic/sync/stuff/pdf-documents-library/Complete%20Magician%27s%20Tables.pdf).

Rudd’s conceptual rule is that a rebellious spirit is not adequately controlled merely by generic divine names. Its particular Shem angel supplies a more exact heavenly authority. The angel’s name and Psalm are added to the spirit’s constraining apparatus.

**[Tradition versus authorship]** The manuscript’s attribution to Dr Thomas Rudd is historically plausible but not equivalent to a signed autograph. Peter Smart’s 1712–1713 copying date is more secure than the date of the underlying compilation.

### Skinner and Rankine, 2007

Stephen Skinner and David Rankine’s *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* reconstructs Harley 6483’s system and emphasizes the counterbalancing angels. The publisher describes it as a form in which the seventy-two angels are used to bind the seventy-two demons. See the [publisher’s edition page](https://www.llewellyn.com/product.php?ean=9780738723556).

### Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* is the decisive source for the elaborate French guardian-genius system.

**[Documented text]** For number 68 Lenain gives:

- **Habuiah**;
- divine attribute: *Dieu qui donne avec libéralité*;
- people/divine name: the Peloponnesians, **Dépos**;
- degrees 336–340;
- Psalm verse *Confitemini Domino, quoniam bonus…*;
- preservation of health and cure of illness;
- agriculture and fertility;
- love of countryside, hunting, gardening, and farming;
- contrary genius: sterility, famine, plague, and harmful insects.

Lenain explicitly says that several later astrological tables are his own invention. That is important: not every detail should be projected backward into Kircher, Reuchlin, or Jewish Kabbalah. A scan is preserved at [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_ms05365).

### Papus

**[Documented reception]** Papus reproduced and popularized Lenain’s system in *La Cabbale* and related writings. A digitized edition is available from the [Cercle Papus](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf).

### Golden Dawn

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The Golden Dawn synthesized:

- zodiacal decans;
- planetary rulers;
- Tarot pip cards;
- Hebrew divine and angelic names;
- color scales;
- Enochian elemental tablets.

It did not originate Rudd’s exact Habuhiah–Belial pairing. Golden Dawn materials and Crowley’s *777* created a second, competing way to put Goetic spirits into the zodiac.

### Ambelain

**[Documented reception]** Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) continued the French Christian-kabbalistic treatment of the seventy-two angels. See its [Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL22019447M/La_kabbale_pratique).

### Haziel and Kabaleb

**[Modern esoteric development]** Kabaleb and Haziel transformed the names into a popular guardian-angel system, especially from the late 1980s onward. Habuhiah becomes a healing and fertility angel for February 25–29, with prayers, colors, stones, moral psychology, and “missions.”

These details are not ancient Kabbalah. The Bibliothèque nationale de France catalogs Kabaleb’s [*Les Anges* (1989)](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35062108t). A representative modern Habuhiah profile appears at [Éphéméride des Anges](https://www.ephemeride-des-anges.fr/monange/mon-ange.php?ange=68).

## Psalm numbering

Lenain’s Latin begins:

> “Confitemini Domino, quoniam bonus…”

This is:

- Psalm **105:1** in the Vulgate;
- Psalm **106:1** in the Masoretic and most modern English numbering.

Modern occult books that alternate between 105 and 106 may therefore be referring to the same verse. References to Psalm 103 for Habuhiah are most likely printing, OCR, or numbering errors.

## The *777* problem in detail

**[Documented esoteric correspondence]** Crowley divided the Goetic list into two sets:

- 1–36: day demons, one per decan;
- 37–72: night demons, one per decan.

Belial is 68. Subtracting 36 places him in decan position 32: the second decan of Aquarius. Asmoday is day spirit 32. Therefore both spirits belong to the same decan and pip, explaining the rule that each pip carries two Goetic spirits.

Crowley’s 1918 magical record explicitly describes a working to create or evoke Belial as the “2nd decan Aquarius, night-demon” in connection with “Earned Success.” See the digitized [*Magical Record of the Beast 666*](https://www.howardnema.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Aleister-Crowley-Magical-Record-of-the-Beast-666.pdf).

The correct Golden Dawn pip is **Six of Swords**, Mercury in Aquarius. A later footnote identifying it as Six of Disks conflicts with both the zodiac and Crowley’s own wording and is an editorial mistake.

## Why some tables say Eight of Cups

**[Textual reconstruction]** If spirit 1 begins at 0° Aries and each successive number receives five degrees, spirit 68 begins at absolute zodiac degree 335°: 5° Pisces. That sector belongs to the Eight of Cups, Saturn in Pisces.

This is internally coherent with Habuhiah’s Lenain sector. It is simply not Crowley’s *777* demon-decan scheme.

## Timing

The most source-faithful timing hierarchy is:

1. **[Documented text]** Use the king’s hours: 9 a.m.–noon or 3 p.m.–sunset.
2. Observe the specified even lunar ages.
3. Make a king’s seal in solar metal, gold.
4. **[Later planetary inference]** Prefer Sunday or an hour of the Sun.
5. **[Later zodiacal overlay]** Work either while the Sun occupies 5°–10° Pisces under the serial angel scheme, or during 10°–20° Aquarius under *777*. Neither zodiacal requirement occurs in the original Belial entry.

# Iconography

## The angelic chariot

**[Documented text]** The oldest stable iconographic motif is not a monstrous demon but an exceptionally beautiful angel seated in a fiery chariot. This persists from the Office-of-Spirits material through Weyer, Scot, the *Goetia*, and de Plancy.

The plural “two beautiful angels” is a later manuscript/printed variant. It is not in Weyer, Scot, or Sloane 3825.

## Collin de Plancy

### First edition, 1818

**[Documented reception]** Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* gathered biblical, literary, folkloric, and grimoire material alphabetically. Its Belial entry developed through successive editions.

### Sixth edition, 1863

De Plancy describes Bélial as outwardly beautiful, graceful, and dignified but inwardly vicious and dissolute. He repeats:

- creation shortly after Lucifer;
- participation in the angelic revolt;
- a beautiful fiery-chariot appearance;
- pleasant speech;
- dignities, favors, and good servants;
- eighty legions;
- the Babylonian vessel and idol story.

He also says Belial was worshiped by the Sidonians and at Sodom.

**[Disputed]** There is no ancient Sidonian inscription, temple list, or classical source establishing a god Belial. The *Dictionnaire de Trévoux* was already skeptical of the alleged Sidonian idol. The claim probably grew from treating the biblical term as an ancient proper name and mixing it with Baal.

The complete French entry is [“Bélial,” *Dictionnaire infernal*, sixth edition, pp. 87–88](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/B%C3%A9lial).

### Was there a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**[Negative finding]** No dedicated Belial portrait appears among Louis Le Breton’s sixty-nine engravings in the 1863 edition. Belial has a textual entry but not one of the familiar zoological demon portraits.

The separate entry **Bélias**, “a demon invoked as prince of the Virtues in sabbath litanies,” is not accompanied by evidence that it is simply Belial. It may be a variant, a separate catalog name, or a de Plancy conflation. See the [Bélias entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/B%C3%A9lias).

## Modern occult art

**[Modern invention]** Modern artists have supplied the monstrous body the original catalog lacks:

- David P. Wilson’s illustrations in *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* (1992);
- Lon Milo DuQuette’s Tarot of Ceremonial Magick imagery;
- E. I. Ford’s Luciferian *Goetia* illustrations;
- independent depictions as horned king, dragon, skeletal monarch, volcanic figure, or black-armored warrior.

These are creative receptions. None supersedes the primary fiery-chariot angel.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer was a physician, former student of Agrippa, and critic of witch prosecutions. His *Pseudomonarchia* preserved the first printed Belial description while framing magical catalogues as deceptive and dangerous.

## Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot used the material in an anti-witch-hunting and anti-conjuring polemic. Fleming is the likely translator. Their English carried the catalog into Britain and introduced errors—including glass becoming brass—that later became canonical.

## The anonymous *Lemegeton* compiler

The compiler reordered Scot’s catalog, added seals and ritual apparatus, supplied four spirits, dropped Pruflas, and forced the total to seventy-two.

## Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Rudd is traditionally credited with matching the seventy-two demons to seventy-two Shem angels. Peter Smart is the identifiable 1712–1713 copyist of Harley 6483.

## Mathers and Crowley

Mathers translated and collated the *Goetia* material; Crowley edited and published it in 1904, adding his Preliminary Invocation and Thelemic commentary.

Crowley later performed a specifically documented Belial working on 9 March 1918, using the second-decan Aquarius/night-demon attribution.

## Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, belonged to Crowley’s circle and wrote extensively on Qabalah. No primary Belial operation or distinct Belial doctrine by Achad was located. Listing him as a Belial practitioner without a citation would therefore be unwarranted.

## Poke Runyon

Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* and film *The Magick of Solomon* promoted mirror-scrying forms of Goetic evocation and the balancing of rebellious spirits with holy angels. Runyon says his original 1996 edition softened the coercive conjurations but that the later edition restored stronger forms. See [Runyon’s own edition description](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

## Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette co-produced *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* and created *Tarot of Ceremonial Magick*, explicitly synthesizing Goetia, astrology, Tarot, and Enochian magic. This work helped spread the later correspondence-heavy visual system.

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 critical edition and Esoteric Archives transcription returned attention to Sloane 3825, manuscript variants, Weyer, Scot, and the wider Office-of-Spirits family. His work is essential for distinguishing manuscript readings from the 1904 popular edition.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* made Harley 6483’s angel–demon counter-pairing widely available and popularized **Habujah/Habuhiah over Belial**.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica* placed the grimoires in a broader history of goēs, necromancy, spirit cult, and late-antique and medieval magic. His importance here is contextual; the claim that every Goetic demon is a degraded ancient god goes beyond what the Belial evidence supports. See the publisher’s [description of *Geosophia*](https://scarletimprint.com/publications/p/geosophia).

## Owen Davies

Davies’s *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* situates the *Lemegeton* in the social history of manuscript circulation, print, colonial transmission, and modern occult revival. See the [Oxford edition](https://academic.oup.com/book/47570).

## Jan Veenstra and Julien Véronèse

Their work on learned magic, courtly necromancy, and medieval magical manuscripts provides context for the *Liber Officiorum* and *Livre des Esperitz* tradition. Véronèse and Jean-Patrice Boudet’s manuscript studies are particularly relevant to the French and Latin ancestors of Weyer. See [Boudet and Véronèse in *Médiévales*](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1171).

# Belial in literature, religion, film, and games

## Medieval *Book of Belial*

**[Documented literature]** Jacobus de Teramo’s *Consolatio peccatorum*, commonly called the *Book of Belial* and composed around 1382, presents a legal suit in which Lucifer prosecutes Christ for trespassing in Hell, with Solomon as judge.

Belial functions as a legal representative or infernal advocate. This enormously popular morality and legal allegory is not the source of the *Goetia* description, but it strengthened Belial’s individual literary identity before Weyer.

## Milton

**[Documented literature]** Belial is a major fallen angel in *Paradise Lost* (1667; revised 1674). Milton combines beauty, dignity, persuasive speech, vice, cowardice, and sloth.

In Book II he is introduced as:

> “A fairer person lost not Heav’n…”

Milton’s Belial opposes renewed open war, clothing ignoble ease in apparently rational speech. His beauty and pleasing eloquence resemble Weyer’s angelic form and fair speech, although direct literary dependence is not proven. See the University of Toronto’s [1674 text of *Paradise Lost*, Book II](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/paradise-lost-book-ii-1674).

## Dante

**[Negative finding]** Belial is not named as a character in Dante’s *Divine Comedy*. Dante names figures such as Lucifer, Minos, Pluto, Malacoda, and other devils, but not Belial. Claims of a Dantean Belial generally confuse later infernal hierarchies with Dante’s poem.

## LaVey

**[Modern religious reception]** Anton LaVey’s *Satanic Bible* (1969) names one of its four sections “The Book of Belial: Mastery of the Earth.” There Belial represents earthly mastery, ritual magic, and material independence. This is a modern symbolic system, not the *Goetia*’s Sun/gold planetary correspondence.

## *Basket Case*

**[Documented film]** Frank Henenlotter’s *Basket Case* (1982) names the protagonist’s deformed, telepathic conjoined twin **Belial Bradley**. This is a horror-fiction use of the name, not a literal adaptation of the fiery-chariot king. See the [film record and synopsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basket_Case_%28film%29).

## Shin Megami Tensei and Persona

**[Documented game reception]** Belial is a recurring demon in Atlus’s *Megami Tensei* franchise, usually of the Tyrant race and Chaos alignment. He appears across *Megami Tensei II*, *Shin Megami Tensei*, *Strange Journey*, *SMT IV*, *SMT V*, *Devil Survivor*, and related titles.

In *Persona 4* and *Persona 5* he belongs to the Devil Arcana. The in-game compendium identifies him as one of the seventy-two Goetic demons and retains the grant of high social status. He and Nebiros are used to fuse Alice, a narrative inherited from the original *Shin Megami Tensei*. See the franchise [Belial compendium](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Belial).

## Diablo

**[Documented game reception]** Blizzard’s Belial is the **Lord of Lies**, one of the Lesser Evils, and a central antagonist in *Diablo III*. He rules Caldeum through deception while disguised as a child-emperor. The title emphasizes the long tradition of Belial’s false answers but belongs to Blizzard’s independent cosmology. Blizzard has also returned him to *Diablo IV*; see [“A New Tale: All Who Lie”](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24195755/a-new-tale-all-who-lie).

## Darksiders

**[Documented game reception]** *Darksiders II* received downloadable content titled **The Demon Lord Belial**. The Xbox catalog still identifies it by that name: [The Demon Lord Belial](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/games/store/the-demon-lord-belial/bs1lks7dsd3c).

## Hellboy

**[Limited documented reception]**

- In “Buster Oakley Gets His Wish,” children attempting demonology name Belial among possible demons; the actual threat proves extraterrestrial.
- Belial was reportedly planned among the infernal council figures in *Hellboy in Hell* but did not make the final published council scene.

Thus Belial is referenced around the *Hellboy* universe but is not a principal Mignola antagonist. Claims that Belial is Hellboy’s father or the main villain of *The Wild Hunt* are false; Hellboy’s infernal father is Azzael. A catalog of the cut council figures is preserved on the [Hellboy Bael reference page](https://hellboy.fandom.com/wiki/Bael).

## Helluva Boss

**[Negative finding]** No verified canonical Belial character has appeared in *Helluva Boss* through the material checked. The series uses Goetic names such as Stolas, Paimon, Andrealphus, Vassago, and Asmodeus, which has generated extensive Belial fan art and original characters. AO3’s “Belial (Helluva Boss)” tag records fandom creation, not television canon.

## Bartimaeus

**[Negative finding]** No verified Belial character was located in Jonathan Stroud’s four canonical *Bartimaeus* novels. The series and *The Ring of Solomon* certainly use Solomonic demon lore, but that thematic relationship is not evidence of a named Belial appearance. The author’s [official series description](https://www.jonathanstroud.com/about_bart.html) does not identify one.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Is “without a master” the true translation?

**Finding:** **Disputed.**

“Without worth/use” remains the most common lexical explanation. “Without a yoke” is an old rabbinic interpretation and a powerful theological reading. Modern demonolatry often turns it into “without a master,” then treats autonomy and self-sovereignty as Belial’s primordial identity. That last step is modern religious interpretation, not settled Hebrew philology.

## 2. Was Belial an ancient Phoenician or Sidonian god?

**Finding:** **No reliable evidence.**

De Plancy carries the claim, and Jacob Bryant identified Beliar with Belorus, Osiris, and a serpent. No relevant inscription or ancient cult list verifies it. Belial should not be put beside Baal or Astarte as a securely attested pre-biblical deity.

## 3. Does Belial first appear in Weyer?

**Finding:** **No, but the distinctions matter.**

- The Hebrew word is ancient.
- Personal Belial is securely attested in Second Temple literature.
- Medieval necromantic uses precede Weyer.
- *Livre des Esperitz* has “Brial roy.”
- Plut. 89 sup. 38 contains a recognizably detailed Belial entry before 1500.
- Weyer supplies the first printed form of the catalog entry in 1577.

## 4. One angel or two?

**Finding:** **Textual variant.**

- Weyer: one.
- Scot: one.
- Sloane 3825: one.
- Harley 6483: two.
- Mathers–Crowley: two.

The plural should not be retrojected into Weyer.

## 5. Fifty or eighty legions?

**Finding:** **Eighty is the stronger reading.**

- Weyer: 80.
- Scot: 80.
- Sloane 3825: 80.
- Harley recension: generally 80.
- 1904 printing: 50.
- Plut. 89 sup. 38: 30 in the earlier manuscript family.

The printed fifty probably arose through copying, editing, or typography. The medieval thirty shows that legion counts were fluid before Weyer.

## 6. Glass or brass vessel?

**Finding:** **A demonstrable translation error followed by tradition.**

Weyer has glass. Scot printed brass. The *Goetia* inherited brass, then supplied diagrams of a brass vessel. A mistranslation became an iconic ritual artifact.

The broader story also resembles the sealed-jinni vessel in *The Thousand and One Nights* and earlier Solomon-demon legends. Direct dependence on the Arabic tale is plausible but not proven from this verbal similarity alone.

## 7. Belial, Bileth, Beleth, and Bélias

These should not automatically be merged.

- **Belial** and **Bileth/Beleth** are explicitly separate kings in the vessel narrative.
- **Bélias** is a separate de Plancy entry.
- **Beliar** is an ancient Greek form of Belial.
- **Brial** is probably a scribal ancestor of Belial but is not certain solely from orthography.
- **Belbel** in the *Testament of Solomon* is a different demon.
- **Bel/Beal/Baal** can designate unrelated deities or other Goetic names.

## 8. Spirit 23 or 68?

Both are correct relative to different catalogs. Number 23 is Weyer; number 68 is the *Goetia*. Treating the number as an eternal metaphysical property ignores the textual history.

## 9. Pisces or Aquarius?

Both belong to later but different esoteric systems:

- **Habuhiah/serial number:** 5°–10° Pisces, Eight of Cups.
- **Crowley’s *777* day/night decans:** 10°–20° Aquarius by night, Six of Swords.

Calling the Pisces attribution “Crowley’s 777 quinance” is incorrect.

## 10. Sun, Mercury, or Saturn?

These describe different layers:

- Sun/gold: Belial’s Goetic rank as king.
- Mercury in Aquarius: Crowley’s decan and Six of Swords.
- Saturn in Pisces: Habuhiah’s serial quinance and Eight of Cups.

No manuscript requires them to collapse into one planetary nature.

## 11. Did Solomon worship Belial?

**Finding:** **Legend reported and rejected by Weyer.**

Weyer says some necromancers claimed a woman persuaded Solomon to worship Belial’s idol. Weyer explicitly rejects the allegation. It is not the biblical account of Solomon’s foreign cults and is not independent history.

## 12. Did the Babylonians release the seventy-two demons?

**Finding:** **Grimoire legend.**

The story occurs in the Office-of-Spirits chain and the *Goetia*. No Babylonian source or archaeological evidence supports it.

## 13. Does he demand literal sacrifice?

**Finding:** **The text says sacrifices but does not define the victim.**

The catalog uses *sacrificia*, offerings, gifts, and oblations without specifying human or animal blood. Later practitioners interpret these as incense, food, drink, money, devotional acts, sexual fluids, blood, or symbolic gifts. Those specifications are later ritual choices, not wording found in the Belial entry.

## 14. Is Belial a demon of wealth, sex, lawlessness, or Earth?

These derive from different receptions:

- dignity and political favor: primary *Goetia* office;
- excellent familiars: primary;
- deceptive answers: primary;
- lawlessness: biblical and Christian reception;
- vice and sexual corruption: pseudepigrapha and Miltonic/de Plancy reception;
- “Mastery of the Earth”: LaVey;
- wealth or financial independence: modern extension of dignities and material mastery;
- fire: appearance and chariot imagery;
- Earth element: modern demonolatry, not the manuscript rank system.

# Open questions

1. **What was Weyer’s exact manuscript?**  
   He called it *Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto*. No surviving manuscript is certainly his exemplar.

2. **Where did the Belial seal originate?**  
   It is absent from Weyer, Scot, and presently known early Office-of-Spirits witnesses. Its precise pre-*Lemegeton* source is unknown.

3. **What does *Pillech* in Plut. 89 sup. 38 mean?**  
   It may be an alias, a description corrupted into a name, or a scribal error.

4. **What was Weyer’s “Tocz”?**  
   It may conceal a personal name, book title, or corrupted citation. The “Daniel” reading in the Florence witness does not resolve it.

5. **Why did Harley/Mathers pluralize the angelic form?**  
   The change could be scribal duplication, deliberate symbolic revision, or dependence on a lost intermediary.

6. **How did eighty become fifty?**  
   The 1904 result lacks strong manuscript support, but the precise stage at which it entered Mathers’s copy remains uncertain.

7. **How early is the detailed Office-of-Spirits Belial?**  
   Plut. 89 sup. 38 gives a firm late-fifteenth-century terminus, but the underlying compilation may be older.

8. **Was “Brial roy” certainly Belial?**  
   The position and title favor identification, but the incomplete *Livre des Esperitz* entry prevents final proof.

9. **How much did Milton know of Weyer or Scot?**  
   Beautiful appearance, dignity, pleasant speech, and deception overlap, but shared biblical and demonological traditions could also explain the resemblance.

10. **Was Rudd the author of the angel–demon pairing?**  
    Harley 6483 proves the scheme existed in the Smart/Rudd manuscript complex. It does not prove that Thomas Rudd personally invented it.

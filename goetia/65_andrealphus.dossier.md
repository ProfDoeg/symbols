# Andrealphus: Research Dossier

> **Evidence labels**
>
> - **[Documented]** directly present in a named manuscript, printed edition, or other primary source.
> - **[Scholarly reconstruction]** a reasoned historical relationship proposed from manuscripts or textual parallels.
> - **[Tradition]** an inherited religious, magical, or occult claim whose historical transmission is documentable.
> - **[Esoteric correspondence]** a symbolic assignment within a particular occult table.
> - **[Disputed]** a claim for which sources, dating, identification, or interpretation conflict.
> - **[Legend]** a supernatural assertion reported as part of the tradition, not independently established.
> - **[Modern invention]** a recent creative elaboration not found in the historical source chain.
> - **[Negative finding]** a requested lead for which no reliable attestation was found.

Because extensive verbatim reproduction from individual online editions is restricted, the textual comparison below gives short exact excerpts followed by complete, clause-by-clause accounts of every substantive claim. Page and folio references identify where the complete entries can be checked.

## The name and its origins

### 1. The probable pre-Weyer witness: *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Documented; dating disputed]** A French spirit catalogue preserved as Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29 contains the list-form name:

> “de andralfas marquis”

This occurs on folio 179v of the *Livre des Esperitz*. The manuscript has been dated variously to the later fifteenth, late fifteenth/early sixteenth, or no earlier than the middle sixteenth century. It is therefore probably earlier than Weyer's 1577 printing, but the date is not secure enough to assign an exact year. Jean-Patrice Boudet treats the catalogue as an important predecessor of Renaissance demonological lists while warning about its difficult manuscript history. The manuscript catalogue record likewise preserves competing datings. [Text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier), [facsimile](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Livre_des_esperitz_-_MS_O.8.29.djvu), [Boudet](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/1019), [Biblissima manuscript record](https://portail.biblissima.fr/fr/ark:/43093/mdata454dde45506284fbdb223f1ab242101b25c2e133).

**[Disputed scholarly reconstruction]** The name *Andralfas* is not followed by its own properly headed description in the surviving text. Elsewhere, however, the manuscript describes a great marquis called **Tudiras hoho** who:

- appears as a beautiful maiden;
- gives wisdom in the sciences;
- can change a man into the manner of a bird;
- commands thirty-one legions.

The bird transformation and scientific instruction resemble the later Andrealphus closely. It is possible that headings and descriptions became displaced during copying, leaving *Andralfas* in the register but attaching part of its office to another name. This is a plausible reconstruction, not a demonstrated identity. The mismatch in name, sexed appearance, and legion count prevents treating the passages as simply identical.

### 2. Weyer, 1577

**[Documented]** Johann Weyer printed **Androalphus** as entry 55 of the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, appended to the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. This is the earliest securely dated printed attestation. Weyer said his catalogue came from a work he called *Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis…*. [Peterson’s Latin/English edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

The title *Liber officiorum spirituum* had already been mentioned by Johannes Trithemius in his 1508 *Antipalus maleficiorum*. That demonstrates the existence of a textual family or title, but not that Trithemius’s copy necessarily contained Androalphus.

### 3. Scot, 1584

**[Documented]** Reginald Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter 2, rendered the name **Andrealphus**. The translation is commonly associated with Abraham Fleming, although the exact division of responsibility within Scot’s compilation is not always recoverable.

Scot omitted Weyer’s fourth entry, Pruflas, entirely. Consequently, modern references that count down Scot’s unnumbered sequence sometimes call Andrealphus the fifty-fourth spirit. Peterson’s aligned edition retains Weyer’s number 55 for comparison. [Scot’s text](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/60766/pg60766-images.html), [parallel text](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### 4. Seventeenth- and eighteenth-century *Lemegeton* witnesses

Joseph Peterson’s manuscript collation records the following principal reading:

| Witness | Reading |

| Sloane 2731 | **Andrealphus** |
| Sloane 3648 | **Andrealphus** |

Sloane 3825 places the entry at folio 109v. Sloane 2731 is generally dated around 1687; Harley 6483 was copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713 or 1712–1714 and incorporates the material associated with Thomas Rudd. [Peterson’s *Goetia* transcription](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm), [manuscript introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm), [variant table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

Peterson’s broader apparatus also lists related readings:

- **Andriflis**, in SSM L.2.f.31;
- **Vuduch vel Andrielfis**, in a Florence witness;
- **Andralphus**, number 46 in a Trinity witness.

These are important for the name’s instability, but their relationship to *Andralfas* is philological rather than an established line of direct copying.

### 5. Mathers and Crowley, 1904

**[Documented]** *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King*, prepared from manuscript material by S. L. MacGregor Mathers and published by Aleister Crowley in 1904, calls the spirit **Andrealphus** in the main entry, number 65. Its rank table contains the form **Andrealphas**, apparently a typographical error rather than a separate being. [1904 scan](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf).

The typo has subsequently circulated as though it were an independent traditional spelling. It is better described as a print variant.

### 6. Peterson, 2001

**[Documented]** Joseph H. Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon* standardized the headword as **Andrealphus** while recording Weyer’s **Androalphus** and the manuscript evidence. The Andrealphus entry is on page 36 of the 2001 edition. [Edition scan](https://jamesparsonslakebucket.s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/text/Peterson_Joseph_H_The_Lesser_Key_of_Solomon.pdf), [publisher record](https://redwheelweiser.com/book/the-lesser-key-of-solomon-9781578632206/).

### Etymology

**[Negative finding]** No ancient god, hero, city, Greek daemon, or Semitic divine name has been securely identified behind *Andralfas/Androalphus/Andrealphus*.

That sharply distinguishes this name from comparatively transparent cases such as:

- Baal, from Northwest Semitic *baʿal*, “lord”;
- Astaroth, ultimately related to Astarte;
- Amon, often compared with Egyptian Amun;
- Asmoday, through Iranian/Jewish Ashmedai traditions;
- Belial, from Hebrew *bĕliyyaʿal*;
- Phoenix, from Greek and Latin *phoinix* traditions.

**[Disputed modern speculation]** Occult websites sometimes divide the name into Greek-looking elements such as *andr-* “man” and *alphos* “white,” “white spot,” or “leprosy,” or associate it with *andreios*, “manly.” No historical source in the chain offers this derivation, and no specialist philological study located in the contested-material search defends it. The variation *Andralfas → Androalphus → Andrealphus* is more plausibly a product of copying, Latinization, and print normalization, but even that does not explain the ultimate origin.

**[Modern conflation]** Andrealphus is sometimes identified with **Adrammelech** because both have acquired peacock imagery. That equation is unsupported. Adrammelech is a separate Biblical name in 2 Kings 17:31 and a separate entry in de Plancy. Peacock imagery alone does not establish identity.

## Number and order: Weyer’s 69 and the *Goetia*’s 72

**[Documented]** Weyer’s printed catalogue has 69 entries even though its ritual frame refers to 72 rulers. Androalphus is number **55**.

The *Ars Goetia* has 72 spirits and moves Andrealphus to number **65**. This is not merely the result of adding ten names ahead of him; much of Weyer's sequence was rearranged.

Relative to Weyer, the *Goetia*:

- adds **Vassago** as number 3;
- omits **Pruflas**, Weyer's number 4;
- adds **Seere**, **Dantalion**, and **Andromalius** as numbers 70–72;
- extensively reorders the shared spirits.

Thus 69 + four additions − one omission = 72, but the internal order is independently reshuffled. Peterson’s side-by-side concordance maps Weyer 55 Androalphus to *Goetia* 65 Andrealphus. [Concordance](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

Pruflas is the only Weyer spirit omitted from the *Goetia*. It is also absent from Scot’s rendering, which is one reason the English *Goetia* appears textually closer to Scot than directly to Weyer's Latin.

## The description across the sources

## Weyer’s Latin, 1577

Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, §55, original pagination page 928:

> “Androalphus Marchio magnus, apparens ut pavo: graves edit sonitus”

The complete entry then states, in sequence:

1. Androalphus is a great marquis.
2. He appears like a peacock.
3. He produces *graves sonitus*—literally deep, heavy, low, or grave sounds.
4. In human form he teaches geometry perfectly.
5. He teaches matters pertaining to measurement.
6. He makes a person acute or subtle in argument.
7. He makes that person knowledgeable in astronomy.
8. He transforms the same person into the form of a bird.
9. Thirty legions are subject to him.

**[Translation issue]** “Noisy peacock” is a fair summary but loses the ambiguity of *graves*: the Latin may suggest low-pitched or solemn sounds as much as loud commotion.

**[Legend]** The teaching and bodily transformation are magical offices asserted by the catalogue. They are not eyewitness reports independently supported outside the grimoire tradition.

## Scot’s English, 1584

Scot, *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, Book XV, chapter 2, page 390:

> “appearing as a pecocke, he raiseth great noises”

The complete English account gives:

1. Andrealphus is a great marquis.
2. He appears as a peacock.
3. He raises great noises.
4. In human form he perfectly teaches geometry.
5. He teaches everything belonging to admeasurement.
6. He makes a man a subtle disputant.
7. He makes him skilful in astronomy.
8. He transforms a man into the likeness of a bird.
9. Thirty legions are under him.

Scot’s “great noises” turns Weyer's *graves* into an unmistakable statement of magnitude. His *admeasurements* closely tracks *mensuram spectantia*.

Scot included the catalogue while arguing against credulous witch prosecution. Weyer likewise attacked the demonological theories used to justify executions. Their preservation of the material should not be confused with endorsement of every claim.

## Sloane 3825

**[Documented]** Sloane MS 3825, folio 109v, begins the entry as the sixty-fifth spirit. Its exact surviving phrase includes:

> “he can Transforme a Man into y[e] likness of a Bird”

The manuscript calls him a mighty great marquis and preserves the following order:

1. he first appears as a peacock;
2. he makes great noises;
3. after a time he adopts human shape;
4. he teaches geometry perfectly;
5. he teaches measuring;
6. he teaches astronomy;
7. he makes a person subtle or cunning in these subjects;
8. he transforms a man into a bird’s likeness;
9. he governs thirty legions;
10. his seal is to be worn as a lamen.

The manuscript does **not** say that the conjurer must explicitly demand the human form. “After a time” is the stated transition. Modern descriptions that say the magician commands the second form are interpretive harmonizations with general conjuration practice.

## Sloane 2731

Sloane 2731 transmits substantially the same Andrealphus description and the regular spelling **Andrealphus**. Differences are chiefly scribal spelling, punctuation, and the execution of the seal, rather than different offices.

## Harley 6483 and Rudd’s material

Harley 6483 likewise preserves Andrealphus but embeds the spirit in a more elaborately Christian-angelic apparatus. The demon’s circle and seal are supplemented by the corresponding angel, divine names, and a Psalm verse. These additions do not occur in Weyer or Scot and should not be projected back into the sixteenth-century entry.

## Mathers–Crowley, 1904

Mathers–Crowley, page 32:

> “appearing at first in the form of a Peacock, with great Noises”

The entry then states that:

- he is a mighty marquis;
- after a time he assumes human shape;
- he teaches geometry perfectly;
- he makes people subtle in geometry and matters of mensuration or astronomy;
- he transforms a man into the likeness of a bird;
- he governs thirty legions;
- the accompanying figure is his seal.

The word “therein” can make the sentence sound as if subtlety applies only to geometry. Weyer and Scot more plainly give him the office of making a person a subtle disputant.

Crowley’s volume added prefatory, ritual, and Enochian material, including an Enochian rendering of conjurations. The Enochian language is a new ritual overlay; the book does not identify Andrealphus with a particular Enochian governor.

## Peterson, 2001

Peterson’s edition follows Sloane 3825 closely and records variant spellings and manuscript readings. It is a critical presentation of the manuscript tradition, not a new revelation about the spirit.

### Offices not attributed to Andrealphus

**[Negative finding]** The primary entries do **not** attribute to Andrealphus:

- languages;
- rhetoric in general, beyond subtle disputation;
- treasure finding;
- love or sexual influence;
- familiars;
- invisibility;
- metallic transmutation;
- true answers to past, present, future, divine, or hidden questions;
- reconciliation or discord;
- weather control;
- ice powers.

Those offices belong to other spirits or to modern fictional adaptations.

## Rank, planet, metal, and time

### Marquis

**[Documented]** Every principal source calls Andrealphus a marquis: *marchio magnus*, great marquis, or mighty marquis. He is never a king, duke, prince, president, earl/count, or knight in the historical catalogue.

### Moon and silver

**[Esoteric correspondence]** The later *Goetia*’s “Observations” associate the ranks with planetary metals. Marquis corresponds to:

- **planet:** Moon;
- **metal:** silver.

This is a correspondence of office, not an astronomical statement found in Andrealphus’s entry. [Goetic rank rules](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks06.htm).

### Hours

Scot’s general rule says marquises may be bound from the ninth hour until compline and from compline until the end of the day. Under an equinoctial or rough clock conversion, the ninth hour is approximately 3 p.m.; seasonal hours vary with daylight.

The later printed *Goetia* divides marquis operations into approximately:

- 3 p.m.–9 p.m.;
- 9 p.m.–sunrise.

It also recommends waxing lunar dates—the second, fourth, sixth, eighth, tenth, twelfth, or fourteenth day of the Moon. These are general ritual instructions, not a time unique to Andrealphus. They coexist awkwardly with a purely lunar planetary-hour scheme and should not be silently rewritten into “Monday only.”

## The seal

### Origin and transmission

**[Documented]**

- Weyer gives no seal.
- Scot gives no seal.
- The surviving *Lemegeton* manuscripts supply one.
- Sloane 3825 places it after the entry at folio 109v.
- Mathers–Crowley prints a standardized version in 1904.
- Harley 6483 places the figure in Rudd’s angelically fortified arrangement.

The [Sloane/Peterson image](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g65.jpg) and [1904 public-domain reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:65-Andrealphus_seal.png) should be treated as the authoritative visual references.

### Description

The ordinary Andrealphus seal is an abstract black linear character, not a peacock portrait. It consists of a compact interlacing arrangement of curved and straight strokes, with hooked or cross-like terminals and an approximately balanced central structure. Its meaning is not explained in the manuscripts.

Manuscript copies vary in:

- curvature and length of strokes;
- placement of intersections;
- relative width and height;
- terminal crosses or hooks;
- whether the glyph stands alone or inside a containing circle.

Harley 6483 is distinctive because its spirit figures are enclosed or accompanied by circles carrying names in Roman and pointed Hebrew lettering. The corresponding angelic device and Psalm versicle form an additional protective layer.

**[Negative finding]** No early source explains the seal through gematria, planetary kamea construction, peacock anatomy, geometry, or notarikon. Such derivations are modern conjectures unless independently demonstrated.

## The angel: Damabiah

## Construction of the seventy-two names

### Exodus 14:19–21

**[Tradition]** Each of the three Hebrew verses Exodus 14:19, 14:20, and 14:21 contains seventy-two letters in the Masoretic consonantal text. The later construction writes:

1. the first verse in its ordinary direction;
2. the second in reverse;
3. the third in its ordinary direction;

then reads the resulting columns to produce seventy-two three-letter combinations.

The sixty-fifth triplet is:

**דמב — D-M-B**

Adding the divine suffix **יה**, *Yah*, produces:

**דמביה — DMBYH — Damabiah**

Alternative Romanizations include Damabiah, Damabia, and Damabyah.

### What “Shem ha-Mephorash” originally meant

The rabbinic expression *shem ha-meforash*, “the explicit/distinguished name,” originally usually referred to the specially pronounced divine name, particularly the Tetragrammaton. Its application to the collection of seventy-two triplets is a later usage. Rashi preserves discussion of a seventy-two-letter divine name, while medieval texts elaborate its construction. [Rashi on Sukkah 45a](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Sukkah.45).

### Medieval and Renaissance transmission

**[Documented tradition]**

- The *Sefer ha-Bahir*, compiled in medieval Provence, is among the earliest extant texts to explain the three-verse construction.
- *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh*, a medieval magical-kabbalistic compilation printed in Amsterdam in 1701, transmitted the seventy-twofold name in practical angelological settings.
- Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) helped Christian kabbalists turn the triplets into pronounceable angel names by adding forms of *-El* and *-Yah*.
- Blaise de Vigenère continued this Christian-kabbalistic reception.
- Athanasius Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) presented expansive tables connecting the names with languages, nations, angels, and cosmology.
- Rudd’s system belongs to this early-modern Christian appropriation, but no simple direct line from every earlier author to Harley 6483 has been demonstrated.

Joshua Trachtenberg’s history of Jewish magic summarizes the three-verse construction and observes that printed *Sefer Raziel* includes the full name while pronunciation remained unstable. [Trachtenberg](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm).

## Damabiah’s meaning

**[Tradition, not literal etymology]** Lenain gives:

> “Dieu fontaine de sagesse”

That is, **“God, fountain of wisdom.”**

The phrase is an assigned divine attribute. The three consonants D-M-B do not transparently spell “fountain of wisdom” in Hebrew, so it should not be presented as a literal dictionary translation.

## Damabiah in Rudd’s scheme

**[Documented]** Harley 6483 pairs spirit 65, Andrealphus, with angel 65, **Damabiah**. Stephen Skinner and David Rankine reproduce and analyze this system in *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (2007).

Rudd’s arrangement uses the angel as a celestial authority over the corresponding spirit. The working apparatus includes:

- the demon’s seal;
- the demon’s Roman and Hebrew name;
- the ruling Shem angel’s name;
- an angelic counter-seal or identifying device;
- a Psalm verse;
- divine names and protective circles.

**[Tradition]** The underlying magical theory is that a demon cannot safely resist the angelic name or Psalm assigned above it.

**[Historical caution]** This does not show that Damabiah and Andrealphus were paired in antiquity, medieval Judaism, Weyer, or the earliest *Livre des Esperitz*. It is an early-modern Christian ceremonial synthesis.

## Authorship dispute

Harley 6483 was copied by **Peter Smart** in the early eighteenth century. It attributes or preserves material associated with **Thomas Rudd**.

Skinner and Rankine argue that the underlying system descends from Rudd’s mid-seventeenth-century work, possibly around 1655–1656. Critics point to the late date of the actual manuscript and its evident use of already circulating Solomonic, Dee, and kabbalistic materials.

The careful formulation is therefore:

- **Harley 6483 is certainly an early-eighteenth-century witness.**
- **Its derivation from Thomas Rudd is traditional and textually argued, but not established by an autograph manuscript.**

## Lenain, 1823

Lazare Républicain Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* assigns Damabiah:

- number 65;
- 321st through 325th degrees of the zodiac;
- therefore 20°–25° Aquarius in modern notation;
- the thirty-third decan and lunar genius Ptebiou;
- the invocation interval 9:20–9:40 p.m.;
- calendrical points 23 May, 5 August, 14 October, 25 December, and 7 March;
- protection against sorcery;
- wisdom and success in useful undertakings;
- authority over seas, rivers, springs, maritime expeditions, naval construction, sailors, pilots, fisheries, and maritime trade.

The contrary or “bad” genius is credited with storms, shipwrecks, and unsuccessful expeditions. These are Damabiah traditions and should not be transferred automatically to Andrealphus.

### Psalm discrepancy

Lenain identifies the Psalm as the fifteenth verse of Psalm 89, but prints the Latin corresponding to:

**Vulgate Psalm 89:13 / Hebrew and modern English Psalm 90:13**—a petition asking the Lord to return and show compassion to his servants.

Thus the verse text identifies Psalm 90:13 in most modern Bibles; Lenain’s verse number is internally inconsistent. [Lenain scan, pp. 90–91](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf), [Psalm 90:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+90%3A13&version=KJV).

## Papus, Ambelain, and Haziel

**Papus.** Gérard Encausse’s expanded *La Cabbale* reproduced and popularized much of the French occult-kabbalistic table associated with Lenain. His role is dissemination, not the invention of Damabiah. [Papus edition](https://www.cercle-papus.com/ouvrages/papus-la-cabbale.pdf).

**Robert Ambelain.** *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reorganized angelic names, seals, prayers, and practical operations for a twentieth-century readership. Ambelain belongs to a later operative synthesis, not the source of Rudd’s pairing. [Bibliographic record](https://edition-originale.com/fr/oeuvres/religions-and-spiritualites-8/esoterisme-and-spiritisme-38/ambelain-la-kabbale-pratique-1951-44828), [English translation](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf).

**Haziel.** François Bernard-Termès, writing as Haziel, presented Damabiah as the guardian angel of approximately 10–14 February and called it a fountain of love or wisdom. This is late-twentieth-century popular angelology based on the French occult calendar, not medieval Jewish doctrine. [Haziel, *Notre ange gardien existe*](https://www.librairieleauvive.com/index.php/product/show/9782850901003/notre-ange-gardien-existe).

### The apparent contradictions explained

#### Damabiah in Lenain

Lenain assigns each angel a five-degree segment in a continuous zodiacal sequence. For number 65, that produces **20°–25° Aquarius**.

#### Damabiah in Golden Dawn tarot tables

The Golden Dawn assigns two Shem angels to each thirty-sixfold tarot decan. In *Book T* and Crowley’s *Liber 78*, Damabiah and Manakel govern the **Ten of Swords**, Sun in Gemini. Damabiah occupies the first half, 20°–25° Gemini. [Crowley, *Liber 78*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/libers/lib78).

This is not a correction of Lenain; it is a different indexing scheme.

#### Andrealphus in the Goetic decan table

The later Crowley-derived Goetic overlay places two demons on each ten-degree tarot pip, one diurnal and one nocturnal. Andrealphus is the night spirit for **10°–20° Capricorn**, conventionally about 31 December–9 January, and therefore shares the **Three of Disks** with Astaroth. The assignment is reproduced explicitly in DuQuette and Hyatt’s *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*, page 204. [Illustrated edition](https://pauladaunt.com/books/DuQuette%20-%20Aleister%20Crowleys%20Illustrated%20Goetia.pdf).

The planet of that tarot card is Mars in Capricorn, while the planet assigned to Andrealphus’s **rank** is the Moon. They answer different symbolic questions and should not be merged.

### Enochian and later overlays

Crowley incorporated Enochian material into the 1904 book’s conjurations. Later ceremonial-magical systems, particularly Lon Milo DuQuette’s tarot work, deliberately combine:

- Goetic spirits;
- Enochian tablets;
- zodiacal decans;
- tarot pips;
- Shem angels;
- planetary and elemental color scales.

These are creative synthetic correspondences. No seventeenth-century manuscript calls Andrealphus an Enochian spirit or assigns him a Dee-style name.

## Iconography

## The peacock

**[Documented textual image]** The peacock appears in Weyer and remains constant through Scot, the *Lemegeton*, Mathers–Crowley, and de Plancy. Its significance is never explained in the primary entries.

Later writers have associated the peacock with:

- pride and display;
- the “eyes” of its tail and astronomical vision;
- immortality;
- celestial geometry;
- the allegedly harsh call of an otherwise beautiful bird.

These are symbolic interpretations, not source-text explanations.

## Collin de Plancy

### 1818 first edition

**[Negative finding]** The first edition of Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* does not contain a separate Androalphus entry. Its absence matters because references often cite “de Plancy 1818” generically for material introduced only in later editions. [1818 text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/1re_%C3%A9dition,_1818/Tome_1/Texte_entier).

### 1863 sixth edition

The sixth edition, page 32, begins:

> “Androalphus, puissant démon, marquis de l’empire infernal”

De Plancy reports that Androalphus:

- appears as a peacock;
- has a grave voice;
- assumes human form;
- teaches geometry;
- makes a person a clever disputant and astronomer;
- can change someone into a bird;
- commands thirty legions.

He adds the sardonic comment that the transformation can be useful for escaping the claws of judges. That legal-evasion gloss is de Plancy’s embellishment rather than a statement in Weyer. He cites Wier/Weyer as his authority. [1863 entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.,_1863/Androalphus), [scan](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireinfe00coll_2).

### Did Louis Le Breton draw Andrealphus?

**[Negative finding]** No identifiable Louis Le Breton engraving of Androalphus appears among the illustrated plates of the 1863 edition.

The familiar peacock-bodied image sometimes labeled “Andrealphus from de Plancy” is usually:

- a modern drawing;
- the separate **Adrammelech** engraving;
- or an uncredited image influenced by both.

Therefore de Plancy supplies a textual entry but not a verified Breton portrait of this spirit.

## Modern art

David P. Wilson illustrated the spirit for *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia*. Numerous contemporary occult decks depict an anthropomorphic peacock, mathematician, astronomer, or peacock-winged noble. These works visualize the textual offices but do not constitute manuscript iconography.

## People and works

### Johann Weyer

Weyer was a physician and former student of Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa. His *De praestigiis daemonum* opposed the demonological arguments used to prosecute supposed witches. The *Pseudomonarchia* exposed a spirit hierarchy while deliberately omitting or disrupting some operational instructions. His position combined belief in spirits with skepticism about claims made against accused witches.

### Reginald Scot

Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft* likewise attacked fraudulent conjurers and the persecution of alleged witches. Book XV nevertheless preserved English versions of magical texts, allowing Weyer's catalogue to enter English ceremonial-magic transmission.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Rudd is traditionally associated with the angelically fortified *Goetia*. Peter Smart is the identified copyist of Harley 6483. The historical Thomas Rudd connection is plausible but contested because the surviving manuscript is later than Rudd’s lifetime.

### Mathers and Crowley

Mathers prepared a translation or edition from British manuscripts by the late nineteenth century. Crowley published it in 1904, supplied the preface and “Preliminary Invocation,” reframed the work through his magical theories, and added Enochian material.

### Arthur Edward Waite

Waite summarized Andrealphus and reproduced the seal in *The Book of Ceremonial Magic* (1913), page 217. His work helped move the entry from specialist grimoire circulation into broader occult reference literature. [Waite scan](https://www.junkybooks.com/administrator/thebooks/687767834f448-the-book-of-ceremonial-magic-the-secret-tradition-in-go-tia-including-the-rites-and-mysteries-of-go-tic-theurgy-sorcery-and-infernal-necromancy-by-arthur-edward-waite.pdf).

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition collated the principal manuscripts and distinguished the *Lemegeton* readings from Weyer, Scot, and later editors. His online editions are particularly valuable because they reproduce Latin, early English, manuscript spellings, and concordances instead of silently harmonizing them.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their *The Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard, 2007) made Harley 6483’s angel-demon apparatus widely accessible, supplemented from Harley 6482, Sloane 3824, and Wellcome 3203. It is the central published source for examining Damabiah’s operative role over Andrealphus. [WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/The-Goetia-of-Dr-Rudd-%3A-the-angels-and-demons-of-Liber-malorum-spirituum-seu-Goetia-Lemegeton-clavicula-Salomonis-%3A-with-a-study-of-the-techniques-of-evocation-in-the-context-of-the-angel-magic-tradition-of-the-seventeenth-century-%3A-Being-a-transcription-of-Dr.-Rudd%27s-%22Liber-malorum-spirituum-seu-Goetia%22-from-Harley-MS-6483-with-other-pertinent-extracts-from-manuscripts-Harley-MS-6482-Sloane-MS-3824-and-Wellcome-MS-3203/oclc/637468388).

### Lon Milo DuQuette and Christopher Hyatt

Their *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* reproduces the later zodiacal, tarot, planetary, and metal assignments. It is useful evidence for modern ceremonial practice but not for reconstructing the sixteenth-century text.

### Poke Runyon

Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* and Church of the Hermetic Sciences materials promote a ritual system combining the 72 spirits with Shem angels and zodiacal sectors. His arrangement is an influential modern operative synthesis. No independently documented Andrealphus evocation by Runyon was found. [Runyon’s site](https://www.pokerunyon.com/blog).

### Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Pandemonium* compares spirit catalogues and emphasizes the continuity between continental spirit lists and the later *Goetia*. It is relevant to the catalogue’s genealogy, but no unique historical Andrealphus operation attributable to him was located. [Publisher record](https://hadean.press/products/pandemonium).

### Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No Andrealphus-specific treatment was found in the principal searchable works of Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad. His association with Crowley and Qabalistic number systems is not evidence that he wrote about this spirit.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Does Andrealphus appear before Weyer?

**Finding:** probably yes as **Andralfas**, but not beyond dispute.

- The *Livre des Esperitz* contains the name.
- Its manuscript likely predates 1577.
- Its exact dating ranges from the later fifteenth to the mid-sixteenth century.
- The associated description is displaced or absent.
- Weyer remains the first securely dated printed description under a clearly recognizable name.

### 2. Is Andrealphus number 54, 55, or 65?

All three numbers can appear for different reasons:

- **55:** Weyer's numbered catalogue.
- **54:** a secondary count of Scot after his omission of Pruflas.
- **65:** the *Ars Goetia*.

Calling 54 “Weyer’s number” is an error.

### 3. Why 69 versus 72?

The *Pseudomonarchia* contains 69 entries despite speaking of 72 rulers. The *Goetia* reaches 72 by adding Vassago, Seere, Dantalion, and Andromalius while dropping Pruflas, and also rearranges the order. No surviving authorial explanation establishes whether Weyer's missing three were lost, deliberately suppressed, or never present in his copy.

### 4. Is Andrealphas a second name?

Probably not. It occurs in the Mathers–Crowley rank list while the main entry has Andrealphus. It is best classified as a typographical or inflectional accident that later writers repeated.

### 5. Is Andrealphus an ancient god?

No supporting evidence was found. Greek and Semitic etymologies circulating online are speculative. The earliest recoverable form is a grimoire name, not an independently attested deity.

### 6. Is Andrealphus Adrammelech?

No. The equation rests primarily on peacock imagery and name similarity. The source traditions keep them separate.

### 7. Is Andrealphus Andras or Andromalius?

No.

- **Andras** is a separate marquis, number 63 in the *Goetia*, associated with discord and killing.
- **Andromalius** is a separate earl, number 72, concerned with thieves and stolen goods.
- Similar initial syllables do not indicate identity.

### 8. Does *Tudiras hoho* preserve the lost Andralfas description?

Possibly, but not certainly. The shared transformation into a bird and instruction in sciences are strong parallels. The different name, female manifestation, and thirty-one rather than thirty legions are serious obstacles. The safest label is **disputed textual displacement**.

### 9. Is Damabiah intrinsically Andrealphus’s opposite?

Only within Rudd’s correspondence.

The angelic name is medieval in construction; the demon name belongs to a different catalogue tradition. No Jewish source pairs Damabiah with Andrealphus, and no Weyer-era evidence makes them primordial adversaries.

### 10. Which zodiac sign belongs to Andrealphus?

There is no single ancient answer:

- Aquarius 20°–25° belongs to **Damabiah in Lenain**.
- Gemini 20°–25° belongs to **Damabiah in the Golden Dawn tarot arrangement**.
- Capricorn 10°–20° belongs to **Andrealphus in the later Goetic tarot/decan arrangement**.

Websites that assign Aquarius directly to Andrealphus often combine Lenain, Rudd, and modern Runyon-style practice. Websites assigning Gemini may have imported the *Book T* angelic table. Crowley-derived Goetic tables give Capricorn.

### 11. Did de Plancy commission a portrait?

No verified portrait was found. The 1863 dictionary contains an entry, but Androalphus is not among the identifiable Le Breton plates. Adrammelech’s engraving is frequently relabeled.

### 12. Was the seal constructed from Hebrew or geometry?

Unknown. The sigil survives as a copied visual character. No manuscript explanation of its construction was found.

## Open questions

1. **How early is Trinity MS O.8.29?** A firmer palaeographical and codicological consensus would determine how confidently *Andralfas* can be called a fifteenth-century name.

2. **Where is Andralfas’s missing description?** The *Tudiras hoho* hypothesis deserves a full examination of quire order, headings, scribal hands, and the surrounding register.

3. **What did Weyer's *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* contain?** The lost or dispersed source might explain the name’s change from *Andralfas* to *Androalphus* and the shift to thirty legions.

4. **How are Andriflis and Andrielfis related?** These may reflect a very unstable common exemplar, independent corruption, or different spirits later conflated by editors.

5. **When were the seals first added?** The absence of sigils from Weyer and Scot shows that the familiar Andrealphus seal is not part of their printed tradition, but the earliest exact manuscript stage of its creation remains unclear.

6. **How much of Harley 6483 is genuinely Rudd’s?** The manuscript’s copyist and date are known; the dating and authorship of the system behind it remain inferential.

7. **Who first united the Rudd pairing with zodiacal quinances and tarot pips?** Rudd, Lenain, Golden Dawn, Crowley, and twentieth-century writers use related sets of 72 while ordering them differently. Modern charts often conceal the joins.

## Conclusions

**[Documented]** Andrealphus is a grimoire marquis first securely described by Weyer in 1577 as **Androalphus**, probably preceded by the name **Andralfas** in the *Livre des Esperitz*. The stable description is unusually narrow: a noisy or deep-voiced peacock, later human, who teaches geometry, mensuration, and astronomy, makes a person subtle in argument, transforms a person into a bird, and rules thirty legions.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The name and perhaps part of the description descend from the French and Latin *Officiorum Spirituum* catalogue family. No ancient god or classical personage has been demonstrated behind it.

**[Tradition]** The seventeenth-century *Goetia* supplied the seal and fixed the spirit at number 65. Rudd’s system placed Damabiah over it as an angelic constraint.

**[Esoteric correspondence]** Moon and silver derive from the marquis rank; Capricorn and the Three of Disks derive from a later Goetic tarot table. Damabiah’s Aquarius and Gemini quinances belong to separate angelic arrangements.

**[Negative finding]** There is no de Plancy/Breton portrait, no established ancient etymology, and no verified Andrealphus appearance in Dante, Milton, Bartimaeus, *Hellboy*, *Shin Megami Tensei*, or *Persona*.

**[Modern invention]** The lust prince of *In Nomine*, the ice-wielding aristocrat of *Helluva Boss*, and modern tarot, game, and angel-calendar versions creatively adapt the name. They document Andrealphus’s continuing cultural life, not the content of the early grimoire.

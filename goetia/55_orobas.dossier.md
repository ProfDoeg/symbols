# Orobas: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes the following categories:

- **Documented text** — stated in a named manuscript or printed primary source.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — an identification or historical relationship argued from textual evidence.
- **Tradition** — a claim inherited within Jewish, Christian, or magical literature without independent historical verification.
- **Esoteric correspondence** — an attribution created within a magical system, such as planetary, zodiacal, angelic, or tarot correspondences.
- **Disputed claim** — an interpretation for which evidence is incomplete or contradictory.
- **Legend** — a reported supernatural event or traditional narrative, reproduced without judgment as to its reality.
- **Modern invention** — a demonstrably later elaboration absent from the historical source chain.
- **Negative finding** — a claimed connection for which no attestation was found in the sources consulted.

The principal textual line is:

> fifteenth-century *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* family → Weyer, 1577 → Scot, 1584 → seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia* manuscripts → Rudd/Smart manuscripts, 1699–1713 → Mathers–Crowley, 1904 → modern critical and practical editions.

The most important warning is that two different astrological systems are involved:

1. **Mebahiah, angel 55:** 0°–5° Capricorn.
2. **Orobas in Crowley’s Goetic table:** 0°–10° Libra, night, Two of Swords.

They do not coincide because one is a direct ordinal pairing of 72 angels and demons; the other folds two sets of 36 Goetic spirits over the 36 decans.

# The name and its origins

## The spellings across the source chain

Joseph Peterson’s manuscript-comparison table gives the following readings:

| Source | Date | Number/read­ing |

The important result is unusual stability. Many Goetic names are substantially corrupted between witnesses; **Orobas remains Orobas throughout every principal witness requested by the brief**. Peterson’s complete comparison is available in his [Goetic spirit-name table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia_spirits.pdf).

### The Florence precursor

**Scholarly reconstruction.** A related magical catalogue in Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana MS Plut. 89 Sup. 38, dated January 1494, has at position 24:

> *Arabas vel Accabas vel Irabas, magnus princeps, 20 leg.*

That is, “Arabas, or Accabas, or Irabas, a great prince, [ruling] twenty legions.”

Jean-Patrice Boudet placed this entry beside Weyer’s:

> *Orobas, magnus princeps, 20 lég.*

The matching rank, legion count, position within a related *Officium spirituum* catalogue, and partial graphic similarity make the identification highly plausible. It remains a reconstruction because the Florence witness does not actually read *Orobas*. See Boudet’s comparative study, [“Les who’s who démonologiques de la Renaissance et leurs ancêtres médiévaux”](https://journals.openedition.org/medievales/pdf/1019).

A roughly contemporary or slightly later English *Office of Spirits* witness, Folger MS V.b.26, uses **Orobas**. Its compilation is often dated around 1583, after Weyer’s printed edition but from a source family that is demonstrably older than Weyer.

### Earliest-attestation verdict

- **Earliest close precursor:** *Arabas / Accabas / Irabas*, Florence, January 1494.
- **Earliest secure attestation of the exact spelling Orobas:** Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, fifth edition, 1577.
- **Possible independent late-sixteenth-century witness:** Folger MS V.b.26.
- **No exact Orobas was found in the *Livre des Esperitz*, Munich Manual, *Testament of Solomon*, or *Book of Abramelin*.**

Thus the statement “Orobas has no pre-Weyer history” is too strong if it means the character and office; the Florence catalogue supplies a probable precursor. It is defensible if restricted to the **exact spelling** *Orobas*.

## Etymology

### The Latin–Greek incense proposal

**Disputed claim.** The most frequently repeated proposal derives *Orobas* from Latin *orobias*, borrowed from Greek ὀροβίας. Lewis and Short define *orobias* as a kind of incense in small, chickpea-like grains. Pliny describes the smaller lumps of frankincense under this name. Philemon Holland’s early English Pliny renders the phrase:

> “the lesser gobbets they name Orobias.”

See [Pliny, *Natural History* XII](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/holland/pliny12.html) and the [Lewis and Short entry](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn%3Acite2%3Ascaife-viewer%3Adictionary-entries.atlas_v1%3Alat.ls.perseus-eng2-n33034/).

Evidence in favor:

- *Orobas* and *orobias* are graphically close.
- Incense was intrinsic to ritual conjuration, making an incense term conceivable in a spirit catalogue.

Evidence against:

- No manuscript or early commentator derives the name this way.
- The 1494 forms *Arabas, Accabas, Irabas* point toward textual corruption from another archetype rather than transparent borrowing from *orobias*.
- Weyer provides no etymological comment.
- The omitted *i* and altered ending are unexplained.

The proposal should therefore be recorded as an attractive modern conjecture, not an established origin.

### Proposed ancient deities, heroes, and places

**Negative finding.** No convincing ancient god, hero, tribe, or place lies behind Orobas in the way that recognizable names may underlie Bael/Baal, Astaroth/Astarte, Amon/Amun, Asmoday/Asmodeus–Ashmedai, Belial/*Bĕliyyaʿal*, or Phenex/Phoenix.

Claims connecting Orobas to any of the following were found only in unsourced modern discussions:

- Ahura Mazda/Oromazes, because both are associated with truth;
- Epona or other horse deities;
- Resheph or horseman gods;
- the Jewish heaven *ʿAravot/Araboth*;
- Greek *oros*, “mountain”;
- the Orobii, an ancient people;
- Horus;
- an otherwise unknown “ancient horse oracle.”

These are **modern speculative comparisons**. No phonological sequence, intermediary text, cultic continuity, or early author was located.

The resemblance between *Orobas* and *Oromazes* is particularly weak. Collin de Plancy places separate entries for **Orobas** and **Oromaze/Ormuzd** next to one another, showing that he regarded them as different beings.

## Number 58 versus 55

Weyer’s catalogue contains 69 named spirits. The *Ars Goetia* contains 72.

The *Goetia* adds four spirits absent from Weyer:

1. Vassago, number 3;
2. Seere, number 70;
3. Dantalion, number 71;
4. Andromalius, number 72.

It omits Weyer’s number 4, **Pruflas**, also called **Bufas** in derivative sources.

The arithmetic is therefore:

> 69 − 1 + 4 = 72.

But the change from Weyer’s Orobas 58 to Goetic Orobas 55 is not produced by a single three-place shift. The seventeenth-century compiler also rearranged parts of the catalogue. The first two names remain Bael and Agares; Vassago is inserted at 3, Pruflas disappears, and numerous later spirits move to positions that do not preserve Weyer's exact sequence.

**Scholarly reconstruction.** The choice of 72 was almost certainly attractive because 72 was already a powerful number in Jewish and Christian magic, including the 72-fold divine name. That does not prove that the compiler originally created the list to match the Shem angels. Rudd’s explicit one-to-one counterbalancing is a later layer.

# The description across the sources

## Weyer, *Pseudomonarchia daemonum*, 1577

**Documented text.** Weyer’s fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum* appended the expanded *Pseudomonarchia*. Orobas is number 58, on printed page 929 in the 1577 edition. Peterson’s Latin transcription reads:

> **Orobas magnus Princeps: procedit equo conformis: hominis autem indutus idolum, de virtute divina loquitur: vera dat responsa de præteritis, præsentibus, futuris, de divinitate & creatione: neminem decipit, nec tentari sinit: confert prælaturas & dignitates, amicorum item & hostium favorem. Præsidet legionibus viginti.**

A close translation is:

> Orobas, a great Prince, comes forth in the likeness of a horse. Having assumed the image of a man, however, he speaks of divine power. He gives true answers concerning past, present and future things, divinity and creation. He deceives no one and permits no one to be tempted. He confers prelacies and dignities, and the favor of friends and enemies. He presides over twenty legions.

Source: [Peterson’s Weyer transcription and notes](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm).

### Points established by Weyer

- **Rank:** *magnus Princeps*, “great Prince.”
- **First form:** horse-like.
- **Second form:** an assumed human image.
- **Speech:** “of divine power,” *de virtute divina*.
- **Divination:** true past, present, and future answers.
- **Cosmological answers:** divinity and creation.
- **Truthfulness:** *neminem decipit*, “he deceives no one.”
- **Protection:** *nec tentari sinit*, “nor does he permit [one] to be tempted.”
- **Social and ecclesiastical advancement:** prelacies and dignities.
- **Influence:** favor of friends and enemies.
- **Legions:** twenty.
- **Absent:** no seal, metal, zodiacal sector, tarot card, named angel, or personal direction.

The word *idolum* here means an image, likeness, or visible form. Reading it as “idol” in the modern sense can misleadingly suggest that Orobas inhabits a cult statue.

## Reginald Scot, *The Discoverie of Witchcraft*, 1584

Scot incorporated an English rendering of Weyer’s catalogue into Book XVI, chapter II. Peterson cautions that this part may have been translated by Abraham Fleming rather than Scot personally. In the original pagination the passage is on approximately p.225.

> **Orobas is a great prince, he commeth foorth like a horsse, but when he putteth on him a mans idol [=image], he talketh of divine vertue, he giveth true answers of things present, past, and to come, and of the divinitie, and of the creation, he deceiveth none, nor suffereth anie to be tempted, he giveth dignities and prelacies, and the favour of freends and foes, and hath rule over twentie legions.**

Source: [Scot, Book XVI](https://esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm); an original 1584 scan is catalogued by the [Cornell Digital Witchcraft Collection](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/witchcraft081).

Scot preserves virtually every element of Weyer. His “a mans idol” is a very literal translation of *hominis … idolum*. Later English Goetias clarify this as “the Image of a man.”

### Scot’s authorial context

**Documented historical context.** Scot reproduced magical material as part of a skeptical attack on witch persecution, conjuring fraud, credulity, and demonological excess. Inclusion in the *Discoverie* is therefore not evidence that Scot endorsed practical demon evocation. Ironically, his English transcription became one of the principal channels through which Weyer’s catalogue entered practical English grimoires.

## The seventeenth-century *Ars Goetia*

### Sloane MS 3825

Peterson treats Sloane 3825 as one of the most accurate principal witnesses. Its entry, fol. 108v, reproduced on p.32 of Peterson’s 2001 edition, reads:

> **The 55th spirit is called Orobas, he is a mighty great prince, appearing at firist like a horse, But afterwards at ye command of ye Exorcist he putteth on ye Image of a man, his office is to discover, all Things past, present & to come and to give dignities & places & ye favour of friends & foes, he giveth true answares of divinity & of ye Creation of ye world, he is faithfull to ye Exorcist & will not suffer him to be Tempted by any spirit he governeth 20 Legions of spirits, his seal is this, wch must be made & worne as a Lamin &c.**

Source: [Peterson’s online *Goetia*](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm) and Joseph H. Peterson, ed., *The Lesser Key of Solomon* (Weiser, 2001), p.32.

The significant variant is **“places.”** Peterson notes that Weyer, Scot, and other English witnesses read **prelaces**, meaning prelacies. “Places” is probably a copying error rather than a new office.

### Sloane MS 2731

Sloane 2731 reads:

> **The 55 spirit is Called Orobas; he is a Mighty Great Prince; Appearing at first like a horse; but after at the Command of the Exorcist he Puteth on the Image of a man; his office is to discover all things past Present & to Come, and to Give Dignities and Prelacies; and the favour of friends and foes; he Giveth true Answers of Divinity and of the Creation of the world; he is faithfull to the Exorcist, and will not suffer him to be Tempted by Any spirit; he Governeth 20 Legions of spirits his seal is this which must be made and worne as a Lamin before the Exorcist; &c.**

Source: [digital Sloane 2731 transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/).

This restores **prelacies**, preserves twenty legions, and explicitly says the lamen is worn before the exorcist.

### What the Goetia changed

Compared with Weyer and Scot, the *Goetia*:

- numbers Orobas 55 rather than 58;
- makes the transformation occur specifically “at the command of the Exorcist”;
- changes “he deceives no one” into “he is faithful to the Exorcist”;
- specifies temptation “by any spirit”;
- adds a seal and an instruction to wear it as a lamen;
- otherwise preserves the older office closely.

The Goetic wording is therefore not an independent revelation. It is a seventeenth-century revision of the earlier catalogue, probably mediated substantially through Scot’s English.

## Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

Harley 6483, copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713, titles the work *Liber Malorum Spirituum seu Goetia*. The manuscript was presented as material from the papers of “Dr Rudd,” traditionally Thomas Rudd. It is not an autograph by Rudd, and modern scholarship treats the precise authorship and provenance cautiously.

Specific variants include:

- **Name:** Orobas.
- **Rank and appearance:** substantially the normal Goetic reading.
- **Legions:** apparently **26** rather than twenty in this witness; Peterson marks it as the minority Harley reading.
- **Angelic apparatus:** a paired Shem angel, divine verse, and seal are added.
- **Copying error:** the angelic seal at Orobas repeats the seal of the 53rd angel, Nanael.

Skinner and Rankine explain that the intended angel is Mebahiah because Harley 6482 correctly pairs Mebahiah with Orobas. They therefore restore the Mebahiah material in their edition. See [*The Goetia of Dr Rudd* searchable text](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html), especially p.157 and its editorial note.

## Mathers–Crowley, *The Book of the Goetia*, 1904

The original edition was published by Aleister Crowley at Boleskine in 1904, crediting S. L. MacGregor Mathers’s manuscript work. Orobas appears on printed p.28:

> **(55.) OROBAS.—The Fifty-fifth Spirit is Orobas. He is a Great and Mighty Prince, appearing at first like a Horse; but after the command of the Exorcist he putteth on the image of a Man. His Office is to discover all things Past, Present, and to Come; also to give Dignities, and Prelacies, and the Favour of Friends and of Foes. He giveth True Answers of Divinity, and of the Creation of the World. He is very faithful unto the Exorcist, and will not suffer him to be tempted of any Spirit. He governeth 20 Legions of Spirits. His Seal is this, etc.**

Sources: [1904 facsimile transcription](https://lo2.org/pdf/celepha%C3%AFs/mathers/Mathers-and-Crowley.Goetia.pdf), [Sacred Texts](https://www.sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm), and [Hermetic Library](https://hermetic.com/norton/classics/goetia/orobas).

This is a normalized rendering of the manuscript description, not a new independent witness. It standardizes spelling, capitalization, and punctuation and corrects “places” to **Prelacies**.

Crowley also added material not native to the manuscript *Goetia*:

- a “Preliminary Invocation” derived from the Graeco-Egyptian “Headless/Bornless” rite;
- an “Initiated Interpretation” that declares the Goetic spirits to be “portions of the human brain”;
- translations of the conjurations into the “Magical or Angelic” language now called Enochian;
- editorial notes and a magical square.

These additions affected the twentieth-century reception of Orobas but do not change the older entry itself. The 1904 bibliographical record is detailed at [100th Monkey Press](https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/goetia_1904/goetia_1904.htm).

## Joseph Peterson, 2001

Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* was first published by Weiser in 2001. Rather than silently normalizing the language, it prints a manuscript-based text, supplies variants, identifies dependence on Weyer and Scot, and distinguishes additions found in different witnesses.

For Orobas, Peterson’s base text is the Sloane 3825 form quoted above, on p.32. His notes identify:

- Weyer’s “a man’s idol” behind “Image of a man”;
- the Harley variant of 26 legions;
- the “places/prelaces” problem;
- the relationship of the Goetic catalogue to Weyer’s order.

Peterson’s continuously revised online edition is available at [Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm).

| Reveals past | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Reveals present | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Reveals future | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Answers about divinity | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Answers about creation | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Gives dignities | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Gives prelacies | Yes | Yes | “places” in Sl.3825; “prelaces” elsewhere | Yes |
| Gives favor of friends | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Gives favor of enemies/foes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

| Prevents temptation | Yes | Yes | Specifically by other spirits | Yes |

### Offices not historically assigned to Orobas

**Negative finding.** The source chain does not say that Orobas:

- teaches languages;
- teaches rhetoric, grammar, logic, philosophy, or the liberal sciences;
- finds hidden treasure;
- produces sexual love;
- supplies familiars;
- grants invisibility;
- transmutes metals;
- transports persons instantly;
- steals or returns property;
- commits robbery;
- cures disease.

These offices belong to other Goetic spirits. A particularly common modern conflation copies attributes of **Seere**—rapid travel, sudden accomplishment, hidden treasure, and an “indifferent good nature”—into Orobas entries. Teaching grammar and rhetoric belongs to spirits such as Ronove or Forneus; invisibility to Bael and others; transmutation to Haagenti or Zagan; treasure to several dukes and princes.

### Dignities and prelacies

*Prælatura* means an ecclesiastical prelacy or high governing office: bishopric, abbacy, priory, or comparable dignity. Later translators often broaden it to “advancement,” “employment,” “office,” or “station.”

De Plancy’s *emplois*, “employments,” is therefore a nineteenth-century secularization of the older ecclesiastical term.

## Appearance and transformation

The source sequence is stable:

1. Orobas comes forth as or like a horse.
2. The exorcist commands a change.
3. Orobas assumes “the image of a man.”
4. In human form he gives rational answers.

Neither Weyer nor the Goetia says:

- horse-headed man;
- centaur;
- winged horse;
- armored knight;
- crowned stallion;
- skeletal horse;
- red horse-demon.

Those are later visual interpretations. The earliest wording suggests a complete horse followed by a complete human likeness.

## Hours and ritual timing

### Goetic rank rule

The manuscript “Observations” assign times by rank. “Prelates”—understood by Mathers as equivalent to Princes—may be called:

> at any hour of the day.

Thus Orobas has no uniquely stated personal hour.

### Planet and metal

The classified list assigns:

- Kings — Sun/gold;
- Dukes — Venus/copper;
- Princes and Prelates — Jupiter/tin;
- Marquises — Moon/silver;
- Presidents — Mercury/quicksilver;
- Earls — copper and silver equally;
- Knights — Saturn/lead.

Mathers objected that this leaves Mars and iron unused and suggested that Earls should perhaps receive iron. That conjecture does not affect Orobas: as a Prince, his traditional rank metal is consistently **tin**, for **Jupiter**.

### Moon observation

The *Goetia* recommends work when the Moon is two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, or fourteen days old. This is a general election, not an Orobas-specific rule.

### Later planetary practice

**Later esoteric correspondence.** Modern traditionalists commonly add:

- Thursday;
- the planetary hour of Jupiter;
- Jupiter dignified or unafflicted;
- blue or royal-blue vestments, lights, or lamen backing;
- Jovian incense such as cedar.

Only the planet and tin follow directly from the Goetic rank table. Thursday, blue, and cedar are conventional planetary-magic extrapolations.

### Astrological timing from *777*

A Crowley/Golden Dawn-derived practitioner may instead elect:

- 0°–10° Libra;
- nighttime;
- the Moon’s face of Libra;
- the Two of Swords.

This is a later decanic overlay, not part of the manuscript ritual directions.

## The seal

### Historical occurrence

- **Weyer, 1577:** no individual seal.
- **Scot, 1584:** no individual seal.
- **Earlier *Office of Spirits* witnesses:** the spirit descriptions generally lack the individual Goetic seals.
- **Seventeenth-century *Goetia*:** the recognizable Orobas seal appears.
- **Sloane 2731:** carefully drawn manuscript form.
- **Sloane 3825:** base form reproduced by Peterson.
- **Harley 6483:** demon seal incorporated into a protective angel–demon lamen system.
- **Mathers–Crowley, 1904:** standardized black-line reproduction.

This chronology argues against claims that Orobas’s sigil is an ancient hieroglyph, Phoenician divine monogram, or surviving pagan cult symbol.

### Verbal description

The standard Mathers–Crowley form is an asymmetrical, continuous-looking linear character organized around:

- a long central upright;
- a small closed loop or oval at its top;
- upper lateral arms that hook and curl away from the upright;
- a crossing or junction in the upper-middle portion;
- a broad, low, curved base;
- curled or looped terminal strokes at the lower extremities.

It has no alphabetic legend, planetary symbol, animal figure, crown, or surrounding circle in its simplest Goetic form.

The clearest public-domain reproduction is the [1904 Seal of Orobas scan](https://fromoldbooks.org/Mathers-Goetia/pages/055-Seal-of-Orobas/).

### Manuscript variants

**Documented but palaeographically modest.** Sloane 2731, Sloane 3825, and the Mathers engraving preserve the same essential skeleton. Differences concern:

- whether small terminal curls close fully into loops;
- the depth and width of the lower bow;
- the angle at which upper arms join the vertical;
- whether intersecting strokes touch or merely cross;
- pen pressure and line curvature;
- the small top loop’s size and orientation.

No principal witness supplies a genuinely different “second seal” for Orobas.

### The Rudd lamen

Rudd’s procedure places the Goetic demon’s seal and the corresponding Shem angelic material on opposite or coordinated sides of a lamen. Skinner and Rankine interpret the angel-facing-the-heart arrangement as protection from the summoned spirit’s “wiles or malice.”

For Orobas, however, Harley 6483’s angelic side is corrupt: it repeats Nanael’s seal. The editors restore Mebahiah from Harley 6482. A practitioner claiming to reproduce Harley 6483 exactly should therefore show the erroneous Nanael seal; one reproducing the intended system normally substitutes Mebahiah.

# The angel: Mebahiah

## Identification

| Number | 55 |
| Spellings | Mebahiah, Mebahaiah, Mébahiah; occasional Mabeth |
| Hebrew full form | מבהיה |
| Root triplet | מבה — mem, beth, he |
| Suffix | יה, *Yah*, a divine element |

## Derivation of the 72-fold name

Exodus 14:19–21 consists, in the traditional consonantal Hebrew count, of three consecutive verses of 72 letters each. The construction proceeds:

1. write Exodus 14:19 in its normal direction;
2. write 14:20 in reverse;
3. write 14:21 in the normal direction;
4. read down the resulting 72 columns.

Each column produces a three-letter divine name. The 55th column gives **מבה**, MBH. Christian Kabbalists and related angelological traditions append **-יה** or **-אל** to produce pronounceable angel names, here **מבהיה**, Mebahiah/Mebahaiah.

The expression *Shem ha-Mephorash* originally meant the explicit or distinctly pronounced divine Name, especially the Tetragrammaton. Its use specifically for the 72 triplets is a later specialized development. It is therefore historically misleading to imply that “Shem ha-Mephorash” has always meant a list of 72 guardian angels.

## Jewish textual development

### Rabbinic and medieval sources

**Documented Jewish tradition.**

- Talmudic sources know traditions of a 72-letter divine Name, though they do not always identify it with the later 72 triplets.
- Rashi’s commentary on Sukkah 45a explains the construction from Exodus 14:19–21.
- *Sefer ha-Bahir*, §§109–110, gives an early extant account of the alternating-direction construction.
- The medieval *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* transmits the 72-fold Name in magical and cosmological settings.
- The printed *Sefer Raziel* gives the triplets without one universally binding vocalization.

Joshua Trachtenberg summarizes the construction in [*Jewish Magic and Superstition*](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/jms/jms09.htm). A translation and discussion of the relevant *Bahir* passage is available at [Hekhal](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-109-110-targum/).

### Legend of the Red Sea

**Tradition/legend.** Kabbalistic and occult writers frequently say that Moses used the 72-fold Name to divide the sea, protect Israel, or overcome Egypt. Exodus itself does not state that Moses pronounced 72 triplets. The claim is a mystical reading arising from the letter structure of the verses.

## Christian Kabbalah

### Johann Reuchlin, 1517

Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* helped transmit the 72 names into Christian Kabbalah. His list supplies angel-like vocalizations through divine suffixes. Mebahiah is attested in the textual index of the modern critical Reuchlin edition.

### Agrippa

Cornelius Agrippa’s *De occulta philosophia*, especially Book III, incorporated the 72 names into a Christian magical cosmos. Agrippa was a major conduit between Renaissance Christian Kabbalah and later ritual manuscripts.

### Blaise de Vigenère, 1586

Vigenère’s *Traicté des chiffres* treated the 72 divine/angelic names in a cryptographic, kabbalistic, and cosmological context. Skinner and Rankine identify Vigenère’s material as an important antecedent for the Rudd tables.

### Athanasius Kircher, 1652–1654

Kircher’s *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* gave the 72-fold Name one of its most influential early-modern visual presentations: divine names, languages, peoples, and rays organized in a grand universalizing diagram.

**Scholarly assessment.** Kircher’s scheme is a Christian antiquarian synthesis, not a neutral record of Jewish Kabbalah. Its assignments to nations and languages reflect Kircher’s seventeenth-century universal history.

A digitized Kircher source is held by [e-rara](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/13226133), with related material transcribed at [Esoteric Archives](https://www.esotericarchives.com/juratus/kircher.htm).

## Rudd’s use of the angel

### Pairing

**Documented esoteric correspondence.** The Rudd recension pairs spirits and angels by ordinal number:

> 55th Goetic spirit Orobas ↔ 55th Shem angel Mebahiah.

The angel is not described as Orobas’s moral “opposite” in Weyer or the early Goetia. This is Rudd’s protective synthesis.

### Function

The angelic name, seal, and Psalm are placed with the demon’s character so that the angelic power:

- constrains or controls the spirit;
- protects the magician;
- supplies divine authority;
- prevents the operator from relying on infernal force alone.

This is consistent with the broader Solomonic principle that inferior spirits are commanded through superior divine and angelic names.

### Harley 6483’s error

Skinner and Rankine’s note is decisive:

- Harley 6483 repeats Nanael’s angelic seal at the Orobas entry.
- The duplicated attribution is an error.
- Harley 6482 correctly assigns Mebahiah to Orobas.
- The editors use Harley 6482 for the proper Psalm and angelic material.

The restored verse is:

> *Tu autem Domine in aeternum permanes et memoriale tuum in generationem et generationem.*

“Thou remainest for ever, O Lord, and thy memorial from generation to generation.”

### Psalm numbering

The same passage can be cited as:

- **Psalm 101:13** in the Latin Vulgate;
- **Psalm 102:12** in Jewish and most Protestant numbering;
- occasionally **Psalm 102:13** in modern occult books that count the superscription or follow a different verse division.

The wording, rather than the printed number alone, is the safest identifier.

## Lenain, *La science cabalistique*, 1823

Lazare Lenain gives a fully developed notice for the 55th genius:

- name: **Mebahiah**;
- attribute: **Dieu éternel**;
- degrees: 271–275;
- zodiacal sector: 0°–5° Capricorn;
- 28th decan;
- decan genius: **Smat**;
- planetary governance: Jupiter;
- invocation time: 6:00–6:20 p.m.;
- Psalm: Vulgate 101:13;
- annual “days of presidency”: 13 May, 24 July, 4 October, 15 December, and 25 February;
- offices: consolation, morality, religion, propagation of faith, and assistance for those desiring children;
- contrary influence: hostility to truth, religion, and regeneration.

A scan is available through [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/download/BIUSante_ms05365/BIUSante_ms05365.pdf) and [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_science_cabalistique_ou_lart_de_conna%C3%AEtre_les_bons_g%C3%A9nies_qui_influent_sur_la_destin%C3%A9e_des_hommes_(IA_BIUSante_ms05365).pdf).

Lenain’s five scattered days should not be confused with the later five consecutive birthday dates. They represent another layer of the system.

## Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” reproduced and praised Lenain’s practical angelic notices in *La Cabbale* (1903). Papus was therefore an important transmitter, but not the originator, of Mebahiah’s meaning, Psalm, offices, or zodiacal ray.

## The Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn integrated:

- the 72 Shem angels;
- the zodiac divided into 72 quinances;
- two angels per decan;
- planetary face rulers;
- the 36 tarot pip cards;
- Christian angelic choirs and Qabalistic worlds.

However, three constructions must be kept separate:

1. The Golden Dawn’s angel assigned to 0°–5° Capricorn is Mebahiah.
2. Its two angels for 0°–10° Libra are Ieiazel/Iezalel and Mebahel, not Mebahiah.
3. Crowley’s night Goetic spirit for 0°–10° Libra is Orobas.

Thus the Golden Dawn can place **Mebahiah in Capricorn** and **Orobas in Libra** without contradiction, because the angel–spirit pairing and the Goetic day/night decan table are different overlays.

## Robert Ambelain

Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) transmitted the Lenain-type system to later French occultism. His Mebahiah material retains:

- degrees 271–275;
- Jupiter;
- an evening invocation window;
- the “Eternal God” gloss;
- the Psalm 102:12/101:13 passage;
- moral and religious offices.

Later editions carried a warning that some seals attributed to the 72 divine names were erroneous. That warning is relevant because modern diagrams are often reproduced without manuscript comparison.

## Haziel

“Haziel,” the pen name associated with a popular late-twentieth-century French guardian-angel literature, repackaged the 72 spirits as personal angels governing consecutive birth periods. In that popular system:

- Mebahiah governs approximately **22–26 December**;
- its essence is intellectual lucidity, faith, truth, family, or high morality;
- prayers and exhortations are expanded substantially beyond Lenain;
- Mebahiah is treated as a personal guardian rather than principally as a five-degree angelic intelligence.

**Modern esoteric development.** This guardian-birthday presentation is not found in *Sefer Raziel*, Reuchlin, or Rudd in its popular biographical form. It descends largely through Lenain, Papus, Ambelain, and modern French angelology.

## Crowley’s *Liber 777*

In table V, column CLXI, “Goetic Demons &c. by Night (Ascendant),” row 22, Crowley gives:

> **55 ♃ Orobas — Horse.**

Row 22 is Libra. The first Libra decan is 0°–10° Libra, ruled by the Moon in the Chaldean face order and assigned the Two of Swords. See the searchable [*Liber 777* table](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06Numerologia_Cabala/Crowley_%20Liber_777.pdf).

The Jupiter glyph beside Orobas indicates the planet of his rank as Prince. It does not mean Jupiter rules the Libra decan; the face ruler there is the Moon.

### Why every pip has two spirits

The 36 numbered tarot cards from Two through Ten correspond to the 36 zodiacal decans. Crowley distributes:

- Goetic spirits 1–36 through the decans by day;
- spirits 37–72 over the same decans by night.

Consequently each pip card carries two spirits. The first Libra decan/Two of Swords contains:

- **Sallos, number 19**, by day;
- **Orobas, number 55**, by night.

This is a systematic modern correspondence, not an office stated in either spirit’s early description.

## Enochian overlay

Crowley’s 1904 book printed Goetic conjurations in the “Magical or Angelic Language,” now called Enochian. This does not give Orobas an individual Enochian governor, tablet square, or Aethyr.

**Negative finding:** no Orobas-specific Enochian attribution occurs in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane Goetias, or Harley 6483.

Later magicians, including Lon Milo DuQuette and some Thelemic practitioners, have used Enochian Calls or elemental tablets in conjunction with Goetic operations. That is a twentieth-century synthesis. It should not be described as an original component of Orobas’s identity.

# Iconography

## Before de Plancy

The early sources are textual. Weyer and Scot give no portrait and no seal. Their only visual information is:

- a horse;
- subsequently the image of a man.

The seventeenth-century Goetia adds the abstract seal but does not illustrate the horse or the human form.

## Collin de Plancy

### The 1818 edition

The *Dictionnaire infernal* was first published in two volumes in 1818. Its contents were extensively revised through six editions.

**Caution:** the famous Orobas engraving must not be dated 1818. It belongs to the illustrated sixth edition of 1863. The first-edition scans consulted did not permit secure verification that the 1863 wording was already present verbatim in 1818.

### Sixth edition, 1863

On p.510 de Plancy writes:

> **Orobas, grand prince du sombre empire. On le voit sous la forme d’un beau cheval. Quand il paraît sous la figure d’un homme, il parle de l’essence divine. Consulté, il donne des réponses sur le passé, le présent et l’avenir. Il découvre le mensonge, accorde des dignités et des emplois, réconcilie les ennemis, et a sous ses ordres vingt légions.**

Translation:

> Orobas, a great prince of the dark empire. He is seen in the form of a beautiful horse. When he appears in the figure of a man, he speaks of the divine essence. When consulted, he gives answers concerning the past, present, and future. He detects falsehood, grants dignities and offices, reconciles enemies, and has twenty legions under his command.

Source: [Wikisource, 1863 Orobas entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Orobas).

De Plancy modifies the earlier tradition:

- the horse becomes a **beautiful** horse;
- “divine virtue” becomes the **divine essence**;
- “deceives no one” becomes **detects falsehood**;
- favor of enemies becomes **reconciles enemies**;
- prelacies become secular **employments**.

These are interpretive paraphrases, not new grimoire testimony.

### Internal rank contradiction

In the article Orobas is a *grand prince*. Elsewhere, in the 1863 “Monarchie infernale” table, de Plancy places Orobas among the **dukes**. This is an internal classificatory mistake or careless rearrangement; Weyer calls him a Prince.

### Louis Le Breton’s portrait

The 1863 portrait is attributed to Louis Le Breton’s designs. It shows:

- an anatomically ordinary, attractive horse;
- standing in profile or slight three-quarter profile;
- head raised and turned toward the viewer;
- flowing mane and tail;
- no wings, horns, crown, human torso, flames, or infernal landscape.

The restraint is significant. Le Breton illustrates only the first form, not a horse-headed anthropomorphic demon. The public-domain image is available from [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Orobas.png).

## Modern iconographic transformations

### *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona*

**Documented modern fiction.** Atlus’s recurring Orobas design is a crimson or maroon bipedal horse-being with a roughly human torso, white hands or hooves, mane, and tail. It fuses the source’s two successive forms into one horse-man.

Orobas appears across several *Shin Megami Tensei* and *Persona* titles, including installments of *Persona 3*, *Persona 4*, *Persona 5*, *Shin Megami Tensei V*, *Devil Survivor*, and *Soul Hackers*. The game compendia retain the historical claims about truth, past-present-future knowledge, rank, and twenty legions. See the [Megami Tensei database entry](https://megatenwiki.com/wiki/Orobas).

### *Bloodstained: Ritual of the Night*

Orobas is boss number 121, described in-game as:

> “A demon who can alter gravity’s pull on himself. Half-man, half-stallion.”

Its skeletal, composite body, horse skull, human remnant, fish-like jaws, and birdlike legs are modern invention. Defeating it grants the “Invert” shard. Fans have proposed that its composite anatomy may reflect confusion with Cimeies/Sowrges, another horse-associated spirit, but that is a speculative production-error theory. See the [Bloodstained entry](https://bloodstained.fandom.com/wiki/Orobas).

### Modern tarot and oracle art

Fabio Listrani’s *Goetia: Tarot in Darkness*, developed through a 2019 campaign and issued by Lo Scarabeo, gives modern psychological and graphic treatments of the Goetic spirits. The publisher explicitly frames the deck as a descent into the unconscious. See [Lo Scarabeo](https://www.loscarabeo.com/en/products/goetia-tarot-in-darkness).

Travis McHenry’s *Occult Tarot* and numerous independent oracle decks likewise assign new pictorial compositions and, sometimes, new tarot cards to Orobas. These deck-specific attributions should not be confused with Crowley’s Two of Swords correspondence.

# People and works

## Johann Weyer

Weyer, physician to Duke William of Cleves, published the *Pseudomonarchia* in the context of opposing extreme witch persecutions and exposing supposedly fraudulent magical literature. He named his source as a *Liber officiorum spirituum*, also called *Empto*.

His importance to Orobas is foundational:

- first secure printing of the exact name;
- rank, appearance, offices, and twenty legions;
- number 58;
- no seal.

## Reginald Scot and Abraham Fleming

Scot transmitted Weyer’s catalogue into English in 1584. Peterson’s attribution of the translation to Abraham Fleming is probable rather than certain. Scot’s English became particularly influential because seventeenth-century English compilers could borrow it directly.

## The anonymous Goetia compiler

The compiler or compilers of the *Ars Goetia*:

- expanded the list to 72;
- reordered it;
- numbered Orobas 55;
- added individual seals;
- added rank-based metals and working times;
- recast Orobas’s general truthfulness as faithfulness to the exorcist.

No documentary basis identifies the author as Solomon.

## “Dr Rudd” and Peter Smart

Thomas Rudd is traditionally associated with the angelic counterweight system, but Harley 6483 was copied by Peter Smart in 1712–1713. The “Rudd papers” provenance rests on later manuscript claims and is not equivalent to an autograph.

For Orobas, the Rudd stream contributes:

- Mebahiah;
- the Psalm;
- the two-sided protective lamen;
- a minority 26-legion reading;
- the accidental substitution of Nanael’s angelic seal.

## Collin de Plancy and Louis Le Breton

De Plancy secularized and popularized Orobas’s offices; Le Breton supplied the horse portrait that became the dominant nineteenth-century image.

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers prepared the normalized English Goetic text used by Crowley. He also reproduced a plate of Hebrew spellings “after Dr Rudd,” while warning that many were incorrect. Orobas appears there as אורובש, a mechanical Hebrew transcription of the European name rather than a Hebrew etymology.

## Aleister Crowley

Crowley published the Mathers manuscript in 1904 and reframed the spirits psychologically. In *Liber 777* he assigned Orobas to the night half of the first Libra decan.

These are separate contributions:

- 1904: editorial and ritual additions, Enochian conjurations, psychological interpretation;
- 1909 *777*: Libra/night/Two of Swords correspondence.

## Frater Achad

**Negative finding.** Charles Stansfeld Jones, Frater Achad, wrote extensively on Qabalah and Crowley’s system, but no specific Orobas working or distinctive Orobas doctrine was found in the works and indexes consulted. He should not be listed as an Orobas authority merely because he belonged to the broader Thelemic milieu.

## A. E. Waite

Waite’s *Book of Ceremonial Magic* paraphrases Orobas as a great prince who reveals past, present, and future, grants advancement and favor, answers about creation and divinity, remains faithful, and defends against temptation. Waite adds no independent historical information.

## Poke Runyon

Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s *The Book of Solomon’s Magick* first appeared in 1996 and integrates Goetic evocation, Shem angels, dark-mirror scrying, planetary correspondences, and psychological/hypnotic interpretation.

Runyon’s table gives Orobas with Mebahiah and the appropriate cardinal and archangelic apparatus. His method treats visionary appearance in the dark mirror partly as a controlled subjective or hypnotic phenomenon. The current edition and the author’s account are described at [Poke Runyon’s official site](https://www.pokerunyon.com/books/the-book-of-solomons-magick-second-edition).

No well-documented personal Orobas operation by Runyon was found; his relevance is methodological and tabular.

## Lon Milo DuQuette

**Legend/personal testimony.** DuQuette devotes pp.91–113 of *My Life with the Spirits* (1999) to “Evocation of Orobas” and its postscript.

He reports invoking Orobas while in financial distress and demanding a rapid reversal of fortune. A friend subsequently arrived with a usable automobile as a gift. A later episode involved an Orobas sigil hidden in a guitar and a friend who became obsessed with horse racing.

These stories are autobiographical testimony, not independently verified experiments. Their importance is that they helped spread the modern image of Orobas as unusually responsive, truthful, and comparatively friendly. The book’s table of contents and chapter pagination are visible at [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/My_Life_With_The_Spirits.html?id=MR7jGMyYPSwC).

## Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 edition provided the first widely accessible modern critical presentation of the entire *Lemegeton*, with manuscript variants and Weyer comparison. His online transcriptions have made the primary texts directly available and have corrected numerous popular conflations.

## Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their 2007 *Goetia of Dr Rudd* published Harley 6483’s distinctive system and drew on Harley 6482 to correct the Mebahiah error. Their most important Orobas-specific contribution is the documentation of that correction.

## Jake Stratton-Kent

Stratton-Kent’s *Encyclopaedia Goetica* and *Geosophia* argue for broader continuities between grimoire magic, necromantic practices, ancient spirit traditions, and Mediterranean religion.

**Qualification:** no specific historical derivation of Orobas from an ancient god was located in his published work consulted here. His relevance is to the general history of goēs/goetia and the spirit-catalogue tradition, not a proven Orobas etymology.

## Owen Davies, Jan Veenstra, Jean-Patrice Boudet, and Julien Véronèse

These scholars are important for the historical setting:

- Owen Davies: European grimoire transmission and readership;
- Jan Veenstra: learned magic, spirit catalogues, and medieval intellectual contexts;
- Boudet: the decisive comparison between *Livre des Esperitz*, Florence catalogues, and Weyer;
- Véronèse: late-medieval ritual magic, *Liber Officiorum* material, and the manuscript setting of the *Livre des Esperitz*.

Of these, Boudet’s *Arabas/Accabas/Irabas–Orobas* comparison is directly Orobas-specific.

# Grimoire ancestors

## The *Testament of Solomon*

**Broad ancestor; negative name finding.** The late-antique *Testament of Solomon* presents Solomon interrogating demons, learning their forms and offices, and constraining them with angels and a divine ring.

That structure resembles later Solomonic catalogues, but:

- Orobas does not appear;
- no Mebahiah–Orobas pair appears;
- the later Goetic seal is absent;
- there is no demonstrable direct line from a named *Testament* demon to Orobas.

It is a conceptual ancestor, not an attestation.

## Munich Manual, Clm 849

Richard Kieckhefer’s fifteenth-century *Liber incantationum, exorcismorum et fascinationum variarum* contains ritual conjurations and demon names. No Orobas, Arabas, Accabas, or Irabas entry matching the later figure was located.

## *Livre des Esperitz*

The *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, is the earliest surviving extensive French demon catalogue of this family. Boudet dates its manuscript to the fifteenth or early sixteenth century, with older material behind it.

It contains names and offices ancestral to many Weyer spirits, but no secure Orobas. Claims that Orobas is directly identical with its spirit Parcas are **unsupported or disputed**; the rank and offices do not supply the strong match found in the Florence *Arabas* entry.

A transcription begins at [Wikisource, fol.179r](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Commancement).

## Florence Plut. 89 Sup. 38

This is the closest pre-Weyer evidence:

> Arabas / Accabas / Irabas — great prince — twenty legions.

The entry is best described as a probable textual ancestor of Orobas.

## *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

The “Book of the Offices of Spirits” was not one fixed text but a family of related catalogues. Weyer names such a book as his source. The Folger *Book of Oberon* manuscript preserves another important witness.

The family explains why names can appear as:

> Arabas → Irabas → Orobas

without requiring an ancient deity or deliberate wordplay.

## *Book of Abramelin*

**Negative finding.** Abramelin’s extensive subordinate-spirit lists contain many names that resemble later grimoire spirits, but no secure Orobas was found. Similar-looking names alone are insufficient for identification.

# Controversies and disputes

## 1. Does Orobas exist before Weyer?

**Answer:** probably as a textual precursor, but not securely under the exact spelling.

- Exact *Orobas*: securely 1577.
- Florence 1494: *Arabas / Accabas / Irabas*, same rank and twenty legions.
- Identification: strong scholarly reconstruction.
- *Livre des Esperitz*: no exact Orobas.
- Ancient world: no identified Orobas deity.

## 2. Is *orobias* the etymology?

Possible, but unproved. It is a real Latin–Greek incense term, not a fabricated word; the missing historical bridge is the problem.

## 3. Is Orobas an ancient horse god?

No source demonstrates this. Equine appearance is not evidence of descent from Epona, Poseidon Hippios, the Dioscuri, Kakasbos, Resheph, or another horse deity.

## 4. Was Orobas originally Ahura Mazda/Oromazes?

**Modern speculation.** The theory relies on partial sound resemblance and both figures’ association with truth. Against it:

- the names are not especially close in their early forms;
- the Florence variants do not support the sequence;
- de Plancy treats Orobas and Oromazes separately;
- no intermediary source makes the identification.

## 5. Twenty or twenty-six legions?

The overwhelming reading is twenty:

- Florence precursor: twenty;
- Weyer: twenty;
- Scot: twenty;
- Sloane 2731/3825: twenty;
- Mathers–Crowley: twenty;
- de Plancy: twenty.

Harley 6483’s 26 is best treated as a copying error, possibly influenced by a neighboring entry such as Gremory.

## 6. Prince or Duke?

Prince is the primary reading. De Plancy’s article agrees, but his general infernal hierarchy mistakenly places Orobas among dukes. Modern “Grand Duke Orobas” entries probably descend from that internal inconsistency or careless copying.

## 7. “Places” or “prelacies”?

“Prelacies” is supported by Latin *prælaturas*, Scot, Sloane 2731, and Mathers. Sloane 3825’s “places” is a scribal simplification or error.

## 8. Does Orobas detect lies, or merely refrain from lying?

Weyer says he **deceives no one**. De Plancy changes this into **he detects falsehood**. The latter became a major modern office, but its first clear wording is nineteenth-century paraphrase rather than the 1577 Latin.

## 9. Does he reconcile enemies?

Weyer says he gives the favor of friends and enemies. De Plancy interprets this as reconciling enemies. Related, but not identical.

## 10. Is Orobas “good-natured” or morally good?

The early sources call him truthful and protective toward the conjurer; they do not call him benevolent, redeemed, harmless, or morally good.

The phrase “indifferent good natured” belongs to Seere in the Goetia. Its transfer to Orobas is a modern conflation.

## 11. Does he find treasure or travel instantly?

No. Those are primarily Seere-like offices. Their attachment to Orobas is a modern copying error.

## 12. Is Mebahiah really the paired angel?

Yes, with a manuscript complication:

- the intended ordinal pairing is Mebahiah;
- Harley 6482 confirms it;
- Harley 6483 accidentally draws Nanael’s seal;
- Skinner and Rankine restore Mebahiah.

Tables giving **Nanael** as Orobas’s angel reproduce the corrupt Harley drawing rather than the intended attribution.

## 13. Why does Mebahiah occupy Capricorn while Orobas occupies Libra?

Because the systems were built differently:

- Rudd: number 55 paired with number 55.
- Angelic zodiac: 72 consecutive five-degree sectors.
- Crowley Goetia: two cycles of 36 spirits over 36 ten-degree decans.

Attempts to force both into the same zodiacal sector create modern hybrid tables.

## 14. Is the tarot card Two of Swords or Two/Three of Pentacles?

Crowley’s *777* yields **Two of Swords**.

Pentacle assignments arise when authors put spirit 55 directly into a 72-part Capricorn sequence or adopt a deck’s proprietary arrangement. They are not Crowley’s Goetic decan attribution.

## 15. Is the Psalm 101:13, 102:12, or 102:13?

All can point to the same sentence under different numbering conventions. The historically relevant Latin is *Tu autem Domine in aeternum permanes…*. In a standard King James Bible it is Psalm 102:12.

## 16. Is Mebahiah’s meaning literally “Eternal God”?

No. **מבה** is a constructed triplet, not an ordinary Hebrew noun phrase meaning “Eternal God.” *Dieu éternel* is Lenain’s traditional divine-attribute gloss, reinforced by the Psalm.

## 17. Are modern “enns” ancient?

The popular chant:

> *Jedan tasa hoet naca Orobas*

does not occur in Weyer, Scot, any Sloane Goetia, Harley 6483, Mathers–Crowley, or Rudd’s Psalmic apparatus. It belongs to late-twentieth-century demonolatry, especially the Dukanté/Connolly stream. It is a modern liturgical invention.

## 18. Are reports of Orobas as patient, friendly, or beginner-safe historical?

These descriptions largely derive from modern practitioners, online testimony, and the spirit’s textual faithfulness. They may be sincere experiential reports, but they are not properties stated by Weyer beyond truthfulness and protection from temptation.

# Open questions

1. **What was the archetypal spelling behind Arabas, Accabas, Irabas, and Orobas?**  
   A complete stemmatic study of all *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* witnesses may establish whether *Orobas* is original or a successful sixteenth-century corruption.

2. **Did Weyer independently regularize the name?**  
   His source is lost. The exact stage at which *Arabas* became *Orobas* is unknown.

3. **How early are the Goetic seals?**  
   No Orobas seal is known from Weyer or the fifteenth-century catalogue. The exact seventeenth-century exemplar from which the Sloane seals descend remains unidentified.

4. **Can the Sloane and Harley seals be stemmatically classified?**  
   Their small differences may reveal copying relationships, but a high-resolution, folio-by-folio palaeographic study has not been published for this single sigil.

5. **Who actually devised the Rudd angel–demon counterweight system?**  
   The attribution to Thomas Rudd is traditional but not secured by an autograph. Peter Smart copied the surviving manuscripts decades after Rudd’s probable lifetime.

6. **Was the Harley 6483 Nanael seal a simple eye-skip, or was its exemplar already corrupt?**  
   Harley 6482 shows that the correct association was known in the same manuscript milieu.

7. **When did de Plancy first add “detects falsehood”?**  
   The 1863 reading is certain. A systematic collation of all editions from 1818 through 1853 would date the innovation precisely.

8. **Was *orobias* ever proposed before twentieth-century popular demon dictionaries?**  
   No early derivation was found. Locating its first printed occurrence would clarify whether it originated as scholarship or dictionary folklore.

9. **Was the *Lovely Molly* figure intentionally Orobas?**  
   A screenplay, concept-art caption, or statement from Eduardo Sánchez would be necessary to move the identification beyond resemblance.

10. **Was Genshin’s Orobashi expressly named from Orobas?**  
    The broader Goetic naming pattern makes it plausible, but a developer or localization source is still needed.

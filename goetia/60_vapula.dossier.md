# Vapula: Research Dossier

*Research current to 14 September 2026.*

## Evidence labels

- **[Documented text]** directly stated or visibly present in a primary source or manuscript transcription.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** a historical or textual relationship proposed by an editor or academic.
- **[Tradition]** a received magical or angelological teaching whose historical existence is documented, without asserting its supernatural truth.
- **[Esoteric correspondence]** a symbolic association constructed by an occult system such as the Golden Dawn or Crowley’s *777*.
- **[Disputed]** a reading or interpretation for which the witnesses conflict.
- **[Legend]** a narrative presented by the grimoire or later tradition rather than independently established history.
- **[Modern invention]** an unattested recent addition, creative interpretation, practitioner claim, or popular-culture adaptation.

A copyright-compliant short exact excerpt is supplied for each principal text, followed by a complete account of its contents and a link to the full transcription or facsimile.

## The name and its origins

### Earliest recoverable history

#### Before 1494

**[Documented absence]** No exact *Vapula*, *Naphula* or *Napula* has yet been identified in the *Testament of Solomon*, the Munich magical handbook Clm 849, the *Book of Abramelin*, or the extant *Livre des Esperitz*. These works are relevant ancestors of the genre, but they do not provide a biography of Vapula.

The *Testament of Solomon*, compiled in late-antique layers, supplies the broad legend of Solomon interrogating and compelling demons. Its named figures include Ornias, Beelzeboul, Onoskelis and Asmodeus, but not Vapula. Consequently, statements that Vapula was personally bound by the biblical Solomon belong to the later Goetic **legend**, not an ancient Vapula tradition.

The fifteenth-century Munich handbook Clm 849 contains recognizable ancestors of several Weyer spirits—such as *Volach* and *Tvueries*—but no accepted Vapula cognate. It does include experiments in which spirits procure a teacher of arts and sciences. That is a thematic parallel to Vapula’s office, not proof of identity. Peterson’s comparative introduction lists the demonstrable Clm 849 correspondences and does not include Vapula among them. [Peterson’s comparative introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

The *Livre des Esperitz*, preserved in Cambridge, Trinity College MS O.8.29, shares numerous names and descriptions with the catalogue behind Weyer. Its surviving text, however, contains no clear Vapula or Balpala entry. [French transcription of the *Livre des Esperitz*](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre_des_esperitz/Texte_entier)

#### 1494: Balpala

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The earliest plausible individual precursor is **Balpala**, number 25 in a demon catalogue copied into Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 89 sup. 38, in January 1494.

Jean-Patrice Boudet’s comparative table aligns:

- Florence: **Balpala**, a great and strong duke, 30 legions.
- Weyer: **Vapula**, a great and strong duke, 36 legions.

Peterson follows that comparison in his source table: “Flor. #25: Balpala” opposite Weyer 59/Goetia 60. [Boudet, “Les *who’s who* démonologiques…”](https://doi.org/10.4000/medievales.1019); [Peterson’s table](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

The correspondence is persuasive because the name occupies the equivalent place in a related catalogue and preserves the same unusual rank formula. It is not conclusive:

- *Balpala* and *Vapula* are not identical spellings.
- The legion count changes from 30 to 36.
- The surviving Florence notice does not preserve Vapula’s winged-lion appearance or instructional offices.
- The copying history between the 1494 list and Weyer's exemplar is lost.

Accordingly, **Balpala is the likely textual ancestor, not a securely demonstrated earlier form of an independently known ancient being**.

#### The lost *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*

Weyer said that his source was a *Liber officiorum spirituum, seu Liber dictus Empto. Salomonis, de principibus et regibus daemoniorum*—a “Book of the Offices of Spirits” attributed to Solomon. Trithemius had already catalogued a *Liber Officiorum Spirituum* in his *Antipalus maleficiorum* of 1508. [Peterson’s Weyer introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The 1494 Florence list, the *Livre des Esperitz*, Clm 849, the Folger *Book of Oberon* material and Weyer’s catalogue are branches of a larger “Offices of Spirits” family. None is simply a complete copy of another.

#### 1577: exact Vapula

**[Documented text]** The first secure attestation of the exact name **Vapula** is entry 59 of the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, added to the fifth edition of Johann Weyer’s *De praestigiis daemonum* in 1577. It occurs on p./column 929 of that edition.

This is therefore the defensible answer to the earliest-name question:

> **Exact “Vapula”: 1577. Probable precursor “Balpala”: 1494. No established ancient god, hero or place behind the name.**

### Etymology

#### What is known

**[Documented finding]** Weyer supplies no etymology. Scot, the Goetia manuscripts, Rudd, de Plancy, Mathers, Crowley and Peterson likewise give none.

**[Scholarly finding]** Unlike Baal, Astaroth, Asmodai, Belial or Phoenix, Vapula has no securely identifiable ancient divine, heroic, geographical, Greek or Latin source.

The name is probably the result of copying and recopying within the medieval *Offices of Spirits* catalogue. The transition **Balpala → Vapula** could reflect ordinary manuscript corruption: interchange of initial *B/V*, loss of a medial syllable and normalization into a more Latin-looking form. Without intermediate witnesses, the exact process remains unknown.

#### Latin *vapulo/vapulare*

**[Disputed modern conjecture]** Latin *vapulō, vapulāre* means “to be beaten, flogged or thrashed.” Lewis and Short record forms such as *vapula* and *vapulet*. [Lewis and Short entry](https://alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=vapulo)

The formal resemblance is real, but there is no historical evidence that Weyer, his source, or any early copyist derived the demon’s name from this verb. Nor does flogging relate to the spirit’s winged-lion form or educational office. It should be treated as a possible pun or coincidence, not an etymology.

#### Hebrew “fallen one”

**[Modern invention]** Attempts to derive *Naphula* from Hebrew נפל, *n-f-l*, “fall,” are chronologically weak. *Naphula* is not an early form: it becomes prominent only in Mathers–Crowley in 1904, more than three centuries after *Vapula*. It therefore cannot establish the origin of the older name.

#### “Vapour,” wind and flight

**[Modern invention]** Associations with Latin *vapor*, English “vapour,” invisible winds or being “whipped by the wind” are unattested before modern websites. They appear to have been inspired by the wings and the sound of the name.

#### “Marvelous”

**[Modern invention]** A recent claim that *Vapula* means “marvellous” probably arose by back-forming a name meaning from Weyer’s word *mirabilem*. In Weyer, however, *mirabilem* describes the **person whom Vapula makes skilful**, not Vapula’s name.

#### Valefar

**[Textually false conflation]** A modern Spanish-language page lists **Valefar** as a Vapula variant. No consulted manuscript or critical comparison supports this. Valefar is a separate spirit: Weyer 14 and Goetia 6. The resemblance is limited to initial *Va-*.

## The description across the sources

### 1577: Johann Weyer

Weyer’s *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*, entry 59, is the oldest complete description.

Short exact excerpt:

> “Vapula Dux magnus & fortis: conspicitur ut leo alis ad modum gryphi.”

“Vapula, a great and strong Duke, is seen as a lion with wings after the manner of a griffin.”

The remainder states that he makes a person subtle and remarkable in:

- *artibus mechanicis*—mechanical or practical arts;
- philosophy;
- sciences contained in books;

and that he commands thirty-six legions.

The complete Latin notice is at [Peterson’s Weyer transcription, entry 59](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm). In the 1577 fifth edition it is on p./column 929.

#### Meaning of *artes mechanicae*

“Mechanical arts” did not mean only modern machinery. In medieval and Renaissance classifications it covered practical, productive and technical arts—the crafts contrasted with the liberal arts. Engineering, construction, manufacture and skilled handiwork are reasonable modern examples, but electronics, automotive mechanics and computer programming are modern applications, not Weyer’s wording.

Weyer’s *scientiae quae in libris continentur* emphasizes learned or book-contained sciences. The description therefore joins practical skill, philosophy and textual learning.

### 1584: Reginald Scot

Book XV, chapter 2 of Reginald Scot’s *Discoverie of Witchcraft* reproduces an English translation of Weyer, probably made by Abraham Fleming. Vapula appears near the end of the chapter, on p. 225 of the 1584 edition.

Short exact excerpt:

> “Vapula is a great duke and a strong, he is seene like a lion with griphens wings…”

Scot continues by saying that Vapula makes a man subtle and wonderful in handicrafts, philosophy and sciences contained in books, and rules thirty-six legions.

The complete text is available in [Peterson’s parallel Weyer–Scot edition](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm); the chapter context is in [Scot, Book XV](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/scot16.htm).

**[Textual observation]** Scot’s “handicrafts” is a sensible vernacular translation of *artes mechanicae*. It does not yet include the Goetia manuscript’s additional word “professions.”

### The seventeenth-century Goetia

The present *Lemegeton* is a composite seventeenth-century collection. Peterson finds that its Goetia depends especially closely upon Scot, including errors found in later editions of Scot. Sloane 3825 is his primary manuscript because it generally preserves the most accurate readings against identifiable sources. [Peterson’s *Lemegeton* introduction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

### Sloane MS 3825

**[Documented text]** Sloane 3825, fol. 109r, numbers Vapula 60.

Short exact excerpt:

> “The 60th spirit is called Vapula, he is a great mighty & strong duke…”

It describes:

- a lion with griffin’s wings;
- the office of making men knowing in all handicraft professions;
- philosophy and other sciences;
- thirty-six legions;
- a seal or character to be worn as a lamen.

Peterson notes that “professions” is absent from Scot and another Goetia witness and may be a gloss added after “handicraft.” He also recommends “mechanical arts” for Weyer’s *mechanicus*. [Peterson’s Goetia, fol. 109r](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Sloane MS 2731

Sloane 2731 reads substantially:

- “The 60 spirit is Called Vapula”;
- “Great mighty & Strong Duke”;
- lion with griffin’s wings;
- teaches or makes people knowing in “all handicraft professions,” philosophy and other sciences;
- governs 36 legions;
- seal worn as a lamen.

The wording is nearly identical to Sloane 3825, with differences in capitalization, spelling and punctuation. [Sloane 2731 transcription](https://grimorium.lat/en/library/goetia-sloane2731/read/)

**[Disputed dating]** An old catalogue date of 1572 appears in connection with Sloane 2731, but the manuscript’s dependency on later materials makes that date untenable as a straightforward date of composition. Peterson tentatively raises 1687 and describes it as a composite copied from more than one prototype. It should not be used to antedate Weyer. [Manuscript discussion](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

### Harley MS 6483 and the Rudd recension

Harley 6483 is dated 1712–13 and contains extensive material attributed to Dr Thomas Rudd. Peter Smart was involved in copying and organizing the Harley material.

Its entry reads **Nappula**, while the seal is labelled **Napula**. It calls the spirit a “great and mighty strong **President**,” gives the lion with griffin wings, assigns knowledge of handicrafts, professions, philosophy and other sciences, and retains **36 legions**.

**[Disputed reading]** “President” is almost certainly an error or isolated innovation:

- Weyer: Duke.
- Scot: Duke.
- Sloane 3825: Duke.
- Sloane 3648: Duke.
- Sloane 2731: Duke.
- Northwestern 65: Duke.
- Mathers–Crowley: Duke.
- Harley 6483 alone: President.

Skinner and Rankine’s editorial note specifically observes that another manuscript has “a Duke.” [Skinner and Rankine, *The Goetia of Dr Rudd*, p. 162](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

**[Modern error corrected]** A later dictionary reports that Rudd gives Vapula thirty legions. The Harley transcription on p. 162 says **36**. Thirty belongs to the possible 1494 precursor Balpala, not to Harley’s Nappula.

### 1904: Mathers and Crowley

The first edition of *The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King* was published at Foyers, Inverness, in 1904. It was based on Mathers’s manuscript work and edited/published by Aleister Crowley.

Short exact excerpt:

> “VAPULA, or NAPHULA.—The Sixtieth Spirit is Vapula, or Naphula. He is a Duke Great, Mighty and Strong…”

It then gives the lion with gryphon’s wings, handicrafts and professions, philosophy and other sciences, 36 legions and the seal to be worn as previously directed.

The entry is printed around p. 42 in the original pagination and appears on PDF page 44 in the linked facsimile. [1904 facsimile](https://biglevi.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/The-Book-Of-The-Goetia-Of-Solomon-The-King-A-Crowley-1904.pdf); [Sacred Texts transcription](https://sacred-texts.com/grim/lks/lks05.htm)

**[Editorial invention or unsupported variant]** Peterson remarks that many alternative names in Mathers–Crowley are not supported by the manuscripts he examined. *Naphula* is probably a normalization or further mutation of Harley’s *Nappula/Napula*, not evidence for an ancient alternative name.

### 2001: Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s *The Lesser Key of Solomon: Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis* (York Beach, Maine: Weiser, 2001), p. 34, follows Sloane 3825 while recording variants and parallels. His online edition likewise places Vapula at fol. 109r.

Peterson’s principal contributions are text-critical:

- Vapula is Weyer/Scot 59 but Goetia 60.
- “Mechanical arts” better represents Weyer’s Latin than a narrowly modern meaning of handicraft.
- “Professions” is probably an explanatory addition.
- The spirit sigils are absent from Weyer, Scot and the earlier *Office of Spirits* witnesses he knew.
- The Goetia’s order is drastically rearranged relative to Weyer.

### What Vapula does not do in the early texts

**[Documented absence]** The sources do not attribute to Vapula:

- treasure discovery;
- love or lust;
- familiars;
- invisibility;
- linguistic knowledge;
- transmutation;
- prophecy;
- answers about past, present or future;
- healing;
- warfare;
- necromancy;
- changing the conjurer’s shape.

Those offices belong to other catalogue spirits. Modern lists sometimes accumulate generic Goetic powers around Vapula, but they are not in the historical notice.

### Second form

**[Documented absence]** Vapula’s notice contains no alternate human or “fair” form and does not say that the conjurer may demand one.

The general Goetia ritual says that Solomon’s hexagram may be shown so spirits become obedient and take human shape. That general instruction is not a Vapula-specific second appearance. [Goetia, hexagram instruction](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

### Rank, metal, planet and hour

Under the Goetia’s “Observations”:

- Duke seals are made in **copper**, the metal of **Venus**.
- Dukes may be bound from **sunrise until noon**, in clear weather.
- The operation is favored when the Moon is 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 14 days old.

These are generic rules for Vapula’s rank, not part of Weyer’s individual description. [Goetia’s observations](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

If Harley’s isolated “President” were taken literally, the relevant metal would be mercury and the permitted time any daylight hour except twilight, with special conditions at night. Because the textual tradition overwhelmingly calls Vapula a Duke, copper/Venus and sunrise-to-noon are the historically stronger attribution.

## The angel: Mizrael or Mitzrael

## The seventy-twofold name before Rudd

### Jewish background

**[Documented tradition]** The seventy-twofold name is derived from Exodus 14:19–21. Each Hebrew verse contains seventy-two letters. The conventional construction writes:

1. verse 19 in its normal direction;
2. verse 20 in reverse;
3. verse 21 in its normal direction;

then reads the aligned letters vertically, producing seventy-two three-letter units.

This “boustrophedon” procedure is attested in medieval Jewish sources, including the *Sefer ha-Bahir*. It is not a discovery by Rudd, Mathers or Crowley. [*Bahir* §§109–110](https://hekhal.org/texts/sefer-ha-bahir-109-110-targum/); [Gershom Scholem, *Origins of the Kabbalah*](https://kronadaran.am/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/G.-Scholem.-Origins-of-the-Kabbalah.pdf)

**[Historical qualification]** In rabbinic literature, *shem ha-meforash* originally referred to the explicitly pronounced divine name, especially the Tetragrammaton. The term was later applied to several expanded names—of 12, 42 or 72 letters or elements. “The Shem HaMephorash” as a fixed list of seventy-two guardian angels is principally a medieval and Christian-esoteric development.

### Sefer Raziel

The *Sefer Razi’el ha-Mal’akh* is a medieval Hebrew magical compilation incorporating divine names, angelology, astrology, amulets and earlier Ashkenazi material. Its famous claim that the angel Raziel delivered secret wisdom to Adam after the Fall is **legend**, not historical authorship. The printed Hebrew compilation appeared at Amsterdam in 1701, although constituent works and manuscript forms are centuries older. [YIVO, “Angels”](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article/2073); [modern scholarship on *Sefer Razi’el*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/asch-2024-2017/html?lang=en)

The book transmits the seventy-twofold divine name, but one must distinguish that from Rudd’s later number-for-number equation of those names with Goetic demons. *Sefer Raziel* does not contain Vapula.

### Reuchlin and Christian Kabbalah

Johannes Reuchlin’s *De arte cabalistica* (1517) treated the three-letter units as angelic names by adding divine suffixes such as *-El* or *-Yah*. This was part of the Christian-Kabbalistic reception of Jewish name mysticism.

### Agrippa

Agrippa explains the construction in *De occulta philosophia*, III.25. Short exact excerpt:

> “then each of the three letters… make one name, which are seventy two names”

He adds *El* or *Jah* to produce angelic names and states that the angels govern seventy-two celestial quinaries. His table includes **Mizrael**, written with the letters corresponding to **מצראל**. [Agrippa, III.25](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/agripp3b.htm)

**[Documented chronology]** Agrippa’s printed *Mizrael* predates the secure appearance of *Vapula* by more than forty years. The two names did not begin as an angel–demon pair.

### Kircher

Athanasius Kircher reproduced and elaborated Christian-Kabbalistic schemes of the seventy-two names in *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–54), including a famous circular diagram relating the names to peoples, zodiacal and divine structures. [Kircher diagram](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AThe_72_Names_of_God_%28Oedipus_Aegyptiacus%2C_1652%E2%80%9354%29.jpg); [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_XjfzMnomDQoC/page/n299/mode/1up)

Kircher helped establish the visual encyclopedic form of the scheme. He did not pair Mizrael with Vapula.

## The name Mizrael/Mitzrael

| Element | Form |
|---|---|
| Three-letter unit | **מצר** |
| Scholarly transliteration | *MṢR* or *MẒR* |
| Occult transliterations | Mizrael, Mitzrael, Mitzraël |
| Expanded angel name | **מצראל** |
| Suffix | **אל, El**, “God” |
| Number | 60 |

Because the name is generated mechanically from three letters distributed across Exodus 14:19–21, its later French “meaning” is not a simple dictionary translation.

Hebrew *mēṣar* can evoke narrowness, straits or distress, which may have encouraged later interpretations concerning oppressed or distressed people. But “God who relieves the oppressed” is an **occult interpretive title**, not the literal grammatical translation of *מצראל*.

## Rudd’s pairing

### What Harley 6483 does

**[Documented early-modern tradition]** Harley 6483 places the seal and name of a corresponding Shem angel with each Goetic spirit. The match is numerical:

- demon 1 with angel 1;
- demon 2 with angel 2;
- …
- demon 60, Nappula, with angel 60, **Mizrael**.

The angelic name, seal and psalm are placed on the reverse of the demonic lamen. Skinner and Rankine explain that the angelic side is worn toward the magician’s heart so that the angel protects the magician and constrains the demon. [*The Goetia of Dr Rudd*, introduction and p. 162](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-goetia-of-dr-rudd-by-dr-stephen-skinner-david-rankine-pdf-free.html)

This pairing depends upon the **Goetia’s number 60**. Vapula was number 59 in Weyer, so the pairing cannot be projected backward into Weyer’s catalogue.

### Date and attribution

The extant Harley manuscript is dated **1712–13**. Skinner and Rankine argue that it copies a Rudd exemplar made around 1655–56, because Thomas Rudd died in 1656 and the source appears to use a 1655 English *Heptameron*. This is an editorial **reconstruction**, not a surviving dated autograph by Rudd. Peterson treats Harley 6483 as one of the latest and most innovative Lemegeton manuscripts. [Peterson’s manuscript survey](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/lemegeton.htm)

### Psalm

Harley and Lenain give:

> “Iustus Dominus in omnibus viis suis et sanctus in omnibus operibus suis.”

A close translation is: “The Lord is righteous in all his ways and holy—or faithful—in all his works.”

The Hebrew is:

> צַדִּיק יְהוָה בְּכָל־דְּרָכָיו וְחָסִיד בְּכָל־מַעֲשָׂיו

This is Psalm **145:17** in Masoretic and most modern English numbering. It is Psalm **144:17** in standard Vulgate numbering. Rudd and Lenain call it the eighteenth verse of Psalm 144, probably because a title or superscription was counted as verse 1. [Sefaria, Psalm 145:17](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.145.17)

## Lenain, 1823

Lazare Lenain’s *La science cabalistique* gives the sixtieth angel as **Mitzrael**, pp. 87–88.

Short exact excerpt:

> “Son attribut: Dieu qui soulage les opprimés.”

“His attribute: God who relieves the oppressed.”

Lenain assigns:

- ecliptic degrees **296 through 300 inclusive**;
- the thirtieth decan genius **Homoth**;
- daily time **7:40 p.m.–8:00 p.m.**;
- the dates 18 May, 29 July, 9 October, 20 December and 2 March in his rotating calendar scheme;
- the cited Psalm 144 verse;
- power to heal maladies of the spirit and deliver people from persecutors;
- governance over illustrious, talented and virtuous persons;
- fidelity and obedience among subordinates;
- bodily and mental qualities, virtue, intelligence, agreeable temperament and long life.

The contrary genius produces insubordination and bad physical or moral qualities. These are **Lenain’s nineteenth-century angelological correspondences**, not content from Exodus, Agrippa or Rudd. [Lenain, *La science cabalistique*, 1823](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1823__lenain___science_cabalistique.pdf)

Degrees 296°–300° in a zodiac measured from 0° Aries correspond to **26°–30° Capricorn**.

## The Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn adapted the seventy-two names to tarot decans and half-decans. Its sequence begins the Shem zodiacal cycle at **Leo**, not Aries. As a result, angel 60 falls at:

- **26°–30° Taurus**;
- the second or nocturnal half of Taurus III;
- the **Seven of Pentacles**;
- Saturn in Taurus;
- Golden Dawn title: **Lord of Success Unfulfilled**.

The first five degrees of the same decan are assigned to Harahel; Mitzrael occupies the latter five. [Regardie, *The Complete Golden Dawn System of Magic*](https://scriptaetveritas.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/ISRAEL_REGARDIE_COMPLETE_GOLDEN_DAWN_SYSTEM_OF_MAGIC.pdf); [Book T transcription](https://jantetteroo.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Mathers_and_felkin_golden_dawn_book_t_th.pdf)

**[Important dispute]** This does not “correct” Lenain’s Capricorn. It is a different starting convention.

## Papus

Gérard Encausse, “Papus,” devoted space in the enlarged 1903 edition of *La Cabbale* to Lenain’s seventy-two angels and helped circulate *La science cabalistique* among French occultists. The Bibliothèque nationale catalogue describes the 1903 second edition as containing new texts from Lenain and other occult authors. [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb304000064); [1903 scan](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/abg405b2732165.pdf)

Papus transmitted the French occult angelology; he was not the originator of the Mizrael–Vapula pairing.

## Ambelain

Robert Ambelain’s *La Kabbale pratique* (1951) reproduces much of Lenain’s Mitzrael material: oppressed persons, mental illness, persecutors, subordinate obedience and the contrary genius. Later English translations correct or at least flag the Psalm-numbering discrepancy. [English translation](https://www.benpadiah.com/otherstuff/elib/magick/Shemhamphorash.pdf)

Ambelain’s text is a twentieth-century continuation of Lenain’s Christian occult Kabbalah, not evidence for medieval Jewish beliefs about Mitzrael’s personality.

## Haziel

“Haziel,” the pen name of François Bernard-Termès, popularized the seventy-two “guardian angels” in late-twentieth-century French angelology. In that system Mitzrael is commonly attached to **16–20 January**, corresponding approximately to the last five degrees of Capricorn, and is treated as a personal guardian governing liberation, repair, authority and psychological balance.

**[Modern tradition]** Haziel’s birth-angel presentation expands Lenain’s material into devotional self-help angelology. It is distinct from both Rudd’s constraining lamen and the Golden Dawn’s Taurus placement. Modern compilations sometimes mix all three without identifying the change of systems.

### Crowley’s *777*

Crowley’s *Liber 777* divides the 72 Goetic spirits into:

- 36 by day;
- 36 by night;
- within each group, ascendant, succedent and cadent decans.

Table CLXV gives, under key scale 24:

- number **60**;
- **Napula**;
- Venus;
- lion with gryphon’s wings;
- night/cadent position.

Key scale 24 is Scorpio, and “cadent” is the third decan. Thus Vapula occupies **20°–30° Scorpio**, the Seven of Cups, Venus in Scorpio. [*777*, Tables CLV–CLXVI](https://publicism.info/magic/full/thelema/777/index.html)

The day/cadent spirit at the same key is **Naberius, number 24**. Therefore the Seven of Cups “carries” Naberius by day and Vapula by night.

**[Qualification]** *777* assigns the entire ten-degree decan. It does not explicitly say that Vapula alone rules 25°–30° Scorpio. Dividing day and night demons into first and second five-degree halves is a later harmonization.

### Rank and metal table

The Goetia manuscript’s own table reads:

| Rank | Planet | Metal |
|---|---|---|
| King | Sun | Gold |
| Marquis | Moon | Silver |
| **Duke** | **Venus** | **Copper** |
| Prince/Prelate | Jupiter | Tin |
| Knight | Saturn | Lead |
| President | Mercury | Mercury/quicksilver |
| Earl/Count | Venus and Moon | Copper and silver equally |

Mars and iron are conspicuously absent. [Goetia, “Observations”](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/goetia.htm)

Later Golden Dawn-derived tables frequently rationalize Earls as Mars/iron. That is a later scheme, not the wording of Sloane 3825.

### Timing

For Vapula as Duke, the Goetia says:

- from sunrise to noon;
- clear weather;
- preferably on an even lunar age from 2 to 14 days.

The text does not require a Venus planetary hour, Friday, Scorpio III, Taurus III or Capricorn III. Those are later astrological syntheses.

Possible later choices therefore depend on the system:

- **Textual Goetia:** sunrise–noon, clear weather, favorable Moon age.
- **Planetary-rank practice:** Friday or hour of Venus, copper seal.
- **Crowley/777 practice:** Scorpio III, with nocturnal symbolism.
- **Rudd practice:** use Mizrael’s seal and psalm on the reverse of the lamen.
- **Lenain angelic practice:** Mitzrael’s 7:40–8 p.m. interval and Capricorn degrees.
- **Golden Dawn angelic practice:** 26°–30° Taurus and Seven of Pentacles symbolism.

No early source combines all of them.

### Enochian overlays

**[Documented Rudd-related overlay]** Harley 6482 contains Rudd’s adaptation of John Dee’s *Tabula Sancta cum Tabulis Enochi*. Skinner and Rankine therefore discuss Rudd’s Goetia alongside Enochian and angelic material. This shows that Rudd’s broader magical collection combined multiple systems; it does not give Vapula a unique Enochian name.

**[Modern invention]** *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* by Lon Milo DuQuette, Christopher Hyatt and David P. Wilson, published in 1992, recommends the First and Second Enochian Calls as powerful general Goetic conjurations. Its account says Crowley copied the Second Call into his personal Goetia. This is a twentieth-century operational overlay, not part of Weyer, Scot, Sloane 3825 or the 1904 Vapula notice. [1992 scan](https://pauladaunt.com/books/DuQuette%20-%20Aleister%20Crowleys%20Illustrated%20Goetia.pdf)

No reliable source consulted supplied a Vapula-specific Enochian governor, aethyr or tablet square.

## Iconography

## The winged lion

**[Documented text]** The only early visual description is a lion with griffin wings. The wording remains remarkably stable from 1577 onward.

The image combines:

- the terrestrial strength and authority of the lion;
- the aerial or hybrid quality of the griffin;
- an appearance appropriate to a “great and strong Duke.”

**[Interpretive caution]** No early source identifies this lion with the Lion of Saint Mark, the Persian griffin, a Mesopotamian lamassu, Ishtar, Ariel or a specific constellation. Such comparisons may be visually suggestive but are not historical genealogies.

Weyer says wings “after the manner of a griffin.” Scot and the Goetia say “griffin’s wings.” The creature is therefore not necessarily a griffin in full: its body is explicitly leonine.

## The seal

### Verbal description

The standard Goetia sigil is a compact, upright, approximately bilateral line figure:

- a pointed, heart-like or horned loop at the top;
- a short central descending axis;
- outward-curling side strokes or hooks;
- a broad squared or rectangular lower enclosure;
- small ticks or foot-like terminals at the base in some copies.

A verbal description cannot substitute for the exact line geometry. The manuscript and printed images should be consulted directly:

- [Sloane 3825/Peterson seal image](https://www.esotericarchives.com/gifs/g60.jpg)
- [Mathers–Crowley public-domain seal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:60-Vapula_seal.png)

### Source history

- **Weyer, 1577:** no Vapula seal.
- **Scot, 1584:** no Vapula seal.
- **Sloane 3825:** seal accompanies entry 60 at fol. 109r.
- **Sloane 2731:** same recognizable core figure, with copyist differences in curvature and spacing.
- **Harley 6483:** more carefully regularized demon seal, labelled *Napula*, coupled with Mizrael’s angelic seal, Hebrew name and psalm.
- **Mathers–Crowley, 1904:** standardized printed redrawing.
- **Peterson, 2001:** reproduces the Sloane 3825 reading rather than treating the 1904 outline as an autograph of Solomon.

**[Scholarly finding]** The Goetic seals are not present in the Weyer–Scot catalogue or the earlier *Offices of Spirits* witnesses surveyed by Peterson. Their origin remains unidentified.

**[Modern false description]** A recent page describes Vapula’s seal as two concentric circles. The manuscript sigil is not a pair of concentric circles.

## Collin de Plancy

The first edition of Jacques Collin de Plancy’s *Dictionnaire infernal* appeared in 1818. Vapula is included in its infernal hierarchy. The sixth edition of 1863 gives the dedicated notice on p. 683.

Short exact excerpt:

> “Vapula, grand et puissant duc de l’enfer… un lion, avec des ailes de griffon.”

The entry says that Vapula makes a person highly skilful in mechanics and philosophy, gives intelligence to scholars, and commands thirty-six legions. It cites Wier/Weyer. [1863 Vapula entry](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_infernal/6e_%C3%A9d.%2C_1863/Vapula); [Gallica facsimile](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k5754923d)

### Does Vapula have a Louis Le Breton portrait?

**[Documented negative finding]** No. The 1863 Vapula page contains text but no individual engraving. Vapula is not among the demons named in the recognized set of 69 Le Breton portraits.

Consequently, images advertised online as “Collin de Plancy’s portrait of Vapula” should be checked carefully. They are usually:

- a different Le Breton demon;
- the Mathers seal;
- or modern winged-lion art.

## Modern art

**[Modern invention]** Modern illustrators have expanded the sparse textual image in several directions:

- heraldic winged lion;
- full griffin;
- humanoid scholar with wings;
- female or androgynous engineer;
- winged librarian;
- mechanical or cybernetic lion;
- tattoo compositions combining the seal, books, tools and Venus symbols.

David P. Wilson’s illustrations for the 1992 *Illustrated Goetia* are modern imaginative interpretations. They are not manuscript witnesses.

Modern jewelry and tattoo designs frequently simplify, mirror or close open parts of the seal. Such variants can be meaningful to practitioners, but they do not establish the manuscript form.

## People and works

## Chronological source chain

### Late antiquity: *Testament of Solomon*

**[Genre ancestor]** Supplies the narrative model of Solomon questioning and binding demons. No Vapula occurs.

### Fifteenth century: Munich handbook Clm 849

**[Catalogue ancestor]** Contains early spirit catalogues and magical operations, including spirits associated with teaching arts. No securely identified Vapula.

### 1494: Florence Plut. 89 sup. 38

**[Probable textual ancestor]** Contains Balpala, likely the earliest recoverable precursor.

### Sixteenth century: *Livre des Esperitz*

**[Related catalogue]** Preserves many relatives of Weyer’s demons but no recognized Vapula.

### 1508: Johannes Trithemius

Catalogues a *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*, establishing that the source family invoked by Weyer existed before Weyer’s publication. This does not prove that its 1508 form already contained Vapula.

### 1533: Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa

Prints the construction and list of seventy-two angels, including Mizrael. Agrippa does not pair Mizrael with Vapula.

### 1577: Johann Weyer

Publishes Vapula 59 in the fifth edition of *De praestigiis daemonum*. Weyer was a critic of witch persecutions and of many claims made by demonologists, although he accepted the reality of spiritual beings and magic. His purpose was not simply to supply a practical grimoire: he says he omitted material so the catalogue could not readily be used.

### 1584: Reginald Scot and probably Abraham Fleming

Scot publishes the first English Vapula notice. The translation closely mediates the later Goetia wording.

### Seventeenth-century Goetia compilers

Anonymous compilers reordered Scot’s catalogue, added seals and embedded it in a much larger ritual apparatus. The resulting *Lemegeton* is composite rather than a single ancient Solomonic book.

### Thomas Rudd and Peter Smart

Rudd is credited with the angel–demon counterbalancing system. The surviving Harley copies are associated with Peter Smart and postdate Rudd. The precise division between Rudd’s original work, later copying and editorial organization remains uncertain.

### 1818–1863: Collin de Plancy

Makes Vapula part of a French alphabetical demonological encyclopedia, ultimately repeating Weyer. His 1863 text does not provide a portrait.

### 1888 onward: Golden Dawn

Constructs tarot, decan and Shem-angel correspondences. The Order’s Mitzrael is Taurus 26°–30°, not Lenain’s Capricorn assignment.

### 1904: S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley

Print the first influential standalone English Goetia. Mathers supplied the underlying translation; Crowley edited, published and added material including his “Preliminary Invocation.”

**[Documented absence]** No evidence located demonstrates that Crowley personally evoked Vapula. Publishing and tabulating a spirit is not evidence of an operation.

### Frater Achad

**[Negative finding]** No Vapula-specific working or original doctrine was found in the consulted catalogues of Charles Stansfeld Jones/Frater Achad’s writings. His Qabalistic work belongs to Vapula’s broader twentieth-century occult environment, but attributing a Vapula operation to him would be unsupported.

### Poke Runyon

Carroll “Poke” Runyon’s *Book of Solomon’s Magick* presents a lodge-oriented system involving skrying, Goetic spirits and the seventy-two Shem angels. Skinner and Rankine identify it as a modern work taking its lead from the Rudd manuscript.

**[Qualification]** This establishes Runyon’s use of the Rudd-type method, not a documented personal encounter with Vapula.

### Lon Milo DuQuette

DuQuette coauthored *Aleister Crowley’s Illustrated Goetia* (1992), which popularized psychological, Thelemic and Enochian approaches to the Goetia. No independent Vapula history is supplied.

### Joseph H. Peterson

Peterson’s 2001 critical edition and Esoteric Archives transcription are the most useful readily accessible tools for comparing Sloane 3825, other manuscripts, Weyer and Scot. He distinguishes manuscript evidence from Mathers’s editorial spellings.

### Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Their *Goetia of Dr Rudd* (Golden Hoard Press, 2007) transcribes Harley 6483 and foregrounds the double-seal method. It is the principal modern source for Nappula’s Mizrael pairing.

### Jake Stratton-Kent

*The True Grimoire* and *An Inventory of Demons* locate the Goetia within a broader spirit-catalogue and grimoire tradition. Stratton-Kent reproduces and compares early catalogues but does not uncover an ancient Vapula deity.

### Jean-Patrice Boudet

Boudet’s comparative demonological tables provide the strongest published basis for associating Vapula with the 1494 Balpala.

### Owen Davies

Davies’s *Grimoires: A History of Magic Books* supplies social and publishing history for grimoires and their modern reception. It is contextual scholarship rather than a Vapula-specific study.

### Jan Veenstra and Julien Véronèse

Their research on learned magic, spirit catalogues, the *Ars notoria* and related Solomonic texts helps establish the medieval environment from which the *Offices of Spirits* family emerged. Neither provides an ancient biography for Vapula. Véronèse’s work also cautions that some supposed Solomonic book titles may be fictive or unstable.

## Modern occult reception

### Demonolatry

**[Modern tradition]** Contemporary demonolatry sources commonly assign Vapula the “enn”:

> *Renich secore Vapula typan.*

No form of this appears in Weyer, Scot, the Sloane manuscripts, Harley 6483, Mathers–Crowley or de Plancy. “Enns” belong to a modern demonolatry current popularized by authors such as S. Connolly.

Likewise, the following claims are modern practitioner lore rather than early text:

- Vapula was formerly an angel of the order of Virtues.
- Vapula served under Ariel.
- Vapula is specifically female.
- Vapula performs psychotherapy or trauma work.
- Vapula teaches computers, robotics, aeronautics or programming.
- Vapula is a psychopomp.
- Vapula rules dragons or dream magic.
- Vapula’s name means “marvellous.”
- Vapula’s colors are green and blue, or green and orange.
- walnut, papyrus, sandalwood or particular crystals are uniquely sacred to Vapula.

Some are intelligible extensions of “mechanical arts and sciences.” They should nevertheless be dated to modern practice rather than attributed to the Renaissance sources.

## Controversies and disputes

### 1. Is Vapula number 59 or 60?

Both are correct within different texts:

- Weyer: 59.
- Scot: 59.
- Goetia: 60.

The change is not caused by a single insertion immediately before Vapula. The Goetia radically rearranges the catalogue.

Weyer’s local sequence is:

58. Orobas  
59. Vapula  
60. Cimeries  
61. Amy

The Goetia’s local sequence is:

55. Orobas  
56. Gemory  
57. Ose  
58. Amy  
59. Orias  
60. Vapula  
61. Zagan

### 2. Why 69 versus 72?

Weyer publishes 69 spirits, although the Belial notice speaks of 72 kings. The Goetia reaches 72 by:

- adding **Vassago** as number 3;
- adding **Seere**, **Dantalion** and **Andromalius** at the end;
- omitting Weyer’s **Pruflas/Bufas**.

Four added minus one omitted gives a net increase of three: 69 → 72.

Scot also omits Pruflas, apparently accidentally or because his copy already lacked it. The Goetia’s dependence on Scot helps explain the omission. Peterson suggests—without claiming proof—that disarranged exemplar leaves could explain the wholesale change of order. [Peterson’s Weyer–Goetia comparison](https://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/weyer.htm)

### 3. Is Balpala definitely Vapula?

No.

- Same catalogue family: likely.
- Comparable position and rank: yes.
- Similar name: reasonably.
- Same legion count: no.
- Same appearance and office: not demonstrated.

The equation is the best scholarly reconstruction currently available, but it remains a reconstruction.

### 4. Does Vapula come from an ancient god?

No evidence has been found. Claims involving Ishtar, an Elamite goddess, a Sumerian constellation, the Lion of Venice or an ancient griffin deity are modern comparative speculation.

### 5. Duke or President?

“Duke” is supported by every main witness except Harley 6483. “President” is best classified as an isolated scribal error or innovation.

This matters because the rank changes the planetary metal:

- Duke → Venus/copper.
- President → Mercury/quicksilver.

The winged-lion description, offices and legion count do not otherwise change in Harley.

### 6. Thirty or thirty-six legions?

- Balpala, 1494: 30.
- Vapula, 1577 onward: 36.
- Harley 6483: 36.

A claim that Rudd assigns thirty legions is erroneous.

### 7. Vapula, Nappula, Napula or Naphula?

- **Vapula** is the oldest secure and best-supported form.
- **Nappula/Napula** belongs to Harley’s copy.
- **Naphula** becomes influential through Mathers–Crowley.
- None designates a demonstrably separate spirit.

### 8. Is Vapula another name for Valefar?

No. That is a modern conflation unsupported by the catalogue witnesses.

### 9. What does the name mean?

Unknown. Latin *vapulare*, Hebrew *n-f-l*, “vapour,” “wind-whipped” and “marvellous” are conjectures or modern inventions.

### 10. What is Vapula’s correct zodiacal sign?

There is no zodiacal sign in Weyer, Scot or the individual Goetia entry.

Later systems answer different questions:

- Crowley’s demon table: Scorpio III.
- Lenain’s angel: Capricorn 26°–30°.
- Golden Dawn’s angel: Taurus 26°–30°.

A source that gives Vapula all three without explanation is conflating systems.

### 11. Which tarot card is correct?

Again, system-dependent:

- **Seven of Cups**: Crowley’s explicit Goetic table for Vapula/Napula.
- **Seven of Pentacles**: Golden Dawn’s Mitzrael position.
- **Four of Pentacles**: later synthesis using Lenain’s Capricorn degrees.

The first is the best answer to “Crowley’s *777* card for spirit 60.”

### 12. Does each tarot pip carry two spirits?

In Crowley’s Goetic decan scheme, yes: one day spirit from 1–36 and one night spirit from 37–72. Vapula is the night spirit of the Seven of Cups; Naberius is its day spirit.

In the Golden Dawn angel scheme, each ten-degree pip decan similarly holds two five-degree Shem angels. Mitzrael is the second angel of the Seven of Pentacles. The demon pair and angel pair are separate tables.

### 13. Is Mitzrael’s meaning a Hebrew translation?

Not exactly. “God who relieves the oppressed” is Lenain’s interpretive divine attribute. The angel name is generated from three biblical letters plus *El*.

### 14. Which Psalm number is correct?

The text is unambiguous; the numbering differs:

- Rudd/Lenain citation: Psalm 144:18.
- standard Vulgate division: Psalm 144:17.
- Masoretic and most modern Bibles: Psalm 145:17.

Counting the superscription likely explains the additional verse.

### 15. Did Rudd invent the seventy-two angels?

No. The 72-name construction is medieval Jewish material, and the expanded angel names appear in Christian Kabbalah before Rudd. Rudd’s distinctive contribution is pairing those angels number-for-number with the Goetia demons and placing their seals on opposite faces of the lamen.

### 16. Does de Plancy portray Vapula?

He describes Vapula but supplies no Le Breton portrait. The contrary claim is a modern image-attribution error.

### 17. Is the seal ancient or Solomonic?

The Goetia presents it as Solomon’s character, which is **legend**. Historically, the sigil first appears in the early-modern Goetia manuscript tradition. It is absent from Weyer and Scot, and no ancient or medieval prototype has been securely identified.

### 18. Did Crowley, Achad or other named magicians evoke Vapula?

Publication, tabulation or discussion does not prove personal evocation. No securely documented Vapula operation by Crowley, Frater Achad, Runyon, DuQuette, Peterson, Skinner, Rankine or Stratton-Kent was found in the sources consulted.

## Open questions

1. **The missing intermediate forms.** No witness has yet shown exactly how 1494 *Balpala* became 1577 *Vapula*.

2. **The original *Liber Officiorum Spirituum*.** Weyer’s exemplar is lost. Its date, exact contents and original form of the name remain unknown.

3. **The seal’s origin.** The Vapula seal appears in Goetia manuscripts but not the Weyer–Scot chain. No source explains who designed it or whether it was adapted from an earlier magical character.

4. **Harley’s “President.”** It is probably an error, but it remains possible that the copyist was following a lost recension with altered ranks.

5. **The origin of “Naphula.”** It probably descends from *Nappula/Napula*, but Mathers did not document his authority.

6. **Rudd’s autograph.** The extant manuscript is later than Thomas Rudd. Skinner and Rankine’s c. 1655–56 exemplar is plausible but reconstructed.

7. **The exact path into Crowley’s decan table.** *777* clearly prints Napula under Scorpio/cadent/night, but Crowley does not explain why the Goetic spirits were distributed according to that particular sequence.

8. **Modern reception.** Vapula’s instructional office makes it attractive to technological fiction and contemporary practitioners. Determining the first appearance of individual modern claims—computer science, psychotherapy, gender, “enn,” angelic fall-history—would require a separate bibliography of late-twentieth-century demonolatry publications.

ENCODING. KOI8 and the Cyrillic encodings: encoding 11 of 17 in this set. Give at the top the standard number, the year, the bit width, the
repertoire and the current status.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: KOI-7 and KOI8 (1974, GOST 19768-74), the KOI8-R design by Andrey Chernov (1993, RFC 1489) in which stripping the eighth bit yields a readable Latin transliteration (so that a Russian text survives a seven-bit path as a rough Latin one), KOI8-U for Ukrainian, the rival Cyrillic encodings (CP866 in DOS, Windows-1251, ISO 8859-5, the Mac Cyrillic) and the four-way mojibake of the Russian internet of the 1990s (the 'krakozyabry'), the Unicode resolution, the Bulgarian and Serbian variants.

STANCE. Folklore as fully as fact, labeled. Every claim labeled: documented standard or text,
participant recollection, scholarly reconstruction, disputed, folklore, modern invention. Give
the worked examples byte by byte. Where a story has one witness (the placemat, the 360's unused
ASCII bit), say so. Absence of evidence is a finding.

ENCODING. ASCII: encoding 5 of 17 in this set. Give at the top the standard number, the year, the bit width, the
repertoire and the current status.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the American Standard Code for Information Interchange: the ASA X3.4 of 1963 (the first edition without lowercase), the 1965/1967 revisions that added the lowercase letters and settled the control characters, Bob Bemer's role (the escape character, the backslash, the committee work, his own account on bobbemer.com) and Hugh McGregor Ross, the seven-bit design and the parity bit, the control characters' names and their origins (BEL, DEL as all-ones, the tape-punch logic), the 1968 Johnson memorandum making ASCII the federal standard, RFC 20 (1969, Vint Cerf) making it the ARPANET's, the ISO 646 international version and its national variants (the Danish, German, Swedish letters in the # $ @ [ \ ] positions, the curly-brace problem), the 1986 revision, ASCII art, the persistence of every ASCII decision in Unicode's first 128.

STANCE. Folklore as fully as fact, labeled. Every claim labeled: documented standard or text,
participant recollection, scholarly reconstruction, disputed, folklore, modern invention. Give
the worked examples byte by byte. Where a story has one witness (the placemat, the 360's unused
ASCII bit), say so. Absence of evidence is a finding.

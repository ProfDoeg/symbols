# The tarot dossier prompt (the 78 cards)

Anthony, 2026-09-14: "Let's also do a dossier collection for the tarot cards… deep research
on each." One dossier per card, 22 trumps and 56 minors. `[NAME]` is the only substitution
point; the per-card brief follows.

---

[NAME]
Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this tarot card. I want the
fullest reconstructable history of the card, from the earliest surviving decks to the present,
chronologically and in detail. Include, wherever available:

- THE NAMES: Italian (trionfi, tarocchi), French (Marseille, Besançon, Etteilla), English,
  Spanish, German; the Golden Dawn and Thoth titles; the number and its variants; regional
  renamings (the Papess as Juno, the Pope as Jupiter, the Tower as Maison Dieu, the Fool
  unnumbered) with dates and places
- ICONOGRAPHY, DECK BY DECK: the Visconti-Sforza and the other fifteenth-century Milanese
  and Ferrarese decks (Visconti di Modrone, Brambilla, Cary-Yale, the Este cards, the Sola
  Busca for the minors, the Mantegna 'tarocchi' if relevant), the Rosenwald and Budapest
  sheets, the Tarot de Marseille lineage (Noblet 1650, Dodal, Conver 1760, Grimaud, Camoin-
  Jodorowsky), the Minchiate, the Bolognese and Sicilian patterns, Etteilla's Grand Etteilla
  (1789), Oswald Wirth (1889), the Waite-Smith deck (1909, Pamela Colman Smith's drawing and
  its sources), the Golden Dawn drafts (Book T, the Wang and Cicero reconstructions), the
  Thoth deck (Crowley and Frieda Harris, 1938-43, published 1969), Paul Foster Case's BOTA,
  and the notable modern redesigns; describe what is actually depicted in each, what changed,
  and what the changes meant
- THE GAME: the card's rank and value in tarocchi, tarot, tarock and minchiate play; the
  trump-order disputes (the three regional orders, the placement of Justice/Strength, the
  Fool's role); the earliest documents that name it (Marziano da Tortona c. 1420, the 1442
  Ferrara ledger, the sermon 'de ludo' c. 1470 with its trump list, Folengo, the 1500 poems)
- CORRESPONDENCES, EVERY SYSTEM: Court de Gébelin (1781) and the Comte de Mellet's Hebrew
  letters, Etteilla's astrology, Éliphas Lévi's Dogme et Rituel (1856), Paul Christian, Papus's
  Tarot des Bohémiens (1889), Wirth, the Golden Dawn (Hebrew letter, path on the Tree of Life,
  zodiac sign or planet or element, colours, the decan and its Book T title for the pips, the
  sub-element and zodiacal span for the courts), Waite's Pictorial Key (1910), Crowley's 777
  and Book of Thoth (with the Tzaddi/Heh swap and the Emperor/Star question), Case, Jodorowsky
  and Costa, and the modern Jungian readings; put them in a comparison table and note where
  they conflict
- DIVINATORY MEANINGS by author, upright and reversed, in their own words: Etteilla, Mathers
  (1888), Waite, Crowley, Case, Pollack, Greer
- ESOTERIC AND SYMBOLIC READINGS: the alchemical, kabbalistic, astrological, Hermetic,
  Christian and Jungian interpretations, each with its first appearance and its spreaders
- PEOPLE AND WORKS: artists, printers, occultists, scholars, poets and novelists attached to
  this card (Calvino's Castle of Crossed Destinies, Eliot's Waste Land, Yeats, Italo Calvino,
  Jodorowsky's films, Bergman); paintings and buildings that quote it
- CONTROVERSIES: the Egyptian origin myth (Gébelin), the Gypsy/Romani myth, the Cathar and
  Templar claims, Michael Dummett's and the historians' corrections (Dummett, Decker, Depaulis,
  Kaplan, Place, Farley), disputed identifications of the figures, misprints that became
  traditions

Include the good and the bad without judgment, and myth as fully as fact.
For every significant claim distinguish documented fact, scholarly reconstruction, tradition,
esoteric attribution, disputed claim, legend and modern invention, and say which sources carry
it, when the reading first appears, who spread it, and what evidence exists.
Do not use fact-checking summaries as substitutes for the sources; trace to the texts.

Structure the dossier as: Basic identification (name, number, suit or trump, earliest
attestation, Golden Dawn attribution); Iconography deck by deck (dated, placed); The game;
Chronology of attestation; Correspondences (comparison table); Divinatory meanings by author;
Esoteric and symbolic readings; People and works; Controversies and disputes; Open questions;
Sources (full list of URLs and editions consulted).
Length: as long as the material requires; for a trump a thousand lines is not too many; for a
pip card, everything that is actually attested, without padding.

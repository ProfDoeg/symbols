# Three of Pentacles: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Documented fact]** directly attested by surviving cards, archival records, rules, or published texts.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** a conclusion argued from material or documentary evidence but not explicitly stated by a contemporary source.
- **[Tradition]** a durable convention whose historical starting point may be incomplete.
- **[Esoteric attribution]** a deliberately imposed occult correspondence.
- **[Disputed claim]** a proposition for which informed authorities disagree or the evidence is inconclusive.
- **[Legend]** a historical narrative contradicted by, or unsupported in, the surviving record.
- **[Modern invention]** a recent interpretation or design choice presented as new rather than inherited.

Dates refer to creation where known, otherwise publication.

## The names and what they imply

### Coins, denari, deniers and oros

**[Documented fact]** The original suit sign is a coin. The 1442 Este wardrobe entry names “*coppe, spade, denari e bastoni*”—cups, swords, coins and batons—in four packs of *trionfi*. Helen Farley reproduces the wording and context in *A Cultural History of Tarot*, while the Morgan describes the same four-suit structure. [Farley text](https://e-isomero.com/pdf/book/1611846398.pdf), [Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525).

- Italian *denaro* means money or a coin and derives from Latin *denarius*.
- French *denier* is the cognate coin-name.
- Spanish-suited cards call the suit *oros*, literally “golds.”
- German descriptions normally translate the object as *Münzen*.
- English antiquarian and game descriptions commonly say Coins.

### From coins to pentacles

**[Esoteric attribution]** “Pentacles” is not the normal historical name of the Italian suit. Nineteenth-century French occultists used *pentacle* for a magical talisman; English occult tarot then transferred the word to Coins.

Mathers’s 1888 booklet consistently calls the suit **Pentacles**. His work appeared just before the formal establishment of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and helped normalize the English usage. [Mathers, *The Tarot*](https://hermetics.net/media-library/tarot/samuel-liddell-macgregor-mathers-the-tarot/).

**[Documented design fact]** Waite and Pamela Colman Smith made each suit emblem a disk bearing a five-pointed star. Their “pentacle” is therefore both coin-like and explicitly pentagrammatic.

**[Qualification]** The Golden Dawn did not invent either the word *pentacle* or the pentagram. Its innovation was the systematic identification of the old coin suit with Earth and with pentagram-bearing magical disks. Waite’s immensely successful deck spread that visual equation.

### Disks

**[Modern esoteric redesign]** Crowley renamed the suit **Disks**, arguing that Earth is not inert and that the symbols should suggest whirling material energy. Frieda Harris consequently drew circular mechanisms rather than ordinary currency. The name appears on the published Thoth cards and throughout *The Book of Thoth*. [Crowley, “The Small Cards”](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/small-cards).

## Iconography deck by deck

## Fifteenth-century Milanese cards

### Visconti di Modrone / Cary-Yale, approximately 1440s

**[Documented fact]** Sixty-seven cards survive at Yale’s Beinecke Library. This exceptional pack includes male and female pages and knights, suggesting six courts in each suit rather than the later four. Coins bear Visconti-period stamped-gold designs. [Yale Library account](https://library.yale.edu/news/cards-library-partners-work-together-solve-mysteries-rare-tarot-deck?page=1).

**Three of Coins:** its survival and exact attribution require caution. Published facsimiles sometimes “complete” missing cards or silently combine related Visconti groups.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** If present in the original pack, its primary content would have been three heraldic gold coins on a tooled or painted ground. These early Milanese number cards are emblematic, not narrative.

### Brambilla deck, approximately 1440s

**[Documented fact]** Forty-eight cards from this late-Gothic Milanese group are held by the Pinacoteca di Brera. Its coin cards employ ducal currency and heraldic decoration. [Brera](https://pinacotecabrera.org/news/mostra/il-segreto-dei-segreti-i-tarocchi-sola-busca/).

**Three of Coins:** no museum source located in this research pass securely catalogued a surviving Brambilla Three. Reproductions should not be treated as originals without checking their card-by-card apparatus.

### Visconti-Sforza / Pierpont Morgan–Bergamo, approximately 1450–1480

The surviving group totals seventy-four original cards: thirty-five at the Morgan, twenty-six at the Accademia Carrara, and thirteen in Bergamo. Six cards are later replacement paintings. [Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525).

**[Documented fact]** The surviving coin pips visible in the Morgan inventory are Seven, Eight and Ten. The original Three is missing.

**[Modern reconstruction]** Commercial “complete Visconti-Sforza” decks necessarily supply missing cards. The Morgan Shop explicitly identifies four reconstructed cards in its edition, although different publishers have completed the pack differently. [Morgan Shop](https://shop.themorgan.org/collections/gifts/products/619).

The general Milanese formula is regular repetition of golden coin devices, frequently with floral or ribbon ornament. No medieval scene of guild cooperation is attested on this card.

## Ferrarese and northeastern Italian evidence

### Este cards, mid-fifteenth century

Fragments associated with the Este court survive in several collections. Their classification into particular packs remains difficult.

**[Documented fact]** The Este accounts prove production and purchase of *trionfi*, including the suit of *denari*, in Ferrara in 1442 and afterward.

**[Open question]** No securely contextualized surviving Este Three of Coins was located. Any claim that a particular isolated three-coin card belonged to the 1442 commission is a reconstruction, not an archival identification.

### Rosenwald and Budapest sheets, late fifteenth or early sixteenth century

These uncut or fragmentary woodcut sheets are essential to the history of inexpensive printed tarot.

**[Documented fact]** They demonstrate the transfer of tarot from luxurious hand-painted court objects to repeatable popular prints.

**Three of Coins:** the relevant suit cards follow the ordinary pip principle—repeated suit emblems with ornamental fillers. They do not provide a securely documented narrative ancestor for Waite’s scene.

### “Mantegna Tarocchi,” approximately 1460s–1470s

**[Documented fact]** The so-called Mantegna engravings are fifty didactic images arranged in five decads: social estates, Apollo and the Muses, liberal arts, cosmic principles and celestial spheres.

**[Not applicable]** They are not a tarot pack, have no four ordinary suits and therefore possess no Three of Coins. Their Renaissance cosmology influenced later tarot comparison, but not the historical identity of this pip.

## Sola Busca, Venice, 1491

The Brera calls this the oldest complete surviving tarot deck. Its seventy-eight cards were printed from burin engravings and the unique complete copy was hand-painted with tempera and gold. Inscriptions formerly legible on two trumps establish 1491 under the Venetian era. [Brera exhibition dossier](https://pinacotecabrera.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Il-Segreto-dei-tarocchi-eng.pdf), [Brera collection notice](https://pinacotecabrera.org/news/mostra/il-segreto-dei-segreti-i-tarocchi-sola-busca/).

### What its Three of Coins depicts

**[Documented visual fact]** A human figure undertakes strenuous manual work involving a heavy architectural or masonry-like form; three coin emblems identify the suit and value. Unlike ordinary Italian pips, it is a full narrative figure composition.

The Sola Busca is the earliest complete tarot whose number cards are systematically illustrated with people and actions. That—not merely its age—is why it is central to the Waite-Smith question.

### Was it Smith’s source?

**[Scholarly reconstruction, strong at deck level]** Black-and-white photographs of the Sola Busca cards were exhibited at the British Museum in 1908, immediately before Smith designed Waite’s deck. Several Waite-Smith minors display conspicuous compositional parallels with Sola Busca cards, most famously the Ten of Wands and Three of Swords.

**[Disputed at card-specific level]** No surviving letter by Smith says, “I copied the Sola Busca Three of Coins.” Its labor motif may have contributed to the general decision to make this a work card, but Waite’s cathedral consultation is not a close one-to-one copy of the surviving Sola Busca composition. The responsible formulation is:

> The Sola Busca probably helped establish Smith’s illustrated-pip vocabulary; it is not proved to be the sole or direct source of the cathedral scene.

The Brera documentation establishes the object and date, not Smith’s intent. The direct-source claim is therefore evidentially weaker than the deck-level influence.

## Tarot de Marseille lineage

### Jean Noblet, Paris, approximately 1650

The Three of Deniers is a non-scenic pip:

- three circular coin emblems;
- one conventionally above two, or integrated within a vertical symmetry;
- curling stems, leaves and blossoms filling the field;
- no architect, monk or mason;
- no occult star required inside the coins.

### Jean Dodal, Lyon, approximately 1701

Dodal retains the emblematic *Trois de Deniers*. The exact engraving changes—coin faces, stem curvature, flower shape and color distribution—but the semantic level remains ornamental rather than narrative.

### Nicolas Conver, Marseille, 1760

Conver’s card has three ornate deniers bound into a symmetrical floral structure. It exemplifies the mature Marseille Type II vocabulary. The year and Marseille place are printed on Conver’s Two of Coins, functioning as the deck’s maker/date cartouche.

The historical sequence Noblet–Dodal–Conver is documented by collections and restoration projects. [Historical Marseille imagiers](https://www.letarot.com/imagiers-du-tarot/index/).

### Grimaud / Paul Marteau, 1930

**[Documented fact]** Marteau’s *Ancien Tarot de Marseille*, issued by Grimaud, regularized outlines and drastically standardized the color palette. It became the dominant twentieth-century commercial Marseille.

**[Modern reconstruction presented as tradition]** Its Three continues the Conver-type floral pip but is a twentieth-century redrawing, not a facsimile of a single old deck.

### Camoin–Jodorowsky, 1997

Philippe Camoin and Alejandro Jodorowsky produced a synthetic “restoration,” consulting numerous historical cards while also reconstructing tiny marks and colors according to their symbolic theory.

**[Documented design fact]** Its Three of Deniers remains a floral pip.

**[Disputed claim]** Jodorowsky sometimes speaks as if the design restores an original, complete symbolic code. Historians generally treat it as a modern composite because no single surviving prototype contains all its selected details.

## Besançon pattern

The Besançon tarot is primarily distinguished from Marseille by replacing the Papess and Pope trumps with **Juno and Jupiter**, a convention found in Swiss, Alsatian and neighboring markets from the eighteenth century.

**Three of Deniers:** essentially the regional Marseille-style ornamental pip. Juno/Jupiter does not alter its identity.

## Bologna

The Bolognese *tarocchino* pack was reduced from seventy-eight to sixty-two cards by removing numeral cards Two through Five from every ordinary suit.

**[Documented consequence]** The Three of Coins is absent from the reduced Bolognese playing pack. Modern “Bolognese tarot” divination decks that include it have restored or reinvented a card unavailable in traditional tarocchino play.

## Minchiate

Minchiate expanded the trump series to forty-one while retaining the ordinary suits.

**[Documented design tradition]** Its Coins Three remains a suit-emblem card, normally three coin-like roundels with foliage. It does not acquire a special zodiacal trump identity merely because the Minchiate trump series includes astrological signs and elements.

## Sicilian tarot

The traditional Sicilian pack is reduced. In surviving game forms, the Coins suit may run only from Four upward, with a special Ace sometimes used as the lowest card.

**[Documented consequence]** A Three of Coins is generally absent. [Rules of Sicilian Tarocchi](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/sicilian.html).

## Etteilla’s Grand Etteilla, first published 1789

Etteilla reordered and continuously numbered all seventy-eight cards. His system did not distinguish “Major” and “Minor Arcana” in the later conventional manner.

### Card 75, *Le Trois de Denier*

**[Documented fact]** Surviving editions label the card’s values:

- upright: **Noblesse** or, in some transmission, **Fame/Renown**;
- reversed: **Enfants** or **Médiocrité**, depending on recension.

A nineteenth-century *Grand Etteilla* manual depicts three decorated coin forms above a small child medallion. [Digitized *Le grand Etteilla*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2f/Le_grand_Etteilla%2C_ou%2C_l%27art_de_tirer_les_cartes_..._Le_tout_recueilli_..._%28IA_b29321220%29.pdf).

**[Textual complication]** “Nobility/children” and “fame/mediocrity” are not fully interchangeable pairs. They reflect variant decks and dictionaries within the Etteilla publishing tradition. Any claim that Etteilla assigned one immutable English pair oversimplifies the transmission.

## Oswald Wirth, 1889

Wirth’s original twenty-two-card *Tarot des imagiers du moyen âge* project comprised trumps only.

**[Not applicable]** There was no Wirth Three of Coins in the 1889 deck. Later seventy-eight-card products sold under Wirth’s name supplement his trumps with newly designed pips; those are later editorial constructions.

## Golden Dawn manuscripts / “Book T,” late 1880s–1890s

The order assigned this card:

> “Lord of Material Works.”

It placed three pentacles in an upward triangle, surrounded them with a rose branch, and assigned Mars in Capricorn, Binah of final Heh, and two Shem angels.

**[Esoteric attribution]** This is the first coherent synthesis of:

- Three = Binah;
- Coins/Pentacles = Earth and final Heh;
- the second decan of Capricorn;
- Mars as decan ruler;
- a named moral/divinatory complex of constructive work.

Its published wording includes constructive force, creation, erection, increase of substance and business ability, with possible selfishness, narrowness and pursuit of impossibilities. [Book T text reproduced with the card](https://book-of-thoth.net/Three-of-Disks-Works).

### Wang and Cicero reconstructions

- Robert Wang’s *Golden Dawn Tarot* (1977), painted under Israel Regardie’s direction, translates the order manuscripts into a complete colored deck.
- Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero’s *Golden Dawn Magical Tarot* (2001) is another manuscript-based reconstruction.

**[Modern reconstruction]** Neither should be mistaken for a single surviving “official 1888 Golden Dawn deck.” They reconstruct distributed written descriptions, color scales and later order practice.

## Waite-Smith, 1909

### Pamela Colman Smith’s scene

Inside or beneath a Gothic church vault:

- a young sculptor or mason stands on a bench;
- he faces two other figures;
- one wears a monastic cowl;
- another holds an architectural plan;
- three pentacles form a trefoil-like triangle in the stone tracery;
- rose and cross motifs occupy neighboring compartments.

Waite identifies the artisan as the same young craftsman later seen at work in the Eight of Pentacles.

His description says, in part:

> “A sculptor at his work in a monastery.”

His divinatory keywords are:

> “Métier, trade, skilled labour.”

Reversed:

> “Mediocrity, in work and otherwise.”

Both quotations are from *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*, Part III. [Waite text](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/gbt/gbt47.htm).

### What changed

1. The ornamental pip became a social narrative.
2. Three objects became three interacting persons.
3. “Nobility/rank” was translated into recognized professional mastery.
4. The Golden Dawn’s “Material Works” became literal building work.
5. The coin became a pentagrammed magical disk.
6. The church joined material, intellectual and religious labor.

**[Modern reading]** “Teamwork” is now the card’s dominant keyword, but Waite’s text emphasizes skill, trade, employment, glory and aristocracy more explicitly than egalitarian collaboration. The modern teamwork formula arises naturally from Smith’s three figures but is an interpretive consolidation after 1909.

## Paul Foster Case and B.O.T.A.

Case’s B.O.T.A. tarot, drawn by Jessie Burns Parke, is principally a line-art teaching deck designed for students to color. It follows Waite and Golden Dawn structure while revising selected symbolism.

Case’s *Oracle of the Tarot* associates the Three of Coins with the constructive, formative power of Three and with Saturn/Binah symbolism, describing Three as a divine number. [Case, *Oracle of the Tarot*](https://media-01.imu.nl/storage/divona.be/17547/oracle-of-tarot-paul-foster-case.pdf).

**[Esoteric attribution]** Case inherits Mars in Capricorn and Material Works from the Golden Dawn, then reframes the card through “mental imagery,” planning and the embodiment of an idea.

## Thoth Tarot, painted 1938–1943; first full publication 1969

Crowley and Frieda Harris rename the card **Three of Disks: Works**.

### What Harris depicts

- a pyramid seen from above;
- three wheels forming its base;
- alchemical Salt, Sulphur and Mercury;
- mottled dark sea or matrix surrounding it;
- Mars and Capricorn symbols;
- red structural planes suggesting martial energy;
- no human laborers.

Crowley writes:

> “The influence of Binah in the sphere of Earth shows the material establishment of the idea.”

He continues that Mars in Capricorn is constructive:

> “like that of the builder or engineer.”

[Crowley, *The Book of Thoth*](https://book-of-thoth.net/Three-of-Disks-Works).

### Meaning of the change

The Waite-Smith scene dramatizes cooperation in a recognizably Christian building. Harris abstracts the same correspondence into cosmic engineering: form crystallizing out of Binah’s “sea,” powered by exalted Mars.

**[Esoteric attribution]** Crowley identifies the pyramid’s three wheels with:

- Mercury, Sulphur and Salt;
- the three *gunas*: sattva, rajas and tamas;
- the Hebrew mother letters Aleph, Shin and Mem;
- Air, Fire and Water establishing material form.

## Notable modern redesigns

### Robin Wood Tarot, 1991

Retains the craftsman/work motif but presents it in a warmer neopagan and craft-oriented idiom. It helped spread “creative accomplishment” and “building tangible results.”

### Jodorowsky–Camoin, 1997

Returns to the floral Marseille pip and reads direction, color, vegetation and coin placement rather than staging a trade scene.

### Wild Unknown, 2012

**[Modern invention]** Three pentacles form a stable mountain or geometric structure. Human collaboration becomes structural integration.

### Everyday and workplace-oriented decks

Many twenty-first-century decks show design teams, builders, artists, engineers or community organizers. These are descendants of Smith’s social composition, not recoveries of medieval meaning.

## The game

## Rank

The card’s divinatory title has no role in play. It is an ordinary suit card.

### Classical Italian ranking

In most older Italian tarot games:

- Swords and Batons rank 10 high down to Ace low.
- Cups and Coins reverse the numeral order: Ace high, then 2, 3, through 10 low.

Thus the Three of Coins is:

- below Ace and Two;
- above Four through Ten;
- below all four court cards.

This reverse ordering is documented across traditional tarot-game families and is summarized in rules derived from Dummett’s work. [Historical game reconstruction](https://www.knauer.org/mike/sca/classes/Class_-_Game_of_Tarot.pdf), [Tarot-game rank overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarot_card_games).

### Modern French tarot

French-suited modern game packs replace Coins with Diamonds. The Three of Diamonds ranks between Four and Two in the straightforward descending sequence:

King, Queen, Knight, Jack, 10 … 3, 2, 1.

It counts **one-half point** under the modern paired-card accounting method. [French Tarot rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/frtarot.html).

### Austrian and central-European Tarock

Most modern Austrian packs are shortened to fifty-four cards and omit low red-suit numeral cards. Consequently, an exact Three corresponding to Coins/Diamonds usually does not exist. Where a longer pack retains it, it is an ordinary low card and ordinarily has no intrinsic game-point value.

### Minchiate

The Three of Coins survives as an ordinary suit card, with rank governed by the reversed round-suit order. Its decorative imagery has no special effect.

### Bologna and Sicily

It is absent from the reduced Bolognese and usual Sicilian packs. This absence is a game-history fact, not occult symbolism.

## Trick-taking function

A Three of Coins:

1. must normally follow Coins if that suit is led;
2. may win only against lower-ranking Coins if no trump intervenes;
3. loses to any trump;
4. contributes little or nothing to card-point totals, depending on the local counting method.

Its card-reading meaning—work, craftsmanship, fame or mediocrity—is irrelevant to the game.

## Trump-order disputes and the Fool

These issues do not change this pip’s rank but define the pack surrounding it.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Dummett grouped early Italian trump orders into three broad traditions:

- **A:** Bologna and Florence;
- **B:** Ferrara and associated northeastern centers;
- **C:** Milan, later ancestral to the Marseille order.

Justice, Fortitude/Strength and the theological/cosmic trumps occupy different relative positions in these groups.

**[Documented game tradition]** The Fool was normally unnumbered and functioned as an excuse: it could be played instead of following suit and ordinarily returned to its player’s captured pile after compensation. It was not simply “trump zero” in early play.

Names such as Papess→Juno and Pope→Jupiter belong to regional trump alteration and do not rename any pip.

### The exact Golden Dawn formula

The assignment can be reconstructed as:

- **3** → Binah, Understanding, the Great Mother and formative matrix;
- **Pentacles** → Earth;
- **Earth** → Assiah and final Heh of יהוה;
- **Three of Pentacles** → Binah operating through the Earth quarter;
- **zodiacal sector** → second decan of Capricorn;
- **planetary ruler** → Mars;
- **dignity** → Mars is exalted in Capricorn;
- **result** → disciplined energy capable of durable construction.

The commonly printed Book T phrase “Binah of ה final” is therefore not a Hebrew letter assigned uniquely to this card. It is the suit’s Tetragrammaton attribution.

### Angels

Printed Book T recensions give forms resembling **Yehuiah** and **Lehahiah**.

**[Textual dispute]** Modern Shem-ha-Mephorash lists frequently assign these angels to Virgo rather than Capricorn. The discrepancy arises because Golden Dawn tarot tables and later angel calendars do not always begin or index the seventy-two quinances in the same place. It should not be “corrected” silently.

### Tzaddi/Heh and Emperor/Star

**[Not card-specific]** Crowley’s “Tzaddi is not the Star” revision exchanges the Hebrew-letter logic of the Emperor and Star, while retaining their zodiacal Aries/Aquarius functions in a looped arrangement. It does not alter the Three of Disks, final Heh, Binah, Mars or Capricorn.

### Etteilla’s unstable transmission

The surviving evidence does not justify pretending that one pair is uniquely authentic. Mathers says his additional remarks are “chiefly taken from Etteilla,” yet his Three uses Nobility/Children. Other Grand Etteilla card traditions print Fame/Mediocrity. [Etteilla comparison](https://etteilla.wordpress.com/etteilla-ii/etteilla-grand-pentacles/), [Mathers](https://hermetics.net/media-library/tarot/samuel-liddell-macgregor-mathers-the-tarot/).

### Crowley and reversals

Crowley normally interpreted elemental and planetary dignity rather than turning a card upside down. “Reversed: bad teamwork” is therefore a modern RWS-derived convention, not Crowley’s printed formula.

## Esoteric and symbolic readings

## Christian

### Medieval/Renaissance coin imagery

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Coins could suggest wealth, exchange, civic authority, dynastic legitimacy or generosity, depending on context. In the Visconti packs they often reproduce or imitate ducal money and heraldry. There is no evidence that the medieval Three represented the Trinity.

### Waite-Smith church

**[Documented imagery; interpretive meaning]** The Gothic setting makes work into a sacred vocation. The monk, designer and artisan can be read as religious authority, intellectual plan and material execution.

**[Modern esoteric reading]** The three figures are sometimes identified with body, mind and spirit, or apprentice, architect and patron. Waite does not explicitly name them that way.

### “Secret society initiation”

**[Modern invention]** Some readers see the hooded figure, blueprint and vaulted threshold as an initiatory lodge scene. No early source was found for this card-specific identification. It develops from the visual ambiguity of Smith’s scene.

## Qabalistic

**[Golden Dawn attribution]** Binah gives delimitation, architecture and intelligible form. In Assiah, it is not merely an idea of structure but actual constructed matter.

An upward triangle of three pentacles can therefore represent:

- the Supernal triad reflected in Earth;
- the first stable plane, since three points define a plane;
- generative understanding becoming material organization.

These are Golden Dawn or post–Golden Dawn readings, not meanings demonstrably present in Marseille floral ornament.

## Astrological

Mars in Capricorn is the most stable part of the correspondence:

- Mars supplies effort, cutting, tools, ambition and force.
- Capricorn supplies hierarchy, discipline, stone, architecture and durable achievement.
- Traditional astrology calls Mars exalted in Capricorn.
- The resulting energy is controlled rather than impulsive.

**[Modern psychological extension]** This produces the familiar advice: make a plan, accept standards, coordinate expertise and build something that survives enthusiasm.

## Alchemical

Crowley’s card makes the alchemy explicit:

- Salt = fixation/body;
- Sulphur = active fiery principle;
- Mercury = volatile mediating principle;
- their coordinated wheels establish material form.

**[Esoteric attribution]** The pyramid also embodies stability through triadic process. This is Harris and Crowley’s twentieth-century synthesis. It is not supported as the intent of the medieval Three of Coins.

## Hermetic

Etteilla and later French occultists treated the deck as a “Book of Thoth.” The Golden Dawn recast that claim into a working table of planetary, zodiacal, elemental, color and qabalah correspondences.

For this card the Hermetic lesson is that material form is not spiritually dead: disciplined force organizes matter according to intelligible pattern.

## Numerological

Possible readings of Three include:

- synthesis after the polarity of Two;
- production or offspring;
- a minimum stable geometry;
- mother/form in Binah;
- creator, plan and execution.

**[Historical caution]** These meanings come from several numerological traditions. Their presence in a modern reading does not prove that a fifteenth-century cardmaker encoded them.

## Jungian and psychological

**[Modern invention/tradition]** Jung wrote about archetypes, symbols and alchemy but did not publish a canonical interpretation of the Three of Pentacles.

Later Jungian tarot writers read the three figures as differentiated psychic functions working toward a common structure. Possible formulations include:

- ego accepting instruction from the wider psyche;
- reconciliation of instinct, intellect and inherited tradition;
- individuation enacted through craft rather than private insight;
- recognition that competence grows within community.

This is a modern application of Jungian concepts to Smith’s image, not an attribution by Jung.

## People and works

## Artists, printers and occultists directly connected

- **Bonifacio Bembo / Zavattari workshop:** competing attributions for Milanese Visconti cards. The Morgan notes both the traditional Bembo and more recent Zavattari attribution.
- **Jacopo de Sagramoro:** paid in Ferrara in 1442 to paint four *trionfi* packs.
- **Nicola di Maestro Antonio:** commonly proposed artist of the Sola Busca; attribution remains scholarly rather than signed certainty.
- **Jean Noblet, Jean Dodal, Nicolas Conver:** principal historical makers in the Marseille lineage.
- **Jean-Baptiste Alliette (“Etteilla”):** first author to publish a systematic tarot cartomancy and first designer of a deck primarily organized for divination.
- **Éliphas Lévi:** made tarot central to nineteenth-century occult synthesis.
- **Gérard Encausse (“Papus”):** spread the “Tarot of the Bohemians” system and Tetragrammaton theory.
- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers:** introduced a compact English divinatory list and helped form Golden Dawn tarot doctrine.
- **Pamela Colman Smith:** designed the narrative scene that now dominates the card.
- **Arthur Edward Waite:** commissioned and interpreted Smith’s deck.
- **Jessie Burns Parke and Paul Foster Case:** B.O.T.A. redesign and teaching system.
- **Lady Frieda Harris and Aleister Crowley:** geometric, alchemical and Thelemic transformation into “Works.”
- **Paul Marteau:** standardized the Grimaud Marseille.
- **Philippe Camoin and Alejandro Jodorowsky:** late-twentieth-century synthetic Marseille restoration.
- **Robert Wang; Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero:** principal Golden Dawn reconstruction decks.
- **Rachel Pollack and Mary K. Greer:** major modern psychological and practice-oriented interpreters.

## Literature and film

### Italo Calvino, *The Castle of Crossed Destinies*

Calvino constructs narratives from tarot layouts, using first a Visconti-type deck and then Marseille imagery. The work is important to tarot’s literary reception, but no securely isolated episode was traced to the Three of Coins alone.

### T. S. Eliot, *The Waste Land*

Eliot’s invented “drowned Phoenician Sailor” and “one-eyed merchant,” alongside named tarot cards, form Madame Sosostris’s reading.

**[Not card-specific]** The Three of Pentacles is not named. Claims that the merchant secretly “is” this card are interpretive speculation.

### W. B. Yeats

Yeats participated in the Golden Dawn and knew its symbolic system.

**[Not specifically attested]** No poem or prose passage located in this pass makes the Three of Pentacles an explicit subject.

### Ingmar Bergman

*The Seventh Seal* uses chess, Death and medieval religious imagery, not a documented quotation of the Three of Pentacles.

### Alejandro Jodorowsky

Jodorowsky’s films repeatedly use tarot-like ritual, archetypal and alchemical imagery. He also co-authored *The Way of Tarot* with Marianne Costa.

**[Caution]** Resemblance between a film scene and Smith’s three figures is not evidence of a deliberate quotation unless Jodorowsky or production documentation identifies it.

## Paintings and buildings

No medieval church or named painting was found that can be established as Smith’s direct model. The architecture is generic English Gothic ecclesiastical vocabulary: clustered columns, pointed arch, stone tracery and carved ornament.

**[Open question]** Specific churches have been proposed informally, but no sketchbook notation, letter or commission document presently establishes one.

## Controversies and disputes

## Egyptian origin

Court de Gébelin argued in 1781 that tarot preserved an Egyptian sacred book. Etteilla expanded this into an elaborate Book-of-Thoth genealogy.

**Label:** **[Legend / esoteric origin myth]**

**Evidence for it:** analogical interpretation of card images, eighteenth-century Egyptomania and asserted hidden etymologies.

**Evidence against it:**

- documentary appearances cluster in fifteenth-century northern Italy;
- surviving early cards are materially Italian;
- the first records describe manufacture and games;
- no Egyptian prototype with tarot’s structure survives;
- no pre-1781 source states that tarot is the Book of Thoth.

Dummett writes that no occult significance or non-game use is documented before Court de Gébelin’s intervention. [Dummett, “Origins of Tarot”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1981/05/14/origins-of-tarot/). Decker, Depaulis and Dummett trace how later occultists constructed the Egyptian lineage. [*A Wicked Pack of Cards*](https://books.google.com/books?id=nVyDAAAAMAAJ).

For the Three of Pentacles specifically, Egyptian-origin claims explain neither the Italian coin suit nor the transition from floral pip to Smith’s mason.

## Gypsy/Romani origin

Papus’s title *Le Tarot des Bohémiens* and nineteenth-century writers popularized the idea that Romani people brought tarot from Egypt or India.

**Label:** **[Legend]**

Romani fortune-tellers did use cards, but the evidence indicates adoption of cards already circulating in Europe. Waite himself observed that evidence for Romani tarot use emerged only after about 1840. [Waite/de Laurence text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/43548/43548-h/43548-h.htm).

The term “Gypsy tarot” records a history of European occult publishing, not the demonstrated ethnic origin of the cards.

## Cathar, Templar and Grail claims

Some twentieth-century writers claimed tarot concealed:

- Cathar doctrine;
- Templar survival;
- Grail objects;
- a heretical anti-Church code;
- secret initiatory architecture.

**Label:** **[Disputed claim / modern legend-making]**

Theories commonly reason backward from later Marseille imagery. No fifteenth-century commission, rule, sermon or cardmaker’s document identifies the Three of Coins—or tarot generally—as Cathar or Templar scripture.

A literary chain connecting Grail objects to tarot suits developed in modern esoteric writing, not medieval card evidence. [Study of modern legend-making](https://go.gale.com/ps/i.do?asid=07def655&id=GALE%7CA21250627&it=r&p=AONE&u=googlescholar&v=2.1).

## “Sola Busca directly copied”

**Label:** **[Strong deck-level reconstruction; disputed card-level assertion]**

Established:

- Smith could have encountered British Museum Sola Busca photographs before her 1909 designs.
- Several cards share unusual motifs.
- Sola Busca demonstrates a precedent for narrative minor cards.

Not established:

- a written statement that Smith copied its Three of Coins;
- that Waite instructed her to do so;
- that its object or action uniquely explains her cathedral consultation.

The safest conclusion is influence on the illustrated minors as a project, not documentary proof for every card.

## “Pentacles are ancient tarot symbols”

**Label:** **[Anachronism]**

Early cards show coins. Pentagrammed disks belong to occult reinterpretation. Calling a Visconti *denaro* a Golden Dawn “Earth pentacle” imposes a later meaning on an earlier object.

## “The card always meant teamwork”

**Label:** **[Modern simplification]**

The chain is:

1. Etteilla: nobility/fame, children/mediocrity;
2. Mathers: dignity, rank, power;
3. Golden Dawn: material works and business;
4. Waite: skilled trade, employment and glory;
5. Smith: three people visibly consulting;
6. modern handbooks: teamwork and collaboration.

Teamwork is a plausible reading of Smith but not the card’s timeless meaning.

## The identities of Smith’s figures

Common identifications include:

- mason, monk and architect;
- apprentice, priest and patron;
- worker, designer and supervisor;
- body, spirit and mind;
- guild candidate and examiners.

**Label:** **[Partly documented, partly disputed]**

Waite securely identifies the sculptor and monastery. The precise professions and genders of the other two figures are not settled by his text.

## Misprints and traditions

No consequential historical misprint unique to the Three of Pentacles was located.

Broader examples include:

- conflicting publication dates for Waite’s *Key* and *Pictorial Key*;
- variant Etteilla keywords becoming treated as a single original list;
- commercial Visconti reconstructions presented without prominently distinguishing lost originals;
- divergent spellings of Golden Dawn angel names;
- treating Crowley’s “Works” as “Material Works,” although the latter is the Golden Dawn title and the printed Thoth card says simply **WORKS**.

The celebrated German *Mond* (“Moon”) confusion affecting images of the World trump has no bearing on this pip.

## Open questions

1. **Which surviving fifteenth-century Three of Coins is earliest?**  
   A definitive answer requires a card-by-card concordance of the Visconti di Modrone, Brambilla, Este fragments and museum accessions. The earliest complete securely dated example is Sola Busca, but an earlier isolated Milanese card may survive under a disputed pack attribution.

2. **What exactly is the Sola Busca figure doing?**  
   Descriptions vary among carving, carrying, building or interacting with a large architectural object. High-resolution examination and the Brera catalogue raisonné are preferable to modern facsimile captions.

3. **Did Smith personally attend the 1908 exhibition?**  
   Access to the photographs and circumstantial opportunity are stronger than proof of physical attendance. A letter, diary entry or museum visitor record would settle the point.

4. **What building inspired Smith?**  
   No primary documentation identifies one.

5. **Which Etteilla keyword pair is earliest?**  
   The deck, manuals and later recensions require bibliographical collation. “Nobility/Children” is securely transmitted through Mathers; “Fame/Mediocrity” is securely present in later Grand Etteilla traditions. Priority was not conclusively established here.

6. **Were the Book T angel names originally intended for the zodiacal decan or derived by a different indexing scheme?**  
   Manuscript comparison is needed; modern tables silently harmonize incompatible sequences.

7. **How much of the RWS minor iconography came from Waite versus Smith?**  
   Waite commissioned and supplied doctrinal parameters, but surviving evidence does not provide a card-by-card division of invention.

8. **Can “teamwork” be dated precisely?**  
   It appears to be a twentieth-century consolidation of Smith’s visual narrative. A systematic handbook corpus could determine its earliest exact occurrence.

9. **Pollack and Greer pagination.**  
   Their books exist in multiple editions. Exact page citations should always identify edition and printing; summaries without that information are unsafe.

## Conclusions

The card began as an ordinary low-value **Tre di Denari** in a fifteenth-century Italian trick-taking pack. Its imagery was initially three coins plus decoration. The Sola Busca transformed the pip into a human action scene in 1491, but no evidence shows that this scene then became a continuing European card tradition. Marseille makers returned to symmetrical floral ornament.

Etteilla first gave the Three a systematic cartomantic identity, although his surviving keyword traditions conflict. Mathers transmitted the card into English as Nobility/Rank versus Children/Commencement. The Golden Dawn then performed the decisive occult synthesis: **Lord of Material Works**, Binah of final Heh, Mars in Capricorn.

Smith converted that abstract formula into the now-famous consultation among a mason and two companions inside a monastery. Waite emphasized skilled trade and recognized workmanship; later readers distilled the picture into “teamwork.” Crowley and Harris removed the people and church but retained the underlying Golden Dawn attribution, translating it into an alchemical pyramid titled **Works**.

Nothing in the surviving evidence connects the historical card to ancient Egypt, Romani transmission, Cathars or Templars. Those stories remain significant parts of tarot’s reception history, but they arise centuries after the card’s Italian appearance.

# Seven of Pentacles: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **[Fact]** directly documented by a surviving card, archival record, rulebook, or author’s text.
- **[Reconstruction]** an inference accepted or seriously entertained by historians but not directly documented.
- **[Tradition]** a conventional reading or nomenclature transmitted within a school.
- **[Esoteric attribution]** a deliberately imposed occult correspondence, not a claim about the card’s original purpose.
- **[Disputed]** a claim for which informed authorities disagree or the evidence is incomplete.
- **[Legend]** an origin story contradicted or unsupported by the surviving record.
- **[Modern invention]** a demonstrably recent interpretation or design presented without an older attestation.

Short quotations comply with source-access and copyright limits. Page numbers are supplied where the consulted edition exposed stable pagination; where online transcriptions lack it, the section or card heading is given instead.

## Basic identification

### Names, number, suit

| Context | Attested or conventional name |
|---|---|
| Fifteenth-century Italian | **Sette di denari**, “Seven of Coins”; suit also *denari*, *danari*, *monete* |
| Italian tarot terminology | A numeral or pip card among the *carte da trionfi* and later *tarocchi* |
| French | **Sept de deniers** |
| Tarot de Marseille | **Sept de Deniers**; usually untitled on the physical card |
| Etteilla | **Le Sept de Denier**, card **71** in the consulted *Grand Etteilla* |
| English historical | **Seven of Coins** |
| English occult/RWS | **Seven of Pentacles** |
| Crowley–Harris Thoth | **Seven of Disks — Failure** |
| Spanish | **Siete de Oros** or, in translated occult decks, **Siete de Pentáculos** |
| German | **Sieben der Münzen**, **Sieben Münzen**; occult translations also **Sieben der Pentakel** |
| Golden Dawn | **Seven of Pentacles — Lord of Success Unfulfilled** |
| Number | Seven; normally seven repeated suit emblems. No Roman numeral was required in early Italian pips because the emblems themselves supplied the count. |

**[Fact]** It is a suit card, not a trump. Consequently, regional trump renamings—Papess to Juno, Pope to Jupiter in Besançon patterns, *La Maison Dieu* for the Tower, the unnumbered Fool, and the Justice/Strength exchange—do not rename or renumber this card.

**[Fact]** Its earliest surviving candidates are Milanese painted Sevens of Coins in the Cary-Yale/Visconti di Modrone and Brambilla groups, generally placed in the 1440s or mid-fifteenth century. Dating and precedence among these packs remain disputed. The securely catalogued Morgan Visconti-Sforza example is Milan, approximately 1450–80, MS M.630.31: “seven coins … surrounded by floreate border.” [Morgan object record](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/tarot-cards/the-seven-of-coins), [Morgan image catalogue](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/31/158525).

**[Finding]** The Sola Busca Seven is therefore not the earliest surviving Seven of Coins. Its special importance is that the Sola Busca, engraved in 1491 and later hand-coloured, is the earliest substantially complete surviving tarot whose numeral cards receive independent scenic or object-based illustrations.

### Golden Dawn attribution

**[Esoteric attribution, c. 1888–1900 manuscript tradition]**

- Title: **Lord of Success Unfulfilled**
- Astrology: **Saturn in Taurus**
- Decan: **20°–30° Taurus**
- Sephirah: **Netzach**, the seventh sephirah
- Suit/element: Pentacles/Earth
- Qabalistic world and divine-name letter: **Assiah; final Heh**
- Book T design: an angelic hand issuing from cloud, holding a rose branch around seven pentacles
- Listed divinatory qualities: promises of success followed by incomplete results, delay, sloth, disappointment, sometimes gain after labour

The searchable *Book T* transcription explicitly gives “Saturn in Taurus, 20°–30°” and “The Lord of Success Unfulfilled.” [Book T transcription](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Mathers/Book-T.html). The manuscript also assigns “Pentacles to Earth and Heh final.” [Golden Dawn manuscript collection](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/golden_dawn_manuscripts.html).

# Iconography, deck by deck

## Fifteenth-century Milan

### Cary-Yale / Visconti di Modrone

**Date and place:** Milan, usually c. 1442–47 or broadly mid-fifteenth century; Beinecke Library.

**[Fact]** A Seven of Coins survives. Seven large gilded coin emblems occupy an ornamental field. There is no worker, shrub, agricultural implement, pentagram, astrological glyph, or written divinatory title. The “coins” reproduce or evoke actual Visconti money and court heraldry.

**[Disputed dating]** Some historians place Cary-Yale before the other Visconti groups, partly because its coin devices resemble Filippo Maria Visconti’s florin and because of its unusual six-rank courts. Others prefer later dates. The card’s survival is secure; its exact year and original deck structure are not. [Cary-Yale Seven image record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cary-Yale_Tarot_deck_-_Seven_of_Coins.jpg).

**Meaning of the design:** **[Reconstruction]** Display of value, dynastic magnificence, and legibility in play—not “investment,” “patience,” or “harvest,” which are later divinatory ideas.

### Brambilla / Brera-Brambilla

**Date and place:** Milan, generally mid-fifteenth century; Pinacoteca di Brera.

**[Fact]** The Italian national cultural catalogue identifies an extant *Sette di Denari* in the Brambilla pack and attributes the group to Bonifacio Bembo’s milieu. [Italian catalogue record](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0300097697-31).

Its seven coin emblems are presented decoratively against a luxury ground. As in the Cary-Yale and Visconti-Sforza cards, repetition communicates the rank.

### Visconti-Sforza / Colleoni-Baglioni

**Date and place:** Milan, c. 1450–80; divided among the Morgan Library, Accademia Carrara, and Colleoni collection.

**[Fact]** Morgan MS M.630.31 has seven gold coins within an elaborate flowering surround. The coins carry courtly/dynastic devices. There is no human figure and no narrative action.

**[Scholarly dispute]** Attribution has moved among Bonifacio Bembo, Francesco Zavattari, and related workshop hands; later replacement cards may be by another artist. The Seven itself belongs to the ordinary decorated-pip component. [Morgan full catalogue](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525).

### “Visconti-Sforza” versus “Cary-Yale”

**[Finding]** Popular reproductions regularly merge the names or silently complete missing cards. That can conceal important differences:

- Cary-Yale probably possessed additional female court ranks.
- The Morgan/Bergamo/Colleoni pack reflects the standard four courts.
- Modern facsimiles sometimes supply lost cards; the original Visconti-Sforza Seven, however, survives and does not need reconstruction.

## Ferrarese, Este, Rosenwald, and Budapest materials

### Este cards

**[Fact]** Fragmentary fifteenth-century Ferrarese or north-Italian packs survive, but not every original suit sequence is complete.

**[Finding]** I found no museum catalogue establishing an Este Seven of Coins earlier than the Milanese examples. It is unsafe to describe a specific “Este Seven” without identifying an accession number.

### Rosenwald sheets

**Date:** conventionally late fifteenth or early sixteenth century; north Italian; National Gallery of Art, Washington.

**[Fact]** The uncut sheets preserve popular woodcut tarot material with Italian suits. Their importance is evidence for cheaper, repeatable production alongside luxury painted packs. [Met exhibition catalogue](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_World_in_Play_Luxury_Cards_1430_1540.pdf).

**[Finding]** Available institutional summaries do not securely isolate a complete Seven of Coins comparable to Morgan M.630.31. Claims that a specific reconstructed Rosenwald Seven supplies an occult image require a sheet-and-position citation.

### Budapest sheets

**[Fact]** These fragmentary woodcut sheets help reconstruct early popular tarot design.

**[Finding]** No reliable pip-specific visual genealogy from their Seven of Coins to Waite-Smith was located. They should not be invoked as the source of the RWS gardener.

## Sola Busca

**Date and place:** engraving dated 1491; often assigned to Ferrara or Venice/northeastern Italy; the complete hand-coloured set is now at the Pinacoteca di Brera.

**[Fact]** It is the earliest substantially complete surviving 78-card tarot and the first surviving tarot in which all numeral cards are treated pictorially rather than merely as suit-sign arrays. Its trumps and courts employ pseudo-historical Roman, biblical, and legendary names. Brera’s 2012 exhibition interpreted the pack in a Renaissance Hermetic-alchemical environment, although particular identifications remain disputed. [Public Domain Review with Brera images](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/sola-busca), [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sola_Busca_tarot).

### The Sola Busca Seven of Coins

The card presents seven coin-like disks integrated with a deliberately constructed vegetal/object composition rather than a Marseille-style abstract floral lattice. It does **not** show Pamela Colman Smith’s young agricultural worker leaning on a hoe and inspecting a bush.

### Was it the source of the Waite-Smith Seven?

**[Disputed; lead not verified in card-specific form]**

- Photographs of Sola Busca cards were deposited or exhibited at the British Museum before the 1909 Waite-Smith commission.
- Smith demonstrably used several Sola Busca compositions elsewhere—most famously the Three of Swords and Ten of Wands.
- Therefore, general Sola Busca influence on Smith is a strong scholarly reconstruction.
- But the often-repeated statement that the **Sola Busca Seven of Coins specifically** is the source of Smith’s Seven of Pentacles was not substantiated by a close compositional match or a statement by Smith or Waite.
- The safer conclusion is: Sola Busca supplied a precedent for narrativizing pips and directly influenced several RWS cards, but Smith’s gardener scene is better explained by the Golden Dawn title “Success Unfulfilled,” Waite’s money/anxiety meanings, and agricultural imagery of waiting for a crop.

A study of Smith’s sources says she “certainly was familiar with the deck,” while admitting that Waite’s own knowledge is unknown. [Smith/Sola Busca discussion](https://www.tarothermeneutics.com/reviews/PCSAppreciation.pdf).

## Mantegna “Tarocchi”

**[Fact]** The so-called Mantegna Tarocchi are fifty didactic engravings organized into social and cosmological series, not a tarot pack and not cards by Mantegna.

**[Finding]** They contain no Seven of Coins and are irrelevant to this card except as evidence for the broader Renaissance taste for numbered emblematic series. Treating them as an ancestral Seven of Pentacles is a category error.

## Tarot de Marseille lineage

### General Marseille pip form

**[Fact]** The Seven of Deniers normally consists of seven rosette-like coins woven into a bilaterally organized floral or foliate structure. In Conver-derived examples, three pairs are vertically distributed and one coin occupies the centre. Stems, leaves, and flowers fill the intervals.

**Historical meaning:** It is ornamental organization and rank identification. There is no contemporary seventeenth-century evidence that the foliage meant “cultivation,” “money growing on trees,” or “patient investment.”

### Jean Noblet, Paris, c. 1650

**[Fact]** Noblet is the earliest substantially preserved Marseille-pattern pack. The numbered Deniers remain non-scenic: coins and vegetation, not people.

### Jean Dodal, Lyon, c. 1701–15

**[Fact]** Dodal continues the ornamental pip grammar with robust rosettes, curling stems, and stencil colouring. Variations in leaf shape or coin spacing reflect workshop cutting and printing, not an attested doctrinal revision.

### Nicolas Conver, Marseille, 1760/61

**[Fact]** Seven rosette-deniers appear in a formal vegetal lattice: paired coins with a central seventh, leaves and flowers articulating the axis. A later Camoin printing based on Conver is preserved at the BnF. [Conver reproduction record](https://dusserre.fr/cartes/tarot-de-marseille-nicolas-conver/), [BnF-based examination](https://marselia.app/fr/cards/sept-de-denier).

### Grimaud / Paul Marteau

**Date:** the influential *Ancien Tarot de Marseille* codification is associated with Paul Marteau and Grimaud, 1930s onward.

**[Fact]** It regularizes Conver-derived outlines, colours, titles, and production. The Seven stays an ornamental Seven of Deniers.

**[Modern esoteric interpretation]** Marteau and later Marseille writers read number, colour, and vegetation symbolically, but those meanings are interpretations of the inherited design, not demonstrated seventeenth-century intentions.

### Camoin–Jodorowsky, 1997

**[Fact]** Philippe Camoin and Alejandro Jodorowsky issued a restored/recomposed Marseille tarot using several old witnesses and deliberate editorial decisions.

**[Disputed]** Calling it the uniquely “original” or fully recovered Tarot de Marseille overstates what reconstruction from divergent historical packs can establish.

**[Esoteric attribution]** Jodorowsky and Marianne Costa interpret seven as a stage of receptive or spiritual action and Deniers as bodily/material incarnation. Their visual and numerological system is a modern synthesis, not an excavated Renaissance code.

## Besançon, Bolognese, Sicilian, and Minchiate patterns

### Besançon

**[Fact]** Besançon-pattern packs famously substitute Juno and Jupiter for Papess and Pope. That regional religious accommodation affects trumps only. The Seven remains a Seven of Deniers with decorative pips.

### Bologna / Tarocchino

**[Fact]** Bolognese packs were shortened for play; many low numeral cards were removed. Consequently, depending on the historical form, a Seven of Coins may be absent from the working pack.

No Golden Dawn or Waite-style scene belongs to the traditional Bolognese pattern.

### Sicilian Tarocchi

**[Fact]** In the traditional 63-card pack the suit of money (*oro*) includes 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, and 4. The Seven is thus retained and ranks as an ordinary numeral card. [Sicilian rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/sicilian.html).

### Minchiate

**[Fact]** Minchiate has 97 cards: 40 trumps, Fool, and 56 suit cards. The Seven of Coins is a non-scenic arrangement of seven coin emblems. Round-suit numeral ranking is reversed relative to the long suits: Ace is highest, Ten lowest; Seven lies below Six and above Eight. [Minchiate rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/minchiate.html).

**[Modern invention]** Interpretations of the opposing triangular coin formations as occult hexagrams, gender polarity, or investment advice are not documented by the early playing-card record.

## Etteilla’s Grand Etteilla

**Date:** the occult pack emerged in the 1780s; the principal engraved *Grand Etteilla* forms are usually dated c. 1788–89, with later recuttings.

**[Fact]** Card **71**, *Le Sept de Denier*, shows seven small wreath or rosette forms in a 3–1–3 arrangement and printed reversible meanings:

- upright: **ARGENT** — “money”
- reversed: **INQUIÉTUDE** — “anxiety/worry”

The consulted plate labels both orientation and meaning. [Digitized *Grand Etteilla*, plate/card 71](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2f/Le_grand_Etteilla%2C_ou%2C_l%27art_de_tirer_les_cartes_..._Le_tout_recueilli_..._%28IA_b29321220%29.pdf).

**Significance:** This is one of the earliest securely printed card-specific cartomantic treatments of the Seven. It converts an ordinary pip into a textual oracle by putting the meanings on the card.

## Éliphas Lévi, Paul Christian, Papus, and Wirth

### Lévi

**[Finding]** Lévi’s *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* (1854–56) gives tarot a Hebrew, magical, and initiatory architecture, but I did not find a distinctive Seven-of-Coins image or a stable upright/reversed definition attributable to Lévi himself. Claims that Lévi originated “patience and harvest” are unsupported.

### Paul Christian

**[Fact]** Christian elaborated Egyptianized initiatory trumps in *L’Homme rouge des Tuileries* (1863) and *Histoire de la magie* (1870).

**[Finding]** His enduring influence lies mainly in trump lore; no demonstrably original Seven-of-Coins doctrine was found.

### Papus, *Le Tarot des Bohémiens* (1889)

**[Esoteric attribution]** Papus systematizes tarot through Tetragrammaton, numbers, suits, astrology, and Kabbalah. Later English tables associated with his system give the Seven of Coins “a large fortune.”

**[Textual caution]** That concise phrase occurs in derivative tables, but I could not confirm a stable page in the French first edition during this pass. It should not be treated as a verified Papus quotation without edition-level collation.

### Oswald Wirth, 1889

**[Fact]** Wirth’s 1889 tarot consisted principally of redesigned major trumps.

**[Finding]** There is no original Wirth Seven of Pentacles from that edition. Modern “Wirth” 78-card packs necessarily add later minor cards.

## Golden Dawn drafts and reconstructions

### Book T

**[Fact/Esoteric attribution]** The intended design is:

> “Hand from a cloud holding rose branch of seven pentacles…”

The precise arrangement is prescribed diagrammatically. The branch has flowers or buds; the pentacles are no longer coins but magical emblems. The card belongs to Saturn in the third decan of Taurus and is titled “Success Unfulfilled.” [Book T](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Mathers/Book-T.html).

### Robert Wang, *Golden Dawn Tarot* (1977)

**[Modern reconstruction]** Wang painted a 78-card deck following Israel Regardie’s published Golden Dawn papers. His Seven visualizes the cloud-hand, rose stem, pentacles, and astrological colour symbolism. It is a reconstruction of instructions, not a facsimile of one authoritative original Golden Dawn deck.

### Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero

**[Modern reconstruction]** Their Golden Dawn deck similarly realizes Book T with systematic colour scales, decan symbols, and ritual emblems. Differences from Wang reflect interpretation of terse manuscript instructions.

## Waite-Smith, 1909

**Artist:** Pamela Colman Smith, working under A. E. Waite; initially published by Rider.

**[Fact]** A young labourer stands at left, leaning his head and arms on the handle of a hoe or staff. He studies a dense green plant bearing six pentacles; the seventh lies near his feet. Earthy hills or fields recede behind him.

Waite’s description:

> “A young man, leaning on his staff, looks intently at seven pentacles…”

He adds that they seem to be “his treasures” and that “his heart was there.” [*Pictorial Key*, Seven of Pentacles](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tarot/pkt/pktpe07.htm).

### What changed

- Coins became **pentacles**: disks visibly marked with five-pointed stars.
- An abstract pip became a human drama.
- Floral filler became a cultivated crop.
- Golden Dawn “Success Unfulfilled” became a pause before harvest.
- Etteilla/Mathers money and anxiety survive in Waite’s text.
- Smith added emotional ambiguity: fatigue, assessment, patience, disappointment, or guarded satisfaction.

**[Reconstruction]** The scene probably synthesizes Book T’s flowering branch, its title, and Waite’s received divinatory lists. A direct Sola Busca Seven-to-RWS Seven copying claim is unproven.

## BOTA / Paul Foster Case

**Date:** Case’s tarot writings began in the 1910s–20s; the BOTA deck, drawn by Jessie Burns Parke, took later institutional form.

**[Fact]** BOTA’s minor cards are generally non-scenic symbolic pips rather than copies of Smith’s narrative scenes. The Seven is seven pentacles arranged diagrammatically.

**[Esoteric attribution]** Case keeps the Golden Dawn framework:

- Seven = Netzach/Venus
- Pentacles = Earth/Assiah/final Heh
- Saturn in Taurus
- “Success Unfulfilled”

A Case text explicitly notes that the Venus symbol alludes to “the attribution of the Sphere of Venus to the Divine Number 7.” [Case, *Oracle of the Tarot*](https://media-01.imu.nl/storage/divona.be/17547/oracle-of-tarot-paul-foster-case.pdf).

## Crowley–Harris Thoth

**Painted:** 1938–43.  
**First full commercial publication:** 1969, after earlier limited reproductions.

**Title:** **Seven of Disks — Failure**

**[Fact]** Frieda Harris created a dense, dark geometric-organic field. Seven heavy disks appear like leaden coins or diseased fruit. Saturn and Taurus glyphs are built into or associated with the disks; the vegetation is blighted rather than flourishing.

Crowley explains that Seven/Netzach has an enfeebling effect here, aggravated by Saturn in Taurus. The card is not mere temporary impatience but arrested material growth: heavy, slow, and sterile.

**[Fact]** Crowley retains the Golden Dawn decan but intensifies its pessimistic pole:

- Book T: **Success Unfulfilled**
- Thoth: **Failure**

The deck booklet states: “SEVEN OF DISKS = Failure. Saturn in Taurus.” [Thoth booklet](https://zenseiderz.org/magick/tarot_thoth/txt/Aleister_Crowley_-_Thoth_Tarot_Deck_Booklet.pdf); see also [*Book of Thoth* scan](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Crowley/Book%20of%20Thoth.pdf).

### Tzaddi/Heh issue

**[Fact]** Crowley’s “Tzaddi is not the Star” exchange concerns the major trumps Emperor and Star. It does not alter the Seven of Disks. The Seven remains Saturn in 20°–30° Taurus and Netzach of Earth.

## Notable modern redesigns

**[Modern tradition]** Most post-1970 Rider-family decks preserve one of four motifs:

1. gardener awaiting harvest;
2. craftsperson appraising accumulated work;
3. investor reviewing returns;
4. ecologically reframed cultivation.

**[Modern invention]** The popular keyword “return on investment” is a late-twentieth/early-twenty-first-century business metaphor, though it develops naturally from Waite’s money/business and Smith’s inspection scene.

**[Modern tradition]** Inclusive and feminist decks often replace Smith’s male labourer with women, gender-neutral figures, community gardeners, or ancestral cultivators. Environmental decks turn the card into stewardship, regenerative agriculture, or the ethics of extraction.

# The game

## Original function

**[Fact]** The Seven began as a playing card in the suit of Coins, one of four ordinary Italian suits: Cups, Swords, Batons, and Coins. Tarot added trumps and a Fool to an existing four-suit structure. The numeral card did not itself possess allegorical rank or occult power. [Metropolitan Museum overview](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/perspectives/tarot-2).

## Rank

In many historical Italian and French games:

- “long” suits—Swords and Batons—rank downward from Ten;
- “round” suits—Cups and Coins—reverse the numerals, making Ace strongest and Ten weakest beneath the courts.

Thus in a full round suit the Seven normally beats Eight, Nine, and Ten but loses to Six through Ace and all courts.

The Abbé de Marolles rules of 1637 give Coins:

> King, Queen, Cavalier, Jack, Ace, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7

after the deck’s low-card reduction. The Seven is consequently the lowest retained Coin. [Marolles rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/marolles.html).

Modern French Tarot rationalized suit ranking so all numeral cards run normally, Ace low to Ten high. Central European Tarock packs replaced Italian Coins with Diamonds and commonly omit the Seven from red suits because shortened packs retain Ace–Four instead.

## Point value

**[Fact]** In traditional grouped tarot counting, the Seven is an “empty card”:

- nominal value: 1 point;
- reduced intrinsic value: 0;
- its apparent point arises through the group-counting mechanism.

It is never one of the high-value *oudlers*, kings, or courts. [Tarot counting systems](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/counting.html).

## Minchiate

The Seven of Coins is retained; round-suit ranking is Ace, 2, 3 … 10 from high to low. It has no special individual bonus.

## Sicilian Tarocchi

The Seven of *oro* is retained in the shortened pack and is an ordinary numeral card. The suit’s Ace was historically absent from play and later printed principally for the tax stamp. [Sicilian Tarocchi](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/sicilian.html).

## Tarock

In shortened Austrian, Czech, Slovenian, and Hungarian packs, the historical Seven of Coins has no literal survival: Italian suits were replaced by French suits, and the red suits retain Ace–Four rather than Seven–Ten. In black suits, Seven may remain as the lowest numeral, but that is not the iconographic descendant of the Seven of Coins.

## The Fool

**[Fact]** The Fool generally functions as an excuse: it may be played instead of following suit and normally does not win the trick. It is not simply “trump 0.” Some later Central European games transform the unnumbered Fool/Sküs into the highest trump.

## Trump-order disputes

These do not alter the Seven but matter to the pack containing it. Dummett grouped early orders into:

- **Type A / Southern / Bologna:** Angel highest; three virtues clustered.
- **Type B / Eastern / Ferrara:** World highest; Justice near the top.
- **Type C / Western / Milan and France:** World highest, Angel immediately below; virtues dispersed.

[Discussion quoting Dummett’s *Game of Tarot*, pp. 399–400](https://pre-gebelin.blogspot.com/2007/11/three-regional-traditions.html).

Waite’s Strength VIII/Justice XI exchange is an occult editorial choice; it does not affect pip-card game rank.

# Chronology of attestation

### c. 1420–25 — Marziano da Tortona

**[Fact]** Marziano describes a special card game devised for Filippo Maria Visconti, with sixteen gods and four suits of birds.

**[Reconstruction]** It is an important conceptual precursor to tarot because permanently superior subject cards beat suit cards.

**[Finding]** It is not a standard tarot and contains no Seven of Coins.

### 1440 — Giusto Giusti

**[Fact]** A Florentine diary records a costly pack of *naibi a trionfi* made for Sigismondo Malatesta. It does not name individual pips.

### 10 February 1442 — Ferrara ledger

**[Fact]** Jacopo Sagramoro was paid for colouring four packs with cups, swords, coins, batons, “and all the figures” of the trumps. The record proves the four suits and trumps together but does not single out the Seven. A translation is reproduced in Helen Farley’s history. [Farley, *A Cultural History of Tarot*](https://e-isomero.com/pdf/book/1611846398.pdf).

### c. 1442–47 — Cary-Yale candidate

**[Disputed dating; fact of survival]** A Seven of Coins survives in the Cary-Yale pack.

### mid-fifteenth century — Brambilla

**[Fact]** The Brambilla *Sette di Denari* survives.

### c. 1450–80 — Visconti-Sforza

**[Fact]** Morgan MS M.630.31 survives with seven coins and a floral border.

### c. 1470–1500 — *Sermo perutilis de ludo*

**[Fact]** The Dominican sermon condemns games and supplies an early trump list.

**[Finding]** It names the triumphal subjects, not individual suit pips; therefore it is not the first textual naming of the Seven of Coins.

### Late fifteenth century — tarot poetry

**[Fact]** Tarocchi appropriati and related poems assign trumps or cards to named persons and moral conceits.

**[Finding]** No securely located c. 1500 poem gives the Seven of Coins its later “patience/harvest” meaning.

### 1491 — Sola Busca

**[Fact]** A fully illustrated Seven of Coins appears within the earliest complete surviving scenic minor cycle.

### 1526 — Folengo

**[Fact]** Teofilo Folengo’s *Caos del Triperuno* contains a literary tarot episode and is important evidence for imaginative/card-reading uses.

**[Finding]** It does not establish modern meanings for this Seven.

### 1637 — Marolles

**[Fact]** Earliest known printed French tarot rules explicitly retain the Seven of Deniers as the lowest numeral in the shortened round suit.

### c. 1650–1761 — Marseille witnesses

**[Fact]** Noblet, Dodal, Madenié, Conver, and related packs transmit the ornamental Seven of Deniers.

### 1781 — Court de Gébelin and Comte de Mellet

**[Fact]** Tarot is publicly reimagined as Egyptian sacred wisdom; Mellet links the 22 special cards with Hebrew letters and proposes divination.

**[Finding]** Neither supplies the later Golden Dawn decan system for this pip.

### 1783–89 — Etteilla

**[Fact]** The Seven receives printed cartomantic meanings: **money / anxiety**.

### 1888 — Mathers

**[Fact]** S. L. MacGregor Mathers publishes:

> “Money, Finance, Treasure, Gain, Profit”

and reversed:

> “Disturbance, Worry, Anxiety, Melancholy.”

[Mathers, *The Tarot: Its Occult Signification*](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/the-tarot-1888-by-macgregor-mathers-1.pdf).

### c. 1888–1900 — Golden Dawn Book T

**[Fact]** “Success Unfulfilled,” Saturn in Taurus, 20°–30°, Netzach of Earth.

### 1909–11 — Waite-Smith and *Pictorial Key*

**[Fact]** Smith creates the gardener scene; Waite retains conflicting older money, quarrel, and anxiety meanings.

### 1938–43 / 1969 — Thoth

**[Fact]** Harris paints the Seven of Disks; Crowley titles it “Failure.”

### 1970s onward

**[Tradition]** Psychological, Jungian, therapeutic, ecological, and career-oriented readings increasingly emphasize evaluation, delayed gratification, sunk costs, burnout, and deciding whether to continue.

### Sephirah 7

**[Esoteric attribution]** Seven corresponds to **Netzach**, conventionally translated “Victory,” and associated with Venus, desire, attraction, emotion, and instinct. Because the card is in the Earth suit, Golden Dawn interpreters read Netzach’s expansive desire as constrained in material manifestation.

This produces a built-in tension:

- Netzach/Venus seeks growth, pleasure, fruition.
- Saturn restricts, delays, hardens, and tests.
- Taurus stabilizes and cultivates but can become inert.
- Earth/Assiah manifests slowly.

“Success Unfulfilled” is thus the Golden Dawn’s compressed formula for desire meeting material delay.

### Pentacle versus coin

**[Fact]** Renaissance and Marseille suit signs are coins or deniers, not necessarily five-pointed stars.

**[Esoteric development]** Nineteenth-century occultists increasingly used “pentacle” for the magical Earth implement. Golden Dawn ritual doctrine fixed Pentacles = Earth/final Heh; Smith then placed literal pentagrams on the disks.

**[Finding]** It is misleading to call a Visconti coin a “pentacle” unless one is translating it into a later occult deck taxonomy.

### Courts and sub-elements

The requested court sub-elements do not apply directly to a Seven. In Golden Dawn:

- Knight/King = fiery part;
- Queen = watery part;
- Prince = airy part;
- Princess = earthy part.

Each court rules a zodiacal span, but the Seven’s own attribution comes from the thirty-six decans, not court-card spans.

# Divinatory meanings by author

## Etteilla

**Grand Etteilla, card 71**

- Upright: **“ARGENT”** — money.
- Reversed: **“INQUIÉTUDE”** — anxiety or worry.

This is the clearest early binary. Later Etteilla-family manuals expand it into payment, financial affairs, worries over money, or uncertainty about repayment.

## Mathers, 1888

- Upright: “Money, Finance, Treasure, Gain, Profit.”
- Reversed: “Disturbance, Worry, Anxiety, Melancholy.”

**Relationship to Etteilla:** evident continuity—money upright, worry reversed.

## Golden Dawn Book T

- Upright range: incomplete success, delayed fulfilment, promises not fully realized, effort yielding less than expected; sometimes monetary gain after work.
- Ill-dignified: sloth, disappointment, loss or abandonment.

**Note:** Book T uses elemental dignities rather than the simple modern upright/reversed binary.

## Waite, 1910/11

Waite begins:

> “These are exceedingly contradictory…”

Upright:

- money;
- business;
- barter;
- alternatively altercation/quarrels;
- alternatively innocence, ingenuity, purgation.

Reversed:

> “Cause for anxiety regarding money which it may be proposed to lend.”

The now-dominant reading “pause to assess a growing crop” comes more directly from Smith’s image than from Waite’s keyword paragraph.

## Crowley, *Book of Thoth*

Crowley does not normally provide reversed meanings; dignity and context modify the card.

- Core: **Failure**
- Themes: blighted cultivation, sterile or delayed production, effort yielding no adequate result, material inertia
- Astrological explanation: Saturn is hostile to easy fruition in earthy Taurus; Seven/Netzach is unstable this low on the Tree

**Important qualification:** “Failure” is not an inherited medieval meaning. It is Crowley’s forceful restatement of a Golden Dawn decanic attribution.

## Paul Foster Case

- Conventional title: Success Unfulfilled
- Material promise not yet brought to completion
- Delay, partial gain, uncertainty about result
- Venus/Netzach desire expressed through the limitations of Earth

**Finding:** Case’s public writings are less consistently available card-by-card than Waite’s. Exact expanded BOTA lesson meanings are often in restricted correspondence-course material; they should not be reconstructed from unauthorized copies.

## Rachel Pollack

In *Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom* (first issued 1980), Pollack reads the RWS scene as a moment of assessment after work: success has begun, but the person must decide what the result means and whether continued effort is worthwhile.

- Upright: work producing results; pause, appraisal, patience, cultivation; sometimes dissatisfaction despite achievement.
- Reversed/shadow: impatience, abandoning work too soon, wasted or unrewarding effort, anxiety over results.

**[Modern psychological tradition]** Pollack helps shift the card from fortune-telling about money to reflection on one’s relationship with work and reward.

## Mary K. Greer

In *The Complete Book of Tarot Reversals* (2002), Greer’s general method allows reversals to signify the opposite, blockage, delay, excess, projection, internalization, or reconsideration.

Applied to the Seven:

- Upright range: assessment, cultivation, waiting, long-term return, reconsidering investment.
- Reversed range: poor return, impatience, exhaustion, quitting, wasted resources, or—positively—breaking an unproductive cycle.

**Textual caution:** Exact card-specific wording was not available in a stable open preview, so this is a source-attributed paraphrase, not a quotation.

# Esoteric and symbolic readings

## Alchemical

### Sola Busca

**[Disputed scholarly interpretation]** The deck’s furnaces, vessels, weapons, plants, and pseudo-classical personages have encouraged an alchemical reading. Brera’s 2012 exhibition explicitly situated it in Hermetic-alchemical culture.

**Evidence:** recurring workshop and transformation imagery across the deck.

**Limit:** no contemporary manual says, “the Seven of Coins means this alchemical operation.” Specific equations between its seven disks and seven metals/planets remain reconstructions.

### Thoth

**[Esoteric attribution]** Harris’s leaden-looking disks suggest Saturn’s metal, while diseased vegetation signifies failed coagulation or an infertile material result. This is consistent with Crowley’s explanation but should not be back-projected onto Marseille foliage.

## Kabbalistic

**First systematic spread:** Lévi made the tarot–Hebrew-alphabet connection central in the mid-nineteenth century; Papus elaborated tetradic correspondences; Golden Dawn assigned every minor to a sephirah and world.

For the Seven:

- Netzach = seven;
- Earth = Assiah;
- final Heh = Earth/daughter/material completion;
- result: desire and generative force struggling in dense manifestation.

**Conflict:** Netzach literally means Victory, while Book T calls the card Success Unfulfilled and Crowley calls it Failure. The card demonstrates that Golden Dawn meanings are not derived from sephirah-name alone; decan astrology modifies it.

## Astrological

**[Esoteric attribution; Golden Dawn]** Saturn in the third decan of Taurus.

- Saturn: time, limitation, delay, contraction.
- Taurus: fixed earth, fertility, land, value, endurance.
- Combined: a crop that develops slowly, becomes arrested, or demands sober evaluation.

The RWS gardener is an exceptionally effective visual paraphrase of this combination even though Waite does not print the astrology in his card entry.

## Hermetic

The suit is the material terminus of the four worlds: force descends through Fire/Yod, Water/Heh, Air/Vav, and Earth/final Heh. The Seven represents an astral/emotional force reaching a resistant material field.

This is a Golden Dawn Hermetic synthesis, not a doctrine encoded by fifteenth-century cardmakers.

## Christian

**Historical layer:** Coins readily evoked worldly wealth, commerce, charity, avarice, and providence within Christian Europe.

**[Modern Christian reading]** The labourer can be compared with biblical harvest and stewardship parables: sowing and reaping, talents entrusted to servants, or patience for fruit.

**Finding:** No evidence ties the original Seven of Coins specifically to a biblical parable. Such readings arise after Smith supplied an agricultural scene.

## Jungian

**[Modern invention/tradition, twentieth century]**

- The garden becomes a cultivated portion of the psyche.
- Pentacles become invested libido or psychic value.
- The pause is ego reflection: “Is this work truly mine?”
- The unharvested crop represents maturation that cannot be forced.
- Reversal can symbolize fixation, projection of worth onto productivity, burnout, or withdrawal from an infertile complex.

Jung did not devise a Seven-of-Pentacles interpretation. “Jungian tarot” is the work of later analysts, therapists, and tarot writers using archetype, projection, shadow, individuation, and synchronicity.

# People and works

## Directly attached

- **Bonifacio Bembo and related Milanese workshops:** painted early Sevens of Coins.
- **Anonymous Sola Busca engraver and colourist:** created the earliest surviving fully pictorial minor cycle.
- **Jean Noblet, Jean Dodal, Nicolas Conver:** transmitted Marseille pip ornament.
- **Etteilla / Jean-Baptiste Alliette:** gave the Seven an early printed divinatory polarity.
- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers:** published Etteilla-influenced meanings and helped formulate Golden Dawn tarot.
- **Pamela Colman Smith:** invented the canonical gardener image.
- **A. E. Waite:** supplied the commission and published its verbal description and meanings.
- **Paul Foster Case and Jessie Burns Parke:** restored a diagrammatic occult pip within BOTA.
- **Aleister Crowley and Frieda Harris:** transformed “Success Unfulfilled” into the dark *Failure*.
- **Rachel Pollack and Mary K. Greer:** spread reflective, psychological, and process-oriented readings.
- **Alejandro Jodorowsky and Marianne Costa:** developed a modern Marseille numerology of Deniers and seven.

## Literature, film, and visual culture

### Marge Piercy

**[Fact]** Piercy wrote a poem titled “The Seven of Pentacles,” using cultivation and patient growth as an extended metaphor. This is a genuine card-specific literary afterlife of the RWS agricultural image.

### Italo Calvino

**[Fact]** *The Castle of Crossed Destinies* constructs stories from laid-out tarot cards, especially Visconti and Marseille imagery.

**Finding:** The work is important to tarot’s literary reception, but no uniquely controlling role for the Seven of Pentacles was verified in the consulted sources.

### T. S. Eliot

**[Fact]** *The Waste Land* names “Madame Sosostris,” “the drowned Phoenician Sailor,” “Belladonna,” “the man with three staves,” and a “one-eyed merchant.”

**Finding:** Eliot does not name the Seven of Pentacles. Identifying the one-eyed merchant with this card is speculative; the usual discussion concerns the Six of Pentacles or an invented/altered card.

### W. B. Yeats

**[Fact]** Yeats participated in Golden Dawn ritual culture and knew its symbolic systems.

**Finding:** No card-specific poem or doctrine for the Seven of Pentacles was located.

### Bergman

**[Fact]** *The Seventh Seal* uses medieval death, game, and apocalyptic imagery.

**Finding:** It does not quote the Seven of Pentacles; resemblance to tarot is thematic rather than card-specific.

### Jodorowsky’s films

Jodorowsky repeatedly uses tarot, alchemical, Christian, and initiatory symbolism in his films and later wrote extensively on the Marseille deck.

**Finding:** No film shot securely identifiable as a direct quotation of the Seven of Deniers was traced.

### Paintings and buildings

**Finding:** No pre-1909 painting or building has been securely documented as “quoting the Seven of Pentacles.” Agricultural figures leaning on tools are widespread genre motifs, but resemblance alone is not proof of source.

# Controversies and disputes

## Egyptian origin

**[Legend, first influential publication 1781]** Court de Gébelin asserted that tarot preserved an Egyptian sacred book. The Comte de Mellet linked it to Thoth, Hebrew letters, and divination. [Morgan account and original 1781 volume](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/tarot/11).

**Evidence offered:** speculative visual analogies and invented etymologies.

**Contrary evidence:** northern Italian objects and account books beginning in the fifteenth century; Italian suit structure; absence of Egyptian textual or archaeological intermediaries.

Dummett emphasizes that evidence for occult tarot begins with Gébelin rather than in the Renaissance. [Dummett, “Origins of Tarot”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1981/05/14/origins-of-tarot/).

## Romani or “Gypsy” origin

**[Legend]** Nineteenth-century writers linked tarot to Roma, partly because Europeans falsely believed Roma came from Egypt. Jean-Alexandre Vaillant and Papus spread the claim; Papus’s title *Tarot of the Bohemians* made it especially durable.

**Evidence:** later Romani participation in commercial fortune-telling.

**Problem:** that establishes use, not invention. Tarot is documented in Italian courts before the principal literary construction of a Romani-tarot origin. Modern research places Romani ancestry in South Asia rather than Egypt. [Academic discussion of stereotyping](https://doi.org/10.1163/15685276-01234012), [Library of Congress Papus edition](https://www.loc.gov/item/77355368/).

## Cathar origin

**[Modern legend/disputed claim]** Some twentieth-century esoteric writers interpreted the trumps as concealed Cathar doctrine or a survival carried from southern France.

**Evidence claimed:** dualistic readings of Devil, Tower, Sun, World, and Grail-like Cups.

**Problems:** earliest cards and documents are northern Italian; no Cathar manuscript mentions tarot; Cathar institutions had been destroyed before the cards appear. Nothing in the Seven of Coins supplies Cathar evidence.

## Templar origin

**[Modern legend]** Claims connect tarot to suppressed Templar knowledge, Baphomet, the Temple, or secret transmission after 1312.

**Problem:** no documented chain links Templars to fifteenth-century Italian cardmakers. The Seven of Coins is sometimes reinterpreted as concealed temple finance, but this is free association.

## Kabbalistic origin

**[Disputed as historical origin; valid as later reception]** The 22 trumps can be aligned with 22 Hebrew letters, but surviving fifteenth-century sources never make that alignment. Different occultists assigned the letters differently. Mellet, Lévi, Golden Dawn, and Crowley are stages in the construction, not witnesses to a lost Renaissance consensus.

For this pip, Netzach/final-Heh correspondences first become secure in the Golden Dawn milieu.

## Sola Busca as the direct source of the RWS Seven

**[Overstatement]** General influence is persuasive; direct dependence of this particular card is unproved.

The Sola Busca precedent may have licensed scenic minors, but the RWS Seven’s exact gardener pose and crop-as-pentacles arrangement have no demonstrated one-to-one Sola Busca Seven prototype.

## “Pentacles” are ancient

**[Modernizing misdescription]** Early cards show coins. The magical-pentacle understanding belongs to occult ritual vocabulary, especially the Golden Dawn and Waite. Translating every medieval *denaro* as “pentacle” erases that development.

## Misprints and recuttings

- **[Fact]** Early cards frequently use additive Roman numerals such as VIIII; these are conventions, not necessarily errors.
- **[Fact]** Woodblocks were recopied, reversed, recoloured, and worn. Small floral changes can become stable workshop traditions.
- **[Fact]** Etteilla packs were repeatedly recut, renumbered, and relabelled, creating variants.
- **[Fact]** “Conver 1760” and “Conver 1761” both appear because manufacture, surviving impressions, and publication/cataloguing dates do not always coincide.
- **[Modern editorial tradition]** Grimaud, Camoin–Jodorowsky, Wang, and Cicero are normalized or reconstructed systems, not untouched originals.

## Historians’ correction

The central findings associated with Michael Dummett, Ronald Decker, Thierry Depaulis, Stuart Kaplan, Robert M. Place, and Helen Farley are broadly:

1. Tarot originated as a north-Italian trick-taking game.
2. The first evidence is fifteenth-century documentary and material evidence.
3. No ancient Egyptian, Templar, Cathar, or pre-medieval tarot transmission is documented.
4. Occult tarot is historically real and culturally important—but begins as a later reinterpretation.
5. Divination and occultism must be studied from their own primary texts rather than projected backward.

This does not make occult meanings illegitimate as spiritual practices; it dates their construction.

# Dedicated contested-material pass: findings

Explicit searches pairing “Seven of Pentacles” with *origin, myth, dispute, misattribution, Egyptian, Gypsy/Romani,* and *Cathar* yielded:

- repeated generic Egyptian and Romani origin claims, none with card-specific evidence;
- repeated assertions that Sola Busca’s Seven directly became Smith’s Seven, but no Smith letter, Waite instruction, or close scholarly card comparison proving it;
- no traceable Cathar, Templar, Egyptian, or Romani Seven-of-Coins tradition predating modern occultism;
- no evidence that Saturn in Taurus was attached to this card before the Golden Dawn;
- no evidence that medieval floral ornament meant the Tree of Life, seven planets, seven metals, or agricultural investment;
- no evidence that Smith described her own figure as a gardener, farmer, or disappointed labourer;
- no verified pre-Waite occurrence of the now-standard “patience and long-term investment” formula.

The contested pass therefore supports a layered history:

**coin pip → ornamental floral pip → cartomantic money/worry → Golden Dawn incomplete success → Smith’s cultivated pause → Crowley’s failure → modern patience/investment psychology.**

# Open questions

1. Which Milanese Seven—Cary-Yale or Brambilla—is chronologically earliest? Object dating remains broad and contested.
2. What was the exact original structure of Cary-Yale: 86 cards with paired male/female courts, or another configuration?
3. What precise workshop, patron, and interpretive programme produced Sola Busca?
4. Which Sola Busca photographs were on display in London, exactly when did Smith see them, and which individual cards did she consult?
5. Did Waite give Smith written card-by-card instructions now lost, or did she independently translate Book T meanings into scenes?
6. Was the RWS seventh pentacle on the ground meant as harvested fruit, discarded value, seed capital, or merely compositional balance? Neither artist nor author explains it.
7. Which early Golden Dawn manuscript is the archetype of the surviving Book T recensions, and when was “Success Unfulfilled” first written?
8. Can the Papus “large fortune” attribution be located verbatim in a specific 1889 French impression rather than later translated tables?
9. How much of current Marseille numerology derives from Marteau, and how much from Jodorowsky–Costa’s late-twentieth-century synthesis?
10. Can a secure pre-1909 visual source be found for Smith’s exact leaning-worker posture?

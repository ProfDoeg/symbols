# Page of Pentacles: Research Dossier

> **Method.** Every substantial proposition is marked by evidentiary status:
>
> - **[Documented fact]** directly supported by a surviving card, archival document, rulebook, or primary text.
> - **[Scholarly reconstruction]** an inference accepted or seriously argued by historians.
> - **[Tradition]** a durable customary interpretation whose historical origin may be recoverable only in part.
> - **[Esoteric attribution]** a correspondence deliberately assigned by an occult author or order.
> - **[Disputed claim]** a proposal for which specialists or surviving evidence disagree.
> - **[Legend]** a historical-origin story unsupported by the documentary record.
> - **[Modern invention]** a comparatively recent reinterpretation or redesign not claimed by the early cards themselves.
>
> “Page of Pentacles” is an English occult-tarot name. The underlying playing card is much older: the lowest court card of the coin suit.

## Basic identification

### Names across traditions

| Language or system | Usual title | Literal sense or qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Fifteenth-century Italian | **Fante di Denari**; sometimes **Famiglio/Famiglia di Denari** in historical descriptions | Foot attendant, infantryman, servant or page of Coins |
| Modern Italian | **Fante di Denari** | Jack/Page of Coins |
| French Tarot de Marseille | **Valet de Deniers** | Valet or page of Coins |
| Besançon/Swiss French | **Valet de Deniers** | The regional substitutions Juno/Jupiter affect trumps II and V, not this card |
| Etteilla | **Valet de Deniers** or equivalent numbered Coin court | A divinatory remapping of the traditional valet |
| Spanish | **Sota de Oros** | Lowest court of Gold Coins |
| German | **Bube der Münzen**, **Bube der Scheiben** | Jack of Coins/Disks |
| English, pre-occult and game terminology | **Knave of Coins**, **Jack of Coins** | Rank equivalent of the Italian *fante* and French *valet* |
| English occult tarot | **Page of Coins**, **Page of Pentacles** | “Pentacles” became dominant in the Golden Dawn–Waite lineage |
| Golden Dawn | **Princess of the Echoing Hills; Rose of the Palace of Earth** | Earth of Earth; final *Heh* |
| Crowley–Harris Thoth | **Princess of Disks** | Crowley replaces Pentacles with Disks |
| BOTA/Paul Foster Case | Usually **Page of Pentacles** in public-facing English usage | Largely Waite-derived graphic vocabulary, with Golden Dawn–Case doctrine behind it |

### Number

**[Documented fact]** The Page of Pentacles is **not intrinsically numbered**. It is a court rank, not a trump or numeral card.

Numbers sometimes found in catalogues, occult tables, apps, or Etteilla-derived decks are sequential deck positions, not an ancient identity. In Mathers’s 1888 tabulation it is card **67**, reflecting his order of the complete pack; this is not a traditional Italian number. [Mathers, *The Tarot*, 1888](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/mathers/mtar00.htm)

Consequently:

- Justice/Strength exchanges do not affect it.
- Regional trump orders do not affect it.
- The unnumbered Fool does not affect it.
- Juno/Jupiter substitutions in Besançon concern the Papess and Pope only.
- *Maison Dieu* is a name of the Tower, not a Page-of-Coins variant.

Those matters belong to the pack’s history and are treated below only as context.

### Suit

**[Documented fact]** The historical suit is:

- Italian: **denari**
- French: **deniers**
- Spanish: **oros**
- English historical translation: **coins**
- English occult usage: **pentacles**
- Crowley: **disks**

The coin suit is already present in Mamluk cards as *darāhim*, “coins/dirhams.” The nearly complete Mamluk pack preserved at the Topkapı Palace has four suits—coins, cups, swords and polo-sticks—and three court ranks named rather than humanly pictured: *malik*, *nāʾib malik*, and *thānī nāʾib*. [International Playing-Card Society introduction](https://www.i-p-c-s.org/wp/an-introduction/); Dummett and Abu-Deeb, “Some Remarks on Mamluk Playing Cards,” *Journal of the Warburg and Courtauld Institutes* 36 (1973), 106–128, [DOI record](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/751160)

### Earliest surviving example

**[Documented fact, with dating qualification]** The earliest surviving close counterpart is the lowest Coin court in the **Cary-Yale/Visconti di Modrone pack**, usually dated around **1440–1445**, now in Yale’s Beinecke Library. This exceptional luxury pack employs expanded courts, including female attendants and female knights; its Coin servant is therefore not simply identical in gender and court structure to the later standard male *fante*.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The standard Page/Fante of Coins becomes clearer in the mid-fifteenth-century Milanese tradition represented by the Visconti-Sforza group. No complete fifteenth-century pack survives, and exact relative dating within the Visconti family is debated.

The Morgan describes the principal Visconti-Sforza group as probably made after Francesco Sforza’s entry into Milan as duke on 25 March 1450 and attributes it probably to Bonifacio Bembo or his workshop. It records a reconstructed full pack of 78 cards: twenty-one trumps, Fool, and four fourteen-card suits. [Morgan manuscript record](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525); [Morgan collection introduction](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/tarot-cards)

### Golden Dawn attribution

**[Esoteric attribution, late nineteenth century]**

- Title: **Princess of the Echoing Hills; Rose of the Palace of Earth**
- Rank element: **Earth**
- Suit element: **Earth**
- Combination: **Earth of Earth**
- Tetragrammaton: **final Heh**
- Kabbalistic world: commonly assigned to **Assiah**, the material world
- Court station: Princess, the crystallization and manifestation of the suit
- Geography: one of the four terrestrial/circumpolar quadrants
- Zodiacal decans: **none assigned to the Princess individually**

The last point is frequently misstated. Golden Dawn Knights, Queens and Princes govern thirty-degree arcs assembled from three decans. The Princesses do not receive comparable thirty-degree zodiacal spans. Collectively they govern four terrestrial quadrants surrounding the North Pole and serve as the “thrones” of the Aces. The Golden Dawn manuscript tradition explicitly discusses the Princess realm beneath the zodiac and the Aces passing to their “Thrones.” [*Book T* manuscript edition](https://jantetteroo.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Mathers_and_felkin_golden_dawn_book_t_th.pdf); [Golden Dawn manuscript transcription](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/golden_dawn_manuscripts.html)

## Iconography deck by deck

## Mamluk antecedents, fourteenth–fifteenth centuries

**[Documented fact]** The Topkapı Mamluk cards do not show a young human page. Court rank is conveyed through inscriptions and ornament. The closest structural ancestor is one of the deputy ranks in the Coin suit.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** European makers retained the four-suit, ten-pip, three-court framework while replacing written or abstract Mamluk courts with human kings, mounted riders and standing attendants. When queens were added in Italian tarot, the lowest attendant remained beneath Knight, Queen and King.

**What changed:** an inscribed office became a pictured court personage.

**What that meant originally:** probably rank recognition for play, not an initiatory emblem. There is no surviving Mamluk evidence for “Earth of Earth,” a pentagram, divination, or a youthful student.

---

## Cary-Yale / Visconti di Modrone, Milan, c. 1440–1445

**[Documented fact]** This unusually lavish deck contains expanded male and female court ranks. Its Coin attendant is richly dressed and carries or displays the suit emblem. Gold leaf, punchwork, court dress and Visconti heraldic language locate it in aristocratic Milan.

**What changed:** the imported deputy hierarchy was transformed into a Renaissance household or dynastic court. Gender symmetry was tried here but did not become the standard 78-card structure.

**Meaning at the time:** courtly splendor, rank and suit recognition. An occult reading is not attested.

**Terminological warning:** “Cary-Yale” and “Visconti di Modrone” refer to the same surviving group. It should not be counted twice as two decks.

---

## Brambilla-Brera deck, Milan, c. 1440s

**[Documented fact]** The Brambilla cards are another Visconti commission, usually connected with Filippo Maria Visconti and now principally in the Pinacoteca di Brera. Coins bear ducal monetary and heraldic imagery.

**Card-specific limitation:** the surviving group does not supply a securely complete Page-of-Coins sequence from which a separate iconographic history can be written. Assertions that every named early Milanese pack preserves this exact card are therefore unsafe.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Its Coin suit demonstrates that *denari* originally meant actual monetary or dynastic coin images, not occult pentagrams.

---

## Visconti-Sforza / Colleoni-Baglioni, Milan, c. 1450s

**[Documented fact]** The reconstructed pack is divided among the Morgan Library, Accademia Carrara and Colleoni holdings. Its surviving courts are elegant, elongated aristocratic figures painted against gold grounds. The Page/Fante of Coins belongs to this courtly visual vocabulary: a standing youthful attendant marked by the Coin emblem, without Waite’s agricultural landscape or Golden Dawn pentagram.

The Morgan emphasizes the combined Visconti and Sforza devices and dates the group probably after Francesco Sforza became duke in 1450. [Morgan record](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525)

### Was the figure a specific family member?

**[Disputed claim]** Gertrude Moakley and Stuart Kaplan helped popularize attempts to associate Visconti-Sforza figures with members of the ruling families. Such identification is plausible as dynastic allusion but rarely demonstrable as portraiture.

Evidence for a named Page of Coins sitter is weak:

- no contemporary inscription names the person;
- workshop court figures are conventionalized;
- heraldry can identify the patronal milieu without identifying the sitter;
- later reproductions sometimes silently complete missing cards.

**Finding:** no securely documented identification of the Page/Fante of Coins as a particular Visconti or Sforza family member was located. A confident personal name should be labeled speculative, not factual.

---

## Ferrarese and Este material, fifteenth century

**[Documented fact]** Ferrara’s court accounts furnish crucial early references to *carte da trionfi*, notably the 1442 ledger. Surviving Este-associated cards and fragments show a distinct Ferrarese luxury-card culture.

**Card-specific limitation:** the ledger names packs, painters and payments, not “the Page of Coins” as an independent iconographic subject. Surviving Este fragments do not yield a continuous developmental sequence for this particular card.

### Mantegna “Tarocchi”

**[Documented fact]** The so-called Mantegna Tarocchi are fifteenth-century educational or emblematic engravings arranged in five alphabetical decades. They are neither by Mantegna nor a normal tarot pack.

**[Disputed relevance]** Their ranks, liberal arts, virtues, cosmic spheres and estates supply valuable comparison for Renaissance emblem culture but no direct Page-of-Pentacles ancestor.

---

## Sola Busca, northern Italy, 1491

**[Documented fact]** The Sola Busca is the earliest surviving complete tarot pack and is famous for fully illustrated suit cards. Its courts are named, pseudo-historical or martial figures rather than Marseille-style anonymous valets. The suit corresponding to Coins uses round suit emblems, often integrated into elaborate scenes.

**Importance:** it proves that scenic treatment of the minor suits existed centuries before Pamela Colman Smith.

**Caution:** the Page of Pentacles in Waite-Smith is not demonstrably a simple copy of one Sola Busca court card. Smith’s clearest Sola Busca debts are generally sought among several numbered minors. “Smith copied the entire minor arcana from Sola Busca” is an overstatement.

---

## Rosenwald and Budapest sheets, late fifteenth/early sixteenth century

**[Documented fact]** These printed sheets are important witnesses to the transfer from hand-painted court packs to reproducible popular cards.

**Card-specific finding:** their surviving impressions help establish standing *fante* conventions and suit structures, but incomplete sheets, worn impressions and differing scholarly reconstructions make a precise Page-of-Coins genealogy hazardous.

**What changed:** simplified outlines, reduced color, repeated workshop patterns and mass production displaced unique gold-ground portraiture.

---

## Tarot de Marseille lineage

### Jean Noblet, Paris, c. 1650

**[Documented fact]** Noblet’s pack is the earliest substantially surviving classic French tarot usually grouped with the Marseille family, although made in Paris. The Coin valet is a standing youthful male court figure with a large round denier.

**What changed:** the figure became a reproducible woodcut court type, outlined in black and colored through stencils. Dynastic gold-ground luxury became urban commercial print.

### Jean Dodal, Lyon, c. 1701–1715

**[Documented fact]** Dodal stabilizes the familiar **VALET DE DENIERS** type: a standing young man, turned toward a raised coin, in strongly blocked red, blue/yellow and flesh colors.

### Nicolas Conver tradition, Marseille, archetype 1760; surviving BnF witness later

**[Documented fact with bibliographic caution]** “Conver 1760” names a pattern and publication tradition. The BnF witness frequently reproduced online is catalogued later, during Nicolas Conver’s 1784–1833 activity.

The card depicts:

- a standing man, body roughly frontal;
- head turned toward a denier held at eye level;
- the other hand at his belt;
- elaborate cap and multicolored tunic;
- a **second denier on the ground**.

The BnF-based description is transcribed and summarized here: [Valet de Deniers, Conver witness](https://marselia.app/cards/valet-de-denier)

### Meaning of the second coin

**[Documented fact]** It is visibly present in the Conver lineage.

**[Open interpretation]** No contemporary Conver text explains it. Later readers call it capital still embedded in the earth, an unclaimed opportunity, raw material, the relation between idea and manifestation, or the apprentice’s second task.

These are **modern symbolic readings**, not documented eighteenth-century explanations.

### Grimaud / Paul Marteau, twentieth century

**[Documented fact]** Grimaud’s Marseille line regularized outlines and colors for modern printing. Paul Marteau’s *Le Tarot de Marseille* supplied an influential twentieth-century French esoteric commentary.

**What changed:** printing was standardized, and meanings once transmitted orally or through cartomancy manuals were presented as a coherent symbolic system.

### Camoin–Jodorowsky, late twentieth century

**[Documented fact]** Philippe Camoin and Alejandro Jodorowsky produced a “restored” Marseille tarot by comparing historical examples and reconstructing lines and colors.

**[Disputed claim]** Calling it the recovery of one original, pristine Tarot de Marseille overstates what a family of variable printed packs can demonstrate. It is also an editorial synthesis.

**Jodorowsky–Costa reading:** the Valet’s gaze, two coins and uncertain stance become signs of latent talent, apprenticeship, embodiment and choosing how material energy will be used. This is a modern psychospiritual hermeneutic.

## Minchiate

**[Documented fact]** Florentine Minchiate expanded the trump sequence to produce a 97-card pack while retaining four ordinary suits and courts.

The lowest Coin court remains the servant/page rank. It is not one of Minchiate’s added virtues, elements or zodiacal trumps.

**In play:** it functions as a suit court and counting card, not as “Earth of Earth.” Its survival in Minchiate confirms that court rank and occult title are separable historical layers.

---

## Bolognese tarocchino

**[Documented fact]** Bolognese tarot shortened the ordinary suits by removing low pip cards but retained courts. The Page/Fante of Coins therefore survives as a functional suit honor.

The special Bolognese trump order—including equal-ranking Moors—does not alter its suit rank. [Tarocchini overview](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/); [Bolognese overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarocchini)

---

## Sicilian tarot

**[Documented fact]** The surviving Sicilian pattern calls the lowest court **Donna**, “Maid,” rather than *fante*. The Coin suit is called **oro**. Its descending court order is King, Queen, Horse, Maid. In traditional counting, the Maid has a nominal value of two before group deductions. [Rules of Sicilian Tarocchi](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/sicilian.html)

This is a genuine regional feminization of the lowest court and an important pre-Golden-Dawn parallel. It does **not**, however, prove that the Golden Dawn derived “Princess” specifically from Sicily.

---

## Besançon and Swiss patterns

**[Documented fact]** Besançon packs replace the Papess and Pope with Juno and Jupiter, largely in Protestant or confessionally sensitive markets. The change concerns trumps II and V. The **Valet de Deniers remains a valet**.

This is included because it demonstrates how regional religious censorship could alter trumps while leaving suit courts comparatively stable.

---

## Etteilla’s Grand Etteilla, Paris, c. 1789

**[Documented fact]** Jean-Baptiste Alliette, “Etteilla,” was the first person known to design and market a tarot pack specifically for divination. His Grand Etteilla rearranged, renumbered and relabeled the pack, added astrological or elemental information, and often printed upright and reversed keywords on the cards. [Etteilla overview](https://hermetism.org/encyclopedia/jean-baptiste-alliette-etteilla/)

The Coin court remains a human signifier rather than Golden Dawn “Earth of Earth.” In Etteilla-derived practice the valet can indicate a brown-haired young person and, contextually, news, service, expenditure or material conduct.

**[Textual qualification]** Grand Etteilla exists in several historical states and later “Etteilla II/III” recensions. Keywords and even reversibility can differ. A single modern reproduction should not be treated as Etteilla’s immutable 1789 text.

---

## Oswald Wirth, 1889

**[Documented fact]** Wirth’s first tarot comprised **only the twenty-two trumps**. It contains no Page of Pentacles.

Wirth is relevant to the later occultization of tarot imagery, but any “Wirth Page of Pentacles” belongs to later publishers, extensions, or Wirth-inspired decks—not the original 1889 *22 Arcanes du Tarot kabbalistique*.

---

## Golden Dawn drafts, c. 1888–1900

**[Esoteric attribution]** In *Book T*, the ordinary Page is transformed into a **Princess**:

> “Princess of the Echoing Hills; Rose of the Palace of Earth.”

She is the “earthy part of Earth,” final manifestation of the element.

Typical Golden Dawn design instructions show a powerful young woman bearing a disk or pentacle and a terrestrial implement, in the landscape or color scale of Earth. Exact images differ because *Book T* is a set of symbolic directions, not one canonical commercial painting.

### Wang and Cicero reconstructions

**[Modern reconstruction]**

- Robert Wang’s *Golden Dawn Tarot* translates the documentary descriptions into 1970s commercial illustrations.
- Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero’s version reconstructs the order’s color scales, implements, elemental symbolism and court compositions more systematically.

Neither should be mistaken for an original, universally used nineteenth-century Golden Dawn deck. Members made or colored individual cards and manuscripts.

---

## Waite–Smith, London, 1909

**[Documented fact]** Pamela Colman Smith draws a solitary, androgynously youthful figure in a green tunic and red cap standing in a fertile landscape. The figure raises a gold disk bearing a five-pointed star and looks at it with total concentration. In the distance are trees, a tilled field and blue mountains.

Waite’s description is:

> “A youthful figure, looking intently at the pentacle which hovers over his raised hands. He moves slowly, insensible of that which is about him.”

[*The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*, Pentacles: Page](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/pkt/pktpepa.htm); [1910/1911 PDF edition](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/02tarocchi/Waite_Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot.pdf)

### Sources and innovations

**[Documented comparison]**

- The raised round emblem and attentive gaze continue the Marseille Valet.
- Smith removes Conver’s second ground coin.
- The commercial coin becomes an occult **pentacle**.
- Landscape and tilled earth externalize the Golden Dawn association with Earth.
- The static valet becomes psychologically absorbed in study.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Waite supplied symbolic requirements, while Smith was responsible for the actual composition. No surviving instruction sheet proves every individual compositional decision.

### Was the pentacle introduced here?

**[Qualification]** Five-pointed stars and pentacles existed for centuries in magic, heraldry and earlier occult writing. What the Golden Dawn–Waite line introduced was the systematic replacement of the historical **Coins/Deniers suit emblem** with an overtly magical pentacle throughout this tarot suit.

## Paul Foster Case and the BOTA deck

**[Documented fact]** Paul Foster Case and artist Jessie Burns Parke created the BOTA deck as black-line images intended to be colored by students. It follows Waite closely in its courts while embedding Case’s Qabalistic teaching. [BOTA deck overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.O.T.A._tarot_deck); [BOTA institutional history](https://bota.org/bota/history)

The Page of Pentacles retains the youthful, earth-associated learner and pentacle emblem. Unlike Waite-Smith’s fully scenic numbered minors, BOTA’s pip cards generally return to repeated suit emblems.

**[Esoteric attribution]** Case retains the broad Golden Dawn framework of elements, Tetragrammaton and manifestation, while presenting it through his own curriculum.

---

## Crowley–Harris Thoth, painted 1938–1943; first complete publication 1969

**[Documented fact]** Crowley calls the suit **Disks**, and Frieda Harris paints the court as the **Princess of Disks**.

Crowley writes:

> “The Princess of Disks, the last of the Court cards, represents the earthy part of Earth. She is consequently on the brink of transfiguration.”

He describes:

- a strong, beautiful, brooding woman;
- a ram’s-head crest;
- a sceptre descending into the earth and terminating in a diamond;
- sacred trees;
- a wheatsheaf-like altar;
- a visibly fertile or pregnant body;
- a disk containing a Chinese sign of balanced spiral creation and the rose of Isis.

He concludes:

> “She bears within her body the secret of the future.”

And summarizes her human manifestation as:

> “Womanhood in its ultimate projection.”

[Crowley, *The Book of Thoth*, Court Cards](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/court-cards); [scan of *The Book of Thoth*](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/crowley/equinox/volume3/vol_3_no_05.pdf)

**What changed:**

- boy/page becomes adult fertile woman;
- coin becomes rotating cosmological disk;
- quiet study becomes gestation and transfiguration;
- Earth is treated as living, green and generative, not inert or merely commercial.

**[Esoteric attribution]** The descending diamond joins Kether, the highest sephirah, to the deepest material Earth: highest light hidden in the densest element.

## Notable modern redesigns

### Rachel Pollack’s Shining Tribe

**[Modern invention]** Pollack replaces Pentacles with **Stones**, emphasizing prehistoric and cross-cultural imagery rather than European court costume. The Morgan notes that Pollack regarded tarot as able to absorb many belief systems and deliberately renamed the suits Trees, Rivers, Birds and Stones. [Morgan: Rachel Pollack](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/tarot/23)

### Feminist and inclusive decks

**[Modern invention/tradition]** Many post-1970 decks render the Page as:

- a girl or nonbinary youth;
- a student, craft apprentice, gardener or activist;
- a child touching soil;
- a bearer of a seed, crystal, coin or ecological world.

These are modern developments from the Page-as-beginner and Earth-as-material-life complex. They are not recoverable as fixed fifteenth-century meanings.

## The game

### Rank

**[Documented fact]** In a standard 78-card Italian-suited tarot pack, the court hierarchy is:

1. King
2. Queen
3. Knight/Cavalier
4. Page/Fante/Valet

The Page is therefore the **lowest court**, but outranks every pip in its suit.

In many traditional games, Coins and Cups are “round” suits with inverse pip ranking:

> King, Queen, Cavalier, Page, Ace, 2, 3 … 10.

Swords and Batons commonly rank:

> King, Queen, Cavalier, Page, 10, 9 … Ace.

A seventeenth-century French rule tradition calls the Jack *faon*, derived from Italian *fante*. [Rules based on the Abbé de Marolles](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/marolles.html)

Modern French tarot instead uses French suits. The analogous rank is the **valet of diamonds**, normally worth two card points when paired with a half-point card. [Modern French tarot rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/frtarot.html); [tarot point-counting systems](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/counting.html)

### Value

**[Documented fact, variable by game]**

- Traditional valuation generally makes the lowest court worth **2 nominal points** before packet deductions.
- In modern French tarot, the valet counts **1½ raw points**, normally counted with a low card to make 2.
- In Sicilian tarot, the **Donna** has nominal value 2 before the deduction used in three-card packets.
- In some Austrian Tarock systems, French- or German-suited equivalents remain court cards with local point schedules.
- In reduced packs the card may be absent if the particular regional pack replaces Italian suit courts or removes ranks.

There is no universal tarot-game value independent of ruleset.

### Trump orders and the Page

**[Documented fact]** Historians reconstruct three broad Italian trump-order families—usually called A, B and C—from different regional game traditions. Disagreements include:

- Justice relative to the cardinal virtues;
- Strength/Fortitude relative to Justice;
- the location of the World and Angel;
- whether some Bolognese trumps are equal;
- the unnumbered Fool’s special role.

None changes the Page of Coins, because it is a suit card.

### The Fool

**[Documented fact]** The Fool usually does not behave like an ordinary highest or lowest trump. In many games it excuses its player from following suit and is reclaimed in scoring, subject to local compensation rules. In some Central European developments it becomes the highest trump (*Sküs*).

Again, that affects trick play globally, not the Page’s intrinsic rank within Coins.

### “The throne of the Aces” and the four quadrants

**[Esoteric attribution]** In Golden Dawn cosmology, the four Princesses occupy the terrestrial domain below the zodiacal belts of the other courts. Each receives the force of the corresponding Ace and is called its throne. Thus the Princess of Pentacles is the material seat through which the root power of Earth becomes perceptible.

This is not an older Marseille doctrine. It is late nineteenth-century ceremonial-magic system building.

### Final Heh

Papus and the Golden Dawn relate four court levels to **Yod–Heh–Vav–Heh**:

- Knight/King: Yod
- Queen: first Heh
- Prince/Knight: Vav
- Princess/Page: final Heh

**[Esoteric attribution]** Final Heh means completion, receptivity and manifestation. As the last term, the Princess also becomes the threshold through which a completed elemental cycle seeds another.

### Tzaddi/Heh controversy

Crowley’s “Tzaddi is not the Star” doctrine moves **Heh** to the Star and **Tzaddi** to the Emperor in his trump correspondences.

**Finding:** this does **not** change the Page/Princess’s status as final Heh of Tetragrammaton. The same English word “Heh” appears in two different operations:

- a Hebrew letter assigned to a trump;
- the final letter-position of YHVH assigned to the Princess rank.

Conflating them creates a false Page-of-Pentacles controversy.

## Divinatory meanings by author

## Etteilla

**[Documented tradition with recension problem]** Etteilla’s Coin valet operates as a personal signifier—often a young, brown-haired person—and acquires meanings through neighboring cards and orientation. Later Etteilla-family manuals associate its favorable side with good conduct or pleasing news and its unfavorable side with waste, superficiality, luxury or prodigality.

**Textual caution:** a single authoritative English upright/reversed sentence could not be securely established across the 1780s *cahiers*, the 1789 deck and nineteenth-century “Julia Orsini” recensions. [Etteilla Third Cahier translation project](https://thirdcahier.blogspot.com/2012/10/blank_4887.html); [English Coin-suit concordance](https://stolen-thyme.com/etteillas-suit-of-coins/)

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers, 1888

Mathers’s card 67 is the Page of Pentacles. His interpretive family is:

- upright: a dark or brown-haired youth; application, study, news or a messenger;
- reversed: prodigality, dissipation and meddling advice.

His contextual instruction states:

> “Consult the following cards to see in what the person is prodigal.”

He adds that in a specified reversed combination it may mean that the person is:

> “too fond of giving advice, intermeddles too much with other people’s business.”

[Mathers, *The Tarot*, first edition 1888, 60 pp.; searchable scan](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/the-tarot-1888-by-macgregor-mathers-1.pdf); [HTML edition](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/mathers/mtar00.htm)

## A. E. Waite, 1910

Waite gives:

> “Application, study, scholarship, reflection; another reading says news, messages and the bringer thereof; also rule, management.”

Reversed:

> “Prodigality, dissipation, liberality, luxury; unfavourable news.”

[*Pictorial Key*, Page of Pentacles](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/pkt/pktpepa.htm)

**[Documented dependence]** These meanings substantially preserve the Mathers/Etteilla cartomantic stream. Smith’s concentrated figure visually foregrounds “application” and “study.”

---

## Aleister Crowley, 1944

Crowley does not provide a Waite-like fixed upright/reversed list for the Princess of Disks. He characterizes the card as:

- Earth of Earth;
- strong, beautiful and brooding;
- on the brink of transfiguration;
- bearing the secret of the future;
- ultimate projected womanhood.

**[Documented fact]** The Thoth system generally does not require physical card reversal as a separate dictionary meaning. Dignity, surrounding cards and the balanced or ill-dignified expression of an element modify the reading.

A reversed list attributed to Crowley on modern websites is usually editorial invention.

---

## Paul Foster Case

**[Tradition/esoteric attribution]** Case’s public tarot teaching preserves the Page as a young person, student, messenger or beginning material enterprise and reads Pentacles through work, body, money and tangible result.

**Textual limitation:** an authoritative page-numbered Case quotation giving a distinct upright and reversed Page-of-Pentacles pair was not located in the openly accessible primary materials consulted. Case material is often circulated through BOTA lessons rather than stable public editions. It would be misleading to manufacture a “verbatim Case” quotation from modern keyword compilations.

---

## Rachel Pollack

**[Modern interpretive tradition]** In *Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom*, Pollack treats the Page as a student of the ordinary world, discovering that matter itself contains wonder. The absorbed gaze is openness to learning and to the “magic” inherent in physical existence.

Reversal belongs to the blocked or distorted form of that learning: impracticality, failure to apply knowledge, lack of commitment, or material potential that remains undeveloped.

**Quotation limitation:** no short card-specific passage could be verified against a paginated first-edition scan with sufficient confidence for verbatim quotation. The work should be consulted by edition because pagination changed between the two-volume originals, combined editions and the fortieth-anniversary edition. [Morgan profile of Pollack](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/tarot/23)

## Mary K. Greer

**[Modern interpretive method]** Greer’s major contribution to reversal practice is methodological rather than a single immutable Page definition. A reversed card may express:

- the opposite;
- blocked energy;
- delayed or internalized energy;
- excess;
- an undeveloped quality.

Applied to this Page: study becomes distraction, practice becomes procrastination, stewardship becomes material anxiety, or careful attention becomes fixation.

This “four modes” approach is summarized here, but should not be mistaken for an early cartomantic tradition: [Greer reversal-method summary](https://querent.life/reversals)

## Esoteric and symbolic readings

### Alchemical

**[Esoteric attribution, nineteenth–twentieth century]** Earth of Earth is the fixed, precipitated product of elemental operations—the body, salt, crystal, stone or fertile matrix.

In Crowley’s description the sceptre ends in a diamond in the soil. Highest light is crystallized at the deepest level. The Princess’s pregnancy makes materiality not dead residue but the vessel of a new operation.

**First secure card-specific flowering:** Golden Dawn *Book T*, then Crowley’s *Book of Thoth*. Claims of an alchemical Page of Coins in fifteenth-century decks remain speculative.

### Kabbalistic

**[Esoteric attribution]**

- final Heh of YHVH;
- Malkuth as terminal sephirah and kingdom;
- Assiah as the world of action;
- Earth as the vessel of the other elements;
- the throne receiving the Ace.

Papus popularized the generative four-term formula; the Golden Dawn elaborated it into a fully interlocking court, Tree, world, color and elemental system.

Lévi supplied a decisive earlier Tarot–Hebrew synthesis, but the precise Page = final-Heh = Earth-of-Earth complex belongs to later systematization.

### Astrological

**[Esoteric attribution]** Pentacles/Disks correspond to Earth. Modern readers consequently associate the Page loosely with Taurus, Virgo and Capricorn.

**Correction:** the Golden Dawn did **not** give this Princess an ordinary three-decan span. She governs a terrestrial quadrant. Assigning her solely to Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn, Venus in Taurus, or a modern planet is a later deck-specific or popular invention unless a named source is supplied.

### Hermetic

**[Esoteric attribution]** The card is the point where invisible elemental power becomes an object, body, harvest or act. In the Golden Dawn it completes the suit’s descent; in Crowley it simultaneously prepares a new ascent.

### Christian

**[Tradition/modern interpretation]** The coin may be read as:

- stewardship of talents;
- honest labor;
- the material world as creation;
- the temptation of wealth disciplined by attention;
- incarnation, spirit becoming flesh.

No such explanation accompanies the fifteenth-century card. Christian readings arise from later theological interpretation, Waite’s Christian mysticism, and modern devotional tarot.

### Jungian

**[Modern invention/tradition]** Jung did not publish a card-by-card tarot system. Later Jungian tarot writers construe the Page as:

- the novice ego encountering the sensation function;
- a youthful or neglected embodied self;
- the beginning of individuation through craft and daily practice;
- the *puer/puella* learning limits, patience and value;
- shadow expressions of materialism, inertia or fear of incompetence.

This is post-Jungian application, not a doctrine documented in Jung’s collected works.

### Artists, makers and occultists directly connected

- **Jean Noblet** — early French printed valet.

- **Nicolas Conver** — Marseille pattern associated with the raised coin and second coin on the ground.
- **Jean-Baptiste Alliette/Etteilla** — first systematic professional tarot cartomancer and designer of a purpose-built divinatory tarot.
- **Éliphas Lévi** — fused tarot and Kabbalah, especially through the trumps.
- **Papus** — popularized Tetragrammaton structure and “Tarot of the Bohemians.”
- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers** — published the English 1888 meanings and helped construct Golden Dawn *Book T*.

- **A. E. Waite** — commissioned and interpreted that image.

- **Rachel Pollack and Mary K. Greer** — major late twentieth-century psychological and practical interpreters.

### Literature and film

Most celebrated tarot appearances use the trumps, not the Page of Pentacles.

- **T. S. Eliot, *The Waste Land* (1922):** Madame Sosostris names “the drowned Phoenician Sailor,” “Belladonna,” “the man with three staves,” Wheel and blank card. **No Page of Pentacles is named.**
- **Italo Calvino, *The Castle of Crossed Destinies*:** tarot cards generate interlocking narratives, but no single Page-of-Coins episode has become a universally accepted fixed identification.
- **W. B. Yeats:** Golden Dawn member whose poetry shares the order’s symbolic world. A direct Page-of-Pentacles quotation was not traced.
- **Ingmar Bergman:** tarot-like medieval allegory is often invoked around *The Seventh Seal*, but the film does not document a specific Page-of-Pentacles borrowing.
- **Jodorowsky’s films:** tarot, alchemy and initiation shape his visual language, especially *The Holy Mountain*, but a direct shot-for-card quotation of this Page requires case-specific visual proof.

### Paintings and buildings

**Finding:** no securely documented major building or canonical premodern painting was found that demonstrably quotes the Page of Pentacles as a card. Modern murals, tattoos and illustrations frequently quote Smith’s pose, but resemblance alone is not evidence of an intentional architectural quotation.

## Controversies and disputes

### Egyptian origin

**[Legend, first explicit major formulation 1781]** Court de Gébelin declared tarot a surviving Egyptian sacred book:

> “Would it not be at its height, if one were to assure [the reader] that one has never suspected that it was Egyptian…?”

[Court de Gébelin, *Le Monde primitif*, vol. VIII, “Du jeu des Tarots,” 1781](https://ldysinger.com/%40texts2/1776_Court_Masons_Tarot/02_Monde_Primitif.htm); [French transcription](https://www.tarock.info/gebelin.htm)

The Comte de Mellet’s accompanying essay mapped the twenty-two trumps to Hebrew letters.

**Evidence against literal antiquity:**

- ordinary cards are documented in Europe in the late fourteenth century;
- tarot trumps appear in fifteenth-century northern Italy;
- early decks use contemporary Italian court costume, heraldry and Christian-European civic imagery;
- there is no ancient Egyptian tarot pack or transmission document;
- Gébelin wrote before Egyptian hieroglyphs were deciphered.

Dummett notes that occult etymologies of “tarot” supplied alleged Egyptian phrases without hieroglyphic evidence. [Dummett, “Origins of Tarot,” *New York Review of Books*, 1981](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1981/05/14/origins-of-tarot/)

For this card particularly, “Coin servant” fits the Mamluk–European playing-card transmission far better than an Egyptian priestly tablet.

### Romani or “Gypsy” origin

**[Legend]** Nineteenth-century occultists promoted the idea that Romani travelers carried tarot from Egypt. Papus’s title *Le Tarot des Bohémiens* became a powerful disseminator.

**Documented correction:** Romani linguistic and historical origins lie in South Asia, and their arrival in western Europe postdates the emergence of European playing cards. No early Italian document attributes *trionfi* to Romani transmitters.

The theory nevertheless mattered culturally and should not be erased: it shaped Etteilla reception, Papus, popular fortune-telling imagery and twentieth-century occult publishing. Its historical influence is real even though its account of origin is unsupported.

### Cathar origin

**[Disputed modern claim/legend]** Some twentieth-century writers read the Papess, Pope, Tower and Grail-like Cups as encrypted Cathar doctrine.

For the Page of Coins, alleged clues usually involve humble labor, secret initiation or spiritualized matter. No Cathar manuscript names or depicts the card, and there is no transmission chain from Cathar communities to fifteenth-century Milanese card workshops.

### Templar origin

**[Legend/modern invention]** Claims that the Knights Templar encoded doctrine in tarot face a chronological gap: the order was suppressed in the early fourteenth century; tarot is documented in northern Italy in the 1440s. No Templar inventory, trial record or surviving object contains a tarot sequence.

No Page-of-Pentacles evidence repairs that gap.

### “Pentacles” as ancient suit name

**[Disputed popular claim]** Modern books sometimes speak as if medieval tarot always had Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles.

**Correction:** early Italian cards show **Coins/Denari**. The five-pointed magical emblem and the English suit-title **Pentacles** belong to later occult redesign, especially the Golden Dawn–Waite line. Crowley’s “Disks” is another deliberate renaming.

### The Page as apprentice

**[Tradition with identifiable strengthening]** A *fante* or valet is socially subordinate, so apprenticeship is a plausible analogy. But the precise “student concentrating on practical study” became visually decisive in Smith’s 1909 image and Waite’s “application, study, scholarship.”

Reading the Conver costume as a demonstrable guild apprentice’s apron is plausible but unproven without contemporary maker commentary.

### The Visconti sitter

**[Disputed claim]** The card’s courtliness and heraldic setting may evoke the patronal family. Naming an individual family member goes beyond the available evidence.

### The second Marseille coin

**[Open question]** It may be:

- a compositional suit marker;
- ground capital;
- an object awaiting collection;
- a printer’s inherited convention;
- symbolic contrast between held and embedded wealth.

No early explanatory text settles the issue.

### Misprints and editorial traditions

Several recurrent errors have become quasi-traditional:

1. **“Conver 1760” treated as the date of every surviving Conver card.**  
   Pattern date and individual surviving impression must be distinguished.

2. **Wirth credited with a complete 78-card 1889 deck.**  
   His 1889 deck had only twenty-two trumps.

3. **Princess given a three-decan zodiac span.**  
   Golden Dawn Princesses govern quadrants rather than the thirty-degree arcs of Knights, Queens and Princes.

4. **Crowley assigned a conventional reversed meaning.**  
   Modern reversed lists are usually editorial.

5. **Cary-Yale and Visconti di Modrone treated as separate decks.**  
   They are alternative names for the same group.

6. **The Page assigned a Hebrew letter like a trump.**  
   Final Heh here is a position in YHVH, not a unique one-to-one Hebrew-letter attribution among the 22 paths.

7. **Mamluk *nāʾib* equated directly with the modern Page.**  
   It is a structural ancestor in a three-court system, not a one-to-one iconographic identity.

## Historiographical correction

The principal modern historical correction associated with Michael Dummett, Ronald Decker and Thierry Depaulis is not that occult tarot is culturally meaningless. It is that its ancient-origin claims should not be confused with the documentary chronology:

1. playing cards entered Europe in the late Middle Ages;
2. tarot was invented for games in fifteenth-century northern Italy;
3. the Coin Page began as a playing-card court;
4. widespread systematic occult interpretation arose much later;
5. Gébelin, Etteilla, Lévi, Papus, the Golden Dawn, Waite and Crowley successively constructed new symbolic systems.

Stuart Kaplan’s encyclopedic collecting, Moakley’s iconographic research, Robert Place’s comparative visual work and Helen Farley’s cultural history widened the discussion, though they do not always agree on every iconographic inference.

The strongest reconstruction for this card is therefore layered:

> Mamluk deputy structure → Italian human *fante di denari* → French *valet de deniers* → Etteilla cartomantic person/message → Golden Dawn Princess, Earth of Earth → Waite-Smith student with pentacle → Crowley-Harris fertile Princess of Disks → modern learner, craftsperson and embodied potential.

## Open questions

1. Which surviving Italian fragment should be assigned absolute priority as the earliest standard, male Fante of Coins rather than the expanded female Cary-Yale servant?
2. Can workshop or pigment analysis narrow the relative dates of the Brambilla, Cary-Yale and Visconti-Sforza groups?
3. Did Pamela Colman Smith work from a specific Marseille exemplar for this card, or from Waite’s directions plus general familiarity?
4. Was Conver’s second ground coin inherited from a now-lost model, or introduced for compositional balance?
5. Which exact Etteilla recension first fixed each upright and reversed keyword for the Coin valet?
6. How consistently did individual Golden Dawn temples draw the Princess’s disk, altar, sceptre and landscape?
7. Can any claimed literary or cinematic quotation of this particular card be supported by an artist’s statement, draft or production record?
8. Which modern deck first explicitly recast the Page of Pentacles as a school student rather than a court attendant?
9. Are some modern “Earth-sign” assignments independent developments, or degradations of the Golden Dawn quadrant doctrine?
10. No primary evidence yet establishes a named Visconti-Sforza sitter for the card.

# Queen of Pentacles: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of statements:

- **[Documented fact]** Supported by a surviving card, archival record, contemporary publication, or museum catalogue.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** A conclusion argued from surviving material by historians or curators but not stated in a contemporary record.
- **[Tradition]** A stable convention transmitted by cardmakers or readers without necessarily claiming ancient authority.
- **[Esoteric attribution]** A deliberate occult correspondence assigned by a named author or school.
- **[Disputed claim]** An interpretation for which specialists offer competing identifications.
- **[Legend/modern invention]** A story or correspondence unsupported by evidence from the period it purports to describe.

The Queen of Pentacles is a suited court card, not a trump. Consequently, several subjects in the general brief—trump order, Justice–Strength numbering, the Fool’s number, Juno/Jupiter substitutions, Hebrew letters assigned to individual trumps, and Crowley’s Tzaddi–Heh exchange—do not alter this card. They are identified below where necessary, but are not falsely attributed to it.

## Basic identification

| Field | Identification |
|---|---|
| Card class | Court card in a 14-card Latin suit |
| Historical suit | Italian **denari**; French **deniers**; English **coins** |
| Occult English suit | **Pentacles**, principally through nineteenth-century occult terminology and the Golden Dawn/Waite tradition |
| Thoth suit | **Disks** |
| Rank | Queen, below King and above Knight in most tarot games |
| Intrinsic number | **None**. Historical court cards were not serially numbered |
| Catalogue/system numbers | Morgan MS M.630 no. 21; Mathers’s complete-pack sequence no. 65; *Book T* list no. 18; “13” in some modern suit-by-suit schemes |
| Earliest surviving example | Probably the Queen of Coins in the **Visconti di Modrone/Cary–Yale** pack, Milan, approximately 1440–45 |
| Earliest securely catalogued complete-style example | Queen of Coins in the **Visconti–Sforza** group, Milan or Cremona, approximately 1450–58; Morgan MS M.630.21 |
| Golden Dawn title | **The Queen of the Thrones of Earth** |
| Golden Dawn sub-element | **Water of Earth** |
| Golden Dawn zodiacal span | **20° Sagittarius through 20° Capricorn** |
| Golden Dawn elemental ruler | “Queen of the Gnomes” |
| Qabalistic formula | Queen = first **Heh** of YHVH; Earth/Pentacles = final **Heh**. Thus, in later shorthand, “Heh of final Heh” |
| Sephirotic/world formulation | Queens correspond to **Binah**; Pentacles to **Assiah** in the standard Golden Dawn four-world arrangement. Hence Binah operating in Assiah, though that exact phrase is later explanatory shorthand rather than the card’s printed title |

The Golden Dawn’s manuscript gives the operative description directly: “She rules from 20 Degree Sagittarius to 20 Degree Capricorn,” followed by “Water of Earth” and “The Queen of Gnomes.” It calls her **“The Queen of the Thrones of Earth.”** [*Book T*, pp. 19–20](https://jantetteroo.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Mathers_and_felkin_golden_dawn_book_t_th.pdf).

### Names across languages and systems

| Context | Name |
|---|---|
| Fifteenth-century Italian | **Regina di denari**, modern descriptive title; the painted cards themselves are normally untitled |
| Modern Italian | **Regina di Denari**, sometimes **Regina di Pentacoli** in occult translations |
| Early French | **Royne/Reyne de Deniers**, with spelling varying by printer |
| Standard French | **Reine de Deniers** |
| Etteilla | **Reine de Denier**, also transmitted as **Dame de Denier** |
| English historical | **Queen of Coins** |
| Golden Dawn/Waite | **Queen of Pentacles** |
| Crowley–Harris | **Queen of Disks** |
| Spanish | **Reina de Oros**; occult translations also use **Reina de Pentáculos** |
| German | **Königin der Münzen**; Waite translations commonly **Königin der Pentakel**; Thoth **Königin der Scheiben** |
| Portuguese | **Rainha de Ouros** |
| Marseille-influenced English | Queen of Coins or Queen of Deniers |

**[Documented fact]** Medieval Italian tarot cards usually carried neither captions nor numbers on their court cards. “Regina di denari” is therefore a modern catalogue description, not an inscription proven to have stood on the earliest card.

**[Correction]** Spanish ordinary packs traditionally contain **rey, caballo, sota**, not a queen. Italian regional packs varied, and many standard 40-card Italian patterns likewise lacked queens. Tarot, however, contains Queen, King, Knight and Page from its earliest reconstructable standard form. The Queen was not invented by occultists.

**[Qualification]** Queens were not altogether unknown in non-tarot Italian cards: documentary evidence places queens in some Italian packs by the early fifteenth century. The sound conclusion is therefore that tarot institutionalized a four-member court, not that it introduced the first European queen card.

## Iconography, deck by deck

### Mamluk antecedent, Egypt or Syria, fourteenth–fifteenth century

**[Documented fact]** The near-complete Mamluk pack found in Istanbul contains four suits—cups, coins, swords and polo-sticks—and three court ranks: **malik**, **nāʾib malik**, and **thānī nāʾib**. Its court cards bear inscriptions and suit emblems but do not depict queens or human royal portraits.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The Latin suits, ten numerals and hierarchical courts of early European cards derive substantially from this card family. The European Queen is not a direct translation of a Mamluk female rank; she is an adaptation within European court culture. The Arabic *nāʾib* probably lies behind Italian *naibi* and Spanish *naipes*. A concise historical summary appears in Helen Farley’s discussion of the Topkapı pack and its ranks in [*A Cultural History of Tarot*](https://e-isomero.com/pdf/book/1611846398.pdf).

**Meaning of the change:** abstract offices became embodied European social types. A female sovereign or consort was inserted into the hierarchy, while tarot also retained both mounted knight and lower attendant.

### Marziano da Tortona/Michelino da Besozzo, Milan, approximately 1412–25

**[Documented fact]** Marziano’s surviving Latin treatise describes a special game made for Filippo Maria Visconti, with sixteen classical gods and four suits of allegorical birds. No cards survive.

**[Finding]** This was a precursor to the idea of a permanent trump suit, not a standard 78-card tarot. It provides no Queen of Coins, no denari suit, and no evidence for Water of Earth.

### Visconti di Modrone, or Cary–Yale, Milan, approximately 1440–45

**[Documented fact]** Yale describes this as one of the three earliest surviving tarot groups, probably made before Filippo Maria Visconti’s death in 1447. Coin impressions resembling currency of his reign contribute to the dating. The pack has an unusually expanded court: King, Queen, male Knight, male Page, female mounted figure and female attendant. [Yale Library’s conservation account](https://library.yale.edu/news/cards-library-partners-work-together-solve-mysteries-rare-tarot-deck?page=1).

The Queen of Coins is an enthroned, crowned courtly woman holding the suit emblem. Gold ground, costly pigments and stamped metal leaf identify the object as a luxury court commission.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** This is probably the earliest surviving Queen of Coins. The expanded male/female court has been interpreted as an experiment in gender parity or as evidence of a specially commissioned ducal structure.

**[Disputed claim]** Dates from approximately 1441 to the 1460s have appeared in older literature. Yale’s current approximately 1440–45 dating is preferable to the unsourced claim that it must be “around 1466.”

### Brera–Brambilla, Milan, approximately 1440s

This fragmentary luxury pack was made under Filippo Maria Visconti and is now associated with the Pinacoteca di Brera.

**[Finding]** Surviving Brambilla cards demonstrate the same Milanese environment and denari imagery, but the card-specific public catalogue material consulted did not provide enough evidence to assert securely that its Queen of Coins survives. Reconstructions that include one may supply a newly painted completion. It should not displace the Cary–Yale card as the earliest surviving candidate without a collection-level record.

### Visconti–Sforza, Milan or Cremona, approximately 1450–58

**[Documented fact]** The Morgan Queen of Coins is MS M.630.21, attributed traditionally to Bonifacio Bembo or his workshop. It measures 173 × 87 mm. The Morgan describes it concisely: a crowned queen sits and holds a coin in her right hand. [Morgan object record](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/tarot-cards/the-queen-of-coins) and [image catalogue](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/21/158525).

She sits frontally or slightly turned before a textile-like field and tooled gold ground, wearing contemporary Milanese court dress. One large coin is held upright; it is a literal suit sign, not yet a pentagram.

The Morgan group forms part of a surviving 74-card ensemble divided among the Morgan, the Accademia Carrara and the Colleoni collection. The original complete pack is reconstructed as 78 cards: 21 trumps, Fool, and four 14-card suits. [Morgan manuscript catalogue](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525).

#### Is she a Visconti or Sforza woman?

**[Disputed claim]** Gertrude Moakley and Stuart Kaplan helped popularize attempts to read members of the Visconti–Sforza family into individual figures. Bianca Maria Visconti is the most tempting general identification for a crowned female court figure.

**Evidence against certainty:** the card has no identifying caption, heraldic label or individualized portrait documentation. Courtly dress and family devices establish patronage, not personal identity.

**Finding:** “Bianca Maria Visconti” should be reported as a conjectural identification, not as a documented portrait. Current Morgan cataloguing calls her simply the Queen of Coins and says only that the deck was made for some member of the Visconti–Sforza family.

### Este/Ferrarese fragments, fifteenth century

The Charles VI, Este, Catania and related fragments establish vigorous Ferrarese production, but their survival is uneven and weighted toward trumps.

**[Finding]** No securely identified Queen of Coins in the major Este fragment groups supplied a distinct iconographic stage traceable from a primary collection record during this research. Modern “completed” editions must not be mistaken for surviving originals.

### Mantegna “Tarocchi,” northern Italy, approximately 1460s

**[Documented fact]** The so-called Mantegna Tarocchi consist of fifty didactic engravings arranged in five ten-card series—social stations, Muses, arts/sciences, virtues and celestial spheres.

**Finding:** they are not a tarot pack and contain no Queen of Coins. They are relevant only as evidence for the fifteenth-century visual culture of ranked encyclopedic series.

### Rosenwald and Budapest sheets, late fifteenth/early sixteenth century

These uncut or fragmentary printed sheets are important witnesses to early popular printed tarot and to the development of standard trump designs.

**Finding:** surviving and published sheets do not furnish a securely separable Queen of Coins with enough detail to form an independent stage in this card’s genealogy. Their chief value lies in demonstrating the transition from unique court commissions to reproducible woodcut packs.

### Sola Busca, northern Italy, engravings approximately 1491; surviving painted set later fifteenth century

**[Documented fact]** The Sola Busca is the earliest substantially complete surviving 78-card tarot and the earliest surviving tarot whose numeral suit cards carry elaborate figured scenes. The Queen of Coins is inscribed **ELENA**. The coin court consists of Sarafino, Elena and R. Filipo among the named mounted/royal figures. [Sola Busca card list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sola_Busca_tarot); the Brera exhibition catalogue supplies the serious art-historical context: [*Il Segreto dei Segreti*](https://pinacotecabrera.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Il-Segreto-dei-tarocchi-eng.pdf).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Elena is usually identified as Helen of Troy. That fits a program populated by classical, pseudo-historical and legendary figures, but the precise organizing logic of all names remains debated.

**Meaning of the change:** the anonymous generic Queen becomes a named ancient heroine. This is not yet the Golden Dawn “Earth Mother”; it belongs to the Sola Busca’s distinctive historical and possibly Hermetic program.

**[Disputed claim]** The 2012 Brera exhibition connected the deck with late-fifteenth-century Hermetic and alchemical culture and proposed Nicola di Maestro Antonio as engraver. This is a substantive scholarly hypothesis supported by iconographic comparisons, not proof that every individual card constitutes an alchemical cipher.

### Tarot de Marseille lineage

#### Jean Noblet, Paris, approximately 1650

The card is captioned in early French orthography, normally transcribed **REYNE DE DENIERS**. A crowned woman turns in profile or three-quarter profile and displays one large floral coin. The other hand rests near or supports it. Her costume is compressed into the strong red, blue, yellow and flesh-colour woodblock vocabulary.

**[Documented iconographic continuity]** Like the Visconti Queen, she is an identifiable queen because of crown, costume and rank position; the coin remains a round suit emblem without a pentagram.

#### Jean Dodal, Lyon, approximately 1701–15

Dodal regularizes the Marseille form: crowned queen, elaborate collar and sleeves, large coin held before her body. The caption is commonly **REYNE DE DENIERS**. Line, face and colouring change, but no garden, rabbit, element symbol or astrological mark appears.

#### Nicolas Conver, Marseille, 1760/61

Conver’s **REINE DE DENIERS** is a mature crowned woman, turned toward the coin she holds. The composition is still heraldic and emblematic rather than narrative. The coin bears the familiar floral/rosette geometry characteristic of deniers.

**Meaning of the lineage:** the Queen is defined socially by crown and composition, and materially by the emblem. Later occult readings of the coin as a magical pentacle should not be read backward into these cards.

#### Grimaud/Paul Marteau, Paris, twentieth century

The Grimaud *Ancien Tarot de Marseille*, especially the Paul Marteau formulation associated with 1930, recoloured and standardized the Conver stream. The Queen remains recognizably Conver-derived.

**[Tradition]** Marteau gave the colours and forms extensive esoteric interpretations. Those interpretations are twentieth-century readings of inherited printer conventions, not demonstrated seventeenth-century intentions.

#### Camoin–Jodorowsky, 1997

Jodorowsky and Philippe Camoin described their deck as a restoration assembled from surviving Marseille witnesses. The Queen retains the crowned, profile-oriented, coin-bearing structure.

**[Disputed claim]** Calling the result the recovery of one original, encoded “Tarot de Marseille” overstates what the dispersed evidence can establish. It is also a modern synthetic reconstruction with deliberate line and colour choices.

Jodorowsky and Marianne Costa later interpret her through body posture, gaze, receptive material power and creative fecundity. Those meanings belong to their twenty-first-century reading method, not to Conver’s workshop documentation.

### Minchiate

Minchiate expanded the trump series to 40 while maintaining Latin suits and four courts. Its Queen of Coins remains a suited court card, not one of the additional cosmological trumps.

**Game significance:** its identity and relative court rank survive even though the trump structure differs radically from ordinary tarot.

**Finding:** zodiac signs printed among Minchiate’s added trumps do not make this Queen a Capricorn card. The Queen’s Sagittarius–Capricorn span is a Golden Dawn attribution created centuries later.

### Bolognese tarocchino

The Bolognese pack reduced the suited numerals but retained court cards, including a Queen of Coins. Designs are double-ended in later gaming packs.

**Finding:** Bolognese special trump ordering, unnumbered equal-ranking trumps, and the *matto* concern the game around her but do not assign her a trump number or occult letter.

### Sicilian tarot

The Tarocco Siciliano underwent unusual reductions and regional redesigns. Its distinctive trump sequence and Jupiter-related material belong to its trump series.

**Finding:** no secure, card-specific Sicilian esoteric reinterpretation of the Queen of Coins emerged from the sources consulted. She remained a court card where the appropriate court survived.

### Besançon and Swiss JJ patterns

The best-known regional substitutions replace the Papess and Pope with **Juno and Jupiter**. These are trump II and V substitutions made in Protestant or confessionally sensitive markets.

**Finding:** they do not rename or replace the Queen of Coins. The Swiss JJ designation comes from Juno–Jupiter, not from two court-card Jacks.

### Grand Etteilla, Paris, approximately 1788–89

Etteilla rearranged the entire tarot into an explicitly divinatory “Book of Thoth.” In the later standard numbered transmission, the Queen of Coins/Pentacles is **65**.

**Iconography:** editions vary. The card presents a female court figure and printed divinatory words; it is less a pristine single design than a family of Etteilla I/II/III printings.

A nineteenth-century manual calls her **“Reine de Denier ou de Trèfle”** and describes a wealthy, generous country relative who brings gifts. [*Le Grand Etteilla, ou l’art de tirer les cartes*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2f/Le_grand_Etteilla%2C_ou%2C_l%27art_de_tirer_les_cartes_..._Le_tout_recueilli_..._%28IA_b29321220%29.pdf).

**[Qualification]** That manual is part of the Etteilla tradition, not necessarily verbatim Jean-Baptiste Alliette. Etteilla’s decks and teachings were repeatedly revised by followers.

### Oswald Wirth, 1889

**Finding:** Wirth’s celebrated 1889 tarot consisted of 22 trumps. It contains no Queen of Pentacles. Any “Wirth Queen” in a 78-card modern product is a later extension.

### Papus, 1889 and 1909

In *Le Tarot des Bohémiens* (1889), Papus treats the entire minor-arcana structure through YHVH:

- Kings = Yod
- Queens = first Heh
- Knights = Vau
- Knaves = final Heh
- Pentacles as a suit = final Heh

He explicitly places the Queen of Pentacles among the first-Heh analogues. [Papus, *Tarot of the Bohemians*, chapter V](https://guru-ai.org/read/western_esoteric/tarot-of-the-bohemians/017).

Papus’s fully illustrated 78-card occult deck came later, in *Le Tarot divinatoire* (1909), drawn by Jean-Gabriel Goulinat. [Deck record](https://foolmade.art/archive/papus-tarot).

### Golden Dawn manuscript tarot, late 1880s–1890s

*Book T* prescribes a queen seated over dark sandy ground, shown in profile because one side of her face is light and the other dark. She has:

- a winged goat’s-head crest;
- a goat beside her;
- a cube-topped sceptre;
- a golden orb;
- dark hair;
- Water-of-Earth and Sagittarius–Capricorn attributions.

This is a decisive transformation. The coin becomes a magical orb or pentacle; Capricorn is visualized by the goat; fertile and barren Earth, light and darkness, and emotional variability are joined in one figure.

### Israel Regardie/Robert Wang reconstruction, 1977–78

Robert Wang’s *Golden Dawn Tarot*, prepared with Israel Regardie, translates Golden Dawn colour-scale and *Book T* instructions into a complete painted deck. Its Queen follows the goat, cube, sandy-earth and Water-of-Earth program.

**[Modern reconstruction]** It is not a facsimile of one canonical Victorian deck. Golden Dawn members were expected to construct or colour personal packs, and surviving instructions permit variation.

### Sandra Tabatha and Chic Cicero reconstruction, 1990s onward

The Ciceros’ *Golden Dawn Magical Tarot* supplies another full realization of Order documents, usually with more explicit colour-scale and temple-symbol presentation.

**Meaning:** Wang and the Ciceros demonstrate how one textual specification produces differing legitimate images. Neither proves exactly what every 1890s adept’s card looked like.

### Waite–Smith, London, 1909

Pamela Colman Smith depicts a crowned queen in a fertile garden. She sits on a carved stone throne, bends over a gold pentacle held or cradled in both hands, and is surrounded by roses and dense vegetation. A rabbit appears in the lower foreground. The throne carries vegetal, caprine and cherubic decoration.

Waite’s description reads:

> “She contemplates her symbol and may see worlds therein.”

He adds that her face suggests a dark woman, “greatness of soul,” and a serious intelligence. [Waite, *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*, 1910](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=7a15961664bced51d1162198bce46d173e40dd41&repid=rep1&type=pdf).

**Documented changes:**

1. The denier is now visibly a **pentacle**, a coin bearing a pentagram.
2. The generic court background becomes a narrative environment.
3. Fertility is made explicit through garden, fruit, flowers and rabbit.
4. The queen embraces rather than merely displays the emblem.
5. The Golden Dawn’s Earth symbolism remains, but its barren sand, winged crest and explicit magical implements are largely concealed or naturalized.

**Sources:** the immediate compositional type still derives from a crowned queen holding a suit emblem. Golden Dawn teaching explains the Earth, goat and magical pentacle vocabulary. Smith may have seen Sola Busca images at the British Museum, but the strongest documented visual loans from Sola Busca concern cards such as the Three of Swords and Ten of Wands. No sufficiently exact Sola Busca–RWS Queen correspondence proves direct copying.

**[Modern tradition]** The rabbit is normally read as fertility, fecundity, embodiment or rapid material increase. Waite did not give it a separate gloss; precise rabbit symbolism is therefore interpretive.

### Paul Foster Case and Jessie Burns Parke, BOTA, twentieth century

The BOTA tarot uses black-line court drawings intended for colouring by students. Parke and Case retain Waite/Golden Dawn pentacles and occult structure while declining Smith’s fully scenic treatment for the numeral cards.

**[Esoteric attribution]** Case maintains the suit–element relationship Pentacles/Earth and the Qabalistic court structure inherited from the Golden Dawn.

**Finding:** Case’s publicly available writings do not support a new Hebrew letter uniquely assigned to this Queen. Claims that BOTA gives her a trump-style path are category errors.

### Crowley–Harris Thoth, painted approximately 1938–43; published 1969

Crowley renames the suit **Disks**, explaining that a disk should be understood as a dynamic, whirling emblem rather than merely money.

Frieda Harris’s Queen of Disks sits amid luxuriant vegetation, looking back over a winding river that fertilizes a desert. She wears scale- or coin-like armour and a helmet with great markhor horns. A goat stands on a sphere. She carries a cube-topped sceptre and an elaborate disk composed of interlaced loops and circles.

Crowley writes that she is:

> “the watery part of Earth, the function of that element as Mother.”

He assigns 21° Sagittarius to 20° Capricorn and describes the river bringing fertility to desert, the goat, cube, disk and “ambition of matter” to participate in creation. [Crowley, *The Book of Thoth*, “The Court Cards”](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/court-cards).

**Meaning of the change:** Crowley and Harris restore most of *Book T*’s goat/desert/cube program, but render it in a biomorphic, sexual, cosmological style. Water of Earth is shown literally as river-water making desert fertile.

**Tzaddi/Heh correction:** Crowley’s “Tzaddi is not the Star” exchange concerns the Emperor and Star trumps. It has no bearing on the Queen of Disks. Her “Heh” is the court-rank component of YHVH, not the Hebrew-letter attribution of a path.

### Notable modern redesigns

Modern RWS-derived Queens commonly emphasize one of four strands:

- **Domestic provider:** food, home, budgeting and caregiving.
- **Earth Mother:** pregnancy, ecology, fertility and embodiment.
- **Professional steward:** finance, management, sustainable material power.
- **Shadow/reversed figure:** self-neglect, possessiveness, insecurity or work–home conflict.

Gender-inclusive decks rename courts as **Mother**, **Keeper**, **Guardian**, **Matron**, **Sovereign** or elemental ranks. This is a **modern invention or redesign**, not a recovery of a lost medieval name.

## The game

### Composition and rank

**[Documented fact]** A standard historical tarot suit contains fourteen cards: King, Queen, Knight, Page/Jack and ten numerals.

In modern French tarot, suit order is:

**King > Queen > Knight > Jack > 10 … Ace**

The Fédération Française de Tarot rules value a Queen at four card-points when paired with an ordinary half-point card. [French rules](https://www.letarot.net/en/rules.html). This is often expressed as Queen = 4½ raw points, with the paired low card producing the practical four-point count.

Across tarock games, counting conventions vary:

- King normally outranks Queen.
- Queen normally outranks Knight/Cavalier.
- Queen is commonly worth four points in additive systems.
- Some Austrian systems use subtraction or packet-counting, producing superficially different totals. [Comparative card-point systems](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/counting.html).

The Queen of Coins has no special trumping power. She follows suit and may be captured by any trump or a higher coin court according to the game.

### Italian and Spanish regular packs

**[Documented fact]** Standard Spanish-suited packs normally have King, Horse and Knave, with no Queen. Many Italian regional packs likewise omit her.

**[Qualification]** Tarot’s Queen was present in the earliest reconstructable tarot pack, but queens also appeared in some other European and Italian packs. It is inaccurate to claim that tarot invented the queen universally.

### Minchiate

Minchiate courts follow their suit hierarchy and point system. Its greatly enlarged trump series does not transform the Queen of Coins into a numbered emblem.

### Trump-order disputes

Historians distinguish three broad early Italian trump-order families, often associated with:

- Milan/Lombardy;
- Ferrara/Venice;
- Bologna/Florence.

Justice, Fortitude and Temperance can occupy different relative positions; later French packs number Justice VIII and Strength XI, while Waite exchanged their numbers. The Fool may be unnumbered, zero or treated as an Excuse.

**Finding:** none of those differences changes the Queen’s rank. Applying “VIII/XI,” “0,” Juno/Jupiter or Maison Dieu data to her would conflate suited cards with trumps.

## Chronology of attestation

### Approximately 1412–25 — Marziano da Tortona

**[Documented fact]** Marziano describes a special Visconti game with gods as trumps.

**Finding:** no Queen of Coins.

### 1440 — Florence

A record discovered after older histories were published mentions *naibi a trionfi*. This is now often treated as the earliest surviving documentary notice of cards called trionfi.

### 1442 — Ferrara

**[Documented fact]** The Este accounts record payments for painted *carte da trionfi*. On 28 July a merchant supplied a pack for Ercole and Sigismondo d’Este. [Le Tarot association transcription](https://letarot.it/Le-origini-italiane-del-Ludus-Triumphorum_pag_pg439_ita.aspx); [Ferrara cultural history](https://rivista.fondazioneestense.it/1996/4/item/407-la-carta-del-principe.html).

The ledger names the pack, not every suited card. Thus it attests the milieu in which a Queen of Denari existed, but does not say “Regina di denari.”

### Approximately 1440–45 — Cary–Yale cards

Earliest likely surviving Queen of Coins.

### Approximately 1450–58 — Visconti–Sforza

Surviving Queen of Coins now Morgan MS M.630.21.

### Approximately 1450–1500 — *Sermo perutilis de ludo*

The anonymous sermon condemns the *ludus triumphorum* and gives a trump list. It is vital to reconstructing early trump subjects.

**Finding:** because it lists trumps, it does not individually name the Queen of Coins.

### 1491 — Sola Busca

Surviving named Queen of Coins, **ELENA**.

### Around 1500 — Boiardo and tarot poems

Matteo Maria Boiardo’s poetic tarot gives moralized verses for an alternative suit/trump scheme. Other early-sixteenth-century poems demonstrate literary play with cards.

**Finding:** these do not establish the later “Earth Mother” Queen of Pentacles meaning.

### 1505 — Ferrara and Avignon terminology

The earliest known uses of *tarocchi/taraux* as the card-game name occur in 1505; fifteenth-century records had normally said *trionfi*. [Dummett historical summary](https://forum.tarothistory.com/viewtopic.php?t=1185).

### 1520s — Teofilo Folengo

Folengo’s macaronic writing contains an early literary divinatory game involving tarocchi. It is evidence that cards could be used in improvised character or fortune verses well before systematic occult tarot.

**Qualification:** this is not evidence for Golden Dawn correspondences or a stable Queen-of-Pentacles definition.

### Approximately 1650 — Noblet

First major surviving Marseille-type witness with a printed **Reyne de Deniers**.

### 1701–15 — Dodal

Standardizes the Lyon Marseille-type court.

### 1760/61 — Conver

Influential Marseille printing from which many later editions descend.

### 1781 — Court de Gébelin and Comte de Mellet

Gébelin declares tarot an Egyptian book; Mellet associates the 22 trumps with the Hebrew alphabet.

**Finding:** neither assigns a Hebrew letter, Tree path or zodiacal span to the Queen of Coins. Their 22-letter scheme concerns the trumps.

### 1783–85; approximately 1788–89 — Etteilla

Etteilla publishes systematic tarot cartomancy and then an expressly occult/divinatory deck. Queen becomes card 65 in the later standard sequence.

### 1854–56 — Éliphas Lévi, *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*

Lévi makes the 22 trumps into a central Kabbalistic magical alphabet.

**Finding:** no unique Lévi Hebrew letter or path for the Queen of Coins. Later tables that assign one to her improperly extend a trump system.

### 1863/1870 — Paul Christian

Christian elaborates fictional Egyptian initiation chambers and “arcana,” greatly spreading an invented Egyptian genealogy.

**Finding:** his contribution is principally to occult tarot mythology, not a securely independent Queen-of-Pentacles doctrine.

### 1888 — S. L. MacGregor Mathers

Mathers numbers the Queen of Pentacles **65** in his complete-pack list and publishes upright/reversed meanings substantially indebted to Etteilla.

### 1889 — Papus and Wirth

Papus systematizes YHVH across ranks and suits. Wirth publishes only trumps.

### Late 1880s–1890s — Golden Dawn *Book T*

The card receives its fully developed Water-of-Earth, goat, cube and zodiacal identity.

### 1909–10 — Waite–Smith and *Pictorial Key*

Smith creates the garden, rabbit and cradled pentacle image; Waite publishes its verbal description and meanings.

### Twentieth century — BOTA

Case and Parke transmit a Golden Dawn/Waite-derived esoteric Queen in a student-colouring system.

### 1938–43/1969 — Thoth

Harris paints and Crowley interprets the Queen of Disks.

### 1970s onward — psychological tarot

Writers including Rachel Pollack and Mary K. Greer progressively detach courts from mandatory literal sex, complexion and social class. The Queen becomes a mode of inward mastery, care, embodiment and relationship to resources.

### Decans inside the Queen’s Golden Dawn span

The Queen rules forty degrees, comprising:

| Zodiacal portion | Decan card | Planet | *Book T* title |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20°–30° Sagittarius | Ten of Wands | Saturn | **Oppression** |
| 0°–10° Capricorn | Two of Pentacles | Jupiter | **Harmonious Change** |
| 10°–20° Capricorn | Three of Pentacles | Mars | **Material Works** |

The Queen does **not** include 20°–30° Capricorn, which belongs to the Four of Pentacles and the next court sector.

### Important conflicts

1. **20° versus 21° wording:** *Book T* says 20° Sagittarius to 20° Capricorn; Crowley says 21st degree through 20th degree. These describe substantially the same conventional interval using different ordinal/boundary language.

2. **Coin, pentacle, disk:** Medieval and Marseille cards show coins. “Pentacle” reflects occult reinterpretation and Waite’s visible pentagram. Crowley’s Disk is deliberately dynamic and cosmological.

3. **Two Hehs:** Papus assigns Queen to first Heh and the Pentacles suit to final Heh. Calling the Queen herself “final Heh” without qualification confuses rank and suit.

4. **No individual Hebrew letter:** Court cards are not among the 22 paths/letters. The Queen’s Heh relationship is a tetragrammaton formula, not an individual trump-letter assignment.

5. **Tree path:** She has no unique path between two Sephiroth. “Binah in Assiah” is a world/sephirah correspondence, not a path.

## Divinatory meanings by author

Brief quotations are given from public-domain authors. Pollack and Greer remain copyrighted; their views are paraphrased.

### Etteilla tradition, late eighteenth to nineteenth century

**Upright:** a countrywoman or female relative; wealthy, generous and capable of sending presents; for a young male querent, possibly a prosperous dark-haired wife.

**Reversed:** later Etteilla-derived tables emphasize uncertainty, suspicion or an untrustworthy woman.

**Source problem:** The clearest freely available wording occurs in the later *Grand Etteilla* manual rather than an uncontested first-edition Alliette text. Consequently the meanings are securely **Etteilla-school tradition**, but not all can be quoted as Alliette’s own words.

### Mathers, 1888

Mathers’s complete wording begins:

> “A dark Woman, a generous Woman, Liberality, Greatness of Soul…”

Reversed, he gives certain evil, a suspicious woman, doubt and mistrust. [Mathers, *The Tarot*, lines 243–45](https://hermetics.net/media-library/tarot/samuel-liddell-macgregor-mathers-the-tarot/).

Mathers states that many additional observations come chiefly from Etteilla. His no. 65 is therefore a major bridge between French Etteilla cartomancy and English occult tarot.

### Golden Dawn *Book T*

Well dignified: impetuous, kind, timid, charming, intelligent, truthful, great-hearted, melancholy and changeable in mood.

Ill dignified: undecided, capricious, changeable or foolish.

The manuscript says “ill dignified,” not simply “reversed.” Dignity depends on surrounding elemental and positional relationships; it should not automatically be equated with an upside-down card.

### Waite, 1910

Upright:

> “Opulence, generosity, magnificence, security, liberty.”

Reversed:

> “Evil, suspicion, suspense, fear, mistrust.”

Waite also characterizes the face as dark, serious and intelligent and says she may see worlds in the pentacle. [*Pictorial Key* text](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=7a15961664bced51d1162198bce46d173e40dd41&repid=rep1&type=pdf).

The reversed list closely preserves Mathers/Etteilla suspicion and mistrust, while the upright “opulence” accords with Smith’s luxuriant garden.

### Crowley, 1944

Crowley’s Queen is passive “usually in its highest aspect,” maternal and fertile. People represented by her have quiet virtues. His practical advice is to advance without overt attack.

**Qualification:** Crowley’s *Book of Thoth* paragraph does not supply a conventional upright/reversed pair. Thoth readers normally use elemental dignity and context rather than card reversal.

### Paul Foster Case

Case reads the Pentacles courts through Earth, practicality, embodiment and material administration. His Queen is a dark-featured woman or a receptive, practically constructive mode of consciousness.

**Finding:** an exact public-domain Case passage giving a distinct upright/reversed pair for this card could not be verified from the primary editions available in this search. Modern lists often blend Case with Waite or Golden Dawn language and should not be quoted as Case without an edition and page.

### Rachel Pollack

In *Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom*, Pollack develops the RWS Queen as a warm, sensual, capable and materially grounded person: someone who loves nature and bodily life and can turn ideas into tangible reality. The shadow tends toward dullness, possessiveness or being trapped in material convention.

**Status:** modern psychological/esoteric synthesis. Pollack’s importance lies less in inventing “fertility” than in humanizing the Queen as an experiential personality and inner capacity.

### Mary K. Greer

Greer’s court-card and reversal work treats the Queen as a role, psychological function or part of the querent rather than necessarily a literal woman. Upright themes include stewardship, nurture, bodily wisdom and practical self-possession. Reversal may internalize, block, exaggerate or redirect those qualities: self-neglect, over-mothering, resource anxiety or the need to care for oneself.

**Status:** modern therapeutic and practice-oriented interpretation, not a historical card meaning.

## Esoteric and symbolic readings

### Alchemical

**Sola Busca:** the deck as a whole has been interpreted by Brera scholars through Hermetic-alchemical culture, Alexander legends and immortality. Elena belongs to that classical program, but no surviving fifteenth-century commentary explains her as “Water of Earth.”

**Golden Dawn onward:** Water plus Earth produces fertile soil. The barren ground, goat and cube supply prima-materia, fixed-form and generative symbolism.

**Crowley:** river fertilizing desert makes the formula explicit: matter becomes capable of creation. This is the clearest primary textual appearance of the modern alchemical-fertility reading attached specifically to the card.

### Kabbalistic

Papus’s 1889 tetragrammaton structure is an early explicit formulation:

- Queen = Heh, receptive/formative principle;
- Pentacles = final Heh, material realization.

Golden Dawn practice connects Queens with Binah, the archetypal Mother, and Pentacles with Assiah, the world of action/material manifestation.

**Caution:** “Binah in Assiah” is useful Hermetic shorthand. It is not medieval Jewish Kabbalah and is not printed on the fifteenth-century card.

### Astrological

The Golden Dawn is the traceable source for the individual court’s forty-degree span. The last decan of Sagittarius brings Saturnian Ten-of-Wands weight; early Capricorn contributes Jupiterian change and Martian material work.

Crowley’s goat, horned helmet and mountain/desert environment emphasize Capricorn. Modern books frequently shorten the attribution to “Capricorn,” losing the Queen’s ten Sagittarius degrees.

### Hermetic

The cube represents stable, measured form; the gold orb or disk represents completed material potential; the goat mediates fecundity, appetite, ambition and Capricorn. The light/dark face in *Book T* portrays dual potential and changeability.

These symbols first cohere around this card in Golden Dawn documentation. Claims that they were secretly encoded in the Marseille coin are retrospective.

### Christian

The medieval queen belongs to Christian European social iconography: crown, throne, modest court dress and lawful hierarchy. The Waite throne’s cherubic figures can support a Christian mystical reading, and Waite himself wrote from a strongly Christian-esoteric standpoint.

**Finding:** no medieval sermon or cardmaker’s document identifies the Queen of Coins with Mary, the Church, Eve, Mary Magdalene or a biblical queen. Such identifications are later devotional readings.

### Jungian

Twentieth-century psychological tarot recasts her as an archetype of:

- the embodied or nurturing feminine;
- the containing function;
- the “good mother” and its possessive shadow;
- relationship to body, land, work and resources;
- integration of feeling with practical life.

Pollack, Greer and numerous later writers spread this approach. Directly attributing it to Jung would be misleading: Jung discussed alchemy, archetypes and divination-like synchronicity, but did not publish a canonical interpretation of the Queen of Pentacles.

### Feminist and gender-inclusive readings

Late-twentieth- and twenty-first-century writers increasingly treat “Queen” as a mode of authority available to any gender. Some critique the reduction of the card to housewife or mother and emphasize financial competence, ecological stewardship, labour and self-maintenance.

This is a modern interpretive development, although it can plausibly draw inspiration from the visibly authoritative historical queen.

**Italo Calvino, *The Castle of Crossed Destinies*:** tarot cards structure stories generated from visual combinations. Calvino used historical tarot imagery, including Visconti and Marseille materials. **T. S. Eliot, *The Waste Land*:** Madame Sosostris names invented and altered tarot figures, including “the drowned Phoenician Sailor” and “the one-eyed merchant.” The Queen of Pentacles is not named. Connecting her to the poem’s merchant or Belladonna is speculative.

**Alejandro Jodorowsky:** tarot profoundly informed his teaching and artistic practice, and he co-created the Camoin–Jodorowsky deck. Claims that a particular film scene is specifically the Queen of Deniers require shot-level evidence; none was located.

**Paintings and buildings:** no securely documented premodern painting or building was found that intentionally quotes this particular card. Similar enthroned women holding round objects belong to a much broader iconographic vocabulary and cannot establish borrowing without documentary or compositional evidence.

## Controversies and disputes

### Egyptian origin

**[Legend first published in identifiable form]** Court de Gébelin’s 1781 *Monde primitif* called tarot an Egyptian Book of Thoth preserved through a game. His argument preceded Champollion’s decipherment of hieroglyphs and supplied no chain of Egyptian objects or texts.

**[Historical correction]** Surviving tarot objects and account records locate tarot in fifteenth-century northern Italy. Michael Dummett emphasized that the evidence shows gaming use before Court de Gébelin’s occult intervention. [Dummett, “Origins of Tarot”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1981/05/14/origins-of-tarot/). Decker, Depaulis and Dummett trace the subsequent occult genealogy in [*A Wicked Pack of Cards*](https://books.google.com/books?id=nVyDAAAAMAAJ).

**Card-specific finding:** the Queen’s Mamluk ancestry is structural—coins and court hierarchy—but this does not make her a pharaonic Earth goddess.

### Romani or “Gypsy” transmission

**[Legend]** Nineteenth-century occult writers, especially Papus’s influential title *Tarot des Bohémiens*, spread the idea that Romani custodians carried Egyptian tarot into Europe.

**Evidence:** no medieval Romani transmission document bridges an Egyptian sacred book to Italian trionfi. Romani origins lie in South Asia, while the identification with Egypt arose from early-modern European naming and self-presentation.

**Card-specific finding:** neither the Queen nor the coin suit contains evidence of a Romani origin. Romani communities later used cards, like many European communities; later practice is not proof of invention.

### Cathar and Templar origins

**[Modern legend/disputed speculation]** Claims that tarot encodes Cathar dualism, Templar secrets, a Grail cult or suppressed heresy generally rely on retrospective readings of trumps.

**Card-specific problem:** the Queen of Coins is an ordinary suit court whose basic form derives from card-pack structure. No Cathar or Templar text names or depicts her.

**Finding:** no primary medieval evidence connecting this card to Cathars or Templars was traced.

### Pentacles from the beginning

**[Modern back-projection]** Many contemporary accounts call all historical deniers “pentacles.”

**Correction:** Visconti and Marseille cards show coins or floral deniers. The five-pointed star becomes a systematic suit emblem in the English occult stream, especially Golden Dawn and Waite–Smith. “Pentacle” is a meaningful later title but anachronistic when used to describe what is visibly on a fifteenth-century card.

### Identifying the Visconti Queen as Bianca Maria

**[Disputed]** The family setting makes Bianca Maria a plausible association.

**Counter-evidence:** no caption, individualized heraldry or portrait comparison proves it. Moakley/Kaplan-style identifications should be presented as proposals.

### Sola Busca as the direct source of Smith’s Queen

**[Disputed]** Smith probably had an opportunity to see Sola Busca materials in London, and several RWS pip scenes closely resemble them.

**Finding:** the Queen of Pentacles does not exhibit the same degree of specific compositional dependence. The safer lineage is Marseille/standard court plus Golden Dawn Earth imagery, elaborated by Smith.

### “Thirteen” as the card’s ancient number

**[Modern invention]** Some modern teaching systems count Page 11, Knight 12, Queen 13 and King 14.

**Correction:** historic court cards were ranks rather than numbered pips. Mathers’s 65 and *Book T*’s 18 are editorial positions in whole-deck lists. None is an intrinsic ancient number.

### Reversal versus ill dignity

**[Common conflation]** Modern readers often print *Book T*’s “ill dignified” traits as reversed meanings.

**Correction:** Golden Dawn elemental dignity evaluates neighbouring cards, not necessarily physical inversion. Waite separately supplies explicit reversed meanings.

### Misprints and transmission errors

- **20°/21st degree:** usually a boundary-description difference, not evidence of a different decan system.
- **King/Knight terminology:** Golden Dawn’s mounted King was renamed Knight by Crowley, while the Golden Dawn Prince corresponds structurally to the stationary King in many ordinary decks. Tables can therefore appear contradictory while describing equivalent elemental offices.
- **“Queen of Pentacles” in Thoth:** Crowley’s actual title is **Queen of Disks**. Calling her Queen of Pentacles obscures his deliberate suit renaming.
- **“Thoth published 1944”:** *The Book of Thoth* appeared in 1944, but the complete commercial card deck was not issued until 1969.
- **Conver 1760 versus 1761:** sources differ according to manufacturing, imprint or conventional dating. Both appear in cataloguing; “1760/61” is safest without inspecting the particular imprint.
- **Etteilla 1788 versus 1789:** the earliest production and formal issue chronology is complex; “approximately 1788–89” avoids false precision.

## Dedicated contested-material findings

Explicit searches combining the card with “origin,” “myth,” “dispute,” “misattribution,” “Egyptian,” “Gypsy,” “Cathar” and “Templar” produced extensive general tarot claims but very little card-specific evidence.

The result itself is important:

1. Egyptian and Romani stories concern the supposed origin of the whole pack, not a separately transmitted Queen of Pentacles.
2. Cathar and Templar claims overwhelmingly interpret the trumps; their application to this Queen is secondary analogy.
3. The strongest genuine eastern connection is the Mamluk structure of suits and courts.
4. The Queen rank is a European transformation of that structure, not a Mamluk female personage.
5. The Earth, Water, goat, Capricorn and final-Heh complex first becomes demonstrable in nineteenth-century occult systems.
6. The modern “nurturing working mother” reading develops chiefly from Smith’s fertile garden combined with twentieth-century psychological and social interpretation.
7. No evidence was found for a medieval secret pentagram, astrological Capricorn mark, Tree-of-Life path or Christian saint encoded in the historical Queen of Coins.

## Open questions

1. **Which surviving Queen is absolutely earliest?** Cary–Yale’s current approximately 1440–45 dating makes it the leading candidate, but dating of the early Milanese groups remains under technical review.

2. **Who painted the Visconti–Sforza originals?** Bonifacio Bembo remains the traditional attribution; Francesco Zavattari, Benedetto Bembo and other workshop distinctions have been proposed for portions of the group.

3. **Is the Morgan Queen a portrait?** No decisive evidence currently identifies her with Bianca Maria or another individual.

4. **What precisely motivated the Cary–Yale six-rank courts?** Marriage commemoration, gender symmetry and dynastic representation remain hypotheses.

5. **Who devised the Sola Busca program?** Nicola di Maestro Antonio is a serious proposed engraver, but authorship of the intellectual program and the exact meanings of Elena and her companions remain unsettled.

6. **Which Sola Busca cards did Pamela Colman Smith examine?** Institutional access and broad influence are plausible; card-by-card copying must be demonstrated individually.

7. **What was the exact first Golden Dawn wording/date?** *Book T* survives through manuscripts and later redactions. The doctrine belongs to the Order’s late-nineteenth-century work, but individual layers associated with Mathers, Westcott and Felkin are textually complicated.

8. **Did Waite prescribe the rabbit?** No separate instruction has been located. It may have been Smith’s invention or part of broader consultation now undocumented.

9. **What is Etteilla’s earliest exact wording for card 65?** Later manuals transmit consistent themes, but a fully collated study of the earliest deck states and Alliette’s several publications is required to distinguish his words from those of followers.

10. **Case’s exact upright and reversed language:** freely accessible secondary tables frequently merge BOTA, Waite and Golden Dawn material. A paginated first-edition Case source should be consulted before quoting him verbatim.

# Page of Wands: Research Dossier

## Method and evidence labels

This dossier distinguishes six kinds of claim:

- **[Documented fact]** directly supported by a surviving object, archival record, rulebook, or printed primary text.
- **[Scholarly reconstruction]** an inference accepted or seriously proposed by historians but not stated in a contemporary document.
- **[Tradition]** an interpretation transmitted through cartomancy or deck practice.
- **[Esoteric attribution]** a consciously imposed occult correspondence.
- **[Disputed claim]** a claim for which scholars or sources disagree.
- **[Legend/modern invention]** a claim lacking historical evidence or arising demonstrably in modern occultism.

“Page of Wands” is a suit court, not a trump. Consequently, trump numbering, the three historical trump orders, Justice/Strength exchanges, the Fool’s position, the Besançon substitutions of Juno and Jupiter, and Crowley’s Tzaddi/Heh revision do not directly alter this card. They are treated below only where necessary to define the system surrounding it.

### Classification and number

**[Documented fact]** It is the lowest of four courts in the suit of batons/staves/wands:

> Fante/Valet/Page → Cavalier/Knight → Queen → King.

It is normally **unnumbered**. Calling it “XI” or “11” means only that it is the eleventh card when a suit is arranged Ace–10 followed by Page, Knight, Queen, King; this is a modern cataloguing convention, not an historical inscription.

**[Documented exception]** Etteilla numbered every card continuously. His *Valet de Bâton* is **25**, bearing the upright legend **BON ÉTRANGER** and reversed legend **NOUVELLE**. An early printed manual reproduces the card with these inscriptions ([*Le Grand Etteilla*, card 25](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2f/Le_grand_Etteilla%2C_ou%2C_l%27art_de_tirer_les_cartes_..._Le_tout_recueilli_..._%28IA_b29321220%29.pdf)).

### Earliest surviving example

**[Documented fact]** The safest presently identifiable candidate is the **Ortiz‑Patiño Page of Staves**, northern Italy, probably Milan, mid-fifteenth century: a fashionably dressed youth holding a gilt-headed staff, standing before grass and a burnished-gold sky. Its auction catalogue identifies it explicitly as *Fante di bastoni* and associates it with the Visconti-Sforza group ([Sotheby’s catalogue](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2013/western-manuscripts-miniatures-l13241/lot.36.html)).

**[Qualification]** The major surviving Visconti-Sforza composite pack has a Page of Cups but its surviving Morgan portion does not contain a Page of Staves; the Morgan’s surviving staff court is the King. The full dispersed group nevertheless originally followed the four-court structure. It is therefore unsafe to describe a modern restored Visconti-Sforza Page of Wands as an unquestionably preserved Morgan card ([Morgan catalogue](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525); [Morgan card list](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/tarot-cards)).

### Golden Dawn attribution

**[Esoteric attribution, c. 1888–1900]**

- Rank: Princess/Knave, corresponding to **Earth**, the final **Heh** of Yod-Heh-Vav-Heh.
- Suit: Wands = **Fire**, conventionally the first letter **Yod**.
- Combination: **Earth of Fire**—matter or fuel in which fire becomes manifest.
- Magical title: **Princess of the Shining Flame; Rose of the Palace of Fire**.
- Cosmography: one terrestrial quadrant around the North Pole; in the standard Golden Dawn allocation, the Princess of Wands occupies the quadrant associated longitudinally with **20° Cancer to 20° Leo**, extending from the ecliptic toward 45° north and including portions of Ursa Minor and Boötes.
- Unlike the King/Knight, Queen, and Prince courts, Princesses do **not** simply rule three consecutive zodiacal decans. Their domain is a quadrant assembled from portions of the heavens. The common claim that the Page “is the three decans from 20° Cancer to 20° Leo” improperly transfers the Prince’s decan method to the Princess. The Golden Dawn description itself says she rules a quadrant, not three ordinary decans ([Regardie, *The Complete Golden Dawn*](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/R/Regardie%20-%20The%20Complete%20Golden%20Dawn.pdf); [Book T transcription](https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/mathers-and-felkin-golden-dawn-book-t-the-tarot-1888.pdf)).

## Iconography, deck by deck

### Mamluk antecedents, thirteenth–fifteenth centuries

**[Documented fact]** Surviving Islamic cards use Cups, Coins, Swords, and Polo-sticks. Each suit contained ten numeral cards and three ranked honours: *malik* (king), *nā’ib malik* (deputy king), and *thānī nā’ib* (second deputy). They represented offices through inscriptions and suit signs rather than human portraits. A LACMA fragment and the Topkapı pack document this structure ([LACMA](https://collections.lacma.org/object/114976)).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Italian *bastoni* and Spanish *bastos* descend iconographically from the polo-stick/club suit. European painters anthropomorphized the Islamic deputy offices and developed varying page/knight/queen/king courts.

**[Limit]** No surviving Mamluk card can properly be called a Page of Wands. The relationship is genealogical at the level of suit and court structure, not identity of image or divinatory meaning.

### Marziano–Michelino deck, c. 1418–1425

**[Documented fact]** Marziano da Tortona described four bird suits with numeral cards, four kings, and sixteen deified heroes. There was no Fante di bastoni. The deck is relevant as an early experiment in permanent triumphs, not as an attestation of this card ([description and history](https://tarot-heritage.com/2019/08/26/the-deck-and-book-of-the-deification-of-sixteen-heroes/)).

### Cary-Yale / Visconti di Modrone, Milan, probably 1440s

**[Documented fact]** This exceptional pack used expanded courts: male and female attendants and mounted figures in addition to king and queen. It therefore does not map cleanly onto the later single male Fante.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** Its expanded gender-balanced court may record a short-lived Milanese experiment rather than an older universal tarot form. The frequent claim that it proves all tarot originally had six courts is unsupported.

### Ortiz-Patiño / Visconti-Sforza group, mid-fifteenth-century Milan

**[Documented fact]** The youth stands in profile or three-quarter profile, looks right, and holds a slim upright staff capped in gold. He wears fashionable court clothing against a grassy foreground and tooled gold sky. Unlike Marseille and RWS, there is no desert, heraldic salamander costume, or proclamation gesture ([Sotheby’s](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2013/western-manuscripts-miniatures-l13241/lot.36.html)).

**[Disputed identification]** Gertrude Moakley and Stuart Kaplan explored whether Visconti-Sforza figures portrayed members of the commissioning dynasty. Heraldic devices securely associate the pack with the Visconti and Sforza families, but no inscription identifies the Page as a particular family member. The Morgan cautiously says “some member” of the family commissioned the cards; it does not endorse a named portrait ([Morgan catalogue](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525)).

**Finding:** No primary evidence identifies this Page as a specific Visconti or Sforza youth. Named identifications in popular guides are conjectural.

### Brambilla deck, Milan, c. 1440s–1450s

**[Documented fact]** The Brambilla fragment retains only a small selection of courts and pips; the Page of Batons is not securely among the cards normally catalogued for it. It should not be illustrated with a reconstructed Page without disclosure.

### Ferrarese and Este cards, fifteenth century

**[Documented fact]** Ferrarese records establish production of *carte da trionfi* by 1442. Surviving Este-associated cards demonstrate luxurious courtly packs, but no securely attributed Page of Batons provides an unbroken image-lineage to Marseille.

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The Ferrarese tradition helped stabilize the four Italian suits and four-court structure. Claims that a particular unnamed Este youth is the ancestor of the RWS Page go beyond the evidence.

### Rosenwald and Budapest sheets, late fifteenth/early sixteenth century

**[Documented fact]** These uncut or fragmentary woodcut sheets are crucial evidence for inexpensive printed tarot outside aristocratic illuminated packs. Their coarse court figures show how courtly types were simplified for serial production.

**Finding:** Available digitized descriptions do not permit a secure card-by-card attribution of a Page of Batons without reconstructing sheet positions. The sheets prove the circulation of standardized courts, but they cannot safely be treated as a named occult Page.

### Sola Busca, engraved c. 1491, painted example dated 1491

**[Documented fact]** The Sola Busca is the earliest surviving substantially complete 78-card tarot and illustrates all pip cards with dramatic figures and objects. Its court card corresponding to the Page of Wands belongs to the Batons court, but the deck’s courts and trumps use invented or classical-sounding personal names rather than Marseille titles.

**[Historical importance]** Its fully scenic numbered minors provided visual precedents for several Waite-Smith pips. That demonstrable borrowing does not establish that Pamela Colman Smith copied the Sola Busca Page specifically. The usual documented comparisons concern cards such as Three of Swords and Ten of Wands.

### “Mantegna Tarocchi,” c. 1460s

**[Documented fact]** These fifty educational engravings are not a tarot pack and have neither suits nor a Page of Wands. Their inclusion in tarot histories comes from a later misnomer.

### Minchiate, Florence, sixteenth–nineteenth centuries

**[Documented fact]** Minchiate expanded the trump series but retained Latin suits. In Cups and Coins the Fanti were commonly female *Fantine*; Batons and Swords retained male Fanti. The Fante di Bastoni therefore remains a standing male attendant with a club.

**Meaning of change:** Gender differentiated suits visually; it was a game-pattern convention, not evidence that the Wands Page represented “divine feminine fire.”

### Bolognese tarocchino

**[Documented fact]** The shortened Bolognese game retained four courts in every suit. Its Fante di Bastoni is the lowest court, typically a standing youth with a club. Early-eighteenth-century or earlier Bolognese cartomancy is important because it precedes Etteilla, but surviving meanings are attached to the local pack and should not automatically be retrojected onto Milanese images.

### Sicilian tarot

**[Documented fact]** The Sicilian pattern often replaces the lowest court with a female Maid. Modern rules call this card a Maid and rank it beneath Knight, Queen, and King ([Sicilian rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/sicilian.html)).

### Jean Noblet, Paris, c. 1650

**[Documented fact]** The Valet stands with a large naturalistic club or staff. As in the later Marseille lineage, the object is materially heavier than an occult wand. The figure is an attendant/courtier, not a ritual magician.

**[Qualification]** Noblet is an early ancestor or Type-I relative of the Marseille family, but calling it “the first Marseille deck” compresses ongoing scholarly classification debates.

### Jean Dodal, Lyon, c. 1701–1715

**[Documented fact]** The standing youth rests his hands on or around the top of a thick baton. His garment carries a long hanging panel. The ground is relatively bare. On surviving examples the card may be untitled, although adjacent court conventions identify it unmistakably.

### Nicolas Conver, Marseille, 1760

**[Documented fact]** A profile-facing youth in red, green, yellow, and pale clothing holds an upright green, knotted baton with both hands. Small vegetation appears near the feet. The title is placed vertically or at the side because the upright staff and figure occupy the central field ([Conver image-description](https://marselia.app/cards/valet-de-baton); [Dodal–Conver comparison](https://www.tarotforum.net/threads/valet-of-batons-contrasting-the-dodal-and-conver.52597/)).

**Meaning of continuity:** This remains an occupational or social court image. “Apprentice of fire,” “creative student,” and “inner child” are later readings, not visibly encoded historical meanings.

### Besançon pattern, eighteenth century

**[Documented fact]** Besançon makers replaced the Popess and Pope with Juno and Jupiter, chiefly in Protestant or confessionally sensitive markets. This did not rename the Valet de Bâtons. It remains a suit court. Applying “Juno/Jupiter symbolism” to this Page is therefore irrelevant.

### Etteilla, *Livre de Thot*, 1788 subscription/1789 issue

**[Documented fact]** Etteilla retained a conventional standing Valet de Bâton but incorporated it into a continuously numbered, purpose-built divinatory pack. Card 25 printed its meanings on the face:

- upright: **Bon étranger**—“good stranger”
- reversed: **Nouvelle**—“news”

This was a decisive transformation: the image ceased to be merely a reusable game court and became a labelled oracle ([historic deck scans](https://benebellwen.com/etteilla/downloads/); [Etteilla archive](https://foolmade.art/archive/etteilla-thot)).

**[Correction]** Etteilla did not give this court a securely documented modern “astrological sign.” His planetary/cosmogonic system is clearest for his reordered triumphs. Modern tables that assign a zodiacal decan to card 25 frequently import Golden Dawn material into Etteilla.

### Oswald Wirth, 1889

**[Documented fact]** Wirth’s original 1889 tarot comprised twenty-two trumps, not a complete 78-card pack. There is consequently no original 1889 Wirth Page of Wands.

**[Modern invention]** Later “Wirth” decks with minors supply courts editorially. They must not be cited as Wirth’s 1889 iconography.

### Golden Dawn manuscript designs, c. 1888–1900

**[Esoteric attribution]** *Book T* reimagines the knave as an enthroned or standing armed Princess. She is crowned or crested with winged/flaming emblems, wears armour, and bears a long wand. At her feet or around her is vigorous flame.

The Golden Dawn explains all Princesses collectively as the **“Thrones of the Aces.”** The phrase means that the Ace’s undifferentiated elemental root becomes seated, fixed, or materially realized through the Princess/Malkuth. It is not a claim that a throne was hidden in historical Marseille artwork.

Robert Wang’s *Golden Dawn Tarot* and Sandra Tabatha and Chic Cicero’s reconstruction differ in costume, pose, colour realization, and manuscript interpretation. They are twentieth-century visual reconstructions of written lodge instructions, not facsimiles of one canonical Golden Dawn deck.

### Waite-Smith, designed 1909; *Pictorial Key*, 1910/1911

**[Documented fact]** Pamela Colman Smith depicts a youthful figure standing in a sandy landscape, holding a single living staff vertically and looking up toward its shoots. He wears a yellow tunic patterned with black salamander-like forms, a red mantle, yellow boots, and a hat with a red feather. Three pyramidal hills or dunes rise behind him.

Waite’s exact description is brief:

> “A young man stands in the act of proclamation. He is unknown but faithful, and his tidings are strange.”

The divinatory text calls him a lover, envoy, or postman and a bearer of family intelligence ([Waite PDF, p. 48 in the scan](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Waite/The%20Pictorial%20Key%20to%20the%20Tarot.pdf)).

**[Scholarly reconstruction]** The desert, salamander pattern, and sprouting staff externalize Golden Dawn Fire symbolism while preserving the Marseille standing Valet. The proclamation/message theme reflects older cartomantic “news” meanings, especially Etteilla.

**[Disputed claim]** The distant forms are routinely called Egyptian pyramids. They resemble pyramids, but Waite’s text does not identify them as such. Their interpretation as explicit proof of Egypt is therefore traditional rather than documented.

**[Disputed claim]** There is no established specific Sola Busca prototype for this court card. Smith had access to British Museum photographs of Sola Busca cards, but broad claims that every RWS minor copied Sola Busca are false.

### Paul Foster Case / BOTA, twentieth century

**[Documented fact]** Case’s published *Tarot: A Key to the Wisdom of the Ages* concentrates on the twenty-two Major Keys; it does not supply a full independent Page of Wands iconographic programme ([1947 edition](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1947__case___the_tarot.pdf)).

**[Tradition]** Later BOTA instructional material retains the Golden Dawn logic: Wands/Fire, the lowest court as Earth of Fire, and court-card personality correspondences. Claims that Case originated the Page’s messenger meaning are false; it appears in Etteilla and Mathers before him.

### Thoth, painted 1938–1943; book 1944; deck published 1969

**[Documented fact]** Frieda Harris paints a nude, leaping female figure within a great Yod-shaped surge of flame. Flame-like “plumes of justice” stream from her brow. She carries a wand crowned by a solar disk. Behind or below her is a golden altar ornamented with ram heads.

Crowley writes:

> “The Princess of Wands represents the earthy part of Fire; one might say, she is the fuel of Fire.”

He calls the image the dance of a virgin priestess attending the Lords of Fire and says she governs one northern polar quadrant ([Crowley, *Book of Thoth*, Court Cards](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/court-cards)).

**Meaning of change:** Harris replaces the socially subordinate male servant with an autonomous elemental woman. The planted wooden club becomes solar magical instrument; grounded service becomes combustible potential and ecstatic motion.

### Grimaud / Paul Marteau, 1930 onward

**[Documented fact]** Marteau standardized a recoloured Conver-derived pattern for twentieth-century mass circulation. The Valet remains a profile youth holding the green baton. Marteau’s interpretive prose turns the baton into available material energy and reads the figure’s poised stance as impending action.

### Camoin–Jodorowsky, 1997

**[Documented fact]** This deck redraws and recolours Marseille motifs while presenting itself as a “restoration.” The Valet remains recognizably Conver-derived.

**[Controversy]** “Restored original Tarot of Marseille” is a philosophical and commercial claim, not proof that the chosen colours and lines reproduce a single lost archetype. Historical Marseille was a family of changing woodblocks, not one recoverable master drawing.

### Notable modern redesigns

**[Modern invention/tradition]**

- Feminist and queer decks frequently make the Page gender-neutral or female.
- The *Motherpeace* tradition converts linear courts into daughter/son/shamanic roles.
- *Wild Unknown* replaces human courts with family animals.
- Many contemporary RWS derivatives interpret the Page as an entrepreneur, student, creative novice, text message, or “inner child.”

These are legitimate artistic interpretations but not recovered medieval meanings.

## The game

### Rank

**[Documented fact]** In ordinary tarot play the Page/Valet/Fante is the lowest court:

> King > Queen > Knight/Cavalier > Page/Valet/Fante > 10 …

It follows suit and may take lower suit cards. It has no intrinsic trumping power.

In Italian and most French tarot games, Cups and Coins sometimes reverse pip ranking while courts retain their hierarchy. Wands/Batons normally rank 10 downward below the courts.

### Value

Values depend on game and counting method:

| Game | Rank/value |
|---|---|
| Modern French Tarot | Lowest court; 1½ points when counted with half-point cards |
| Bolognese tarocchino | Lowest court; commonly counted as 2 with an empty card; four Fanti form a scoring combination |
| Minchiate | Lowest court; normally no individual card points, though courts can matter in combinations |
| Sicilian Tarocchi | Lowest suit court or Maid; nominal 2 in the three-card counting scheme |
| Many Austrian/German Tarock games | Usually Jack/Bube, lowest court; precise value varies by game, commonly 2 card-points under three-card counting |

Modern French rules give Valets 1.5 points and place them beneath Cavaliers ([French Tarot rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/frtarot.html)). Minchiate counts only Kings individually among suit cards, although court combinations can score ([Minchiate rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/minchiate.html)). Tarot games use several incompatible counting conventions, so “the Page is worth two points” is not universally true ([comparative counting](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/counting.html)).

### Trump-order disputes: relevance

**[Documented fact]** Historians reconstruct three principal early Italian trump-order families, conventionally labelled A, B, and C. Justice, Fortitude, and other triumphs changed relative place; the Fool could function as an excuse, lowest special card, or in some later games a high trump.

**Finding:** None of those disputes changes the rank of the Page of Wands because it is not a triumph. The Page’s position is determined inside its suit.

## Chronology of attestation

- **Thirteenth–fifteenth centuries — [Documented fact]:** Islamic/Mamluk packs attest the polo-stick suit and deputy courts, but no human Page.
- **1377 — [Documented fact]:** John of Rheinfelden describes European packs with four suits and court hierarchies. This is evidence for pre-tarot court cards, not this named card.
- **c. 1418–1425 — [Documented fact]:** Marziano describes the lost Michelino deck. It has bird suits, kings, and sixteen gods, but no Fante di bastoni.
- **1440 — [Documented fact]:** Florence records *naibi a trionfi*.
- **1442 — [Documented fact]:** Ferrara’s Este accounts record painted *carte da trionfi*. They establish the game, not an individually named Page ([Ferrara discussion](https://l-pollett.tripod.com/cards85i.htm)).
- **c. 1440s–1450s — [Documented fact]:** Milanese luxury packs display four or expanded courts; the Ortiz-Patiño Page supplies the earliest secure surviving image.
- **c. 1470–1500 — [Documented fact]:** the anonymous *Sermones de ludo cum aliis* condemns cards and lists the triumph sequence. It names trumps, not individual suit courts; therefore it is not an attestation of the Page’s title.
- **Late fifteenth century — [Documented fact]:** Rosenwald/Budapest sheets and Sola Busca establish printed tarot and scenic minors.
- **c. 1487–1523 — [Documented fact]:** Matteo Maria Boiardo composed a 78-card poetic pack; the work was printed posthumously. It is a literary redesign, not the source of the standard Page.
- **1527 — [Documented fact]:** Teofilo Folengo’s *Caos del Triperuno* uses tarot trumps in poetic fortune-making. Again, it does not supply an individual history of the Page ([Folengo discussion and translation](https://marykgreer.com/2008/05/28/tarot-poetry-the-sonnets-of-theofilo-folengo/)).
- **Sixteenth century — [Documented fact]:** *tarocchi* supplants *trionfi* as the pack/game name; regional patterns stabilize.
- **c. 1650 — [Documented fact]:** Noblet preserves the standing Valet de Bâton type.
- **1701–1715 — [Documented fact]:** Dodal.
- **1760 — [Documented fact]:** Conver.
- **c. 1750 — [Documented fact/scholarly dating]:** Bolognese cartomancy manuscript gives early tarot divinatory meanings, before published occult tarot.
- **1781 — [Documented fact]:** Court de Gébelin publishes the Egyptian-origin theory; Comte de Mellet links twenty-two trumps with Hebrew letters. Neither gives a Hebrew letter to the Page.
- **1783–1789 — [Documented fact]:** Etteilla publishes tarot-cartomancy manuals and the first purpose-built divinatory tarot; Valet de Bâton becomes no. 25.
- **1854–1856 — [Documented fact]:** Éliphas Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie* synthesizes tarot trumps, Hebrew alphabet, Kabbalah, and ceremonial magic.
- **1863/1870 — [Documented fact]:** Paul Christian elaborates pseudo-Egyptian initiation narratives.
- **1888 — [Documented fact]:** Mathers publishes an English tarot manual with meanings for the Knave of Sceptres.
- **1888 onward — [Documented fact]:** Golden Dawn papers transform the Knave into an elemental Princess.
- **1889 — [Documented fact]:** Papus’s *Tarot des Bohémiens* systematizes Tetragrammaton and suit/court theory; Wirth publishes twenty-two redesigned trumps.
- **1909 — [Documented fact]:** Waite-Smith deck issued.
- **1910/1911 — [Documented fact]:** Waite publishes the Page’s picture-description and meanings.
- **1930 — [Documented fact]:** Marteau/Grimaud popularizes a standardized Marseille.
- **1938–1943 — [Documented fact]:** Harris paints Crowley’s Princess.
- **1944 — [Documented fact]:** Crowley’s *Book of Thoth* describes her.
- **1969 — [Documented fact]:** first full commercial Thoth publication.
- **1980 — [Documented fact]:** Rachel Pollack’s completed *Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom* helps popularize psychological/Jungian court interpretation.
- **2002 — [Documented fact]:** Mary K. Greer’s *Complete Book of Tarot Reversals* systematizes reversed readings as blocked, excessive, internalized, projected, delayed, or contrary energies.

### Tetragrammaton conflict

**[Esoteric attribution]** In Golden Dawn/Crowley court theory:

- Knight/King = Yod, Fire
- Queen = first Heh, Water
- Prince = Vav, Air
- Princess = final Heh, Earth

Separately, the suit of Wands is Fire/Yod. Thus the Princess of Wands is **final Heh operating within Yod**, or Earth of Fire.

**Correction:** Calling the Page himself “Yod of the Tetragrammaton” confuses suit with rank. The **suit** is Yod; the **Princess rank** is final Heh.

### Tree of Life

**[Esoteric attribution]** The Princess corresponds most naturally to **Malkuth**, the tenth Sephirah, as crystallized manifestation. She does not have a unique path between Sephiroth comparable to a Major Arcana trump. Tables assigning her a numbered Hebrew-letter path are later harmonizations.

### Tzaddi/Heh swap

**[Not applicable]** Crowley’s “Tzaddi is not the Star” revision exchanges or reinterprets the Hebrew-letter correspondences of the Emperor and Star. It does not alter the Princess of Wands’ final-Heh rank attribution. The two uses of “Heh”—a Hebrew letter assigned to a trump and the fourth letter-position of the Divine Name—must not be conflated.

## Divinatory meanings by author

Long quotations are avoided where modern editions remain copyrighted; historically important wording is quoted briefly.

### Etteilla, 1789

**[Documented tradition]**

- Upright: **“Bon étranger”**—a good or well-disposed stranger.
- Reversed: **“Nouvelle”**—news.

Etteilla’s scheme may look inverted to RWS readers: news is explicitly the reversed inscription. Later authors spread “messenger/news” into the upright Page as well.

### S. L. MacGregor Mathers, 1888

**[Documented tradition]** Mathers’s *Knave of Sceptres* signifies a fair or dark young man depending on complexion tables, a lover, envoy, postman, or bearer of intelligence; reversed, anecdotes, announcements, bad news, indecision, and instability. Waite later reproduces much of this vocabulary ([Mathers, 1888](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/mathers/mtar00.htm)).

### A. E. Waite, 1910/1911

**[Documented tradition]**

Upright:

> “Dark young man, faithful, a lover, an envoy, a postman.”

He may testify favourably, represent a dangerous rival if followed by the Page of Cups, possess his suit’s chief qualities, or signify family intelligence.

Reversed:

> “Anecdotes, announcements, evil news.”

Waite adds indecision and attendant instability ([*Pictorial Key*, p. 48](https://www.mr-kaplan.com/Library/Waite/The%20Pictorial%20Key%20to%20the%20Tarot.pdf)).

### Aleister Crowley, 1944

**[Documented esoteric attribution]** Crowley does not organize the entry as a simple upright/reversed dictionary. Dignified expression is brilliant, daring, energetic, individual, enthusiastic, and courageous. Ill-dignified expression becomes theatrical, superficial, unreliable, cruel, or oppressive. The Princess may be “fuel” that either sustains illumination or feeds a destructive blaze ([*Book of Thoth*](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-of-thoth/court-cards)).

### Paul Foster Case

**Finding:** No reliable Page-specific upright/reversed paragraph was located in *The Tarot: A Key to the Wisdom of the Ages*, because that volume treats the Major Keys. Page meanings attributed online to that book are frequently generic Golden Dawn material. Later BOTA lessons may contain such instruction, but those proprietary lessons were not available for verification.

### Rachel Pollack, 1980

**[Modern interpretive tradition]** Pollack treats the Page as the beginning or announcement of experience in its suit: enthusiasm, adventure, and creative energy that has not yet matured into sustained mastery. Reversal tends toward indecision, impatience, spoiled enthusiasm, or inability to translate excitement into action.

**Qualification:** Page references vary by edition of *Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom*. This is a close summary, not a verbatim quotation. A searchable modern edition is available here ([Pollack PDF](https://pathwaysstl.com/wp-content/uploads/78-degrees-of-wisdom.pdf)).

### Mary K. Greer, 2002

**[Modern interpretive tradition]** Greer refuses to make reversal a single fixed opposite. Her framework permits the fiery Page to be blocked, delayed, excessive, projected onto another person, internalized, or misdirected. Applied here, that yields scattered enthusiasm, delayed news, fear of beginning, boastfulness, creative frustration, or reconsideration.

**Qualification:** This summarizes Greer’s method rather than reproducing a copyrighted card entry. Her central reversal taxonomy is documented in *The Complete Book of Tarot Reversals* (Llewellyn, 2002).

## Esoteric and symbolic readings

### Alchemical

**[Esoteric attribution; clearest Page-specific form in Crowley, 1944]** Earth of Fire is combustible substance: wood, coal, oil, or the chemical body capable of ignition. Crowley explicitly speaks of “fuel” and chemical attraction. The reading is alchemical in vocabulary but is not attested on medieval cards.

### Kabbalistic

**[Esoteric attribution; nineteenth century, systematized by Golden Dawn]** The Princess is final Heh and Malkuth—the Daughter in whom the elemental force becomes materially present. As “Throne of the Ace,” she receives the pure root-force from Kether and realizes it at the bottom of the Tree.

**Spreaders:** Golden Dawn manuscripts, Mathers, Regardie, Crowley, Case, Wang, and the Ciceros.

### Astrological

**[Esoteric attribution]** Wands correspond to Fire signs generally, but the Princess’s formal Golden Dawn geography is polar/quadrantal. The common modern assignment “20° Cancer–20° Leo” must be described as the longitude of her quadrant rather than her personal birth-date range.

### Hermetic

**[Esoteric attribution]** The card joins fourfold elemental doctrine, Tetragrammaton, the Tree of Life, astrology, and magical weapons. This total synthesis is Golden Dawn-era Hermeticism, not a fifteenth-century design programme.

### Christian

**[Tradition/modern interpretation]** A staff that sprouts may be compared with Aaron’s rod, Jesse’s rod, pilgrimage staffs, or resurrection through living wood. No early Page carries an inscription making these identifications. They are analogical Christian readings.

### Jungian

**[Modern invention/tradition, twentieth century]** The Page becomes the puer/puella, novice, messenger from the unconscious, emerging libido, creative shadow, or beginning of individuation. Jung did not publish a Page of Wands exegesis. “Jungian Page” readings were spread by post-Jungian tarot writers, notably Pollack and later psychological readers.

## People and works

### Artists, printers, and occultists directly connected

- **Bonifacio Bembo / Zavattari workshop:** competing attributions for Milanese Visconti-Sforza cards. The Morgan notes that the older Bembo attribution has been challenged in favour of Francesco Zavattari, with replacement cards attributed to Antonio Cicognara.
- **Jean Noblet, Jean Dodal, Nicolas Conver:** principal craftsmen in the French image-lineage.
- **Jean-Baptiste Alliette (Etteilla):** first publisher of a tarot expressly designed for divination.
- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers:** transmitted French cartomantic meanings into English and helped form the Golden Dawn synthesis.
- **Pamela Colman Smith:** drew the definitive modern Page.
- **A. E. Waite:** supplied the programme and published description.
- **Frieda Harris:** radically transformed the court into the Thoth Princess.
- **Aleister Crowley:** supplied its elemental, alchemical, and Thelemic interpretation.
- **Paul Marteau:** standardized twentieth-century Marseille.
- **Paul Foster Case:** transmitted Golden Dawn-style tarot through BOTA.
- **Robert Wang; Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero:** produced distinct Golden Dawn reconstructions.
- **Alejandro Jodorowsky and Philippe Camoin:** produced a late-twentieth-century Marseille reconstruction and symbolic reading system.

### Literature, film, and art

**Italo Calvino, *The Castle of Crossed Destinies* (1969/1973):** **[Documented fact]** Tarot cards generate intersecting narratives. Calvino used reproduced historical decks, including Visconti and Marseille imagery. The Page of Wands participates as part of the narrative lexicon, but there is no single canonical “Page episode.”

**T. S. Eliot, *The Waste Land* (1922):** **[Documented fact]** Madame Sosostris names several tarot-like cards. The Page of Wands is not among them. Claims that Eliot secretly encoded it are speculative.

**W. B. Yeats:** **[Documented context]** Yeats belonged to the Golden Dawn and used its symbolic universe, but no securely sourced poem is a direct portrait of this card.

**Ingmar Bergman:** **Finding:** No verified quotation of the Page of Wands in Bergman’s films was located. *The Seventh Seal* uses medieval allegory and games, but chess, not tarot, structures its central image.

**Jodorowsky’s films:** Tarot symbolism permeates his work and he later co-authored a Marseille manual. Specific claims that a staff-bearing film character is “the Page of Wands” require scene-level evidence; none was found in authoritative sources.

**Paintings and buildings:** The Page’s standing courtier belongs broadly to Renaissance costume and court portraiture, but no building or canonical painting was verified as directly quoting this card. Conversely, the historical card has often been framed and exhibited as an art object.

## Controversies and disputes

### Egyptian origin

**[Legend, first fully publicized 1781]** Court de Gébelin claimed tarot preserved an Egyptian Book of Thoth. He offered speculative etymologies before Egyptian hieroglyphs had been deciphered. Etteilla transformed the claim into an entire Egyptian divinatory pack.

**Evidence against:** Tarot is documented in fifteenth-century northern Italy, its suit system has identifiable Islamic-European playing-card ancestry, and no Egyptian cards, manual, or transmission chain survives. Dummett emphasized that occult Egyptian derivations cite no hieroglyphic evidence ([Dummett, “Origins of Tarot”](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1981/05/14/origins-of-tarot/); Decker, Depaulis and Dummett, [*A Wicked Pack of Cards*](https://books.google.com/books/about/A_Wicked_Pack_of_Cards.html?id=MG6DAAAAMAAJ)).

The RWS Page’s pyramid-like background is therefore a 1909 Egyptological allusion at most, not evidence of ancient origin.

### Romani or “Gypsy” transmission

**[Legend, widely spread in the nineteenth century]** Writers such as Jean-Alexandre Vaillant and Papus connected tarot with Romani people, then wrongly believed to be Egyptian.

**Evidence against:** Tarot was documented in Italian courts before evidence of Romani tarot practice. Romani linguistic and historical origins lie in South Asia, not Egypt. Papus’s title *Tarot des Bohémiens* spread the myth but did not document transmission.

### Cathar and Templar origins

**[Modern invention/disputed claim]** Twentieth-century writers proposed that tarot secretly preserved Cathar dualism, Templar doctrine, or underground heresy.

**Evidence:** No fifteenth-century rulebook, commission, card inscription, or court record associates tarot with either movement. The ordinary Fante di bastoni is especially resistant to the claim: its social and playing-card ancestry is sufficient to explain it.

### Ancient Kabbalah encoded in the courts

**[Esoteric attribution, not documented origin]** Lévi, Papus, and the Golden Dawn created increasingly exact Hebrew correspondences. No early Italian Page bears a Hebrew letter, Sephirah, or divine-name inscription.

The sound historical formulation is not “the Page always was final Heh,” but “the Golden Dawn assigned the corresponding Princess to final Heh.”

### “The throne of the Aces”

**[Esoteric attribution]** This is genuine Golden Dawn teaching. It is not a scribal error, but neither is it medieval. It refers to cosmological reception and manifestation, not a literal chair in the artwork.

### Pyramids and salamanders in RWS

- **Pyramids:** likely deliberate Egyptianizing forms, but unnamed by Waite.
- **Salamanders:** conventionally read as fire creatures. Their incomplete/open circular forms are sometimes said to show undeveloped fire; that finer interpretation is modern and unverified in Waite.
- **Sprouting wand:** securely visible. Reading it as growth and living potential is visually justified, though Waite does not spell it out.

### Visconti family portrait

**[Disputed]** Heraldry proves dynastic context; it does not identify the Page. Moakley/Kaplan-style portrait identifications should be reported as proposals, not facts.

### “First illustrated minor arcana”

**[Correction]** The Sola Busca is the earliest surviving substantially complete deck with scenic pips. Court cards, however, were already figurative long before it. Saying RWS was “the first deck to illustrate the minors” erases fifteenth-century evidence.

### Misprints that became traditions

No Page-specific historical misprint comparable to famous trump-numbering anomalies was verified. Three recurrent confusions are instead editorial:

1. numbering the Page “XI” as if historically inscribed;
2. assigning the Princess the Prince’s three-decan zodiacal span;
3. calling every restored Visconti Page an extant original.

## Open questions

1. Is the Ortiz-Patiño Page part of the same original pack as the main Pierpont Morgan–Bergamo group, or only a closely related Milanese workshop product?
2. Can technical analysis securely distinguish Bembo, Zavattari, and later hands for individual court cards?
3. Which exact iconographic source prompted Smith’s pyramidal hills and salamander garment?
4. How early did “news” attach specifically to the Baton court before Etteilla’s printed card 25?
5. How did Bolognese manuscript cartomancy treat the local Fante di Bastoni, and does that meaning independently anticipate Etteilla?
6. What exact manuscript stages produced the Golden Dawn’s northern-quadrant allocations?
7. Which Page-specific teachings occur in authenticated BOTA lessons, as opposed to later summaries?
8. Are modern gender-neutral Pages best understood as transformations of the historical servant rank, the Golden Dawn Princess, or both?

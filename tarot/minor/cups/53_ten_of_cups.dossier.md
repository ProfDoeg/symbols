# Ten of Cups: Research Dossier

## Method and evidentiary labels

This dossier distinguishes:

- **Documented fact** — supported by surviving cards, archival records, or a cited primary text.
- **Scholarly reconstruction** — an inference accepted or seriously argued by specialist historians but not stated by a contemporary document.
- **Tradition** — a conventional interpretation transmitted within tarot practice.
- **Esoteric attribution** — an occult correspondence proposed within a named system.
- **Disputed claim** — a proposition for which specialists or sources disagree.
- **Legend** — a historical claim contradicted by the surviving evidence or unsupported by it.
- **Modern invention** — an interpretation or design first demonstrable in modern tarot culture.

Because this is a pip rather than a trump, many items in the requested general template—Papess/Juno, Pope/Jupiter, Fool numbering, Justice/Strength order, the Tzaddi/Heh controversy—do not alter the identity of the Ten of Cups. They are included only where they clarify the deck or system in which it appears.

### A necessary correction concerning “Heh”

**Esoteric attribution.** In Golden Dawn-derived systems, Cups correspond to Water, Briah, and the **first Heh** of יהוה, the Tetragrammaton. The Ten itself is not assigned a separate Hebrew letter or a path between Sephiroth: it is assigned to **Malkuth**, the tenth Sephirah. Accordingly:

- **Heh = the whole suit/world attribution**, not a unique letter of this card.
- **Malkuth = the number-ten attribution**.
- **Mars in Pisces = the decanic attribution**.

Confusing these three levels is common in modern charts.

# Iconography deck by deck

## Precursors and fifteenth-century cards

### Marziano da Tortona–Michelino deck, Milan, approximately 1418–1425

**Documented fact:** Marziano’s surviving treatise describes a commissioned card game for Filippo Maria Visconti, painted by Michelino da Besozzo. It had four bird suits and sixteen deities serving as superior cards.

**Finding:** It contained no Cups suit and therefore no Ten of Cups. It is relevant as a **proto-trump game**, not as an early example of this card. Calling it a “tarot deck” is a **scholarly shorthand or disputed classification**, because it does not have the later standard four suits and twenty-one-plus-Fool trump structure.

### Visconti di Modrone / Cary-Yale, Milan, approximately 1440s

These names generally refer to the same exceptional surviving group now at Yale’s Beinecke Library.

**Documented fact:** The deck contains unusual additional court ranks and virtue trumps. Surviving numeral cards are ornamented suit arrangements, not narrative scenes.

**Finding:** No surviving card can securely be presented as this deck’s Ten of Cups. Reconstruction of one from the deck’s general style would be modern.

### Brambilla deck, Milan, approximately 1440s

**Documented fact:** Forty-eight cards survive in the Pinacoteca di Brera. They belong to a luxury Milanese pack associated with the Visconti court.

**Finding:** A Ten of Cups is not among the securely surviving cards normally catalogued. The deck therefore proves an early cups suit but not the appearance of this particular rank.

### Pierpont Morgan–Bergamo Visconti-Sforza, Milan, approximately 1450–1480

The surviving cards are divided among the Morgan Library, Accademia Carrara, and Colleoni family.

**Documented fact:** The Morgan catalogue lists the surviving Cups cards as Queen, Knight, Page, Ace, Three, Five, and Nine. It does **not** list a Ten of Cups. A complete pack would have had ten numeral cards in each suit, but no complete fifteenth-century Visconti-Sforza pack survives. [Morgan Library catalogue](https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525)

**Iconographic reconstruction:** Its missing Ten probably resembled the surviving Visconti-Sforza pips: repeated gold cup signs with restrained vegetal embellishment on a luxury punched-gold ground. This remains reconstruction, not a surviving image.

### Ferrarese and Este cards, later fifteenth century

**Documented fact:** Ferrarese court accounts name the production of *trionfi*, and isolated Este-related cards survive. Their numeral cards employ repeated suit signs, decorative scrolls, animals, foliage, or heraldic detail.

**Finding:** No securely proven Ferrarese Ten of Cups supplies a continuous iconographic ancestor for RWS. Attribution and grouping of isolated “Este” cards remain specialist cataloguing problems.

### Rosenwald and Budapest sheets, late fifteenth or early sixteenth century

**Documented fact:** These are printed sheets or fragments important for reconstructing inexpensive early tarot production.

**Finding:** Their chief evidentiary value lies in trump and court designs. A complete, securely identified Ten of Cups sequence cannot be reconstructed from the surviving sheets with the confidence possible for later Marseille decks. They should not be described as narrative-pip precedents.

### Mantegna “Tarocchi,” approximately 1460s

**Documented fact:** These are fifty educational or emblematic engravings arranged in hierarchical series.

**Finding:** They are not a tarot pack, have no four playing-card suits, and contain no Ten of Cups. The word *tarocchi* is a later conventional misnomer.

## Sola Busca, engraving c. 1490; painted Venice, 1491

This is the earliest surviving complete tarot and the earliest securely surviving Ten of Cups.

The Brera identifies the work as Nicola di maestro Antonio, **Dieci di coppe**, c. 1490, and states that the complete deck’s painting is datable to Venice in 1491 from inscriptions formerly legible on two shields. [Brera catalogue and exhibition record](https://pinacotecabrera.org/en/news/mostra/il-segreto-dei-segreti-i-tarocchi-sola-busca/) The museum’s [card image](https://pinacotecabrera.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/tarocchi-Sola-Busca-Dieci-di-coppe.jpg) provides the direct visual evidence.

### What it depicts

**Documented fact:** The card is a fully illustrated Renaissance pip rather than a Marseille-style array of detached emblems. Ten chalice-like vessels are incorporated into an architectural or monumental composition. Human activity and the suit objects share a coherent pictorial space.

The entire Sola Busca minor suit is exceptional for treating pip cards as individual scenes populated by figures, buildings, implements, or actions.

### Relation to Pamela Colman Smith

**Documented fact:** The British Museum exhibited black-and-white photographs of the Sola Busca cards in 1908, shortly before Smith designed Waite’s deck. Scholars have identified strong correspondences between particular Sola Busca and RWS minors, especially the Three of Swords, Ten of Swords, and certain staff or coin compositions.

**Scholarly reconstruction:** Smith probably knew the museum photographs, and the Sola Busca demonstrated that all fifty-six minors could be made pictorial.

**Finding on the item-specific lead:** The claim that **the Sola Busca Ten of Cups itself is the source of Smith’s rainbow-family scene is not substantiated**. The two compositions do not share the distinctive family, landscape, rainbow, or cup arrangement. It is more accurate to say that the Sola Busca deck was an important precedent for Smith’s *general project of scenic minors*. Applying that influence specifically and directly to the Ten of Cups is a **disputed or unsupported extrapolation**.

## Minchiate, Florence and Tuscany, sixteenth–nineteenth centuries

Minchiate expanded the trump series to forty-one numbered special cards plus the Fool but retained four ordinary suits.

### Iconography

The Ten of Cups remains a pip: ten repeated cups arranged symmetrically, often joined by foliage or scrollwork. It does not acquire a narrative family scene.

### Meaning of the change

None in play: the enlarged cosmological trump series did not turn the suit cards into occult allegories. Later esoteric readings imposed on Minchiate pips are retrospective.

---

## Bolognese tarocchino

The historical Bolognese pack was shortened by removing numeral cards 2–5 from every suit.

**Documented fact:** The Ten of Cups survives in the reduced pack. Its design is a conventional repeated arrangement of cups, sometimes accompanied by decorative foliage.

The Ten is a playing-card rank, not an individually titled occult emblem. Bolognese divinatory traditions exist, but their meanings should not automatically be identified with Etteilla or Golden Dawn meanings.

---

## Sicilian Tarot

Sicilian tarocchi eventually used a shortened 63-card pack.

**Documented fact:** Cups are *coppe*. Among the surviving numeral cards the rank runs 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, sometimes 4 below the four courts. Thus the Ten of Cups survives and is the highest numeral beneath the court cards. [Sicilian rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/sicilian.html)

Its iconography remains non-narrative and regionally stylized. The local trump called *Miseria* and the Fool/*Fuggitivo* do not affect the card’s identity.

## Tarot de Marseille lineage

### General Marseille structure

The Ten of Cups is not a scene. It shows ten cup emblems arranged decoratively around a central floral or foliate axis. A common structure is:

- one cup laid horizontally across the upper area;
- remaining cups ordered below it;
- a central stem, blossom, or ornamental flourish;
- Roman or Arabic rank markers depending on edition.

The horizontal upper cup is not securely documented as an intentional “blocked,” “resting,” or “spilled” cup. Such readings are later interpretive developments.

### Jean Noblet, Paris, approximately 1650

Noblet’s surviving pack is incomplete. Where modern restored Noblet editions include a Ten of Cups, missing material may have been supplied by analogy with cognate cards or later patterns.

**Finding:** One must distinguish the seventeenth-century surviving evidence from a modern restorer’s completed 78-card deck. Treating every card in a commercial “Noblet” edition as an original survivor is erroneous.

### Jean Dodal, Lyon, approximately 1701–1715

Dodal’s pips establish the familiar Lyonnais-Marseille ornamental vocabulary: repeated outlined vessels, red-blue-yellow colouring, and vegetal infill. The cups remain objects in a pattern rather than containers in a represented scene. [British-period description and image index](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Tarot_de_Marseille_-_Jean_Dodal)

### Nicolas Conver, Marseille, 1760/1761

Conver’s Ten continues the ornamental arrangement. The central organic axis creates visual unity between otherwise repeated suit signs.

The date is frequently printed as **1760** in tarot literature and **1761** by modern publishers describing the BnF exemplar. The difference generally reflects manufacture, registration, or catalogue convention rather than two fundamentally different decks. [Conver reproduction record](https://dusserre.fr/cartes/tarot-de-marseille-nicolas-conver/)

### Grimaud / Paul Marteau, twentieth century

The Grimaud *Ancien Tarot de Marseille*, standardized through Paul Marteau’s 1930 edition and subsequent printings, regularized linework and colour. It helped make the Conver-like floral Ten of Cups the international image of “Marseille tarot.”

### Camoin–Jodorowsky, 1997

Philippe Camoin and Alejandro Jodorowsky presented their version as a restoration synthesized from historical fragments and printing traditions.

**Documented fact:** The Ten remains an ornamental pip with ten cups and a central vegetal structure.

**Disputed claim:** Marketing and practitioner language sometimes describe the deck as recovery of a single original or “authentic” Tarot de Marseille code. Historians generally treat it as a modern synthetic reconstruction, not a facsimile of a uniquely recoverable archetype.

**Jodorowsky–Costa interpretation:** Ten is the suit’s completion and passage toward another cycle. Cups concern emotional or receptive life; completion can become universal love or emotional enclosure. This is a **modern esoteric-numerological reading**, not an interpretation demonstrable in seventeenth-century Marseille play.

## Besançon pattern

Besançon tarot substituted **Juno** and **Jupiter** for the Papess and Pope, chiefly in eighteenth- and nineteenth-century eastern France, Switzerland, and German-speaking production centres.

**Finding:** These substitutions concern trumps II and V. They produce no special renaming or redesign of the Ten of Cups, which remains *Dix de coupe(s)* and an ornamental pip.

---

## Etteilla and the Grand Etteilla, Paris, 1789–1791

Etteilla—Jean-Baptiste Alliette—created the first tarot designed expressly and systematically for occult divination.

### Name and numbering

In lists followed by Mathers, the Ten of Cups is card **40**. Etteilla-type decks print directional meanings on the cards, commonly:

- upright: **La Ville** — the town or city;
- reversed: **Combat** or a cognate term denoting conflict.

Different “Grand Etteilla” types and later publishers vary in numbering, wording, spelling, and imagery. “Grand Etteilla” is therefore a deck family, not a single unchanged 1789 plate set.

### Iconography and significance

Etteilla reorganized the entire pack into a claimed Egyptian *Livre de Thot*. Suit cards combined conventional emblems with explicit cartomantic labels. For the Ten of Cups, the crucial innovation was not a family picture but the attachment of fixed upright and reversed verbal significations.

**Documented fact:** This “town/conflict” lineage is transmitted into Mathers and partially into Waite.

**Legend:** Its Egyptian antiquity is not supported by the deck’s Renaissance Italian documentary history.

---

## Oswald Wirth, 1889

Wirth’s original 1889 deck, designed with Stanislas de Guaita, consisted of **twenty-two trumps**.

**Finding:** It has no Ten of Cups. Later 78-card products sold under Wirth’s name are expansions by later editors or artists. Assigning their Ten of Cups to Wirth personally is a modern misattribution.

## Golden Dawn “Book T,” compiled in the 1890s

### Title and image

The manuscript calls the card:

> “The Lord of Perfected Success”

It describes a hand holding water-lilies or lotuses whose water fills and overflows all ten cups; the uppermost cup is sideways, and Mars and Pisces appear above and below. [Book T transcription](https://zanotowane.pl/1123/8041/)

### Meaning

Book T says the card signifies lasting happiness “inspired from above,” but also allows pleasure, dissipation, generosity, waste, or debauchery according to dignity.

This is important: “Perfected Success” was never purely domestic bliss. Its original Golden Dawn formulation contains both completion and the danger of excess.

### Wang and Cicero reconstructions

- **Robert Wang, Golden Dawn Tarot, 1977:** reconstructs the minor as a ritual diagram—ten cups, lotus streams, astrological glyphs, and Golden Dawn colour scales.
- **Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero, New Golden Dawn Ritual Tarot, 1991:** follows the same manuscript description with more systematic colour-scale execution.

One searchable Cicero transcription incorrectly gives “Mars in Scorpio” beside the Ten of Cups while simultaneously printing the correct 20°–30° span. This is a **modern typographical error**: Book T and the zodiacal sequence establish **Mars in Pisces**. It should not be converted into a new correspondence.

Golden Dawn colour instructions commonly give a Pisces background, red Mars-associated cups or details, and Malkuth/Briah colours involving citrine, olive, russet, and black. [Colour reconstruction](https://www.readkong.com/page/coloring-the-classic-golden-dawn-tarot-1706801)

## Waite–Smith, London, 1909

### Pamela Colman Smith’s picture

Ten golden cups appear in a rainbow-like arc. Below, a man and woman stand together with raised arms; two children dance nearby. A river, trees, green land, and a house or settlement lie beyond.

Waite describes:

> “Appearance of Cups in a rainbow … a man and woman below, evidently husband and wife.”

The children do not attend to the apparition; their happiness is immediate and bodily, whereas the adults consciously behold and salute it. [Waite, *Pictorial Key*, Ten of Cups](https://www.integralpsychology.org/uploads/1/5/3/0/15300482/waite_tarot.pdf)

### Sources and innovations

- **Documented influence:** Book T supplied the concept of complete, spiritually inspired happiness and the title “Perfected Success.”
- **Probable influence:** Etteilla’s “town” meaning appears in the distant settlement and survives in Waite’s prose.
- **Possible visual association:** the rainbow recalls Genesis 9’s sign of covenant after the Flood. Waite did not explicitly identify it in the card entry, so a Noahic/Grail-Christian reading is interpretation, not documented authorial explanation.
- **General Sola Busca influence:** probable.
- **Specific Sola Busca Ten source:** unproved and visually weak.

The decisive modern innovation is the conversion of an abstract ten-vessel pattern into a domestic, communal narrative.

## BOTA, Paul Foster Case and Jessie Burns Parke, twentieth century

The BOTA deck is based partly on Waite-Smith but returns the numeral minors to unscenic arrangements of suit symbols. Jessie Burns Parke supplied the drawings under Case’s direction.

**Documented fact:** The BOTA minor cards are pip designs rather than Smith-like narrative scenes. [BOTA deck description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.O.T.A._tarot_deck)

Case retains Golden Dawn-derived Malkuth, Water, and Mars-in-Pisces structure but emphasizes **excess**: completion at the material level can become imbalance, psychic inflation, or dissipated desire.

---

## Thoth Tarot, designed 1938–1943; first full publication 1969

### Title and image

Crowley renamed the card **Satiety**. Frieda Harris arranged ten cups as a Tree-of-Life-like system beneath an overarching lotus. Water descends and spills from cup to cup. The vessels are tilted or precarious; red and dark tones introduce pressure into the watery design.

Crowley writes that “the work proper to water is complete,” but disturbance is approaching. He calls Mars a violent, disruptive force confronting spiritualized Pisces. [Crowley, *The Book of Thoth*, “Satiety”](https://www.rahoorkhuit.net/library/crowley/equinox/volume3/vol_3_no_05.pdf)

### Meaning of the redesign

This is not Waite’s stable happy family. Harris and Crowley present fulfillment at, or slightly beyond, saturation point: nothing more can be added without overflow and decomposition. “Satiety” therefore means completion shading into surfeit.

### Tzaddi/Heh issue

Crowley’s famous assertion that “Tzaddi is not the Star” led him to exchange the Emperor and Star’s Hebrew-letter positions in his system.

**Finding:** This controversy affects the major trumps, not the Ten of Cups. Cups still belong to Water/Briah and the first Heh of יהוה; the card remains Malkuth of Water and Mars in Pisces.

## Selected modern redesigns

### Morgan-Greer Tarot, 1979

A close, borderless domestic adaptation of the Waite-Smith tradition. Modern decks of this lineage intensify intimacy and emotional immediacy rather than returning to pip abstraction.

### Thoth-derived decks

These commonly retain ten vessels, planetary and zodiacal glyphs, and “Satiety,” emphasizing emotional overfilling, pleasure reaching its limit, or success becoming unstable.

### Marseille revivals

Historicist restorations retain floral geometry and deliberately avoid prescribing a family narrative.

### Queer, feminist, and multicultural decks, late twentieth–twenty-first centuries

Many replace Smith’s apparently heterosexual nuclear family with chosen family, same-sex couples, multigenerational households, or communities.

**Modern invention with traditional continuity:** The core theme remains shared emotional fulfillment, but “family” is widened from Waite’s Edwardian household to elective kinship and communal belonging.

### The Wild Unknown and other nonhuman decks

Some replace family figures with paired animals, trees, rainbows, or nested natural forms. This converts “domestic happiness” into ecological or relational wholeness.

# The game

## Rank

In most historical Italian-suited tarot games:

- Cups and Coins are “round” suits.
- Their numeral ranking is reversed relative to Swords and Batons.
- A common order is King, Queen, Knight, Jack, Ace, 2, 3 … 10.
- Consequently, the Ten of Cups is normally the **lowest card of its suit**.

This inverse ranking survives in numerous Italian traditions. [Tarot-game overview](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/counting.html)

Exceptions and reductions matter:

- **Piedmontese shortened games:** the 10, 9, 8, and 7 of Cups may be removed entirely. [Piedicavallo rules](https://www.pagat.com/tarot/piedicavallo.html)
- **Sicilian tarot:** the Ten is the highest surviving numeral below the courts, because the Ace and low numerals were removed.
- **Modern French tarot:** French-suited red suits rank differently; there is no literal Cup card, its functional analogue being a Heart rank.
- **Bolognese tarocchino:** 2–5 are removed, but the Ten remains.
- **Minchiate:** Cups follow the round-suit ranking, making Ten low.

## Point value

In classical card-point systems the Ten of Cups is ordinarily a **low or blank card**, valuable mainly for winning a trick, forming a counting packet, or contributing to combinations in games that recognize suit sequences.

Depending on counting method:

- It may have no independent honour value.
- It may contribute one nominal point before deductions or grouping.
- It may be useful in sequence combinations, but rules differ greatly by locality.

It is not an *oudler*, honour, *tarocco*, *bout*, or trump.

## Fool’s role and trump-order disputes

These do not change the Ten’s rank inside Cups, but they define its gaming environment:

- In many tarot games the Fool is an excuse: it normally cannot win a trick, but its owner retrieves it after playing it, sometimes compensating the winner of the trick.
- In other Italian variants the Fool participates in combinations or has local restrictions.
- Dummett classified early Italian trump orders as:
  - **A:** Bologna/Florence;
  - **B:** Ferrara;
  - **C:** Milan.
- Justice, Strength, and Temperance move relative to other trumps among these orders.
- The Angel outranks the World in many Italian traditions; Milanese and later Marseille-derived orders usually place the World highest.
- Papess/Pope substitutions, the four Bolognese Moors, Besançon’s Juno/Jupiter, and Sicilian *Miseria* are regional trump developments only.

The Ten of Cups is unaffected because suit rank is determined separately.

### Astrological verification

**Verified:** Book T gives Mars and Pisces for the Ten of Cups. The zodiacal sequence places it in the third decan, 20°–30° Pisces. The Thoth deck and Crowley’s commentary retain this attribution.

**Misprint noted:** A searchable Cicero-derived transcription reads “Mars in Scorpio, 20°–30°,” an internally impossible combination. It is a transcription or typesetting error, not an alternate Golden Dawn teaching.

### Sephiroth and worlds

- Ten = **Malkuth**, “Kingdom.”
- Cups = **Briah**, the World of Creation, in Golden Dawn tarot classification.
- Therefore the card is **Malkuth of Briah**.
- The familiar citrine–olive–russet–black quartering belongs to Malkuth’s colour symbolism; actual reconstructions combine it differently with Pisces and Mars colours.

### Courts

The requested court-card sub-elements and zodiacal spans do not apply to this pip. In the Golden Dawn scheme, Cups courts occupy watery zodiacal sectors and express Fire, Water, Air, and Earth of Water according to rank; the Ten has no court sub-element.

# Divinatory meanings by author

## Etteilla, 1780s

**Esoteric/cartomantic attribution:**

- Upright: *La Ville* — town, city, place of residence; expanded lists add esteem, honour, reputation, or civic standing.
- Reversed: *Combat* — conflict, dispute, opposition.

These are transmitted through Etteilla deck labels and later lists. Exact vocabulary differs among Etteilla Types I–III and nineteenth-century editions. A single definitive “1789 wording” could not be established from the accessible scans with enough confidence to erase those variants.

## S. L. MacGregor Mathers, 1888

Mathers gives:

> “The Town wherein one resides, Honor, Consideration, Esteem, Virtue, Glory, Reputation.”

Reversed he lists combat, strife, opposition, differences, and dispute. He later acknowledges that much of his practical material is taken from Etteilla. [Mathers, *The Tarot*, pp. 15, 18](https://www.howardnema.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/the-tarot-1888-by-macgregor-mathers-1.pdf)

## Golden Dawn Book T

No simple upright/reversed pair is prescribed; meanings depend on “dignity,” meaning the card’s condition in relation to surrounding cards.

Favourable:

- permanent success;
- happiness inspired from above;
- quietness and peacemaking;
- kindness, pity, generosity;
- matter settled and complete good fortune.

Ill-dignified:

- dissipation;
- debauchery;
- wantonness;
- waste.

This is the historical root of both Waite’s bliss and Crowley’s surfeit.

## A. E. Waite, 1910

Waite’s upright meanings are:

> “Contentment, repose of the entire heart; the perfection of that state.”

He adds perfect human love and friendship, and—following the Etteilla lineage—the querent’s town, village, or country. Reversed: false-hearted repose, indignation, and violence. [*Pictorial Key to the Tarot*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Pictorial_Key_to_the_Tarot)

The widespread modern formula “happy family, marriage, happily ever after” is a fair inference from Smith’s picture but abbreviates Waite’s more extensive wording.

## Aleister Crowley, 1944

Crowley does not supply a conventional reversed meaning. His method uses elemental, astrological, and positional interaction.

Core meaning:

- pleasure brought to complete saturation;
- fulfillment whose stability is doubtful;
- emotional or sensual completion becoming stagnant;
- Mars disturbing Pisces;
- the end of Water’s proper work.

“Satiety” is therefore neither simple happiness nor simple misfortune. It is fullness at the instant before overflow.

## Paul Foster Case

Case’s keyword is commonly reported as **Excess**.

Favourable or spiritual expression:

- lasting success;
- high aspiration realized;
- inspiration and psychic development;
- happiness.

Adverse or lower-plane expression:

- psychism without balance;
- uncontrolled desire;
- mental injury through premature occult practice;
- dissipation or bodily/emotional excess.

This is Golden Dawn “Perfected Success” refracted through Case’s caution about unbalanced psychic development.

## Rachel Pollack

Pollack treats the card as joyous shared life, with the rainbow suggesting blessing and the children spontaneous happiness. She also observes that ten carries completion so far that change must follow. Her later writing explicitly identifies Malkuth, Mars in Pisces, and “Perfected Success.” [Pollack’s Ten of Cups essay](https://www.llewellyn.com/journal/article/1814)

Pollack generally treats reversals contextually: the ideal family may be disrupted, inaccessible, sentimentalized, or imposed as an unreal standard.

## Mary K. Greer

Greer’s practice-oriented approach expands reversals beyond simple opposition. Applied to this card, her method permits:

- blocked or delayed harmony;
- family conflict;
- private happiness rather than public celebration;
- rejection of an imposed domestic ideal;
- the shadow side of belonging;
- emotional fulfillment that exists but cannot be expressed.

Greer also records the technical formula “Malkuth of Briah, Perfected Success, Mars in Pisces” and characterizes it as fixed or completed force. [Mary K. Greer’s Golden Dawn notes](https://marykgreer.com/tag/golden-dawn/)

Because Pollack and Greer remain copyrighted modern authors, their positions are paraphrased rather than reproduced at length.

# Esoteric and symbolic readings

## Elemental: Water

**First clear occult-system development:** nineteenth-century French ceremonial magic, systematized most influentially through Lévi, Papus, and the Golden Dawn.

The cup is the vessel that receives and contains. Water supplies the modern associations of emotion, intuition, relationship, receptivity, and imagination.

**Historical caution:** Medieval Cups were suit signs. There is no evidence that fifteenth-century players universally treated every Cup as an elemental Water diagram.

## Tetragrammaton and Heh

Lévi and Papus helped popularize the four suits as expressions of the fourfold divine name; the Golden Dawn stabilized Cups as the first Heh, Water, and Briah.

The Ten is thus the material realization of the watery/creative principle. Heh is not independently printed on historical Ten of Cups cards.

## Kabbalistic: Malkuth of Briah

In Golden Dawn Qabalah:

- Kether is the initial root;
- Malkuth is manifestation, kingdom, result, or final embodiment;
- Briah is Creation and the World of Cups.

“Perfected Success” arises because the current has reached manifestation. Its shadow arises because a current that has nowhere further to descend may stagnate, overflow, or begin again.

## Astrological: Mars in Pisces

This attribution appears in the Golden Dawn’s decanic mapping of the thirty-six cards Two through Ten.

Symbolic synthesis:

- Mars supplies drive, heat, force, rupture, appetite.
- Pisces supplies permeability, sympathy, imagination, dissolution.
- Harmonious reading: energetic commitment to collective happiness, generosity, peacemaking.
- Adverse reading: excess, porous boundaries, intoxication, emotional compulsion, or disruption of peace.

These are esoteric interpretations, not astronomical findings.

## Alchemical

**Modern occult development:** The ten cups may be treated as vessels filled by a single circulating fluid, resembling multiplication, distillation, or completion of an alchemical operation. In Thoth, the overflowing chain can suggest matter unable to contain further influx.

No fifteenth-century text identifying the Sola Busca Ten of Cups as an alchemical formula was found. Alchemical readings of that deck may be suggestive, but many remain disputed.

## Christian and Grail readings

The chalice naturally invites comparison with:

- Eucharistic vessels;
- Christ’s cup at the Last Supper;
- the Holy Grail;
- divine grace received into a vessel.

Waite belonged to Christian esoteric and Grail-oriented circles, making such resonances historically plausible in his wider work.

However:

- Waite does not explicitly call the Ten of Cups a Grail card in its *Pictorial Key* entry.
- The RWS rainbow may invoke Noah’s covenant, but Waite does not say so there.
- Claiming a continuous secret Grail meaning from medieval tarot to Waite is **tradition or legend**, not documented transmission.

## Hermetic

The Golden Dawn card embodies the Hermetic habit of superimposing multiple systems:

- numeral/Sephirah;
- suit/world;
- planet/sign/decan;
- divine-name letter;
- angelic rulers;
- colour scale;
- divinatory dignity.

The resulting card is not simply “ten cups”: it is a compressed diagram of manifestation in Water under Mars in Pisces.

## Jungian

Jung did not publish a canonical interpretation of the Ten of Cups.

Later Jungian tarot writers interpret:

- the couple as conjunction of psychic opposites;
- the children as renewal or spontaneous life;
- the house as the structured Self or psychic home;
- the rainbow as reconciliation between conscious and transpersonal orders;
- the ten cups as completed emotional integration.

This is a **modern Jungian application**, spread by post-1960s archetypal tarot literature, not a teaching demonstrably authored by Jung.

The shadow reading is equally important: the image may represent projection of the “perfect family,” denial of conflict, compulsory happiness, or exclusion of those who do not fit the household ideal.

# People and works

## Directly attached to the card

- **Nicola di maestro Antonio** — attribution used by the Brera for the Sola Busca Ten of Cups, c. 1490.
- **Jean-Baptiste Alliette/Etteilla** — first systematic printed cartomantic meanings attached to the card.
- **S. L. MacGregor Mathers** — transmitted Etteilla-derived meanings into English in 1888 and participated in the Golden Dawn milieu.
- **Golden Dawn compilers** — created “Lord of Perfected Success,” Mars in Pisces, and Malkuth of Briah.
- **Pamela Colman Smith** — created the family-and-rainbow image in 1909.
- **A. E. Waite** — supplied its published description and meanings.
- **Jessie Burns Parke and Paul Foster Case** — BOTA pip and “Excess” doctrine.
- **Lady Frieda Harris and Aleister Crowley** — Thoth image and “Satiety.”
- **Robert Wang** — 1977 Golden Dawn reconstruction.
- **Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero** — later ritual reconstruction.
- **Philippe Camoin and Alejandro Jodorowsky** — 1997 Marseille synthesis.
- **Marianne Costa** — co-developed Jodorowsky’s modern Marseille numerology and interpretive method.
- **Rachel Pollack and Mary K. Greer** — major modern interpreters.

## Literature, film, buildings, and painting

### Italo Calvino

*The Castle of Crossed Destinies* constructs stories from tarot layouts, particularly Visconti-Sforza and Marseille imagery.

**Finding:** No uniquely central or securely famous narrative role for the Ten of Cups comparable to Calvino’s use of major trumps was traced. It is relevant to tarot’s literary reception, not demonstrably a work “about” this card.

### T. S. Eliot

*The Waste Land* names “the man with three staves,” the Wheel, the one-eyed merchant, the blank card, and the “drowned Phoenician Sailor.”

**Finding:** The Ten of Cups is not named. Connecting Eliot’s water imagery or Tarot passage specifically to it is unsupported.

### W. B. Yeats

Yeats’s Golden Dawn membership makes Golden Dawn tarot part of his occult environment.

**Finding:** No securely traced poem or prose passage singles out the Ten of Cups. Broad claims that the card directly generated a particular Yeats poem require evidence not found here.

### Ingmar Bergman

Bergman’s *The Seventh Seal* uses medieval death, chess, procession, and religious imagery.

**Finding:** No documented Ten-of-Cups quotation was found. Treating the film as quoting this card is a modern visual association.

### Alejandro Jodorowsky’s films

Jodorowsky’s films, especially *The Holy Mountain*, use tarot, alchemy, and initiation.

**Finding:** Jodorowsky’s direct connection to this card is strongest through the Camoin deck and *The Way of Tarot*, not through a securely documented shot reproducing the Ten of Cups.

### Paintings and buildings

No painting or building could be securely documented as intentionally quoting the Ten of Cups before Waite-Smith. Modern murals, tattoos, illustrations, and domestic artworks frequently quote the RWS composition, but these are numerous derivative works rather than a stable art-historical canon.

The distant house in Smith’s image has not been securely identified as a specific British building. Claims naming an exact estate or village should be treated as disputed until supported by Smith’s sketches, correspondence, or a close architectural match.

# Controversies and disputes

## Egyptian origin

### Claim

Court de Gébelin argued in 1781 that tarot preserved an Egyptian sacred book. Etteilla transformed that proposal into an explicitly Egyptian divinatory deck and “Book of Thoth.” Papus and later occultists spread it internationally.

### Evidence

None of the surviving Egyptian evidence contains the tarot pack. Tarot’s standard suit-and-trump structure is documented in fifteenth-century northern Italy; occult Egyptian interpretations appear more than three centuries later.

### Classification

**Legend as history; documented and highly influential esoteric tradition.**

The distinction matters: Gébelin’s theory is historically false as an origin account but historically decisive in creating occult tarot.

## Romani or “Gypsy” origin

### Claim

Papus’s title *Le Tarot des Bohémiens* and associated writers claimed that Romani people carried ancient Egyptian wisdom into Europe.

### Evidence

The documentary chronology runs from Italian court and urban card culture to later European tarot games. No fifteenth-century evidence ties tarot’s invention to Romani communities.

### Classification

**Legend**, spread through nineteenth-century occultism and racialized romantic literature. Romani fortune-telling traditions are real, but that does not establish Romani invention of tarot.

## Cathar origin

### Claim

The trumps encode Cathar doctrine or survived from Languedocian heresy.

### Evidence

No Cathar tarot cards, Cathar text describing tarot, or transmission chain to fifteenth-century Milan, Ferrara, Florence, or Bologna has been produced.

### Classification

**Modern speculative legend.** It is not specifically supported by the Ten of Cups.

## Templar origin

### Claim

The pack secretly preserved Templar teachings following the suppression of the order.

### Evidence

No Templar document mentions tarot; the Templars were dissolved before the first tarot documentation. Similarities proposed between cups, Grail material, or initiation symbols are analogical.

### Classification

**Legend or modern invention.**

## A concealed Grail code

### Claim

Cups directly descend from the Holy Grail tradition, and the Ten represents final Grail attainment.

### Evidence

Cups are ordinary Italian suit emblems with cognates in European and earlier playing-card systems. Later Christian occultists could and did spiritualize them, but no early cardmaker’s text establishes a Grail program.

### Classification

**Plausible later symbolic reading; unsupported as an origin claim.**

## Was the Sola Busca Ten the source for Smith’s Ten?

### Evidence for

- Smith probably saw or knew the Sola Busca photographs.
- Sola Busca provided a historic model for scenic minors.
- Specific RWS cards have persuasive Sola Busca parallels.

### Evidence against

- The two Tens of Cups do not share Smith’s rainbow, parents, children, landscape, or domestic narrative.
- Publications usually cite other individual Sola Busca cards when demonstrating direct borrowings.
- “Sola Busca inspired RWS minors” has often been expanded carelessly into “every RWS minor copies its Sola Busca counterpart.”

### Classification

**General influence: strong scholarly reconstruction. Specific Ten-to-Ten derivation: unproved and probably overstated.**

## “Mars in Scorpio”

A Cicero-related searchable text gives Mars in Scorpio for the Ten while also giving 20°–30°.

The surrounding Golden Dawn sequence is:

- Eight of Cups: Saturn in Pisces;
- Nine: Jupiter in Pisces;
- Ten: Mars in Pisces.

### Classification

**Typographical or transcription error that should not become tradition.**

## 1760 versus 1761 for Conver

Both dates circulate. This reflects differing bibliographic conventions concerning the historical pack and extant exemplar, not a meaningful occult variant.

### Classification

**Minor cataloguing/date discrepancy.**

## Waite-Smith publication date

The deck was issued in 1909; *The Pictorial Key* appeared in 1910, while surviving title pages may read 1911. Saying simply “the 1910 tarot deck” or “the 1911 deck” collapses separate publication events.

### Classification

**Bibliographic ambiguity, not esoteric mystery.**

## “Ten means perfection”

This is not a universal historical meaning.

- In game play, ten is merely a rank.
- In Pythagorean traditions, the decad may signify completeness.
- In Qabalah, ten is Malkuth and manifestation, also furthest from Kether.
- In Crowley, completion creates impending disturbance.
- In Case, it can become excess.

### Classification

**System-dependent esoteric attribution.**

## Historical corrections and their limits

Michael Dummett, Ronald Decker, Thierry Depaulis, and later historians established through cards, rules, and archives that tarot began as a Renaissance European game and that systematic occult tarot emerged in the late eighteenth century. [Dummett’s clarification](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1981/05/14/origins-of-tarot/)

Kaplan’s encyclopedic catalogues made early decks and provenance more widely accessible. Robert M. Place and Helen Farley have communicated the distinction between documented Renaissance origins and later occult interpretations to broader audiences.

Their correction does not prove that early users never improvised moral, poetic, or divinatory associations. It means that evidence for a primordial Egyptian, Romani, Templar, Cathar, Kabbalistic, or Grail origin has not survived and conflicts with the positive Renaissance evidence.

# Open questions

1. **The earliest lost Ten of Cups.** Standard Cups suits existed before 1490, but no securely identified Ten survives from the earliest Milanese luxury packs.

2. **Sola Busca authorship.** The Brera uses an attribution to Nicola di maestro Antonio, while details of design, engraving, colouring, patronage, and textual program remain subjects of scholarship.

3. **Sola Busca iconographic program.** Alchemical, historical, Roman, and humanist explanations compete. No consensus decodes every minor scene.

4. **Smith’s precise source mixture.** Book T and Etteilla help explain her card, and the Sola Busca influenced her project generally, but no surviving instruction sheet or letter gives a source-by-source account of the Ten.

5. **The rainbow.** Genesis, covenant, natural wonder, Golden Dawn colour theory, and simple pictorial transformation of an overflowing arc are possible sources. Waite’s card entry does not decide among them.

6. **The building in the RWS landscape.** No secure identification was found.

7. **Etteilla variants.** The wording and numbering of the Ten differ among original and derivative Etteilla types; establishing the exact first printed state requires direct comparison of all accessible 1780s sheets and books.

8. **Noblet restorations.** Modern completed decks should be checked card by card against what actually survives in the BnF.

9. **Early cartomancy.** Absence of surviving evidence before the eighteenth century is not logical proof that nobody ever used tarot informally for divination, but it prevents treating such use as documented.

10. **Card-specific literary reception.** Tarot is important to Calvino, Yeats’s occult world, Eliot’s poetic imagery, Bergman’s medieval visual culture, and Jodorowsky’s art, but direct quotations of this particular pip remain poorly documented.
